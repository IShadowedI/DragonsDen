Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Sequencing with Shang-Chi and the Ten Rings

July 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Azorius kinda gets a bad rap in Commander. When you take a color known for interesting catch-up mechanics and toolboxy answers, and put it together with the color of interaction and on-stack operation, you're somehow left with a languid, sluggish control identity that's honed in on blinking and lifegain. That reputation is deserved, of course, with cards like Hope Estheim & Brago, King Eternal being two of the most popular options, but Azorius is not without its sleeper hits. A few months back, I broke ground on bringing to light a deck centered on Krile Baldesion, putting tempo and self-mill at the forefront of the colors, so I'm no stranger to making Azorius a bit more exciting. I want to once again urge Blue-White players towards some spice with a new option from Marvel Super Heroes: Shang Chi and the Ten Rings. While this card reads as a banal refrain of lifegain & card draw, as you might expect in the colors, the precise way he works leads to some of the coolest pop-off turns I've seen in a recent Commander. After all, we can count to 10 more than once, right?

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Ten Hut

First, let's get it out of the way—this version of Shang Chi is pretty good in combat. With First Strike and the capacity to grow at Instant-speed, it's rare he goes unblocked, and rarer still someone fails to respect the fact you could feint and present an extra 2-5 power on the stack. That's fine enough, but the meat of the card is in what I'd term as its 'quest', because when Shang Chi hits 10 +1/+1 counters, you immediately get a trigger to draw 5 cards and gain 5 life (thereby adding another 5 +1/+1 counters, one at a time). The thing is, that trigger is untethered to the counter gain itself. That means if, as an example, you removed one of his 10 counters before the new ones were placed by way of drawing 5 with that second trigger, you'd go back to 9, then to 10...and do it again. Importantly, you can do this recursively around the resolution of the first trigger to draw 5, as Shang checks for every card drawn individually, meaning you can potentially spin up to drawing a quarter of your Library with relative ease. Azorius can't remove counters easily though, right?

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

At Instant-Speed, there's not a ton of ways to do exactly what Shang wants infinitely, without some source of mana input. Sure, you can tap O'Aka, Travelling Merchant, or pay {2} into something like Sage of Fables/Ray Fillet, Man Ray, but it's much harder to go infinite with these cards. Instead, I'd like to opt into something Blue-White can search with relative ease: A Legendary Artifact by the name of Agatha's Soul Cauldron. If we stick cards like Spinal Parasite, Walking Ballista, or Arcbound Reclaimer under the cauldron, Shang will be able to turn his counters into something more productive, ad infinitum. Moreover, if we lean into the fact -1/-1 counters and +1/+1 counters cancel out, even Barrenton Medic does the trick here. So, that's the pitch: We flex Blue & White's ability to tutor Artifacts (specifically Legends) and various affinity-adjacent tools that can freely remove counters, to have Shang Chi put the 10th counter on himself over & over. Not just that, but because we're already on Walking Ballista as a core facet of the strategy, we can flex it as a win condition alongside Heliod, Sun-Crowned once we draw through the deck.

Ten-Thousand Tenth Birthdays

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

It's certainly not new ground to use Agatha's as a 'Secret Commander' of sorts, but using it as what amounts to a counters-matter storm enabler is novel indeed. We've seen Agatha's at the forefront of decks like Rachel Weeks' Sisay deck on Extra Turns, and I certainly consider it given my proclivity for activated abilities, but Shang-Chi is doing something fairly novel with it; we're not just A+B as a combo, but instead forced to steer through a repeated sequence, often losing access to the soft loop if we allow all triggers to resolve. That's why sequencing is the big focus here, as if we stack too many counters with no draw-5-gain-5 trigger on the stack, our pop-off is essentially over without some new means of drawing a card. Instant-speed cantrips are good here (as they double as combat tricks, and alongside some unblockable Equipment like Brotherhood Regalia we can kill folks with Commander Damage), but they do ultimately cost mana. What defines the bracket on this list isn't so much how fast you manage to pop off, but how mana-intensive the rest of the gameplan is, following. If you tear through your Library and arrive at a hand full of moxen & mana-positive Artifacts, you can be far less conservative about when you try to wrap things up.

Was this just another excuse to jam things like Workhorse, Crystalline Crawler, & Cryptic Trilobite in another list? Perhaps, but I do honestly feel going the Agatha's route is the best way of actually getting Shang Chi to feel broken, rather than merely good. You can still get a ton of value from flexing options like Hopeful Initiate & Scholar of New Horizons, especially in the midgame, but they're just too slow or expensive to be worthwhile as potential combo pieces during the endgame. I think too many decks are focused on getting to the initial 10 counter trigger, especially when things like Windfall, Whirlpool Rider, and even weird ones like Flux/Tolarian Winds/Induced Amnesia exist. Blue has no shortage of ways to rip through your deck, and whether it's looting, cantripping, or wheeling, edging Shang-Chi close to his threshold is of low concern. As an added bonus, the wheels that put cards in the yard are fantastic means of readying counter-removal Creatures for Agatha's.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Yet this is where I have to say 'decklist pending', because while all the constituent parts of Shang Chi are clear in my mind, the actual 99 remains somehow...elusive. I kept running into the fact that, if Shang got removed too early because people were worried about him as a combat threat, we'd never generate enough counters on the second or third cast to start doing combo things, before just winning with Heliod+Ballista. High Bracket 3 is where I think that version of Shang Chi sits, and even just cutting Heliod does a ton to drop him down to a more modest version of B3. Yet still, holding up mana for countermagic felt bad, even in Blue-White, when we just wanted to progress the gameplan. Going down in card advantage via free spells like Misdirection felt worse here than normal, because so many of our counter accelerants care about discarding/shuffling our hand back and drawing that many, meaning they'd not be high enough value if we kept pitching. I know Shang Chi should be good, should be GREAT even, but there's an odd push & pull between Azorius' best interactive suite and how Shang needs to scale. Perhaps slowing down and accepting Flux might not be especially good for the Commander is wise, who's to say. I mention this because my intent is to arm you, dear reader, to come up with a Shang Chi I haven't yet, and crack the code on this powerful new Commander—bring some new speed & heat to an anemic control pairing, and punch your opponents into the stratosphere.
