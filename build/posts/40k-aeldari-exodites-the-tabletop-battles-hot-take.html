[40K] Aeldari Exodites: The Tabletop Battles Hot Take

August 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Ahead of the release of the Kill Team: Exodite set this weekend, the full details of using Exodites in 40K (crucially including points costs) have been added to the Warhammer 40K app, so we can now take a look at how they might fit into Aeldari armies in the 41st Millennium.

The painted Exodites in this article are from a review copy provided to us by Games Workshop. Check out our Model Review for details of how building and painting them went!

Credit: Sky Serpent

General Rules

All the Exodites, both Characters and regulars, share quite a few common attributes. All their profiles are nearly identical - they're all M10" Mounted Mobile units, and defensively they're T5 with 4W (which is good), but flimsily armoured at only a 4+ with no invulnerable save. They're all OC2, and all except the Clanblade are Ld7+ (the noble leader gets 6+). They also all have an Extra Attacks offensive melee profile from their steeds, which is a respectable 3A WS3+ S5 AP-1, which doesn't light up the world, but combines with their regular output to let them really bully most other chaff.

All the Exodites also have the Aeldari Battle Focus army rule, providing them with extra speed and/or movement tricks, and crucially all have the Asuryani faction keyword, which ensures they're compatible with Stratagems and Enhancements from the Codex that care about this. That's especially relevant for the Warhost and Windrider Host Detachments, but has a few other niche benefits like the Stonesinger activating Seer Council Stratagems, and the units working fully with Ynnari.

The Units

Dragon Knights

Your regular Exodite squad is Dragon Knights, which come as either three models for 90pts or six for 180pts, with the standard statlines above.

Exodites are famous for fighting with laser lances, and those are on show here, each model getting a 3A WS3+ S6 AP-2 D3 Lance melee profile on top of their dragon's claws, and also one close range shot at S6 AP-2 D3. For shooting, each also packs a solar carbine, which is not too shabby - 18" 2A BS3+ S4 AP0 D1 with Rapid Fire 2 giving a decent amount of shots at close range if they need to clear out a cheap screen.

Their unit ability leans into being hard to pin down, with the unit being able to fall-back, shoot and charge freely. They can also fight in a fairly loose melee formation thanks to the Agile Reach ability, which means that as long as the unit is engaged with a foe, models in the unit that are within 3" of the enemy get to fight rather than just 2".

Finally, for each three models in the squad they can take two Drakolithe tokens. When an enemy ends a move either within 8" of an unengaged unit of Dragon Knights, or engaged with them, you can spend one token to try and deal them a Mortal Wound on a 3+. It's a neat extra bonus, and don't forget that Pile Ins are a move, so if an opponent charges you then piles in, you can blow multiple tokens in quick succession.

How does this stack up? At baseline, this unit is pretty deadly for 90pts, and although the lack of an invulnerable save is painful, just being T5 with 4W each makes them quite a bit sturdier than some other things in the roster that you can spend this many points on, though obviously with a larger footprint. Like a lot of Aeldari units, they do suffer significantly from the faction being desperately short of re-rolls, which means they have to be quite judicious about picking targets, and can sometimes just whiff.

By themselves, I think these are at least worth a look in Warhost, Windrider Host and Devoted of Ynnead. As a Recon Detachment, Warhost is pretty happy to spend 90pts on a reasonably fast unit that can expect to charge something and do meaningful damage before going down. You can also use Lightning Fast Reactions to improve their durability, and boosted Battle Focus can either help get where they're needed, or get them out of trouble. In Ynnari, meanwhile, being a T5 Mounted unit is really good with Macabre Resilience, as this is unconditional -1 to Wound, so can badly blank anything that tries to handle these with S4 stuff, and Ynnari are also generically happy to have a 90pts brawler unit rolling around, plus fast OC6 to tip an objective against 5-model chaff when playing Priority Assets.

Both of those pale against the biggest game in town, which is obviously Windrider Host. Everything in this Detachment only cares about MOUNTED, not MOUNTED FLY, so these work with pretty much everything, including freely leaping back into reserves, moving after killing something, Advancing and Charging and (most importantly) getting a 4+ Invulnerable Save against shooting. They compete with Shining Spears for a slot, but it feels like there's a real argument for taking some of each, especially as the Exodite lances are S6 rather than S5, so hit better into Gravis-style Infantry. A full six-model unit is also easier to position than an equivalent Shining Spear squad thanks to the large round bases on the latter, particularly with Agile Reach to help. Definite game here for both sizes of unit.

These three Detachments are also where you might choose to boost your squad with some added Characters, and you get two options for this.

Clanblade

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

The Clanblade is a force multiplier for Dragon Knights who also diversifies their melee output a bit. As a Leader, he gives the whole unit Sustained Hits 1 in melee, which is very nasty on a squad's laser lances. He also leans into them being used as disruptive bully pieces via the Cornered Prey rule, which means any unit falling back from them has to use Desperate Escape, with all the risks inherent in that. Unlike some rules like this, it does affect enemy Monsters and Vehicles, so can be particularly good for pinning down enemy hulls.

The Clanblade also brings their own weapons to the fight, swinging with moonblades that hit at 5A WS2+ S4 AP-2 D2, and adding reliability via both Lethal Hits and Twin-Linked. There's a little bit of tension here with this being a pretty pure anti-Infantry profile where the rest of the unit can flex between most targets, but the abilities smooth that out so that the Clanblade won't do nothing into a hull, and adding Sustained to the rest of the unit's lances there seems great.

As a Character, the Clanblade can also take Enhancements, and there are some great options in both Windrider and Warhost. Windrider's best options are either Firstdrawn Blade, providing Scouts 9" at a bargain price, or Mirage Field to give the unit a permanent -1 to Hit, which is tempting if you're going for a full squad. Warhost, meanwhile, adds the Gift of Foresight enhancement, providing a free use of Command re-roll each Battle Round, which these seem better suited for than most places you can currently put that.

The Clanblade comes in at 70pts, so a basic squad with one runs you 160pts which seems OK as a counter-charge threat with enough reach to go out and press the attack when needed, but might fall in that awkward in-between spot where they're a little too pricy for a throwaway unit (whereas three regulars alone are cheap), but not consistently deadly enough that they make the cut over something spicier. They also need a pretty big staging spot, which are somewhat at a premium on the current maps. In Windrider Host I don't think that matters and you're very happy just taking a squad, and I think they could work in Warhost, but that you may end up just taking a bare bones squad and calling it a day.

Sky Serpent: I really like the Clanblade but it feels to me that if it’s a choice of adding one to a unit or more Dragon Knights, I’d find the 20pts and just go for a bigger unit. The Clanblade leading a Dinosaur Deathstar is an enticing prospect but likely a little costly too, just go all in with some sprinkling of Windrider Host enhancements that Wings suggested above.

Stonesinger

Credit: Sky Serpent

Your other Character option is the Stonesinger, who is a Support for the Knights. This means you can't take them alone (and you low key might otherwise), but can choose to add both Characters to a squad if you so choose.

Whether you would actually do that is a little uncertain, as the Stonesinger pulls in a quite different direction than the rest of the setup here, being focused on ranged attacks. The ranged attacks they have are none too shabby, supplementing the regular solar carbine with a psychic attack for hitting Monsters/Vehicles and an acid-spit Torrent attack for elite infantry. The Psychic attack is three shots at BS3+ S4 AP-2 D3 with Anti-Monster/Vehicle 3+, while the torrent is anti-Non Monster/Vehicle 3+, and has 3 shots with Blast at S3, and AP-2 D2. Both of these are good profiles, even if they want to target completely separate things, and increase the unit's multi-phase threat quite considerably (and the spit is decent enough for Overwatch). In melee they're a bit weaker than the rest of the squad, swinging with their staff, which does get to be anti-Non Monster/Vehicle 2+, but only gets three attacks at AP-1 D1.

The last trick they have up their sleeve is a Psychic ability that you trigger at the end of your Fight Phase as long as the unit isn't Battle-shocked. If you do, you roll a d6, and on anything except a 1, pick a visible enemy Monster/Vehicle (not Titanic) to be Ensnared until your next turn, getting -2" to move (this does not stack with Pinned from Night Spinners). This is OK, but has some risks to it - if you roll a 1, your unit gets Battle-shocked, and more generally if you can see an enemy Vehicle they can see you, which is often ominous for this unit's survival prospects.

The Stonesinger is slightly cheaper than the Clanblade at 60pts, so if you desperately need to squeeze an Enhancement on, they're a slightly cheaper route to that. They do also diversify the unit's capabilities a fair bit, as in some situations they'll be able to do some meaningful damage without rocking into melee. That might make a unit with one interesting in Warhost, where you can be more cagey with how you use them, but I think the biggest draw for the Stonesinger is that it switches on a bunch of stuff in Seer Council. They're a pretty good place to put the Torc of Morai-Heg, since they'll generally be close to the action, and that also makes them excellent for triggering Isha's Fury or Presentiment of Dread. If you really want to ruin the day of Thousand Sons specifically, you could also take Runes of Warding here.

I think on balance, outside of Seer Council, I'm more interested in taking a Clanblade than a Stonesinger, but being cheaper does create some appeal. Seer Council is also the main place I'd be especially excited about a unit with both - this can fill the dual purpose of providing a moderately sturdy counter-charge piece and moving a large-based Psyker into position on a key turn, particularly if you add the Torc.

Leystalker

Credit: Sky Serpent

Last, but certainly not least, is the Leystalker. They have the standard profile for the units, but are much harder to pin down and deal with thanks to having Lone Operative. Supplementing this with Scout 9" makes these ideal position pieces, and particularly good in Recon or Priority Assets plans.

They also have a decently nasty shooting attack, firing two shots at 36" with BS2+ S6 AP-2 D3 with Precision, and Devastating Wounds against non-Vehicle/Monster targets. Any non-Vehicle/Monster that they hit also has to take a Battle-shock test at -1. In exchange for this their melee is pretty paltry, but they do get a couple of Drakolithes (that work the same as for the Knights) to take a few foes with them if charged.

This package runs you 80pts, and that's pretty great. They're slightly pricier than the Death Jester as a Lone Operative, but they have some upsides that recommend them (and you can also just take both, and you can't normally take more than one Jester). The large base makes them better for screening, and OC2 on a Lone Operative is quite rare, and lets them trump single models touching a point. They're also the first Asuryani Lone Operative, which gives them some interesting Enhancement options. Warhost shines here - they're another place you'd be perfectly happy to put Gift of Foresight to increase their reliability, and if you're willing to go spendy, they're one of the best places for the Phoenix Gem. There's also a surprisingly relevant option in Guardian Battlehost, with Craftworld's Champion giving them OC5. That's extremely good on a single model, and can really help smooth over the Primary at key points, or stopping enemy attempts to flip your expansion.

The Leystalker seems near certain to see play, as they're just a good unit. Like the others, Windrider host boosts them even further, as being able to pull them into Reserves adds even more value, but I can see taking the Leystalker in almost any Aeldari list.

Wrap Up

Some much-needed Aeldari reinforcements here, and honestly pretty fairly priced - there are certainly places I'm keen to try these, without any of them screaming out that they're busted. Good stuff.

Sky Serpent: I think the release of a subfaction that many Eldar enjoyers have been pining for for decades is so incredibly exciting and these datasheets are a great start. You’ll likely see many players start Aeldari armies just to field these models and the next puzzle to solve will be what to field alongside them; dino fingers crossed for a 1DP Exodite Detachment in a future update.
