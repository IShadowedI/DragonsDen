Games Industry News Roundup- June 23rd, 2026

June 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Games Workshop Announces Big Summer Preview Show

Credit: Games Workshop

We’re starting off the summer in style in the wide world of Warhammer, as Games Workshop announced a major preview show for this upcoming Friday.

The preview trailer teases big reveals for many of GW’s most popular game systems, including a new army for Age of Sigmar, the upcoming Kill Team set, and a new box set release for Necromunda. Unlike other previews, where there’s been visual clues and gags to tease more specifics for the releases, the only clue we have for this preview appears to be something Ogor related, with a few references to being greedy and hungry after mentioning Age of Sigmar. 

Alongside releases for all of their other games, Games Workshop will also show off the first codex of 11th edition, as well as the accompanying releases. With the recent Armageddon set pitting Space Marines against Orks, it's almost certain the aforementioned codex will be for one of these two armies, alongside multiple releases to accompany the book. 

The Big Summer Preview Show is available to watch this Friday on Warhammer’s official Twitch channel or their YouTube channel at 7PM BST/ 2PM EDT.

Trench Crusade Nominated for Award for Excellence in Gaming

Credit: The Diana Jones Award for Excellence in Gaming

Trench Crusade has been massively successful with tabletop gamers around the globe, and it looks like this breakout hit is getting its flowers from one of the most prestigious awards in gaming. Trench Crusade joins four other nominees for this year's Diana Jones Award for Excellence in Gaming. 

According to the Diana Jones Website, the award, which has been around since 2000, “celebrates everything that’s the best about gaming. That can mean a game system or supplement, a magazine, a company, a designer, an event or convention, or any other entity that shows gaming at its absolute zenith.” Run by a group of industry professionals from around the world, the final winner of the award is announced in a private ceremony held annually at Gen Con. Previous winners include Rose Estes and the board game Daybreak in 2025, the United Paizo Workers Union in 2024, and TTRPG Coyote and Crow in 2023.

The finalists for the Diana Jones Award (besides Trench Crusade) include Mischief Toy Store, a store in ST Paul, MN, that joined in a lawsuit against Trump’s tariffs and stood up to ICE in the local community, game designer and journalist Rob Weiland, who passed away last year, Price Johnson, COO of Cephalofair Games, and Molly House, a board game where players take on the roles of gender-defying mollies from 18th century London. 

The winner of this year's Diana Jones award will be announced on July 29th at Gen Con, and presented the traveling Diana Jones Trophy in person at the ceremony.

Upcoming Steam Machine Price Soars Above $1,000, Original Pricing “No Longer Viable”

Credit: Valve

Bad news for anyone who was hoping the upcoming Steam Machine, which went on preorder this week, would be easily affordable. Valve finally unveiled the price of their upcoming console competitor on Monday, and its cheapest model, the 512GB variant, comes in at a whopping $1050 USD. Other versions of the Steam Machine are equally eye watering, with the 2TB variant costing $1350 and $1500 for the 2TB with controller.

Valve is aware of the high cost of the Steam Machine, and blames the high cost of RAM and other computer parts for forcing their hand, writing “We felt like we had a good understanding of how those costs might change over time when we first started sourcing them for Steam Machine back in 2023. That understanding was born from the many years of data we all have about the evolution of PC hardware prices – primarily, that it tends to get cheaper over time as new technology arrives.”

“Over the past year or so, that has changed quickly and significantly, most visibly for RAM and storage components. There are a variety of reasons, all of which are affecting hardware products everywhere. The overall effect is that our original goal for the price of Steam Machine is no longer viable. So the prices we're sharing today reflect the state of the world for manufacturing; or, more accurately, it reflects the price of the components as we've secured them over the past 6 months.”

According to IGN, Valve engineers stated that the price increase for the Steam Machine due to component shortage was probably similar to that of the Steam Deck, which saw a 35% price increase last month. According to their “napkin math”, IGN speculates that the Steam Machine’s 512GB version would’ve originally cost $750. 

The shortage also resulted in a shortage of Steam Machine products to sell, which Valve has decided to offset this in the most fair way possible while limiting resellers by implementing a lottery system. Those who signed up prior to June 25th would be placed on a list, which would be randomized for priority for who would get the chance to claim their Steam Machine. Valve justifies this method by claiming “a launch that starts at a specific day and time tends to reward bots, people with fast internet connections, talented gaming fingers for quick F5/refresh reactions, and those who can schedule their life around that moment. By accepting reservation signups over the course of a few days, without any incentive to be first, we're hoping to take away some of that friction.”

The Steam Machine is currently sold out on Valves website, despite the high costs, and the company has not responded to IGN’s speculation on the original price. 

Modiphus Reveals Miniatures for XCOM Tabletop Game

Credit: Modiphus

After releasing an enigmatic teaser website earlier this year, Modiphus has finally pulled back the curtain on their upcoming XCOM tabletop game, showing off models and giving us more details about gameplay.

XCOM: The Miniatures Game will be a solo or cooperative experience based on Modiphus’s Five Parsecs from Home ruleset. In it, you take on the role of a squad of elite XCOM soldiers fighting against a mysterious alien threat in multiple different mission styles, reflecting the tense tactical experience the video game became famous for. The game will also have rules for customizing your squad members, researching new tech, and building up your XCOM base of operations. 

For those who prefer to be the bad guys, the 180 page rulebook also contains rules for a player versus player mode. In this version, rather than all players working together as XCOM squad members, one player will take on the role of commanding the alien menace in various missions, making the already dangerous extraterrestrials even more threatening. 

Those eager to get their hands on XCOM: The Miniatures Game won’t need to wait long, as Modiphus confirmed that preorders for the game will open this July. Alongside the rulebook, the first wave of miniatures up for pre-order include six XCOM operatives, Sectoids, Sectoid Commanders, Thin Men, Outsiders, Floaters, Chryssalids, Seekers, and Infection Pods. Additional units from the game, like Mutons and MEC Troopers, will come with later waves of releases. As an added bonus, those who preorder a physical rulebook will receive a digital copy immediately, so that they can enjoy the game with proxy miniatures. 

Modiphus hasn’t stated a specific day in July to keep an eye out for yet, though there is a newsletter available for those who wish to stay up to date. 

Vampire Survivors Team “Reviewing” Fortnite Collab Due to Epic’s AI Use 

Credit: Poncle

A wise man once said that there’s only three things in life that are certain: death, taxes, and drama in Fortnite. I think it was Ben Franklin? Sounds like something he would say.

This time, the news comes from Vampire Survivors developers Poncle, who stated last week that they’re considering backing out of a recently announced Fortnite collab due to Epic’s use of AI in their recently announced Unreal 6 Engine.

The Unreal 6 announcement came last week, with the new engine combining the Unreal Engine and the Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN) into one platform with upgraded tools powered by the new Verse system. While the announcement touted several upgrades for Unreal, such as featuring better ease of use tools and more portability across different games and engines, one particular feature stands out: a heavy emphasis on AI use. Epic writes that Unreal 6 “we see LLMs, generative  AI models, and tools like Claude and Codex playing a central role in helping you build content faster while maintaining the creative control you need."

This has drawn backlash from many corners of the gaming world, including Poncle. Their hit game, Vampire Survivors, was one of several games announced for upcoming Fortnite collaborations, with the other confirmed games being Sonic Racing Crossworlds, Control Resonant, and Phantom Blade Zero. Following the Unreal 6 announcement, Poncle appears to be reconsidering this, writing on their official discord "following today's news about gen AI usage by Epic to create all sort of game assets, including Fortnite characters, we're currently "reviewing" our collaboration with Fortnite. We'll let you know if anything moves forward."

Unreal Engine 6 is slated for early access release in 2027. As of this writing, Poncle has not yet released an official statement on if they will or will not withdraw from their announced Fortnite collaboration.
