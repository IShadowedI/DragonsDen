Thoughts

Polar opposite T'au build square off here, hordes of Kroot facing a bunch of Crisis suits and a Tiger Shark. Now, to be clear - the battlefield here is going to end up absolutely strewn with Kroot corpses, but the question is whether that will actually matter. The two lists would, honestly, both be pretty happy with their Opponent's Primary in this game - the Kroot will be able to flood the board for at least a few turns at the start of the game, while the suited and booted T'au would be very happy killing them off one objective per turn and flipping it.

As is, the Kroot have to look for whatever opportunities they can find to pick up the odd objective kill while prioritising holding as many points as possible turns 2-4,  while the other side need to table the Kroot and then hope they can pick up enough table quarter points later on to clinch it, because earlier it may literally not be possible for them to be in the positions required. Recon into T&H is highly scoring constrained, and pretty flat for how it scores through the game - if the suit T'au manage Hold One for all available turns and table the Kroot they're only at 29VP, meaning they need at least two 6VP turns from quarters to get to the max. If they try and get that early, they risk giving the Kroot kill points and running out of stuff, so they have to kill the Kroot really fast, not ideal for Kauyon that doesn't spin up quick. The Kroot have the advantage of a much more front-loaded scoring cadence if they can make it work - it's very plausible for them to pick up a couple of 14VP turns on two and three, after which they don't need that much of a rearguard to come out of this on a solid score.

In general, such situations do tend to favour the list with the backloaded scoring, as they have a bit more agency to pick up late game secondaries alongside it, but here I think the structural factors favour the Kroot more, and I'd narrowly give it to them. The methodical murder of Kauyon proved me wrong, taking a narrow win in the other direction.

John sent us his thoughts from the game, so without further ado, take it away!

John: As we were both playing Take and Hold, the Mission was Battlefield Dominance, aka Linchpin with a small bonus. Alex was playing a list that leaned heavily on Lords of Change shooting, backed up by a Bloodthirster with Fade to Darkness. That's already a poor matchup into my 20 (mostly) 4 wound T6 Terminators with -1 to wound, as this blunts the Lord's 3 damage shooting. Unfortunately for him my use of Beastslayer also adds some significant complications. With a 6" reactive move available, it's very difficult for the Fadethirster to get into anything I don't want him to, as I can always guarantee a 9" charge for 1 CP. Lethal hits into the relevant portions of his army also means that basically everything is a threat for chip damage, and Terminators do a lot of damage with oath involved.

As such, my gameplan was simple: Start Arjac's unit and the Headtakers in reserves, deploy the wolf scouts aggressively but safely near his expansion, and deploy the rest of my army hidden, as the birds weren't fast enough to threaten my deployment zone with 15" detection range. If he goes first, I can force him to come out and then jump front line, while solidly holding the middle and my expansion, and possibly using the uppy downy on Logan's unit to position them aggressively. If I go first, I can threaten to jail him in his deployment zone with Arjac's turn one deepstrike, and force him to deal with that. As luck would have it, I won the roll off, and Arjac decided to do me one better than just preventing him from touching the middle objective, rolling an 11+1 on the turn one charge.
This allowed him to wrap the Lord of change on his home objective, and lured in a heroic Intervention from the Fadethirster. Both large monsters were still standing at the end of my turn, but had taken some damage, and between the two had managed to kill 2 terminators. He fell back with both, unloaded with the two other lords of change, and killed another 2 terminators. At that point, it was basically all over but the counting, as I was able to screen the rest of the board from his Terminators in deep strike/various uppy downy abilities, and he didn't leave his deployment zone for basically the entire game while I racked up easy 15 point primary turns for just standing on my expansion/middle/home and thus scoring control more as well. Eventually he got Arjac's unit down on the bottom of 2 after the Fadethirster made a 9" charge into him, and then lost the Fadethirster on round 4 when it failed a charge and Wulfen caught up with it.
I don't think there's anything I'd change about the list right now, as it played extremely well throughout the tournament. Recon (my rounds 1 and especially 2) felt like the toughest matchup, but both were doable, just required a lot of work and positioning. We'll have to see what the next dataslate brings, but I do really think the benefits of having a flat 6" reactive on incredibly durable pieces like Wolf Guard Terminators is being slept on.
