Games Industry News Roundup- June 9th, 2026

June 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

En Garde Plans to Continue Following Creator John Blanche’s Passing

Credit: Harrowhound Miniatures

It’s been a hard week in the world of miniature wargaming with the passing of John Blanche, one of the most iconic artists in the wargaming space and key figure in defining the look and tone of Warhammer. While the community mourned his passing, others wondered about the fate of Blanche’s wargaming Kickstarter, En Garde- Vanguard. Last week, the company behind En Garde, Harrowhound Miniatures, released an update that made it clear that En Garde will still release, despite Blanche’s death.  

“We are fortunate that John had already finished the concept art for the project, and detailed the creative vision on which the game is based,” Harrowmaster writes. “Though his leadership, artistic guidance and creative oversight will be sorely missed, it is now the duty and privilege of those of us left behind to try to honour what John set out to do to the best of our abilities.”

John Blanche’s En Garde- Vanguard is a tabletop skirmish game where players take on the role of shambling Victorian-era pirates and duelists, designed by John Blance in his iconic surreal style. The crowdfunding campaign was a massive success, launching on May 1st of this year with a $10,000 goal. This goal was surpassed the same day the kickstarter launched, and as of this writing En Garde has raised over $167,000.

Despite Blanche passing before the project could be delivered in September of this year, the team at Harrowmaster believes that continuing to work on the game and build even broader horizons is the best way to honor his legacy.

“We gave our word to John that we would see the game to its completion. It will help his family, and allow us to share some of the final pieces of art John ever created. It will be a great chance for a new generation of talent associated with the project to share their work, for John was all about fostering and growing new artists. We also believe En Garde will be a thrilling game that people will enjoy for many years to come.”

En Garde- Vanguard is scheduled to be delivered to backers in September of this year, with a second kickstarter launching later this year or early next year for a full En Garde game.  

Steamforged Cuts Roles in Board Games as Focus Shifts to Warmachine

Credit: Steamforged Games

Layoffs and the news roundup, name a more (sadly) iconic duo. At least it's not in video games this time, I guess? 

As reported by Boardgamewire, Steamforged Games (SFG) cut a number of roles in its board games department, particularly crowdfunding, to focus more on Warmachine, which has been quickly rising in popularity.

Prior to purchasing the rights to Warmachine in 2024, SFG’s main claim to success was its booming Kickstarter business for various Video Game tabletop adaptations, such as Monster Hunter and Dark Souls. Through campaigns such as these, SFG raised millions in the crowdfunding space, and became one of the biggest success stories in the field. 

Since purchasing Warmachine, the game has seen a huge increase in demand for the game, with one spokesperson telling Boardgamewire that the company’s Warmachine Revenue has tripled in the past 21 months, with demand requiring SFG to significantly expand its production centers in both the United States and Europe.

“Steamforged has always had two strong sides, both retail and crowdfunding, and was originally founded as a miniatures game company,” SFG told Boardgamewire. “Warmachine’s growth has shown clear demand over and above expectations and has become a core focus to support that growth.”

With Warmachine on the rise, and a shrinking market for crowdfunded board games, SFG began the process of downsizing the board game department, significantly reducing its staffing. According to anonymous sources who spoke with Boardgamewire, these layoffs represent around a third of SFG’s workforce, and cut deep enough across all departments that it could threaten the ability for SFG to fulfill its remaining Kickstarter campaigns. 

SFG denies this, however, stating that they intend to deliver on all of the current active projects, saying that the company ““retain[s] both the internal resources and the external capabilities needed to deliver on our commitments”.

GOG Sends Nazi Iconography in Newsletter

Credit: GOG

Yes, you read that correctly. And before you ask, yeah, it's bad. Real bad. 

Last week, as a part of an effort to promote the game “The End of the Sun”, GOG sent out a newsletter to subscribers that contained multiple symbols associated with Nazis and Neo-Nazis, including the unmistakable double lightning bolt associated with the Nazi SS. 

GOG quickly took to social media to apologize for the use of these hate symbols, writing “We made a series of mistakes: We created an unfortunate visual association by placing the runes incorrectly, we used the wrong logo for The End of the Sun, we did not check the display of the newsletter on mobile devices - which came out worse - and we didn’t port the feedback from our German QA to other languages.”

According to GOG, the issue was caught after about half the emails had already been sent out. GOG closes the post by promising to increase their review protocols to prevent this from happening again.

Contradicting this statement that no one noticed until it was too late, however, is a post by GoG representatives on Reddit where they say they were aware of the “possible” association caused by placing the two lightning bolt runes together, but still elected to go through with the email in non-German regions, writing, “I also recognize that placing two such runes next to each other could create an unfortunate association with symbols used by the Nazi regime. This was noticed before distribution, and out of respect for local sensitivities, the material was not sent to the German community.”

The development team behind the game also came out to clarify on Reddit that their game is not associated with Neo-Nazis or their ideals, and that they don’t know why GOG chose to place those symbols in the Newsletter email. 

As many on social media and news outlets like PC Gamer have rightly pointed out, this apology doesn’t come close to properly explaining how multiple very recognizable hate symbols made it out on an email headline, nor if anyone at GoG has been disciplined for their inclusion.

Mantic Brings Grunts and UNSC Marines for Halo: Flashpoint

Credit: Mantic Games/343 Studios

Mantic continues to expand Halo Flashpoint, announcing even more recognizable characters and units brought in from the greater Halo universe during its summer Flashpoint preview last week.

The biggest highlight of the reveal was, oddly enough, the little guys. Mantic revealed that with the upcoming battle for Zeta Halo, the company is releasing UNSC marines to reinforce the forces of humanity, while rounding out the Banished roster with Grunts and Jackals. Both of these releases are long-requested by fans, and round out the roster for both factions with every kind of soldier seen in the lore of Halo. Alongside this release, Mantic showed off a box of deadly Spartan Killers for the Banished, a dangerous trio of Elites and Brutes who specialize in hunting the most dangerous warriors of humanity.

It’s not all just new releases either, as many older releases are returning or getting updated with the upcoming 1.5 Flashpoint rules update. By popular demand, the Master Chief paint collection set from Army Painter is returning to store shelves, giving potential Flashpoint players a perfect entry point into the game with Halo’s most famous figure. Mantic is also repackaging the Spartan Edition Starter Set with new cards and a rulebook that reflect the rules changes made in the 1.5 update for Flashpoint, and alongside the free digital resources for the 1.5 update, those who order the Zeta Halo army bundle from Mantic will also receive updated physical cards and rulebook. 

All of these items are available to pre order now through Mantic’s website, with a planned shipping date set for September 2026. 

Dark Horse Comics Voluntarily Recognizes Union

Credit: Dark Horse Comics

Unions continue to be a big new topic in the world of games and its related media, which makes sense given the uncertain economic times and relentless waves of layoffs within the industry. This week’s union news is a little different though, as the leadership at Dark Horse Comics did something that’s truly wild: They voluntarily recognized the Dark Horse Workers Union.

Organized through the Communications Workers of America (CWA) Local 7901 Branch, the Dark Horse Workers Union (DHWU) was officially formed on May 27th following a supermajority of votes from staff. The 59 employees in the union organized in large part due to uncertain times at Dark Horse, with the company founder Mike Richardson leaving Dark Horse in March of this year, leaving interim CEO Jay Komas in charge.

In that same month, Dark Horse staff wrote collectively to Komas, stating “The looming uncertainty from recent layoffs, wage/hiring freeze, change in leadership power, emergence of artificial intelligence, and return-to-office policies (despite their economic impact on employees) have fueled us to organize and collectively address our concerns. These are just a few examples of why we are seeking a democratic workplace.”

The DHWU gave Komas until June 3rd to voluntarily recognize their union, and stated their intention to petition the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) should Komas fail to do so. Fortunately, Komas released a statement on the 3rd that Dark Horse would voluntarily recognize and negotiate with the DHWU.

“I've read your statement carefully, and I realize that organizing a union is not something people do lightly.” Komas writes. “It takes courage, coordination, and a level of trust in each other. I understand the employees’ disappointment with how issues have historically been addressed. You’ve taken this step because you believe Dark Horse can be better. So do I.”

Komas also recognizes just how rare it is for companies to voluntarily recognize unions, but stands by the decision, writing “Voluntary recognition is an unusual step for employers, but in doing so Dark Horse intends to reaffirm its commitment that this new leadership wants to do things differently and enter into good-faith bargaining. Not because we are required to, but because it is the right thing to do. The path to a strong Dark Horse runs through a workforce that feels heard, respected, and genuinely invested in the company's future, not one that feels like it must fight just to be seen.”

No announcement has been made at this time as to when leadership and the union will come to the bargaining table, but it’s exciting to see that both sides might, for once, actually come to that table in good faith.
