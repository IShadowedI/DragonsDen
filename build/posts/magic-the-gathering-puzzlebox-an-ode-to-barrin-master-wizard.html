Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – An Ode to Barrin, Master Wizard

May 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Nobody forgets their first real Commander deck. For many, that's a precon, and as the relative power and care in their design has increased, that's become all the more common. Still, your first step into the format that placed you at the helm, with a carefully-crafted 99 and a Legendary Creature that drew your eye—that's perhaps more special. I started playing Magic when Aether Revolt came out, in 2017, and only two years later did I try my hand with Commander; even back then, my tendency towards the odd and underexplored loomed large, and for that reason my first paper Commander deck was helmed by Barrin, Master Wizard. There's a few things one might immediately notice about the card, that it's old enough to not have printed Creature types beyond 'Legend', that it's still sitting at a cool ~200 decks on EDHREC, or perhaps that it was a key component of a later combo alongside the now-banned Dockside Extortionist. More than anything though, one can notice that it has an odd phrase for a Monoblue card: "Sacrifice a permanent". I wanted to take a look at my original list from Barrin, from ~7 years ago, to not only see how far someone can come as a deckbuilder, but also to showcase one of my all-time favorite underplayed Commanders.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Straight Outta Tolaria



Here it is, my first 99. There's some fairly obvious things going on here, with a number of the best tools available to Monoblue in its day; High Tide, Blue Sun's Zenith, and Laboratory Maniac are all pieces that dominated the color, and even to this day are often staples of decks looking to grind out with goodstuff & Islands. The more shocking thing is that there's a dearth of cards we're extremely excited to sacrifice to Barrin, as beyond a few cool Enchantments like Hatching Plans & Induced Amnesia most of the time it looks like we'd be forced to sacrifice errant tokens, or really whatever was left on the board. Barrin was a tempo piece that kept us competitive with the big, stompy 'battlecruiser' Commanders of the day, as we pushed towards a combo that came to fruition in the set I started playing: 'Dramatic Scepter'. Pairing Isochron Scepter with an imprinted Dramatic Reversal, and mana rocks producing 3+ mana, generated infinite mana which could be used on a huge X-cost Spell, or via Barrin to bounce away the board or repeat our own Enters effects. Dramatic Scepter is old hat now, even arguably outpaced in today's day & age, but at its inception it was the talk of the town! Being available in Monoblue just made sense, and given the density of mana rocks being played, was often on by the time the game was in its closing turns.

In looking to update this list given what I know now, there was one clear objective: Make Barrin weirder. Notice that I didn't say 'better', because that's likely going to come as a serendipitous little side benefit of working with modern cards and a modern outlook. There's fewer than a dozen nonland cards in this old list that actively do something cool with Barrin in mind, so step one is to increase their density. Much of the sauce here is via Enchantments, so Mirrormade & Copy Enchantment are shoo-ins for doubling up on the existing Hatching Plans & Amnesia, and we can lump Flash Photography & The Everflowing Well//The Myriad Pools in there too. Then, looking into keyword searches, a card catches the eye: Ordeal of Thassa. Normally, you need to work towards getting enough counters for it to pop, but Barrin can be rid of it far faster! Talking about leaving the battlefield? Throw a Reality Acid in there! In essence, we're aiming to find odd duck permanents which appreciate being sacrificed at Instant-speed, especially if there's an enters trigger to repeat.

Old Meets New

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

What's more interesting to me than new tools available to this strategy, such as MDFCs and cards not yet printed, is those which did exist and I simply missed. This is mostly in the form of obscure Blue cards that yearned to be sacrificed, which wouldn't have come up in my normal queries, or in videos I'd seen. Slow Motion, Dance of Many, & Aether Barrier all strike me as cards I'd not included for reasons of not knowing they existed; more interesting still is perhaps my favorite card in the entire list, Saprazzan Bailiff. Because of how his triggers work, you can bounce him in response to the initial exile effect, allowing you to refresh everyones' hands with each Artifact & Enchantment in the graveyards. In fact, Bailiff may just be an underrated card in general, because the same can be done with sacrifice effects in a number of strategies, especially those looking to combo off with 'cheerios', or cheap self-sacrificing baubles. The shift in philosophy at play here also rears its head, with far less 'goodstuff' Monoblue pieces like the Counterspells & Fact or Fictions of the world, and a greater density of that which has some semblance of internal synergy. It's the changeover of Barrin being a control top end, to now being the deck's main enabler, a showcase for what he can do rather than the reverse.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.



In order to extract maximum value from Saprazzan Bailiff, we're not only on the Merfolk tutor in the color, Merrow Harbinger, but also a few cards that keep it one-sided. Both Soul-Guide Lantern & Dauntless Scrapbot get rid of opposing graveyards while leaving your own, meaning the only goodies coming back to hand are those you own. This all comes to a head with what used to be a value piece in Barrin, that now takes on the role of its new combo: Ugin's Nexus. Alongside Drafna, Founder of Lat-Nam, or Esoteric Duplicator, we can create copies of the Nexus over and over, taking infinite turns. With the latter though, we need a sacrifice outlet, and for that Barrin is able to thankfully step in! While taking infinite turns is technically verboten by the strict rules of Bracket 3, this line exists in the list as a means to showcase how cards have been re-contextualized in the years since the original was built.

I'm going to be very frank: This new version of Barrin is sick, and does things no other Monoblue list is approaching with its Enchantment-sacrifice subtheme. While you do need to see at least one relevant Enchantment to get going, in a test game I cycled through Induced Amnesia a cool 5 times, getting a new hand on demand while removing pesky opposing Commanders. Doing so with all of these old-border, classic looking cards is a huge bonus as well, with pieces appearing here that are sure to turn heads from folks playing for longer than even I! Barrin remains a strong tempo piece, forcing mana to be repaid for value engines that want to stick on the board, and he works well with related bounce tools like Cresting Mosasaur (conveniently, also a sacrifice piece). I'm not about to say that Barrin has aged well, especially given the number of beneficial enters triggers has skyrocketed, but in refocusing to making his cost the buildaround, rather than his effect, we can mitigate some of that. Sure, repeatedly bouncing opposing threats is worse now than it was in ~2019, but sacrificing your own stuff? That's evergreen!

On the topic of cheating downsides, this week on Commander Focus we'll be looking at how you can resolve multiple Paradigm copies of a single card...concurrently. You won't want to miss that, as it's combining two mechanics players love to see together: Manifest & Stickers. I should hope my sarcasm is palpable, but no, seriously we're putting Stickers on face-down permanents—it's going to be a mechanical nightmare, and a technical dream! That's coming this Thursday, for folks looking to see how far we can push Paradigm, so keep an eye out! If you have any concepts or questions about this Puzzlebox, let me know in the comments below!
