Roundtable: Codex Adeptus Custodes (11th Edition)

October 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Our review of the 11th Edition Codex: Adeptus Custodes dropped last weekend and the new book has sparked a ton of conversation among our 40k authors about the new faction, their direction, and how they’ll play. We sat the crew down to talk about the new book, offer their thoughts, and talk about what they like, don’t like, and how they see the army in the meta shaping up as we head into the end of the year.

THE ROUNDTABLE

  • Robert “TheChirurgeon” Jones
  • Struan “Struj” Robertson
  • Jag the Power Armor Enthusiast
  • Liam “Corrode” Royle
  • Isaac Terada

What’s your overall impression of the new book?

TheChirurgeon: I honestly like the new book. I think philosophically it makes a lot more sense for how the Adeptus Custodes should feel on the tabletop - a very small number of incredibly elite soldiers who can take on any threat in the galaxy. The Ork and Space Marine codexes both felt fairly generic and directionless to me, and while some of that is likely to be a result of those being armies with too many units, there’s clearly an idea behind what Custodes are supposed to be, and that makes for a stronger book overall. The rules here are good, they make you want to play the army, and they communicate what the force is all about -  and that’s what should happen when you pick up a codex.

Jag The Power Armor Enthusiast: The book does an amazing job of giving Custodes a badly needed wider toolbox with almost flawless internal balance to the Custodes themselves. There are more “optimal” takes on the whole. But you can take almost anything in this book and be competitively viable, that is worthy of high praise especially in comparison to the last book that had been kept together with duct tape and prayers of hope to the Emperor on his Throne. This Codex should be the gold standard for future codexes as it does not single out only one type of model in any case, but focuses on tight groupings of models. Thrilling to behold! There are small issues. But I can see these being handled in a dataslate update with a little luck. 

Struan: I think it’s great. As a competitive player first, a reluctant painter second and a lore enthusiast some way behind in 3rd, this book has me reading up on the Auramite clad Demigods conducting Blood Games… What have I become? The detachments feel thematic and do the things that the Shield Hosts are supposed to do and the models feel strong like they’re supposed to. None of this “Space-Marine-Terminator with +1T” from 10th Edition. I don’t think it’s perfect, but I think it’s a very promising sign of future 11th Edition codices.

Liam: To me, the 11th edition codexes so far show a similar philosophy of re-imagining factions, getting down to the very fundamentals of what a model is meant to represent. I disagree with Rob that Orks and Space Marines were directionless; I think they have a very clear direction along these lines. If you want to make Orks have meaningful shooting, why insist on it hitting on 5s, just because that’s how it was 30 years ago? Why must a Space Marine be T4? Why are boltguns a standing joke instead of a terrifying volley of explosive shells? The Custodes codex takes this re-imagining and runs with it, turning your Custodians into the true battlefield demi-gods the fluff would like them to be. Just a few years ago people were turning up with lists with bricks of 10 Custodians in them, quickly cut down to 5 - but then you just ran twice as many. At the end of 10th edition you’d see 6 units of the things inside of three Land Raiders - a model count not meaningfully different than any other power armour faction. Now the maximum you have in any unit is 4 models, including a character, and they’re all hard as nails and hit like trains. If you really want to spam you can run nothing but basic Custodian infantry and attached characters, giving a total model count of about 21 and room for very little else. It makes them feel genuinely elite; each dead guy is meaningful because it’s 25%-33% of your unit’s total strength.

TheChirurgeon: It’s not that I think having shooty Orks or T5 marines is a problem - though it’s funny and weird that almost nothing is T4 now - but that these books have armies which can just do everything and so feel much less focused. If Orks have good shooting, fast vehicles, good melee, and T5, what are they actually bad at? It’s not that Orks can have good shooting or that it has to be BS 5+; it’s that they have to be bad at something. That’s how good design works. 

In that regard, Custodes have an even clearer identity this time around, and I think that’s just easier when you have fewer units in an army - makes internal balance easier when you can give everything a defined role.

Isaac: Overall I think the changes made to this book are night and day to the 10th version of Custodes. Do I think it is perfect? No, there are some issues with the codex overall but I think this is a third codex that has really shown what GW is looking at when trying to design 11th codexes with the detachment point systems in mind. Looking inside the codex, these new detachments, the templating of abilities, the updated datasheets, all of it comes together to really make me, a player who never really saw an interest in Custodes, want to play them, which I think is exactly what you want from a codex revamping a faction from the ground up. 

What does the new book get right?

Struan: Detachment Balance. There doesn’t appear to be a “correct” way to take the army and its list-building. I think that’s a huge win for fans of Adeptus Custodes. Some detachments are looking more powerful than others initially, which is only natural, but I’m yet to see anything stand out head-and-shoulders as the objectively strongest way to do it. Mixing and matching detachment options is actually interesting with really tough trade-offs to “grab the good stuff”.

Jag: Almost everything is awesome in this book, gone are the days of Triple Wardens and Blade Champions, Gold Horde and Draxus as the must haves. Whoever wrote this should get a raise in that regard. Every Custodian option has a role, it's no longer “Well, maybe you run Axes if X-Y-Z line up.” No, you can run a highlander list of every option and be a genuine terror to behold. Also, allowing Trajann to join Terminators is one of the smartest choices, more named Characters should be able to jump in and attach to different unit types, do more with this GW, people will love it! Every detachment combination is at least in theory a frightening sight at its best combination, or good under the right meta at worst. That's a feat of good writing when I can assemble my Shadowkeepers fluff list and operate on the assumption that when I face a melee heavy assault, I am ready for it. But if I go to a tournament, I can still switch in for Dread Host with Lions and Morotoi. This is a badly needed injection of flavor to the game, and I hope this is the design ethos for future codexes with regards to balancing flavor and balance competitively. I am excited to play Custodes for the first time in years, and I praise Games Workshop's writers for doing so. The expectation bar had fallen through the basement and into the foundation, these writers not only met the bare minimum, they surpassed it in almost perfect form.

Struan: Hear-hear. Liam: Flicking through this book for the first time was genuinely exciting. Everything looked cool, everything had big and meaningful changes. Integrating the units from 30k into the baseline codex was a great choice. The detachments have strong, meaningful direction and purposeful design that I think is missing a bit from Space Marines. I think you can make a case for any of the Shield Hosts, and the rest all work for their intended purpose really nicely - whatever direction you want to take your list in, there’s something to suit. As you probably already guessed, I’m a big fan of the way that all the dials have been turned up on the datasheets to make the units much more elite-feeling.

TheChirurgeon: The first impression of this book was definitely eye-popping - and I think that’s a good thing. You want someone reading a book to be excited about the rules and the possibilities of what units can do - that’s good design and good marketing. ANd I think they nailed it in that regard. The points also feel solid; once I saw them I felt better about the datasheets.

What does the new book get wrong?

TheChirurgeon: I really wish the Shield Host Detachments were 2 DP (with some added stratagems/enhancements to match). This is part of an ongoing problem I have with 11th edition Detachment philosophy generally, where I think the most common way to build should be to take a 2 DP + a 1 DP detachment. This has the added benefit of not needing any of the SHIELD HOST tags; if they’re all 2 DP then they’re already mutually exclusive. 

Struan: The naming of the infantry variants. Sentinel Guard Sodality and Custodian Guard Sodality… shorthanding these guys is a nightmare! ‘Sentinel’ and ‘Custodian’ is out because the army are all Custodians… ‘Guard’ and ‘Guard, well that’s self-explanatory and the same can be said for ‘Sodality’. Shield Boys/Girls and Spear Boys/Girls it is… until people see your Wardens with spears… FML.

Jag: Why, and I must ask this. WHY were Sisters of Silence, so badly, badly shafted this round? There is so much more that can be done here with Sisters of Silence! Why not have their ability interact when in proximity to a Psyker trying to use their abilities? I'm not saying they should uninteractively shut OFF Psychic powers, no. That's going too far. But have their fluff of being a walking psychic 5G Dead Zone cause other problems? Like say “On a 1/2/3 when casting, your power causes the unit to battleshock” make it so facing the Sisters is a legitimate threat to Psykers in more than just a Feel No Pain to Psychic attacks and an Aura that boosts the Aegis ability. Yes it's incredibly funny when one single Sister of Silence no sells Magnus, but it's not funny for the player controlling Magnus. Have Sisters do more, especially when Sisters of Silence are now a single box with Aleya being sent to Legends and reducing the model count even further. It's such a kick to the gut for the fans of Sisters of Silence and the faction is clinging to hope for more models, more support, anything. My only other major complaints with the datasheets is the weird shafting of the Venatari that are packing pistols and buckler, I get the idea behind it, but for a unit with so little offensive power? I'd hope for a buff to their shooting, or at the very least, a points drop, especially when the Ka'Tah enhancements render the need for a generic action performer unit much lower than before for Custodes. Also, why is the Coronus allowing disembark and shoot? The Special Spears are legends, and Terminators can deep strike, so… what's the point of this? Let it have Assault Ramp, if you're worried about the return of Triple Land Raiders and Golden Horde style plays, no, it's really hard to do that now GW. 

I also second The Chirugeon's complaints regarding Shield Host, this focus on a ton of 1DPs while fun to try and build around, limits fluff and design space in my view as we have mostly just “well X unit gets Y ability” cool, but how about some zones of control, or synchronized auras? Looping back to the Sisters Discussion, Talons of the Emperor was a shining diamond in the otherwise festering disaster of the 10th edition book and I hope at some point it's added back as a 2DP, it was fun, it made Sisters better, while offering a unique style to Custodes that rewarded synergy, this Codex has small bits of that, but this is where I wished the Codex had experimented more with. I think this is where the Chapter Specific Detachments from the Space Marine Codex can feed into ideas for subfaction detachments in the future. Still, as lengthy as my remarks are, it's a comparative small complaint in comparison to the wide successes of this Codex. 

TheChirurgeon: As a Thousand Sons player, I’m fine never seeing Sisters of Silence on the table again. But also, they just brought back the goddamn army-wide Feel No Pain for Custodes against mortals so they’re a pain in the ass even without Sisters on the table. I'm just generally not a fan of anti-psyker rules in the game as they tend to disproportionately punish 2-3 armies and do nothing against another third.

Liam: I just don’t have it in me to care that much about Sisters of Silence as they currently exist. They’re not interesting or exciting at all, they’re cheap filler to allow you to run a handful of units for mission play when sending 200pts of Custodians to do it would be excessive. It’s like getting upset about lack of Gretchin support in Orks - there’s just not enough meat on those bones for it to ever be a viable playstyle, so if you were expecting more you were only setting yourself up for disappointment. SoS getting the significant range expansion that one 30k book suggested might happen would be cool and turn them into a real faction, but for now, whatever.

Otherwise, I don’t think there’s many misses in this book really. The Aquilons with solarite gauntlets maybe struggle a little for a role, as do the gun and shield Venatari. The sword and board Shield-Captain is so wildly better than the other versions that you wonder a little why the other choices exist. I typically agree with Rob that 2 + 1 is a better design approach for detachments than 1 + 1 + 1, but I think it actually works here; you basically pick a Shield Host, then pick two types of units to buff, in an army where you probably only have two types of units because of how few of them you can take. That works for me, philosophically. I think the Shield Host approach is also helpful because it has the anchor of being themed around existing sub-factions, which is what was missing from Space Marines and Orks, and it creates a sensible reason for them to be UNIQUE.

TheChirurgeon: I think Sisters could be a viable army with a little work, but I don’t think they should be - they’d be massively overtuned against Grey Knights and Thousand Sons and undertuned against everything else. It’s kind of like the Grey Knights/Deathwatch problem, where they serve much better as an add-on unit or small force than a full army. And while I like them and want them in the army, I also think that pure Custodes armies should be viable. If you can’t run an army entire of golden armor walking tanks, what are we doing here? 

Struan: Chipping back in with a slightly more sensible gripe than naming conventions, I agree with Jag and Liam that the Pistol Venatari are a little lacking and I think they’ll struggle to get on the table at a non-narrative game. But come on guys: Witchseekers are very real into a whole host of targets. That flamer is no joke. And a 7” move unit with 7” scout with assault flamers is always going to be at least OK. AND they are pretty aggressively costed in comparison to the rest of the units.

TheChirurgeon: Yeah the turn 1 Movement on the Witchseekers is like the first thing I noticed in the book once I saw that Land Raiders were gone - “Who is the Shock Assault rule for?” I asked, as I whipped through the pages. Then I saw the Witchseekers and went “ooooooh.” Yeah the flamers aren’t Rubrics good but they’ll do just fine.

Honestly the biggest problem with the new book is that it’s good - our negative review of the bad Codex did way more page views and generated a ton more discussion. No one actually wants to be happy.

How did GW do with the points?

Note: The final points aren’t yet confirmed, but we expect few changes between now and the release.

Struan: I think they did a great job. I think they are objectively overcosted, especially on the infantry. As someone playing in the competitive sphere I think it’s totally correct to go too high than too low on release. I am sad as someone looking to play as the Adeptus Custodes, but I am so so grateful as someone that might need to play into the Golden Horde.

TheChirurgeon: I think everyone on our team who looked at the rules for Custodes before points spent the first ten minutes going “oh wow this is bullshit,” followed by an exasperated “well, we’ll see what the points look like.” When the points finally did show up, I think they got them about perfect. I agree that Terminators and non-terminator infantry might be a bit too close in cost, but I don’t think Custodians and Wardens can go any lower.

That said, having really high point costs on models makes balancing very difficult - when your cheapest models are 80 points, shifting a list’s point costs by 10-20 points makes no difference. And the difference between two and three units of Wardens is massive - large enough that it will be difficult to make small tweaks to the army.

Liam: Yeah, the first impressions of this book were that it would be wildly powerful, and then when we saw the points and realised they had actually gone as extreme on “very elite but very pricy” as it looked like it ought to be we breathed a sigh of collective relief. Don’t get me wrong, these guys are good, but for my first go at putting a list together I wrote down what I thought was a pretty conservative, low-unit-count army and when I added it up it came out to 2,470pts. I can definitely see a world where the army gets caught in a trap a bit like the one big Knights have - 4 is too few, 5 is too many - and it’s a struggle to make meaningful balance changes via points, but at the moment I think they’re in a good place and we can worry about that down the line.

Jag: Echoing the sentiments here, there's some real rough decisions made here with things like Dreadnoughts being a cheaper take than the infantry. Especially Wardens. Yes, always active AoC is powerful and will make a lot of armies packing majority AP-1 weapons beyond frustrated, but the dreaded and hated 4+ FNP is gone GW, you can let the points come down. I should not have to pay more than a Grav-Tank for 3 infantry bodies who will drop dead to a few good swings of a Thunder Hammer. 

How do you think the army will play? What will successful lists look like?

TheChirurgeon: One of the most interesting shifts here is that while Custodes generally got deadlier by model, the infantry kind of got less deadly by unit. Yeah, you do more damage with your spears and axes, but you don’t have as many shots or attacks as you did before when you were running units of 6. So you have this interesting push-and-pull where Custodes got way more durable, but are on the whole less deadly with their infantry - when I look at Allarus Terminators with Axes, my thoughts are “wow 4 damage” followed by “oh but only 3 attacks, who cares?”

Where things did get deadlier though, is vehicles - those are so nasty here, and Dreadnought lists are going to be a nightmare.

Jag: This I think is going to test a long held belief of mine. Namely that GW has historically overvalued Wounds and toughness. Marines going to T5/6, and Custodes going to T7/8 is impressive on paper and has triggered sentiments of “Custodes are basically War Dogs now!” The reality is way less obvious. If I have a squad of 3 Custodian Guard get attacked by 10 Thunder Hammer and Stormshield Terminators? They are not going to last when in the face of copious amounts of 2D/3D/D3+2/D6+DIE weapons. In a vacuum, T7 5W a model is nasty, but when I lose 2 of my 3 Custodian Guards, the swing back is way *less* intimidating, especially when SH/LH army wide in melee is gone (except for Shield Host). And to be clear, I welcome that change, SH+LH has been a crutch of too many armies for too long. If anything, we can soon see a change with Custodes if things get too bad by changing squad sizes. We've seen GW make that change in the past and with good results. I think this means a lot of lists will be focused on Dreadnoughts with light infantry support, and I fully believe that a mechanized Custodes force of Caladius tanks, Pallas shoot and scoot, Dreadnought frontliners and the bare minimum of infantry are going to be pants browning monsters to deal with for a lot of armies. Could be wrong, but when the choice is a Contemptor or a squad of Guard? You go with the Contemptor a lot of the time. Not always, but it's hard to turn down an even faster and more destructive killing machine. 

Struan: I agree with the above points. The jump to S8 on the classic Guardian Spear doesn’t really move the dial on lethality. When compared to our S7 in 10th Edition you’re still going to wound elves & Guardsmen on 2s and you’re still going to wound Marines and Terminators on 3s. One more attack doesn’t help when you have 2 or 3 fewer guys.

Meltas are a big problem with the flattening of their damage. 6W models really, really hate new melta profiles where every failed save is 80-100 points removed from the board.

All that said, I think we’re likely to see builds with Characters and Dreads early on. People are going to roll out a very expensive Warden squad into a few different profiles and go “that’s it?” and want to nuke things. You know what nukes things? Dual Caestus Fists baby.

Jag: Make sure you play Pacific Rim music when your Telemon is punching a Norn to death. Liam: The possibility not mentioned here is massed jetbikes, which are both tougher (Gyrfalcons substantially so) and faster than anything else. That feels like an avenue at least worth exploring.

Otherwise yeah, the lists I’ve run so far have been one of each Dreadnought with some infantry support, and that felt great.

It seems like once the book releases we’ll have that meta for the rest of the year. How do you think Custodes will fit into that meta?

Jag: We've seen a few test games where new SM and Orks have not liked facing Custodes. Yet that's not a good barometer in my opinion. There's a clear divide with 11E Codexes and 10E Codexes and Supplements. With the newer codexes, rerolls have been heavily reigned in, LH+SH are conditional (mostly), and there's a concerted effort to try and make these armies match their fluff even if it does not always meet the mark. Try putting a squad of Custodians up against Drukhari who will laugh their asses off at this thing called “Toughness” and instead Anti-Infantry the Golden Bananas off the board, or Howling Banshees who will throw enough 2D Anti-Infantry swings to give Custodian Guard pause. Or just throw Rogal Dorns at your opponent, or Riptides, hell! Flawless Blades in Emperor's Children are looking damn scary with their way of basically playing AoS and ignoring Toughness values. This codex and army looks intimidating on paper. But remember that 11E has skewed towards shooting heavily. Custodes are still at their core a melee army. They're a lot faster, they're kinda tougher, but this edition is ramping UP the lethality. When we see 4D weapons on Axes and Trajann that is… concerning. To say the least. So, are we headed for a power armor meta? Maybe. I love my power armor, I love watching everyone fight it out in melee. But I don't think the doomsayers are quite accurate. We shall see. 

Struan: Weirdly, I can’t see it changing things very much. Custodes as an army to play as and against will change dramatically but it does very much feel middle of the pack (at least initially!). With the new toolbox available, I think strong players will commit some atrocities, but I think weaker players will really struggle. The first time someone overextends their hyper-elite army into a gunline and loses 70% of them, those golden lads and ladettes will be placed gently back on shelves. Either way, I’m excited to play more with them and test out the genuinely varied options available.

Liam: I think it ends up in a similar place to where Knights often do, as an army with a good but unspectacular win rate, the ability to gatekeep poor players off the top tables, but maybe not quite enough oomph to reliably take down a whole event. And, much like Knights, it’ll divide opinions. Bad players playing against Custodes will feel like it’s oppressive, as their opponent no-sells all their offence, seems to have the right kind of karate for any situation, and deals out high-quality attacks all over the place. Bad players with Custodes will overextend themselves and get their small, valuable units killed for no benefit. It’ll be very difficult for Custodes armies to bring meaningful screening except maybe running a bunch of Witchseekers to push out early with Scouts, which makes them vulnerable to other armies with deep strike/Rapid Ingress threats. It’ll be interesting to see where an experiment like this lands and whether the design team are able to keep it working throughout the edition.

TheChirurgeon: Bad players with Custodes are always such a dangerous prospect. Yeah they overextend and try and do too much but did you count on them just passing a bunch of 4+ saves and no-selling your damage, idiot? You did not, and now that Shield Captain is taking a dump on your home objective. 

Ultimately I think Custodes have a playstyle that’s focused more on durability and holding objectives than pure offense, and that’s a problem in 11th edition, where even the most durable units can just get wiped off the table by land speeders on the game’s first turn. I think the competitive lists in the mix are going to be ones that rely either on dreadnoughts or bikes, as the improved stats on things like axes are more interesting than they are effective - 4 damage is really neat, but you’re paying a hundred points per model for a guy with 3 attacks. Ask any CSM player about Daemon Prince melee for a quick rundown of how disappointing that can be. 

Isaac: I have played one game so far into Custodes, I sent my army in, bounced, and then died. I think that will be indicative of all future games from here on out. That's fair, right?
