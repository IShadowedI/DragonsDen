Unit Focus: Slaves to Darkness - Heroes

November 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

In this article series we’re taking a look at the various unit options available to each faction, looking at what units they pair with, and talking about the combos available to those units as well as tips for running them solo. This article will cover your Hero options and how to build around them and use them on the table. You can find the rest of our Start Competing Slaves to Darkness Articles here.

Slaves to Darkness units typically are split into three main roles - purely beatstick characters; purely support characters; or some sort of hybrid that you can build around which both needs additional support and provides some itself. This is a combat focused army, so we want things to help reinforce our strengths - playing a stronger tempo game and dictating the fights on the board. This means being in the right place at the right time - wizards are the best way to do this, so you’ll notice a lot of value placed on running them here.

Before we get into this, it’s worth noting that I’m going to be assuming you have a base understanding on how each unit works, and won’t be going through their whole kit in detail; this will more be a focus on how to use each unit.

Regiments

Regiment options aren’t too crazy here, but the important thing to know is that most of the smaller Warriors of Chaos heroes (Sorcerer Lord, Exalted Hero) are restricted to Warriors of Chaos while Darkoath heroes are mostly restricted to Darkoath, with the odds and ends like the Ogroid Myrmidon and Centurion Marshall having even more restricted regiments beyond that. For the most part, you’ll want things like Unique characters and Chaos Lords of different flavors to lead regiments if you want the best unit access. It’s also worth noting we have great subcommanders here - Chaos Lords of each flavor, Centurion Marshall, Exalted Hero, and Ogroid Myrmidon warscrolls are all options we can take in regiments, which are solid choices even though we can’t get any wizards as subcommanders.

Abraxia, Spear of the Everchosen

Sadly, we’re going to start off with one of the weaker options here. Abraxia might be one of the coolest models in the roster, but sadly her rules are a weird mix of things that make her a reasonably good but somewhat inconsistent hero duelist without much of another role. She is intended to support Varanguard, making it so their “fight twice fight last” ability doesn’t force them to get strikes last on the second fight, letting them use both their fight abilities back to back if you charge in and tag a bunch of things. In theory this isn’t bad, but the range is a little awkward when you’re often trying to Daemonic Speed your hammer anyway and send them really deep, and it’s only a once per game ability you’re bringing support for.

At the end of the day, she’s fine as a beatstick, but for her points you have to directly compare her to Chaos Knights, of which she’s currently 10 points more expensive than - and on that alone, she often comes up shy.

Archaon, The Everchosen

Big Arch is a real decision you have to make when including in your list - being one of the “god models” that takes up a third of your roster means you have to really invest in making a list that wants to support him. Archaon’s got a few things going for him: first, he’s got a lot of wounds with good defense piled in the same place, he’s a tough cookie to crack between that and 14” of flying movement to maneuver his big base around. Second, he brings a lot of utility for a fighty model. He gives you the ability to play a turn out while knowing what the next priority roll will be, which often can mean things like forcing someone to take a really bad double turn or leaning hard into an aggressive double turn yourself. He can delete manifestations, heal himself, and dish out reliable mortal bombs. Slayer of Kings is neat, but you often aren’t going to strategize around the potential hero one shot, although it’s great when it does come up. 25 wounds means you can power through against the overwhelming majority of the game.

So what does he like for support?

  • Healing: Radiance of Dark Glory is your best friend here, being able to send him forward and power through back to your castle to heal up is a core part of his gameplay.
  • Speed: Archaon is fast, even with his big pie plate base making it not always seem that way. You want at least some of your army to be able to keep up with him, even though he often will be powering through when able.
  • Self Sufficient Hammer/Brawlers: Archaon is a pretty greedy model - aside from being a Wizard 2, he doesn’t multiply your force like other god models do. The rest of your list is usually built around supporting him, so bringing units that can fare well on their own means you can dedicate more resources towards the big man himself.

One of the key things to remember is that Archaon isn’t a real hammer, this is an army that has units that can deal tons of damage, and when you scale Arch up against them, you realize that he shouldn’t be treated like a raw damage dealer. The advantage of him is that he offers a lot in one single package. He’s durable, flighty, and fast, and can use the spell lore for the faction to threaten 3D6 charges and hand out Strikes Last. There are quite a few lists you can build around Archaon from HeroHammer with Be'lakor to running him with a strong anvil and fast support pieces, but either option will center around making sure you get the most juice out of Big A.

Be'lakor - Credit: RichyP

Be’lakor, the Dark Master

Big Bel is pretty much the opposite of Archaon. He’s got alright combat stats and a swingy but potentially powerful defensive profile (4+ unmodifiable armor with a 6++ ward), and packs 14” of flying movement. He packs a really strong warscroll spell that can hand out -1 to hit and no crits on combat attacks to an enemy unit within 18”, which varies from generally useful to absolutely back breaking in the right matchups. His last two features are his most standout: first, The Dark Master is a once per game ability that allows to you attempt to shut off a unit from any ability they use until the start of your next turn on a 3+, which is worth mentioning because it can whiff, but usually will at least hinder a unit from being fully utilized in a turn. He also can hand out strikes first to nearby allies and himself during the enemy combat phase, but only on a 4+. All this combined means that Be'lakor ends up adopting an interesting castle strategy, making it very difficult for your opponent to challenge him and his retinue without the risk of getting ripped up. The downside is that Bel isn’t 100% reliable in basically anything he does, and there are games where he just won’t make his rolls for casting, Dark Master, and Strikes First and will feel like dead weight. This doesn’t happen often, but it’s by far his most notable drawback. His upsides definitely outweigh this though - two casts means he can do our most powerful supporting spell combo (Soulscream Bridge + Daemonic Speed) to deliver one of your hammers into your opponent at mach 5, and he provides a layer of defense for your list that’s pretty unrivaled. He does risk getting shot off the board at times, so having healing on hand for him via Radiance of Dark Glory is always handy.

Overall, Belakor is up and down depending on the current state of the meta. At the time of writing, he’s still pretty popular in lists but isn’t necessarily performing the greatest, as he’s a pretty big investment for a support piece. That being said, there are a lot of great options to pair with him including Chosen, a big block of Chaos Warriors, and even blocks of Darkoath Marauders.

One last note about Belakor - his strikes first ability has great synergy with things that can chain activate. Chaos Lords and Ogroid Myrmidons are great, as you can attempt to give them strikes first and then let them chain activate with a unit of Chosen/Warriors/Theridons, giving a pseudo strike first and making his 4+ that much more reliable.

Now that we’re done with the big three named characters (who, hilariously, are in alphabetical order), we’ll move from the epic heroes into some of the generic options.

Centurion Marshall

CM is a bit of a weird one. He’s got fine combat stats and a chain fight ability with non-hero Daemons and Chaos Legionaries. That’s…not very exciting. So what are his use cases? Realistically, he has two:

  1. He makes a fantastic caddy for Radiance of Dark Glory if you don’t have a more synergistic choice, as he has a very large base for his points and a decent movement speed.
  2. He has great synergy with a Slaugherbrute, as he can both be its handler as well as chain fight for +1 to hit.

That’s really it, we’ll cover the Slaughterbrute synergy more in its entry, but realistically the only time you want this guy on his own is carting around auras.

Before we cover the next few entries, a quick reminder that all of the regular chaos lord options are all really accessible subcommanders, so that’s always worth evaluating as an army that can often gain a lot from staying in low drops.

Chaos Lord [On Foot]

The classic ChLord: simple, cheap, and effective. Has a +1 to wound chain fight with Warriors of Chaos Infantry, so Warriors and Chosen. The humble Chaos Lord often finds a home in Be'lakor lists for the aforementioned synergy with chain fighting as well as him being a bit more reliable on the defensive play patterns you have in a castle list - if you’re using things like Soulscream Bride/Daemonic Speed to fling a unit up the board, it’s likely the Lord stays behind. Worth a shout that he’s also a pretty good Radiance carrier since he often stays by your castle anyway.

Chaos Lord on Daemonic Mount

Oft nicknamed Claude (derived from CLoDM), the mounted lord has the same ability as the foot lord, but it works for Knights/Varanguard instead. His combat profile is pretty solid, and 7 10” move 3+ save wounds makes him a decently sturdy fighter for a cheap hero. He can be alright as a lone operator in some lists, but he really shines by bringing out the power of Chaos Knights, making them a true hammer as a package of Claude + 5 Knights. You won’t often run him with VG because it’s simply too expensive and already will overkill most things, and he can be extremely clunky to manoeuvre with 10 knight blocks. Not to be a broken record, but he also pops up as a Radiance carrier due to his speed, however he’s a great candidate to pack Deathmonger and just smack the heck out of things.

Chaos Lord on Karkadrak

The Kark Lord is a fast, fighty character option who forgoes the chain fighting abilities on the other lords to pick up a bad D3 charge mortal ability and a once per game +2 to charge aura for himself and 2 nearby non-hero non-cavalry Warriors of Chaos units. This is actually really solid, as you often really don’t want to be left hanging when trying to connect Chosen or Warriors to the right spot. He is great to carry any enhancements that buff his damage as he’s a really good fighter in his own right, like Deathmonger or Rune of Murder. Not every list will be able to fit him, but he’s really good in brawler lists alongside Chosen especially if you aren’t packing a two cast wizard for Bridge + Daemonic Speed.

Chaos Lord on Karkadrak (Scourge of Aqshy)

The seasonal variation of the Kark Lord, this one is just too expensive at the time of writing to see play. If he was a bit cheaper, the extra damage would make him a fantastic Deathmonger missile and he would have solid play in lists that want to just use him to smash down on a key target and sneak some extra movement in at the end of the turn. He buffs other companion weapons which is funny, but it’s often not going to really impact the output for anything but Varanguard which he is absolutely going to be too expensive to run alongside.

Chaos Sorcerer Lord

The default single cast wizard, Warriors of Chaos keyworded and restricted to only them in their regiment. 3+ abilities are always a bit of a feels bad thing currently, but the ability to make sure you can get your Chosen or Warriors rocking a 5++ for when you need it is invaluable, and often can be worth trading off the extra cast that some of our other wizard options have. Most commonly seen in lists that aren’t running other wizard and/or are packing Chosen, as 3D6 charging Chosen with a 5++ ward will scrap well against basically anything.

Slaves to Darkness Daemon Prince. Credit: SRM

Daemon Prince

Getting a new lease on life this season, the Daemon Prince has gotten a huge glow up from the new Despoilers battle formation letting him hand off his Pledge to nearby units and the new seasonal enhancements making him an actual combat force. Strikes first every combat on a 4+ and starting with a Pledge is also a nice feature of the base warscroll (you’ll often run Nurgle, as the 5++ is pretty potent compared to the other choices). Deathmonger + either Rune of Murder or Darkflame Pendant depending on how aggressive you want to be is generally the way to go. Wings are usually the default choice, although I do think Trophy Rack is a pretty solid choice if there are a lot of powerful fighting heroes in your meta that you can counter-punch well. Currently, the Daemon Prince really only will get played if you’re running Despoilers and want to utilize him for extra Pledge economy on top of buffing him to the nines with enhancements. His partners in crime are the Scourge of Aqshy Chaos Warriors, who are the best thing right now to benefit from the early Nurgle mark, although there’s a case for him to roll alongside some Darkoath as well.

Darkoath Chieftain [on Foot]

The foot Chieftain is often the default regiment leader for Darkoath packages, pairing really well with reinforced Mauraders to give them a bit more bite. Since you need heroes on the board for Darkoath Horde, you often run quite a few total DO heroes, so likely you’ll have one of these to spare that can join the fray for the extra attack chain fight. It’s less often used due to the awkward unit sizes, but the Be'lakor chain fight synergy can also come into play here as well.

Don’t worry about Oath of Murder, it’s nifty but you shouldn’t let him get blown up to try and gamble on killing a foot dork for it unless it’s really easy to achieve.

Darkoath Chieftain on Warsteed

The mounted Chieftain lets you re-roll charges for nearby mounted Darkoath units. While that’s nice and definitely has utility if you’re deep on fellriders in a pure DO list, the main utility for this guy is being a great Place of Power camper. Fast, cheap, and enough wounds he won’t get blown up by a random mortal bomb. Place him near one and farm up those Fury levels.

Darkoath Warqueen

The more defensively focused foot hero, getting a 5++ for Darkoath contesting whatever objective she is on is a really powerful thing if you’re either running a lot of darkoath or exclusively using them to gum up the board. Her oath isn’t unreasonable to achieve if you’re pushing up the board either, giving a solid +1 to hit aura for Darkoath - not a lot on its own, but pretty solid if you’re stacking this with the Chieftain and other buffs. Overall, the Warqueen is a solid choice, but definitely goes up in value the deeper you are on Darkoath. If you’re just running a smaller darkoath package in your list, the Chieftain will more often be the default pick.

Eternus, Blade of the First Prince

Eternus is just really annoying. That’s literally his job. He runs around the board threatening to shut off commands, pick off lone heroes, and reanimate himself when he dies. He’s overall a mixed bag, as he brings weirdly niche utility for an army that often doesn’t really want it. He does, however, bring some strength to herohammer lists with Archaon and Be'lakor as a reliable Place of Power runner who can generally go do a bunch of random things while you’re busy brawling with the big boys.

Exalted Hero of Chaos

A little cheap foot hero who can get bonus rend and damage against heroes and monsters. While this might make you want to run him as a disposable duelist, he still won’t always have the punch to meaningfully kill things. This season, his main role is the fact that he’s a really cheap hero. He can run around to help with tactics, screening, and Places of Power while still having just enough bite that other lone heroes will have to watch themselves around him. Realistically, you only jam him in lists where you can’t afford anything else and don’t need another screen, but he’s one of the cheapest ways you can field a Radiance caddy that doesn’t die to a stiff breeze.

Gaunt Summoner

OK, first, Ignore his abilities. A 160 point wizard 2 that has an alright shooting attack and a 5++ is pretty solid value on it’s own, and often the reason I’m thinking about bringing this unit is because I want a 2-cast wizard but don’t want to invest in Be'lakor. In my opinion, that has a ton of value on it’s own and is worth weighing against the 5++ ward from the regular Sorcerer lord. He also comes base with Mark of Tzeentch, so don’t forget that he can use that from the get go.

Now, let’s check out the Silver Tower. There’s a lot of risk/reward to this mechanic. If you deploy something in the tower and successfully get off Divert Realmgate when you need to, you can put a very scary unit in a very threatening spot - Theridons, Karkadrak Lords, and even Skarbrand from the Regiment of Renown are common choices here. The caveat is that the Gaunt Summoner is not a tough model, and if it’s killed prior to you dropping your unit out of the tower, it’s gone. You have to balance how much you’re willing to invest with how much you’re willing to risk going away if things go haywire and you lose the Summoner, so there are frequently matches where I will just leave the tower empty in deployment if I think the threat is too rough for the Gaunt to handle. Overall though, it’s a solid package and definitely has a place in the faction, especially if you don’t care as much about the ward handout from the Sorcerer.

Gaunt Summoner on Disc of Tzeentch

Listen, this warscroll is just bad. You lose a lot of the power of the Silver Tower by swapping Divert Realmgate for a funny spell that can sometimes kill a hero, but requires you to be in combat in the Hero phase to actually use it. I assure you, this model will not survive combat against anything you’d actually want to kill with this. It’s a very fast 2 cast wizard, but that’s all it really has going for it.

Ogroid Myrmidon

Foot hero for Theridon synergy. Chain fights with +1 to hit, which is VERY strong on a unit that hits on 4’s. Has really solid attacks and gets more attacks when damaged, which has great synergy with Rune of Murder and Deathmonger. If you’re running reinforced theridons, you absolutely want to pack this guy, as he bumps up their effectiveness a ton while still being great at brawling with anything, especially monsters. He’s still a bit squishy, so don’t get too reckless unless you know you’re going to be swinging first.

Brand’s Oathbound

We’re going to talk about all of them as a package, because that’s really the only way they come. This is actually a solid choice to take now, as you get quite a bit for what you pay for. The catch is that you need to re-evaluate how you view this: Gunnar and Singri are both random foot dorks, and you’re really paying to get a solid wizard with extra goodies. Singri might be in contention for one of the best Place of Power campers, even though she’s very vulnerable to spells that can chuck mortals. Gunnar can sometimes spike and throw out some mortals, but realistically his main role is similar to the Exalted Hero where you’re using him for utility and screening, and occasionally he’ll hop in the fray if you need him to go try and punk something squishy. Broken Nadja is the real sauce, making The Oathsworn Kin an (effectively) 9 health wizard with a pretty solid warscroll spell to reduce a hero’s attacks by 1 that has an 18” range and goes off on a 5!

The only main drawback is that they pretty much always will have to be in their own regiment, so if you’re a list that wants to hang around 2 drops, you often won’t be able to slot these in without going up a full drop entirely. If you don’t care about drops however, this is a great choice this season to have reliable PoP presence and casting power.

Slaves to Darkness Chaos Lord. Credit: Loxi

How to Pick Your Characters

Your hero selection is going to boil down heavily to what units you want to support with them. Your first main decision point will be if you are picking a build-around hero. If you’re going with Archaon or Be’lakor, having someone to hang on to Radiance of Dark Glory and sit by them will be invaluable. Beyond that, you have to consider what unit “packages” are valuable to your list. This isn’t a comprehensive list, but generally you’ll want to be picking units first and deciding what heroes to back them up with. For build around characters:

Archaon - either mounted Chaos Lord, Centurion Marshall if you’re tight on points.

Be'lakor - Whatever can chain fight with your main frontline castle unit - Chaos Lord is usually the choice if you’re in a Warriors of Chaos list, Darkoath Chieftain for DO.

Abraxia - This is the trap choice here - she isn’t really worth bringing with Varanguard and arguably isn’t worth it at all right now, but if you are going to use her, just use her as a beatstick and don’t try to give her more support.

If you aren’t running a build around expensive character like above, here are often going to be your picks for your mainstay units:

Chosen - In contention for one of the best tools in the book. If you’re playing defensively with Be’lakor, make sure to try and have a Chaos Lord. Sorcerer Lords are the best choice for brawling with the 5++, but the Gaunt Summoner is better if you want to go full send with Bridge + Daemonic Speed. Chaos lord on Karkadrak is also fantastic here, especially if you aren’t going to bridge them.

Chaos Warriors - Sorcerer Lord for the ward, Chaos Lord for the chain fight can be decent too. They don’t need a ton of support, but reinforced they’ll be happy to have the Sorcerer Lord backing them up.

Scourge of Aqshy Chaos Warriors - Daemon Prince (assuming you’re playing Despoilers, which you should be if you’re running a DP) to hand out Nurgle early and brawl with them.

Ogroid Theridons - Ogroid Myrmidon unless you’re going to run them MSU.

Chaos Knights - Chaos Lord on Daemonic Mount if you want them to actually be a little hammer package.

Darkoath Marauders - Darkoath Warchief. If you’ve already got a few, then go for the Warqueen.

Aside from these choices, think about what utility they provide your list. Your goal is going to be centered around fighting your opponent as that’s what Slaves to Darkness specialize in, so consider what will help you either:

  • Get your combat units where they need to be
  • Help you combat units do their job better
  • Support the rest of your plan while your combat units are fighting

Places of Power are very important this season, so having at least one hero that you’re fine with sitting by one to farm Fury is very worthwhile, especially if you’re fielding Aqshy Warriors.

Final Thoughts

One of the nice aspects of the faction is that there are very few bad hero selections - I really only rate Abraxia and the Gaunt on Disc poorly, as every other option has a role, even if they are niche. While you’ll often be gravitating towards specific hero choices (Chaos Lords of all flavors, Be’lakor, Warchiefs), there can be a home for most of these depending on how points fluctuate or what your specific list needs to utilize.
