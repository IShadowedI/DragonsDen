Tabletop Battles Recaps: The July 2026 40k Balance Update

July 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The first big balance pass of 11th edition has arrived, following a shockingly reasonable initial meta. For as powerful as things like Orks & Mont'ka T'au were, this has overall been one of the most balanced edition launches we've seen in recent memory, especially compared to the horrors of 10th's initial Index: Aeldari. As such, for many factions the touches are fairly light, with a few ups and downs of 5-10pts for various units. In terms of overall trends, Dedicated Transports saw nearly-unilateral point decreases, as Transports haven't been especially good thus far in 11th, while certain crab-shaped Daemon Engines were once again hit with point increases across the board. The biggest shift, however, is to that of Dispositions—essentially all 1 DP options lost Purge the Foe, and for many of the game's best 3 DP Purge options, they're now forced to play things like Priority Assets or Disruption. It's great to see them use this balancing knob immediately, as a way to hopefully incentivize players towards different Detachment combos & Dispositions, but it will take some time to tell how much of a nerf this actually is. In a similar vein, a couple strong 2 DP options were shifted up to 3 DP, something I'm genuinely shocked they're doing this early, and the relevant articles go in-depth as to how this affects potential playability. Huge R.I.P. to Hexwarp Thrallband though, ouch.

For this week, our analysts broke down all the changes from this balance pass into four individual articles, covering the most notable shifts to each faction & how their standing in the meta overall will be impacted. While for many armies this isn't a groundbreaking set of changes, there's just enough here to shake up the meta, and hopefully force players into new directions for their lists. Let's get into it!

All of these sections have their associated articles linked below, where we go significantly further in-depth on each of these topics.

Balance Update: Overview, Rules, and Missions

  • Changes to Core Systems & Overall Thoughts

On the whole, a lot of the clarifications here arise from the fact that the designers didn't realize how many little syntactical things needed to be shifted around from 10th to 11th. There's clarifications on stratagem names on datasheets, how Precision interacts with reviving characters, and wording/timing changes on holding objectives. It's about what you'd expect, but there's also changes to some of the game's maps, which is absolutely wild to see this early on; the fact these maps can change with balance passes is another great lever for the designers to have, and here it's been employed to hopefully make Disruption a bit better. On the whole, our thoughts boil down to 'this is a good, albeit modest, first pass', and for a meta that wasn't too horribly broken to begin with that's a huge win.

The July 2026 Balance Updates: Chaos

Defilers and Vashtorr
  • Chaos Review, Post-Balance Update

The armies of Chaos that have been able to take Defilers have faced difficulties in 11th, because the crab itself solves so many problems. It is the epitome of a 'centralizing' unit, which applies pressure while being reasonably tanky, and often has utility depending on which faction's fielding it. This has meant anywhere from 1-3 Defilers have appeared since it debuted, usually closer to ~2 in 11th, and Games Workshop has made it clear in this balance pass their opinion is that you ought to play 1. The vast majority of lists across armies like Chaos Space Marines, Death Guard, etc will still find room for their first Defiler, but points begin to look rough as you aim to include the second, as Thousand Sons & Emperor's Children likely still have to. On that note, Thousand Sons were hit with some serious nerfs to their best list, but the faction's fundamentals are good enough that pivoting to a different build path likely saves it for competitive play. There were a number of Disposition changes for Chaos, largely away from Purge and towards PA, and this benefits the various forces of the dark gods differently—barring CSM, which have just Creations of Bile to provide Purge now. That hurts. For Daemons & Knights, there's a few shifts, with Plague Legion affected negatively by the character resurrection changes, and the Abominant receiving Ignores Cover on its Volkite, but overall things will remain the same in practice.

The July 2026 Balance Updates: Space Marines

Credit: Kevin Stillman
  • Astartes Review, Post-Balance Update

This one's a doozy. There's a number of changes here meant to hamper the two dominant builds of Marines at present, being Ironstorm+Fulguris, and the various soups of Librarius Conclave (largely with Forgefathers). The usual suspects of Sternguards, Librarians, & 10-man Infernus squads went up, as well as a few flavors of speeder. Still, these changes likely come out in the wash, with a few point decreases elsewhere. The real meaty nerf here is to the Repulsor Executioner, which will be felt, given the tank was one of Marines' premier anti-tank platforms. Still, perhaps the biggest shock is that the Redeemer remains a cool 250, so expect to see 1-2 in many, many lists. Templars didn't receive any nerfs to their 100-Crusader horde lists, although it's harder to fit a suite of Repexes in there now, as is the case with all Marine lists. Blood Angels & Deathwatch both got no help, and Space Wolves did...but it isn't enough. That said, there's one non-compliant chapter worth talking about, because Dark Angels, there's an army on the rise. While the ISC change to their hit debuff does hurt, mounted/mechanized DA appears to have legs post-slate, and we'd recommend looking to Kostantinos Argyros' winning list at the Athens Open for inspiration.

The July 2026 Balance Updates: Xenos

Goff Bigboss by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen
  • Xenos Review, Post-Balance Update

High highs, low lows for our Xenos friends. Both flavors of elf got some new tools, and point decreases, with Aeldari finally positioned to have play in 11th. While Warhost is likely an appealing generalist option, there's something to be said of Windrider Host with the new Exodite models; they're a bit of an unknown factor, and a few of the bikes did receive minor shifts down in cost. GSC & Votann got a few small point cuts, which is nice to see, and spamming light transports in Votann does certainly have appeal with their capacity to Rapid Ingress 2 Sagitaurs. Onto the nerfs, Orks got...not enough, probably. Sure, the change of Green Tide to 3 DP is a big one, but even so the army remains remarkably well-positioned. We'll have to wait and see what the Codex brings, but rarely does one launch too weak. Necrons lost their fun croissant gimmick, and can no longer readily resurrect a Precisioned Techno to your Wraith blob, but were handled with a fairly light touch. Tau though received significant point hikes to some of their best models, and also the Tiger Shark. I jest, but I did run up against the plane at a recent GT, and in terms of raw math it probably made sense to stop players from putting it on the table. There's something to be said of the Mont'ka Disposition change being a sidegrade, although opinions are mixed at the TTB offices as to how impactful this will be. Making a pivot towards fast utility pieces like Piranhas in order to score PA likely works out, but the days of all-in Purge Mont'ka are surely dead.

Points - Imperium

Assassin Execution Force. Credits: That Gobbo
  • Imperium Review, Post-Balance Update

Starting with Sisters, they received a swath of point cuts and a set of rules changes that shake out to largely being nerfs; this is a buff to the faction on the whole, but mostly because you're able to put more on the table, not assemble silly combos. Knights & Custodes received small balance changes, although the lack of easy access to Purge for IK is something to worry about. Their second-best option in Gate Warden Lance also lost the silly 'big line', which is how it was most often played prior to this balance pass. Good riddance. Agents...actually received a shockingly solid balance pass, now able to pair the majority of their Detachments with Veiled Blade, and coupled with the point cuts I genuinely expect to see mad lads & lasses putting Agents on the table at events. As for Guard, the faction wasn't doing well in the hands of the laity, and these changes to the big silly tanks should help with that; it wasn't a bad faction in 11th, but the way most people wanted to play it...was bad. The whole Guard saga was an exercise in managing expectations, but hats off to Art of War for not just standing their ground, but proving everyone wrong. Onto Admech, and we're likely to see the faction's playability rise by virtue of cheaper Transports and potentially Exterminators, but the big change is to Haloscreed Battle Clade. With access to PA, the best Disposition for Admech writ large, it is undoubtedly the best Detachment for the army in singles, now. Finally, Grey Knights did receive a modest nerf, but likely not enough to be dead. If you want to spam Grey Knight Terminators, perhaps consider doing so in Agents? Kidding of course. Mostly.

Ruleshammer - Transports

Chimera. Credit: Rockfish
  • Transport Rules in 11th

One of the major point shakeups seen across all factions during this balance slate was a reduction in the cost of transports, and thankfully, Tabletop Battles has you covered if you're brushing up on how they work in 11th. While yes, the new disembarkation rules make them a bit of a rolling coffin, properly staging with (and leaving) transports can be an incredibly efficient means of throwing units up the board, as they have been for decades. Of course, certain factions appreciate having them more than others, such as that of the elves, Admech, & Votann, but in a world where Take & Hold is gaining ground, being able to be the first onto objectives can put you way ahead. Just don't get caught dead inside one.

On the whole, this was a fairly light pass, with the bulk of text being clarifications or minor fixes to how rules interact in an 11th edition world. For the substantive changes, we've seen the designers reckon with the fact that Purge is a bit too easy to score (especially at lower levels of competitive play), and anything that approaches cover removal is grounds for being spammed. To that end, it looks like the meta is shaking out to be a fittingly-thematic duel between Marines & Orks—I put my money on Dark Angels—until their codices launch in the early fall. At the very least, I'd expect to see less of the boogeyman of 11th's launch, although the deaths of armies like Tau & Thousand Sons has been overstated. Shockingly, they've gotten the meta pretty bang-on from the get-go, minor quibbles about Dispositions & Detachment diversity aside.
