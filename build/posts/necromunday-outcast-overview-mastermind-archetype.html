Necromunday Outcast Overview: Mastermind Archetype

June 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In the back room of every bar, and the parlor of every gambling den, someone calls the shots. If a shady deal is done, a hit carried out, there is always a Mastermind behind it. Always someone in the shadows pulling strings and ensuring they get the best deal, even if those being manipulated don't realize it. 

The Mastermind, like the Survivor, is kinda difficult to encapsulate in the world of Necromunda (the Game). The Mastermind is the guy who works from the shadows, and does shady deals and takes hits. Their role is as a sniper, information broker/business savant, and leader. As with any of the Archetype choices, you have your ideal choices and options to choose from for your leader, their characteristic profile, weapons, and Skill choices. To aid you in understanding this better, we will look over these choices and give you our general thoughts and opinions of what you should do.

Some dedicated Squat players had “adventurer with robot pet” models before it was cool… Credit: Manofwaaagh

Like The Sopranos but Competent

As we covered extensively in the initial Outcasts article, the Leader of your gang is the most important person, and the most influential to the way your gang performs. Thus, choosing your leader is one of the most foundational parts of your gang. For the different Archetypes, this choice will vary, and thus you will need to weigh your options differently. First, let’s begin by picking a profile to use!

Characteristic/Profile

Of the characteristic profiles for the ideal Mastermind, there are two generic profiles that work best along with some that do well from the larger populace of profiles that abound in Necromunda. Your two generic options are a quite fast fighter with very good BS and a can-do attitude. The other generic is still quick, but has the ability to fight in both melee and ranged combat, as opposed to just being good at shooting. With that said, you still should focus on shooting, as the Mastermind really isn’t the kind that would want to throw themselves into the fray of melee, that’s what they have underlings for!

In terms of a profile from other gangs, the Delaque Master of Shadows or the Escher Gang Queen would both fit well into this niche, being good snipers and also the kind of people you would think to be controlling things from the shadows. Others, of course, will work well too. The Van Saar Prime is an excellent shooter, and other gangs have similarly skilled ranged combatants. Any guys with a good BS are good choices for the Mastermind Leader.

Honestly, if you were willing to take the risk and were ok with having a leader that’s not quite as good, even just taking a regular Van Saar Tek as your Mastermind Leader. Maybe he’s decided to strike out on his own and is using guile and trickery against the uneducated masses since he doesn’t have his Clan’s tech to support him? Either way, even the basic ganger has a fair BS so he could fit the bill for what you want.

Loadout Options

Weapons

The Mastermind absolutely wants a good long-range weapon. As in, not just 18-24” of maximum range, longer than that (unless you’re on Zone Mortalis). So, for the most part, sniper rifles. Long Rifle, Longlas, those are where it’s at. Especially the Long Rifle, Knockback is just a really useful trait. The Heavy Stubber can be an ok starting point as well, because of the range, I personally just dislike the -1 to hit at Long Range. Not to mention, I think the Mastermind really maximizes their potential when they are using a non-Unwieldy weapon so they can get really good positions to snipe with. However, if it’s an Autocannon or something of similar punch, I can understand it, at least if you make sure to get a Suspensor.

Shorter ranged weapons can be ok if you at least have good justification for them. The Plasma Gun (or Hrud Fusil), for example, for its ability to deal out heavy punches. Or the Needle Rifle if you need to snipe from the shadows (this one does fit the Mastermind well). Just make sure whatever you have is a good ranged weapon both in terms of its performance and its range. Your leader is a Mastermind; the Hive Scum are there to run in front of him and obscure the enemy's vision while he picks them off sneaky-like.

Credit: 40kHamslam

General Loadout

The general equipment is largely the same as with any Outcast Leader. Give the guy either Light Carapace or Mesh Armor with an Armored Undersuit. For personal equipment, a Grapnel launcher could be good for reaching good sniper positions, or give them a Cameleoline Cloak to be harder to hit once they do get there. Suspensors will be useful if they are using Heavy Weapons, and Photo-Goggles are always good to have in case your enemy is using lots of Smoke.

Speaking of, smoke is, as usual with the Outcast list, a great thing to have for making sure you are protected from aggressive enemy fire. With this guy, considering he’s a sniper, maybe smoke grenades aren’t the best option? Unless you’re giving him Photo-Goggles then, at which point they can lay down a smokescreen on his position and see out of it. Alternatively, as will be discussed later, having your Hive Scum lay smoke on top of the enemy will also work, though you might want to then give them Photo-Goggles too, which is expensive.

Skills

The Outcast Archetype has three Primary Skill sets: Cunning, Savant, and Leadership. You also get access to Agility and Shooting as Secondaries. The two Primary sets that you have (because Leadership is always primary on the gang leader) are pivotal to making a good cunning killer. So, let’s get into which skills you should have, and what they’re good for with this gang:

Cunning (Primary)

Infiltrate is a godsend to the Mastermind. Taking advantage of sniper positions is already what you want to do, and being able to just get there at the start of a battle is ALWAYS a preferable alternative to having to slog it there. Plus it means you can possibly get some shots off against the enemy early on and knock out their own boss to limit their potential later on in the fight! 

Y’know, I don’t particularly care for the Overwatch skill, just personal preference, but I actually think it would be pretty good here. Taking a Long Rifle or maybe a Needle Rifle (or a heavy weapon of some sort if you have a Suspensor for it), and blasting some poor bastard from across the way right when he’s getting ready to do something cool or important. It’s especially good if your enemies are clumped up (to potentially make some of them Broken by sniping some idiot out of the middle of the group) or standing particularly close to an edge to fall off of!

Escape Artist could be worthwhile to have if you are just unlucky enough for your leader to be captured when they’re your long-range sniper. I wouldn’t take it as the starting Skill though, it would be best as a ‘well I don’t have much else to get’ type of deal. Evade is in the same boat; because again, as a sniper, they’ll be far away and probably in Full Cover to maximize the potential of a good spot. Though if you tend to play on more open maps, it could be good to start with to increase your survivability. 

Then there’s Backstab, couldn’t be a less useful Skill for a sniper. That’s it that’s all I can say about it for this guy.

Leadership (Primary)

Honestly, the only Skill I think the Mastermind would want from this tree is Iron Will. The rest of the Skills require you to be fairly close to your fighters to use them. At the very least, Iron Will lets you possibly pass your Bottle rolls a little easier once you get to them, so that your Mastermind can have the last laugh plinking off a few more enemies before he has to retreat. Because let’s be honest, you are probably going to be doing lots of Bottle Checks with how shit the Hive Scum are; so being able to hold on a little longer before they flee so that you can actually just tell your opponent to fuck themselves by blasting a couple more of their people is useful, and pretty damn funny.

Savant (Primary)

The Savant tree has fairly cool Skills that a Mastermind could want. Fully half of the Skills are actually useful. The other 3 Skills are either related to trading or are Medicae, which isn’t as useful for a ganger who isn’t staying near his fellows. Fixer is good though, it gets good credits, and is always a nice tag-along to take if there’s truly no other Skill you really want. It may not make you a combat master, but 20 Dollars is 20 Dollars.

Ballistics Expert could be a good Skill to take as well. You will be sniping anyway, so why not take something that gives you a fairly good chance to hit even easier? BE can be coupled with Shooting Skills for a very good sniper character that is fully able to pop heads even when the enemy is barely visible. Plus, you’re most likely taking a profile that is a Leader or Champion style character, and thus probably has good Intelligence; making it all the more likely that you’ll get the bonus.

Munitioneer is a really good option too, especially if you are starting out with a Long Rifle or other piece of weaponry with a poor or middling Ammo characteristic. Rerolling your failed Ammo roll has the potential to keep you blasting and doing what the Mastermind does best, on the field of battle at least. 

An Enforcer Sactioner Automata confronts some scummers. Credit: 40khamslam.

Agility (Secondary)

Catfall, Clamber, and Mighty Leap are actually fairly useful to the Mastermind. Specifically, for positioning, especially on a map that is very vertical. Naturally, the Mastermind wants to be somewhere high up while sniping to mitigate cover as much as possible, so Clamber makes sense with making climbing easier. Mighty Leap allows you to clear gaps to ideal sniping positions without fear of falling. Finally, Catfall gives you a good chance of not immediately falling to your death if you happen to be shot off a gantry overhanging the map. 

Sprint might also be useful for getting to the area they intend to snipe from, which gives them an advantage in reaching the perfect sniping position quicker.

Dodge is an ok Skill, it’s not perfect but it can be something you want. It’s good for avoiding anyone attempting to do countersniping, though it’s not very reliable so I don’t think it’s really the ideal option for a Secondary Skill. Spring Up is great though. Taking a free test to stand up is great when you are wanting to aim before you shoot. 

Shooting (Secondary)

Fast Shot is perfect for the Mastermind. Shooting twice with a Long Rifle or Plasma Gun or whatever other punchy weapon is so killer and awesome. You can shoot two dumbasses off a ledge in one activation, or take a couple of idiots off the side of a vehicle Mad Max style. As long as you’re not using an Unwieldy weapon, you will be a-o-good and can blast away all day.

Aside from that one being excellent, Trick Shot is another guarantee to blast targets to pieces, especially since it negates shooting penalties for being Engaged, allowing you to have your Hive Scum just charge in and beat people over the head with hammers while the Mastermind blows their limbs off from afar. Marksman and Precision Shot are both great to have as well, and give you potential to deal tons of damage to the enemy. These two are good but not necessarily great, since they require you to roll really well on your hit roll.

Athena defends the House of Gilded Grace. Credit: Games Workshop

Champions and Gangers

Of course, what is a Mastermind without loyal henchmen to do his bidding for him? A Mastermind should be felt and heard, not seen. Thus, a loyal cadre of goons to work on their behalf are required.

Champion Armament

You really got two options with the Champs; either they can support the Mastermind and do the same thing as him, or they can support your Scum and be sort of like a heavy troop. In reference to the second point, I mean having them be like a bruiser that supports the Hive Scum and draws aggro, keeping the focus of your opponent firmly on the unwashed masses surging towards them, and not to the well-equipped sniper(s) in the back picking off their Leader and Champions. 

To that degree, if you are going to give the Outcast Champions the Mastermind Archetype as well, your best bet is to give them either a Long Rifle or a Grenade Launcher. This way, they can lay down fire along with their Leader, or lob grenades at the enemy to pin people so the Hive Scum can catch them with their pants down. If you go with the GL, consider buying Smoke Grenades as well, so they can drop clouds of smoke in advance of the Scum. If you make the Champ a bruiser, give him a melee Archetype and load him up with a good melee weapon and a pistol. 

In either case, the Champion should get Mesh Armor and Photo-Goggles so they can see through the smoke. If you have the funds, an Armored Undersuit is always a good option for survivability. 

Champion Skills

Champion Masterminds get access to Cunning as their Primary Skill set, with Leadership and Savant being Secondary. For the Cunning Skills, Infiltrate and Overwatch are easily the two best options. They let the Champ get to a good position for early-game sniping, or let them take out a threatening enemy for the cost of their own turn. I actually would suggest Overwatch as the starting skill for them, as using them to deny the activation of a scary melee threat so that your Hive Scum don’t get mulched as they approach to fight is very valuable.

Leadership Skills are Leadership Skills, if you have the Champion as a junior Mastermind, Leadership won’t do much good for them, just avoid it. 

Savant actually offers some pretty good options for the Champion. Fixer, of course, exists and can give free money, always a good option for a little extra spending cash. However, Munitioneer and Ballistics Expert are where it’s really at though. Rerolling Ammo checks for them and anyone within 6” is a great support tool for your Mastermind Leader, and also lets the Leader use their XP for more valuable Skills or other advancements. BE, on the other hand, gives you that extra sniping capability the Outcast Champion lacks due to their 4+ BS; though it is dependent on passing an Intelligence Check.

Hive Scum Armament

Hive Scum sure do exist. It’s so easy to equip them. Give them Flak Armor and either a Lasgun or a pistol and melee weapon. Give them Smoke Grenades too, so they can lob them at the enemy and create cover for themselves. Then, since they are up front and obscured, your Mastermind Leader can do his thing and don his Photo-Goggles to pick off enemies while the Hive Scum try to beat their heads in or blast them from behind some cover. 

Credit: 40khamslam
