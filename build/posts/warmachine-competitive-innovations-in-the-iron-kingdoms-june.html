[Warmachine] Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms: June and July 2026

August 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

Welcome back, Warmachine fans, to Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms! We’ve got two months of events to catch up on this time around, and this data is extra juicy since this data gives us a look at the post-June balance update world, and to see Fane of Nyrro hit the scene. Did we reign in the problem children enough? Are the factions struggling prior to the update now back on the straight and narrow? How are the vampire elves doing out on the tabletop? Let’s get right into it and find out!

As we start, a quick review of the filters we use for the data pulled here. For this series, we are only looking at ranked tournament games and are excluding online only events.

June and July Stats

In June and July we saw 2583 games played at 115 events, with a few bigger events in June, including The Siege Invitational, Bytown Beatdown IGQ, and the Welsh Masters.

Note: All of the Legacy factions currently unavailable as a part of Warmachine 3D are grouped together in the “Legacy Armies” section, due to their lower number of games played per faction and lack of widespread availability.

Games Played

A fairly even spread of factions here, maybe the most evenly spread we’ve seen so far. This time, all of the prime factions are sitting within the 10%-5% each sweet spot, with House Kallys sitting at the most popular at 498 games played and Old Umbrey nipping at their elven heels with 479 games. Meanwhile, the bottom three played factions are all within 10 games of one another, with Necrofactorium, Storm Legion, and Winter Korps all sitting at 254, 253, and 245 respectively. It would have been nice to see the Winter Korps and Storm Legion numbers boosted a bit here, considering both factions were targets of mostly buffs during the balance pass in June, but while its possible the changes made here could have elevated their win rates a touch, it hasn’t boosted their player population. Fane of Nyrro is the only Prime faction with a small playerbase during this time, but that makes sense considering how new the faction is, and these 146 games played represent games from the most daring or most dedicated Nyrro fans wanting to get their toys on the table as soon as possible.

Meanwhile, the Warmachine 3D factions for the June-July time period are nearly identical to their player populations in May. Gyrmkin remains hugely popular, with numbers rivaling mainline Prime factions, and Infernals, Dark Operations, Crucible Guard, and Thornfall Alliance have a much smaller player population. Gyrmkin’s housing crisis list was a major target of the June update, and though the house that kills you isn’t quite as good at, well, killing you anymore, the faction’s fun time Halloween theme and unique playstyle was able to prevent the playerbase from abandoning the faction completely.

Faction Winrates

Someone call Bernhardt, because the Century of the Vampire has come to Warmachine.

Fane of Nyrro is not only dominating the metagame with its staggering win rate of 65%, it’s managed to do that while only having a small portion of its model line available. That last piece here is particularly alarming, as the faction will only become stronger as it gains access to a larger portion of its toolkit, with little other factions in the game will be able to really do to affect it. For comparison, Kithguard had a 41% win rate in April, which was in the middle of the release window for the Kithguard line. Now that almost all of the planned models for the faction are available, Kithguard’s soared to a 55% win rate. While I don’t foresee Fane of Nyrro jumping up much higher than 65% once its full line is released (mostly because there really isn’t very much higher we can to go here), a 65% win rate is already very, very high and unless SFG steps in, it's likely that this number won’t significantly drop.

Image Credit: Steamforged Games

On the high end, it doesn’t look like the balance changes made to Old Umbrey slowed the faction down at all, still keeping a nearly 60% winrate post-nerfs with the second most games played. On the other end, Necrofactorium is languishing at the bottom with a 42% winrate. The faction did get a major buff in the balance pass to Mortenebra, boosting her feat to make her more dangerous when she brings back a jack from the dead, but even after the change she is struggling with a 33% win rate out of 39 games. There is some good news for Cryx fans though, with the Hellsinger Phantom and the Mechanithrall Swarm Warden hopefully patching up some weaknesses in the faction.

In brighter news, most of the rest of the game is in a very healthy state balance wise, with a majority of the factions in the game sitting pretty in the 55% to 45% sweet spots. Despite the problems on the higher and lower ends of this scale, the overall balance of the game seems to be in a good place, and the balance changes did accomplish several of its outlined goals for the metagame. Storm Legion is living large with a cool 52% win rate thanks to the new and improved Stryker, and Winter Korp managed to squeeze into the balance sweet zone at 46%. Plus, Gyrmkin are still doing great even after the nerf to the Slaughterhouse, with a strong 55% winrate. There's definitely some worrying problems for the metagame here with Old Umbrey and Fane of Nyrro, but overall the game continues to be in a great place post-balance updates.

There are two events we’ll be covering today, The Siege Invitational and the Welsh Masters, with a showdown focus for The Siege Invitational. Our very own wolfe_the_cat played at the final table for the third big event, the Bytown Beatdown, and wrote about his experience here on Tabletop Battles with Old Umbrey. If you want some insight on his experiences as he fought his way to glory, you can give it a read here.

TOTAL POINTS 100/100

It’s easy to see why Old Umbrey has become such a metagame terror when looking over Andy Cash’s Kpitan Galina Lissya list, because boy howdy is it lean and mean as hell. All four of the cohort models in this list are just nasty. The Feral gives access to the Rush animus for +2 speed and Pathfinder on a target, while also using its wings and Vault to reach almost any angle for a well placed stun shot to reduce enemy DEF, while the two Primevals and The Starved rush in and cause terrific damage for their points cost from both up close and afar. Lissya can keep them safe on the approach too, with her feat handing out +2 DEF and Reposition [3”] as well as Stealth from her spell Shadow Pack. With speed buffs from her gun, rush, and a free charge from Wild Aggression, the Primevals and The Starved can have some truly mind boggling threat ranges, tear apart their targets, and then slip right back into safety with Refuge. Combined with other deadly combat units like The Chosen of the Witch, resource management from Shearlings and Vorony, and a smattering of tough objective holders like The Hounds, Cash’s list is primed to brawl like few other lists can, all while keeping Lissya relatively safe from assassination. Its difficult to think of any list sticky enough to hang on against an onslaught like this.

If any list was going to do it though, its Brad Park’s Exulon Kythos list. This list is just about as wide as you can possibly make a list in Warmachine, with plenty of models to chew to chew through, like the Dominated Stormblade Legionnaires, two units of Criterions, two monstrosities, and the Invictus and Magnus super junior wombo combo. All of that is before factoring in the -2 to attack rolls and Dodge from Kythos’s feat turn, plus easy recursion and healing access. On top of this, Park has multiple ways to arc some dangerous spells like Deadweight and Psychic Assault, bolstered with extra dice from Occult Whispers. Park’s list may not be as killy as Cash’s Old Umbrey shaped ballistic missile, but it’s game plan to walk up the board and stay there is dangerously effective.

In the end, it looks like it ended up being a high scoring knock down, drag out fight to find out which list would blink first: the unstoppable force, or the immovable object. When the dust settled, it looks like Old Umbrey lost steam first, giving the game to Park’s Dark Operations list.

Result: Brad Park scenario win, 12-8

Image Credit: Steamforged Games

First Place: Brad Park - Dark Operations

Archetype: Khythos Tough Attrition and Thexus Scenario Schenanigans

Lists: See Showdown

The Cephalyx have been really good at two things ever since their inception: putting up walls of cheap beef, and tricky magic forced movement shenanigans, and Park brings both to bear with his list pairing. Exulon Thexus isn’t quite as beefy as Kythos here, but even with that being said Thexus still jams the board with tons of cheap Drudge units supported by two monstrosities. These dorks jam up the works, while Thexus can use a litany of spells to boost their defense against shooting and speed. Once Thexus gets a bit closer, he can drop his feat to move every enemy model in his control range 2”, which can set up both devastating scenario or assassination plays if the opponent lacks a good way to stop it. Nostilla and Aberration also pop up in this list to add on even more mind domination from Nostilla on top of Aberration being one of the toughest models to crack in the game. As we see in the showdown, Kythos lacks the same number of tricks but leans hard into being a wall of meat that most opponents can’t chew through fast enough before losing the game. Brad Park’s 1st place win here is a great reminder for anyone in Warmachine that the Cephalyx aren’t to be taken lightly just because they’re an old school faction.

Credit: Ryan “Wolfe_the_Cat” Schmidt

Second Place: Andy Cash - Old Umbrey

Archetype: Lissya Fast Brawlers and Morozov Shifted Beef

Lists: See Showdown

Lissya and Morozov were two of the OU casters not touched by the most recent balance patch, which has opened them up to more experimentation from players. As we covered in the showdown, Lissya has a list that is both crazy fast and crazy deadly, able to supercharge her warbeasts threat ranges to thrash anything on an objective or punish any opponent who puts their caster too far forward with an assassination run from downtown. Morozov, meanwhile, is a bit slower than Lissya (but only a little, the two Primevals here are still very fast), but makes up for it with a big pack of shifter models who love nothing more than to get stuck in and throw hands. Having to deal with every single shifted model at the same time when Morozov drops feat is a big ask for most armies, and if you don’t deal with it quickly these units are very liable to tear you up. Only one army the whole weekend was able to deal with anything Cash threw out this weekend, and that guy ended up winning the whole event. Props to Andy Cash here on a great run, showing just how nasty OU is, especially in the hands of a skilled player.

If Maerschalck and Cash are any indication, it looks like Lissya and Morozov are the new meta pairing for Old Umbrey, and what a deadly pairing it is. While there are a few differences in Maerscalck’s lists here compared to Cash, both are operating under the same general gameplan. If you need to lean on something tougher to play a drawn out brawl, you’ve got all the tools you need with Morozov. If you want to play from farther back and strike hard and fast like a bolt from the blue, Lissya’s got you covered. Maerschalck was able to pilot this deadly combo right up to a dominating first place finish, reminding all of us Old Umbrey is very good at what it does and what it does isn’t very nice.

TOTAL POINTS 100/100

Brezel took two lists this weekend, and while both Hellyth and the Phantasm have some great tricks, Brezel opted on taking Morayne all six games, with a list that offers a staggering amount of assassination threat in the form of Moraynes lengthy list of debuff and movement spells alongside three Mage Hunter Assassins units who are very capable of killing almost any caster they reach thanks to Decapitation on their weapons. Combined with Ambush or Advanced Deployment on the Assassins, and Morayne’s feat for stripping defensive upkeeps, this list is very well positioned to punish the opponent for poor positioning on their caster with a decisive assassination run. Meanwhile, Dreadguard archers can act as either a great unit to clear screens at range or a scalpel for annoying solos, Krueger can threaten a flank to even further keep the opponent on their toes, and Imperatus is more than capable of acting as an extremely solid scenario anchor, since it can stand right back up after boing down with Rekindled. The only player who could effectively stop Brezel’s gameplan over six games was Marschalck in round 5, taking Brezel to a great 5-1 record and a wild Mage Hunter weekend.
