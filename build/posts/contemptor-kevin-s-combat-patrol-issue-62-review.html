Contemptor Kevin's Combat Patrol Issue 62 Review

August 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back Combat Patrolers! After a long run of model issues, we finally circle back to a paint issue. In addition to receiving two extremely solid paints, we get a fair amount of narrative materials and even some gaming materials. What all do we get? Let's find out.

As always, thank you to Tabletop Battles and Hachette for the opportunity to review these models, magazines, and materials.

Hobby Materials

This issue presents us with two solid Warhammer Paints: Caliban Green and Wild Rider Red. Caliban Green, as it name suggests, is GW's main midtone green for Dark Angels. This color is a dark, solid green with good coverage and mixes well with many other GW greens (such as Moot Green) for good looking highlights. In the context of the Warhammer Paints in the Combat Patrol subscription, this is a far easier green to use than Orruk Flesh.

The other paint, Wild Rider Red, is an orange-red highlight color. I use it to highlight my red weapon casings for my Dark Angels and Orks. Layered over reds, it can create a clear lighter highlight before using another orange or flesh color for a second stage highlight.

Both of these colors are solid workhorse paints, and getting this issue for me was quite nice because both of my previous pots of the colors had dried after several years and several units worth of use. Thank you, paints!

The issue comes with a paint guide to use both colors to basecoat and highlight the previous Combat Patrol models with Wild Rider Red, and a section on how to do green Space Marine eye lenses (Caliban Green base, Orruk Flesh highlight crescent at the bottom).  This is a very helpful guide.

Narrative Materials

The Genestealer Cults' goal is "The Path to Ascension", which starts once a Genestealer Patriarch establishes itself on a world and concludes once everyone gets eaten. For an entity as big as a Tyranid Hive Fleet, it can take many generations for the chefs of the Genestealer Cults to prepare a civilization as an entree. So to that end, the cult will infect and infiltrate the host society while creating specialized bioforms to better facilitate the Hive Fleet's invasion. The Cults have a broodmind, which lets them coordinate their guerilla warfare against Imperial authority quite effectively. Then the Tyranids invade and sever the broodmind, leaving the poor dupes of the Genestealer Cults to get eaten by everyone.

Genestealer Cult original pewter patriarch. Credit: Michael O "mugginns"

The issue then swerves to something that the Genestealer Cults lack: Heavy Vehicles. We get examples from the Guard (Rogal Dorn Battle Tanks), Chaos Predators, Primaris Repulsors, and Aeldari Fire Prisms.

Heavy Armor featuring Caliban Green and Wild Rider Red.  Credit: Kevin Stillman

The last narrative material of this issue is a short story called "Enmity of Hope". A crew of space salvagers explore a Space Hulk, the Enmity of Hope. It's clearly an old Imperial ship (in part), and the crew wants various bits of equipment to replace parts from their own ship that aren't working. It's creepy and empty, until they find a locked door. They breach the door, and enter a cavernous chamber where the Imperial ship had phased with another alien ship with reversed gravity (which winds up splatting one poor woman). The crew continue to explore, then a mysterious six-armed insectoid steals another crew member. The two remaining crew members try and flee, but the Genestealer kills one and infects the second.

Genestealers. Credit: Pendulin

Gaming Materials

This issue does not have much in the way of gaming materials this time around. We have two more campaign modes: The Leagues of Votann versus Space Marines and Tyranids. In the Space Marines v. Votann game, Imperium mining colonies near the galactic core in the Hephaestus System are attacked by brutal raids from the Leagues of Votann. Luckily, a Space Marine Strike Cruiser carrying Strike Force Ocatvius is in the region, and comes to help out. The Tyranids versus Votann mission is set on a "verdant world of expansive forests, deep oceans, and towering mountains", "teeming with life". The Leagues set up a colony, but the Tyranids arrive to eat the world.

The Final Verdict

As much as I am personally fond of the paints in this issue for painting my Dark Angels, or painting in general, the paint issues are always a worse deal vis a vis the model issues.  You get two paints that usually top out at $12, and the retail value of the issue is $15.  So this issue is subsidizing the last several weeks of heavily discounted models if you've bought the entire package.

That being said, this one is an excellent paint issue!  You do get two workhorse paints that work well for Space Marines, Orks, and almost any other army you would wish to paint.  They also fit in well with the paints curated by this collection.  The narrative materials are fun, if a bit on the depressing side for all involved.  The gaming materials....well, now that we know that the Combat Patrols featured in this subscription will eventually get 11th Edition Rules, so 8 of the 9 Patrols available in the subscription aren't rendered useless for the subscription ends in the US.  That's definitely a good thing.

Until next time Combat Patrolers!
