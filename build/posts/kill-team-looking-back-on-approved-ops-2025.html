[Kill Team] Looking Back on Approved Ops 2025

November 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The next season of Kill Team, Armageddon, was teased earlier this year at the Big Autumn Preview show, alongside the new Approved Ops deck for 2026. With how the release schedules following that preview tend to go, its likely that we will see these new rules for the game sooner rather than later, putting us at the tail end of the Approved Ops 2025 season. With that in mind, a few of our Kill Team writers sat down and gave their thoughts on the last Season of Kill Team, providing their thoughts on the good, the bad, and the ugly of Approved Ops 2025.

The Good

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Things we liked about Approved Ops 2025.

James ‘Boss Grubbz’ Grubbs: Approved Ops 2025 improved upon its previous iteration by adding tactical variety to its Crit Ops mission pack. Allowing players multiple avenues to score Victory Points from, incentivized different playstyles and approaching each match up in a unique way. Whether it was Plant Banner forcing a contest over a critical Tac Op point or needing one more kill to advance your Kill Grade and secure the victory in Turning Point 4, there were plenty of opportunities for fun game interactions. Most of the Crit Ops encouraged interaction, though I did find a few lacking in that department.

TheArmorOfContempt: Crit Ops were expanded and diverse enough to make matches feel different in one's strategic approach, while Primary Op introduced a new element for players to track and plan around. While there continues to be issues with Tac Ops this most recent iteration of them was superior to those that came before with less options that were of no use to any team, and instead certain options only being useful to certain teams. While this feels like a backhanded compliment it's important to say that despite all its flaws Approved Ops 2025 was superior to the previous mission pack.

Swiftblade: Games Workshop made a pretty concerted effort for doing rebalancing passes on Approved Ops 2025 to address the balance between Tac Ops post launch, and I think they deserve their flowers for that. While there are folks who whine whenever GW makes sweeping balance updates and changes, ultimately keeping their finger on the pulse of the metagame and addressing Tac Op options that feel either under or overpowered is better for players both on the casual and competitive ends of the spectrum. The dream is that when Approved Ops 2026 releases, it’s perfect on the first try and doesn't need any rebalancing ever, but realistically that's just not going to happen. If it can’t be perfect, then I hope Games Workshop continues to try and keep on top of balance and errata.

As for the mission deck itself, I was very impressed on how much better the Tac Ops were in Approved Ops 2025 versus 2024. AO24 had a real problem where Infiltration had basically no “good” options for Tac Op selection, while Seek and Destroy and Security both had Tac Op options that would vary wildly in usefulness depending on what team you were playing, who you were up against, or even the map. Recon was the only archetype that had reliable options, to the point where having access to Recon Tac Ops was a significant power boost for a team, and being stuck with Infiltration was an albatross around a teams neck. While Recon is still the clear winner out of the four again, each archetype has at least one Tac Op that is very playable no matter what team, and honestly the only real dud Tac Op is Track Intelligence from Infiltration. If the design team improves on the AO25 Tac Ops with AO26, I think we are in for a real treat next season.

The Bad

Credit: Games Workshop

Things that didn’t quite work, and how we hope they can be improved in Approved Ops 2026.

Benjamin “Toll” Scott: Initiative was a hotly contested topic going into Approved Ops 2025. Players can make every decision correctly and still lose the game because they just happen to be on the lower end of four rolls of a die. Rightly, Games Workshop decided to do something about it, but initiative cards didn’t quite have the landing or impact they probably desired. Many times players will simply save their initiative cards for the final turning point, often meaning players will both be at a functional +3. While the ability to use the cards to subtract from your roll adds some decision making in theory, in practice I rarely if ever saw this function used. Ultimately, the cards often brought little if any change to a game and their intended use was rendered moot. Initiative still needs some changes, and hopefully AO26 brings something exciting with it.

Swiftblade: I agree with Ben here on the initiative cards. I remember when these were first announced, there was a widespread panic that these would somehow break the game, and instead ended up being little more than a wet fart. They never really felt like they would make or break a game, they either didn’t really end up mattering for the initiative rolls or a player would have such a huge bonus to their roll that it was basically advertised in neon lights that they would win the initiative the following turning point, and you would just plan accordingly. In a weird way, the initiative cards incentivized losing dice rolls, as losing the roll off for TP 1 and 2, where going first usually wasn’t a big deal, meant you could stock up on initiative bonuses for the late game where it mattered. Wanting to lose a dice roll is counterintuitive and weird. I like rolling big, I don’t want to be excited to roll small. That's the way of the coward.

I’d like to see GW return to something more like the Scouting phase from AO24, the rock-paper-scissors concept for determining first turn initiative was pretty neat in theory. Its biggest problem was the fact that the Equipment scouting choice was almost always the best, which threw off the whole system, but if they can make the possible selections more balanced between one another I think it would be much more engaging than the current initiative cards.

TheArmorOfContempt: Failure to update Gallowdark and Bheta-Decima. This last year of Kill Team can essentially be viewed as the year of Volkus and Tomb World, which is a shame because there is no reason they couldn’t of provided layouts to improve and fix the issues with Gallowdark and Bheta-Decima, which in essence cut the amount of viable maps in half.

James ‘Boss Grubbz’ Grubbs: While Kill Grade is a great idea for improving game interactivity and incentivizing getting into skirmishes, it is not the best implementation of this. Some of the break points in the chart can make for an extremely uphill battle. Short of dominating the Kill Zone and tabling your opponent, which could be quite challenging to accomplish into defensive playstyles, there were plenty of times where you could win Crit Op and Tac Op and still lose the game on Kill Op.

Credit: Warhammer Community

The Ugly

Things that just did not work and we hope don’t come back.

TheArmorOfContempt: Lack of play testing; Tac Ops and Archetypes continue to be based on vibes as opposed to how a team actually plays. While this problem is not new to this Approved Ops Pack, we continue to see that some Tac Ops are literally unplayable depending on your team. For example Martyrs is fantastic in the hands of a horde team, while it is a fast track to auto loss if played by elites. The Archetype system exacerbates this issue by locking Tac Ops into categories.

James ‘Boss Grubbz’ Grubbs: The initiative cards. Initially this felt very convoluted to understand and once that changed it was admittedly easier. That doesn’t mean it was a good solution for initiative. It did improve upon the previous Approved Ops initiative system, but left for some ugly Tac Op interactions with Tac Ops like Data and Download where it felt bad to win initiative all game and still lose because you couldn’t stop them going first Turning Point 4. A flat +2/-2 or giving a reroll to whoever doesn’t have initiative would be simple solutions.

Swiftblade: There are a few crit ops that are real stinkers here. I don’t know if anyone, in the history of ever, enjoyed playing Energy Cells, and Orb is just brutal if you don't have as many activations as your opponent. I get that we are trying to branch out here with designing these Crit Ops, but I’d really prefer that we keep all of these to selections that feel fun and exciting no matter what team you’re running.

I also really hope that for the next release cycle isn’t nearly as long as the gap between Approved Ops 2025 and 2026 has been. As of this writing, we’re still waiting on an announcement for when AO26 will be released, putting us at least 14 months since the main missions and the game were updated. By the time a year rolled around, everyone was ready for a new set of missions, maps, and ops to shake up the game, so being saddled with two (if not more) more months is a real slog. Next time, I hope that Games workshop keeps the release window as timely as possible to avoid this drain on the community at large.

AO2025 Highlights

Some personal highlights or games from this season of Kill Team.

James ‘Boss Grubbz’ Grubbs: Getting the most out of 4 APL Operatives and doing as many actions as possible in one activation. Playing Kommandos and Scout Squad most of this Approved Ops cycle allowed me the opportunity to use 4 APL operatives. This let me squeeze up to as many as 5 actions out of these operatives using an Ammo Cache. The peak of this for me was my Boss Nob getting me a triple kill with an Ammo Cache, Charge, Fight, Fight, Shoot a Harpoon into one of my local players. Doing Gork and Mork justice.

Swiftblade: I mean, the easy highlight for my time with AO2025 is winning Best Overall at the Dallas Open this year. It's an achievement that I’m very proud of, as it was a culmination of all of the hard work I’d put in for both becoming better at playing the game as well as with painting the models. I’d been gunning hard to win Best Overall at Dallas, though I tried everything in my power to play it cool and not get my hopes up during the event. Oh, but when my name was called, the vindication that shot through my body was amazing. Just electric. I felt like I was walking on air the whole way to grab that Golden ticket, and I’m pumped to travel to Barcelona later this year to play at Worlds and give AO2025 one last ride!
