Tabletop Battles Reviews: Warhammer Tone Pro Paints

September 26th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Games Workshop's new line of Warhammer Tone Pro paints have been announced, promising great coverage, intense colours, and usability across different application methods. They are also presented in pentads, with each set of five paints sharing a name, and receiving a number from 1 (dark) to 5 (light). Rob and Rich have had some hands on time with the paints, ready to share their initial thoughts.

We'd like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a preview set of these paints for review purposes.

TheChirurgeon: A set of these dropped on my door Wednesday night, giving me a selection of the new paints. Specifically, there was a full set of Blue paints, i.e. #1-5 for Blue Gorkshroom, a trio of Reds (Bloodtoof Red), a trio of greens (Hierotek Green), Black/Grey/White, and one each of Pink, Orange, Yellow, and Bright Green. That's a pretty solid set, though it did leave me wanting more pinks and yellows to play with - my specific interest in these is what their coverage looks like for light colors over black primer, because priming white is for cowards.

There were some issues with the paints, but these struck me as more QC issues than issues with the paints themselves. Specifically, in both of the sets I opened, one or more of the pots was leaking. It didn't seem like they were properly sealed, leading to paint dripping out and/or separating.

Loading...

I opened two different boxes of these - the preview set I was sent by Games Workshop and a second box at my local Warhammer store - and both had this issue. Like I said, I don't think it's a problem with the paints themselves but more how they're packaged - it seemed like the lids either weren't on completely or weren't closed. That said, the boxes we were sent are notably not going to be sold on shelves, and are more just sampler packs for demos, so the final product may have better quality control.

What About That Coverage, Though?

My big immediate reaction on seeing these paints was "I want to try the pinks to do Emperor's Children." And that was my immediate first go-to here, testing the paint on a pair of Emperor's Children models which I had already primed black. Painting the pink on these is usually a very involved process, starting with multiple thin coats of Pink Horror and working my way up to Emperor's Children. If I can replace that with a single step of pink, or just two and some shading, I'm in.

Loading...

There are basically four things I'm really testing when it comes to these paints:

  1. How's their coverage? This was advertised as excellent, so I want to know how good it is. I want to test lights over darks, and black over everything.
  2. How does it handle being "gooped on" to the model? This is another area where early reviews and preview video have suggested it'll look great.
  3. How does it blend? Can I mix it with other Tone Pro colors and how smooth are the transitions when I layer them up?
  4. How will it look when I start using other paints, especially my older paints?

One key thing about these paints is that they seem to be alcohol-based, and so will not mix with the water-based paints of the standard Citadel range.. They tend to dry pretty rapidly still - even goopy I was looking at about 15 minutes for them to dry - and they tended to produce a nice finish, though I think you'll still want to use your brush to avoid leaving obvious brush marks or streaks.

Loading...

Coverage on the pink was generally very good. I really felt it needed to coats to get there, but the coats could go on pretty thick and resolved nicely into something that didn't lose detail. They were also incredibly vibrant - the pink is much brighter and pinker than the pink I painted over black with my other models using Pink Horror and Emperor's Children. After I laid down those coats, I ended up using Carroburg Crimson to shade the edges and glaze coats of Emperor's Children to blend up to a lighter pink before using Fulgrim Pink to do my edge highlighting and the filigree work.

You can see the difference in saturation: The Tone Pro model is on the left; the older, Pink Horror/EC model is on the right. I'm really happy with how the model turned out and while it's a bit pinker than my older models, I'm OK to keep using this moving forward.

Loading...

After pink came yellow. My best opportunity for doing yellow testing right now is hazard stripes on Iron Warriors so I picked up one of my Mutilators and used him as a test model, painting one coat of Khemri Sands (4) over the black primer. I'd later correct with Blackstone Void (1), which I gotta tell you - it's an amazing black. I previously used Black Legion Contrast paint for doing base rims; I can tell you right now that I will never do that again - Blackstone Void is now the black baserim color of choice. It's just really smooth, covers well, and tends to smooth out brushstrokes as it dries, which is amazing.

Loading...

I tested the reds on some Red Corsairs models, using all three tones for highlights and I was impressed with how they seem to self-blend into lower number colors. Normally I do some semi-complicated wet blending with my reds for Red Corsairs armor panels but this time just layering on 2-3-4 of Bloodtoof Red worked remarkably well. I finished it off with Carroburg recesses and Wild Rider Red highlights, but Ideally I can do both of those with Tone Pro paints in the future.

Then there was the white. I don't paint a lot of models white these days but I'll say that the Tone Pro White (Blackstone Void 5) did a fantastic job covering over black. You'd still want two coats - I only used one above - but I'd easily place it on par with my Monument Hobbies white paint or Reaper's Pure White.

I went back and did a second coat of white (Blackstone Void 5) on the Death Guard model I originally put a coat on, and I was pretty confident that I could do two coats to get a full white over black if I wanted to on future models. It's really solid.

Cronch: I'm really intrigued by these paints, and jumped at the opportunity to try them out. Given Rob's efforts above, I decided to focus on two main areas. First, I'd use the full range of Blue Gorkshroom 1-5 to paint something, choosing a nice flat area to cover smooth basecoating, glazing, and edge highlighting. Secondly, I'd try out some of the other common techniques on another piece - airbrushing, washing, and drybrushing.

Tone Pro test subjects. Credit: Rich Nutter

For my flat panel, I chose the missile launcher arm of a Ballistus Dreadnought, which I'd handily built earlier this week. I primed it up in Colour Forge Standard Grey spray, then set to work. My goal here was to be able to both shade down and highlight up, so I based the panel in Blue Gorkshroom 3. The paint was thinner than I was expecting, but I thought it covered surprisingly well given this. I did thin it down a little on a wet palette, but not much. The size of the flat areas on the arm did mean I needed to do roughly 4 thin coats, and compared to several other examples of Tone Pro's shrinking and self-leveling properties I was a little disappointed here. Although the paint did dry quickly and smoothly, I felt that if an area was too wet it was still quite likely to leave tide marks behind as it dried. I expect this would be less likely on something more organically shaped though, and I was able to smooth it out with subsequent coats.

Basecoat: Blue Gorkshroom 3. Credit: Rich Nutter

Following the basecoat, I glazed some shadows towards the top of each panel, first with Blue Gorkshroom 2 then subsequently with Blue Gorkshroom 1, thinning the paint with water and applying in thin coats. The paint thinned down well with water on the palette, with colour still seeming pretty saturated in the mix and no issues with splitting whilst sat. I did find once again that I was getting some tide marks, although I do have an important caveat, in that I was doing this with limited time and trying to work faster than I normally would. In all likelihood, my coats weren't as thin nor my brush as empty as they really needed to be. I'd be interested to retry this using the two different Tone Pro mediums and see if that made a difference.

Glaze shade: Blue Gorkshroom 2. Credit: Rich Nutter

Glaze shade: Blue Gorkshroom 1. Credit: Rich Nutter

After shading I tidied up with Blue Gorkshroom 3, before highlighting some edges with 4 and 5. I also added some scratches with 5, which I reinforced with Blackstone Void 1. I felt that the paint applied well here, thinning well for highlights and staying workable on the brush for a decent length of time. Despite the paint's relative thinness out of the pot (sidenote: I did knock a pot over, and wow I spilled a lot of it in the time it took to catch) it didn't flow off the brush too fast to be controllable, and it also retained a strong colour, saving the need to reinforce the highlights multiple times.

Edge highlight: Blue Gorkshroom 4. Credit: Rich Nutter

Spot highlight: Blue Gorkshroom 5. Credit: Rich Nutter

I was left with one issue at the end, which is that these paints are really quite a shiny satin, a fact which I think was exacerbating my issues with the tide marks of the glaze step. As an experiment, I opted to hit the piece with an ultra-matte varnish, and I think that does show off the results a bit better. I don't think it's a necessity, of course, as you may prefer a different finish on your models!

After ultra-matte varnish. Credit: Rich Nutter

Rob: Damn, that looks great.

Cronch: I decided to stick with Blue Gorkshroom for some more testing, so I dug out a Space Marine Librarian from Armageddon, already primed in Colour Forge Bestiary Brown. I basecoated through the airbrush with Blue Gorkshroom 1, before doing some rough zenithal with 2 and 3. I thinned the paint somewhere between 1:1 and 2:1 thinner:paint, using regular Vallejo Airbrush Thinner. I was really impressed with how these airbrush! I had a solid coat in roughly 2 passes, and the colour intensity was really notable. The paint dried well, and stayed smooth.

Basecoat: Blue Gorkshroom 1. Credit: Rich Nutter

Zenithal: Blue Gorkshroom 2. Credit: Rich Nutter

Zenithal: Blue Gorkshroom 3. Credit: Rich Nutter

I figured it was worth trying out the Tone Pro Shading Medium too, so I mixed down some Blackstone Void 1 with roughly 5 brushes of medium per brush of paint, and then washed it all over the model. The wash seemed pretty good at finding the recesses whilst avoiding unsightly pooling, and I'd say it stained the same or slightly less than current Nuln Oil - that is, it did change the surface colour, but not too much. It mixed well, and I can see this being a great tool for mixing washes of light colours to fill in glowing eyes, plasma weapons and the like.

Wash: 5:1 Shading Medium:Blackstone Void 1. Credit: Rich Nutter

Once the wash was dry, it was time to try out some drybrushing! Using a soft, round Artis Opus drybrush, I built up a coat of Blue Gorkshroom 3 to re-establish the colour, before moving on to 4 and 5 to catch the edges and define the model. The paints drybrushed pretty well, I wouldn't say it felt like anything revolutionary but the saturated colours combined with the thinner paint did feel a little like it wasn't clogging up the brush as much. It's certainly a viable technique though, and it's always nice to have more ways to use your paints.

Drybrush: Blue Gorkshroom 3. Credit: Rich Nutter

Drybrush: Blue Gorkshroom 5. Credit: Rich Nutter

Drybrush: Blue Gorkshroom 4. Credit: Rich Nutter

Just as an aside, I know it's a bit odd to go to the effort of airbrushing some volumes just to wash and drybrush over them! Primarily, I wanted to test the different methods in a limited amount of time, and for an actual project I think you'd be able to get a good result with any mix of methods.

Finally, I decided to do a quick coverage tester on some brighter colours.

Tone Pro bright colours test. Credit: Rich Nutter

Left to right, that's Hierotek Green 3 (two coats), Chotec Orange 4 (three coats), Plague Toad Venom 5 (three coats), and the confusingly-named Blackstone Void 5 (two coats). I'm impressed with the coverage on all of these, especially the white which made me exclaim out loud as I applied the first coat. The orange is a good match for Troll Slayer Orange, and the green for Warpstone Glow. That's a pair of paints with pretty diabolical coverage, so these could easily find a place as more reliable replacements. Overall, the coverage hasn't come at the cost of detail - these were all applied with a decently sized flat brush, straight from the pot, and there's no significant obscuring of detail. My apologies to these Uruk Hai Scouts, who are now thoroughly the wrong colours for a life in Middle-Earth.

Final Thoughts

TheChirurgeon: These are an absolute departure from the standard paints Games Workshop puts out, and I'm very impressed with them. It took me a long time to become comfortable with Contrast paints - I think they're actually pretty bad for what they're advertised as - and using them for specific effects really changed how I felt about them. These on the other hand, have more or less convinced me out of the box. I'm not planning to toss my existing paints, but I'm definitely interested in picking up specific sets of these and planning armies around them. I don't know that this is how I'll be doing all of my Emperor's Children from now on - I need to color match new models to the ones I've already done - but the next army or two is likely to be one I paint using these for the base coats.

Cronch: It's been quite a while since we've seen any real innovation from Games Workshop when it comes to paint ranges, and these do appear to be a genuinely different offering. Time will tell as to whether they become an integral part of a painter's toolbox, but I am quite taken with the saturated colours and the thinner consistency. I'm really looking forward to trying more of the pink range, as I've been impressed with how well the brighter colours cover. I'm looking forward to hearing more peoples' thoughts as we see the full range released!
