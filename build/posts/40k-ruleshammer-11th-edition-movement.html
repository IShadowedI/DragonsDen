[40k] Ruleshammer 11th Edition: Movement

July 31st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back to Ruleshammer! In this series we take a detailed look at the rules of key games, but Warhammer 40k in particular. This week I’m taking a look at Movement.

Movement Is Always a Straight Line!

Each time you move a model in a straight line, move it horizontally across the battlefield. Measure from the same point on its base at the start and end of that move, and add that distance to any other distance it has moved since its unit began that move. The distance moved cannot be greater than the maximum distance of that move type.

No bending your tape measure around walls or obstacles in 11th edition; movement is now explicitly made up of straight-line sections measuring from a single point on the base. Some models have the FRAME (17.02) keyword which essentially allows the player to measure to or from any part of the model; whilst most models with the FRAME keyword don’t have a base, it’s not all of them.

FRAME (17.02)

Some models do not have a base; many of these are MONSTER/VEHICLE models. Such models have the FRAME keyword, as do some other large models. Whenever a rule refers to a model’s position in relation to anything else on the battlefield (e.g. when measuring distances), if that model has the FRAME keyword, unless otherwise stated, measure to and from the closest point on that model (so not necessarily from its base, if it has one).

When rotating a FRAME model as part of a move, if that model does not have a base, turn it any amount around its central axis, while keeping it upright.

Moving Through Other Models

Somewhat frustratingly for the casual rules reader, the rules for moving through other models in 11th are not all in one place. The general movement rules allow models to move through friendly models.

    • It can be moved through friendly models.
    • It can be moved through any space its base can fit through.
    • Its base cannot be moved through enemy models.
    • Its base cannot cross the edge of the battlefield.
    • All stated ‘While Moving’ conditions must be met.

But then Monster/Vehicle models have some extra rules to consider.

Each time you make a normal or advance move with a unit, MONSTER/VEHICLE models in that unit can be moved through friendly and enemy models (excluding other MONSTER/VEHICLE models).

There’s some ambiguity here on if the clause in parentheses relates to both friendly and enemy models or just enemy models. Personally I think it relates to both, preventing MONSTER/VEHICLE models from moving through any other MONSTER/VEHICLE models friendly or enemy.

Not that this rule only applies to Normal and Advance moves. A MONSTER/VEHICLE model CAN make a charge move through a friendly MONSTER/VEHICLE model without issue, but they can't charge through enemy models, unless that vehicle has the FLY keyword and can Take to the Skies.

There is however an FAQ that addds confusion to this issue that is worth mentioning here.

    • Q: While making a move type other than normal/advance move, can my MONSTER/VEHICLE unit move through friendly units?
    • A: Yes.

This FAQ does not mean that MONSTER/VEHICLEs can make normal/advance moves through friendly MONSTER/VEHICLE models. It's reaffirming that when making any other type of move the normal movement restrictions apply, friendlies can move through friendlies, friendlies can't move through enemies. I'd normally be specific here and use the word "model" but one of the other odd and confusing aspects of this FAQ is that is says "units" can move through "units". There isn't actually a general rule against this though, if your opponent leaves a 2" gap between their models and you models are on bases less than ~50mm wide they can move through that gap. In previous editions the rule against moving within engagement range would have prevented this but that rule is gone in 11th.

What about moving within Engagement Range?

In 11th Edition, models can move within Engagement range with most move types, they’re just not allowed to end specific move types such as Normal, Advance, and Fall Back moves engaged with enemy units.

Does the FLY rule change anything here?

Yes, but only if the model “takes to the skies.” FLY/FLYING as a keyword doesn’t inherently change how these models move, but while a model has taken to the skies:

    • It can move through all types of model (including enemy models and MONSTER/VEHICLE models).
    • It can move horizontally and vertically through all categories of terrain feature.

It is the wording chosen here that causes me to believe MONSTER/VEHICLE models are not able to move through friendly MONSTER/VEHICLE models when moving without taking to the skies. The exception FLY provides when using its special rule specifically allows models to move through MONSTER/VEHICLE models without specifying friendly or enemy, which I think supports the idea that they can’t typically move through friendly MONSTER/VEHICLE models.

I still want an FAQ to confirm this specifically but in the meantime I’m fairly confident this is the intention.

What about FLYING models with HEAVY weapons?

If a FLYING models takes to the skies it gets to ignore all vertical movement it takes during that move.

Ignore all vertical distance for the purposes of how far it has moved

And models gain the benefit of HEAVY if they have not moved more than 3”.

No model in that unit has moved more than 3" this turn.

So yes, presuming the FLYING models move less than 3” horizontally they can ascend to the top of a terrain feature and still have the HEAVY benefit apply. This interaction has been called out and affirmed by an FAQ in the Warhammer App.

    • Q: Does vertical movement in a unit that has taken to the skies count towards the distance a model has used for the purposes of heavy?
    • A: No.

What about moving around/through terrain?

Terrain features in 11th have their own set of specific movement quirks which vary based on the category of terrain feature and the keywords the model has. I’ve covered these details in my Ruleshammer 11th Edition: Terrain Guide.

Rotating Is Free!

The big change for rotation in 11th edition is that it’s free; all models of all sizes can rotate as much as they like and it doesn’t cost any movement.

Each time you rotate a model, turn it any amount around the centre of its base, while keeping it upright. Note that rotating a model does not count towards the distance it has moved.

For models on round bases without the FRAME keyword, this doesn’t make much of an impact, though it can allow you to hide bits that are sticking out from behind terrain and such if you want the model to remain out of sight.

Rotation can get you slightly closer!

Be aware that rotation can be used to end moves with some models, ones with FRAME or on oval bases, closer to specific targets than others in excess of their movement.
