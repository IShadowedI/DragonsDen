[40K] 11th Edition Points Review: Xenos Factions

June 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The final piece of the 11th Edition puzzle is revealed today, with the Munitorum Field Manual landing and providing points costs and Force Dispositions for all factions. Our team have dug in to analyse what this means for the early 11th Edition metagame in our massive review. This part focuses on the Xenos factions.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with early access to these rules. Any details we mention are subject to change until published on Warhammer Community or the Warhammer 40K App.

Welcome to our review of Xenos Faction points for 11th Edition. As it's a whole new world out there, most factions in the game get lots of changes, so we're not going to try and list every single one. For each faction, we're focusing on the changes that we think will have the biggest impact on listbuilding, and covering off where we think the army lands in the new edition.

You can find the rest of our review, covering other factions and a general overview, via the links below:

Aeldari

Aeldari pulled their signature move of busting the game right open with their Codex in 10th Edition, and survived repeated rounds of nerfs till they finally got smashed hard enough to fall to near the bottom of the pack. Even after that, players have still found ways to put in occasional solid performances with the army, as the pool of datasheets and detachments is very strong, just pricey.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Howling Banshees: -10/25pts, no steppers. Banshees get a big point cut, presumably on the basis that Fights First isn’t as good anymore. That’s true, but pushing down their price makes them shine just as trade pieces that can tick off positioning requirements at the same time. Jain Zar also drops by 15pts, so I cannot imagine leaving home without the first Jain Zar/Banshee unit at 190pts total, and will often look for space for the 10-model unit.
  • Warlock Conclave (4-model unit): -10pts. I am very hot on Seer Council as a core for various builds, and cheaper Warlock Conclaves only increases that, especially because this is a unit that gets better in the new edition, as their mind flamers not having Ignore Cover was a weird downside before.
  • Wraithblades/Wraithguard: -10/15pts. Having a few tough units really helps smooth over scoring in 11th, and the new cost of both Wraithblades and Wraithguard is super aggressive. I am especially hot on D-scythe Wraithguard at 145pts, yes please. Wraithlords also go down by 5pts again, which is presumably in recognition of them suffering somewhat from the new Cover rules, but 125pts is still an attractive price.
  • Death Jester: -10pts. The value of access to a good, cheap Lone Operative goes up in 11th, both new Harlequin detachments have some sauce for Jesters (multiple tricks in Fateful, the Scout enhancement in Twilight), and Battle-shock is better than ever. In that context, pushing the price down makes these super exciting.
  • Avatar of Khaine: -15pts. It should probably be more, but it’s a start. Yncarne gets the same.
  • Guardian Defenders: -10pts. On the one hand, Storm Guardians still shouldn’t have gone up to 110pts (where they have sadly stayed). On the other, it’s good that there’s finally some recognition that Defenders should be cheaper. Your first D-cannon also gets cheaper, so if you want to build a full doomstack in Seer Council or Battlehost, you can.

While not strictly buffs, there are also a couple of things that don’t change that I would describe as welcome. Fire Dragons stay at 120 for the first two units, and only step up at three squads, and Fire Prisms stick at 150 with no stepper. Since Armoured Warhost adds some pretty good value to the latter, that’s very helpful to anyone who wants a more murderous Aeldari build. Solo Warlock Skyrunners also stick at 45pts, and are going to be invaluable for packing in Action/positional scoring pieces.

Nerfs

  • Swooping Hawks: +5pts (first two 5-model units)/+15/20pts (third unit 5/10). Oof. So - Swooping Hawks and Warp Spiders are pretty much the poster children for why points steppers need to exist, which makes it baffling that for both Aspects, they only step at three squads, but get price hikes on the first two units. At some point you’ve got to look at the price on these and ask what we’re even doing here - five Swooping Hawks now cost 15pts more than five Jump Assault Intercessors (who went down, by the way. Known terrible unit Jump Assault Intercessors. Never seen in lists). Perhaps the thinking is that Hidden makes small speedy units like this more valuable, but that’s just not really true for stuff that needs to push up the board to act, particularly not when their output is pure shooting. Baharroth also goes up by 10pts.
  • Warp Spiders: +10pts (first two units, both sizes)/+25pts (third unit, both sizes). See above. At least Lhykhis doesn’t change, but her squad now runs you 250pts.
  • Shroud Runners: +15pts (both sizes). The betting here seems to be that Ignore Cover is worth a lot more to you, and that’s probably true, but it’s a shame that this slightly puts a damper on their cool new Detachment, and these were rare enough in lists that I think you could have left them as is and waited to see if they were now too strong.
  • Eldrad Ulthran: +10pts. Rude. You’ll still take him quite a bit, and Seer Council builds can likely recover the points elsewhere.
  • Corsair Skyreavers/Rangers: +5pts for 5-model units. A small extra tax on getting cheap utility pieces in, making Warlock Skyrunners even more important. Rangers end up a lot closer to Striking Scorpions in price (as those went down to offset the Scout/Infiltrate combo not being a thing any more), and you definitely want to take the better unit where possible. Much like the Swooping Hawk comparison above, it is also completely ridiculous that a squad of Space Marine Scouts is now only 5pts more than Rangers.

Outlook

Loser

Thoughts

Wings: My current feeling is that Aeldari are going to underperform early in 11th, because I don’t think they’re going to be able to stand up to the kind of gut punch builds that some factions are going to launch with. End-of-10th Aeldari aren’t in the best spot anyway, and the dynamics of 11th Edition’s maps and terrain rules seem more likely to hurt them than help.

If that prediction turns out to be wrong it’s going to be because people find builds that can really force through scoring on either Reconnaissance or Priority Assets, both strong Dispositions that Aeldari have good tools for. This is where the point increases to the fast scoring pieces bite, however, and I worry PA will be a wash because the Purge matchup falls apart if the foe can just roll through you - and I think they can. I do wonder if there’s a legitimate dark horse Ynnari PA list, since Venom-split Wyches with Battle Focus may genuinely be the single best Priority Assets unit in the entire game, and taking three units gives you the depth to stay on the board in the Purge matchup. The Yvraine/Storm Guardian/Warlock Conclave squad also does a good job of providing the one solid high-OC tarpit unit that this setup also wants.

My overall position remains pessimistic, but I do think 11th is wider open than 10th was for someone finding an angle on a build that really forces scoring, and there are just enough good tools for that left (Warlock Skyrunners in particular, faction MVP right there) that I’m not going to write the army off completely.

Drukhari

Drukhari were the last Codex to release in 10th, and while they had some good options (especially Spectacle of Spite) there’s pretty much always been a bigger fish in the pond to outclass them. They have a solid pool of Datasheets to work with though, and their army has always been crying out for something like modular detachments to capture the multiple themes.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Ravager (with Disintegrators): -10pts. This is a slightly complex one as there is a 10pt tax for your third Ravager plus Dark Lances now cost 5pts per weapon buttttt…. Ravagers are looking pretty hot right now especially with Kabalite Agonysts detachment rule while their Agonising Suppression rule stacks with cover to help protect your units. A traditional DL Ravager is still good but a Dissie Ravager is now really well costed and versatile.
  • Hand of the Archon: -10pts. These hand-picked teams of ruthless killers have had a hard time in life with numerous points drops and rules changes (splitting via Venoms) still not enough to see them on the tabletop in the past. A further points drop and their Archon’s Will ability now working from inside a Venom means they might become a staple to hide 15OC on your expansion.
  • Kabalite Warriors: -10pts. The most expensive of the Drukhari battleline likely still pay the price for toting so many weapons but with people likely moving away from Spectacle, Kabal stocks are high.
  • Haemonculus: -10pts. Now tied with the Succubus as the cheapest Commorrite character, the flesh sculptors benefit from numerous 11th edition changes such as Battle-shock and their ability to resurrect a wiped unit of Wracks.
  • Scourges (with Heat Lances/Splinter Cannons: -20pts. While your popular flavours of Scourges net out at the same points as you pay now, there is a cheaper alternative in the lesser seen loadouts. Splinter Cannons may remain a slightly niche choice but the ability to deep strike into Melta range now makes Heat Lances a lot more appealing.

Nerfs

  • Hellions: +5pts for 5, +10pts for 10, third unit is +15/+20. Hellion gangs have been one of the standout Drukhari units post-Codex, great in any detachment and perpetuated by Spectacle of Spite. This points increase has felt a long time coming but feels fair and reasonable, especially considering the aforementioned detachment’s nerf.
  • Mandrakes: +5pts for 5, +10pts for 10, third unit is +15/+20. As discussed previously in the article, small skirmishing units have taken a small tap and the shadowy denizens of Aelindrach have always yo-yo’d in their cost. Their Fade Away ability is still great and in 11th means you can hoover up some late game secondary points.
  • Reavers: +5pts for 5, +10pts for 10, third unit is +15/+20. Just like the Mandrakes, everyone’s favourite jetbikes have caught a small points increase and the tax means you’re now unlikely to see a third unit. Still looking pretty pretty good for their auto advance 8 and action in Exhibition of Slaughter.

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Sky Serpent: Drukhari have come through the inaugural Munitorum Field Manual looking pretty good with only minor taps meaning a lot of lists will likely net out at a similar cost. Those heading deep into the Kabals might come away even lighter while Wyches escaped unscathed and are great for their points for mission play.

There’s a lot of positivity in Commorragh right now with the new detachments which have opened up a lot of combinations with Skysplinter Assault likely to be the first detachment to rise to the top of the spires.

Drukhari players will likely be able to build a strong list skeleton which works well across multiple detachments with minimal unit changes.

As an Anhrathe addendum, I’m a big fan of Kharseth and their abilities that they bring to the Drukhari, their 10pt decrease might see more Archons invite the Aethermancer to their raids.

Genestealer Cults

As is their habit, Cults have had a confusing ride through 10th, landing a bit weak, needing their army rule re-written, but also having periods of being one of the strongest armies in the game via horde Host builds. Aberrant-heavy lists and Final Day builds with allied Tyranids have also put in solid work.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Achilles Ridgerunners: 0/-10pts for 1/2 models. +10pts per Heavy mining laser. Tax of 10 pts on your third unit. If you’re  taking the indirect (you probably are) these got cheaper, while the mining lasers should have an easier time finding targets on 11e boards. The only green spot in a sea of red.
  • Locus: -10pts
  • That’s it!

Nerfs

  • Aberrants: +15/0pts for 5/10 models. Tax of 10 pts on your third unit. The Aberrant datasheet rule requires a character to be attached to function, and they can’t be respawned with Cult Ambush if you attach a character which is to say units of 5 are worse than they were before, so this is a bit confusing.
  • Abominant: Tax of 10 pts on your third unit.
  • Acolyte Hybrids with Autopistols: +5/0 pts for 5/10 models.
  • Acolyte Hybrids with Hand Flamers: +5/10pts for 5/10 models.
  • Atalan Jackals: +5/10pts for 5/10 models, tax of 10pts on 3rd unit.
  • Benefictus: +5pts
  • Goliath Rockgrinder: Tax of 10 pts on your 3rd unit.
  • Hybrid Metamorphs: +5/10pts for 5/10 models. Tax of 10pts on your 3rd unit
  • Neophyte Hybrids: +5/0pts for 10/20 models.
  • Patriarch: +5 pts.
  • Purestrain Genestealers: +5/0pts  for 5/10 models. Tax of 10 pts on 3rd unit.
  • Reductus Saboteur: +5pts
  • Sanctus: +15pts. This one is a bit silly. They could have just baked more points into the enhancement in the Heroes of the Uprising detachment, without which you probably won’t run a Sanctus (and at 35pts, it means a useful Sanctus will run you 100pts in total, which is probably reasonable for what it does).

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

TwoHorse: Genestealer Cults have been hit by points nerfs across the board, and the core change to character attachment is a substantial nerf to the Cult Ambush army rule (if you join a character to a unit you can’t respawn it). That said, they benefit defensively from the changes to cover and hidden while their offensive output is relatively unchanged, and they’re well positioned to lean into any of the dispositions on offer. Expect to see GSC as a frequent flex-pick in team events, and they have a wealth of tools to build a counter-meta terror once the launch dust settles.

Leagues of Votann

Votann’s codex was quite late in the 10th release schedule, and was aggressively fine, and has trucked on in that state since.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Sagituar: -5 points, just in time to boom back in the meta with their Armoured Trailblazer detachment.
  • Uthar the Destined: -5 points, no idea why but its a nice 5 point drop that may help your list.
  • Ironkin Steeljacks: -5/-10 points for the first 2 units on both steeljack varieties. Steeljacks getting cheaper might make them a contender to sneak into lists now.
  • Kapricus Defenders: -5/-10 points. This unit never really saw the table in 10th edition, maybe with it getting cheaper and toe-ing cover to shoot through, it finally will.
  • Einhyr Champion: -5 points, a nice little drop for the Hearthband enjoyers in the room.

Nerfs

  • Hekaton Land Fortress: +10 points, +25 points for 2nd+. Land forts going up is kind of expected considering it ignores bs/hit mods. So the cover changes are a straight buff to this unit, especially with the amount of AP-1 shooting it has. Having to pay a bunch extra for 2 or 3 isn’t ideal, especially since it's the only transport for close to half our infantry units.
  • Brokhyr Thunderkyn: +10/+10 for the 3rd unit. This will slightly hurt shootier builds via points shifting.
  • Einhyr Hearthguard: +5/+10 for the 3rd unit. Slight nerf to Hearthband (maybe offset by the Champion drop).
  • Arkanyst Evaluator: +5 points. With the changes to Hazardous meaning he could live longer, this seems like a reasonable increase.
  • Hernkyn Pioneers: +10/+10 for the 3rd unit. Taking 3 of the 3 model units added a lot to lists, so this hurts a little.

Force Dispositions

2 DP Detachments
  • Brandfast Oathband - Take and Hold
  • Delve Assault Shift - Purge the Foe
  • Hearthfyre Arsenal - Priority Assets
  • Mercenary Oathband - Take and Hold
  • Needgaard Oathband - Purge the Foe
  • Persecution Prospect - Disruption
1 DP Detachments
  • Armoured Trailblazers - Disruption
  • Farseekers - Reconnaissance
  • Hearthguard Covenant - Priority Assets (Unique: Hearthband)

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Shane: Minimal points cost changes for Leagues of Votann. A lot of units got minor drops, which should help cover any units that went up. Overall it looks like most lists will have about the same amount of stuff it did before. Hearthband being the only 3DP detachment was a little surprising, but makes for a lot of potential overall. Depending on what the Dispositions shake out in the meta, there might be some changes to what people want to take. Needgaard being Purge I think will make it become popular again, unless the Hearthband enjoyers find that Priority Assets is not only doable but good.

Curie: Amusingly I looked at these points and slapped in my codex-release Needgaard list and it’s exactly 2000pts with adding two copies of the new upgrade to the Yaegirs from the new Farseekers detachment. Time is a flat circle.

Orks

Orks suffered from success in 10th, overperforming after release more because people didn’t know how to deal with them than because they were actually too good, and paid for it in nerfs. They also had a more genuine round of this with the More Dakka detachment, briefly the most broken thing in the game, which then pulled down the much more balanced Taktikal Brigade alongside it. Despite all this, War Horde lists still do well sometimes, and there’s a healthy crop of detachments waiting for the right points cut to get them on the table.

Key Points Changes

Great news - there’s a lot of drops here, though a handful of units do see rises. We also get points for the new models, which we’ll set out separately.

New stuff

  • Bannernob: 50pts. Cheap and cheerful for his effect, and don’t forget he’s OC6!
  • Bigboss: 55pts. Again, a pretty cheap character to have as an option.
  • Big Mek Dakkarig: 100pts (110 for 3+). Reasonable value for the amount of shooting these bring and their relative toughness.
  • Wartrakk: 60pts. Cheap and cheerful.

Buffs

There are a LOT of 5-10pt drops here for Orks, on a lot of units you would like to take. A few drops are even more substantial, so let's take a look at those.

  • Battlewagon: -15pts. An interesting one as the ‘ard case now becomes a +15pt option, so if you were running one before they stay the same, but if you want to run one without (say, as a platform for shooting), you make a saving.
  • Beastboss on Squigosaur: -15pts. Chunky, and there’s a big drop to Squighog Boyz to come too, so the overall unit drops significantly.
  • Beast Snagga Boyz/Boyz: Both drop 5pts on the 10 model unit, but 20 on the big unit. We highlighted in our review articles that 20 model units might struggle a bit and GW presumably agree, but that’s quite a healthy discount.
  • Breaka Boyz/Tankbustas: -15pts. Both units drop considerably, down to 125. This reverts the nerfs from a while back, and brings them to a much more sensible cost.
  • Deffkoptas: -5pts on the 3 model unit, but -20 on the big one. You were seeing these in Kult of Speed lists anyway, so making the bricks 20pts cheaper makes them much easier to squeeze in.
  • Mozrog Skragbad: -20pts. Again, Squighogs took a big drop too, so this is doubly beneficial. Mozrog was already seeing play, so this may help to make him even more popular.
  • Squighog Boyz: -10pts on the 4 model unit, but a chunky -30 on the 8 models. Along with the drops to the Beastboss and Mozrog, it’s good times for Da Big Hunt.
  • Stompa: -200pts is probably the biggest drop you’ve seen on anything. Does it make the Stompa any good? Who knows, but you want to try one now, don’t you?

Nerfs

  • Gretchin: +5pts for the 11 model unit. Still the cheapest thing in the army so you’ll keep drinking that garbage, and trying to generate CP with them. No word on what you’re meant to do with the 10 guys from the Armageddon box - we suggest finding a spare model to be a Runtherd.

With just the one slight bump to Gretchin and small to medium drops on lots of other stuff, Orks come out of this pretty well.

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Corrode: Orks were only ok at the end of 10th edition, with a lot of their best stuff getting points hits around the More Dakka/Taktikal Brigade era that never got a second look. There’s a lot of points cuts here, enough that lists gain a full unit or two as a result. There’s a lot of good dispositions on offer here too - you have a lot of options on Take and Hold and Purge the Foe, likely the most straightforward ones, and while we didn’t rate Rollin’ Deff too highly as a detachment it does give you a way to flex into Priority Assets. We’re going with Neutral rather than Winner here mostly because Orks and their nearly army-wide BS5+ may struggle with the new cover rules, and we’ll need to see how that shakes out for them to get a true picture of the faction’s potential.

Necrons

Necrons have had a fantastic ride in 10th Edition, receiving one of the best early Codexes and great ongoing support from digital detachments and new Datasheets. Even in Defiler metagame, they’ve still been winning some events, and they have a really deep pool of viable options.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Necron Warriors: -10pts (both sizes). If you want to play for objectives, Necron Warrior blobs are your friend, and getting a big one slightly cheaper (and with access to the new options from Hand of the Dynasty) is sweet. 90pts for 10 is also a price point where you starting thinking about taking them to sit off to one side somewhere, perhaps with a Psychomancer now that they have to join a unit.
  • Lokhust Destroyers: -20pts (first two 6-model units). Regular Lokhusts are massively improved in 11th, and big units get substantially cheaper at the same time. They’re easier to move round the table, AP-2 is a better place to be, and full re-rolls against targets on objectives is better than ever. Some smaller unit sizes also go down, as does the Lokhust Lord and their heavier brethren. It’s Lokhust season.
  • Night Scythe: -20pts. The Night Scythe gets a bit point drop alongside getting made into a much more normal and functional unit, and is extremely good now. Helps that big Immortal units also go down by 10pts, and I have been enjoying dropping a full squad with Plasmancer, Tools of Dominion and Canoptek Crawlers out and blowing things away.
  • Annihilation Barge: -10pts. 95pts is incredibly inexpensive for a fast, moderately tough position piece.
  • A bunch of 5/10pts drops on stuff you wanted anyway: I was already going to take some Flayed Ones in every list, so I will simply not be complaining that they are 5pts cheaper, ditto Canoptek Reanimators and Tomb Blades. Canoptek Macrocytes also drop to cost the same as Tomb Blades, so you may now consider them sometimes. You also get small drops on Skorpekh and Lychguard, again not really enough to change whether you wanted them, but nice if you did.

I will give a couple of honourable mentions too. Your first Wraith/Technomancer unit doesn’t go up at all, and boy are they good in 11th. Units 2+ get stung, so stick to one, but that one is high value. Also, the Obelisk. It’s 290pts with the Upgrade from the Phaeron’s Armoury and that might actually be somewhat real. What a world. Finally, two-model Canoptek Spyder units drop by an eye-watering 40pts to 110pts. They still only move 5”, and don’t really do anything well enough to be really exciting, but it’s an impressive number.

Nerfs

  • C’tan Shards: Variously +15/20pts. Big FLY Monsters with InSvs go up in cost in 11th, as a rule, as the new rules theoretically favour these, but C’tan have had quite a few nerfs stacked up at this point. The Void Dragon comes out of this looking OK at 345pts, particularly because the AP-1 Sustained shooting attack also improves in the new edition, and you may still consider the Nightbringer as well, but the Deceiver is a definite loser, as the new Cover rules don’t help them, their small base helps less with the new way engagement works, and their lower speed means new FLY isn’t as good for them.
  • Monolith: +20pts (1st)/+40pts (2+). I find this change genuinely baffling, if I’m honest - Monoliths aren’t good! They haven’t been for ages! The only reason they’re this expensive is that they get obnoxious when you spam them, but that’s what steppers are for! This feels like a missed opportunity to reset the first Monolith to 350pts and have a steep jump from that (50pts+)  to discourage spam.
  • Chronomancer: +15pts (1st)/+25pts (2+). Honestly this is more of an FYI than an actual nerf, because these absolutely deserve this, as non-Stealth -1 to hit as a Leader effect is worth its weight in gold.
  • Illuminor Szeras: +10pts. Again, he’s still super good and you’ll take him anyway, but it’ll cost you a few more points.
  • A few other random things I guess: If you’re taking three Doomsdays, the third is +20pts, so probably don’t do that. A few Leaders go up by 10pts, but that’s almost always cancelled out by the discounts on the stuff they want to lead.

Outlook

Winner

Thoughts

Wings: Necrons look super good heading into 11th Edition, with a bunch of different viable Detachments, and lots of solid units to work with. The closest thing they have to a problem is that a lot of their Detachments pigeonhole them into Take and Hold, but they’re one of the armies best equipped to make that work for them. They can also get access to Purge the Foe via Cursed Legion, which synergises well with many of the units that got cheaper (and is a good combo with Hand of the Dynasty). I am extremely excited to try out a bunch of different lists, and worry my poor elves are going to be badly neglected for a few months as a result.

T’au Empire

T’au have had a good run in 10th, as while they have a pretty small pool of Detachments and top units, their best stuff is really good at what it does, and has been able to continue winning even in the face of other very pushed late-Edition armies.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Crisis Sunforge Battlesuits: -15pts (-5pts for unit 3). I mean sure. This unit wasn’t bad before, but it’s better now, and one of the potential lures to try a Kauyon build with Experimental Prototype Cadre so they can drop within melta range (and they’re also fine in either Mont’ka or Ret Cadre lists).
  • Broadside Battlesuits (3-model): -15pts with HYMPs, -30pts with Rail Rifles. This is a top-tier unit in current lists, so cheaper is good.
  • Sky Ray Gunship: -10pts. This is also currently a good unit that sees play.
  • Plasma Fireknifes/Burst Cannon Starscythes: -20pts. These win big from equipment pricing, because these are the weaker versions of these two units, but they’re not 30pts weaker than the better versions, so are now extremely pushed - people already take these sometimes!
  • Commander Farsight: -5pts. Free real estate.

Also worth mentioning here is that your first two Ion Riptides stay the same price, which is incredibly good news. The third is +30pts, so maybe we’ll see a bit less Triptide, but you can also drop to the burst cannon on one to shave 20pts back. Ghostkeels also get equipment pricing, but are cost-neutral on the Ion Raker for the first two units.

Nerfs

  • The Twin Lance: +20pts. You’ll probably take them anyway, but it’s an actual tax you can’t avoid.
  • Missile Fireknifes/Flamer Starscythes: +10pts. The flipside of the above.
  • Solo Piranhas: +5pts (+15pts for unit 3). People absolutely do take three of these, and probably won’t any more as +25pts total is a decent tax.
  • Kroot War Shaper: +10pts. Makes setting up the free strat combo in Mont’ka slightly pricier. You probably still do it because something that can skulk Hidden on your home is useful.
  • Pathfinders with three Ion Rifles: +5pts. Only if you take all three Ions. I’m grasping at straws for stuff that matters here.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Mont’ka - Purge the Foe
2 DP Detachments
  • Kauyon - Priority Assets
  • Retaliation Cadre - Purge the Foe (Unique: Battlesuit)
  • Kroot Hunting Pack - Take and Hold (Unique: Auxiliary)
1 DP Detachments
  • Advanced Acquisition Cadre - Recon
  • Auxiliary Cadre - Disruption (Unique: Auxiliary)
  • Experimental Prototype Cadre - Priority Assets (Unique: Battlesuit)

Outlook

Winner

Thoughts

Wings: Well, I have bad news for the T’au players who don’t like it when people grumble about their faction being too good, because the next few months are going to be rough.

T’au look like the faction to beat for early 11th. They are extremely well-placed to exploit the new terrain rules, have extensive access to Ignore Cover to mitigate any impacts from it, get the extremely simple combination of Mont’ka and Purge, and their points changes read as if they were previously struggling rather than a top five faction. T’au are going to absolutely rock people’s worlds, and they have such depth of quality to work with that it’ll probably take a few rounds of changes to get them under control.

Tyranids

Tyranids were 10th’s victim of the first Xenos Codex curse, struggling with a book that wasn’t quite as focused on working with the new rules as later ones would prove to be. They did well out of some balance updates though, and have several strong detachments that have been doing OK in late 10th.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Gargoyles: -5/10pts. Any sort of objective plan wants lots of these, so cheaper is good.
  • Hive Guard: -10/20pts. Presumably this is because indirect gets worse, but people have been using six-model shockcannon units, and as AP-1 weapons those improve quite a bit. People may well be hunting for some additional anti-tank, as Tyrannofexes don’t love the cover changes, so I can see these actually getting into builds.
  • The Swarmlord: -10pts.Technically not a straight buff because his Vect less good, but that buries the lede a bit since for Tyranids he’s also their CP farming Character, so you still want him a lot of the time (and as a melee-only monster with an invuln, Cover changes are good for them).
  • Norn Assimilator: -25pts (1st unit)/-5pts (units 2+). The new Norn detachment looks hot, and people have been genuinely kicking around taking triple Assimilator with Assimilation Swarm alongside it, so going down in price (even allowing for the stepper) is good. It means that the 4+ Invulnerable upgrade on the first one only takes you back to the original price.
  • Von Ryan’s Leapers: -10pts/20pts. These are super cheap now whether you just want them as Infiltrators that guard an objective well, or big units with the new detachment.

Honorable mention-wise, 20-model Termagant/Hormagaunt units get 10pts, and while big swarms obviously are worse in 11th because of the positioning changes, but these do currently see play and they get cheaper. Old One Eye also drops 10pts, and sure people already took him so why not.

Nerfs

  • Tyranid Prime with Lash Whip: +10pts. Unsurprising given that these are so pushed at 65 that almost every build has had minimum two, often three. Still takeable at 75, but you at least think about whether you should have a Lictor instead now (especially in the new detachment)
  • Maleceptor: +10pts (+20pts for 3rd). These have been good forever and get better in 11th, as they both usually benefit from the cover changes defensively (since they’re 3+/4++), and can apply -1 to Hit to a decently large amount of space on a push turn. That does come with a slightly elevated cost, but these should absolutely still see play.
  • Neurolictor: +10pts (+20pts for 3rd). Also improved in 11th, so also go up in price, which is reasonable but it opens a big gap between them and regular flavour Lictors.
  • Winged Hive Tyrant: +15pts. The standard tax many big FLY threats get, but arguably hurts more here than in most places since a lot of the appeal was cheaper access to free Stratagems compared to the regular flavour.
  • Neurotyrant: +10pts. They’re good, and you potentially need to lean more on Zoanthropes with one for anti-tank, so you still take it, but again there’s a small tax.

While not increased in points (except for the third one), it’s worth saying that Exocrines and (especially) rupture cannon Tyrannofexes really hate new Cover - they have enough AP that they were usually bouncing off invulns before, and don’t have access to full or semi-full re-rolls like similar gun platforms in other armies. Tyranids have also historically been a faction where people take lots of three-ofs, and there are quite a lot of 10pts steppers at three units, so you may need to revise that plan.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Invasion Fleet - Take and Hold
  • Subterranean Assault - Disruption
2 DP Detachments
  • Crusher Stampede - Purge the Foe
  • Assimilation Swarm - Priority Assets
  • Synaptic Nexus - Disruption
  • Unending Swarm - Take and Hold
  • Vanguard Onslaught - Recon
1 DP Detachments
  • Ambush Predators - Disruption
  • Talons of the Norn Queen - Take and Hold
  • Warrior Bioform Onslaught - Take and Hold

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Wings: Tyranids seem fine - they’ve been doing OK in late 10th, and their points treatment is relatively light compared to some of their competitors (and their Army Rule is a lot better now). The biggest challenges for them are that their go-to tank hunter (the Tyrannofex) becomes high variance in 11th, and also that the gap in quality between their 2DP and 3DP detachments is fairly large, and neither of their 3DP ones gets Purge or Recon. I think they do have a couple of sweet combo builds to try out - Vanguard/Ambush Predators on Recon seems lit, and I think Assimilation/Talons on Take and Hold seems very plausible. There’s plenty to work with here, but you’re definitely going to look at Subterranean being handed Disruption while Mont’ka gets to vibe on Purge and shed some tears.

Wrap Up
