Everything You Need to Know About Our Grand Concert in Umamusume: Pretty Derby

July 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With July coming to a close, that means that it is time for the next scenario in Umamusume: Pretty Derby to hit the stage: Brighter Together, Our Grand Concert is coming on July 22nd! But what does Our Grand Concert change, and how can you best take advantage of it? Never fear, because I’ve got all the information you need to get started with this new scenario and the big changes it makes to previous scenario expectations. We’ll also talk about the state of the game going in to the new scenario, and the upcoming Pal card that coincides with the scenario and if you want it (yes) and how many copies you want. If you’re just getting in to Umamusume or returning after an absence, you’ve picked a great time to do so, as the dreaded Trackblazer/MANT era has come to a close in Global and the state of the game really only goes up from here!

Brighter Together: A New Future

Let’s talk about the new scenario itself and what you can expect from it. Perhaps one of the best things I can tell you off the bat is that the scenario is roughly equal to the length of URA Finale and Unity Cup, rather than Trackblazer’s grueling 1 hour (at least) career runs. This means that for all intents and purposes, Our Grand Concert is your best shot at training new aces for Champions Meetings and Team Trials, and that there’s likely little reason to run the other career modes for now (although a bit more on that later). There is something to be said about running Trackblazer solely for parents, but that really is a personal choice and best left to you to decide if you want to try that; the biggest reason is the racing bonuses and potential inherits that can provide due to Trackblazer’s heavy racing focus.

Our Grand Concert also brings back the more individualistic trainee events that Trackblazer got rid of. This means that specific trainees that are looking for specific events (like Silence Suzuka to get Runaway) are going to be able to train in the new Scenario and not have to sacrifice stats for events, like Trackblazer. It is worth mentioning that this means that trainees with more difficult scenarios, like Mejiro Ardan or Narita Taishin, ALSO have their negative events back, so prepare to have that variance return to your runs that can have some pretty devastating effects if you’ve forgotten how they can impact your run!

As with all new scenarios, Our Grand Concert has a unique, scenario specific event: Concert performances. Much like Unity Cup’s team races, every 6 months of a Grand Concert run will have your trainee compete in a Performance, which has its own set of Performance Points, Songs, and Techniques that you’ll need to learn in order to master the mode effectively. This addition has a few quirks that can be a little confusing at first, but the biggest thing to master is that you are looking to hit specific thresholds of Support Trainings to obtain points in the Performance categories: Dance, Vocals, Passion, Composure, and Visuals. When you use these to learn Songs, you get stat bonuses and career buffs, and Techniques grant bonuses, but not stats. This is somewhat similar to the items you’d get in Trackblazer, although there are some vast differences and Grand Concert is far simpler and less inventory management oriented.

Finally, the biggest thing to know and the reason why you’re going to want to train your upcoming race aces in Grand Concert is that the mode raises the cap of stats beyond what is available even in the July update version of Unity Cup: Speed goes to 1600, Stamina to 1300, Power to 1300, Guts to 1500, and Wit to 1300. Although Trackblazer has superior Stamina (at 1900) and URA has a flat 1400, the biggest thing to pay attention to here is the 1600 Speed. Speed is going to be the most important determining factor when it comes to Champions Meeting trainees, so if you’re training in another scenario where you are going to be 200 to 300 max Speed below other people, you’re going to lose regardless of how well you’re doing in other areas.

Say Hello to Light Hello

Much like Riko, expect to see and hear about Light Hello for the next 3-4 months.

Much like Unity Cup’s ever present Riko Kashimoto, Grand Concert has a new Pal card: Light Hello, the SSR Pal card that is tied to Grand Concert. Much like Riko’s cards, Light Hello provides scenario specific buffs to Grand Concert that makes her incredibly valuable for your Support Deck, and will be a card you’re going to likely want to have access to either in your own deck rotation, or by borrowing her from other people. Her biggest benefit comes from how she interacts with the Performance mechanic, providing bonuses that makes the Performance mode easier to tackle with her in your deck. She also provides fairly solid bonuses as a Support Card: She gives 40% event recovery at LB0, 15 Guts, 25 Friendship, Race and Fan Bonuses, Event Effectiveness, 20% Failure Protection and 35% Energy Cost Reduction, making training with her valuable, easier, and her base stats are good enough that even an LB0 version of her is effective.

So how many copies of Light Hello are you going to want? Well, the good news is that you don’t need to MLB her, but the bad news is you’ll still probably want an LB1 or ideally an LB2 copy of her. However, keep in mind that you could always borrow her as well, if you have the rest of the cards you need in your deck to compensate for not having her yourself. If do you happen to fall in love with Light Hello and want to MLB her, she does provide Speed bonuses starting at LB3, but 15 to 30 Speed stats is probably not worth the toll its going to cost you in terms of carats.

She also comes with a unique skill, called “See Ya Later!”, which is the Gold version of “Playtime’s Over”, a very useful skill for almost all trainees across running styles; the only other way to access “See Ya Later!” is from Mejiro Palmer’s Potential Levels, so unless you are training Mejiro Palmer herself, Light Hello is the only way to get access to this skill and one you are going to likely see in a lot of CM builds once the update drops. If you do somehow happen to not luck into a SSR copy of Light Hello and also can’t borrow one (unlikely, frankly, but hey you never know!) there IS a R version of the card. It does have some downsides in comparison.

Other Meta Support Cards: Goo Goo Babies Forever

As with most meta shifts, there is also the usual question about what cards to look out for and what cards you’ll be using. Perhaps one of the nice things about Grand Concert is that most of the cards that are considered highly meta are cards that are already in the game. The newest card that you’ll likely see is the new Agnes Tachyon SSR Speed card, which comes on the same banner as Light Hello. Otherwise, you’re likely going to want to have (or look out for) the usual staples: Kitasan Black remains valuable, as does the new Summer Maruzensky card. Fine Motion and Mr. C.B. are top tier Wit cards, providing much of the same benefits as they have before.

A new meta choice, Agnes Tachyon's speed card is VERY good.

The reasoning for this is that Grand Concert puts the focus back on training over racing, which means that, sadly, almost all of the cards that were valuable for Trackblazer are now second best or niche picks for skills over other benefits; Grand Concert really doesn’t want you racing as much as Trackblazer did, and really doesn’t care to have you race more than your career likely calls for (except for specific events, like Silence Suzuka’s Runaway trigger), so these cards are important because they provide a lot of training bonuses.

¿Jugamos al goo goo babies?

The final card on this list is the ever present Super Creek, for the same reason she has been meta since release and will remain meta for a long time: the recovery skill Swinging Maestro. There is still no better Gold Recovery skill in the game available to every position, and while there are positional gold recoveries that are good, Swinging Maestro remains THE recovery skill. If you’re training Medium or Long distance trainees, you are very likely going to want Super Creek in your deck for Swinging Maestro, and at this point in the game’s lifecycle, you should be looking for LB3 or higher Super Creeks to get the best possible benefit from her card bonuses, as her lack of starting friendship can make her hard to use. Generally, Support Decks in Grand Concert are going to be Three Speed, Two Wit, and Light Hello, although you could always sub out a Speed card for a card with needed skills or events you want to experience, OR 2 Speed, 2 Wit, 1 Stamina (Super Creek) and Light Hello for trainees that need recovery skills.

Prepare to Perform!

Hopefully this primer has helped give you some idea of what to expect from Brighter Together, Our Grand Future when it comes out on the 22nd. Next week, we’ll do a more in-depth step by step discussion of how to tackle the scenario to get the best possible trainee you can get out of it, but for now go ahead and give Grand Concert a try on your own with the information you’e got from here!
