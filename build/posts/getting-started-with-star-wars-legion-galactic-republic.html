Getting Started with Star Wars Legion: Galactic Republic

July 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone, to another episode in our Star Wars Legion series, where we cover the fundamentals of the game! This time, we're taking a look at one of the core factions of the game, the Galactic Republic. So grab your blaster or don your Jedi robes, it's time to save the galaxy.

Thank you to Asmodee for sponsoring a series of articles on Star Wars: Legion.

What Is the Galactic Republic?

The Galactic Republic maintains order across a vast collection of star systems ranging from the Core Worlds to the Outer Rim Territories. These systems enjoyed many millennia of peace and prosperity while under the governance of the Galactic Senate. However, a schism between the systems grew, which led to the creation of the Separatist Alliance. Now, the Galactic Republic must deploy its clone army in order to quell the Separatist uprising and restore order to the Republic.

Courtesy of AMG

Essential Media

The Prequel Trilogies (Episodes I-III): Whilst focused primarily on the discovery and rise of Anakin Skywalker, the prequel trilogies offer some insight into the Galactic Republic, its politics, and the Jedi Order's position within it as its de facto military/peacekeeping force. As the second movie of the trilogy unfolds, we see the introduction of the Clone Troopers, who form the backbone of the new Galactic Republic, both by the end of that movie and the faction we discuss here. 

The Clone Wars: The Clone Wars series is a seven-season TV series, which spans the 3 years long “Clone Wars”, with the Galactic Republic and Separatist forces battling across the galaxy for supremacy. Set primarily between Episodes II and III (with the final seasons venturing a little further past that), the series delves into the Jedi Order as a military unit, the Clone Troopers, and their relationship. We not only get to see more of Anakin, Yoda, and Obi-Wan, but we also get to explore more of the lesser-known Jedi (Plo Koon, my beloved). It’s not only the Jedi, however, that get the limelight, but many of the Clone Trooper squads, Battalions, and individual members, with the latter focusing on the individuality of each trooper (again, Plo Koon, my beloved). The relationships between each Jedi and their Clone Troopers give an incredible amount of sombre depth to the events we see at the execution of Order 66, and how, despite individuality, the Clone Troopers (for the most part) execute the order with complete disregard for the friendship they have built with their respective Jedi generals. 

How Do They Play? 

At its core, the Galactic Republic plays a more elite army playstyle, relying on team coordination, reliable save rolls, and strong Commander and Operatives to ensure victory. Team Coordination is achieved through the Clone Trooper rule. Clone troopers may spend an Aim, Surge or Dodge token that currently belongs to another Clone Trooper unit, as long as it is within 2 and within Line of Sight. This allows you to share tokens among your Clone Trooper units as long as they are close enough. Ensuring your Clones don’t go down too quickly, all Clone Trooper units have Red save dice as a base. Throw in the aforementioned token sharing, and you’ve got some tanky units that are hard for your opponent to take down. Lastly, your Commanders, Operatives and even your Command Cards offer not only powerful single abilities, but also help generate tokens that you can then share amongst your Troopers.

General Kenobi. Credit: Rich Nutter

Strengths

  • Synergy: The quintessential ability of the Republic is its use of tokens from nearby Clone Trooper units. Couple this with token-generating cards and abilities from Commanders or Operatives like Padme, and even the Troopers generating tokens through their own actions,  and you’ve got yourself a full-stack synergy setup. 
  • Survivability: Red by thy colour, and by the Force is it good to have. With the exception of a couple of vehicles, all of your Clone Trooper and Jedi units are saving on red dice, which is just such a reliable way to ensure you can survive incoming hits. 
  • Force Users: Do I really need to explain this one? I mean, it kinda speaks for itself, right? Regardless, the Republic has access to a wide array of Jedi Commanders and Operatives. Anakin, Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Ashoka are currently available, with more of the Jedi Council already announced for Summer 2026 (Plo Koon, my beloved). And that is just the named characters! With a wide array of Jedi to choose from, and a chance to create your own customisable Jedi Commander or Operative, there are plenty of options to fit your particular playstyle. Anakin is, of course, an offensive powerhouse, with 5 Red Attack dice, with Impact 3 and Pierce 3, whereas Ob-Wan sits on the opposite side, with defensive capabilities thanks to Soresu Mastery and Guardian. 
  • Reliable Firepower: With the ability to transfer surge and aim tokens, you can really push an efficient gunline and dish out consistent damage when things work out. It’s not as effective as something like an Empire gunline, but you can get pretty close.

Weaknesses

  • Low Activations: Begun the activation wars have, and it’s not looking good for GAR. With elite token-sharing units comes a price, and the price is, well, a high price for your units, and thus low activations. In the current meta, increasing the number of units in your army is generally the way to go, as you can activate your less valuable units first to bait out your opponent and to have more board presence and secure objectives. 
  • Attrition / Keyword vulnerabilities: With an elite army comes issues of attrition. There’s only so long your Red Save Dice will keep you around. Couple this with specialised keywords like Pierce and High Velocity.
  • Troopers and Jedi or bust: Whilst I’ve mostly spoken of Clone Troopers and Jedi, there are other units with the GAR roster that you haven’t touched upon yet, namely the Wookies. Now, I’m not saying there is no viability at all with taking Wookies, as there is a separate Battle Force dedicated to fielding them, but you are missing out on possible Clone Trooper units to spread the token-sharing love if you do decide to take some. The more non-Clone units you take, the less powerful your synergies become. 
Phase Two Clone Troopers Credit: LunarNight

How Do I Get Into Them? 

As with all the factions, there is a starter box that contains a bunch of models, dice, tokens, and measuring sticks, all at a reduced price compared to buying them individually. With that in mind, the GAR starter box contains the following model-wise:

  • 1 Obi-Wan Kenobi
  • 1 Jedi Guardian 
  • 22 Clone Troopers
  • 7 ARC Troopers
  • 7 ARF Troopers
Credit: Atomic Mass Games

The great thing about this box, as with the others, is that there are enough models to build a 600-point list for Recon mode, so you can get to experiencing the game right away, without having to buy any more models. 

We covered a Starterset list for GAR, as well as the other factions, in a previous article here, along with a quick overview of how the list looks to perform.

What Else Do They Have? 

Beyond the starter box, the Republic has a lot on offer. Starting off with the various Clone Trooper units, you have units like the Clone Marksmen, Clone Commandos, and specialist Clone Operative units like Rex, Commander Cody, or even the Bad Batch themselves. For Force Users, there is a plethora of options. Anakin and Ashoka make a great power pair to run, along with the aforementioned Rex. Yoda is a fantastic Force User who brings a lot to the table on his profile alone. 

One thing I’ve yet to mention is the Republic vehicle roster, and let me say, we’ve got some great picks. The Republic AT-RT is a great vehicle to bring along, not only to keep your allies safe when hidden behind it, but also because its Flamethrower upgrade can make short work of enemy units. The Infantry Support Platform is a nice vehicle for its point cost that can do some work, and my personal favourite is the Saber-Class Tank, which brings a hell of a lot of firepower within a high-armoured frame and works amazingly well in a 212th Battalion Battle Force. Speaking of Battle Forces…

Battle Forces

Before I show off another list that expands upon the starter list, we need to talk about Battle Forces. Battle Forces are ways to change and mix up how you play the base faction. Think of them as sub-factions or variants that provide pros and cons compared to the vanilla faction experience. AS of now, there are 3 Battle Forces available for the Republic: 

212th Attack Battalion: The 212th Attack Battalion is the Battalion led by Obi-Wan Kenobi, known for its orange armour. The main special rule for the Battle Force is that your non-Commander and non-Operative vehicle units can use tokens from nearby non-Commander and non-Operative vehicles or Clone Troopers, in the exact same way as your standard Token sharing ability. Basically, your vehicles can now participate in your token sharing! However, do keep in mind that your infantry Clone Troopers cannot use tokens from your vehicles. 

When it comes to army-building restrictions, the important ones are that only Obi-Wan and Commander Cody can be your commanders, and that the Jedi Knight is your only Operative. Another major change here is that you may not take any ARC Troopers, which are a major unit in your roster. This is quite the downside, but with Commander Cody on your roster, you can push your vehicle and Trooper units just a bit further. As this is the vehicle Battlefore, you are required to take at least one Heavy unit. 

There are other upgrade changes, which restrict who and what you can upgrade your units with. 

501st Legion: The iconic 501st Legion was led by Anakin Skywalker during the Clone Wars and served as the personal force used to purge the Jedi from the Jedi Temple on Coruscant. On the table, there are no special rules for using this Battle Force, but you do get some important list-building changes. For the major changes, only Anakin, Ashoka, Captain Rex and a regular Clone Commander can be your Commanders, and no mandatory operatives must be taken, but you can only include 1-2 Commander AND Operative units, with at least 1 Commander. You must take at least 1-4 Corps, Special Forces and Support units, but you may take Arc Troopers in the latter two, which is fantastic. 

Wookie Defenders: For fans of our big, hairy friends, there is the Wookie Defenders Battl Force, which simulates the Republic army during the Battle of Kashyyyk. Within this Battle Force, you Wookie Warriors (Noble Fighters) count as Corps for the purposes of List Building and Battle Cards (very nice!), and you must include at least 3 Wookie Trooper Units. Lastly, as a small bonus for your Wookie Troopers, each round, they may make a Speed-1 movement should they have lost at least a single model. 

When it comes to army composition, you may take only Yoda, Chewbacca, or a Wookiee Chieftain as your Commanders, and a Jedi Knight as your Operative. For Corps, you can take up to 2 Clone Trooper Infantry units (since you need a minimum of 3 Corps units, you will need to bring some Wookie Noble Fighters).

Republic BARC Speeder, in 501st Legion colours. Credit: Rich Nutter

Expanding the Starter Set

After you have tried Bair’s handy starter list and you have enjoyed it, you’ll want to expand upon it, whilst still retaining as much of the starter list as possible, so you can start your 1000-point game with purchasing as few models as possible:

    • --DC-15x ARC Trooper Sniper (26)

So for this list to work, you need to purchase Commander Cody, an AT-RT and a Sabre-Tank. The first two are relatively easy to acquire and easier on the wallet, with the tank being a more pricey purchase. 

The list itself focuses on the 212th Battalion without technically being the 212th Battalion. You can switch to that Battle Force if you would like, but you would need to remove the ARCs and perhaps add another vehicle (an AT-RT or an Infantry Support Platform could work here) to replace them. 

Final Thoughts

One particular aspect of the Republic I discovered during my own journey into the faction is just how varied it can be. With a big selection of Force Users and Clone Trooper units, you can really customise a force that suits your playstyle perfectly. There’s a good, solid thematic theme with the Battle Forces, without sacrificing competitive viability (at the end of the day, balance is ever-flowing, after all). 

How does the future look for the republic? Well, as I mentioned, the Jedi Council releases later in the summer, with 4 new Jedi Models added to the Republic Roster. With that, there is the potential for new Battle Forces (please, for the love of Republic, AMG, make a Wolf Pack Battle Force!).
