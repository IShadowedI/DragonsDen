TheChirurgeon's Road Through 2026, Part 38: Days 1-2 of Challenger's Cup

September 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I was doing some final prep for Salt Lake City. And by "final prep" i mean "playing a lot of HeroQuest," though I did get in one extremely important practice game against my friend Andrew Corban on Labor Day. Anyways, this week I'm talking about the 2026 Challengers Cup. This is ostensibly four days of Warhammer - two travel days and three days of games - so I'm going to talk about Thursday and Friday today and then tomorrow I'll be back with an extra instalment to talk about days two and three of the event and the trip back. This will hopefully let me get a bit more "travel diary" about things and let some of the prose breathe. or something.

Anyways, here's ten thousand words about my Warhammer vacation with the boys.

Thursday: The Boys Return to Town

There are a limited number of direct flights from IAH to SLC every day, so I had to leave around midday and fly back Monday morning. That effectively turns this into a five-day event, which is a lot, especially if you aren't blessed with the boon of working from home and largely being able to just work from the airport and/or plane.

Also pictured (left to right): Erik Nelson, T Chambliss, Andrew Corban, and David Miracle

As luck would have it, I was on the same flight to Salt Lake City as Sanctum, the Houston team of local degenerates which makes up a large portion of my regular play group. We sat in the terminal and talked for a while, grabbing beers until it was time to board. Then our flight was delayed for mechanical reasons and we were deplaned. Then we were delayed due to lightning strikes - the tarmac shuts down when there's visible lightning in the area to protect workers - for another hour or so before we finally set out.

Once I arrived in Salt Lake City, I just had to wait for James "Boon" Kelling to pick me up - he'd rented a car. We had a great location for this event: 15 minutes from the airport, 15 minutes from the venue, which meant we could get away with a single car and make round trips to the venue/airport and back. Speaking of which...

The Team House

The house itself was a large house meant for ski trips. Whenever you do these 8-man team events, you always have two big challenges:

  1. The car. Unless you rent a bus or full size van, nothing is going to seat eight of you, especially once you start factoring in the space needed for armies and miniature cases. This usually means you have to rent two cars. Or do what we did and make two trips every time you go somewhere.
  2. The house. Finding a house which can sleep eight people who don't want to share a bed is nearly impossible. Even with big houses, you're usually talking four or five bedrooms at most, and maybe a couch or two. Which usually means someone has to sleep on the floor unless you have a lot of couches and/or fold-out beds.

When I book a house for something like this I'm usually looking for 5 bedrooms and 3 couches, or a fold-out bed or two. I also tend to focus on getting a decent kitchen and making sure we have a grill at the house. The grill makes cooking meals a lot easier, and one of the things I emphasize at teams events is doing big family-style dinners with cooked meals. We eat healthier, we eat cheaper, and we eat as a team.

This particular house had a solid grill and a great back patio with some truly amazing views. The landscape out in Salt Lake City is absolutely something, and it's a bit surreal when you're walking around and surrounded by mountains. On our way back to the house we stopped at the grocery store to do our preliminary grocery run. This is one of my favorite parts of any team or group vacation: I love planning meals and doing the grocery run. I feel like the quartermaster whenever I hit the grocery store on that initial run, sending runners to different aisles, investigating meat and picking fruit, doing it in absurd quantities - MY BOYS GOTTA EAT.

Dinner our first night was burgers and dogs. I biffed this a bit, in that Chase is a vegetarian and I didn't account for that, but he made do with the large variety of snacks, fruits, and vegetables on the first night, then on the second night we picked up some quick substitutions for him to ensure we had proper coverage. Burgers and dogs are usually my night 1 go-to; they're quick and easy, just do the burgs about 7 minutes per side on the grill with some salt and pepper, maybe some Montreal steak seasoning and worcestershire sauce if you wanna get fancy, and heat the dogs while you heat up some chili and cheese. Flip the burgers once.

We waited up for our final team member, Wallace, to arrive, and had a proper reunion chat and some beer before calling it a night. We spend some time on the porch and talk about lists, look at our round 1 pairing, and talk theory. As it turns out, Chase is a sicko who loves to do pairings, which is GREAT, because I hate doing pairings. I'm purely a vibes guy. And so is Chase, actually - he's just way better at it than I am. I end up crashing around midnight, with a dessert which consisted of "a fistful of pepto bismol."

Meet the Team

The Tabletop Battles team is not a traditional team for this kind of event; most of these teams are located near each other, or have players who can regularly practice. Our team is spread out all over, and no two players in our group have the ability to regularly play in person. This means we have zero hours of practice as a group, and have to largely rely on player skill to get through our matchups. the good news is, we're all at least decent players, and some of us are very good. Ok, it's not an ideal list. The idea here is that I'm taking Warpmeld Pact purely to get Purge the Foe while leaving space for the Ritual of Regeneration Detachment, which is where the real value comes from. Being able to bring back dead Terminators and Rubrics is huge, and the Stratagem to essentially grenade a unit you're in combat with is massive for being able to survive combats against elite infantry and keep your units around. It's also crucial when you don't have a fall back and shoot/fight stratagem.

Other key tricks in this Detachment pair:

  • The Tzaangor Shaman with Eruption of Vitality can get back up on death - and doesn't count as a new unit when he does so. This makes him great for being my Beacon unit, since he can die, get back up, and score the secondary. I can also use the Gift of Change Stratagem on him when he dies to make a Chaos Spawn, then he can get back up, and make another Chaos Spawn when he dies the second time. Which is hilarious.
  • The two Scarab bricks can dish out a ton of hurt but do tend to but up against each other as only one can really use the re-rolls/AP buffs at a time. That said, the redundancy is important.
  • The Forgefiends do good damage and support the Scarabs - the idea is to use them to give enemy units -2 to hit and -1 to wound a unit of Scarabs, keeping them on the table.
  • The Mutalith Vortex beast can advance and charge or advance and shoot with the Touched by Tzeentch Stratagem.
  • The Tzaangors can go back into reserves, then deep strike 6" away from enemy units using the Twisted Mirage Stratagem, which forces an opponent to screen me out at 6" late game or let me sneak in to score free points.

It's not amazing. It's barely functional. But it scores for killing things, and it's good at killing things. It's got decent movement, and some nasty board control overwatch. This is not a 20-0 Defender list; I barely care about the terrain layout. This is a 10-10 list designed to win close games or keep things manageable against a wide variety of lists. I'm going to be the attacker this time, and getting thrown on grenades with a hope of keeping things close.

I pair into John Kilcommons, running Lions of the Emperor Custodes. This list will more or less be in every team we play and I'm not the ideal partner for it; it runs three Terminator bricks to my two and that makes it hard for me to get the upper hand unless I'm going second.

Unfortunately, I'm going first. So my goal is to play very cagey and not let him get off any turn 1 charges with his army. I need to stay out of melee as long as possible. I score easy Display of Might turn 1 and work on staying ahead on primary all game but it's hard to do do that for most Purge missions these days, especially going first.

So very little happens the first two turns, then we start mixing it up. I end up dropping in the middle and controlling the center of the board for most of the game, but the Allarus are a big problem whenever they collide with my units. The big money moment in this game comes when I Overwatch John's Shield Captain to death with a lucky shot from my Forgefiend, and then he fails to get back up on death, buying me a huge swing in win probability.

Unfortunately while I stay ahead a few points on primary most of the game, John just outdraws me on secondaries, especially on rounds 3-4. I'm ahead going into the final turn but John draws what he needs to pull out the win with Cleanse for 2, giving him an 83-82 win. I kept this one close in a way that makes me pretty happy, but I'd have liked to score 11 or 12.

Those extra points would have helped, because we'd end up in a close tie with their team, pulling an 83-77 draw; two more points and we'd have had the W. Still, we're not upset about starting with a draw - we're undefeated, after all.

John was a fun opponent and we played a tight game with some crazy swings in both directions. I enjoyed it quite a bit and we had a good time. Great opponent.

Lunch is a mad dash to the food trucks outside. The lines are long and the sun is brutal. I decide to avoid sitting in line by ordering via QR code from the burrito truck. They run out of food and I wait a full hour before going to them to cancel my order. At that point I go to the next truck and ask for the fastest thing they have, which turns out to be birria potstickers.

They were delicious, and sorely needed at this point. I was flagging after a long day, and those dumplings saved my life.

The heavy bolters are bad news for my 2W units, and to make matters worse, Trevor is on Priority Assets, which is a rough matchup for Purge, especially if you go second. I need to maintain control of the center objectives to keep Trevor from repeatedly scoring for his dropped markers.

I can't afford to play super cagey on this one; I need to hold the middle to prevent scoring. Things start off OK for me, but I make a big blunder: Trevor comes out quickly early to try and kill my Beacon-holding Tzaangor Shaman. My plan: Let him die, turn him into a spawn, then use the spawn to block a charge into the Shaman. I forgot of course that the Spawn has a 5+ Feel No Pain however, and he gets shot to death - barely, to the wound - by the sisters who were in the Immolator. As a result, Trevor can charge and tank shock the Shaman, and when he does he spikes the dice and gets 5 wounds off, killing the Shaman.

That was a tough mistake on my end, and one I won't make again. Trevor spends most of the game feeding me units piecemeal, but is able to break things open after I have a disastrous melee turn against his Sacresants. The Scarabs charged in, dropped 21 attacks with swords, connected with exactly five of them, and ended up killing only two models. They then murdered half the unit, and that was a backbreaker I couldn't afford.

Trevor's Sacresants stayed untouchable most of the game, constantly crushing their saves. Trevor also got off another two critical tank shocks, doing 5 damage each time on 10 dice. Going first was a brutal punishment here, ensuring I'd also get bodied on the final turn for some additional points after I failed to kill a vehicle or Ephrael Stern on the final turn, missing out out some crucial points. If I'd had those, this could have been a close game, but as it was, I ended up losing 6-14. Not my favorite game by outcome, but Trevor was a great opponent and we had a good time.

The good news is that we tied again, 82-78. At this point we were pretty happy with our UNDEFEATED result, and joking about going 0-0-6 for the event; the ultimate "friendmaker" score.

We do our two trips back and grab a few missing grocery items this time around. Friday night is TACO NIGHT at the house. We cook up about 3 lbs of hamburger with taco seasoning, then add shredded cheese, lettuce, diced tomatoes, sour cream, Spanish rice, and refried beans to the mix, plus a good mix of corn tortillas and hard shells. To top things off, I made some of my extremely creamy guacamole. The general recipe here is:

  • 6 avocados
  • 1 diced tomato
  • half a clove of finely chopped garlic
  • 1/3 a red onion, small diced
  • salt
  • pepper
  • lime juice
  • a fistful of cilantro
  • paprika
  • cumin

I tend to err on the creamier side with my guac, and it turned out really well. We certainly finished the night with none left, which is always a good sign. Tacos are a great pick for a dinner at these things; you can buy most of the ingredients pre-made, and they allow players to kind of build however they want, making it easy to just put out ingredients and let people go to town. Because we're finishing kind of late even with two-round days, dinner can run late. So you ideally want meals which don't take a long time to cook.

The spread itself was solid:

It was a crazy amount of taco meat, but the good news is we had plenty left over for breakfast tacos the next morning. I think I had something like eight tacos and we had plenty of food left after that. Overall a very solid night and a great cap to a great first day for the event. We'd hang out and drink beer and talk for a while, recap the day, and talk about tomorrow's opponents for a while. I crashed around midnight.

Final Thoughts

OK, that wraps up days 1 and 2. Tomorrow I'll be back with the second half of this report. recapping days 2 and 3 of the event and our final results. See you then.
