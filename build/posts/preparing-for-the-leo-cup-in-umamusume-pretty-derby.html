Preparing for the Leo Cup in Umamusume: Pretty Derby

July 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The first Champions Meeting following the huge surprise July patch is here: Leo Cup’s Rerun! If you took the chance to get back into the game following all of the big changes to Unity Cup and Ura Finale, Leo Cup might be a surprisingly difficult bar to clear if you’re looking to prepare for competition suddenly, but with a bit of planning and a few good attempts under your belt, you can very likely make an excellent set of competitors to challenge the other Umamusume out on the turf!

If you missed the big July news, we wrote about it last week, and I highly suggest you check it out before attempting to train anyone for the Leo Cup, as the competitors you are likely to see out on the turf are going to be from those who have been taking advantage of the changes, or at least very keenly aware of them. With that said, let’s get on with what you need to know to take on Leo Cup 2026!

Leo Cup 2026: A Sprint to the Finish

The Leo Cup rerun brings us to one of the least common types of Champions Meeting types: Sprint, which means that the competitor pool is already pretty small and very much one that will be dialed in to take advantage of the new changes to the game. Leo Cup is a 1200m Sprint run from the Right Outer Posts during Summer in Sunny Weather on Firm Conditions on Nakayama Turf. Since you may need to start from scratch to get an Ace or even a team put together for this race, let’s go over the very common skills you’ll want to consider tracking down, as Green Skills will be very valuable to the Front Runners in the race especially.

Firm Conditions is still one of the most widely available Green skills in the game, and with the release of Nishino Flower as a trainee/parent option, you have a lot of ways to potentially obtain the skill either through cards or inheritances. Firm Conditions is probably one of the skills you should always shoot for on your trainee when looking at Greens, especially for Front Runners, simply because it is so easily available that you can consider a pretty sure bet. If you’re training a Front Runner, you could even consider trying to snag Firm Course Menace from Narita Top Road’s SSR events. However, a note about this is that Nishino Flower is considered one of the top tier competitors in this competition, so if you’re training her, you’ll essentially have this skill locked in; if you’re not training her, try to inherit it.

Nakayama Racecourse does have a decent array of cards that you can obtain it from, and many of them are pretty strong if you are looking to do either Wit or Guts builds (or both) for your current runs through Unity Cup or otherwise. Your options here are Wit Yukino Bijin or Wit Nakayama Festa, Guts Rice Shower, Yukino Bijin (both versions), Mejiro Palmer, or Special Week, as well as Power or Guts Oguri Cap, Stamina Nakayama Festa, or the infamous Throne card. Throne is a good card to borrow if you didn’t pull it (you didn’t, right?), so you could consider it here.

Right-Handed tends to be a bit of an oddball skill, because it only tends to come on cards you are unlikely to run in a Support Card deck; however, it also happens to be on Wit Fine Motion and Wit Matikanefukitaru, both of which work great for Unity Cup Wit or Wit/Guts builds; your other options are Speed Narita Brian, Stamina Brian or Mihono Bourbon, Guts Special Week or Speed Zenno Rob Roy, and frankly none of those are spectacular, but Speed Brian is free, if that is your only option.

Standard Distance is another fairly common skill, and primarily comes from Nishino Flower’s cards, as well as Kawakami Princess Power, El Condor Pasa Power, Eishin Flash Speed, Bamboo Memory Power, and Ines Fujin Wit. Sadly the cards that provide this aren’t great, and Nishino Flower Power is probably the best card in this pool. Finally, Sunny Days can be obtained from El Condor Pasa Power, Satono Diamond Stamina, Mejiro Dober Wit, and Hishi Akebono Guts; if you’re trying to get numerous Greens, you could look to run El Condor Pasa and target both Standard Distance and Sunny Days for maximum efficiency.

Summer Runner can be found on exactly four cards, which is great because that number actually increased since last time in which it was one, so. Your options are Speed Maruzensky (the current hot card in the meta), Wit Fuji Kiseki and Nakayama Festa, and Guts Sakura Chiyono O. You may also want to consider some other greens this time, particularly Competitive Spirit, as you are very likely to run into teams running trainees in the same positions you are. Comp Spirit can be found on a number of cards: Wit Tokai Teio and Daiwa Scarlet, Guts and Speed Twin Turbo, Speed Shinko Windy, Power Daiwa Scarlet (both versions), Guts Grass Wonder and Guts Mejiro Palmer. If you noticed, there are some cards that repeat here from other skills, and Guts Palmer especially is a great card to consider for your deck here as it also comes with a chance at Front Runner Savvy. If you want FRS but don’t want to run Palmer, you could consider Speed or Guts Silence Suzuka, Power or Guts Smart Falcon or Wit Seiun Sky.

If you’d like to try your luck at a competitor who is not going to run with the pack in the more common positions, you could consider a trainee with Lone Wolf, and Leo Cup in particular historically had a very strong but full field of Front Runners and Pace Chasers. It is a bit of a gamble, because any other competitor having it negates it, but that also means you are negating THEIR Lone Wolf also, so you could even consider it on a trainee who is not your Ace, but instead is acting as someone to help mess with the other competitors; that said, it may only be worth it if you have the extra Skill Points on a Debuffer or third member. You can find it on numerous cards, but the most notable one is that it is available from Riko Kashimoto, who you are very likely considering in some deck rotations now that Unity Cup is back.

Trainees to Consider

At Sprint Distance, the crowd is quite thin when it comes to choices, and there are going to be some pretty noticeable gaps in quality and ease of training when it comes to who you can select. For that reason, Leo Cup is probably one you should focus on training the best possible trainees you have available over engineering one, but you can obviously pursue whatever method you like; this is, sadly, a fairly Free to Play unfriendly race, but you do still have options.

Bakusin! And... no one else for F2P. Sorry.

Free to Play: Sakura Bakushin O. That’s it.

Two Stars: Two Star wise, you have some more freedom and you likely have many of these trainees: Mayano Top Gun, King Halo, Air Groove, Mejiro Ryan, Agnes Tachyon and Grass Wonder can all work in their given spots.

This horse is 2 apples tall and she's going to kill you.

Three Stars: Perhaps unsurprisingly, Valentine Mihono Bourbon once again shines in this particular race, as does Summer Maruzensky and Taiki Shuttle (both version). Nishino Flower is exceptionally strong as a Pace Chaser option here, perhaps one of the strongest trainees in general, and if you aren’t running her, you should try to get her skill on other trainees. Curren Chan, Hishi Akebono, Ballroom Kiseki, Bamboo Memory all work. If you’re looking for Debuffers, consider Autmun Gold City, Cheer Nice Nature, or Valentine Eishin Flash, as well as the old reliable Symboli Rudolf.

There are a lot of other choices you could pursue, but as a Sprint race, these generally mean you will need to engineer sparks and fight against career modes that aren’t exceptionally interested in your trainee running against their intended targets; this can be really frustrating, but if you wanted to, you can perform really well with a Turf Smart Falcon or Sprint Sparked Seiun Sky or Silence Suzuka, as well as base Oguri Cap.

Stats and Skills

Sprint Races are kind of uncommon for Champions Meetings, and most of that is because they are one of the biggest extremes when it comes to training; you want almost nothing other than Speed, Power, and Wit, to the point that entirely ignoring Stamina is completely correct when it comes to designing your training strategy and support deck. This also means that you likely want to avoid running Stamina Cards at all in your deck, and that a Wit/Guts or even just completely Wit deck is very likely a way to get where you want to be. The general target stats for a top tier competitor in Leo Cup is 1300 Speed, 400 Stamina, 1300 Power, 600~ Guts, and 1000 Wit, with more Wit always being better if possible. If You’re trying Open instead of Graded, well, bad-ish news: 1200 Speed, 300 Stamina, 1000 Power, 500 Guts and 800 Wit are what you’re looking at there, meaning that there’s not that big of a gape between the two of them and that it might be easier to just shoot for Graded League trainees overall.

Consider borrowing this card in your decks from people who were, uh, unfortunate enough to MLB it.

Sprint races don’t really care about Stamina or Recovery skills, primarily because the races are so short that they don’t tend to have a lot of time to activate. Guts also tends to fill the void of Stamina here, activating as the back-up stamina that it is meant to be, and also providing slightly better returns if that’s where your extra points end up. You’ll also not really be looking for any recovery skills at all in your trainee’s profile, so try to avoid getting them and spending your points on other skills instead. Sprint Skills are always good here, and depending on what position you’re racing at, you’ll want to look to get Angling and Scheming on your Front Runner, Budding Blossom and Shooting for Victory on your Pace Chaser (as well as Triumphant Pulse, if possible), Let’s Pump Some Iron and Triumphant Pulse on your Late Surger or End Closer.

Front Runners are absolutely going to want Groundwork to function, so don’t forget that they need 3 Skills to activate early; while this is usually accomplished through Green Skills, you can try Early Lead and Focus also.

Support Deck Options: Unity or Trackblazer?

Fundamentally, Unity Cup is now a superior method of training your Umas until the next Scenario hits; the cap on Stats being raised and the ease of training in Unity alone makes it the better choice, but depending on what cards you have and your own proclivities, you can make both scenarios work for you if you want to give it a shot. They do tend to require different decks, and historically Trackblazer is what Japanese players were dealing with, meaning most guides you’ll see are using the Japanese guides. For that reason, I’m providing notes on how to make decks for both scenarios.

If you’re running Unity Cup, you’re going to want to focus on the Speed/Wit combos that take advantage of the changes to Unity’s stats and also by focusing on the stat that really matters: Speed. A very successful and easy to run deck here is 3 Speed and 3 Wit cards, although you can opt to change one of the Wit cards out for the Throne card if you have it available from someone to borrow. If you’re training a Pace Chaser, you do want to be mindful that they actually require more stamina than other positions, and something like a Guts cards can be a good way to ensure you get the extra Guts/Stamina you’ll need to have your Pace Chaser survive.

If you’re instead running Trackblazer, your deck is going to look very much like it has been looking for the past few months: Wit, Guts, and Speed are what you’ll be looking to utilize here, going for a 2/2/2 combination. You can use the Throne card as a Wit card replacement here also, if you have it available, otherwise you’re going to want to focus on your best available Speed, Wit, and Guts cards. Admire Vega Guts shines heres, as does Summer Maruzensky, and Wit wise you can do very well with very “cheap” Wit cards like Marvelous Sunday and Agnes Tachyon, if you don’t happen to have cards like Fine Motion or Nice Nature available to you.

A Full Year of Umazing Gameplay!

It really is hard to believe that we’ve had a year of Umamusume: Pretty Derby to talk about here, and even more so that I’ve been able to write about it for you all for all this time! The game is really getting into some exciting territory, as the Trackblazer / MANT lull is coming to an end even faster than Japan, and much of what awaits is a lot more fun and engaging than Trackblazer was; in a few months, we won’t even remember how much of a burnout this game could actually become!
