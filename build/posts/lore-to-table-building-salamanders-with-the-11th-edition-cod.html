Lore to Table: Building Salamanders with the 11th Edition Codex

October 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The new release of the 11th edition Codex: Space Marines gave us a ton of new Detachments to play with, including a mix of generic and chapter-specific options to choose from. In this article series, we're going to be looking at different chapters and how to represent them on the table, talking about different combinations and lore-accurate forces.

In our first article in this series, we talked about the Imperial Fists and how I missed subfactions; that's still true, but now we've taken it upon ourselves here at Tabletop Battles to rectify that iniquity a bit by coming up with playable lists that draw heavily from lore inspiration to really show off the different flavors of each Marine Chapter. This time around we're going to be looking at the Salamanders.

The Salamanders represent both an easier and tougher challenge than you'd expect. The obvious starting point is "just take a lot of flamers and melta weapons, problem solved." But as with all of the chapters in our series, there's more to the army to that, and more that's worth exploring. As with the Imperial Fists, we're going to dig a little into the lore of the chapter and find out what other aspects of the chapter we can build around and where the rules help us close those gaps. And as we build, we'll look at competitive lists for our templates where possible - just because we're building to lore doesn't mean we can't kick some ass.

What Kinds of Armies Do We Want to Build?

The Salamanders hail from the burning world of Nocturne and are "a band of warrior smiths and battle artificers, as adept at crafting the tools of war as they are in using them."

The easiest starting point is flamers and meltaguns. The Salamanders are "the flamers chapter," and their basic Detachment - Forgefather's Seekers - plays into this, albeit indirectly, by making your close-ranged attacks +1 Strength. But they're also the chapter of master artificers, who make ornate pieces of wargear and hone their craft to insane levels. The Salamanders are a plenty versatile army, and just as likely to rely on Tactical Squads and Intercessors as any other chapter. They're also known for being sturdy and self-reliant.

I think that there are really two main forces we want to build here, and we can stretch to a third if we really want to:

  • Flameblade - This is our flamer army, full of flamers, meltas, and heavy flamers designed to burn the world. Salamanders Marines are naturally resistant to heat, and that makes them more likely to use flamers and heat-based weapons to defeat foes.
  • Master Artisans - With this army we're going to represent the chapter as master artisans, working on bringing high-end wargear such as relic blades.
  • Elimination Force - This is our stretch goal; a more balanced army meant to show off that at the end of the day, the Salamanders are still a Codex-compliant chapter and bolters do a fine job killing things inside 12".

This gives us three concepts to build around; a short-ranged force filled with flamers and meltaguns, a force filled with master artisan weapons (and thunder hammers, most likely), and a more tactical force focused on mid-range firepower. The good news is that Codex: Space Marines was very kind to the army's flamer- and melta-armed units; Infernus Marines, Flamestorm Aggressors, and melta Eradicators are all units which are not only playable (especially in Forgefather's Seekers), they're downright solid.

Infernus Squad. Credit: Rockfish

Flameblade

The most obvious option, the Salamanders are known for rocking flamers and melta weapons. The good news is that Infernus Marines are very good in 11th edition, and are still interesting (if a bit less potentially jaw-dropping) with the transition to the new Codex. Pyreblasters gaining [BLAST 1] gives them some very nasty output, and they combo well with Eradicators thanks to the ability to strip cover from targets. Getting +1S here on ranged attacks within 12" is a big deal, and we want to combine the unit with the Immolator Enhancement to maximize our output.

Although a bit worse than their pre-Codex incarnation, Flamestorm Aggressors can also be a solid play here, having [BLAST 2] to make up for the loss of D6+1 shots and now with CLOSE-QUARTERS to dig out of problem spots in addition to having power fists, plus they can push to S6 as well by getting within 9" of their target. Eradicators are also just a solid option, and taking short- and mid-ranged units in Impulsors is a solid competitive play.

This is a 5-0 list created by Bahrunur Bahrunur for the Hunt for Red Ork-tober event which ran on October 10. It's a list running Assault Brethren and Forgefather's Seekers and runs heavy on flamers and meltas, using a mix of Infernus Marines in Impulsors - always a strong play thanks to Firing Deck 7 and the ability to make Shock Disembark moves. There's also a Land Raider Redeemer for full flamer goodness - the Redeemer is one of the game's deadliest overwatch threats, and here it's used to carry Adrax and a unit of Bladeguard Veterans into battle, and likely a unit of Eradicators as well.

The odd men out here are the Vanguard Veterans with Jump Packs - they give the list some important melee punch, but don't fit our theme of "all flame, all the time." If you want an alternative variant of the list, there's always Jason Moskalik's list from the Gamehole Con 40k Event held on October 17, which went 4-1 and opts to attach Vulkan to a unit of Company Heroes while adding Storm Speeder Thunderstrikes for more ranged output.

The Legends Version

For some reason we still don't have points or Legends Datasheets yet for some of the things passing into Legends with the recent Codex release but I don't have a ton of changes I'd make here. The big Salamanders unit in Legends is Bray'arth Ashmantle, who got the Legendary Proxies treatment, making him just a regular Dreadnought. If you're interested in going that route, I'd consider using the Crucible of Champions rules to do something a little more special/custom, but you can also use the rules for the Brutalis Dreadnought to more directly model his actual rules. There you can give him twin fists and multi-meltas instead of heavy bolters, plus a Conversion Field and Rites of Battle.

Salamanders Venerable Dreadnought Bray'arth Ashmantle. Credit: McWhat

Master Artisans

The other major thing the Salamanders are known for? Being master artisans and making high-quality wargear and master-crafted weapons and armor. Historically I tend to associate this with the master-crafted rule, generally either improving your damage or letting you re-roll one hit roll, or with their ability to create better Thunder Hammers. In this case, I think I'd tie their ability as artisans to vehicles. And this list focuses more on ranged damage output while still using Forgefather's Seekers, combining it with Ironstorm Spearhead, where we'll pretend the auto-targeters are actually master-crafted weapons.

This time around we're dropping Adrax but keeping Vulkan - though he's riding solo again. The list triples up on Eradicators running one Bolter unit and two units of Meltas, and at least one of them is riding in an Impulsor to get around more quickly. The other big addition here? A full unit of six Flamestorm Aggressors, riding in a Land Raider Redeemer with Vulkan, ready to immolate anything they come across. This list gets a lot of its anti-vehicle punch from the Gladiator Valiants, each equipped with a twin las-talon and pair of multi-meltas.

Primaris Eradicators - Heavy Melta Rifles. Credit: Rockfish

Elimination Force

The Salamanders are still a Codex-compliant chapter despite their devotion to flame and melta weapons and the odd tendency to do things like "protect human life" and "treat civilians with respect and dignity." They're just as likely to run balanced forces using a wide variety of units as they are to go crazy on flamers. For this last list, we're looking at something a little more balanced, but still with a solid track record.

Jeroen Janssens ran this list to a 3-2 finish at That 6++ GT last weekend, but started 3-0 in the process. It's a list proudly running two Redeemers, each filled with Flamestorm Aggressors, two units of melta Eradicators in Impulsors, Heavy Intercessors, Inceptors, and Scouts. It's heavy on Gravis marines, in part because it's running the Gravis Siege Force instead of Assault Brethren, giving it extra durability against incoming attacks.

Final Thoughts

If you're running Salamanders right now, chances are you're going to run Forgefather's Seekers. It's both thematically appropriate and well, it's just a really good Detachment which encourages you to take some of the army's best units anyways. The Land Raider Redeemer is one of the game's nastiest units, and it remains every bit as nasty now that the flamestorm cannons are 4 shots with [BLAST 2] instead of D6. You're averaging fewer shots, but now you have [DEVASTATING WOUNDS] against smaller targets to make up for it. Overall the Salamanders give you quite a few options for different ways to build, and whether you want to take Impulsors full of flamers or trundling Flamestorm Aggressors, you have options.
