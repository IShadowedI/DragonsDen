[Warmachine] Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms- September 2026

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our monthly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each month our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the month’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

As the weather cools outside the Warmachine meta has heated up to a boiling point! Compared to previous months the actual total number of games has taken a sharp nose dive leaving the actual stats to be a bit muddled but the competitive innovations are on point as new innovations emerge and finalize ahead of what many consider to be the biggest event of the year: WARMACHINE WTC. For some it was time to recede and grind dozens of games in preparation, for others it was a month of getting those last chances to sharpen their skills at a high competitive level to stay in peak condition ahead of the global showdown.

As we start, a quick review of the filters we use for the data pulled here. For this series, we are only looking at ranked tournament games and are excluding online only events.

September Stats

In September we saw 1112 games played at 60 events. The NOVA IGQ and UK Nationals were the highlights of the month and continued to show how a global perspective helps show differing meta trends.

Note- All of the Legacy factions currently unavailable as a part of Warmachine 3D are grouped together in the “Legacy Armies” section, due to their lower number of games played per faction and lack of widespread availability.

Games Played

Compared to August, the number of games played cratered to almost 1/3 as summer came to a close. Between waning weather, kids going back to school, and gearing up for WTC fewer events were played but the general higher quality of those events tell a pretty clear story. The 2025 summer hotness of Old Umbrey and the 2026 summer hotness of Fane of Nyrro dominated the numbers due to a combination of raw faction strength and people wanting to just play with either their finally fully painted toys and their new toys. Normally we would be ringing alarm bells for breaking the 10% danger threshold but when data shrinks to this degree, odd spikes are expected. Outside of those two outliers the numbers generally leveled out as people defaulted to what was familiar and comfortable as for most not traveling to Europe, the final major event of the year after this month is Lock and Load US.

Faction Winrates

Fane of Nyrro along with Wicked Harvest (formally Grymkin in our data) dominated the winrates and while the overall playrate wasn't a concern by itself, the correlation of both the highest playrate AND the highest winrate is absolutely a red flag for balance. As a result, players of both factions should be bracing for very pointed adjustments in January if they haven't already. While factions like Old Umbrey, Shadowflame Shard, Kithguard, and Dark operations operating in the normal high ~50% range, a quirk of less games sampled allow for the 2026 under dog Thornfall Alliance to rocket up to the staggering 57% winrate off of very few games played by excellent pilots. Similar wild shifts between factions is evident all over the data meaning theres little to gleam outside of the absolute extremes. Both house Kallys and Gravediggers stand out for having enough games to draw worth while conclusions. The original representatives of Dusk have found their way back to the light through a sequence of events where skillful players have managed to properly position answers to the current meta. Bandit on the flip side has successfully brought many players back to the Cygnaran trenchers by heavily simplifying in-game options, but players broadly haven't quite had the same time as the pre-80mm die-hards to position the ranged army into a meta that started the year heavily tuned into gunlines.

September Event Breakdown

Credit: Steamforged Games

With a reduction in games played comes a reduction in events covered. That being said its not as if those games are of lesser quality. Both the venerated NOVA Open and UK Nationals showcased some of the best and most cutting edge that Warmachine had to offer. For many these were the events of the year, a culmination of their work with their best factions. For others they were the final crucibles to hone and master themselves in leading to the clash of countries WTC.

IGQ at NOVA

48 Players, 6 Rounds, September 5-7, District of Columbia, USA

https://www.longshanks.org/event/34176/

Credit: NOVA Open

One of the biggest table top events of the year, the health of the NOVA Open is typically a leading indicator for the health of any miniatures game. The more the event allocates its precious space to a given game the more it shows there's a ravenous demand for that game. With more sponsors signing on to the Warmachine side in Centurion Tactics and Gearforged Hobbies, its clear to see other companies are taking notice of our rapidly regrowing hobby.

Two major heavyweights to the tournament scene duked it out at one of the last major US Events of the year and the final major proving ground before WTC (World Teams Championship). This was the final round a brutal gauntlet of games for both players as this finals was bisected by the NOVA schedule with the Teams event the previous day. So bare minimum this was game 9 for both of them over 3 back to back to back days of playing. Graciously they were both able to share their thoughts going into the matchup:

Akers: Knowing the matchup well, Mother was the objectively correct choice into the Classic Old Witch set up. The scenario and board did not impede the plan in any way but at best it was going to be a 50/50. Typically this showdown comes down to either leveraging the army to clear the house or clear the other scoring models. These are plans that heavily depend on the opponent. The main strength of Convergence is the flexibility in list building but also the unparalleled flexibility in play, allowing him to pivot and move his primary strategy turn over turn due to the new-to-this-year extended range of the induction mechanic and the "Free" nature of powering up the Vectors (Convergence's name for their warjacks).

CDemunn: Not being as familiar with the matchup on a physical practice level as Akers, the choice was still obvious. With the potential for Akers to drop Mother, heretic was simply not the correct drop. Good guns for shooting infantry just rips that list to shreds. He wasn't certain which list Akers would choose but the only truly horrendous result would be that Mother vs Heretic game and nothing about terrain or objective placement swayed his thinking in any way. It would all come down to Old Witch vs the Iron Mother.

Credit: Team Warmachine at NOVA Open

Result: CDemunn Victory - Scenario Win - 13 to 10

First Place: (CDemunn - Grymkin)

Archetype: Witch’s Cottage, Heretic Aggro

Lists: See Showdown

Eeking it out after a failed assasination run by Akers, CDemunn proved definitively that he is a living legend of this game by winning back to back NOVA opens. Like many Grymkin players we have covered the past few months, the Old Witch was mono-listed throughout the event. Per our brief discussion there simply wasn't any perfect predators for Witch that necessitated Heretic’s use. A truly dominant showing for an exceptionally dominant player.

Second Place: (Akers - Convergence of Cyriss)

Archetype: Lucent Brickhouse, Mother Toolbox

Lists: See Showdown

Proving beyond a shadow of a doubt that Convergence is an underrepresented powerhouse, Akers once again shined in this event. The latter half of this year has been a sharpening of his Iron Mother list and this event proved to everyone that he could also play Lucant, the conventional front runner for best Convergence Caster. The list pairing as-is represents the standard sword and shield. Iron Mother is an adaptive sword able to utilize her feat for drastically pumping up the MAT and RAT of her army to laser down certain targets, spells that extend range threat, ways to arc her spells though her cohorts, and personal Servitors that increase damage. Whatever way Akers wanted to scalpel threats he had tools for. Lucant on the other hand is an anti-gun WALL capable of stacking armor to oblivion and dish damage back. Constantly reviving and empowering models to create an endless horde of metal bodies to throw into the wood chipper and dominate the board. Definitely a combo worth eyeing for the rest of the year and into the future 2027 meta.

Despite a very inconsistent year for Storm Legion, Scott proved there is more to the faction than just ramming Strykers down the opponent’s throat. Unfortunately he did not dissuade the idea that Huxley is the head and shoulders best caster in the line up by mono listing him throughout the event. Jamming many efficient tools found in faction, what sets apart this list is the use of Thunderhead over a Super Jr and turning a few Strykers into gun Coursers to bring a second Zephyr and the Black 13th. While the character unit is run by preference in Huxley lists, a second Zephyr is very much a statement in how efficient the Solo is and Huxley can run them in waves thanks to his upkeep Storm Rager which super charges them. I enjoy the Caine list as it makes use of combo-ing Calamity with Constance Blaize helping you play a very dynamic gunline with a relevant melee focus. Most likely this would have seen play into factions like Necrofactorium or Shadowflame Shard where being able to clear annoying work horse units would be premium.

Storm Legion has been in a weird spot this year. Thunderhead looked to be an incredible addition to the faction and at the time of release was considered the most powerful and versatile of the new character 80mm cohorts that defined much of the releases of 2026. After a shaky inconsistent start to its competitive history, this makes the second major event of the month to prove that Thunderhead 2 has new life in Mk 4. This time its paired with Magnus for a second premium damage piece. Far more of a melee focus than we saw at NOVA, nonetheless this list did prove its grit into multiple Shadowflame players including the SFG lead game dev Jamie “Kitchen Table Legend” Perkins. Another way Graham has differentiated himself this month is by actually playing his Calder list on his road to the podium running down Nymera out of Fane of Nero. Macro stats aren’t everything and the swans of Cynar prove they aren't ready for their song just yet.

Orgoth was the standout star of balance back in January for how well it fixed internal balance between the Warcasters. The big standout was Oriax, considered in 2025 to be the worst new caster in Mk4. Being in a top placing pair is quite the achievement even if David’s recorded game with the mysterious male witch was a loss. Tailored for killing infantry spam, Oriax’s Star crossed is excellent into melee matchups especially backed by High Def mercenary options. Along with ARM reduction options in both Oriax’s kit and Anathia’s, the list could crack even the toughest of enemy cohorts. All that said, what rode the pair to victory was of course the standout of 2026 Sea Raiders, the Butcher himself. Multiple cheap Tyrant bullets in the chamber and the classic double Rhok package helps project a prodigious amount of threat in both range and damage while making piece trading uncomfortable for the opponent.

Brineblood Marauders started the year on the back of the dominant Firequill gunline. The mid year update heavily tampered that list but didn't kill it, leaving Firequill still one of the better colossal supporting casters but now in a more balanced build. Even still Zilvinas mono-listed the capable crackshot throughout the event building up a suite of Scenario victories. On one side a gunline on the other his trusty Boomhowler list that has all the makings of a dedicated Anti-gunline build thanks to Ol’ Scuttlebut enabling incredible melee threat for the marauders and Rough Seas protecting them on the advance. With all of the matchups being melee focused, its no wonder she stayed firmly in the back pocket.
