Tabletop Battles Reviews: Cookie Run Braverse - The Dark Enchantress War

August 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Trading card games have been going through a bit of a rennaissance of late, with increased interest in the genre leading to numerous games and properties dipping their toes into the medium. Although some of this comes with the downside of "investors" looking to make money off of cards and creating a barrier to entry for players looking to enter the game, there are numerous other games that have built a good medium between collectability and playability that have helped them carve out a solid following aside format monoliths like Magic: The Gathering and Pokemon. These barriers can be even higher for those looking for games that might appeal to younger demographics, because if they're not interested in Pokemon, there aren't a lot of other all ages friendly games either, but that's one of the reasons why Devsisters Cookie Run Braverse is such an appealing game.

If you're unfamiliar with Cookie Run, the series recently celebrated 17 years since the release of 2009's Cookie Run Ovenbreak, spanning numerous mobile games and other media spinoffs, with perhaps the most popular being 2021's Cookie Run Kingdom, which just celebrated 5 years of live service. Cookie Run as a franchise features anthropomorphic cookies who inhabit various versions of a world called Crispia, going on (mostly) lighthearted adventures that (sometimes) also involve world ending catastrophes and contemplation on the meaning of life and the purpose of living.

Yeah.

Cookie Run is a charming series that has a global following, and there's a good chance you (or someone you know) has likely played or enjoys one of the various games, and that popularity helped inspire Cookie Run Braverse, a TCG pulling from the various Cookie Run games to inspire cards and set themes. The newest set, The Dark Enchantress War, is based on the climatic ending to Cookie Run Kingdom's first 5 years of storylines. Today we'll be taking a look at the new Dark Enchantress Set, as well as whether or not the game might be right for you!

Thanks to Devsisters for providing us with a box of Dark Enchantress War cards for the sake of this review. 

Crisp and Simple

Cookie Run Braverse is not specifically targeted towards children, but the game is very much playable by younger gamers, targeting 6+ as the baselinef or the demographic. I found that this actually made the game very easy to understand and get into, and the cards follow by having somewhat simplistic and easy to follow text and gaming instructions. That isn't to say that you should only consider the game if you're a parent looking for a game for your children to play with you, but basically it should instead be viewed as a game that has a fairly low skill-floor and is very easy to get into and understand. There's certainly some validity to comparing this to Pokemon, and there are a lot of ways in which the games are similar, but Braverse does enough to stand on its own aside from the thematic elements.

Cookie Run's design really clicks once you realize "Evil Buddha Who Tells You Life is Meaningless and Kills You" goes incredibly hard as an antagonist design.

Braverse has a slightly different winning condition, which is that whenever a player's Break Area is filled with Cookies whose levels equal or exceed 10, they have lost. This is a little different from players having discrete life points, like Pokemon or Yu-Gi-Oh!, and also doesn't use deck-as-life mechanics like Pokemon Prize Cards or Bandai games like One Piece or Gundam. In playing through Braverse, this often meant that games had a little bit of time to breathe and a good flow of back and forth for both players, as the win-condition is something that just cannot be achieved in the first few turns, and instead allows players to interact for a bit and the pace is somewhat relaxed.

This also means that the various color identities in Braverse are able to take advantage of establishing strong thematic elements that carry over to gameplay; for example, Black deck cards like to cycle cards through Discarding and drawing, even restoring cards from the Trash, while Green cards love to interact with the Support area, which is where cards are placed to pay for various costs in the game. Despite the fact that the game is meant to be accessible and simple, I found that there's quite a bit of interactive depth and strategy that reminded me a bit of Magic's color-wheel style deck identities.

Flavor to Spare

Cookie Run has always built a strong identity off of the character and whimsy of the franchise, with each Cookie having a very strong identity that is usually tied to their visual representation and in-game personalities; GingerBrave is the ever peppy protagonist, White Lily Cookie is the self-sacrificing type, Mystic Flour Cookie is the always amazing answer to the question of "What if Buddha, But Evil", and Dark Cacao Cookie is #1 Dad. Even if none of this means anything to you as a casual reader who has never encountered the franchise before, looking at the cards helps sell the fact that the Cookie Run franchise has strong identity, with every Cookie being instantly recognizable and having their own unique charm. In Braverse, this also helpfully translates to them playing exactly as you'd expect; sometimes in licensed games, the character's game representation doesn't always seem to coincide to how one would expect them to play.

Cookie Yuri, your honor.

Cookie abilities also play into that, with them doing things you would somewhat expect that character to do; Shadow Milk Cookie, being a deceiver, has a lot of abilities that create shenanigans, while Mystic Flour Cookie, a living representation of apathy, slowly whittles away our opponent's units will to live by draining their HP. Color strategies tend to be easy to understand and help with deck construction, which also is aided by the fact that the cards generally do exactly what they say, making it easy to explain their effects and for players to understand what is happening or can happen in the game.

There are also various other types of cards other than Cookies: Items, Stages, Traps, and FLIP cards, all of which have unique abilities and adds some interaction into the game, something that I very much enjoy; while that may sound kind of obvious, I tend to dislike TCGs in which players do not really interact very much during turns, instead becoming something of a "who drew better" or "who drew their engine" style of game. In Braverse, players have the ability to take advantage of various abilities, and many of them have easy to understand triggers: Some will say "Your Turn", others "Once Per Turn", or in the instance of Trap cards, can only be utilized when you are being attacked, but give you an ability to respond to the attack. FLIP cards reminded me of Gundam TCG's Burst mechanic, in which flipping the card over when taking damage has some sort of benefit to you to try and alleviate the act of taking damage.

Cookie Lore Not Required

For this particular set, the Dark Enchantress War is the setting and theme of the set, which is what made me most interested in it; while I enjoyed Cookie Run, I am primarily a Cookie Run Kingdom fan, so my knowledge of some of the other games and iterations of various Cookies from those games didn't always resonate with me as well. Also, seeing some of my favorite recent Cookies and events represented in this set helped get me excited, and it was cool to see how the game worked in Dark Enchantress Cookie herself and helped define some deck identities around her; Cookie Run always focuses very heavily on flavor and theme, and I really found this particular set did a great job of sticking to that theme. If you're already a Braverse player, I think it is pretty easy to recommend this set as it introduces new cards and strategies, but if Cookie Run Kingdom is what got you interested in the universe, this set is probably an ideal jumping on point as the cards and characters represented in the set are going to be far more familiar than with some of the previous sets.

This is a game about Cookies, trust me.

There is something to be said about the fact that the game doesn't really require you to have any actual knowledge of these things, though; if you are simply looking for an accessible game that can be played across various age groups, Braverse does an excellent job of providing that. There's no content that requires concern from a parent, as there is no gore or horrific imagery, no suggestive art, and an exceptionally colorful game that plays quickly and easily. So although the newest set would love for you to recognize characters and events from the recent climactic CRK storyline, you don't need to know any of that to enjoy the game yourself!

These newer Starter Decks are great for getting in to the game, if you find them, as they have more updated mechanics and cards.

In our testing, we found that the new cards also easily slot into or can be built into decks that already exist, and both sets of Starter Decks available for the game can be played with using some of these new cards to provide a few new strategies. I will say that the original Starter Decks showed a bit of age compared to the new cards in terms of mechanics and options, but the recent Legendary Starter set that revealed 5 new decks (one for each color) worked a lot better in combination with the cards we had pulled. Although a box of Dark Enchantress War cards will provide you with a pretty solid set of cards to start with, if you're really serious about getting in to Braverse, you may want to consider looking up decklists from events and working from there; the game is quite affordable, and thankfully most higher rarity cards are printed at various levels to make it easier to obtain copies to play with rather than collect.

Recipe for Success

Cookie Run Braverse is a fun, easy to learn game that has consistently increased in quality since the initial EN release in 2025; if the game appeals to you, or you're looking for an easy non-Pokemon game to pick up to play with younger or more casual players, I highly suggest giving Braverse a try. If you're already a fan of Cookie Run, then Braverse is a very easy sell, as the game rewards your knowledge of the franchise through flavorful representations of characters and events, and unless you are hunting down high rare variants, the game is very affordable and accessible thanks to the fact that the community is somewhat niche compared to other IPs. About the biggest reservation I have in recommending Braverse is the availability; while I haven't had much problem in finding Braverse locally, my LGS carries the game and has a very active community; I've otherwise had a harder time finding it in other stores that aren't solely dedicated to cards, and with so many card shops focusing on the "investing" community, you're likely going to find Braverse in stores with stronger player bases and local communities than those that service "investment". Otherwise, go forth and run like GingerBrave to give Braverse a try and enjoy the colorful and fun little world of Cookie Run!
