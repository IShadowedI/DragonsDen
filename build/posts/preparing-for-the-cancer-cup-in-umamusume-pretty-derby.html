Preparing for the Cancer Cup in Umamusume: Pretty Derby

June 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer

June is half over, but that doesn’t mean we’re out of the woods as far as Champion’s Meetings are concerned! No, instead we have the rerun of the Cancer Cup coming on the 21st, meaning you’ve got about a week to prepare for it if you haven’t worked on any new trainees for it yet. And although some of these reruns have been doable with perhaps your stable of aces, Cancer Cup throws a few curveballs into the mix thanks to changes in its stats. Never fear, we’ve got all the information you need in order to get yourself out ahead of the pack and try to claim Gold or better on the turf once again.

If you’re new to Umamusume: Pretty Derby or are curious to try it out, we have a number of guides that would be a great place for you to start with, and although dropping headfirst into a PVP event like this might be kind of difficult, there’s no time like the present to cut one’s teeth on the premier competitive mode in the game. There’s also likely to be more events coming up as the game moves from June to July, so getting up to speed now could be a great option, especially as we start moving out of the Trackblazer era and into the next scenario. But now, let’s get on to how to conquer Cancer Cup’s rerun!

Cancer Cup Returns With Curveballs

The biggest issues with Cancer Cup compared to some of the other races is that it changes up some of the usual skills that have fairly universal use. While numerous other races have a lot of the same conditions, races like Cancer Cup make it harder to reuse previous trainees as they’ll be unable to use some of the skills you imparted. For specifics, the rerun of Cancer Cup is a 2200m Medium Distance race run from the Right Inner Posts during Summer in Cloudy Weather on Good Conditions on Hanshin Turf. If you’re a bit of a veteran, you know that means that you’ll be unable to use Standard Distance (as it requires the distance to be divisible by 400), but the race also takes place on a somewhat uncommon racetrack compared to most other competitions, which use Tokyo Turf. Cloudy Days is also pretty uncommon compared to Sunny Days, although not as uncommon as you might expect. Wet Conditions is the skill you’ll need instead of Firm Conditions, and while it is also pretty available, most other races rely on Firm, so it is likely you’ll need to start from scratch for a Cancer Cup competitor.

Wet Conditions is at least fairly easy to track down, coming on a variety of cards in Speed, Stamina, Power, Wit, and Guts; of these, Guts and Speed are probably the easiest two to consider when it comes to building your deck, so consider cards like Special Week Speed, Shinko Windy Speed, or Taiki Shuttle Speed, or if you’re using a Guts build, Special Week Guts, Mejiro Ryan Guts, Sirius Symboli Guts, and Smart Falcon Guts are all good choices. You could also try Oguri Cap Guts and Mejiro Palmer Guts, which require Career Events rather than Hints. If you have no access to ANY of these cards, you could probably just skip Wet Conditions, but if you absolutely need it, you could try Smart Falcon Power, Biwa Hayahide Poewr, Mejiro Ryan Power, Manhattan Cafe Stamina (either), Mejiro McQueen Stamina,or  Nakayama Festa Stamina.

Hanshin Racecourse is a bit harder, but it does make a good case for considering Biwa Hayahide or Mejiro Palmer’s cards from Wet Conditions as both cards offer both of these skills (And Palmer can also give you Front Runner Savvy, if you’re training a Front Runner). Otherwise, your options here are any of the Nishino Flower cards or Marvelous Sunday; ignore Goldship’s cards, because despite her providing them, her cards are actually terrible.

Right-Handed has a few options, thankfully one of which is Fine Motion Wit, one of the best cards in the game. If you can borrow it and want this skill, absolutely go for it. Without that card, you have a few other options, namely Narita Brian Speed or Stamina (use Speed), Mejiro Bright Stamina, or Special Week Guts. You could also look to inherit this skill, and if you are training a Front-Runner, Seiun Sky can pass this skill down pretty easily, which might be a better option.

Non-Standard Distance is, unfortunately, a little restricted. Your best option is Narita Top Road’s Speed Card, which you may be borrowing (or using), but you could also try Mayano Top Gun Speed or Stamina, Meisho Doto Stamina or Guts, or Special Week Guts. If you were instead hoping to inherit this skill, Meisho Doto, T.M. Opera O, Gold Ship, and Mejiro Bright all have it as an option. Finally, Summer Runner is… available on one card: Fuji Kiseki Wit. Yeah.

Trainees to Consider

If you’ve followed our coverage for a while, you may already know what I’m about to say: Medium Races are basically free game. Almost every famous horse in Japanese racing was a Medium Distance competitor, and that’s reflected very heavily in the sheer number of trainees that can run Medium Distance races well, either from A or B in terms of aptitude. It is a staggering amount of possibilities, so what I’ll do here is tell you the specific “Best” options that the Japanese meta considered using, but just know that if someone you can train can be easily made into an A Medium competitor, you can run them, so feel free. Train your favorites. Go wild.

Smart Falcon can be a compelling choice if you make her Turf Friendly.

Free to Play: Daiwa Scarlet and Gold Ship are your best options. Daiwa Scarlet is run as a Front Runner here, and Gold Ship as an End Closer, but both can be highly successful old reliables.

Two Stars: Mejiro Ryan is a great option for Late Surger, and you could consider Agnes Tachyon, Nice Nature, or Grass Wonder for Debuffers, if you plan on running one.

Three Stars: Some of the usual suspects are here; again, you can use pretty much anybody, but the trainees with the best records are as follows by position.

Ol' Reliable, Seiun Sky remains a great choice for Front Runner or as a parent.

Front Runners are Valentine Mihono Bourbon, Summer Maruzensky, Kitasan Black, Seiun Sky, and Smart Falcon.

Pace Chasers include Christmas Oguri, Fine Motion, Mejiro Ardan, Satono Diamond, and Anime Teio.

Late Surgers include Mejiro Ardan, Mejiro Dober (Camping), Oguri Cap, Agnes Digital, Satono Diamond, or Tamamo Cross.

End Closers include Oguri Cap, Narita Brian, Tamamo Cross, Agnes Digital, and Inari One.

Finally, if you have access to “deluxe” Debuffers, you should consider Symboli Rudolf, Valentine Eishin Flash, Cheerleader Nice Nature, or Sweep Tosho.

Stats and Skills

The Stats you’ll want to shoot for in Graded League are probably what you can expect of CMs of late, which is very high and almost approaching stat caps in numerous categories. Most very competitive Aces are going to want 1200 Speed, 1000+ Power and 850~ Stamina, 500 Guts and 1000 Wit, a fairly brutal mixture that requires excessively good rolls during Trackblazer runs and very specifically chosen decks (and luck. Lots of luck and patience). If you’re considering trying Open League, you’ll find that the stats are… actually a bit more comparable than you expect, but can be a little forgiving: 1200 Speed, 700+ Stamina, 9000 Power, 500 Guts, and 800 Wit, the last two of which are pretty easily obtainable. The biggest challenge with Open League is riding the line of getting to these numbers and NOT exceeding them, because moving your trainee from A+ to S makes the unable to compete and yet also not good enough for Graded either.

Remember me, Eddie?

You might see other content creators discuss the Throne Group SSR that I mentioned in the June preview. This card is controversial because while it is very good, it also is basically extremely limited in how useful it is and by the time it releases (around the same time the Cancer Cup will already be underway), you’ll have very little time to make use of it and wasting your carats on it is an exceptionally bad idea. For that reason, I’ve excluded it from any of the suggestions for Support Decks that I’ll have below, and I highly suggest you do as well. If you want to try and train someone after round 1 of Cancer Cup with this card, only do so by borrowing it from someone who made the decision to MLB it. You really will thank me later for saving your carat budget.

Rice Shower Power is an excellent source of Swinging Maestro, if you're looking to avoid running Super Creek for it.

Anyway, a pretty solid strategy for your Support Card deck is going to be 2 Speed, 1-2 Power Cards, and 2-3 Wit Cards; you can however consider swapping in Riko Kashimoto for the third Wit card if you aren’t able to or don’t plan to try and use Throne, as she provides functionally similar benefits (and is another good argument against spending on that card). If you’re looking to do a Guts Build, you’ll still want at least 1 Speed Card, 3 Guts, and 2 Wit cards to round things out. Speed wise, Narita Top Road is probably your best Speed option and likely worth borrowing, especially if you are going to utilize your own Kitasan Black Card; if you’re doing Guts, use whichever speed card you have if you can’t fill in with 3 Guts cards and need to borrow one. The SSR Haru Urara Guts card really shines here, and you can also get a lot of mileage out of SR Admire Vega if it is MLB; other good options include Ines Fujin Guts and Bamboo Memory, as well as Yukino Bijin’s Festival Guts SSR. Guts has a lot of build variety, but can be a frustrating run at times, so consider what you want to try and experiment a bit before cementing on one or the other.

You’ll probably want to try inheriting a few very specific uniques: this time around, with Seiun Sky’s Angling and Scheming remaining a big one, as well as Fuji Kiseki’s Lights of Vaudeville for for Front and Pace. Pace Chasers, Late, and End Closers will also want to consider Oguri Cap’s Triumphant Pulse, and Late Surgers and End Closers could consider Mejiro Dober’s Moving Past, and Beyond, as well as Mejiro Ryan’s Let’s Pump Some Iron! As classic callbacks. You should also try to get at least one recovery, like Swinging Maestro, or consider accessing a few recoveries to help cover the distance debt and the likelihood of Debuffers in your lobbies.

Grand Live is Almost Here!

While June is a pretty slow month for the meta of Umamusume and only has a single competitive event to it’s name, that’s okay, because it is time to start preparing for the next scenario: Grand Live! We’ll hopefully start getting some news about that as it begins to move closer, but expect Grand Live to show up sometime late in July or early in August, likely coinciding somewhat with the official first year anniversary of the Global Client (not to be confused with the first year anniversary in the game, which happened a few months ago. I know. Accelerated schedules are weird). Until then, if you have any questions, feel free to shoot me an email over at marcy@tabletopbattles.com, or drop by the Discord and ask me there!
