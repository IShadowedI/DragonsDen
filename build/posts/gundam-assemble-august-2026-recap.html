Gundam Assemble: August 2026 Recap

August 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Greetings to all our resident Newtype and Newtype haters (and Newtype-hating Newtypes to let Char feel included). Gundam Assemble still isn't out, but the October 2026 release date is getting closer with each passing moment! Since our last update in March, there have been a few conventions where demos of Gundam Assemble were available to test pilot as well as an update video that came out early Monday morning. Between these, as well as a quiet update to the official Gundam Assemble website, there's enough stuff to warrant another look from the Tabletop Battles team. Join us as we overanalyze the little scraps of information available while waiting to get our hands on the game itself.

The Product Update Video with Erik Lonnquist (organized play manager) and Justin Gary (lead game designer) dropped right after GenCon. Despite its dry name, it is full of interesting tidbits that shed light on what Bandai's near future plans are for the game as well as what we can expect when October rolls around and people can start playing. If you prefer reading an article over watching a 25-minute video, you're basically this site's demographic and we love you so continue reading for the highlights.

Road Map

Before diving any further, I'll go ahead and throw out a chart that I made showing all the announced products and their current release dates. Most of this information has been available for a while, but one of the releases has been pushed back a month while a few other newer ones have been added recently.

Current product schedule for Gundam Assemble as of August 2026. Credit: Jake Bennington

Currently, there are five different types of products that are in the launch window for Gundam Assemble. I also want to take a moment to appreciate the naming and numbering system because it is going to make figuring out releases so much easier this way. I'm imagining someone stumbling across the game six months after it released and they'll immediately be able to see what the last few releases are without having to refer to some nerd's hand crafted spreadsheet that they shared on the internet. I'm speaking as a nerd who has created exactly that spreadsheet… a few times.

Starter Set 01. Credit: Bandai

Anyway, Starter Set 01 is intended as the starting point for players. It contains everything that two players will need in order to immediately start jamming games – six models with their corresponding unit cards, enough tactics cards for both players to build their nine-card decks, two very different looking scenarios to play, game board, dice, and terrain. The rules presented in the Starter Set sound like they're going to be the framework for the game that any other changes (such as in the Deluxe Sets) can be then modify.

All of this with a MSRP of $35 USD is a crazy low price for this hobby! These 3v3 games are advertised as taking under an hour; this game size was also outlined as being the "default" size for games, which makes me think it'll be the standard for future organized play events. This is the format I'll probably be focusing on the most because I dig the idea of fast playing games and the idea of mixing-and-matching to make my ultimate dream team is appealing.

Deluxe Set 01 through 03. Credit: Bandai

The various Deluxe Sets serve to spice the core gameplay rules up. Like Starter Set 01, the Deluxe Sets contain everything needed for playing so they could serve as another starting point for players. However, the purpose of Deluxe Sets seem to be introducing twists and reimagined versions of the core rules to serve as framework for playing out iconic moments in the Gundam universe. For instance, Deluxe Set 01 is going to be centered around the Battle of A Baoa Qu which served as the dramatic final fight of Mobile Suit Gundam. These scenarios have tweaks to the rules alongside story specific tactics cards that can only be played in their relevant scenarios.

Due to their increased size and bespoke scenarios, these games are going to be longer and could play out over 2-3 hours per game. This gives me the vibe that it's going to be more of a handcrafted narrative experience for those types of players who want to relive cinematic battles from the shows on the tabletop. They're also the most expensive products from the game, ranging from MSRP $100 to $170. That said, you're getting over a dozen models in them so the dollar-per-model breakdown might not be that bad.

Expansion Pack 01 through 09. Credit: Bandai

Expansion Pack 01 and Expansion Pack 02 are going to be the first two of a series of smaller, non-standalone releases. These will contain two or three models within the same Gundam franchise along with their tactics cards for a MSRP of $40. The current plan seems to be releasing one or two of these Expansion Packs every month. On the one hand, that's exciting because it means there will constantly be an influx of new game pieces to keep the game fresh, but on the other that's a lot of stuff to try and keep up with! If this release schedule is maintained, it might make Gundam Assemble into more of a game where you pick up the pieces that are interesting to you instead of trying to "catch 'em all" depending on your threshold. So far nine of these Expansion Packs have been announced.

Next up, Scenario Pack 01 is a new type of product that showed up on the Gundam Assemble website in mid-July. They briefly touched on the concept of the Scenario Pack in the update video. It is, unsurprisingly, going to contain an assortment of scenarios beyond what are available in the Starter Set and Deluxe Sets. A separate statement mentioned the intention of creating scenarios intended explicitly for organized play, so my guess is that they'll show up in here alongside other more narrative focused scenarios. Given how varied the few scenarios we've seen are, I'm more interested in seeing the contents of this pack now than I was when I first saw it show up in the planned releases. It isn't coming out until February 2027, so hopefully the variety we have within the Starter Set and various Deluxe Sets will keep everything fresh for the game's first six months.

Finally, we have Paint Pack 01. It's going to have paint. Also some models. There hasn't been much information about this yet and I'm curious what type of paint the Gundam Assemble line will be. Is it going to be something like the fairly normal for miniatures acrylic? Will it be alcohol based? Enamel? I'm not sure I'll be shifting off of my current lineup, but it'll be interesting to see what's here. After a tip from a helpful reader telling me they're acrylic, I looked into it a bit more. Looks like they're part of the Mr. Hobby line of ACRYSION paints which are water-based acrylics. Neat!

Gameplay Information

Alongside showing us all the variety of pretty toys that Bandai would love for us to buy, there were also some additional insights as to how the game will be played that we haven't seen before.

TP-007B Zaku II. Credit: Fowler

First up is how the game will handle duplicate units. As of now, there's a rule that you can only play one of any particular unit unless it has the Mass Produced tag on the unit card. Sample Mass Produced units are exactly what you'd expect, allowing you to field multiple of the same Zaku suit or even a full Ball squad. It's worth noting that this restriction is based on the full name on the unit card – this means that someone could play with the RX-78-2 named "Hero of Side 7" alongside the RX-78-2 named "White Devil" because they have different names and are thus different units. Sure, they're all the same granda Gundam piloted by Amuro, but they are snapshots of different parts of the story or different tactically focused versions of the suit. One is more beam rifle focused, another is beam saber focused, and so on. Does it make sense? Not really, but that's kind of the point. Gundam Assemble can "make the impossible possible" as Justin said. I can't wait to have Team Char running around.

Sleeping Leviathan and Azure Fang scenarios from the ST01 box. Credit: Bandai

Scenarios were also expanded on with a focus on the two found in Starter Set 01 – Sleeping Leviathan and Azure Fang.

If you've seen pictures of demos performed at conventions, or had the luck to play one yourself, it's been Sleeping Leviathan. This is the desert looking board with a large hill dominating the center of the playing area. Objectives, garrisons, and power ups are scattered around the battlefield, but the objectives located to the edge of the playing are worth more points than the centrally located ones. This means you'll have to decide whether you want the commanding position of high ground in the middle or go after the higher scoring points in the peripheries.

Azure Fang flips this layout on its head by featuring a lush green board with a lake smack dab in the middle. The lake introduces water as a new type of terrain which will make it both harder to hit a mobile suit in the water but also reduce the accuracy of anyone wading through it. Additionally, the objective mechanics are changed here. Instead of tagging an objective and running off to still score it at the end of the phase, models will have to pick up the objectives and keep them safe to score points.

I appreciate how even in the starter box, the most simplified version that this game will ever be in, there are two distinctly different scenarios that will require different approaches. Maybe the meta turns out to favor fast units on Sleeping Leviathan to get around the board to tag all the objectives while Azure Fang will reward more durable options to pick up objectives and survive with them long enough to score. Time will tell, but I'm eager to experiment. Scenarios seem to play an enormous role in the game and aren't simply slightly different layouts on a board.

The map in Deluxe Set 01 is going to be much larger. It has to be in order to fit in all the extra models without it feeling cramped! There will be two scenarios for the 3v3 mode, but there are also two 5v5 battles utilizing the mobile suits alongside carriers such as White Base. Space was also hinted at being yet another terrain type. Deluxe Set 02 was even teased to feature a 3v5 battle alongside other "even more crazy" changes.

Gundam Assemble Models, Heroic Beginnings starter set. Credit: Greg Chiasson

One thing I've seen asked plenty of times and wondered myself is how the models from the Gundam Card Game crossover will work in Gundam Assemble. It's been officially confirmed that those models are valid for play! Whenever one of the expansion sets releases that contains one of the figures that had been released in this crossover promotion, the unit cards will be "print on demand" for players. The fact that these models are on round bases while the forthcoming releases will be on hex bases is also confirmed to not matter because Gundam Assemble doesn't have any facing mechanics. Use whatever base shape you want!

A minor thing that wasn't pointed out specifically in the video is that the stats of the models showcased are different from earlier versions shown at demos or in the earlier gameplay video. It's not particularly shocking that a game in development continues to change, but it does make me feel better for not going deep into theorycrafting the game just yet. There will be plenty of time for that later. And now that it's August, the release is only two months away!
