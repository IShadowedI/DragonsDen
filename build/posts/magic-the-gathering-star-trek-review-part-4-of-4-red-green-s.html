Magic: The Gathering Star Trek Review, Part 4 of 4 – Red, Green, & Scene Box Cards

November 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Space, the final frontier. These are the voyages of the starship Enterprise...as well as around 34 other new Spacecraft. Our continuing mission: To explore in detail the ramifications of newly-printed cardboard; to speculate upon their effects on formats such as Commander, Standard, & Modern; to boldly joke about the 43 new Creature types printed in this set unlikely ever to be seen again! Yes, Star Trek has arrived to Magic, and with it a host of new cards sure to shake things up. Most impactful is the full realization of Spacecraft as an Artifact subtype, more than doubling the number thusly printed. Today we're covering the set's red, green, and scene cards.

Star Trek officially releases to tabletop on November 13th, and on Magic: The Gathering Arena on November 10th.

Red

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Brazen Bluff

Saffgor: This card is sweet, but it is also very likely...bad. Punisher effects like this always give you the worst possible option, and even in its best case scenario this is just a fancy ritual. Cast a bomb on the cheap, but spend a card to do so, or be a counterspell to a card your opponent doesn't care about. Really cool design, but this is part of a rather unfortunate lineage.

BPhillipYork: I love this card because it's fun, but the way actually playing it out will generally work isn't going to make it terribly strong.  Still, non-creature spells include enchantments and artifacts, so this could be a fun way to sneak out a conditional win enchantment during an opponent's turn.  The real problem is if your opponents think what you're doing are sufficiently threatening they'll just let you exile the spell, which means if you're trying to play it to free cast something you have to actually cast it on something you really want to counter.  Anyway, I think it'll lead to more fun games, and that makes a good card.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain James T. Kirk

Saffgor: No Haste being granted makes me nervous about this, because being functionally a draw and a 1/1 for 3 mana, on the condition you're hellbent, doesn't inspire. Tersa Lightshatter compared to this guy just looks way better in essentially all formats.

BPhillipYork:  This seems fine, fun if you want to go balls out and have no cards in hand, still not that strong, generating officer tokens is useful for, well just tokens in general but also to enable things like Captain Benjamin Sisko to go nuts.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dominion Infiltration

Saffgor: I don't hate this card, but it feels like Devastating Onslaught tends to scale better. Cheaper for the first, the same for the second, and then it gets outpriced...but you get to plan around internal synergy, rather than relying on opposing options.

BPhillipYork:  This effects just don't ever really seem that worth it.  Only good if your opponents have really good creatures already or you have some wacky trick, and super expensive.

FromTheShire: 

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Encounter the Unknown

Saffgor: I mean this just seems unbelievable for 60-card, right? Instant-speed Pyroclasm that can be advantage if you're resource-light, that's just fantastic for midrange. It doesn't even hit Planeswalkers, so you can stick a Chandra and hold this up.

BPhillipYork:  Really solid utilitarian and value instant.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Erica Ortegas, Plucky Pilot

Saffgor: Fun Landfall enabler as a means to loop your triggers, especially with bouncelands. 4 mana is a bit dicey, but you could do worse if this ends up being a combo piece.

BPhillipYork:  This has potential for like, decks with lots of landfall triggers to just keep cycling lands back in and out, which is sometimes an issue for those decks, though graveyard play and fetchlands usually solves it.  Probably wouldn't recommend as a commander, because like, what you're going to ramp once you hit 4, sort of?  In mono-red?

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Exploratory Mission

Saffgor: The fact you're assured a nonland is great here, as it means Red isn't running out of gas. If this recoups mana, you're really happy with this effect. Look, I don't love this starting at 4 mana, but the actual effect is pretty stellar—if you're playing in Bracket 2, and have Landfall synergy, this goes in. It's going to effectively draw & pay for itself twiceover most games.

BPhillipYork:  This is nuts.  Like wtf.  Thankfully it's expensive enough to not be totally busted, but over any kind of long game, especially in stax type decks this is a crazy value engine.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Genesis Effect

Saffgor: This is mentioned as a sweet card for Pauper Wildfire, where you couple effects like this and Cleansing Wildfire with Indestructible Lands to ramp. Probably 2 copies make it in there, but I think this vastly improves that deck.

BPhillipYork:  I guess there's some neat things you can do with this, there's always the like, destroy indestructible land woohoo free land but for 5-mana, nah.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Infinite Diversity

Saffgor: I love what this card represents, but mechanically I am not its audience. This is a Timmy card, and that's okay.

BPhillipYork:  Infinity is like a lot bigger than what, 10?  12?  How many non-borg creatures are you likely to control at once?  Still, dangerous if you are able to cast some nasty instant a bunch of times, or just take like 7 extra turns.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lwaxana Troi, Charming Guest

Saffgor: One-woman Roaming Throne, and I mean, I don't hate it! Lacking the Ward from the Throne is tough, and frankly I've found myself just...playing that card outside of typal, naming one of my Commander's types. This might be fine enough to sit in a similar role as far as that goes, but she's no Throne.

BPhillipYork:  Doubling triggers is nice, by default you're probably just targeting your commander, since your deck is built around them, and that can be fairly nasty.  Throwing this onto say Prosper, Tome-Bound is going to let you probably end the game that turn.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Naked Time

Saffgor: This might be the best card in the set for Standard from some early results, and while I don't love the art, at least it isn't Those Old Scientists. 2 mana ramp, into a pressure trigger, and then you shrug about that downside. The card's good, but it just does flat nothing once you've exited the early game, so I'm not sure about its merit. Jim Davis is on an Iroh, Tea Master donate deck with this, and that's...concerning.

BPhillipYork:  I don't uh.  I wouldn't probably play this card.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pah-Wraith

Saffgor: This not granting Haste keeps it from being a Land-based Craterhoof, but it remains a decent card to cheat out, I suppose.

BPhillipYork:  Well, that's basically a red sort of finisher creature, with the haste and buffing all your creatures for probably at least 4 or 5, which is pretty nice to have.  No trample and not handing out haste makes it a lot less equivalent to End-Raze Forerunners or other cards of that ilk.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Q, Stacking the Deck

Saffgor: Oh Q, what a shame it has been to brew you. Q was the very first card I covered in terms of Star Trek's new toys, and my final verdict—as it remains—is that he's more a fun puzzle to solve, than a deck to actually play. In any list that trims down to 1-5 nonlands, you just end up being a clock more than a person. Q basically can't be disrupted once he resolves, but because your gameplan from then on is so telegraphed, you tend to be focused down or otherwise played around like a force of nature, and not someone to politic with. Q robs you of options in play, even if he's a marvelous canvas in the deckbuilder.

BPhillipYork:  Yeah this is great.  Crazy.  Go hog wild.  Just like, see what happens, fun card.  Or do something boring and abusive and win the game but that's not very Q at all.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Quark, Humble Proprietor

Saffgor: What I love about Quark is that he quietly disincentivizes your opponents from attacking you on the ground in Commander, for fear of turning on your Treasures. Obviously they can be used for Improvise, Ghirapur Aether Grid, etc, but where Quark excels is in affording you time, and once you're unleashed after he dies, a truckload of mana. Great card.

BPhillipYork:  This is interesting, multiple ways now to just tap treasures for mana or other effects means this can be potentially pretty good, and you can always just sacrifice him or something.  Building up treasures that fast is pretty dangerous.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tasha Yar, Out of Time

Saffgor: She doesn't belong in the Command Zone, but in the 99 of graveyard decks? Maybe, sure.

BPhillipYork:  You could definitely be looping this with things like Alesha, who smiles at death, or various other recursion schemes.  As a commander, like you could cast her, sacrifice somehow, then use the ability, but that's like 5 mana to net 2 exiled playable cards and then your commander tax is at 2, so is that good?  Final answer is no.  No it is not.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Will Riker, First in the Field

Saffgor: Now this is a Landfall Commander! Works like a charm with extra combats, forced end turn effects, and even stuff like Aggressive Mining. I am a huge fan of this design, and while Landfall isn't an especially exciting archetype, doing so in Monored breaks some fairly-new ground, especially at this power level.

BPhillipYork: So this is interesting, with all the lands from yard sources and landfall triggers this will generate a useful board state if you build around it, or enable landfall decks that include red.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Worf, Intelligence Contractor

Saffgor: Grab three things, chuck 'em at an opponent, then sacrifice them. Seems straightforward to me, and Worf seems great in any sacrifice deck that has Red.

BPhillipYork:  Where is Worf, beaten up by the adversary of the week to show how strong it is?  Crazy to imagine putting on that much makeup that many days of your life to most get used as a punching bag.  But, this is okay?  Steal and sacrifice or trade or something, flicker him I guess.

Green

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Jonathan Archer

Saffgor: Porthos lacking evasion is a shame, because otherwise this would have shades of Minsc & Boo. As it stands, I don't buy it.

BPhillipYork:  Making a legendary dog token is kind of old hat.  I guess some kind of reusable super giant growth is nice, nothing I'd really write home about but good if you have a particular creature you're going to be maybe double striking or something.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Kathryn Janeway

Saffgor: My goodness that's a lot of exploring. Twists and Turns, Topography Tracker, even Wildgrowth Walker just get an asinine amount of value from her, and all of those are now extremely findable given Enchantments can be tutored in Monogreen. I expect to see her at the LGS, and moreover, I expect to lose to her at least once in the first month following Star Trek's release. Bet on it.

BPhillipYork:  This is a very landfall focused commander that's like, all value no payoff, but in mono-green there's typically just more useful sorts of things.  But as an enabler in a landfall deck, it's pretty nuts, especially since with landfall triggers that generate token creatures this will trigger, letting you explore and potentially play another land, generating another trigger etc..

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

David and Carol Marcus, Creators

Saffgor: These get you a uniquely-named Land that can be duplicated with token doublers, and at worst are a source of 3-mana ramp. In the right deck, that's going to lead to a ton of Planet tokens.

BPhillipYork:  I guess you can like flicker them.  Token lands are a new thing, basically, and they do color fix which is kind of nice.  Almost no way I'd run this as a commander, I think my defacto would be more like in a reanimate deck where I wanted to sacrifice them for value.  For reasons .

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dr Joseph M'Benga

Saffgor: Just an uninspired design, but that doesn't make it a bad one. These are two strong effects stapled together on a Legendary Creature, so while the direction is clear and the nuance is absent, neither of those stop it from being a solid card at Bracket 2.

BPhillipYork:  Counter doublers are kind of boring but they do their thing, handing out hexproof also is pretty nice.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy

Saffgor: Holy shit is that a Green Undying Evil effect? That's new territory for the color as far as I'm concerned, and while the trigger being slow to return prevents a lot of combos, Flash does a lot of heavy lifting. Probably not a candidate for the Command Zone, but any deck with clones in Simic will have their eyes on McCoy.

BPhillipYork:  It's important that he can't return timelords.  But this is like, I dunno, sort of okay.  Maybe if I planning to beam him back up I guess it's useful holdable protection.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Final Frontier

Saffgor: This is the most disappointing card from the set, because they refused to print a single good, new Planet worth searching. The ones from Edge of Eternity remain your best options, and while Evendo is a fantastic piece it could have been so much better. 2 mana ramp from the yard is still probably good enough writ large, but I weep for what could have been (and might be, if they keep printing Planets).

BPhillipYork:  This is really below the curve of Kodama's Reach analogs, so mostly can be ignored.  Only real use is fetching Planets from the other space set.  The original space set if you will.  If you won't it's called Edge of Eternities and you'd probably be fetching Evendo, Waking Haven, or Uthros, Titanic Godcore.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Genesis Device

Saffgor: Scapeshift on a permanent that doesn't exile itself, my lord. See above for my woes on Planets, but this being a Scapeshift that grabs a Land to start with is pretty fantastic.

BPhillipYork:  Nice way to generate a huge amount of landfall triggers at once as a wincon.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Holo-Engineer

Saffgor: The 'instead' here is a concern, because it stops you from getting Treasures, Clues, etc. That, and being 4 mana, kills this guy for me.

BPhillipYork:  I mean, it's okay, I guess.  I feel like this card just exists for emergency hologram fanboys or something, like if you're generating a lot of tokens there are way better token replacers than getting 3/3 colorless illusions that are vanilla.  Only big upside is that they artifacts, and things could be done with that.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy

Saffgor:  Wears buffs well, is herself a buff as needed, and even adds the mana without need to tap. Seems great to me, especially in a post-Badgermole Cub ban world.

BPhillipYork:  This is going to give you parallel sort of ramp, basically another carpet of flowers, slower, but more impactful over time, though only green mana.  The backup ability to save something in a pinch, or get counters going, is super useful to have, especially if you draw into her late and don't really need the mana anymore.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

MACO Strike Team

Saffgor: I think this is better than Pawpatch Recruit? Not a 1:1 comparison, as it definitely has a worse ceiling without Offspring, but retaining a body on board if it's targeted seems like what decks playing this would want, more than just a counter.

BPhillipYork:  On paper it's like, oh wow.  But in practice getting 1/1s when your more valuable stuff gets blown up, isn't good.  Most targeted spells only target one thing, if you're routinely getting lots of your stuff targeted somehow, then it becomes okay.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Rok-Tahk, Budding Scientist

Saffgor: This adds 4 types for Federation you'd probably not have otherwise, and makes all your bodies into superdorks. Is that worth 6 mana? In the right deck, yeah, I could see it, but broadly speaking the Federation mechanic is on underwhelming enough cards as to reduce the allure of Rok-Tahk.

BPhillipYork:  If you have 6 mana, then you can be tapping your creatures for GG, reads like a win more card to me, mostly.  Maybe you generate a ton of tokens then use this to end the game, in which case, fine.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Saurian Explorer

Saffgor: {6}: Get a Landfall trigger. Horrifying with Agatha of the Vile Cauldron, obviously, but also quietly a mana outlet for any Landfall deck packing a combo. Scute Swarm and Ashnods also goes infinite readily with this, among other things. This card might be a common, but it's going to end games, mark my words.

BPhillipYork:  Well that's potentially a lot of planet tokens, for 6 mana, at some point you'll just have all the planets I guess.  But if you already have 6 mana, generally more mana isn't what you need, so much as something to do with 6 mana.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Stable Wormhole

Saffgor: This is a pretty absurd card to print at a glance, but I think it's weirdly pretty fair. Bit of extra ramp if you go to combat each turn, and while it does snowball a bit you do need to legitimately be aggressive if this is coming down turn 2/3, and you want to get max value.

BPhillipYork:  This seems like a potentially dangerous card in any game that goes long.  Just getting tons of value over time, it fits nicely into a stax strategy or anything that really slows the game down, since eventually people start missing land drops but you won't be missing planet drops.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Test of Courage

Saffgor: Amazing card for sideboards. About as flexible a card one can ask for at a single mana, at Instant-speed.

BPhillipYork:  Good instant speed green interaction is rare, this is more of it, and that is good.  Doesn't really do anything that new, but having both on one card is flexible.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Discovery, at Black Alert

Saffgor: I've had a Monogreen Spacraft-matter deck in paper since EOE, helmed by Maester Seymour, and this set was sure to bring some cool new toys for that. This is one of them, but in all honesty it may be such that this becomes the Commander of that strategy. This is a green Pantlaza if you squint, and while Monogreen has fewer mechanisms by which to repeat this effect, that's a lot of free value to contend with.

BPhillipYork:  Black alert?  That's dumb.  I'm so glad I didn't watch that dumb show.  So, discover 5 is really solid.  6 charges isn't that hard to attain, which makes this a strong value play over time, a 5/5 flyer is generally too big to be stopped easily and can, in a 4 player game probably generate it's discover triggers with pretty much impunity.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Voyager, Lone Wanderer

Saffgor: This is the most commonly-played version of Open the Way (a good card) that comes with an eventual body...oh, and you can flicker it. Obviously this is a great card, and any deck running 4 mana ramp will slot this in with gusto.

BPhillipYork:  It seems strange to me that this card doesn't really do a good job of encompassing what Voyager was about.  Like, if you fail to reveal any lands then you should get something (aka the Voyager got home).  But it's okay, grab 2 lands at 4 mana but you get 3/3 flyer is... fine.  You can flicker it so that's potentially a way to generate additional value.

Promos

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Borg Queen, Perfection Manifest

Saffgor: So they uh, hid one of the set's best Monoblack Commanders in the Welcome Decks. This is expensive but potent, and represents a pretty clear gameplan of running out small Artifact Creature hordes and a few massive stolen threats, all while reusing her via Undying Evil effects. No Finality counters either, meaning you can get back that same big threat over and over. I quite like this card, but why isn't it in the Borg Precon?

BPhillipYork:  This feels underwhelming for what the borg queen represented.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Badgey, Malicious Glitch

Saffgor: Fun card to twiddle, and can do some Pili-Pala loops well.

BPhillipYork:  Too expensive to be much other than a meme card.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Divergent Timelines Scene Box

Saffgor: Bad news for the Abrams-verse fans: These cards are pretty bad, across the board. Chekhov and McCoy is likely the best among them, being a Terrasymbiosis in the Command Zone, but the rest just don't do enough. Uhura starts too expensive, Kirk is just an anthem, and Spock is probably outcompeted by the main set Uhura as a value engine for {2}{U}. Sulu has cute text which largely doesn't matter, and Scotty...I mean dude, if you want mass unblockable just give your board Horsemanship. I suppose he can give your opponents' Creatures unblockable? Is that the idea?

BPhillipYork:  Well Uhura is good, Sulu is good, Spock is potentially good, and Kirk, Man of Action goes well to support the real kirk, and I guess he buffs all of them, I dunno these don't really excite me that much.  Triptychs seem good enough, a six-tichs just seems overdone.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Enterprise-Q Scene Box

Saffgor: See this is how you do a Scene Box. Among these options, Troi, La Forge, & Q are standouts—Troi is an absolutely fantastic payoff for all of the White group hug draw, La Forge goes infinite in a number of ways, and Q obviously affords Faeries a genuine 5c option, on top of outrageous value. Even the worse of this set still have their uses, what with Picard making your Faeries enter as (at minimum) 3/3s, and Worf lets you swing out with a wide board. Data isn't great here, but he might just be reasonable enough in some of these other inexplicable Faerie typal Commanders. You could conceivably lead a powerful, interesting deck with 5/6 of these, and that's a pretty big win.

BPhillipYork:  This whole scene is much better thought out then the Kelvin timeline one.  And a lot more fun.  Q makes faeries and these guys benefit off of or make faeries and you use the faeries cast the cards in your decks, which is all fun and good and hangs togther.

Commander

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Beacon of Kahless

Saffgor: This is an okay 3 mana rock at worst, a pretty fantastic 3 mana rock if you swing out, and with extra combats this is making a wild amount of mana indeed. I'm quite bullish on the Beacon, but only for some very specific strategies. The one tragedy is this not working with Myriad, as is the case with all Battlefront cards.

BPhillipYork:  This is nice, solid, fitting the theme over the last few years of good 3 cost, colored, mana rocks.  Alongside things like Cursed Mirror, but stronger.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Grilka, Widowed Warrior

Saffgor: Go infinite with Aggravated Assault potentially using an opponent's Creature? That's pretty sweet, and Grilka does nasty things with cheap temporary clones on herself as well. 6 mana is a tough ask, but we got Queen's Diamond in the set, so...maybe?

BPhillipYork:  Too expensive unless you have some way to recur it endlessly.  Okay if you're going to cheat it out maybe and sacrifice what you borrowed.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Glory to the Empire!

Saffgor: 6 mana to always attack with 3 5/4s that turn, and likely afford your board +3/+3 on attack. This is a fun finisher for Bracket 2, and does its job well for that.

BPhillipYork:  Handing out melee is kind of dangerous giving how much the melee type triggers and myriad type triggers are starting to stack up.  6 is a lot of mana though, and there are typically stronger effects available, often on big fat creatures, for 6 mana.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Galvanizing Tenor

Saffgor: Seems like a decent piece for goad strategies, but the question there is 'how good is goad'. Really wish this also tapped its targets, or triggered when you attacked period, and not with it.

BPhillipYork:  This is fun, and annoying, but for 4 mana it's kind of low impact, but not big enough to be impossible to just block and kill.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Gagh Slinger

Saffgor: Warrior typal piece that will slowly rise in price due to its inability to be reasonably reprinted. Calling it now.

BPhillipYork:  Wow.  This is potentially really dangerous, what with cards like Najeela, the Blade Blossom existing.  Lots of ways to make warriors, and this will make each of them ping, and is also a cost reducer, which is pretty nice.  I think the whole klingon thing will be almost totally ignored.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Koloth, Tribble Hunter

Saffgor: This is analogous to Hansk, Slayer Zealot, but with none of the parts of that card which make it good. This card is bad outside of decks which care about goaded creatures, which Koloth does admittedly make a ton of.

BPhillipYork:  Okay, I kind of like this. Like hords of tribbles goaded and buffed is funny.  Not sure why these tribbles are bigger than the other tribbles, but who cares.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Own the Day

Saffgor: A 3-mana extra combat in the big '26. This card is just fantastic, and even gives you the extra Main phase too for all the shenanigans we'd hope. Outside of reprints this is likely going to be the best card from the Klingon precon.

BPhillipYork:  This is solid and well thought out, good in commander, rewards aggression and gives red a decent contingent type finisher.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Janice Rand, Essential Aide

Saffgor: If you have two Commaders via Partner/Backgrounds this is a truly absurd amount of ramp. It can't even fail, really, given it isn't nabbing basics like most ramp. It's a hard sell if you don't have 2 cards in the Command Zone, but among those lucky to be paired-up, Janice is your new best friend.

BPhillipYork:  This is really dangerous with partner, partner with, but also things like Derevi, Empyrial Tactician, or like Brago, King Eternal it's obviously out of color, but any kind of repeating flickering, or a commander that gets reanimated over and over will get kind of nuts.

That wraps up our look at the cards of Star Trek. We’ll be back next week with our regular content.
