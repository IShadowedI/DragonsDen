[40k] Ruleshammer 11th Edition: Transports

July 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back to Ruleshammer! In this series we take a detailed look at the rules of key games, but Warhammer 40k in particular. This week I’m looking at Transports in 11th edition!

Transport Capacity

Every model has a specific set of criteria regarding which models it can carry and how many of them, not that this limit is purely a max model count though Transports can contain more than one unit.

TRANSPORT models have a transport capacity listed on their datasheet. This determines the type and maximum number of friendly models that are eligible to embark within them. More than one unit can be embarked within the same TRANSPORT model at the same time, provided it has sufficient transport capacity.

Some of these transport rules can be quite specific and will vary between different transports in your army. For instance Rhinos and Impulsors differ in specific models they can transport. The Rhinos:

RHINO TRANSPORT RULE: This model has a transport capacity of 12 ADEPTUS ASTARTES INFANTRY models. It cannot transport JUMP PACK, WULFEN, PHOBOS, GRAVIS, CENTURION, TERMINATOR or TACTICUS models (excluding TACTICUS CHARACTER models that began the battle attached to a non-TACTICUS unit).

vs. the Impulsors:

IMPULSOR TRANSPORT RULE: This model has a transport capacity of 7 TACTICUS or PHOBOS INFANTRY models. It cannot transport JUMP PACK models.

For many armies there’s a distinct progression of transports capable of carrying larger and larger models; Space Marines, for instance, need a Land Raider before they can carry models as big as Terminators or Centurions.

LANDRAIDER TRANSPORT RULE: This model has a transport capacity of 12 ADEPTUS ASTARTES INFANTRY models. Each JUMP PACK, WULFEN, GRAVIS or TERMINATOR model takes up the space of 2 models and each CENTURION model takes up the space of 3 models.

Now that we know what models we can put in our transports, let's move on to using them in a game.

Before the Battle Begins

Players decide what units will be in which transports during the Mission sequence; it’s not something you’re locked into when you muster your army. You actually know a bit of information about the battle you’re about to fight before you have to make these decisions.

Before the battle, in the Declare Battle Formations step, your units can start embarked within any friendly TRANSPORT model that has sufficient transport capacity remaining for the whole unit.

The Declare Battle Formations step is fairly straight forward.

Players secretly note down in order:

    1. Which of their units will start the battle embarked within which TRANSPORTS.
    2. Which of their units will start the battle in Strategic reserves.

Be aware that if you do put a Transport into Strategic Reserves, both the Transport and the units embarked inside it count towards the 50% points limit for Strategic reserves.

Unless otherwise stated, the combined points value of all of your strategic reserves units (including those embarked within TRANSPORTS that are themselves placed in strategic reserves) cannot exceed 50% of your points limit for your battle size.

One aspect of this that players don’t have a choice in since the July 2026 update, though, is if their Dedicated Transports can begin the battle empty or not. Missing from the launch version of 11th edition, that requirement has been re-added to rule 18.01:

A friendly unit must be embarked within each friendly DEDICATED TRANSPORT model. At the end of this step, each friendly DEDICATED TRANSPORT model without an embarked unit is destroyed, but they do not trigger rules that are triggered when a model is destroyed.

So Dedicated Transport models like the Space Marine Rhino, the Leagues of Votann Sagitaur, or the T'au Devilfish MUST have a unit embarked inside of them at the start of the battle or they are destroyed.

Embarking

Embarking in transports requires a unit to have met a series of criteria and made some specific move types; it is not, however, limited to any single phase or even just the controlling player's turn. I’ll get back to this nuance in a moment.

Once the first battle round has started, a friendly unit can embark within a friendly TRANSPORT model after making a normal, advance or fall-back move, if all of the following conditions apply:

    • Each model in that unit is within 3" of that TRANSPORT.
    • That unit was not set up on the battlefield this turn.
    • That unit is eligible to embark within that TRANSPORT, as described on that TRANSPORT’s datasheet.
    • That TRANSPORT has sufficient remaining transport capacity for each model in that unit.

Most of these criteria are pretty obvious; that you can’t embark inside a transport that doesn’t have room for the whole unit shouldn’t come as a surprise. Some of the others, though, are a bit more nuanced.

Each model in that unit is within 3" of that TRANSPORT.

This criteria, for instance, has varied from edition to edition, but in 11th edition the requirement is that every model in the Unit must be within 3”. Here’s what that looks like.

That unit was not set up on the battlefield this turn.

Without some special ability your units can’t arrive on the battlefield and then embark in the same turn; remember that Disembarking counts are being set up on the battlefield, though! You can’t disembark from one transport and embark into another without a special rule saying your unit can do that or something similar, such as the Drukhai Aerialists rule that lets a unit embark at the end of the fight phase.

Take note, though, that this limit only applies to the turn, NOT the round. This is where the lack of a phase or turn limit comes into play. If you have some way of making a normal, advance, or fall-back move in your opponent's turn, units can use this move to embark.

In your opponent's Movement phase, if an enemy unit ends a move within 8" of this unit, if this unit is not within Engagement Range of one or more enemy units, this unit can make a Normal move of up to D6"

What Can Embarked Units do?

A short question with a fairly complex answer. The July 2026 update has added a number of clarifications for Embarked Units.

01.02.04 Not on the Battlefield

A unit that is embarked within a TRANSPORT or that is in strategic reserves is not on the battlefield. The following applies to such units.

    • That unit is NOT visible to any other units (units are visible to themselves).
    • Any other unit is NOT visible to that unit.
    • Players cannot measure distances to or from that unit (units are within range of their own abilities).

This means that units not on the battlefield cannot be selected or targeted by any attack or rule that requires a unit to be visible or within a certain distance (other than their own abilities).

Such units can still use their other rules, and are still units in the controlling players army and so can be affected by rules that require a player to select a unit from an army, as well as rules that affect all units in an army.

The control player must take battle-shock rolls for units that are not on the battlefield in their Command Phase if they are battle-shocked, or if they are below half strength.

It’s hard to really summarise this into any fewer words than it already is. Suffice it to say the important elements here are that you can use stratagems on embarked units, embarked units can still benefit from their own abilities, and embarking a unit doesn’t prevent you from needing to do Battle-shock tests.

So abilties like Abbadon's The Warmaster would still function, and allow the controlling player select an aura for him as usual, though the aura itself wouldn't benefit any other models as you can't measure distances to embarked units, so no unit can be within the aura. Abbadon would technically be within range of his own aura whilst embarked, but he doesn't actually have the keywords to be affected by it so that doesn't do anything.

Firing Deck, Leader Abilties and Enhancements

Firing Deck is much the same in 11th edition, though considerably more wordy about it.

This ability always takes the form Firing Deck X. In your Shooting phase, each time this TRANSPORT is selected to shoot, if one or more units are embarked within it, resolve the following sequence:

    1. Select up to X models embarked within this TRANSPORT (excluding models whose units have already been selected to shoot this phase).
    2. For each selected model, select one of its ranged weapons (excluding [ONE SHOT] weapons).
    3. Until this TRANSPORT has resolved all of its attacks, it has all of those selected weapons in addition to its other weapons.
    4. Until the end of the turn, units embarked within this TRANSPORT are not eligible to shoot.

The Transport gains the weapons of the embarked unit, and now you might wonder, if the units' abilities are working can Leader abilities and Enhancments affect these weapons that the Transport has gained. No. As of the July 2026 FAQ it's been ruled that only the attached unit benefits from leader abilities and enhancements, and the Transport isn't part of the attached unit.

Q: While an attached unit is embarked within a TRANSPORT, do weapons that TRANSPORT shoots with using the Firing Deck ability gain any benefit from any abilties that attached leader has (e.g. a Lieutenant's Tactical Precision ability that grants weapons in it's unit the [LETHAL HITS] ability?

A: No. Firing Deck enables a TRANSPORT to be equipped with a number of weapons equipped by it's passengers (Core Rules), bu the TRANSPORT itself is not part of that attached unit.

So whilst not ambiguous, I would forgive players for thinking that an enhancement or ability that changes those weapons would still have changed them when the Transport borrows them but alas not.

Disembarking

Disembarking is perhaps the most codified it’s ever been in Warhammer 40k in this edition. There are now three distinct modes of Disembark you need to be aware of.

from Warhammer 40k 11th Edition Core Rules - Games Workshop

Rapid Disembark: This mode is for Transports that have moved or arrived via an ingress move this phase. Models making a Rapid Disembark need to follow the same rules the Transport had to follow when disembarking whilst also needing every model to be wholly within 3” of the Transport. Units that perform Rapid Disembarking can not charge in that turn.

Tactical Disembark: This mode is essentially “normal disembark;” it follows the normal Set Up 03.02 rules models are normally required to follow whilst again also needing every model to be wholly within 3” of the Transport. Units that use this mode can make normal and advance moves after disembarking, and there are no restrictions on their ability to charge.

Combat Disembark: This last mode lets you disembark into engagement range but only with units the Transport is engaged with, and it comes at the risk of losing models to hazard rolls. You're only forced to use this mode if there’s not enough space to disembark using the Tactical mode, which would allow your unit to move elsewhere or charge without risking the Hazard rolls.

Emergency Disembarking

If your unit is unlucky enough to be embarked inside a Transport that is destroyed, it is forced to make an Emergency Disembark move. Be aware that this rule has changed quite significantly in the app though! here's the full rule as of July 2026.

from the Warhammer Core Rules App - Games Workshop

The first thing to note about this rule is that while the disembark distaince is 6", models using this disembark move must be set up as close to the Transport as possible. So if there's room for the model to be placed touching the transport it has to be placed there. This is why this all happens before the transport is removed.

If a TRANSPORT model is destroyed, before removing it from the battlefield, the active player must make an emergency disembark move (see opposite) with each unit embarked within it.

The added lines is pretty fundamental though, adding an escape clause for models that can't deploy following the the usual Set Up 03.02 rules.

OR: if the above is now possible while remaining unenaged, set up that model wholly within the set-up distance of that Transports, and as close as possible to that transport, and engaged.

Note that this clause is a "while moving" one, which means that you don't need to keep shifting models around looking for a configuration where none of them are engaged. Instead it's check that occurs each time you place a model. If the only position available where the model fits is one where it's engaged with an enemy unit you can place the model there.

Note on this though that the priority is to place models unengaged, if there are two positions available and one of them is closer to the transport but would result in the model being engaged you must put the model in the position that doesn't result in the model being engaged.
