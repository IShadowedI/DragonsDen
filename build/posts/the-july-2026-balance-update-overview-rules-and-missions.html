The July 2026 Balance Update: Overview, Rules, and Missions

July 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
The first balance update of 11th Edition is here, and we’re looking at how it impacts all the game’s Factions. This article focuses on core changes to the game and missions.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with an advanced copy of this update. Any rules or points changes are subject to change until they appear on Warhammer Community or the App.

The first big balance update of 11th Edition is here, and it’s pretty hefty. We’ve got core rule clarifications, points changes across every faction, updates to Force Dispositions, even some mission changes.

And it’s good! 11th Edition has launched well, and this set of changes solidly improves it. There are a few points changes that maybe don’t quite land, but mostly? Almost everything is in the right direction, and the game should be even better with this applied.

If you haven’t already, you can check out our run down of the changes to the game’s factions across our standard set of four superfaction articles, linked below.

  • [40K] The July 2026 Balance Updates: Space Marines
  • [40K] The July 2026 Balance Updates: Imperium
  • [40K] The July 2026 Balance Updates: Chaos
  • [40K] The July 2026 Balance Updates: Xenos

The rest of this article will cover changes to the core rules and a few updates to missions, plus highlight some changes that appear across enough factions that they almost feel like general changes. This is the stuff that affects everyone, and there’s lots of good clarifications and quality of life improvements to enjoy. Let’s dig in.

Common Faction Changes

There are five things that change across a lot of factions, so update your assumptions accordingly:

  1. Reposition effects get fixed to new 11th Edition terminology, and all of those from 10th Edition that force the unit to return in the next turn can be used in the first turn of the battle, overriding the normal rules that stop you Ingressing in this case.
  2. Most units that previously had flat -1 to Hit (or applied it as a Leader effect) now have Stealth at range, and -1 to hit in melee. Most of these units were costed high in the first MFM to account for how powerful stacking -1 to Hit with Cover was, but it’s clearly been decided that this was too swingy against some factions. Most of them come back down in price to compensate. Worth saying that Stratagems applying -1 to Hit do still work, and remain very powerful.
  3. Dedicated Transports are back to having to start the battle with units within them, and a huge number of them get 10pts cuts to compensate. Running empty Dedicated Transports wasn’t super common, so this is generally helpful to all armies with good options here.
  4. Stratagems that revive a Character now revive a Leader/Support model outside of their Attached unit, ensuring that if you Precision out a revivable Character, you haven’t wasted your time. 
  5. The wording on all Enhancements that provide extra Leader/Support choices has been updated to remove the 10th Edition wording for how you attach, and the extra units they can lead are now specified in the Munitorum Field Manual. Please stop panicking! They’re still there! Your cool toys still work!

Now onto the actual changes.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Rules Changes

A lot of these were already trailed by the Tacoma event FAQ that was released by Games Workshop ahead of their showcase event, covering ways to read some rules that still used 10th Edition terminology. 

Some of these have changed in Faction Packs, but where there’s a very standard mapping, they’re now properly incorporated into the core rules. 

Updates from 10th

For generalised updates from 10th, we have the following:

  • Tank Shock and Grenade Stratagems: Any rule that refers to these is now assumed to refer to the new Crushing Impact and Explosives Stratagems instead. Pretty obvious, but worth writing down.
  • Desperate Escape and Hazardous: Any rule that specifies that an enemy unit falling back must take Desperate Escape Tests now forced them to take both Hazardous rolls and a battle-shock test, same as if they’d selected the Desperate Escape fall back mode. In addition, if these rules apply a modifier to “Desperate Escape Tests”, it means the Hazardous rolls.
  • Any rule that refers to being in range of an Objective Marker applies if you are within a Terrain Objective.
  • We also bring back a few of the general Balance Dataslate rules from 10th back, ensuring that nothing slips through the net in terms of general updates. These are:
    • Stratagems preventing units being shot outside 12” apply this at 18” instead.
    • Only effects that name a specific Stratagem let you use a Stratagem multiple times in a phase.
    • Only effects that name a specific Stratagem let you use a Stratagem for 0CP, otherwise they reduce the cost by 1CP instead.
    • Any Stratagem that adds a new unit to your army identical to a destroyed unit can only be used once per game.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Updates and Clarifications

We also have some changes and clarifications to 11th Edition, covering some burning questions people have had since day one. We’ve grouped a few of these that fit together. 

We haven’t covered absolutely everything, because some things were common sense, so do check out the FAQ section in the App yourself if you’re dealing with particularly ornery rules lawyers (unlike the blessed, ethical rules lawyers of the Ruleshammer team), as if they are trying to argue that a unit on the second floor of a ruin is somehow not within a terrain feature (picking an example), it might have been answered!

Attached Units and Enhancements

Good news for Space Wolves players - you can take Upgrades on a unit with an Epic Hero in it. This is included as an FAQ question, and the wording on Muster Armies has been updated to specify that it’s Epic Hero models that cannot take upgrades. The only sadness here is for Sororitas, as Hagiomnifex is changed to be applied to a Character model, so still cannot be used with Vahl or other Epic Heroes.

It is also clarified that yes, Upgrades are a type of Enhancements, so you cannot take both an Enhancement and an Upgrade in an Attached Unit.

Stratagem Costs

Three more things on Stratagem costs in addition to the import of the 10th Edition balance rules:

  • If you modify the cost of an opponent’s Stratagem such that they can no longer afford it, they don’t spend the CP, but the Stratagem does still count as being used this phase, so cannot just be activated again.
  • Conversely, if they can afford it, they must continue with the activation, even if they don’t like the new price.
  • Stratagems with modal costs like Heroic Intervention get a clarification that the modal cost is not a modifier - it sets the base cost for the Stratagem. This prevents things getting funky when interacting with modifiers to costs.

Weapon Abilities

A few tweaks here.

  • First up, Devastating Wounds can now have keyword restrictions in-line, so Devastating Wounds: Monster/Vehicle only applies against (as you might guess) Monsters and Vehicles.
  • Next, Torrent weapons can never have Precision or Indirect Fire. You cannot no-scope someone with a flamethrower, and are politely asked to stop trying.
  • Finally, it’s clarified that Precision and Devastating Wounds work together as you’d expect - the Mortal Wounds go onto the Character you’ve picked for Precision allocation.

Heal

The rules for healing have been updated to say that they can never add a CHARACTER model back to a unit, and Reanimation Protocols is updated to use this so you can no longer bring back a Precisioned-out Technomancer, or whatever.

Dedicated Transports

As trailed above, these have to start with passengers embarked again. Also on the transport theme, there’s a rule added for the vanishingly unlikely scenario that you try and return models to an embarked unit that would exceed its capacity - it doesn’t work.

Attacks that Change During the Attack Sequence

Closing off a genuine loophole next, a very small number of residual rules from 10th Edition can change the characteristics of an attack during the attack sequence (e.g. +1 AP on a Critical Wound). When this happens, the current pool of attacks being processed gets split in two, and these are then resolved the rest of the way through in turn, in the order of the attacking player’s choice. I suspect the plan is to never write rules like this again, but it’s nice for the ones we do have to function clearly.

FLY, Heavy and Vertical Distance

One treat for the cleverest kids out there - vertical distance moved when you Take to the Skies doesn’t count towards the 3” limit for Heavy, so if you’re under a ruin floor, you might be able to pop up and get both your Plunging Fire and Heavy bonuses. There aren’t that many units which benefit, but Marine Suppressors or Drukhari Scourges can potentially have some fun.

Objective Consolidation

When a unit uses the Objective Consolidation mode, they must end their move Unengaged. This handles the gap in the rules that existed previously where you could, for example, be within 4” of an enemy unit (so no eligible for Engaging Consolidation), but reach Engagement via objective Consolidation, after which they wouldn’t get to attack. This closes that loophole, and does create a bit of edge case strategic value you can squeeze out of this step as the active player. If you have a Consolidation-eligible unit on an objective your opponent is hoping to consolidate onto, it might be possible to move them to be just outside 3” of that enemy, at which point their engagement range will stop the foe being able to move onto the point. I’m not totally clear on why this is the fix rather than changing the range on Engaging Consolidation to 5” - that feels more natural.

Objective Control Timing

Control of objectives is now determined as the first thing that happens in the End of Phase step of each Phase. This means that Stratagems that secure an objective at the end of a phase work as intended. It also prevents Necrons from using Reanimation Protocols to claim objectives, as that happens at the end of the phase (most other Command Phase revivals happen earlier, so are unaffected). It does also have a niche impact that if you Rapid Ingress or Heroic Intervention onto an objective, that won’t flip control until the end of the next phase, which can impact on abilities that provide re-rolls for or against units on Objectives they control. I don’t feel like this is quite there yet - the logic of making the objective secured effects work is sound, but the interaction with Ingress/Heroic is sufficiently counter-intuitive that it’ll annoy people. I would suggest that control is determined at the start of the end of phase step, and then again at the end if level of control changes during that step.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Mission Changes

First up, the rule stopping you gaining more than one additional CP per battle round is now included in the singles mission pack as well as the teams one. Panic over.

Next, it is made clear that End of the Battle scoring doesn’t count towards your scoring cap in the fifth battle round. This can be a powerful comeback tool for Dispositions with Primary Missions that award this. Notably, this does not apply to any of the Secondaries in the current deck - while some (e.g. Beacon) that normally score at the end of the Opponent’s turn have an alternate timing for the fifth Battle Round, they score at the end of the fifth Battle Round, so would still fall into the cap.

Finally, we’ve got some mission changes. Nothing on scoring for now, but eight maps get a shakeup. Six of these are for Purge the Foe, specifically against Take & Hold and Disruption, where the central objectives have been split into two rather than being a single giant block. 

In both of these matchups, the non-Purge player has to control a Central Objective at the end of their turn for their Primary, while Purge is rewarded for killing foes off objectives. With just the single central point, some armies facing Purge just found their central objective pieces getting churned through turn after turn, with not much counterplay. This allows scoring while being much more cautious, forcing more proactive play from the Purge side to get their points. This feels like it was solidly needed for the Disruption matchup, less certain on Take and Hold. Disruption detachments and objectives tend to more of an MSU/glass cannon style, so I can see why this was a struggle, but armies building for Take and Hold should have something that can push onto the centre and soak up violence, that’s part of the challenge!

Reconnaissance vs. Disruption also gets two of its maps changed, and this looks more like a specific fix to the maps rather than a balance thing. Map A’s mid-board becomes a little less of a thunderdome, letting both sides be more cagey, while on map C, the sightlines from the centre towards the corners are reduced by moving a small terrain piece, again allowing for more of a guerilla skirmish from the two Dispositions that tend to favour this. I will put my hands up and say I haven’t played this mission, but both look pretty sensible on paper.

It’s cool to see this actively considered as a lever to work with, and for scrutiny of how the maps are playing out. Hopefully more of this, but not too often so our poor dev team don’t sink into despair as we keep Tabletop Battles in sync with the measurements.

Impact

Wings: It’s good, I reckon. Nuclear hot take here, but it’s been a busy day in Warhammer land. As ever, there are probably a few factions that skate by a bit too softly (Orks and some Marines, maybe Necrons), and there are a few unit buffs that are a bit questionable, but I’m overwhelmingly positive on this, and if we get something of this quality for the next few months, we’ll be in a fantastic spot. I like them taking bold swings with things like the map updates and changing detachment dispositions too, though I do have huge sympathy for any poor WTC teams that are now having to scramble to change their composition, rather than just handle point changes. They suffer so that we can prosper.

Curie: Generally a good update - this captures nearly every open question from the last few weeks I’ve had to field from players. The lack of clarification on how Devastating Wounds and damage modifiers interact is a bit troubling, as I certainly hope that the intent isn’t to let Devastating Wounds bypass those mods. For player and TO sanity I would say that for now devastating wounds should behave like normal damage and be modified before conversion to mortal wounds (same as we experienced in 10th edition). So far the approach of “there’s a gap here, let’s presume it works like 10th” has worked well enough (see the latest update on Devastating Wounds and Precision). Otherwise most of these changes are going to be very good for the health of the game.

Rob: Yeah this is is fine. There's a lot of good and necessary improvements here but I'm not sure why they've left things like the objective consolidate as an unintuitive mess. But everything else is fine. 11th wasn't the mess 10th was when it released but there were definitely a lot of warts where they didn't properly think about how the new rules overlaid the old. I'm glad those have been largely solved.
