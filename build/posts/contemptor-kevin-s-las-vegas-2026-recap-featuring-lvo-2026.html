Contemptor Kevin's Las Vegas 2026 Recap (Featuring LVO 2026)

October 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer

One of the drawbacks to living in the Greater Washington D.C. Metropolitan Area is the summer.  Summers in D.C. are known for rather disgusting humidity, to the point where the United States Congress takes August off because for the majority of the first 200 years of the country the capital was too miserable to be in.   Luckily, America has an answer to the humidity of the capital: the dry desert on the other side of the country!  And in that desert, we have Sin City: Las Vegas Nevada.  While hot and brutally sunny, the casino megaresorts are cool and pleasant.  And gaming companies have noticed!  Which is why I, along with RagnarokAngel and many others, went to the LVO 2026.  

But!  The LVO was not my *only* gaming and hobby event to Vegas in 2026.  The LVO is now in October, and you many have noticed that I was talking about August.  Well, in early August of 2026 I decided to leave a particularly rain-hammered DC to go to Vegas for STLV, the convention formerly known as Star Trek Las Vegas.  There, Wizards of the Coast would be having a presentation with Star Trek Strange New Worlds’ Jess Bush for the upcoming Universes Beyond: Star Trek set.  They also were offering demos of the set.   The convention also featured hobby classes.  By going to Vegas early I would *also* be able to scope out the hotel Alice and I were staying at and getting to enjoy certain aspects of Vegas I would not have time to hit while at LVO.  

Part 1: Contemptor Kevin’s Quantum Subspace Tunnel to STLV

STLV is held in the former home of the LVO, the Rio Casino and Resorts.  The good news is that the hotel has been undergoing extensive renovations since January of 2025.  This means that many better amenities from the Rio still existed.  However, the hotel was sold out for part of the time I would be in Vegas.  I therefore decided to get a jump on the hotel that Alice and I would be staying at for LVO: The Excalibur Casino and Resort.  The Excalibur is next door to where we stayed in 2025, and so the two of us had hit the hotel for food and drink a few times last year.  However, since we would be staying there for almost a week, I wanted to make sure the Excalibur was sufficient for our needs.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

Reader, the Excalibur was an excellent hotel to stay in.  It had a relatively low cost, was pretty easy to navigate through, and had a large selection of various restaurants, stores, and other amenities from which to enjoy our stay.  They also had $3 margaritas.  We stayed in the refurbished Resort Tower.  This was very close to the gym and not a bad walk from either the main entrance or the rideshare entrance.  The only downside to the room was an insufficient number of outlets for all the technology I needed to plug in and charge.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

This year for STLV, Wizards of the Coast decided to debut the upcoming Universes Beyond: Star Trek set for Magic: The Gathering with a full-court press.  The ticket level that I purchased came with one of the welcome decks - sadly, I got the Black welcome deck.  This was disappointing because none of the actors who featured in the set were at the con so I could not get autographed cards this year.  At the Con, they also had a room dedicated to Magic: The Gathering demonstrations.  So I decided to roll up my sleeves, and do something I hadn’t done in 30 years: Play Magic.

I played against a nice fellow from WOTC named Chris, and I played the blue starter deck, since I like Mr. Spock.  While this game was not particularly complicated (as Magic goes), many of the main concepts from Magic have not changed in thirty years.  My deck shuffling was suboptimal, so I wound up drawing a lot of lands to start off with.  This meant that before my life ran out, I drew some big creatures for defense before making my big push.  I won, with two life left.  Hooray!  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

One of the last major panels for STLV was a discussion between Gavin Verhey and Jess Bush (the actor who plays Nurse Christine Chapel on Star Trek: Strange New Worlds).  Ms. Bush was told stories about how Ethan Peck (Mr. Spock) got her into playing Magic, and was thus given the opportunity to reveal the card based off of her.  We also got to see Ms. Bush twerk it to the Star Trek: The Next Generation theme.  WOTC also handed out a convention exclusive Captain Kirk card - sadly, I was unable to get mine autographed by William Shatner since I did not get a Shatner autograph ticket.  Mr. Verhey also explained that all the Lower Decks-themed cards in the set were of common or uncommon rarity, because they cannot get promoted.

The other hobby-related activity I did at STLV was to go into the room that used to be LVO’s Hobby Hangout and paint.  Creation has started to pay a fellow named Ed Mueller to go to their conventions and host a painting class slash hobby room.  One can get their pictures taken at a slow photo booth with digital inserts of Star Trek cast members, while there was also a jewelry making station.  The centerpiece was a Bob Ross-style painting class, because Mr. Mueller is a certified Bob Ross painter.  The class I took was on using a stencil to paint a canvas with Star Trek ships.  First, we painted a starscape, then selected a stencil outline of a Star Trek ship.  I selected the USS Enterprise E, as that one is my favorite of the TNG-era Enterprise designs.  My goal was to finish my Enterprise painting over the weekend, and luckily I’ve taken enough miniature painting classes to finish.  I had to dive into the vendor’s hall a couple of time to reference some large models of the Enterprise E to get the pattern right, but ultimately I was happy with the outcome.  

The other stuff I did at the convention was 1) shop and 2) meet and greet various Star Trek actors.  Indeed, I’ve never been able to meet the geniuses behind so many Star Trek characters all at once.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

STLV was a very fun convention, and a great way to get my appetite whetted for the Las Vegas Open.  While I did wind up having friends in Vegas at the time, I would not realize that my one Warhammer friend was in town until we met back up at LVO.  LVO was therefore set up as my other big vacation for 2026.

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman. The first model I ever saw and where my road to TTB started.

Part 2: The Continuing Adventures of Kevin and Alice

Credit: Alice "RagnarokAngel" Lirette

Alice and I decided early on to get into Vegas early so that we could spend more time at Area 15.  Last year, we wanted to do the new Interstellar Arc.  However, Interstellar Arc would not open until a week after we left Vegas, so that was put at the top of the list for 2026.  There were a plethora of other activities at Area 15 that we wanted to do (the Museum of Ice Cream, the Escape Game franchise, and various food and drink).  Last time we went to Area 15 the two of us were exhausted, so we made sure to hit this first.  

I got in Tuesday night, and immediately went with Alice and her friend Rey to the Venetian for dinner.  Why go from one end of the strip to another?  Because the Venetian has a food court with a Howlin’ Rays franchise.  Howlin’ Rays is a Pasadena-based hot chicken restaurant with huge and excellent chicken tenders.  I have been to their Pasadena location a couple of times in the past, but the Pasadena experience tends to be: 1) During Pokémon Go Fest Tour (so I want to be out catching Pokémon and doing various Pokémon raids) and 2) Require me to spend an hour or so waiting in line.  While GoFest Tour is in late February, Pasadena is pretty sunny and so it’s warm.  This means to avoid sunburn and the phones overheating, I have to huddle under an umbrella.  By contrast, the Venetian location has no line.  I just went up, ordered my tenders and beer, and enjoyed.  

Wednesday morning, after I finished working out, Alice and I Ubered to a local Whole Foods in order to pick up an order of various items we needed delivered: power strips, Monster Zero, Ramen (for Alice to avoid paying hotel snack prices), and various food containers.  Next to the Whole Foods in Vegas was a very large and comprehensive gaming store called Knight and Day Games.  The staff at Knight and Day games, despite it being a random Wednesday morning at 10:00 am, were quite friendly and enthusiastic.  Alice snagged a Magic booster box, while I picked up some primer and additional Ultramarines upgrades for my army.  We also spied the under-construction paint racks for Warhammer Tone Pro.  

Afterwards, we met up with Rey and hit Area 15.  After lunch, we went through the Interstellar Arc.  This is akin to a VR-based exploration game, where we are learning about an attempt in the far future to colonize another planet in an ecologically sound manner.  Beyond the fact that it was quite lovely, it also had a lot to discover.  I only managed about a 67% completion of all the secrets of the Arc, while Alice was able to get up to the 80s.  Next time, I want to come back and see if I can’t get a higher completion percentage.  

The second experience we did was the Museum of Ice Cream.  This involved us having to come up with what ice cream flavor identified us (I was “Calgar Creamcicle”), learning the secrets of toppings, and then ultimately going to the unlimited ice cream bar.  I was a bit disappointed in the ice cream bar because of a lack of peanut butter flavored ice cream.   We also went down various slides to land in giant plastic sprinkles.  Surprisingly safe!  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

The third experience we did was Alice and I’s first escape room of the trip: The Escape Game’s Prison Break scenario.  I very much enjoyed this room and the unique mechanics behind it.  We were able to escape with 19 seconds left to spare, because we wound up brute forcing a couple of puzzles we were not supposed to brute force.  Still, the theme was fun and the puzzles ingenious.  While in retrospect I would have gone with a different room (that’s unique to Vegas) versus Prison Break (which I can’t do with my home crew), it was still a fun experience.  

Credit: The gamemaster at the Escape Game Area 15

Afterwards, Alice and Rey wanted to hit up the John Wick experience while I had something of an upset stomach and decided to go back to the room and go to bed early.  After the three weeks leading up to the event, I was tired! 

Part 3: Thursday LVO Day One and Kevin’s Gundam Assemble Adventure

Having gone to bed early, I woke up early and worked out.  After I showered, I dragooned Alice and Rey to the Buffet and then the LVO so we could pick up our badges and swag bags.  Unfortunately, they were not able to get into the John Wick experience the night before.  They decided to skip the initial day of LVO to instead go to John Wick and drink.  I got to see my friend Phil from the 2025 Masterclass, and chat with him for awhile.  We’ve met up at the intervening Adepticons, NoVas, and LVOs, and once again we were both doing masterclasses.  I was in Dan Ozborne’s class and he and Nick were taking Jan Soukup's Oils class.  

This year, I had gone with both the High Roller package and the Masterclass package.  Unlike previous conventions where I was handed a bag brimming with miniatures, the vendor swag portion was available through a series of tickets were could take to various booths.  Phil explained to me that was how other conventions (specifically, DefCon) handled attendee swag.  This way we would be encouraged to travel to different booths instead of just having a bunch of random odds and ends in a bag.  Normally at a gaming convention, the 40K booth tends to be the biggest booth and biggest draw. And this year, GW did have their big event store and a table where folks could spend a few minutes trying out the Tone Pro demo kit.  But as Rob pointed out in his two recaps of the LVO, the belle of the LVO ball was Gundam Assemble.

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

Bandai went all-out for the event, with multiple demo tables, photo opportunities, a mini-golf course, and a “scavenger hunt.”  And I made it my quest to do as much as I could on that initial day.  After quickly securing the starter set, I secured time cards to do both the paint demonstration and the gaming demonstration.  I knew absolutely nothing about the game when I did the demo, and found myself pleasantly surprised at the game flow.  The demo was, for the most part, relatively scripted:  We were told which actions to take, with some of the power ups being random and the dice rolls being random.  I liked the pacing of the game, which means I’m not stuck waiting for awhile for my turn.  In the demo, I did not feel as if I had much agency during my opponent’s turn (no saving dice, for example), but we also only had one use of tactics cards in the demo.  I played the Federation side of the board, which let me use the RX-78 Gundam and crush Char and his Zakus.  At the beginning of LVO the demo tables were packed.  Towards the end of the first day, folks were able to just walk up and play.  

My Masterclass pals Nick and Phil. Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

The Scavenger Hunt had four stations: the Lobby photo booth/Gunplay demo, a station at the far end of the convention center lobby (where you had to play a round of mini-golf), a station just inside the convention hall, and a station at the booth.  Every time you completed one of the scavenger quests, you got a new round of flair to add to your badge.  At the end, you received a raffle ticket to win a Gundam Assemble starter set.  Spoiler alert:  I did not win.  As I already had a starter set, this was not a huge disappointment.  

The most interesting part, for me, was the painting demonstration/opportunity.  While the booth was offering promo versions of the three Federation mechs (Guntank, Guncannon, and Gundam RX-78) in yellow plastic for High Rollers and for following them on Instagram, the painting demo was handing out three Zakus: Char’s Zaku, a Zaku II Linebreaker (IIRC the one with the cannon), and Zaku II Enforcer (the one with the axe).  I got handed a Zaku II Linebreaker.  While I am not the first TTB author to get one of these, or even the first TTB author named Kevin to get one of these, I believe I’m the first author to use the new Mr. Hobby Gundam Assemble Color.  

The models were supplied to us on-sprue, and unfortunately for me this was the one time in 2026 where I was at a wargaming event without my hobby supply bag.  I had left it back in the room!  I used the provided clippers and some emery boards I had in my backpack to try and smooth the surface of the Zaku.  After assembling the model, it was time to paint it.  We had a few of the paints: Gundam Assemble Color NGA-05 Black, NGA-04 Red, NGA-08 Light Green, and NGA-45 Dark Green.  I think we might have also had NGA-43 Steel, but I never wound up using it.  These come in screw-top bottles, and do not have internal mixing balls.

I decided to use NGA Black as the base coat, and I believe I made a terrible mistake.  While the paint was able to stick to the plastic relatively fine, the black was very glossy.  Trying to figure out where shapes on the model began or ended was very challenging in the harsh convention center lighting.  The paint was also extremely slow to dry and painting more than one color onto the model in the limited time I had was a struggle.  I was able to get both NGA Light Green and Dark Green onto the model, but I was not very impressed with those initial results.  The booth was also handing out free bottles of NGA White and NGA Blue if you followed them on YouTube.  While I did not have the change to play with them in Vegas, I did once I got home:

The White has decent coverage.  While it doesn't have the same smooth feel as Pro Acryl's Bold Titanium White, my initial tests have the two compare pretty similarly.  Both are very bright, though I felt the NGA White did not want to be thinned down very much.  Sadly, at the time of writing I did not have the opportunity to directly test this against Warhammer Paint Blackstone Void 5 White.  

Left to Right, Top to Bottom: WH Paint Ulthuan Grey, Gundam Assemble Color NGA-01 White, Underlying Vallejo TMM Sapphire Blue, ProAcryl Bold Titanium White. Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

The Blue came out as a dark blue.  It has very good coverage, and is not as purple as Warhammer Paint Macragge Blue.  It is not as bright as Pro Acryl Ultramarine.  It has some similarities with Warhammer Paint Caledor Sky, except maybe a smidge more purple than that.  I used the paint on my Repulsor Executioner's guns for some of the bracketing as the undercoat/shadow color, before highlighting up with Ultramarine and eventually Bold Titanium White.  I was happy with the result.

From left to right, top to bottom: ProAcryl Ultramarine, Gundam Assemble Color NAG-02 Blue, WH Paint Caledor Sky, WH Paint Macragge Blue. Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

In order to paint my Zaku II model at LVO though, I headed over to the Hobby Haven, took my trusty drybrush (a Citadel Medium Drybrush), and asked the Warhammer Tone Pro demo table if I could dab said drybrush in Hierotek Green 3.  While this color did not give me the same drab green that the Zaku II is famous for, the punchy green of Hierotek Green 3 and 5 served to give me a very bold Zaku, and one I was quite proud of speed-painting. 

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

Afterwards, I met up with John “Can you Roll a Crit” Rees to meet him, collect various other High Roller swag bag giveaways, and chat about Gundam Assemble.  He went off to go play the Assemble Demo, while I decided to head back to the hotel and get dinner.  Alice and Rey had returned from their Area 15 adventure, and followed me down into the Excalibur’s food court for food.  I had a good enough but severely overpriced smashburger and fries, then hit the hay.  Tomorrow my Masterclass with Dan Ozborne would begin, and I would have the opportunity to meet new friends, make new friends, and see even more old friends!

Until next time LVOteers!
