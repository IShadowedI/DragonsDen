Tabletop Battles Reviews: Kill Team Exodite Dragon Masters Narrative Content

July 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The new Exodite box for Kill Team contains a load of narrative content. We have the first full explanation of the Exodites as a faction and a huge twenty-mission Joint Ops campaign. There’s also a section talking about how to build a board for your campaign, which would be a significant hobby project all on its own. This article will look over all of this stuff. 

Thanks as ever to Games Workshop for giving us a copy of Exodite to review. We split it between NotThatHenryC, who got the book and cards, and Skyserpent, who got the Exodite models. NotThatHenryC has had a go at building some Maiden World terrain and played a bit of the Joint Ops campaign, while Skyserpent has been reading the lore and painting individual scales on dinosaurs for a long, long time. [Editor's note: Skyserpent will cover the lore in a future article!]

Regrettably this means NotThatHenryC didn't have any Exodites to show you on his Maiden World terrain. Some of his over 25-years-old Rangers and Ork Warbikers will have to do for now.

Maiden World terrain. Credit: NotThatHenryC

What’s in the Box?

Exodite contains a full Exodite Kill Team of three Eldar on dinosaurs and two Dracolithes. You also get a bunch of orks to use as NPOs: ten Beast Snaggas and four Squighog Boyz. 

The dossier is bigger than usual and hard-backed. There are two card decks, one for the Exodites and one for the Joint Ops campaign. You get the usual token sheet and three paper envelopes to track your operatives’ progress in the campaign.

The token sheet contains all the usual stuff you’d expect and also some movement templates. These let you move and pivot your dragon knights with a bit more precision than if you just tried to eyeball 45 degrees.

You also get a pack of cards as usual for a kill team, containing their datacards, stratagems, equipment and so on. This deck includes a card for each of the upgrades your operatives can have.

Exodite Narrative accessories. Credit: NotThatHenryC

There’s another box of cards for the Joint Ops campaign. You get datacards for all the operatives, ork event cards that are the equivalent of NPO strategic ploys. For the Exodites there are battle scars to track their injuries and encounter cards, which are used for side-quests between games. 

Finally we are given three envelopes, one for each of our operatives, to track their progress in the campaign. Any upgrades or battle scars can go in there between games.

Building Your Maiden World Board 

There’s no terrain in the Exodite box but the book contains extensive instructions on building a board, entirely from scratch. 

This came as a big surprise to us. Games Workshop has often provided narrative terrain for Kill Team in the past. They’re about to release an entire pre-painted board for 40K. But here we are asked to build a board out of plasticard, sticks and plastic plastic plants. These are things that Games Workshop doesn’t sell. This is… different. Here's my attempt to do that. I'll talk through what I did.

It’ll be a ton of work getting ready for the Joint Ops campaign. Not only do you have to make and paint the Exodites but you also have the orks to build and a whole board to create. All of this is intended just for this campaign. We aren’t given maps to use it for approved ops or anything like that. This is a very high barrier for entry.

As it happens I had a bunch of plasticard in a drawer, plus a cutting mat, metal ruler and so on. This left me with no excuse but to have a go at building some of this stuff.

There are much easier ways to make this kind of thing. You could probably cut them out of a single sheet of foam board, for quicker but far less durable results. Anyone with a 3D printer could also make something really good, I’m sure. Personally I enjoyed making this stuff by hand. As time-consuming as it was, it was fun making these weird alien shapes.

One other surprise was that these big shape things aren’t really used in any obvious way for the joint ops missions. Those require some big areas of Heavy terrain and some light ovals of rocks and vegetation, not big arch things. I didn’t realise this until after I’d made my first gate thingy, unfortunately. For the subsequent ones I made bases the right size to make up one edge of the Heavy areas. They also give you designs for smaller things called Star Steles, which are a bit more versatile. They’re ideal for marking out the corners of the Heavy areas and can also be put on the smaller Light ovals with some sticks.

Loading...

For my stuff I measured out the plasticard and drew the curves by hand. Now you can download the templates from Warhammer Community and print them. I’d definitely recommend doing that. It’ll be a big help getting all the shapes identical.

You then need to cut a strip of plasticard to fill in all the way round the edges. The templates say 1cm wide but it could be any thickness. I recommend fixing the two sides together first, using some of your edge stuff to create an internal structure. Then you can go round the shape cutting a piece to the length of each edge and sticking it in place. You could also fill in between the sides with something like Miliput.

For the curved edges I got the plasticard into the right shape before sticking it down. If you hold the piece in a curve and then run a paintbrush up the inside you can form it how you need to, hopefully without breaking it in the process. The thinner your plasticard is the easier it’ll be to do this.

There’s a really nice detail on the Star Steles where you cut up plastic tube to make a groove. I made the base of my one a little smaller so that it makes a continuous shape with the groove round part of it.

Loading...

I sprayed these architectural bits with Colour Forge Renegade Green, followed by Montana white marble - which is the weird stringy stuff. This has created quite a decent marble pattern I think. I put a glaze of Waywatcher Green over the whole thing. The groove on the little Star Stele is gold. In the instructions they suggest painting on lines of script (or even carving them into the plasticard) but I thought that would be too hard and time-consuming so I haven’t bothered. I’ll leave that to the 3D printers.

Maiden World terrain. Credit: NotThatHenryC

Currently the bases are just the same marble as the arches themselves and I don’t mind that. I’m not sure whether to leave them like that or add some mud. It might look good to have vines climbing the arches themselves. 

For the actual jungle stuff I cheated, using the old Deathworld Forest Killzone instead of scratch building things. This also provided a perfect Killzone board to play on, covered in nasty looking vines and stuff. Painting the plastic stuff was probably more work than it would have been to paint sticks, now I think about it, but the end result is quite fun.

I'm also using some of my Octarius terrain for parts of the campaign. The smallest scrap pile is about right for one of the light terrain areas they want and the ruins work for the heavy areas, in later scenarios to represent the Orks starting to build stuff. There's a mission where they ask you to build a totem but I'm going to use the drilling rig for that. I imagine my Exodites would be extremely cross if Orks started drilling for oil on a Maiden world, so it'll do the job nicely.

Altogether I am very happy with how this has all come out. I'd like to make a few more of bits of the terrain for some variety but for now this does the job very well. Now all I need are some actual Exodites!

Maiden World terrain. Credit: NotThatHenryC

The Joint Ops Campaign

We’ve had a number of Joint Ops campaigns in the past, some featuring a ladder structure where the results of one game affect the next. None comes close to the Exodite campaign, which is unique in several ways. 

For one thing, Exodite introduces mechanics for developing your Kill Team over the course of the campaign. You start with just the three Exodites from the team without any upgrades or Dracolithes. Over the course of the campaign you’ll collect upgrades and probably end up as powerful a “full” kill team. Your operatives may also pick up injuries if things go badly.

This is a welcome development for Joint Ops. In previous campaigns it has felt a bit odd that your team just reset every game, despite people being eaten by the red terror or something. It’ll be nice watching your team (hopefully) develop as you progress through the campaign. It could also be a good way to learn their mechanics, which will be introduced over time when you earn the cool stuff. 

One disadvantage is this limits this campaign to only the Exodites unless you’re prepared to change it quite a lot. No other team has a comparable upgrade system or dracolithes to add in the same way. That’s probably okay though, given that the campaign comes boxed with all the models you need to play it.

The Exodites are also a team of just three models of course. It won’t be all that easy to divide them up to play through with friends, unless you have exactly two friends. I expect most people will play this solo.

The book actually tells you not to read too far ahead in the campaign due to spoilers, so I don’t want to give too much away here. But basically you get some choices in the path you follow after the Orks' initial invasion, which will give you different missions to complete. The next mission is also determined by the results of each game. These choices give you more re-playability than previous campaigns we’ve had as you can choose a different path each time. 

After each game there’s also an Upkeep process to go through before you can start the next one. There are three stages.

First, anyone who got taken out of action has to roll to see if they get a battle scar (on a 1-3), if they’re not scarred but can’t be picked for the next two stages (4-5) or are fine on a 6. A battle scar takes up the “slot” for an upgrade on your operative, meaning it can’t be used till the scar goes away - which doesn’t matter if you don’t have upgrades yet. An operative that survives a mission can clear a battle scar it previously picked up.

Then you get to give out an upgrade or pick a new piece of equipment for your stash. If you choose an upgrade, the operative who gets it tends to be whoever did best at the mission, meaning you don’t necessarily get to choose the beneficiary. Check the victory conditions before you start, if you want to make the “right” person win. If the winner is unable to be upgraded due to being taken out of action, you lose out.

Finally you have an encounter. You choose one of your eligible operatives - not one who just got hurt - and draw a card to see what happens. Most of these are just a challenge written on the card that you have to do, like building a tall stack of dice. If you succeed you get an upgrade or piece of equipment but you can get a battle scar if you fail. Each operative also has their own special mission to do, which has them go and do a solo game vs NPOs, offering greater rewards if they succeed.

The NPOs

The box contains a full unit of Beast Snaggaz and also some Squighog Boyz. The missions send these at you in various combinations. You’ll quite often have the infantry on the board to start with and then random reinforcements, which can include Squighogs - each counting as two reinforcements. I used a mob of Boyz from Armageddon and some Warbikers for my NPOs.

Maiden World terrain. Credit: NotThatHenryC

In my experience so far the infantry are relatively harmless but you have to take them out to prevent them from doing something bad, like burning down your forest. The Squighogs are much nastier, with a 4/5 melee attack that will hurt you a lot and a surprisingly nasty ranged attack, with P1 and Accurate thanks to the grot riding pillion. They have 20 wounds and follow the same mounted rules as the Exodites. They’re limited to 1APL for each of their two activations though, which means they won’t be able to Fight you if they had to Turn. It’s pretty painful to have a Squighog Rider spawn and charge you before you can get out of the way. 

There’s also a Bomb Squig, similar to the ones we’ve seen before, but it isn’t part of the normal reinforcement table and I haven’t met one yet.

In the back of the book you get rules for running a Beastboss on Squigosaur. If you keep winning your games there’s an ever-increasing risk of this 40-wound nightmare turning up and ruining your day.

There are also rules for a couple of NPOs that are specific to the scenarios, like an “enraged beast” that can show up and start indiscriminately eating Orks and Exodides at random. This lets you play a three-player game all by yourself. Luckily for me, my kids have an extensive collection of toy dinosaurs, so I was easily able to find a velociraptor of just the right size for this.

Clever girl! Credit: NotThatHenryC

Final Thoughts

As a narrative product, the Exodite box is very interesting. This is a properly involved solo campaign that someone at GW has put a ton of work into. Unfortunately you’ll also have to do a lot of work to get ready to play it. If you enjoy the craft side of the hobby then this is absolutely brilliant. If you don’t, then this is a solo play experience so if you choose to just play on Volkus or Octarius, or with cut up cereal boxes, nobody will know.

It’s a real shame they didn’t make the Maiden World into a true killzone that was suitable for every team to play on. Instead we have essentially an open board with no levels to climb. That makes sense for the Exodites, who can’t climb up things, but would be a bit boring for normal games. A scratch-built killzone would offer a different way into playing Kill Team, cheaper but more laborious.

If you do manage to prepare all the stuff, or perhaps proxy things in, the campaign will be a really nice “choose your own adventure” experience. Having only three operatives is perfect for a RPG-style game. They’re all individuals who will get injuries and experience, probably with some fun anecdotes to go with them. This wouldn’t have worked so well for a team with more operatives or where you swapped people in and out each game. You’d have had too much book keeping or lost that sense of continuity.

Unfortunately it looks like very few people are going to get to play the Exodite campaign. Production seems to be very limited, perhaps because of the factories all churning out 40k starter sets, so my FLGS is only getting a few. I don’t know if I’ll actually be able to get hold of a proper copy. Previous Joint Ops mission packs have been uploaded to the app and Warhammer Community but so far this one hasn’t. Realistically if you’ve read this far then, if you haven’t secured your set already, it may well be sold out. Hopefully the dossier will go on sale on its own soon.
