Magic: The Gathering Marvel Super Heroes Review, Part 3 of 4: White and Blue Cards

June 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
This week we tune in to watch all of your favorite Marvel heroes enter the realm of Magic, presumably with a mandatory Excelsior! In this episode, Magic players are being introduced to a swath of new cards as their favorite Heroes and Villains duke it out with the power of friendship and dastardly plots. In this article we’ll talk about the white and blue cards from the set, and offer some thoughts on what they mean for Commander and other formats.

Marvel Super Heroes will release to Magic: the Gathering Online and Arena on June 23rd, and to the tabletop on June 26th. Commander: Marvel Super Heroes will release on the same date.

White

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Agent Maria Hill

FromTheShire: This is a possible 60 card format sleeper. Right now the teamwork cards aren't overwhelming good but turning Helicarrier Strike into 1 mana kill something, get a counter, and draw a card is very nice. Go Nuts!, HULK SMASH!, and Team Tactics become very interesting as well.

Saffgor: Unfortunately, a lack of repeatable Teamwork cards keeps this from the Command Zone, and I don't really see a Commander whose 99 would play this, for what is largely a limited-locked mechanic.

BPhillipYork: Utility creature that meshes with the set mechanic in white. To be honest to me this is nigh unplayable unless you've got a re-usable teamwork cost on a creature, maybe your commander, in which case it becomes a quite useful, but there aren't, and there just isn't enough teamwork, so she isn't.

Loxi: Very solid turn 1 play for limited, but yeah probably not going to see much commander play.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Avenger Assemble

FromTheShire: The flash and the end step trigger are at odds here which makes for a bit of a strange card. You basically need to be holding up 5 mana to possibly do something else at instant speed and then do this if you don't need to, or else you already want to cast this pre combat to get your anthem and your draw in which case the flash doesn't matter. Usually for 5 mana in a typal deck I'm looking for something like Coat of Arms to drop and end the game right away, not make myself look threatening while also leaving everyone alive.

Saffgor: 5 mana for this only becomes worthwhile once you're getting 2-3 cards out of it each turn, and that requires ample Flash, blink, or other means of forcing Heroes to enter outside of your turn. Not a fan.

BPhillipYork: If you can flicker a creature repeatedly, like on each players turn, this becomes amazing card draw. Bunch of flash Heroes or something like that, decently strong. Also a solid way to survive an Earthquake or something like that. So if you're playing Hero-typal it's a fine card.

Loxi: Flash salvages this a bit, but you definitely need to make sure you're getting your money's worth on the card draw here. Anthems are good, but sometimes these general value cards don't quite cut it when modern commander has so many explosive things you can do when you start casting higher curve spells.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain America, Super-Soldier

FromTheShire: This feels very on theme and you'll be happy to slam it in your Heroes deck. Giving your team hexproof is obviously great, and you would be surprised how many annoying spells and abilities actually target you once you start paying attention to it.

Saffgor: It goes in Hero typal, and it's fine there, but I think the type itself lacks the sauce in Monowhite for him in the Command Zone.

BPhillipYork: Hexproof is super strong, and yes they could pretty easily be pinged off of him but this really turns into a 2 for 1 and most people aren't going to bother. A 3/2 for 3 with first strike is fine, could easily see him showing up in Human or Soldier decks.

Loxi: More of a support piece than a build around, I think even if you're just running a one-off hero as your commander this can be a decent inclusion just for the sake of double protection.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain America, Wings of Freedom

FromTheShire: At least it has flying built in so it doesn't die the first time you swing with it. Asking you to both go wide with Heroes and also have a few Equipments attached in order to get any kind of significant buff seems a little unrealistic, although there is certainly an Equipment matters subtheme with them so maybe you're happy to give 2-3 other guys +5/+5 with a couple of Swords attached for instance. Heroes is also still a pretty thin type so this may just generically synergize well enough that it beats out a bunch of other useless creatures.

Saffgor: Certainly a more interesting Monowhite Hero Commander, with all the Toughness-boosting Equipment and Hero token generators from Final Fantasy. Surprisingly, this speaks to me as a far better option than the Super-Soldier above, in spite of being far simpler to deploy. Champions from Beyond, eat your heart out.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty funny with his shield that gives a massive toughness boost on him, which I guess is the intent? Solid enough buffer for Hero typal and not much else.

Loxi: This was tailor made to do crazy stuff with Captain America's Shield, and that rules. That being said, basically any other artifacts that boost up your toughness here could make for a neat hero-supporting voltron commander that can still threaten wins over the top with commander damage.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Marvel, Earth's Protector

FromTheShire: A whole lot of these power-up cards feel underwhelming to me. It's neat but I don't think losing first strike and gaining flash makes Baneslayer Angel playable in current formats. And you really want to be casting her for 7 anyway because that full power-up cost just for an indestructible counter isn't worth it.

Saffgor: Boring as sin, who's playing a trumped-up Baneslayer Angel in 2026?

BPhillipYork: The 5 power flash is sort of ok, but she doesn't really do anything. Not a great beater, not an enabler, just... basically a non-Angel angel.

Loxi: Listen, I don't care about this really in commander. Where this really shines is as a limited bomb - Flying and Lifelink with a way to protect itself on your top end? Nice, solid stuff.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Mar-Vell, Space-Born

FromTheShire: This on the other hand is pretty great in Commander. As with all flash enablers you have to spend more than you would like and go shields down for a turn but if you untap with it being able to play on whoever's turn you would like is extremely powerful. It's more fragile than Vedalken Orrery or Leyline of Anticipation by virtue of being on a creature, but getting another copy in a deck that wants the effect is totally worth it.

Saffgor: Okay now this is interesting. It's a tad expensive, but the fact it operates as a Flash enabler in the Command Zone is rather neat, and could make for a cool piece of a control strategy.

BPhillipYork: This is a fun way to get flash, kind of unique in that aspect. The 4/4 flying and vigilance is kind of meh, but solid with Avengers Assemble. Really just another enabler for Hero typal I guess. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Colleen Wing, Street Samurai

FromTheShire: This feels like it's a great roleplayer if a Repartee and/or Teamwork deck emerges or if mono-Red aggro pivots back to Boros and not going to see much play otherwise.

Saffgor: Impact is a bit low, here. Limited card for sure.

BPhillipYork: This is real solid with prowess decks, if you're doing a pump and rush Boros deck it's pretty solid. Other than that, not much impact. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Nick Fury, Agent of S.H.I.E.L.D.

FromTheShire: Fury seems fun if a bit limited if played fairly. Mainly he is here to helm a 5 color Superfriends deck in the more traditional sense and occasionally spin for some extra value. Or to be played as the only 1 mana 5 color commander in cEDH to give you access to maximum goodstuff.

Saffgor: As above, this is already seeing experimentation in cEDH as a 1 mana 5c Commander at worst, and a mana outlet to find win conditions once you have infinite mana and a sac outlet. For his purpose as a superfriends Commander, or outside of a cEDH context? I doubt it's worthwhile.

BPhillipYork: Well a 1 cost 5 color commander is a new thing, and dumping out a fattie is solid, but only looking at the top 7 is a bit rough. And you can only do it once, unless you can flicker him (or just blow him up and commander him back out) I guess he's the obvious default commander for 5-color Hero typal, which is, fine. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Night Nurse, Healer of Heroes

FromTheShire: Lots of uses for this ranging from general recursion to self mill selection to even general ramp purposes with fetch lands or the like. And it's an enters trigger so blinking it continues to pay off as well.

Saffgor: Samwise the Stouthearted, but you trade a tempt for Lifelink. Could be decent in 60-card, but I actually prefer tempt for Commander.

BPhillipYork: This is not good, like, you'd almost certainly rather prevent your creature from dying unless it's some sort of aristocrats deck and you are sacrificing wanting to get it back from your yard, in which case it's an okay-ish value piece. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Origin of the Avengers

Saffgor: A slow cantrip+board buff at worst, and more mana than it costs at best. If a Hero deck works in Standard, this will be in it.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty decent 2-cost Saga.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Political Triumph

FromTheShire: Nothing crazy but 1 mana for 4 scries and a counter for whatever creatures stick feels like pretty solid value.

Saffgor: Listen, Wedding Announcement was a Standard staple in its day, and this (while not exactly the same) has a similar play pattern. It fixes draws and cycles through, although there's far less value if you're already behind.

BPhillipYork: I like the way this works, since it lets you drop it on an otherwise dead turn then start dropping creatures. That being said, I don't think we really needed a new type of enchantment that there's going to be like 8 of and only matter for one set (bring back blocks). Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Super-Soldier Serum

FromTheShire: Attaching any number of Equipment you control on attack is extremely powerful, and the +2/+2, first strike, and vigilance is probably worth the 2 mana investment all on its own. Obviously you also have to get said Equipment into play but one of the main balancing levers for them is the Equip cost, so cheating something like a Colossus Hammer onto your creature is a proven strategy. Like there was a whole Modern deck about it. I don't think this brings said Hammer time back there but in Commander especially look out when this hits the field.

Saffgor: Seems like a standout in Voltron strategies, and to be quite honest +2/+2 and two fantastic combat keywords is solid rate for 2 mana regardless. Pushed card for the right home.

BPhillipYork: This is a really solid piece for Boros (or other white containing) Equipment decks. The ability to attach a bunch of Equipment quickly for free is pretty brutal, and there are creatures that will instantly blow people out this way.

Loxi: I've played a ton of equipment decks, and anything that helps bypass equip costs is worth a shout. Extra points to stuff like Sram, Senior Edificer that also has some passing synergy with auras.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Super Villain Lockup

FromTheShire: This is going to be an interesting case study in where we are at power creep wise. Back in Dominaria Standard Seal Away saw heavy play, but with how fast the game is today can it still hang or will this former staple be relegated to Limited All-Star?

BPhillipYork: Oh okay it's this, cheap 2-3 ish enchantment that exiles until it goes away.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Mind Stone

FromTheShire: An Infinity Stone is powerful, news at 11. Does your deck play creatures with enters abilities? Would you like to cast said creatures ahead of curve? Then this goes in your deck.

Saffgor: Annoying this exists, and these exist, because essentially all Monocolor decks will be champing at the bit for these over Arcane Signet. It's not even insane, but the opportunity cost is so low that it doesn't have to be.

BPhillipYork: For 8 mana that is a frankly a weak effect. It's fine as a like, mana-rock with upside if the game goes long, since it's an enters untapped 2 cost mana rock that generates colored mana. Really solid there, but the sort of "show off" ability is disappointing.

Loxi: I've mentioned this previously with the other stones, but yeah I'm with Saffgor. The opportunity cost for this is basically the floor, so if you have any bounce synergy at all these are going to be worth a look if you're optimizing.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Sentry, Golden Guardian

FromTheShire: Notably The Void does not have vigilance, be a real shame if it immediately attacked into your indestructible blocker and then got Locked Up. Is this combo good? Seems unlikely but it's certainly a fun thing you can do that you might see on an Against the Odds episode or similar. In Commander this is a great political piece to make an ally and then potentially team up to beat someone to death quickly. Having a vigilant indestructible creature as your commander also opens up all kinds of Pariah and Entangler shenanigans, and with a bunch of blink effects you can hand each opponent their own weapon of destruction. I could definitely see building this to be just the worst kind of person.

Saffgor: I just think this card is too fair, to be quite honest. There's no value, and while the downside is minimized unless The Sentry himself gets removed, it's an absolute disaster if they exile or blink this guy.

BPhillipYork: Okay, this is at least interesting. For Commander pretty funny, because presumably they will not attack you since you will have a 5/5 blocker around. I don't know that I'd necessarily run it, 4 for a 5/5 with flying, vigilance, and indestructible is definitely undercosted, but it's basically just an undercosted Angel which is just a bad beater.

Blue

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Attuma, Atlantean Warlord

FromTheShire: It's not cheap but tacking card draw on to your Lord effect is solid, and in Commander you're happy to pay a little extra for it.

BPhillipYork: Awesome, another Lord of Atlantis, costs twice as much, but includes card draw.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Echo, Perceptive Prodigy

FromTheShire: Arcanis the Omnipotent go brrrrr. Or more accurately this goes in a deck that is already doing tap and untap manipulation and serves as another massive value or combo piece.

Saffgor: If this were cheaper, say 1-2 mana, I think we'd be talking. As is, I prefer Peter Parker's Camera.

BPhillipYork: This is solid, especially if your commander has something you want to copy, and then it's just gravy for that. Her flavor text makes no sense though, this should be one of those cards that gains abilities of other creatures right? Flying tokens and stuff.

Loxi: I think this is pretty decent. It's a reasonably efficient way to copy things but is limited to your own goodies. Deck depending of course, but in the right home this will be better than something like Strionic Resonati

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ironheart, Clever Champion

FromTheShire: Know what's better than every card coming with a Treasure token you have to sacrifice these days? Not having to sacrifice them because they are straight up mana rocks now. Cheating mana costs is busted and this lets you do it en masse, so be prepared because once this sticks they are going to be hitting you with X = 20 spells before you can blink.

Saffgor: Absolutely insane this doesn't say nonartifact. Ironheart is here to help you combo off, and I'd expect to see her a ton in B4, but oddly enough there's not been interest on the cEDH side. She isn't inherently deterministic, and having to steer through an Artifact combo through Rhystic isn't especially appetizing.

BPhillipYork: This is dangerous as hell. Having all your non-creature spells have improvise and how easy it is to generate loads of tokens now, this can get really brutal quickly. Thankfully it's only monocolor, if this was say red and blue, she'd helm nasty spell slinger decks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Iron Lad, Diverging Destiny

FromTheShire: Solid value for artifact decks that gives you both knowledge and card draw.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty solid, always seeing the top is nice, and drawing when it's an artifact is really nice.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Kang the Conqueror

FromTheShire: Refresh my memory, did we just see a card that could double activated abilities on creatures extremely cheaply? Hrm, weird. Having an extra turn in your Command Zone is uhhhh real good, and unfortunately it also means that people are likely going to lean in to that being the thing this deck does and you're going to have to sit through turbo turns and find out if they fizzle or if you are dead. Make them play it out though, don't scoop like a coward. If you have to suffer so do they.

Saffgor: Enjoy debating whether you're allowed to copy this ability in Bracket 3 or below. I just don't think playing Kang at most tables is a recipe for having fun, because he's so obviously A Problem™ that you're going to be shafted from the get go.

Loxi: I'd be curios to see if there's merit in trying to do some weird blinking shenanigans to see if you can loop this.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Leader, Super-Genius

FromTheShire: I don't think this is the most nakedly powerful card in the set, but it is sure up there on the list of the most flavorful. It's extremely unfortunate that there are like 20 cards total in blue that have some form of connive, and probably 1/3 of them are at least kind of playable. Instead Leader will slot in to things like Norman Osborn or Ultron, Unlimited.

Saffgor: This is a super fun payoff for all the Connive-matters cards in the set, and I do actually think this can get there in Bracket 3 and below. The floor is pretty high, even if it is fundamentally fair.

BPhillipYork: Okay, the connive doubler is here. I guess connive is sort of evergreen or deciduous at least, so there's been a decent amount of conniving creatures. Mono-blue connive doesn't seem very compelling, but as a value piece in a connive-centric deck this is good. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Loki, God of Mischief

FromTheShire: Urza's Glasses becoming playable was not something I had on my calendar for 2026 but I am extremely here for it. This whole deck is going to be hilarious, frankly.

Saffgor: Poke some stuff, draw some cards. Amazing piece in Crime decks, but a lot of Ux strategies can slot in some fiddly little tappers or targeters to ensure he draws 4 card each turn cycle. Not flashy, but probably pretty good!

BPhillipYork: This is pretty good if you abuse it, use Nomads en-Kor or something like that that will let you easily target each turn and just draw a card every turn. Also really really good in a crime deck which at least tracks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Mister Fantastic, Reed Richards

FromTheShire: You only draw one per batch but uh, this is pretty wild to not be limited to once per turn or anything. May I interest you in any of the many creatures that make a token when you draw your second card each turn? Or simply any Treasure creator?

Saffgor: This gets out of hand pretty quickly, but being a 4 mana card that does nothing on its own ensures Reed here is a prime target for removal.

BPhillipYork: Wow. That's um, dangerous as hell. So many ways to have tokens just keep entering.

Loxi: One or more keeps this in check for things that make a ton of tokens at once, but for incremental gains this is pretty insane. Even if you just nail 3 token off this, it's pretty great on efficiency.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ms. Marvel, Kamala Khan

FromTheShire: Blue has approximately one trillion 1 and 2 mana spells that give flying or shadow or straight up unblockable while also drawing a card, and now all of those actually draw 2 cards and punch you to death at the same time.

Saffgor: This is pretty cute, especially given that draw isn't once per turn (like so many cards in this set). Auras, combat tricks, self-bounce, you name it. She's both a solid enough draw engine with Shimmering Wings and the like, as well as an actual combat threat. The more I consider what a list for her looks like, the more I'm a fan.

BPhillipYork: You could definitely abuse this is in various ways, and if you're just self buffing via Instants or Auras or something like that it's pretty good. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Multiversal Incursion

FromTheShire: Did you put good creatures in your deck? Then this card is also good. 7 mana is a lot so ideally you are either winning outright or getting so far ahead on board you will win if you untap.

Saffgor: Bracket 2 wincon that gets played once on Game Knights and is met immediately with a wrath. Cue talk show segment.

BPhillipYork: Wow, that's 7 mana, which is a ton, but doubling all your Legendaries is pretty nasty. However, it's 7 mana, and dumping that much mana into a spell you should be threatening to or winning the game, so whatever you're doubling better be dangerous enough that you can shortly win. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Namor the Sub-Mariner

FromTheShire: The flying is a problem for everyone else, given that Merfolk accidentally make about 3 tokens per turn without even meaning to. Fortunately he's not the best commander for them since you want to be in Simic so at least you will be dying to regular damage.

Saffgor: Monoblue Devotion is a cool strategy that's been missing a modern Commander deck, and Namor can pretty quickly present lethal if you're jamming Cryptic Commands and the like. I think Merfolk typal is a trap, and you really ought to trust his triggered ability creates enough, playing solely Merfolk that are already competent.

BPhillipYork: Dang that is a lot of Merfolk. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pym Particles

FromTheShire: Given how hard Izzet decks want to slam you with prowess creatures right now, them getting a vigilance and unblockable on a 1 mana cantrip seems like it might come up. It even means you can use the same creature to pay for the teamwork cost on Team Tactics. Tap out at the wrong time and you may have some unblockable hasty double striker punching you for like 12+ out of nowhere. Yeesh.

Saffgor: Really good way to shove damage or an attack trigger for essentially no cost. Retain the blocker, break even on cards, this is quietly one of the better Sorcery-speed cantrips they've printed in years.

BPhillipYork: Fine if you really need unblockable, and it's basically just a cantrip, which is often useful to have. Most of it's use will probably be as a cantrip for decks that want to trigger off stuff like that. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Rewrite History

FromTheShire: I like how this plays with connive as well as its own internal synergy. It's not flashy but it's more methodical, plotting value that is right at home in Villain decks.

Saffgor: Pretty fantastic filtering that sets up the yard for itself, so long as you have things to tap. The most important thing is that you can, with infinite tap/untaps, cycle through your entire deck and resolve the sacrifice trigger afterwards, to dig for a win. In that case, it's a phenomenal piece of redundancy for the fringe cEDH Unctus, Grand Metatect list that turbos Aphetto Alchemist.

BPhillipYork: So these are basically just like the cards that get quest tokens but they are plan tokens instead. I wonder if they will retcon the old ones, probably not. Maybe this is a new thing in Magic, but I doubt it, I think it's more so Villains can have their plans thwarted, which is very comic book-y. However, cards selection for 4 is pretty decent in this case, and then getting two instants or sorceries back is solid. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Secret Invasion

FromTheShire: Neat way to slam one of your opponents with effectively their best creature out of nowhere, I dig it.

Saffgor: Cowardly not to print this as able to target any Creature. Let me perform shenanigans!

BPhillipYork: Cool, you enchant a token and exile your opponents' commander most likely, but something useful. Solid for 3 mana. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

S.H.I.E.L.D. Flying Car

FromTheShire: Seems like a fine way to get a little extra value off of enters triggers, or resetting power-up abilities if you've managed to pull those off.

BPhillipYork: This doesn't seem good, I guess it's like a rescue then it's just a 3/3 beater. Yay it can fly, it's a boring vehicle.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Shuri, Wakandan Inventor

FromTheShire: There are a frankly ridiculous number of artifact cost reducers at this point so you may as well get a second copy. Play it in a 5 color deck and see if you can Door to Nothingness everyone.

Saffgor: This is quietly one of the best Commanders from the set, enabling one third of The Reality Chip lines, and allowing you to both turn off your own stax pieces for the turn, and accrue additional uses of things like The Stasis Coffin. Expect an article soon.

BPhillipYork: This is potentially brutal, turning a Treasure or something into something fat. Personal favorite, just copy Door to Nothingness then obliterate someone. Making artifacts cheaper is super useful also, and she'll see a ton of play for that reason. This is just an all around solid card.

Loxi: Yeah this is pretty bonkers, this basically does everything you want for a mono-blue artifact commander option - gives you better access to control pieces and combos, as well as a cost reduction to boot. Pretty much the only thing she lacks is card draw, and I have great news about what Blue is good at.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Wondrous Wasp

FromTheShire: Floodpits Drowner and Tishana's Tidebinder are both already seeing play in Dimir lists but are due to rotate before long, so I won't be surprised if this sees play. It is less good in multiples which does hamper it.

Saffgor: Worse Tidebinder is still probably pretty good at 2 mana.

BPhillipYork: Well this is a nasty way to lock out a commander most likely, for 2 mana it's pretty solid for shutting something down, and also a 2/1 flyer is kind of useful. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

We Say Thee Nay!

FromTheShire: This isn't a terrible counterspell but it's nothing noteworthy - except that it's one of the two the first Arcane spells we have seen since the original Kamigawa block along with Hex Magic. While it remains to be seen if this is a one time thing or the start of getting further Arcane spells, it's very cool to see them return, as is having a more playable spell than Disrupting Shoal that could actually interact with Desperate Ritual.

Saffgor: More Arcane please, and while you're at it, bring back Splice?

BPhillipYork: This is a solid counter spell, weird that it's Arcane, but neat to see an old mostly gone mechanic show up occasionally. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Wiccan, Rising Magician

FromTheShire: Really great value engine with enters triggers, and also makes it basically impossible for your opponents to ever remove your things with targeted removal which is excellent. Not strong enough to be a commander in their own right but an excellent roleplayer in blink and control decks.

Saffgor: Balanced Displacer Kitten...in the Command Zone. That's probably good enough in all honesty, as the kitten is among Blue's most ubiquitous Creatures in the format. The one sad bit is that Wiccan can't protect himself.

BPhillipYork: This a worse Displacer Kitten but like so much worse. The delay will keep it from going infinite, which is probably a good thing. The value of a delayed blink will probably be vastly overestimated.
