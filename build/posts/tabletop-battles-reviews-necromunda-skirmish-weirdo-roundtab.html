Tabletop Battles Reviews: Necromunda Skirmish - Weirdo Roundtable

August 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Now that you’ve presumably read our comprehensive review of all the other aspects of the updated Necromunda rules, we thought it might be fun to chat through our overall opinions, which can get lost in the flood of new detail and facts to absorb.

What are our favorite changes in the new rules?

Genghis: It’s a difficult one. Amongst many changes I think are the highest impact – cover, ammunition, movement and damage are all different in significant ways – I am most fond of the new character development. The random-but-with-choice system of rolling Advances seems to me the best of both worlds, and there is much higher incentive for models to gain interesting Skills, rather than just pumping key stats. There seems like much faster progression for your cheapest models (without a better idea of starting XP for different Subtypes we can’t be certain about the others) and better handling of promotion within a gang. 

Hive Market: I’m happy to say there’s almost too much to choose from. Honourable mentions would include the reworks of Blaze and Insanity as well as the updated Post-Battle and Post-Cycle Sequences. Overall, though, it’s got to be the full update to Close Combat. Factoring Initiative into it, allowing for Simultaneous Attacks, limiting Reaction Attacks in a way that better facilitates Assists and Interference, and adding the Engage (Single) action as an option for models within a few inches of a fight all go a long way towards making melee a more exciting and nuanced experience. If nothing else, it sure beats N18 super champs turning everyone else into paste with their Reaction Attacks.

Fowler: Lowered costs for special and heavy weapons is very interesting. We will get a better idea of what this means across the board when the Gangs books arrive, but I love how much more you can fit into a 1,000 credit gang. Some oft-maligned weapons have the potential to jump up a few tier levels.

Primaris Kevin: Believe it or not, I'm going to say the changes to the multi-melta and everything it represents. It no longer has a blast radius, the melta trait is gone, and as a Heavy weapon you have to make some serious sacrifices to even field it. On top of that it's the most expensive weapon in the game and if you acquire it at the Trading Post you're not likely to get anything else. Is it still terrifying? Absolutely. But it seems like Games Workshop did a great job of making it terrifying while also eliminating all of the not-fun elements. It seems like that philosophy has permeated throughout the rules in a way that's very encouraging.

What will we miss from the N18 rules?

Genghis: I think on some level I liked the insanity of Vehicles shunting around while they bashed into each other or took fire. It wasn’t very practical for full scale games, I admit, but it was fun. More widely, I think it will take time for the game to regain the depths of esoteric weird stuff it had in late-N18, say from 2023 onwards.

Hive Market: It’s unavoidable when you're starting from scratch with a new edition, but I sure will miss the level of depth and player options we managed to get to with N18. There was undoubtedly a ton of bloat, a number of inconsistencies, and an increasing sense of imbalance to the game, but it really felt like our game these past few years. I am optimistic we’ll get to a good place after a few books have been added to this edition.

Genghis: Yeah, fair point. I do want all that stuff and will excitedly pour over new releases even when they're re-releases, but for now what's important is this is a solid foundation to build from.

Primaris Kevin: It's easy to take whatever is missing from older editions and port it over. For example, I don't think it would take us more than a day to convert everything we did for the Monster Hunter Necromunda weekender to something compatible with N26.

Fowler: I will miss (and not miss) the blaze condition. For every time something hilarious happened, there were two or three instances of noninteractive misery.

Any Missed Opportunities in the New Rules?

Genghis: I have a major concern about how the Suppressed Status doesn’t protect models from being shot at (or even make it more difficult). Although there are fewer barriers to melee combat, with longer Charge ranges and the new Engage Action, that one omission makes it more viable for shooty gangs to just lay down fire and keep trying until their enemies are all taken out.

Hive Market: I’m admittedly still trying to absorb everything we did get within the rules. With this being such a thorough revision, it’s been a process of trying to unlearn enough of N18 to understand all of the new interplay here. This has been complicated by the need to hop all around the book to ensure I’m correctly interpreting the new material, as with something like Out of Ammo taking you from the Shooting rules to the Reload action and then the Rapid Fire (X) and Ammo (X+) weapon traits and then the Scarce (X+), Limited, and Single Shot traits after that. It all makes sense, but you will be going on many cross-book journeys to verify when and how the new rules replace the old.

Fowler: Falling feels like it should have one more bracket. Insta-OOA on a 6 inch fall is rough, and it makes many of my favorite boards into even worse death traps than they already were.

Primaris Kevin: I think the benefits to shooting are at least slightly offset by the changes to Injuries, since when you're Seriously Injured and nobody is around you can Tend Wounds (Double) and to get a leg up on the Recovery test and Out of Action only has a 1 in 6 chance of going off. I also suspect that the presence of Ammo (X+) on the nastier weapons like plasma and melta will seriously slow down how effective they are. In N18 a Champion with a plasma gun and Fast Shot could lay waste to entire gangs. Now that same fighter has wasted a skill, as the plasma gun is going need to be reloaded 86% of the time.

Genghis: [Colombo voice] oh, and another thing - they should have allowed Consolidate moves to re-enter the Engaged status.

Primaris Kevin: For me the biggest missed opportunity is with the campaign system. Snowballing is never fun, and it's possible for a consistent winner to earn three times what a consistent loser does. This is particularly risky based on how income generation is primarily driven through battles. The system can allow an aggressive player to accumulate many territories in the first cycle. I think it would have been nice to add a bit more language about this, such as by encouraging challenges to prioritize players who have yet to fight that cycle or limiting the number of territories that can be acquired in a cycle. Or income could have been capped by the most successful result you had among all of games you played that cycle. I've just seen a lot of examples of where a player who's behind decides to stop playing, and I always want to avoid feelsbad moments.

What are we Excited to See in the Future?

Genghis: I think the first new campaign or setting books we see will bring some more of that esoteric equipment, weirdo hangers-on, and hopefully more elaborate custom Scenarios, back into the fold. I am a fan of the basic Scenario generation in the rulebook, I think it’s exactly what most groups need most often. But for dedicated weirdos with experienced Arbitrators, I think there’s room for more. Not a huge loss in the meantime, since I’m capable of adapting older Scenarios.

Hive Market: I’m with Genghis here in looking forward to some of the re-weirdo-ification of things as the game expands back out. The lengthy edition cycle stretching back to 2018 gave us 27 proper book releases and 17 more Apocrypha PDFs, which is a Cargo-8-load of material to shed when resetting the game. This edition certainly gives a strong foundation to build on, with the blueprints from all of those past releases up in the ether for GW to pull from and refine going forward.

Fowler: This might sound minor, but a couple of rules in the book belong to walker-class vehicles. My hope is that walkers get a bit of a glow-up here. They always stuck me as a missed opportunity. You could have a chassis like the Cawdor Ridgewalker, all the way up thought  a knight. It would be nice to have some points and stats assigned to these builds.

Primaris Kevin: If we're going with a wish list then I would love to see an Arbitrator-focused product, something that really emphasize the Gamemaster-like role that a good Arbitrator has in ensuring the success of a campaign. It could provide a list of alternative campaign styles, introduce some ways to make campaigns feel a bit more narrative, and if half the book is guidelines on Scenario generation and other stuff then I would be a happy weirdo. Other than that, I still want to know what the hell a Scimitar looks like.
