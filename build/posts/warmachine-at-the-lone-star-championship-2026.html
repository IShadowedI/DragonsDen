Warmachine at the Lone Star Championship 2026

September 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Last weekend (August 22nd and 23rd of 2026) I attended the Lone Star Championship and Iron Gauntlet Qualifier, hosted in Dallas Texas.  The event went wonderfully and I was able to get 5 games in.  There were a total of 32 players, and  I ended up 5th overall with a 4:1 record.

My Two Lists

The two lists I brought were pretty much on the opposite end of the scale when it comes to playstyle.  Shyryss double Trik leans heavily into two colossal who are able dominate the battlefield, while Nyxyan is an infantry spam list with a mountain of high defense models on the table.  Currently Shyryss has become the primary list, with only opponents who are able to easily kill two Trik’s (like Grmykin) getting Nyxyan dropped into them.

This ended up being my primary list all event.  It operates by leverage a few key things.  First, Shyryss has Host of Shadows giving her entire battlegroup ghostly.  This lets the Trik’s ignore building, terrain, or anything else I don’t want to deal with.  Two colossals on the table can struggle around terrain if you don't have a way to bypass it, and this is my way if bypassing it. Hand of Destruction, also applied by Shyryss lets the Triks hit consistently and harder than you would expect against key targets.  Shyryss feat subtracts 3” from your opponents charge distance when charging Khymeara models letting her out threat most opponents.  One of the Triks has arcane negation on the tail letting them negate key spells like Stranglehold, Crippling Grasp, or Deadweight ensuring they are not trivially shut out of the game.

The typical course of action with the list is to walk within 11” with Shyryss, throw a few daggers against a key target then use Witchmark to put Hand of Destruction on the key target.  Shyryss will then Feat, then reposition backward to be 14” away from my opponent.  This combined with her feat means unless someone threatens 17” she is safe.  The two Triks then walk to within 12” and open fire with both of their guns, ideally against a target with hand of destruction on it.  If your opponent can’t threaten 15”, you will be safe from most counter attacks.  My opponent is then left with a choice either back up, and take another round of shooting and forfeit scenario position or walk into no mans land in front of the Trik’s and let them get a potent charge off.  I’m happy with either choice.

Beyond the two colossal, I also have 3 units of Quick Fangs and the Shades.  These bring enough units to be able to put scenario pressure out to compensate for all the power in the Triks.  The Shades operate as needed, hunting random character models that my opponent has.  The Shades have Fortune Hunter, giving them an extra dice on hit and damage rolls against characters.  Both units also benefit greatly from Shyryss feat leveraging their high defense to dodge away from attacks and generally just be a pain to remove.

My un-played lists focuses on a mountain of high defense infantry who can threaten up to 14".  The damage output of the basic infantry spikes when I can bring in a potential 6 point ARM swing (Master of Ruin + Prey from Nyxyan + Rust from the Quick Fang Master)  I bring two Wraithwing Paragon's in order to stop all the Quick Fangs from just dying to blasts.  The last piece of the puzzle is Aklyss who allows Nyxyan to cast Caustic Mist creating a cloud wall that many opponents simply can not deal with.

Game 1

I was paired into Nercofactorium where my opponent selected Eviscerus. The list had a mountain of hard hitting pieces, with a Hellraker, Deathjack, Malfessor, and Mangus and Invictus.  With Eviscerus’s feat providing +2 speed, and Redline my opponents Hellraker and Deathjack would threaten 15”, which is enough to hit my Triks even with my feat.  The big downside was he had very few units and struggled with scoring key scenario elements all game.  I knew he could get a powerful alpha strike if I let him, so I ended up playing the scenario very cagey.  The scenario was payload which ended up being key. My opponent selected a building to be his flag which he struggled to protect all game.  My flag was placed on a piece of scatter terrain which my Triks immediately crushed.

Lone Star Open 2026 - Turn 2

I initially deployed my Triks centrally but quickly realized I needed to move to my right flank to use buildings to block my opponents charges.  We spent a couple of turns just circling each other.  On my left flank I used one unit of Quick Fang Stalkers to contest his scenario piece while most of the battle was in the center, and slightly to the left.

I brought my Triks to the right, setting myself up to start scoring the 50, while also threatening his 40mm scenario element.  My opponent did not have many units to score those elements, and I was able to use my Quick Fangs to score my 40 and my caster to score my 50.  While my opponents list had several hard hitting units, it did not have much ranged output and it was not able to hurt my Quick Fangs on my 40 without trading way to much away.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 1 turn 2.

The following turn, the unit of Quick Fangs on my 40 moved into his 40, blocking some key charge lanes while my second unit of Quick Fangs came forward and sat on my 40.  The Shades also charge Invictus and rolled insane and somehow killed him - my dice went a little nuts.  In that position I was able to score my 40 again, my flag, my 50, his flag all while contesting his 40 and 50.  This gave me a massive scenario lead.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 1 Pic 3

The following turn was the bloody turn of the game, but it really didn’t matter.  He killed a Trik, I swung back and didn’t kill much.  I ran my caster to safety, and threw the kitchen sink at his caster since it didn’t really matter.  The Shades mixed with some shooting from a Trik were able to kill his caster.  In the end it ended up being an assassination win but I also was in an 11:4 scenario lead after I was able to move my 50 to his flag and score the extra payload points.

In hindsight this might have been better to drop Nyxyan into.  The 15” threat is terrifying, and everything in the list hits like a freight train, but it struggles to hit def 14 and 15.  The grenades from the Hellraker would have eaten a unit, but that is a fair price to pay to remove a colossal.  His list also has very few eyeless sight units.  It doesn't matter how fast you are if you can't see anything.

Game 2

Game two was against Brandon Owens running Fane of Nyrro.  This ended up being my one loss of the event.  He dropped Vorsalys.  I figured my Triks could handle his spells so I dropped the Triks.  The scenario was trench warfare.  I don’t recall who won the roll for first or second, but I remember I ended up going first.

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 Game 2 Seconds before disaster.

Turn 1 was my typical run forward as much as possible with my opponent positioning out of threat range.

Turn 2 I played too cagey and moved my Triks up using the building to protect them.  This was also my crucial mistake of the game.  I didn’t notice that I had nothing contesting my cache until after both Triks had activated so I had to use the Shades to protect my cache.  My opponent noticed that, and ran forward to contest all my scenario elements, scored both of his, killed a couple of Shades, and scored my cache giving him a 4:0 lead.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 2 Pic 2

Turn 3 I did the math.  I had no way of contesting his 50, which meant I needed to score 3 points to not lose the game.  I would easily be able to score my 40 and 50, but that would result in a 5:2 victory for him.  I made a desperate attempt to clear his flag and score it – but I was unsuccessful.

Post game I was talking the game over with Brandon and he pointed out that even if I had contested my cache with my Trik he still would have attempted to score it, using Vorsalys feat to make it inconsequential and using a MOUNTAIN of hunger and fury to power through the arcane negation tail.  We never got to see it due to an error on my part so for the time being my judgment of Vorsalys is still pending. Put away the pitchforks.  (For now.)

Game 3

I played into Thexus in Dark Operations.  I was pretty happy with the matchup since I was functionally immune to his feat since he couldn't push the Triks (joys of being a colossal), and I would be able to shut down Crippling Grasp with Arcane Negation letting my Triks run rampant.

The Triks shot the teeth out of drudges, but killed fewer than I had hoped with the drudges benefiting from deceleration and tough.  The Triks went into the center and killed basically everything.  The Quick Fangs usually are able to win the flanks, but my opponents Iconclast Drudges were able to win both fights putting me behind on scenario.  My other key mistake was killing all the drudges in a unit, but not realizing a mind bender was still alive.  My opponent resurrected a couple of drudges which promptly exploded killing an entire unit of Quickfangs without having to roll dice.  Moral of the story – kill the blasted mind bender!

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 3 Pic 1

At this point my opponent realized the Triks would eat his caster and he promptly ran to my right flank in an attempt to stay alive while throwing things in front of them.  I was able to unjam myself, and bulldoze key piece to create a landing zone.  One of the Trik’s then killed his caster.  Final results was an assassination victory with my opponent up on scenario 8:5.

The Triks were absolutely the right drop for this.  I was looking over rules after the game, and I think my biggest mistake was not boosting blast damage on the mind bender every chance I got.  If I would have killed the mind bender I would have taken away all of my opponents shield guard and the ability to explode.  Instead, I incorrectly thought that it was the drudges with shield guard, not that it was from him.  Lesson learned I guess.

Game 4

The second day of the event started with my playing into Wyrmwood on Two Fronts.  In what should not come as a surprise I dropped Shyryss with the Triks.  Wyrmwood has Hellmouth which just eats models in Nyxyans list, while the Triks can shut down Stranglehold.

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 I'm sure there is a joke about measuring something somewhere out there.

I deployed the Triks centrally, while my opponent leaned into my left flank.  I was initially not worried about the Well of Orboros, but it ended up being his MVP.  It basically ate a unit of Quick Fang Stalkers every turn.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 4 Pic 2

I moved up aggressively turn 1, and my opponent did the same.  He was worried about my shooting and the slam head on one of my Triks and he ended up feating turn one.  In our post game analysis we decided this was a mistake.

I basically just ignored his feat turn 2.  There was a Gorax Rager that was visible on the flank that I was able to see, so I threw a few shots into it, but otherwise just moved forward to control the two 50’s on the flank.  I ran some Quick Fangs up to contest the 40’s on the right flank. On my opponents turn he cleared the 40’s and threw a few things at my Triks but they bounced off.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 4 Triks shifting to the left

Turn 3 I cleared both 50’s letting my score extra points, while also securing the flag in the middle.  I was able to clear a few Tharn he moved to close to the Triks, but overall he played very conservatively. My opponent then scored both 40’s.   That would become the theme for the game - me scoring both 50's and him scoring both 40's.  He did a little contesting, but one Trik just camped on both 50’s while the other creeped from right to left, shooting a few things every turn.  In the end I was able to clean the 40’s with some Trik shooting, throw the remaining quick fangs at one of them, secure the flag, and keep camping the 50’s.  He didn’t have a way to push back and I skyrockeded on points on the final turn.  Results – scenario victory, 21-12.

Game 5

Game 5 was a mirror into Lylyth.  I had more ranged output, but he had Lylyth with her Crit Stagger spay.  The catch was, if he moved forward enough to use it, I would be in a position to assassinate him.  The scenario was Fault Line.

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 - Game 5 Pic 1

My Trik’s were deployed centrally, with mirage going on a unit of Quick Fang Stalkers on the left flank.  The plan for them was to hide behind a forest then pop out and charge into the few units my opponent had there.  On my right I put a single unit of Quick Fang Stalkers who’s only job was to run forward and score and contest.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 5 Pic 2

I choose to go first, and ran forward.  Turn two I shifted to the left to protect the two 40’s there, while also being I a position to kill anything my opponent used to control or contest the 50’s in the middle.  I also threw a unit of Quick Fangs to contest his 50.  This was likely a mistake.  They gave my opponent something to use to trigger run and gun.  I also feated, keeping my models safe from any long charges.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 5 Pic 3

My opponent move forward and put a few shots into me, but failed to meaningfully damage my Triks.  Lylyth used her Armor Piercing spray but failed to crit with 4 dice (2 base dice, boosting, and deadeye), he also forgot to put puppet master on her and this is still a dice game.  In a second string of bad luck my opponents Wyvern charged in failed to kill a single mode with it’s spray.  This meant it was not able to run and gun to get away.  My opponent also ambushed a unit of Quick Fang Stalkers on the left to jam everything up.

Lone Star Open 2026 Game 5 Pic 4

My turn 3 I killed the Wyvern with a Trik, and cleaned up my 40, while also positioning to score both 50’s and my 40’ on the right flank hiding behind the building.  The result was I scored something like 6 points which put me in a near unreachable position.  My only really risk at this point was assassination, but I was hiding well away from everything.

My opponent took his final turn and cleaned up the 40’s on the right flank and threw a few models in to contest, but I ended in another scenario victory for me.  Final score – 8 to 4.

Final Ranks

I ended up going 4-1, playing Shyryss all event.  Overall I’m very happy with my Trik’s.  That said, dropping the same list all 5 games is a big of a red flag for my pair.  Nyxyan likely need to be re-evaluated if she is not pulling her weight.  My one loss was due to a scenario blunder on my part, which is a “live and learn” mistake.  Hopefully I won’t make it again.  (Spoiler – I will).

Winners

The ultimate winner was Alex Kosma running Grymkin.  He played against Brad Park’s Dark Operations in the final.

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 Finals Game

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 Winner Alex Kosma (Right) and Max the organizer (Left), not pictured Jared assisting judging and taking all of the photos (Thanks Jared!)

Most events prize support tends to be store credit, but Max, the organizer, was able to coordinate some great trophies since most folks were local so store credit wasn’t a big deal for them.

Photo Credit: Jared Olson. Lone Star Open 2026 Swag

Overall the event was a blast, and I’m looking forward to it next year. Max and Jared put in a ton of work for the event and it looked like everything went of without a hitch.  Folks came in from all over.  Hopefully next year we can get even more folks down!
