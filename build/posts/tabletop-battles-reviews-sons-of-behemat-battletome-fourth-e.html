Tabletop Battles Reviews: Sons of Behemat Battletome Fourth Edition

September 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer
As always we’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of the Battletome. We have also been provided with a provisional Battle Profile list for the army to use when writing the review, but please note that these points are subject to change until they are published on Warhammer Community. 

A Gargantuan Update

This Battletome represents something of a sea change in the conceptual identity of Gargants as an army, and whilst we’ll cover everything in detail, it’s probably worth highlighting the major changes to the army straight away, as it’s useful to have in mind when evaluating the new rules.

The Mega-Gargants themselves have undergone a hefty change and unification in profile, and they are dramatically less powerful - Health is down to 25 and they’re now 10 Control. Both of these changes to their statline render individual Mega-Gargants much easier for your opponent to deal with, but the flip side to this is that points are such that you can now fit five Megas into an army. This still represents an overall reduction in Health for the army (140 for four old-style Megas vs 125 for five of the new ones), but a whole extra Mega-Gargant is a large increase in potential offensive output. 

Speaking of offensive output: The Mega-Gargant attack profile has been streamlined and unified. The single death grip attack is gone and the stomps have lost an attack and their anti-infantry bonus rend. However, the really crucial change is to the main weapon of the Mega-Gargants. Previously, these were unique to each Gargant and varied a lot in quality, which led to some real balancing headaches (poor old Warstomper). Now each Mega-Gargant has the same choice of attack profile on their ‘main’ weapon. They can either make a Crushing Blow attack, which is essentially the old Gatebreaker weapon, with six Damage 4 attacks; or a Calamitous Sweep of sixteen Damage 1 attacks that can crit (auto-wound). 

The back of the napkin maths on this is that smashing is just baseline better on average, but is much more prone to spiky dice as it hits on 4s. Sweeping will generally give you lower output, but with a smoother curve. There will also be occasions where Sweeping will end up better into certain opponents as it hits on 3s with lower rend; it’ll likely prove useful into Nighthaunt or matchups with lots of -1 to hit being thrown about. Personally, I find the concept of a Mega-Gargant doing 1 Damage to be a repelling taste, but it’s a useful ability for each one to have.

This change to weapons is overall a win for Kraken-Eaters and Warstompers, a mild loss for Beast-Smashers and Gate-Breakers, and a quality of life win for the Sons player, as you now only have to worry about what abilities you would like access to from your Megas, rather than how good or bad at fighting they are relative to each other.

Battle Traits

The big list of rampages have been moved to being their own set of upgrades, which has freed up space for a brand new idea space for battle traits. We’ve got four traits that each trigger at the end of a specific phase for a wide variety of effects. It’s a classic in the genre of “quite tricky to explain but not that bad in practice” 4th edition wordy abilities. The key is that each ability lets you pick any number of (non-Kragnos) units as targets, and then you roll a die - if it’s lower than the number of targets then the ability fails, otherwise each target gets to pick an effect from a list. So what we’ve got here is, basically, a gamble on having your battle traits do nothing vs being able to leverage lots of effects. But you can always just choose one target and have a guaranteed effect. It’s a fun mechanic, and feels very Grand Alliance Destruction.

You only get to pick each effect once, but with one exception: each one of these has an ability to deal D3 Mortals on a 2+ to something they’re in melee with, which can be picked multiple times for different Gargants. It’s not great, but if the ability doesn’t do anything for you right now (or you already used them all up) you always have a back up option.

Take a Run At It (Movement Phase)

Your options are:

  • If the target is within 3” of a board edge, they can just run off the board edge and go into reserve. Very funny, cute trick that brings them back at end of turn. Because this isn’t a Move ability, you can first move there, then proc this to activate the ability. They will come back at the end of the turn (or are otherwise destroyed as they run off into the sunset) by coming back within 1” of a board edge. Given their huge base size, this means you can potentially get cheeky and steal an objective close to a board edge.
  • Target can move as though it had Fly. Mega-Gargants lost Longshanks but this brings it back even better for one Gargant per turn. In most cases, that’s probably all you needed anyway.
  • Choose a target to Build Momentum. This does nothing on its own but leads into the next ability.

Argy Bargy (Charge Phase)

Less exciting; you can either:

  • Get an additional 3” Pile in. Can be helpful for skirting around chaff into the stuff that matters, but otherwise you probably will just do the mortals.
  • Alternatively, if you have a target who is Building Momentum, pick them and they are now Getting Ready. Again, this does nothing on its own but is building up for the next ability.

Wrathful Rampaging (Combat Phase)

Aside from the mortals, these options will only work for those who are Getting Ready. A Little who is Getting Ready loses it, but gains Strike First. A Big loses it and can choose to either have Strikes First or go for Prepping The Big One. What's The Big One? Glad you asked.

The Big One (End Step)

Easily the funniest ability in the battle traits. You have to do a lot of build up to get here and there’s a lot of failure points along the way where the dice can screw you, so what is the payoff? You get to grab any number of dice, roll 'em and add up the damage as mortal wounds. If you get a single one though, the entire thing fizzles.

Now, the funniest image is grabbing like 20 dice and throwing them and hoping for the best. Someone will do this and win a GT, I’m sure of it. Realistically though, your smartest play is to cap out around 4, that’s when it starts to lean against you and is more likely you’ll get a 1.

Number of Dice Average Damage Odds of Rolling a 1
1 4 16.6%
2 8 30.5%
3 12 42.1%
4 16 51.7%
5 20 59.8%
6 24 66.5%
7 28 72%
8 32 76.7%
9 36 80.6%
10 40 83.8%

Aside from the potential for failure, you do have to consider that there’s not that much stuff with that high a wound count. Even Gargants have been reduced to 25 wounds so more than 7 is probably overkill. But you know your worth; I’m just giving you numbers.

All in all, I think these are a very interesting set of battle traits; there’s a lot of potential to risk it all for huge results, but it’s also perfectly possible to play for guarantees. I like this balance and honestly for most phases you’re only ever likely to be wanting to activate 1-2 units, as taking the risk of the entire ability failing on punting on a bunch of bad d3s seems like a fool's errand. 

Gorlagg Knight-Kicker, Gatebreaker Gargant. Credit: Raf Cordero

Battle Formations

It’s your standard set of four to choose from and unusually, they’re all relatively compelling. Looting Leviathans lets your Big or Little units that have charged make a pile-in through non-Monster enemy units. Remember that you can give a unit a 6” pile move in each of your charge phases, so this has potential for some really powerful play against opponents trying to tightly screen you off an objective.

Ten-League Strides gives Big and Little units a minimum 3 for run and redeploy rolls. Being able to guarantee redeploys is the money here - people will very often try to combo-charge multiple units into Megas, and redeploy can be a key tool for forcing your opponent to block their own secondary/tertiary charge attempts by shifting your model sideways. Or just know that you can step back 3 and be safe, sometimes that’s good too.

Conquering Stomp is a funny one, handing out -1 to hit for combat attacks that target your Big and Little  units in the first battle round, so long as they haven’t charged. This is going to be somewhat meta dependent, but very useful to protect against alpha strikes, especially with Gargants having a lower health pool. 

Heirs of the Old Ways is a pretty diabolical punishment for enemy casters - you pick a Big or Little unit in your hero phase and then pick an enemy wizard or priest within 18” of them. If you can roll over their control characteristic then you cap that unit’s power level at 1 until the start of your next turn and instantly banish all manifestations that the target had summoned. There’s a lot of limitations here - in matchup (it’s going to do nothing vs KO or big monster wizards), in being a dice roll, and whether the cap matters. The banishing part of this is very unusual and really powerful, though. Rolling a 6 vs Kroak on this has got to be one of the best feelings in the entire game. 

Heroic Traits

There are 6 to choose from, which is very welcome for an army that has historically wanted for customisation. Brute of Brutes lets you ignore negative modifiers to hit and wound - this is fantastic with so many attacks hitting on 4s in a meta with quite a lot of -1 to hit hanging about. Spell-Chomper lets the hero unbind and banish as if it was Wizard (1), and each time you banish a manifestation you heal d3. Not bad, but with the hero options now available to Sons, this isn’t your only way to interact with spellcasting anymore, so doesn’t feel vital. Always First In is a deployment phase 2d6” move, though it can’t get you more than 9” from enemy units. Getting onto objectives early is good, especially if that pressures your opponent on battleplans where scoring is more limited. In the context of this Battletome, I’d have concerns about leaving a single Mega-Gargant hanging out by itself like that. 

Unreasonable Tyrant lets you pick a friendly monster unit wholly within 12” and give them +1 to charge. Unspectacular but almost always useful, it’s nice that it works in both charge phases, but is a downgrade from the aura of the faction pack. Thundering Titan is a rampage that can target a non-Hero enemy unit that you’ve charged, if you can beat their Control characteristic then your Gargant can’t be picked to be the target of a pile-in move while there are other friendly Sons of Behemat units in combat with the target. This is really rather radically niche, though it’s worth pointing out that there’s no end clause to this, so once you’ve thundered with your titan it’s forever. Movement tech is rarely bad per se, but there are more consistent options here. 

Finally, One-Hit Wonder is yet more gambling for those so inclined. This is a core, attack, fight, rampage ability for the combat phase that picks an enemy unit in combat and rolls a dice. Add 1 to the roll for each battle round that your Gargant was on the table and didn’t fight in both combat phases. On a 6+ you do 4d6 mortals. No thanks!

There are some nice options here, but it all feels a little tame (no health boosts like the faction pack). I imagine most players will gravitate towards Brute of Brutes or Unreasonable Tyrant for consistency. 

Artefacts

Another six options here, and there are some nice picks as well. Glowy Lantern lets you pick either Wizard (1) or Priest (1) and give that keyword to your Gargant for the rest of the battle. That’s massively stronger than the faction pack version of this and I suspect we’ll be seeing it a lot. Krakenskin Sandals lets you power up your Gargant’s stomp attacks with +1 Attack, Rend and Damage. It’s a relatively large boost to the output of your stomp attacks, but in the grand scheme of the Gargant attack profile it’s likely just a couple of extra points of damage per activation. Enchanted Portcullis ignores all modifiers to save rolls versus shooting attacks. Shooting’s back on the menu at the moment, so being able to keep your 4+ vs KO or Longstrikes is actually pretty handy and saves you CP on All-out Defence. It does mean your opponent will try to prioritise other targets, but this can work to your advantage with covering fire target selection and trying to split their output across multiple Gargants. 

Massive Stuffin’ Sack picks an enemy unit every combat phase and rolls 3 dice, for each roll that’s double the target’s Health characteristic, you auto kill a model in the unit. Having to double the Health here is rough, as it basically limits you to 1-2 Health models, and being able to pick up a couple of them in a combat phase isn’t that useful. Mantle of the Wood-Kings gives your Gargant +1 to hit… if it charged and is in combat with 2 or more enemy units. Honestly, with bases this huge that’s not a huge ask. It’s less consistent as an offensive output than the sandals, but I like it more as the hit roll is a huge barrier for Gargant damage output, but taking the negatives to save from All-out Attack is a huge drawback. Finally we have Protecty Bits which ignore the first damage point allocated to your Gargant each phase. We’ve got a consistent theme of massively toning down the defensive capabilities of this army, as a ward save would have been a lot more helpful than this. 

Realm-Shaking Rampages

Hey, it’s unique rampage enhancements, that’s cool. With Aqshy, that gives your Gargants 4 different types of enhancement, and I dig that a lot for list building in what was previously quite a dull army to build. As with the other enhancements in the tome, there’s 6 to choose from, and they can go on any of your non-unique Big units. 

Hammer Throw (20) starts us off with a core, shoot ability only for your shooting phase. You pick a friendly Mancrusher within combat range and roll 3d6. On a 4+ you pick that Mancrusher up and set them down again wholly within a number of inches equal to the roll, even into combat. The only downside is the Mancrusher can’t charge if you’ve yeeted them, but you can fastball them straight into combat anyway. If you roll well enough. Should you punt your Mancrusher straight into an enemy unit you also do d3 damage to your poor Mancrusher, 1 mortal to each enemy unit in combat with them and then the Mancrusher counts as having charged. Really really funny rampage that will appeal to a certain type of player, but that certain type is not the “I’m trying quite hard to win the game” player. 

Earthshaking Roar (20) is your classic combat phase 3+ to turn off commands. Always useful, unless you roll a 1-2. Battered Shrimp (20) can only target non-monster Hero units that you’re in combat with and makes you roll off. If you win the roll off you roll off again and keep doing that until you lose. Do mortal damage to the hero equal to the number of roll offs that you run. More gambling, more “fun”, but the damage on offer here is realistically pretty pitiful for the limitations of what it can target. One time someone is going to roll like a god down the club and auto-kill Sigvald and everyone will cheer, but that doesn’t change this being bad. 

Windmill Walloping (20) is ah.. look, the names for all of these abilities are great. Someone had a lot of fun here. Kudos to them. Pick an enemy monster or warmachine, roll a number of dice equal to their Health characteristic, add 1 to each roll for every consecutive turn you’ve done this to the target. For each 6+ they take a mortal that they’re not allowed to ward. I like this more than the shrimp one? I guess? There’s no cap on the dice pool for this, so vs other Gargants this is a deeply cursed ability. 

Colossal Slam (20) also picks a monster or warmachine and is the rampage you were hoping to see here. You have to either roll a 3+ (or 4+ if the target somehow has a higher Health characteristic) or this can go off automatically if you have 2 or more Big or Little units in combat with the target. You get to remove them from the battlefield and set it up again in combat with the Gargant doing the rampage, then do d3 mortals. Last and possibly least, Rancid Flatulence (20) is a once per battle ability that caps enemy units at a control score of 1 whilst in combat with your Gargant. 

Realistically, you’re picking between Earthshaking Road and Colossal Slam here and hopefully they’ll tweak the points for the rest. 

Mancrusher Gargants. Credit: Fowler

Spell Lore

A Gargant spell lore?! Wonders will never cease. Also, it’s 3 pretty banging spells.

  • Energy Surge CV6, Unlimited. Pick a friendly monster wholly within 12” and if they’re Big they get +1 to rampage rolls and if they’re Little they get run and charge. Both effects are actually pretty handy - Run and charge is always a great option and getting more reliability out of Colossal Slam can be game winning.
  • Pipsqueak Curse CV7. Pick an enemy within 18”, they get -1 to wound. Debuffing wound rolls is always impactful because buffs to them require specific army tech that isn’t always there. Note that this will also debuff shooting, as it’s not just combat attacks.
  • Thornfang’s Wrath CV6. Pick a friendly monster wholly within 12” and they get Crit (Mortal). Crit (Mortal) Kragnos. Critnos.

Prayer Lore

  • World Titan’s Stride CV3/6, Unlimited. Pick a friendly monster wholly within 18”(!) and they get +3” to move and can move as if they had fly. On the 6+, they can deal some mortals on any enemy unit they move over. A solid prayer that can get your Megas motoring up the board, not worth chasing the enhanced version though.
  • Might of the Earth CV4/8. Picks every friendly Sons unit on the battlefield and heals them d3. On an 8, it’s a flat 3. Healing Gargants is always really solid, though is a bit less impactful now with the lower starting Health. The infinite range and targeting every model is great, though.
  • Behemat’s Vengeance CV4/8. Pick a friendly monster wholly within 18” and they get +1 attack on their melee weapons. On an 8 you pick two models. Another excellent prayer - honestly Sons have made out like bandits with the spell and prayer lore which will have a downstream effect of pushing those named characters and the glowy lantern. It’s nice for the Gargant player in terms of power level, but I do worry it will flatten listbuilding a decent amount.

Units

It’s worth pointing out, given they’ve been mentioned a lot throughout the review that all of the Mega-Gargants have the Big keyword and the Mancrusher, Boss-Stompers, Rock-Hurlers and Ma Maegran all have the Little keyword. Kragnos and the Ancient Ghyrochs don’t have either, which locks them out of a lot of the army rules. It’s also worth pointing out that the old ‘Son of Behemat’ rule on each Mega warscroll is gone, so they’re vulnerable to instant-death abilities. 

Big

King Brodd (430) remains a Mega-Gargant-But-Better, having 28 Health and a slightly better melee profile on his Obelisk of Tor Crania. He’s still a Priest (1) with his own prayer to add Rend to a friendly unit’s melee weapon. The upside to his unique prayer getting split up between him and the new prayer lore is that the CVs get to be lower and this new Rend boost doesn’t target specific weapons, so boosts stomps as well on Megas or can even be cast on baby gargs. Court of Creepers has been changed to now target an enemy hero or monster in combat and on a 3+ you get +1 to hit the target for the turn, they can’t use rampages for the turn and they lose any artefact they had for the rest of the game. Dicey, but a fabulous ability. Finally is Set ‘Em Up, Then Knock ‘Em Down is a rampage in the charge phase with a range of 18”. You pick an enemy unit and if you have 3+ Big or Little models in combat (so, a unit of Boss-Stompers, realistically) it goes off automatically otherwise roll a 3+ and the target gets -1 to hit whilst in combat with 2 or more Big or Little models. It’s interesting that Brodd himself doesn’t need to be involved in this combat at all, and that if you are running Boss-Stompers they effectively get a free -1 to be hit. As usual, Brodd is very strong for only a small price premium over a standard Mega, I’d expect to see him a lot.

The Beast-Smasher (380) is the Mega that’s ended up on the worst end of the change to their attack profile, but is much more in line with the price of the others to compensate. Ability wise, it’s also a total change. Leader of the Pack is a rampage that triggers in reaction to using a Fight ability, and you can pick another friendly unit wholly within 12” and they get Crit (2-hits) on a 3+, or 2+ if they’re Little. Like Brodd, this likes to synergise with Boss-Stompers, who throw out a lot of dice in melee. Primal Aura is yet another rampage where on a 3+ you subtract 1 from ward rolls made against abilities used by the Beast-Smasher. You get +1 to the roll for each other Big or Little unit in combat with the target. There are quite a lot of ward saves kicking around the game at the moment, so any version of a rule like this is typically something an army will want to pick up.

Kraken-Eaters (370) have lost, I’m afraid, the ability to kick objectives around. Pour one out. Get Orf Me Land! now picks an objective or terrain feature at deployment to be your loot hoard. Whilst contesting a loot hoard the Kraken-Eater gets +10 control, whilst it’s not contesting it you instead get +2 attacks on the stomp. This is quite handy, it gives you an easy way to bring back the oppressive 20 Control onto a key objective. Note that the ability is once per battle (army), so there’s no benefit to spamming Kraken-Eaters. Greedy Show-Offs is a rampage that picks a Big or Little unit wholly within 12” in your hero phase - if you’re contesting your loot hoard it’s automatic, otherwise roll a 3+ for +1 to charge on the target for the rest of the turn. A nice bonus, and can stack with Unreasonable Tyrant for a +2 to charge.

The much maligned Warstomper (390) is the biggest winner of the Mega-Gargant attack profile flattening, so you’re not now actively making your list worse by taking one. They’ve forgotten how to throw bodies, in favour of more stomping around related activities. Almightier Stompin’ is a rampage for d3 extra attacks on your stomps, but all of your stomps have to target the same enemy unit. Stomp attacks aren’t amazing, but more attacks isn’t a bad thing. The money here is that if you kill any enemy models with your stomps you cap their Control score at 1 whilst they are in combat with this unit. Note that that’s the only ending clause on the ability, so even if you stomp one model to death the Control 1 will persist for as long as they’re standing next to your Warstomper. Don’t Stomp Those; Stomp Them! Is an ability that makes me think that in my heart I do not believe that a Gargant would know what a semi-colon is. Anyway, you get to pick a Big or Little unit wholly within 12” that has not been set up this turn and they get to move 2d6”, it can end that move in combat if it started the move in combat but it can also just leave the combat if it wants. Some funky tech for stuff that’s engaged in a fight, but it’s also not a core move ability so you can use it to give something a 2d6” move and it can move against afterwards. 

Finally, the Gatebreaker (400) is the only Mega-Gargant that’s remembered how to shoot things and can hurl boulders at people. It’s 1 shot with 4 Damage at 18”. Rip It Up And Chuck It is a fun rule for messing with terrain, you pick a terrain feature within 3” that has not been uprooted and then pick a friendly unit within 3” of that terrain feature and on a 3+ you can either give that target to shoot like a Gatebreaker if it is another kind of Mega, get +1 attack if it’s a Gatebreaker or Little and then make the terrain uprooted. If it’s faction terrain you also deal mortals to it equal to the rampage roll. It’s a wordy ability for a little bit of extra shooting, but they’re pretty high quality attacks, especially getting the attack boost on a whole unit of Rock-Hurlers. Then there’s Primal Reckoning, which is once per battle (army) and gives you +1 attack on the Fortcrusha Flail crushing blow for every friendly unit that has been destroyed, to a max of 10. This is the least fancy of the Megas in terms of the tech going on really, but it has lots of potential to do damage.

Overall, I think the mix of abilities on the Mega-Gargants is really well done. I like there’s a decently compelling reason to take each of them, and that the more powerful abilities on offer here are capped at the army level to discourage spam. It’s a nice way to prod people in the direction of diversifying their army, though I do still have concerns that other factors will push players towards named characters that will then end up limiting the diversity of your regular Megas.

Ma Maegran and Ancient Ghyrochs, Sons of Behemat - credit Bair

Little

Ma Maegran (370) has to come with her two Ancient Ghyrochs buddies, which goes some way to explaining the cost of a model that otherwise has 15 Health and a 4+ save. She’s OK in a scrap, with 4 attacks at Damage 4 and decent Rend, but she’s really here to cast spells, being Wizard (1). The Wandering Witch makes her invisible to enemy units more than 12” away if she’s wholly within 12” of another friendly monster, which should at least do something to keep her alive, but armies that can easily get within that bubble like Stormcast and KO will have no problem wiping her out instantly. Creepy Cantrips is her unique spell and casts on a 6 with an 18” range for +1 to rampage rolls and hit rolls for the target. It’s odd that it repeats the rampage roll bonus from the unlimited spell in the lore, but I can’t deny that this is a powerful spell for the army that addresses two problems at once. Then we come into two really wordy abilities with Shepherdess picking her angry goats if they’re wholly within 12” and then on a 3+ (or auto if they’re within her combat range) she can pick one of: +1 damage on the Ghyrochs hooves, a 3” bonus move that can take them into combat, or +1 power level for Ma. So keep the goats with her and she’s Wizard (2), easy enough. Chooser of the Mighty picks another Little unit wholly within 12” to become mighty. Then you pick one of two effects: for the rest of the turn mighty units that roll a 6 to wound do a cheeky extra mortal on the target (don’t choose this one), or you pick a point within an inch of the mighty unit and set up a non-unique Big unit that was destroyed earlier in the battle and bring it back within an inch of that inch with 10 damage allocated to it. Then destroy the mighty unit. You, uh, choose that option of the two. 

Ma Maegran is interesting. She’s really expensive for how incredibly squishy she can potentially be, but she brings a couple of extra models with her along for the ride and the ability to straight up recycle a Mancrusher into a (damaged) Mega-Gargant has huge implications for her efficacy. Obviously the trick is getting that Mega killed whilst she is still on the board to use the ability. Blessedly, Chooser of the Mighty works in any movement phase, so you can pop it in the enemy turn in situations where you are being doubled.

I’ll be a tease by covering the humble Mancrusher Gargant (140) first of all. I guess the surprising thing here is that they still exist, and I suspect that is largely to be a thing that exists to fill potential gaps in listbuilding. Essentially - if you take certain (but not all) configurations of 5 units, you can end up with a gap large enough for this. You can only take single Mancrushers now, the units are entirely the job of the new stuff. Defensively, we’re looking at the same profile as before, so very fragile, and they’ve seen the same decrease in Control down to 5. The good news is that their thrown rock now might hit something, as its hits on 4s by default, though has lost a point of Rend in trade. In melee they’ve traded their lone kick attack for an extra swing with their main weapon and lost a point of damage on the ‘eadbutt. Overall a downgrade in damage, but a very minor one. Abilities are totally new. Excitable Followers costs a command point and lets the Mancrusher fight twice in a combat phase if it’s wholly within 12” of a hero, with the usual requirement for the second swing to strike-last. It’s OK? Fighting twice is nice, but this isn’t a tricky model to kill and costing a command point is rough, it would probably have been kinder on the Mancrusher for it to be once per battle. Bet You Can’t… is a deployment ability that lets you pick one effect from a list that will apply to every Mancrusher: +1 attack in melee whilst they’re in combat range of an enemy hero or monster (note that they’re not forced to attack that hero or monster), make a normal move (with a cap of 3 Mancrushers being allowed to do it), get their control 10 back. The Mancrusher is basically fine, it does the job that it’s there to do but isn’t particularly exciting. 

More exciting is the new stuff! Boss-Stompers (370) come in units of 3 and with 12 Health are now by far the best Health density for cost in the army - though the 5+ save will see them take damage at a faster rate than a Mega. With 7 damage 2 swings and a single damage 3 ‘eadbutt attack - if all three get to swing they can lay down some serious pain into a target, and will be more consistent than a Mega-Gargant. On top of this they’ve got a reverse version of the standard 4th edition fight-after ability, where after they have a pop at something in melee they can pick a Big unit to fight after them. I’m usually down on abilities like this when there isn’t a buff involved in the ability as well, but here you’re getting a double activation out of some seriously heavy hitting units. I like this unit a lot, they’re not super flashy but they’re quite efficient and I can see them appearing a lot with how buffable they are now. Brodd, a Beast-Smasher, Ma Maegran and a unit of these gives you space for one more unit and gives an interesting base for an army, lots of variety and potential for fun combinations. 

Rock-Hurlers (380) drop their melee profile down to 4 attacks and an ‘eadbutt in favour of hurlin’ rocks at people. The rocks have a 12” range and they get 3 attacks with d3 damage. It’s actually not bad pokey shooting, and becomes quite scary if a Gatebreaker hands them extra attacks. The trick will be getting them to shoot and fight, where their combined output ends up being shockingly similar to the Boss-Stompers - they’ll just take the melee buffs a bit worse because they throw fewer dice there. Drop, Toss or Roll is a once per turn (army) ability in the shooting phase that lets you pick one effect to apply to the unit for the rest of the turn: shoot in combat for the rocks + you can covering fire in combat, +6” range, +1 to hit. This is an extremely nice selection of buffs for your unit, if you run a single unit they’re effectively going to be 3+ to hit unless at “long range” or in melee and that’s a fine thing for Destruction shooting! These are also a pretty compelling choice, and I like that both versions of this new unit feel viable and have their own roads you can go down with list construction around them. It is worth mentioning, before you have dreams of units of 6 Rock-Hurlers, that neither of these new units can be reinforced.

Kragnos, End of Empires by FreshPrinceofBielTan

Neither Big Nor Little

Kragnos (580) is still Kragnos, and Destruction players will be familiar with what he brings - he’s a fast, relatively fragile beatstick with an ability to hand out 3d6 charges to units wholly within 12”. That aura does mean Megas have to stick quite close to him, as those 130mm bases are large to be wholly within. In terms of him within the army, he can make use of one of the battle trait abilities to do d3 mortals in the combat phase, doesn’t interact with any battle formation, but can benefit from the Crit (Mortal) spell, the Heal prayer, the +3 move prayer and the +1 attack prayer. With +1 Attack and Crit Mortals, Kragnos is doing a frankly unreasonable amount of damage, and it’s not that hard to set up.

Ancient Ghyrochs (0) are along for the ride with Ma Maegran but are actually a unit of their own (and the reason why half the rules in this tome specify “Sons of Behemat Monster”, because they’re a Beast). You get two and they’re 7 Health with a 5+ save, so not particularly tough but not totally trivial for your opponent to remove, they’ll likely have to commit more than just chaff. They’re actually alright in a scrap, with damage 3 on the charge, but it’s nothing to write home about in the context of the rest of this army. They’d be decent screen clearers and flank players if they were an actual unit you could buy, but realistically these are going to be glued to Ma Maegran all game. Ability wise, all they’re doing by themselves is being a Beast and healing themselves at the end of each turn. 

Rules of Renown

Armies of Renown

I confess writing Armies of Renown for this probably was very difficult. Armies of Renown are often either gimmicks, or embrace a playstyle that might be popular to people. The problem with Sons of Behemat is the army is the gimmick. You get to pilot a handful of big models, that’s the gimmick. So where do you go from here? Basically, it’s just 2 different ways to run lots of Littles. Even knowing that, I find these utterly baffling.

Stomper Tribe

Stomper Tribe can take a single Warstomper and any non-Unique Little. That’s it. The point seems to be for people who want to run lots of Littles (who are bullied by one big guy). 

Since you can’t bring any other Bigs or Unique Littles, you won’t have any Spells or Prayers. They do have access to some cute combat tricks, to compensate for this and they’re not bad. On a 5+ you can inflict Strike Last (you get a +1 for each model in combat with the target so you’re encouraged to swarm), you can add 1 to the damage of littles or 1 to the rend of the Warstomper. You can also reroll charges, get free moves and add 1 to the characteristics of ranged weapons for Littles.

The enhancements are fine, 3D6 Charge or +2 attack for the Warstomper’s Stomp if it didn’t fight last turn isn’t bad, and a 6+ Ward aura goes a long way on the artefacts. The Littles have their own unique enhancement for Mancrusher Gargants called “Little Toes”, which are pretty standard. Run and Charge is nice, as is the ability to do the Lord “I fight then pick a unit to fight at +1” ability (in a cute subversion, it’s because the Mega is jealous of being outdone).

Overall I just don’t see it. Running lots of Little units is a legitimate strategy, but the base rules already support it quite well, and the Warstomper is pretty much the third best Mega-Gargant for running with them. Top it off with the fact that the points don’t really work and you’ll either be 10 over or massively under and this is some classic AoR filler.

Matriach’s Mob

Ma Maegran gets her own personal Retinue, so you have to include her. And then you can include any Little Unit and Ancient Ghyrochs.

As Ma Maegran is the focal point here, all of the rules are built around her. She gets a 5+ Ward whenever near another Mancrusher, and that Mancrusher gets to ignore debuffs to their hit and wound which is a nice touch. 

In the Hero Phase she can pick from a list of buffs, all of which are pretty nice. They include doing D3 mortals to enemy units within 12”, reducing an enemy control score to 1, adding +1 attack to a unit or +1 to her own casting. In the combat phase she can pick 3 Mancrusher Gargants to get Strike First, and then at the end of the turn she gets to deal a few mortals to every enemy non-Fly, non-Monster unit. It’s based on the battle round but it’s chip damage all the same.

Since the only hero here is a Unique, there are no heroic traits or artefacts of power. Instead there are 2 Unique Enhancements for the Littles. One lets you rip them back to Ma’s side if they get too far away and the other lets you heal D3 to any unit wholly within 12” of the bearer.

Finally, Ma’s spells. She actually gets a very solid, if not crazy imaginative, suite of buffs. 6+ Ward, +1 Damage and free movement in the Hero Phase (even letting them retreat if they’re in combat) is all pretty good.

I like this one a lot better than Stomper Tribe, its concept is a bit more well realized, but ultimately it just feels a bit, well, stunted.

Boss-stompers, Sons of Behemat - credit Bair

Regiments of Renown

Regiments of Renown is usually one of the more barren parts of the book but because this army was more or less designed with the idea of allying with other armies in mind, it has quite a few here as the “named” Gargants.

Odo Godswallow

Ally to any Destruction faction, Odo Godswallow is the Beast-smasher Gargant. He picks up a 6+ Ward and a Combat trick to gain Crit (2 Hits) but only against a Monster and all swings must go toward that. It’s alright, very Anti-Monster tech which are big right now due to the Fury Mechanic in Aqshy encouraging bringing Monsters with big damage numbers but his abilities really want another Gargant with him and so he won’t be bringing those with him.

Bundo Whalebiter

A Kraken-eater who can join any Order or Destruction faction. He is Dead Cunning, For a Gargant which is a combat trick that gives him Strike-Last and a 5+ Ward but he gets one of 3 effects:

  • Shut off non-Companion weapon abilities
  • +1 to hit rolls and ignore negative modifiers to hit and wound
  • Enemy Units have a control score of 1

Honestly? All really solid options. Get in your opponent's face and then tank the hit and choose the best option to shut them down. If you shut off weapon abilities, he can probably survive attacks from a lot of the game’s hammers. Then you can follow up for the swing.

One-Eyed Grunnock

Chaos and Destruction factions, this is a Warstomper. He can retreat and charge, and also subtracts 1 from hit rolls for enemy infantry and cav he’s in combat with. So he can work alongside your squishier units to make them more difficult to kill, which isn’t a bad little trick.

Big Drogg Fort-Kicka

Death and Destruction get a Gatebreaker. Fortress Wrekka is a bit anemic, basically when you choose the ability to rip up a terrain piece to add +1 to your boulder’s attacks, you can roll a number of dice equal to the battle round and for each 4+ add another. Far as I can tell, this stacks with the base ability, but it’s once per game and often games are over by like round 3 so you likely won’t see much use from this.

Grievous Halitosis is pretty funny though. A horde clearer at end of turn, roll a number of dice equal to models in a unit, each 6 is a mortal. Usually these aren’t great but if it kills anything, their control is dropped to 1. This is pretty good if you need to fight 2 targets, kill the bigger one then breathe on the horde to make them impotent and snap up the objective. Shame you can’t bring the Aqshy Gatebreaker.

Krong the Club

This is the Stumeblefoot Gargant, they just gave him a name. It’s a Mancrusher who can be taken by any army in the game and his ability is…strange. If he dies, he triggers Devastating Collapse and you roll off and if you win, you deal 3 mortals to every unit within his combat range. He also has Jump Up and Down which is a combat trick that lets him do a horde clearer where you roll dice equal to the models in the enemy unit, each 6+ is a mortal but if he gets any 1s he does the same thing he does when he dies (he doesn’t actually die, but he will inflict mortals on himself so he might, which would trigger it again).

So basically he's just a bomb you send into the enemy and hope he does enough mortal wounds on the way out.

Final Thoughts

Great idea design team, let’s make every Sons of Behemat army cost nearly £700.

Cynicism aside, I think this is a legitimately good direction for the army to move in. This tome represents probably the biggest change in identity for an AoS army since Nighthaunt. I think most people agree the Nighthaunt tome was a bit of a failure in that regard, but I’m much more confident here. The traditional Gargant gameplay of being a ‘DPS check’ army that stood on circles and invited their opponents to try and shift them was pretty negative to play against, and not particularly interesting or exciting for the Gargant player. The desired shift in gameplay here is clear: be aggressive, present a lot of threat and hit your opponent hard. Whether 5 less-healthy Gargants (or equivalent) running across the table at you is any more fun to play against remains to be seen, but it feels like the best thematic fit for a Sons of Behemat army design yet. 

I appreciate that a lot of effort has gone into trying to give Gargant players a lot of open choices: the differences in Megas being tied to the abilities that they bring rather than their output, the three tables of six enhancements to choose from, the large expansion of unit types for the army. If I had a concern here though, it’s that a couple of design choices could ruin this intent by making certain choices a no-brainer. The presence of powerful spell and prayer lores being tied to named characters or a particular artefact is particularly rough and will lead to pretty formulaic choices at the top end of list construction. Due to the way the army works, I don't know that this is an issue that can actually be addressed with points without breaking things and that a lot of the good work that went into this tome will get buried by a handful of options. Still, a step in the right direction for an army that's been a bit of a nightmare to balance ever since its inception.
