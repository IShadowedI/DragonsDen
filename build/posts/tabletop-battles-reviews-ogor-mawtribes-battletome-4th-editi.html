Tabletop Battles Reviews: Ogor Mawtribes Battletome 4th Edition

July 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer
As always we’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of the Battletome. We have also been provided with a provisional Battle Profile list for the army to use when writing the review, but please note that these points are subject to change until they are published on Warhammer Community.

IT'S.

OGOR.

TIME.

Ogors have had a pretty decent track record through 4th. They launched in a decent place, with Gluttons and Ironguts being solid infantry pieces with their high wound count and rather high damage. If an Ironguts unit got into the most powerful unit the opponent had to pop their Fight Twice ability, it pretty much was guaranteed they'd pick them up. Scourge of Ghyran was unexpectedly kind to the Ironblaster, despite being a ranged unit in the season notoriously bad for ranged units; it was a pretty nice piece of damage and could present a real challenge for any targets who left themselves in the open.

With the new book bringing about a whole bunch of new infantry models and retiring old stuff, how will things shake out for the Followers of the Gulping God?

Battle Traits

You get a veritable buffet of Battle Traits to work with although some are a bit specialized. Eat ‘Em Alive is the headliner, letting you pick each Ogre that fought this turn and an enemy unit in combat with them. The enemy unit takes D3 mortals (not on a 2+!) and you get a buff to apply to the unit for the rest of the battle. These don’t stack and you can’t do mortals to the same enemy unit more than once, but there’s a great toolbox here. Raw and Bloody Flesh gives the unit -1 to incoming rend, which is a great defensive bonus for your pretty elite army. Crunchy Bones gives the unit Crit (2 hits) or Crit (Mortal) if they already have them. Exotic Giblets is a bit more situational but nonetheless powerful, giving -1 to Ward rolls this unit attacks. Steaming Brains gives the unit +2 to control, letting them bully objectives much more effectively. Slick Innards  gives the unit Run and Charge, which isn’t as good as you might think considering if they’re fighting they probably are already where they need to be, but it’ll make your opponent think twice about cheap screens or pin pieces. Lastly there’s Mystery Meat which makes Wizards and Priests -1 power level, which is pretty specific but can be crippling in the right contexts.

Bull Charge is the next ability in the roster and it's an interesting one. You can pick one unit that charged and didn’t use a Rampage ability yet to use this ability (note, this isn’t a rampage ability). You pick an enemy unit in combat with that unit and your opponent needs to move them up to 3”. They can elect to not move them, but after the move you roll for every model in the Ogre unit and on a 3+ (or 2+ if they’re Gutbusters) you do a mortal wound. So if you set your guys up right and they don’t move, that’s gonna be a lot of mortals. This effectively either can dish out a ton of mortals for your larger units or can force screens out of the way or enemies off objectives. After all of this has resolved you get another free Pile In move. This is even more extra movement on top of everything else, which makes this ability a mighty powerful one. If nothing else you can always just charge and use this ability just for the extra pile in.

Lastly is the pair of abilities Jaws of the Beast which lets you pick a Beastclaw Hero leading a regiment and a unit in that regiment to go in reserves and Closing the Jaws which lets you deploy them within 9” of a table edge. Beyond just the standard good reasons to have things come in from reserves, there are a lot of Beastclaw units that have some pretty solid shooting, which can make this ability a great way to support the anvil of your force.

Ogor Irongut - @mawqueenminis

Battle Formations

You get the standard 4 formations to work with. First is Hunger-Filled Tribe which gives you an extra 3” move for an Ogre unit that hasn’t powered through and isn’t in combat, which can be used to move them into combat. This is great for locking down enemy units staging for a turn or just getting some extra movement. Next up is Vanguard of the Mawpath which lets you pick a unit to move 3” after charging if it rolling an 8+. This seems, just worse than Hunger-Filled Tribe, but it can let you set up some interesting Bull Charge shenanigans. Prowling Predators is an interesting one which gives Ogors -1 to hit if they’re wholly within 9” of a battlefield edge and didn’t charge. This is good for staging and works well with your deep striking shooting units. Lastly there’s Maw-Cult Fanatics which lets you heal D3 off of Eat ‘Em Alive. This is probably the default pick, but extra movement is hard to argue with and the defensive buffs of -1 to hit can be very strong for this army.

Heroic Traits

You get 3 Heroic traits to use as is standard at this point. First is Leave Not a Morsel which is your standard “Soulblight cannot play the game” enhancement, stopping things within 6” from being healed or allowing models to be returned to them. Dreaded Far and Wide lets you ignore modifiers to control, Hits and Wounds, and charge rolls. This is a great all around pick, allowing your guy to attack with impunity. Lastly there’s The Crusherguts which lets you roll against an enemy unit’s Health characteristic and kill a model if the roll exceeds it. This one’s solid as the rest but its a bit disappointing there isn’t a force multiplier trait here to help the rest of your units.

Artefacts

Artefacts also get the standard set of 3. First is Trophy Rack which gives you either Anti Monster, War Machine or Cavalry, which you pick during deployment. The flexibility is nice here and makes using this enhancement a better pill to swallow even though its scope is a bit narrow. Carvalox Hide gives you a 6+ Ward or 4+ from spells and prayers. This is nifty on something like a Thundertusk or big monster but the 4+ ward rider is pretty situational. Lastly there’s Mantle of Entrails which once per battle lets you pick an enemy unit in a Hero Phase and for the rest of the turn, you turn off commands on a 3+ for that unit. This requires some setting up, but can be devastating if used correctly. Like the Heroic Traits this is missing some kind of force multiplier to help your other units (Scourge of Aqshy has you covered if you're looking for those), but there are some strong picks here.

Spell Lore

You get the standard 3 spell lore here. Your unlimited spell, Blood Feast, gives a simply +1 to hit out. Considering your whole army hits on 4’s, you’ll get a lot of mileage out of this although its probably not as good as the extra attack it used to give. Next is Shincruncher which does a D3 Mortals each time an enemy unit targeted by this spell finishes a Move, this is good denial and can be devastating if you can set it up correctly. Lastly there’s Tallowflage which can hide an Ogre unit from enemies outside 12”, very useful considering how hard it can be to hide your units from shooting. Overall this is a great spell lore although with the exception of Blood Feast it all goes off on a 7, so you’ll need the new Gutseers to keep these reliable.

Prayer Lore

Huskards are still here and are still Priests so you still get a prayer lore. Your unlimited prayer is Freezing Tailwinds which grants +1 to charge on a 3+ or +2 on a 6+, nifty for getting your models where they need to be. Pulverising Hailstorm goes off on a 4+ and does a flat 3 Mortals and on an 8+ allows you to pick a second target, its not flashy but it removes a lot of variance from the equation. Lastly there’s Fortifying Hoarfrost, which gives a unit -1 to wound on a 4+ and -1 incoming Rend on a 8+. This works really well with the army trait and the -1 to hit formation, making for some horrifyingly durable units.

Units

Ogor Gluttons and Tyrant
Credit: @badusernametag

You probably expected that quite a few units would get Legends, and the expected suspects are here. Firebelly, Icefall Yhetees and Frost Sabers are in resin purgatory and Leadbelchers did not receive a suitable replacement with the new Ogor kit. Rather surprisingly, Gnoblars also got the boot. The kit was pretty damn old, and they always felt weird within the army, but it does leave the army without a cheap screening unit. Also surprisingly, the Great Mawpot is gone without explanation. 

As with previous Legends models, you do still have a year to use them, and they keep their old warscrolls.

Monster/Warmachine Heroes

We’ll kick off with the big lady herself, Morga the Mighty. She’s a monster with a 10” Move, 16 Wounds, a 3+ Save and 10 Control. This gives her a pretty beefy defensive profile that, when combined with the other defensive buffs in this book, makes for a very difficult to shift unit. Offensively, she has two profiles. The Meatfist Masher is 4 attacks with 3+ to hit, 2+ to wound, -2 rend and 3 damage with Crit (mortal) while Grutta her mount has 8 Attacks with 4+ to hit, 2+ to wound, -2 rend and 3 damage with Charge (+1 Damage). This is a pretty nasty offensive profile that can mess up most units in the game. Her abilities are nothing to scoff at either. An Ogor’s Ogor allows her to have every Eat `em Alive ability active if she can keep using the ability. -1 incoming rend and Run and Charge are probably the go to ones to rush for but after that things get somewhat situational. A Light Snack has you roll a D6 and if it’s greater than the health of something in combat with Morga, you automatically slay the model. This gets dicey on units with more than 3 health, but eating an Annihilator is a pretty tasty prospect.

She also has a command ability, The Right Motivation, which is an interesting buff. You pick an Ogor Infantry unit and for the rest of combat if that unit isn’t hurt this phase they reflect mortals on incoming 1’s to hit and if they are hurt they get +1 attacks. This incentivizes you to pick a unit that you plan on fighting with later in combat, so they can benefit from both effects which can also have your opponent think twice on who to allocate attacks to. Lastly, there’s Been There, Ate That which Heals this unit D3 wounds if she killed a non-monster this turn, or D3+3 if she contributed to killing a monster. This can be combined with the Maw-Cult Fanatics formation to make for some brutal healing effects. Overall Morga is a pure and simple powerful piece on the field. There’s not a lot of subtlety to her presence on the battlefield, but she’s an Ogor so that’s expected. There are some amazing buffs you can stack on her to make her -1 to hit, wound, and -2 incoming Rend all before factoring all out defense as well as the ability to give her a 6+ ward. This is hard to line up, but the 3+ save enables some great defensive capabilities.

Next up is Redd The Maw. Redd and his jolly band are a Wizard (2), Warmachine with 18 Health, a 6” Move, 10 Control and a 4+ save. Combat wise they get two profiles that are both 4+ to hit, 2+ to wound and rend 2 with 5 attacks doing 3 damage and 8 doing 2. Could they have just averaged this out to just be all 3 damage attacks? Probably, but they wanted to differentiate I guess. The abilities are where this model really shines though. Son of the Hungering one gives all Ogors a 6+ ward and a 5+ ward to Mawseekers. This includes Butchers, Cleavers, Gutseers and Gorgers. You were probably going to take some Gutseers to help make sure Redd can get his spells off consistently so a buff is nice for him, and Cleavers with a 5+ ward are a force to be reckoned with. Next up is From the Depths of the Cauldron which is a 1CP command that lets a non-Hero unit use the Eat `em Alive ability. This unlocks a lot, most notably is probably run and charge but getting these abilities online before you commit to combat will help your Ogors hit as hard as they need to. Lastly, there’s his Warscroll spell The Maw Opens which goes off on a 7+ and has you pick an Ogor unit to move D3+3” if its not in combat towards the nearest enemy and if it is in combat instead do D3 Mortals as well as heal D3. There’s an added bonus that if the target is a Mawseekers unit you can change those D3s to flat 3s, which can be pretty nasty combined with using Eat `em Alive. If you’re looking to do a Mawseekers focused force, Redd is an auto-include since he gives them the staying power necessary to stay on the table. Outside those armies, he still has a lot of value from being a Wizard 2 and a pretty durable and lethal piece while enabling some early alpha strikes.

Lastly for named characters is Kragnos who does the same thing he does for all Destruction armies. The 3d6 charge for everyone is still the headline, and combining that with Run and Charge is pretty gnarly.

The Tyrant on Glutthorn is the generic version of Morga and is very similar. They have the same defensive profile, as well as the same offensive profile but hitting on a 4+ for the rider’s attacks and one less attack for the mount. They get 2 abilities, with the first being Pitiless Warlord which lets you reroll 1 Dice from a charge roll made wholly within `12” once per turn and Glutthorn Stampede which imposes Strikes-Last on a unit charged by this unit on a 4+ or 3+ if the target is Infantry. This is a pretty solid offensive piece which can help almost any list. Both of it’s abilities being Once per Turn Per army is a bit of an issue, but I can see a list where you take a couple of these.

Thundertusks and Stonehorns return and so do their riders. Stats wise, they’re all the same with 14 wounds on a 14+ that can move 10”. Stone Horns also keep their Stone Skeleton Rule which ignores the first point of damage each phase while Thundertusks keep their Ice Blast ranged weapon. 

Ogor Thundertusk - @mawqueenminis

For the Huskards the ranged weapons profiles have all been simplified to a unified 1 Shot weapon that hits on 4+, wounds on a 3+ is rend -1 and Damage 3. This is probably for the best since there was always a best option but at 1 shot this is probably not going to be very impactful. Huskards are also both still Priest 1, and your army’s only priest. The Huskard on Stonehorn lets you pick either a friendly or enemy unit in combat and on a 4+, or 2+ if this unit charged this turn, either gives a friendly unit’s Companion Weapons Crit (2 Hits) or makes an enemy unit -1 Attacks. This is a major glow up from their Index ability and can be a huge problem for certain enemy units with high model counts and 2-3 attacks. The Huskard on Thundertusk meanwhile has the ability Freezing Aura which now gives an enemy unit -1 attacks on a 3+ if the Huskard didn’t charge this turn, and otherwise it has Winter’s Cruelty which forces -1 Damage on an enemy unit if you can roll below the damage done with the Ice Blast. This can be done in any shooting phase and while the Ice Blast hits on a 4+ this can be devastating if used in conjunction with the previous abilities.

Frostlords meanwhile are more offensively focused. The Frostlord on Stonehorn keeps its Earth-Shattering Charge ability, which is good since this ability can allow you to do all sorts of positioning tricks, and gains the Enraged Roar ability which gives Companion weapons of Ogor units within combat range +1 Attacks. Unfortunately this doesn't work on the model itself, but buffing up Grunta or other Beastclaws has value. The Frostlord on Thundertusk meanwhile gives a nearby Ogor unit the ability to Shoot and Charge after Retreating or Running that turn on a 3+ with Blessed of the Old Ice and Sweeping Tusks is the same old crowd buster that it had before but without the 3+ stipulation. With most of these abilities being Once per Turn per Army, you probably won’t see these spammed like back in 3rd edition, but these absolutely have a place in the army.

Infantry Heroes

There’s a lot of these, so let’s hit the exciting new stuff first. Grell Firefist is basically a named Tyrant but she doesn’t fit well into any of the on foot archetypes. She carries around a huge cannon that has a range of 12”, but can double their attacks within 6”. If she shoots a unit in combat with a friendly unit, that unit gets a free retreat and they can still charge. Her last ability is probably the most dangerous, where if an enemy unit uses a command ability within 6” of her, she can deal D3 mortals to a nearby friendly unit to counterspell that ability. While they get their command point back, the ability counts as being used so they can’t just use it again elsewhere. Overall she seems really good, real good. I think she’ll be a staple in a lot of lists.

The Tyrant…Ok it’s not new but got a new model. Their combat profile got an extra attack which is appreciated, they also got their Big Names back, giving one of three choices while within combat with this unit.:

  • Enemy Infantry with 1-2 Health cannot hold objectives
  • Enemy Cavalry cannot use Retreat abilities
  • Enemy Monsters cannot use Rampages

This is a deployment phase ability so you get to wait to see what your opponent’s list looks like before picking, which is nice. It also has the standard foot lord ability of “I fight, then pick a unit to fight” with a +1 to hit, which is nice for the Ogor’s 4+ to hit.

The other resurrected unit, the Butcher is probably going in every list, simply for being your lone non-Unique wizard option. It can hand out a 6+ Ward to any unit, or a 5+ to a Mawgorger unit (which includes the new Cleavers and Gutseers) on a 3+, and while it’s just power level 1, it bumps up to power level 2 once you do any damage with its melee weapon or a manifestation it summoned, so with the right luck you can get that by the end of round 1 or 2.

The Bloodpelt Hunter has been reworked in a way that I think works to his benefit. His melee weapons were rolled together into one, with 4 attacks at D3+1. On average that’s a lot better damage wise, and it still does max damage to Monsters. It can also “Tag” a unit that it shot to give all Beastclaw units a +1 to hit. In addition to the obvious Stonehorns and Thundertusks, it includes the new Mawbeasts and all the “hunter” type units so it’s a pretty wide range.

Lastly the Mantrapper stays. Completely unchanged, strange he even showed up.

Infantry

First of course we have to talk about Gluttons, your bread and butter unit. Their attack profile remains unchanged, but with them transitioning to a 5 man unit instead of 6, they technically do have less attacks than before. They get +1 to save if they didn’t charge, so their primary purpose is hanging out on your objectives to protect them, but a reinforced unit can do some serious damage with Bull Charge.

Ogor Irongut Unit - @mawqueenminis

Similarly, Ironguts kept their profile but went from a 4 man unit to 3, in line with Sigmar’s more elite infantry, so it is lost damage. The real kick in the head is that they lost their swing twice once per game, which really drags down their damage output. Instead their role is now as a bodyguard for heroes, giving both a 5+ Ward, and that they can do Bull Rush even if someone else did, which does help bring up the damage a little bit. Overall though, these guys are not the must take they used to be, even if they’re still a threat.

Our last returning infantry unit is the Gorger Mawpack and they are absolutely identical to the original. Good stuff, deep strike unit that can pop up behind the enemy and has a decent punch to them even if they can’t necessarily take it in kind.

Enough with the old stuff, let’s check out the new stuff. While Frost Sabres are gone, the sort of but not really replacement is the Hunters with Sabrefangs. They’re a neat little unit, during deployment they can be teleported wholly within 3” of a terrain feature (but can’t move the first turn), and then have a solid shooting attack that is marred by the fact that only 3 out of 5 of the unit has crossbows. They’re also OK in combat but with a 5+ Save and no ward they’ll cave pretty fast. Might be worth including to overwatch over an objective to put the opponent off their game (unfortunately due to the 3” thing you can’t be put on an objective).

The new dual kit, the Cleavers and Gutseers have an Ironguts attack statline but much better combat abilities, so they seem to be aspiring to replace the Ironguts and letting the Ironguts take up a defense role instead. Cleavers can roll a die and on a 3+ they can get Crit (2 Hits) or Crit Mortals if they already have it (most likely from Eat Em’ Alive) which means they can be devastating if set up right. I think you bring at least 1 reinforced block of these guys.

Gutseers are a weird unit, they have a similar statline to Cleavers, but they give +1 to cast/chant and spells can be cast through them. It’s a weird role to be in, with a statline like that you want them up front, but they seem to exist more to hang back and support casters.

Cavalry

The new Dual kit for cav comes in a melee and ranged variety. The melee version, the Maulbeast Cavalry is a weird switch hitter. The combat profile is Ironguts for the riders, and almost the same for the beasts (with one worse rend, and 2 damage, but it has charge so it hashes out). If they charge, they can cause -1 to wound to an enemy unit on a 3+, and if they are charged then Charge abilities don’t work. This lets them fulfill a dual role of strong cav on the charge and a very tank anvil when charged. At 3+ Save and 7 wounds, that’s a lot to carve through.

The ranged variant the Maulbeast Raiders have an OK ranged attack, 3 shots at Damage 2 from 15”. They can move D6” if an enemy unit isn’t within 9” of them in the enemy movement phase, and it doesn’t count as a Move ability so you can then redeploy after if you want. Their melee weapon attacks are naturally not that impressive, but the mounts are just as good so if you have to charge, they can still fight. It’s still a bit of a weird inclusion with an army that’s getting a lot of crossbow units now.

Ogor Maulbeast - @mawqueenminis

Warmachine

There was much lamentation when the Scourge of Ghyran versions of these units went away but they’ve returned here mostly intact.

The Ironblaster got its SoG statblock which is a huge upgrade, the included ability is…less impressive. Instead of shooting normally you can pick an enemy unit within 18 and draw a line, On a 3+ it deals that many mortals to the target and then D3 mortals to each unit in the way on a 3+. This isn’t really a good trade unless there’s a very appealing line in the way, but its there if the setup happens. While this technically means the unit lost Crit (2 Hits) and an attack, Rend 2 Damage 5 is pretty damn good.

Even though Gnoblars are gone, the Scraplauncher remains. It got a new twist on the SoG ability, that I think can potentially work out better. Instead of halving moment, it reduces movement by the damage points allocated to the unit, so especially on slower armies you can reduce them to a dead stop if you roll well enough. Instead of reducing the die on a charge roll on a 5+, it can shut off Run and Charge abilities on a 3+ (don’t forget that Redeploy is a Run ability!). Still dicey, but at least a touch more reliable.

Monster

You have your Beastriders, of Stonehorn and Thundertusk variety. Both had a lot of consolidation done to their warscroll attacks, ranged weapons and are all identical, Punches and Kicks are no longer useless, and the Hooves got rolled into the Horns attack so it’s not random attacks with random damage. Genuinely, great stuff.

The Stonehorn keep their Stone Skeleton ability that their hero cousins have and picked up Stonehorn Avalanche as their new Rampage, which on a 3+ does 3 Mortals to a unit or 6 to a terrain piece. It’s not crazy but not needing to roll after passing the 3+ is nice. You also can’t use it in combination with Bull Charge, but thats ok, this is basically better damage for them.

Thundertusks have a unique ability to let a non-reinforced infantry unit within 6” hitch a ride on them, removing them from the field and then putting them back down after the Thundertusks moved. The downside is they can’t charge after, but depending on what you want to do with them that’s a free 10” of movement (it can be used after the infantry unit moved, but not set up). Plus it can teleport your heroes if you need to get them across the field fast to set up an aura. They also have an ability to inflict a -1 to hit in the combat phase but at a 4+, that’s a bit rough, not super reliable.

Terrain

The Mawpot is gone, likely because it was an older kit and Ogors being the only army with 2 Faction Terrain features makes it feel like to be an experiment they tried and decided wasn’t worth the effort. That means you only have the Mawpit which may be frustrating to hear for some players that didn’t see the need to switch over to it in the past.

It fulfills a similar role as its previous incarnation, dishing out D3 mortals to enemy units but it has an interesting game attached to it now. At the end of each player’s turn you can pick a terrain piece within 12” to be a Sinkhole, which then does D3 mortals to every enemy unit within 3” of both itself and the targeted terrain piece. The range of this increases by 3” every battle round after the first, or increases by 6” if there’s a Butcher garrisoned inside.

The warscroll seems to have a typo on it, because it says you have to pick a terrain piece you “already picked” to be a Sinkhole. We assume the intent is a terrain piece you didn’t already pick, otherwise it’d be impossible to use and the intent is likely that you have to choose a different terrain piece each time you use this ability.

Overall, it’s fine. It’s a bit finicky but its a free ability and is great for pinging opponents who hide around obscuring terrain, or just in general like Sylvaneth.

Rules of Renown

Army of Renown - Beastclaw Alfrostun

Cow Town is still around with this Army of Renown! Beastclaw Alfrostun is focused on your large furry friends as well as some of your hunter-themed units. At first glance, it would seem like this would be the “push my big guys into my opponent’s mouth” type of AoR, but it actually has some neat tricks and synergies using some infantry units and shooting. 

Battle Traits

First off is Masked by the Endless White, which sounds like what I do in my room by myself sometimes. This ability makes your Alfrostun units (which is the keyword to every unit in this AoR) hidden to enemy units that are more than 12” away. This is great for those Stonehorns and Thundertusks you don’t want getting turned into goo by a Chaos Dwarf Deathshrieker on the first turn. Run ‘Em Down does an extra D3 damage to enemy units that try to retreat from friendly units. Once you’ve put your opponent in Cow Town, they either have to stay in this Ice Age edition of Monster Jam or take some big ouchies on their way out. 

A Clan of Hunters allows two units of Hunters with Sabrefangs to use their warscroll ability that gives them a way to infiltrate the middle of the board during deployment. Any potential access to movement and positioning based shenanigans is a win in my book. Grasp of the Everwinter is a callback to what I have always called the “because I’m cold mortals.” This ability remains mostly the same, where you pick every enemy unit in combat with any number of your units, and on a 2+ on a D3 you deal that many mortals to the enemy. Last off we have Fearsome Breed, which is luckily a noun and not a verb. This makes your large adult sons (quadruped monster connotation) more difficult to be charged, reducing charge rolls by 1” within 12” of a friendly monster, and 2” within 6”! Any additional help that your trucks get to survive is super important. 

Heroic Trait

Hard-Bitten Chieftain makes it so weapon abilities other than Companion that target this unit by units that have charged in the turn have no effect. This is a fantastic addition to give some more resilience to your favorite hero. 

Artefact of Power

The Alvagr Rune-Tokens give you a once per battle +2 to chants for every friendly Alfrostun unit wholly within 12” of the unit with this artefact. I much prefer a massive once per game buff that gets used and is ‘point and click’ rather than something that requires a dice roll. With the plethora of priests and prayers in Ogors, I think this is a great item.

Prayer Lore

Keening Gale is back, and is your unlimited prayer for this AoR. After picking a friendly Alfrostun unit wholly within 12”, on a 3+ you can add 2 to the run rolls for the target, and on a 6+ that unit also gets +2 to charge rolls. When you’re ready to belly-bump your opponent’s models with your army, you can pump out a bunch of chants of this prayer to get them in there! Otherwise, this is still solid to give any of your units an additional little boost to get them where they need to be. 

Terrors of the Old Ice is a pretty gnarly trick that can completely change the tide of a game. On a 4+, the objective or terrain feature wholly within 18” that you picked subtract 10 from the control scores of enemy units while they are contesting the target. On an 8+, the max score allowed by enemy units contesting the target is 1! 18” is a massive range, and having a Huskard on Big Friend with Big Base size makes it even more abusable. I particularly love this prayer since it synergizes well with the Monster style that the Beastclaw generally has and being able to make it difficult for your opponent to sneak objectives off of you. 

Last but not least we have “Big Ogor Yell,” or as it’s written in the book, Avalanche Voice. In summary, after picking an enemy unit within 18”, and on a 4+, it allows you to roll a dice whenever that unit is the target of a command until the start of your next turn. On a 5+, that command costs 1 additional CP and also has no effect. If the chant roll was an 8+, it cost 2 additional CP! This prayer seems pretty insane, as you can potentially have your opponent burn through all of their CP for no benefit to them. The 5+ restriction is pretty risky, so I’m guessing this prayer is pretty much only going to be used when you have an extra chant and nothing else to use it on. 

Thoughts

Although the “Monster” AoR for Ogors isn’t the traditional “plow your models into your opponent and charge mortal them into the stratosphere” that many would expect, but I really like how well rounded Beastclaw Alfrostun’s playstyle seems to be on paper. I think even within the AoR itself we should see a decent amount of list variety. 

Army of Renown - Meatfist Mawtribe

Guys, Gals, and all their Ogor pals on foot are hitting the front lines in this belly focused AoR. Meatfist Mawtribe has your ordinary Gutbusters getting into the slog and staying in it, while also making sure your opponent has no choice but to aid in the satiation of hunger for the Meatfists. 

Battle Traits

Bloodthirsty Arrogance bounces back a mortal for save rolls of 1 from your Meatfist Infantry during the combat phase. The other quick and dirty passives that this AoR gives are Fleshy Stampede, which gives +2 to run rolls, and Gluttonous Conquerors, which increases your Control characteristic by 1 if you are in combat. They are nothing too crazy, but these abilities give some extra ‘oomf’ to the belly brigade. 

Meaty Charge will feel right at home for Ogors players…mostly. This gives you your impact mortals on the charge again, rolling the amount of dice equal to the charge roll. The caveat here is now the mortals are on a 5+, and go to a 4+ if you have more models in your unit than the enemy unit. It’s one more hoop to jump through, but I’ll take it. It is not limited to Once per Army so that means MSU mortal bombing your opponent in the charge phase is absolutely still a viable and exciting strategy. The Unstoppable Feast synergizes well with your +2 to run rolls passive, allowing you to point and click one unit in your charge phase to be able to use a charge even if it did a Run or Retreat previously in the turn. Being able to retreat and charge is insane too, so if you get pinned you can still shimmy and charge to try and get yourself out of a bind with the mortals from Meaty Charge. 

The last two traits to round out the Meatfist suite are Greedy Chompers and Gruesome Devourers. The Greedy Chompers give 3 of your units in combat a Heal (x) on a 2+ from a dirty D3, equal to the roll during the end of any turn. Gruesome Devourers is also at the end of any turn, where you get to disable Retreat abilities from an enemy unit that had any damage inflicted by a friendly unit during the turn on a 3+, until your next turn. This can be a neat way to dissuade your opponent from taking a double turn. 

Ogor Irongut - @mawqueenminis

Heroic Traits

Since there is no Prayer Lore for this AoR, we have ironically been blessed with a few choices on Heroic Traits. First off is Thick Blubber, giving the equipped hero -1 to be wounded in shooting. This seems like a teams or meta counter pick, but isn’t too exciting otherwise. Next up is Force of Savage Personality, forcing all enemy Manifestations to be set up outside of 12” of this unit rather than the normal 9”. That’s all fine and dandy, but the real sauce is Overpowering Mass, giving you re-rolls of 1 on your meaty charge rolls. I imagine this is the one we will be seeing the most of, as re-rolls are hard to come by as-is but also being able to have another chance to convert the meaty charge dice into more mortal wounds is fantastic. 

Artefacts of Power

Akin to the Heroic Traits, we have a few Artefacts to choose from as well. Snott, Gnoblar Thief can yoink your opponent’s artefact so they can’t use it! At the start of any turn, you pick a visible enemy hero that has an artefact and roll a dice. If the roll is equal to or lower than the current battle round, that hero no longer has an artefact. This seems very fun and exciting, but in my opinion I believe there are some more impactful choices that one could make – such as the Rusty Leadbelcher Gun. Once per battle in your shooting phase, either deal D3 damage and half the move characteristic of a unit that lost any models from this ability, or inflict D6 mortals to the target, which is an enemy unit within 12”. A once per game point and click mortal ping is pretty neat, and the half move option is a cool trick that can certainly be relevant in some matchups. 2” move Dwarves are certainly less scary. Last is the Trapper’s Arsenal, a once per battle ability in the combat phase where you pick an enemy unit that charged this turn, and on a 4+ they have Strike-Last for the rest of the turn. For a once per battle ability, still requiring a 4+ is pretty steep, and I don’t see this Artefact ever being used. 

Thoughts

Although the Artefacts are pretty lackluster for this AoR, the Battle Traits more than make up for it. With great mobility, healing, and mortal output, I think we will be seeing plenty of the Meatfist Mawtribe hitting tables and charging armies soon enough.

Army of Renown - Mawseeker Gollop

What’s cool about this Army of Renown is that no matter what list you put together, you’ll be cooking either way. It is all about picking the right Feastmaster and spending the raw ingredients gathered from destroying enemy units. 

Battle Traits

It’s time to Prepare the Feast! This ability simply allows you to pick a friendly hero to be considered the Feastmaster, who, for the rest of the battle, is the catalyst for many of your auras and buffs triggered by spending the ingredients gained. The ability allowing you to gain ingredients is Ohh, That Looks Tasty, which explains that the number of raw ingredients gained is based on the health characteristic and the number of starting models multiplied together – IE if the destroyed unit has a health characteristic of 2 wounds and a starting size of 10 models, it would give 20 raw ingredients when it is destroyed. Now that we understand how we gain our fix-ins for our meaty brew, how do we use these on the battlefield? By cooking up recipes of course!

Gland Goulash costs 10 raw ingredients in any Hero Phase to give your casters wholly within 12” of your Feastmaster +1 Dice to cast! Next on the menu is Bone-Slurry Soup, which for 5 ingredients you get to heal D6 to each friendly unit wholly within 12” of your Master of Feasts in the shooting phase. For this next course in the combat phase, The Organ Platter gives Crit Mortals to every friendly unit wholly within 12” of the Feastmaster for 20 ingredients. During the movement phase, serving Unwashed Innards for 5 ingredients gives +4 to run rolls for friendly units wholly within 12” of a friendly Feastmaster. Who doesn’t like some Fried Limbs? During the charge phase, for 10 ingredients you can add +3 to charge rolls for friendly units that are wholly 12” around the Feastmaster. For dessert(?) we have Gore-offal Dumplings, allowing every friendly unit wholly within 12” to use a charge ability during the End of Any Turn with 1D6 instead of 2. This 10 ingredient recipe can surely cause some pretty tasty plays. 

Heroic Trait

All of this…interesting food has given one of your heroes Indomitable Guts, which adds 1 to ward rolls for that unit. 

Artefact of Power

Overflowing Larder, which is also what they called me in highschool, gives you a once per battle raw ingredient gain of D6, or D3+3 if this unit is your Feastmaster in your hero phase. 

Spell Lore

The unlimited spell is Bonecruncher, an 18” D3 mortal zap that gives you raw ingredients equal to the damage inflicted for 5 power level. Next is Bloodgruel, where on a 7 you pick a friendly unit wholly within 12” to either Heal 6, or add a slain model back to the unit! With the average amount of wounds per model in this AoR being around 4, this is a great way to resupply your forces. Lastly, you can teleport your hungry horde on a 6 using The Cosmos Writhes, picking a unit wholly within 12” and setting it up again anywhere on the battlefield outside of 9” of your opponent’s units. 

Thoughts

This AoR has a LOT to unpack, but I’m loving not only the flavor but also how in depth the ingredient engine is. I’m looking forward to seeing how people mold this AoR into a Rube Goldberg machine of delectable combos and synergies. 

Regiments of Renown

Okar’s Torrbad can be taken by any other Destruction army, and includes:
  • 1 Huskard on Stonehorn
  • 1 Thundertusk Beastrider

What makes this regiment really interesting is it ditches the usual synergies and actually works on any friendly unit, and not just their own guys. The Huskard on Stonehorn gets a prayer (CV4) that grants the following to 3 friendly units within 12”:

  • +3 to run rolls
  • Can retreat and charge
  • Does not take damage for retreating

The Thundertusk Beastrider, meanwhile, lets any infantry unit that isn’t reinforced grab on for the ride, using their warscroll ability. The fact that this grants cross-faction synergy actually makes this very usable and while it’s no Hurtlin’ Hogz or Cogfort, I can see playing this in a lot of Ork lists (both kinds).

The second Regiment is a bonafide mercenary unit, Urgarr's Maulerguts usable by a fairly hefty group of factions from all the grand alliances. It includes:

  • 1 Tyrant on Glutthorn
  • 2 Maulbeast Cavalry

They’re not super interesting however. They just can go into reserves and then drop in on the flanks during your movement phase. It’s not terribly exciting as most factions will have better cav options on hand.

Final Thoughts

Overall the book is solid. It's a huge change up from the Faction Pack that makes the army less straightforward but packs a ton of interesting movement tools and cascading buffs that reward running over the enemy and killing lots of stuff. This is an army that rewards building momentum and continuing to build on that throughout the game, which feels very thematic.

Though the army is less of a blunt instrument than before, it's capable of a lot of damage when maneuvered correctly and we expect to see good things out of it.
