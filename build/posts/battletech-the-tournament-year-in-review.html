BattleTech: The Tournament Year In Review

August 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Howdy ‘Mechwarriors and welcome to something new that I hope to make an annual occurrence! We’ve been to quite a lot of Battletech Tournaments this year and have had quite a lot of fun doing it! From the stiffness of box springs and the Pharaoh's curse upon my back to the beauty of the drive to Kentucky, and the delicious mountain of steak we’ve had, it's been a very good year.

A year of fighting. A year of tournaments. A year of prizes. A year of friends new and old. A year that comes to a wrap in this: The Year in Review.

So, join me as I run through what we’ve been up to, what’s come of it, what we did, how it did, what it all means, and where it’s all headed!

Pirate Hunt, a work I commissioned of a Marshal taking a moment of rest. Credit: Gecko, Commed by Liberty

The Results

There’s a point to start ahead of all of this before we even get into the lists, the tournaments, or the conclusions: The Stats.

We, meaning at least 2 or more of our authors together, went to 7 events last year with a collective of 8 things we could consider as ‘tournaments’. Every event and tournament was its own challenge, in one manner or another. From the damnable jump and pulse caps, to era restrictions that drug our list building eyes one way or the other, to strange optional or homebrew rules; there’s a lot of variance from one tournament to the other. That in mind, we can still pull some reasonable stats out of it:

Liberty: 7/8 Tournaments won, giving an 87.5% win rate at events overall. At the individual game level I won 26/28 total games for a game specific win rate of 92.85%. Congratulations to Vapor at Brawl in the Bluegrass and Peri at Richmond Open– even if it didn’t actually stop me :p –for taking games off of me. As well significant mention to my game 2 opponent at the South Carolina Classic for nearly punking me down and out and to Lynn for putting my ass on the edge of the run in our game at Richmond Open!

Peri: I Missed a couple of events, went 3/0 at Brawl in the Bluegrass, and otherwise had a solid year of middle of the pack finishes due to wildly experimenting with different list styles and play styles before finding something that worked consistently (damage maximization). Also, Liberty is a horrible demonic entity of my own creation and I hate how much better at this game he is than me. I don’t remember my specific win/loss because my brain is made of bad meat, but if I remember correctly its somewhere around a 60-70% winrate for individual games, I tended to only lose my last game several events in a row.

Lynn: From WarZone Atlanta ‘25 to WarZone Atlanta ‘26, I participated in eight tournaments of my own - nine if we count the Trial of Bloodright at Brawl in the Bluegrass, but my incredibly lopsided round one loss there… doesn’t really feel worth mentioning. I ended at… 4th, 14th, 3rd, 17th(?), 12th, 33rd, kind-of-ambiguous-but-in-the-bottom-three-of-people-who-played-all-rounds, and 2nd, an extremely feast-or-famine set of results. In terms of games, I went 16:13:1 W:L:D, for a 55% winrate, if I’ve counted correctly. I have some distinct thoughts about how my year (plus, WZA was significantly later in ‘26 than in ‘25) turned out that way, but I’ll save those for later.

Jack: Being on the west coast where we don't have anywhere near as many tournaments (and some of the ones we do are ones I organize), I only managed to make it out to one event, Cascade Clash. It was their first time running Battletech in addition to their other events, and I ended up going 1-2 at it.

The Events

Stats are all well and good but they don’t mean much if you don’t remember where the numbers were forged and where you put yourself to the test! There are a lot of great events here in the Mid-Eastern circuit and we went to just about every one we could manage to get to. From the depths of South Carolina to the heights of Richmond and out to the hollers of Kentucky we’ve been all over the place. There are plenty we wanted to go to that didn’t pan out, and there’s already even more already slipping past that we can’t attend like the Atlantic City Open and the White Plains Open! Hopefully we can get to them next year though!

The Boys, Pictured being in town for the first time. Credit: Liberty

Richmond Open 2025

Where we kicked off our year-long sprint! A wonderful tournament hosted and run by the even more wonderful Thiel! If you have a chance to go to one of his events I thoroughly suggest that you take it! The list building for this one had all but no restrictions, just pick a faction, pull anything they’ve ever had, and don’t grab two of the same exact ‘Mech! This was quite fun and allowed for a lot of freedom in coming up with effective, fun forces! On top of that the scenarios were published ahead of time and were simple, easy to understand, and very good for motivating engagements to avoid what we started referring to as ‘Two Ships in the Night Syndrome’. Even with that freedom and foresight a lot of things showed consistent, after the tournament we consulted with Thiel to learn a few things, most notably that the most common ‘Mech in that tournament was some variety of Fire Moth which is incredibly funny.

Lists

Liberty: I started my year off with a bout of my beloved, homegrown, General Motors Generalist Mercenaries. The Corporate Enforcers, as they were so aptly named, consisted of a wall of GM made steel, and their buddy Dave. A Nightstar NSR-9J, a Templar III TLR2-OA, and a Marauder MAD-5T were supported quite handily by a Grenadier II A, and B, squad and the ever present ‘man of the company’ Dave in his Wasp WSP-5A. Aside from Dave at 5/6 all of the ‘Mechwarriors were 4/5 and the BA were 4/6.

I went into this list with a goal in mind, a goal of testing if building a list with only one manufacturer's ‘Mechs could work. Interestingly I found something out quite quickly: General Motors does not build things that are light. You’ve got some Wasps, the Stealth, the Rattlesnake, and the Hollander III… that’s about it. But they do get a shitload of thick lads.

This list worked beautifully and was all too happy to pick a target and hammer it apart at a worrying rate every turn that it was allowed to do so. This was the first tournament I had gone to where I actually knew how to play the game, having attended the RO the year before for my 8th game. It was a bit enlightening for what I liked gameplay wise, and would serve to outline quite a lot of what I wanted from a list moving forward.

Peri:This was the start of things and is the first BattleTech tournament I had ever participated in, but I had run 2-3 (I forget) of them in the past. I knew going in from playtesting the missions that I was gonna want some speed and some durability, so I ended up on a light mech/assault mech split that would end up becoming pretty common for me at events going forwards. I slowly ended up diversifying from this but to this day I think that a lot of mechs in the middle of the pack tend to be poor compromises.

It really shouldn’t be surprising that I play Draconis Combine, huh. On a related note, this event was probably the origin point of all of the billion Tenshi-OA’s that we ended up seeing over the rest of the year at various events. I hyped the thing up quite a bit in the leadup article and I do maintain that it is a very high grade competitive pick, even if tournament pulse restrictions frequently make it hard to squeeze in.

WarZone Atlanta 2025

Lynn: I did not attend Richmond Open in 2025, but WarZone Atlanta ‘25 was the first tournament which I covered for the site then known as Goonhammer, and the start of my own “getting kind of serious about BattleTech” tournament arc. This was MRC guidelines, 9k BV Civil War, faction-locked, and 30 BSP of Strikes, with a set of optional rules which included both Forced Withdrawal and cumulative PSR penalties from 40+ damage taken in a turn, a punishing environment for Beefy Boys.

I brought Nova Cats, running a Gargoyle Prime, Viper (Dragonfly) Prime, Gargoyle C, Kingfisher Prime, one point of Clan Medium Battle Armor (“Rabid”), and one point of Salamander BA, with the Gargoyle at 3/5 and everything else at base 4/5 skills, filling up 8,985 BV. This was one of my better tournament results, with a 4-1 run and fourth place in the standings. In honesty, I think I benefitted from a relatively “soft” meta at the time; the overall skill level of the Atlanta BT scene has really been improving by leaps and bounds since then! This was also a relatively straightforward “Good Stuff” list compared to some of my later gimmicks…

Task Force Stampede stands ready for action. Get your six shooters ready Ladies, Gents, and Themperors. It's time for a shootout at the Atmoic Corral. Credit: Liberty

Atomic Empire 2025

Another Thiel Classic down in Durham North Carolina! A land of road work, Biscuitvilles, Adam Steiner’s terrible Ass, and even worse beds! A few more restrictions over the Richmond Open but still a largely very open tournament that had generous allotments for BV, pre-published scenarios with some changes made from lessons learned at Richmond, and plenty of new friends and opponents to be had!

Lists

Liberty: At the end of Richmond open we were ready and raring to go for another tournament but worried about finding one being difficult. Luckily our worries were poorly placed as Thiel was running yet another tournament only a few months later down in North Carolina! So, what did I bring this time? Well, there was a thought of doing the GMGM again, after all it’s a good gag. But I had something more… direct in mind.

The Fronc Reaches, as I’m sure you know by now dear reader with my endless prattling about my beloved space cowboys, is a low tier periphery state with a pretty ok to absolute dog-shit availability. Peri, somewhat, challenged me to put my money where my mouth was after winning at Richmond and prove that a Periphery nation had it in them to put the hate down at a tournament. So what did I do with it? Simple: Cheap. Big. Guns. Thusly, Task Force Stampede was born.

Task Force Stampede consisted of a Cyclops 10-Z, a Charger 1A5, a Thunder 1L, a Marshal 6FR, a Spider 9M, and a Spider 8M. That’s 3 AC/20s with a collective 9 tons of precision ammunition. All of them were at 4/5 and were all too happy to close to worryingly close ranges and throw 20 point haymakers at anything that got anywhere near them. TF Stampede worked like a charm, the Spiders and Marshal were happy to do their job and go do OBJ work while occasionally bouncing a target and hitting them with the lasers while the big boys hammered towards the nearest high priority target and set about dismantling it with prejudice. If you’ve never AC/20 maxed you should, it’s fucking funny.

Peri: This is the event I brought an Iron Cheetah B to. My list was basically the same as the Richmond Open list above, just with a little bit of rejiggering of specific variants. This would be the last event of the year without some sort of annoying pulse cap, and I pulsed the shit out of this event with a Tenshi OA (4 of them showed up to this event which is hilarious), that horrifying Cheetah, and a pair of Spiders. In hindsight I have a fairly low opinion of the Cheetah B as a competitive mech. It is very scary in theory but the high price and somewhat limited damage output for that price make it a sub-optimal pickup for most event lists. You can do better elsewhere.

The other issue was that I was playing this event while nursing the literal worst hangover of my life after drinking Gin out of a bucket while watching the BattleTech animated series. I cannot recommend playing a tournament while too hungover to stand up straight. I also had a pretty miserable game 3 here due to a really bad mismatch in expectations of what a tournament was for, but that happens sometimes.

Porble's Comstar Battle Group

Summer Fever II

Lynn: I wasn’t at Atomic Empire ‘25, either, but I did attend Summer Fever II in my local Atlanta scene. This was run using MRC Guidelines, 6.5k BV, Helm Renaissance era, faction-locked, 30 BSP of Strikes. Forced Withdrawal was out, but cumulative PSR modifiers from damage were still turned on.

I played a ComStar list painted by Porble, which biased me towards plastic mechs he hadn’t yet painted for the GH/TTB archives. I ran a Spartan N2, basic Crockett, Mongoose 66, Javelin 10N, Firefly 4C, and, uh, unarmed flatbed truck, with the Firefly at 4/5, the Spartan and Mongoose at 4/4, the Crockett and Javelin at 3/5, and the heroic truck driver at 2/0 for comedy value, to the tune of 6,493 BV.

This was an awful tournament for me, in large part due to environmental factors - the host store’s air conditioning had just broken, during a hot Georgia summer, and I was incredibly overheated and cranky during the latter 2/3rds of this tournament. I went 1-1-1, with a final game which stalled out with only three rounds played at a 0-0 tied score. Just miserable.

Toys for Tots 2025

Yes, I did paint them myself this time! Photo Credit: Lynn C.

Lynn: I got in another tournament in my local area before Liberty and Peri’s next outing (which I was able to join in on!), the annual Toys for Tots charity tournament. This used MRC guidelines, 8k BV, Civil War era, faction-locked, and 30 BSP of Strikes. The optional rules for this one added engine explosions, oh joy. This was my first significant crossover with The Goonhammer Universe, with Discord stalwart Tentacle coming all the way down from Virginia to play, and helping me refine my list in the lead-up.

This time I leaned on my beloved Free Worlds League, albeit with the help of the Civil War era’s generous proliferation of Clan tech onto IS General, bringing a Kingfisher Prime, Hercules 9001, Orion ON1-MA, Wraith TR1, and Ostsol 5M, all at base 4/5 skills, coming to 8k BV exactly.

This was a really fun tourney; I played some great games against great opponents, squeaked out wins in every round, and made the podium, coming in 3rd (behind Tentacle at 2nd, and regular reader and Georgia Meta Menace ChahDresh in 1st)! I really like the Herc 9001 and the Ostsol 5M, they’re mechs I really need to get back to the table soon!

The Posse goes forth to the distant world of North Carolina - Credit: Liberty

Southern Assault V

Back in North Carolina again we go to one of the biggest tournaments on the east coast hosted by Fortress games and run by the guys from the Mechbay podcast! A fun tournament with a lot more restrictions that forced us to consider a few things and definitely absolutely were in no way shape or form shaped by the concept of Task Force Stampede.

Lists

Liberty: This time around we took a slightly longer drive on down deeper into North Carolina to go to Southern Assault V for its first annual Classic Tournament! We had a great time before the event hanging out with friends we’d made at previous ones and browsing the tide of old BT merch and minis that had been brought along for sale!

The list this time though… Well I’ll be honest it didn’t have much of a challenge. It just had ‘holy fuck I love the Kodiak, I wanna run the Kodiak’ disease. This group was painted up in Froncian Colors but was pulled from the Dark Age Mercs MUL. Task Force Bear Country was a real pain in the ass of a list that had two forward moving hammers that were all to eager at slamming through a force while taking return hits square on the chin and a pair of zippy well moving mediums ready to do objectives or hit a striker run on someone who’s in the wrong spot.

TF Bear Country consisted of, of course and most notably, a Kodiak, a Sunder OE, a Menshen OF, and a Thunderfox TFT-L8. All of them were at 4/5 and carrying normal ammunition loads… largely because they couldn’t do much else. The list worked a treat and, aside from my game 1 against Lynn, the Kodiak never even really took too much of a beating… the Sunder sure did die every game though. They have a tendency to do that, I’ve found. This tournament sure did teach me something though: big fuck you assault ‘Mechs that vomit damage and move well enough to get where they need to be can, and will, win you a game so long as you’ve got other friends that you can bait shots into to keep them alive longer with.

Peri: I had a Regent Prime, a Sunder OE, and a Dire Wolf Widowmaker for this event, backed by a Dasher II. My trend of running just a bunch of assaults and a basically light mech continued here, and it did not serve me well. My list struggled to close out games and while I didn’t really lose anything aside from the Sunder which died in literally every game, I was mostly incapable of doing objectives or making proactive plays for the entire event. The somewhat low damage output for BV of the Widow and Regent Prime compared to the premier damage vomit assaults I would end up taking later in the year ended up biting me hard. This event also enlightened me to how insanely good the Hunchback 6S is when one ran behind my Sunder and killed it instantly with a shot to a side torso ammo bin from the rear, just a nasty little mech.

Lynn: Okay, this time I actually made it to the same tournament as Liberty and Peri! I was on my first (but hardly last) pure gimmick list of the year for this outing, fielding an All TSM Melee list from the Dark Age Regulan Fiefs: Grand Titan 13M, Banshee 8S, Nightsky 6T, Scarabus 9T, all at 4/4 except for the Scarabus at base skills.

This was a bad list, and I knew it (I’d been tabled in both of the two practice games I’d managed to squeeze in before the tournament), but I had a ton of fun going 1-2 with it. It also taught me the valuable lesson that coming into a tournament with a firm idea of how to manage your mechs’ heat can dramatically speed up play!

Seeker Darius knows what you have. Hand it over. - Credit: Liberty

South Carolina Classic

This time around we head way south and end up in South Carolina for the Inaugural South Carolina Classic Tournament, run by Hoovie! An awesome tournament that was run incredibly well, had great participants, and was an absolute blast! Also… it let us have two of the same ‘Mech. 

Lists

Liberty: So, this list allowed for two of the same ‘Mech, chassis and variant. So… I brought two Kodiaks and a Lancelot C from the Scorpion Empire with some misc. ‘they sure are some dudes’ power armor suits to burn initiative with. Everyone, except the suits, was 4/5.

This list was affectionately referred to as Two Bears Highfiving and it worked like a treat. It killed people like no one’s business and firmly and resoundingly backed up a core belief about Battletech that I hold: They can’t do the Objective if they’re dead.

Peri: I wasn’t able to make this one due to a horrible, random bout of god-knows-what horrible sickness, unfortunately..

Lynn: What is this “way south” you speak of? I drove north for this, Liberty!

I ran FWL for this one… I can’t resist the call of the Flashman 9M when the era is Jihad. Alongside the 9M, I ran the Banshee 3S, the Banshee 3Q, a Crusader 7W (because reading comprehension failed me when looking at the Longbow 13C), a Blitzkrieg 3F, and a squad of Longinus (Magnetic) BA, with the Banshee 3Q at 4/4, the BA at 4/2, and everything else at base 4/5 skill. The Banshees were both running Precision ammo, and the Crusader had a smattering of Inferno SRMs.

The missions frustrated me a little in this outing, and I ended 1-2 again, but it was once again a great time regardless!

Got a Certifricate and everything, Credit: Liberty McKenna

Brawl in the Bluegrass - Trial of Bloodright

A departure from the usual, the Trial of Bloodright is an incredibly entertaining time and, more importantly, has a very different prize at the end. While we didn’t build any lists we did draft a fun toy to start with and got to get a few real interesting games out of them! Now, while you’re probably thinking this isn’t a tournament I must remind you: The TO said it counts!

Lists

Liberty: This was a blast of a time and somehow my only regret is winning it, which means I can’t do it again next year! At least it got me into the fun world of the Raven Alliance! I started with a Loki and got really lucky scoring a breach check into the CT of my opponent. I drug home their Ebon Jaguar and used it to take home a Gargoyle and a Warhammer IIC 2. The Warhammer IIC 2 goes on to flog down a Loki MK. II C and finds itself suddenly accompanied by a whole new roster of friends! The Whammer then got a fun long range ‘we sure are shooting at each other’ engagement with a grizzly before staving it apart… eventually. Last but not least my Omen, a new favorite brought to me by this Trial that I refuse to give up and am convinced stole much of my luck, fought a bloody, and stupid, battle with a White Raven. I managed to put it down and won myself the Bloodname: McKenna.

Peri: I had a reasonable time here, winning my first game with a Phoenix Hawk IIC (the one with the hatchet) brutalizing the other guy’s “Hellstar at home” Loki MkII, but my second game ended in defeat after the same Phoenix Hawk IIC got brutally slammed into the ground by an ATM variant Crossbow, humbling me thoroughly. Sometimes mass isn’t everything I guess.

Lynn: I don’t count this as one of my tournaments, which definitely isn’t sour grapes from getting eliminated in Round 1 by a Timber Wolf S jumping rings around my Gargoyle E after nerfing my missile to-hit numbers via environmental condition, nosiree.

Task Force Dancer, Protect the Burger Town! Credit, Liberty

Brawl in the Bluegrass

The real main event of our trip on out to Kentucky, Brawl in the Bluegrass was a monster of a tournament with a whole lot of competitors present! In addition to the size of the tournament itself the lists were big too with a 10k BV cap to work with.

Lists

Liberty: So… I made a fucky-wucky here. I originally had an evolution of Two Bears Highfiving that could’ve run riot on this tournament… but I got enticed by the siren call of Task Force Dancer and took an idiot brigade force that was a hilarious time as a– mostly –Cannon compliant Colonial Marshals force. A King Crab 001 at 2/4, a Warhammer 10T at 3/3, a Marshal 6FR at 4/4, and an Anubis T1 at 5/4. They were backed by a pile of Tinstar Original squads that at 4/4… just in case.

I’ll be honest, I don’t regret it, it was a fun list, it worked well when I actually used it to do what I wanted it to do, and it befuddled a couple of my opponents quite well. That said, it lost, and I’ll be even more honest: It was my fault. I misread the packet, in part due to the time I had on target with the clock already ticking on the round, and in part due to being rather tired, and stupid.

Peri: This is the first event where I broke away from the “Assaults and Lights” paradigm, and is easily the event I am the most proud of. I took a very simple list of a pair of Sphinx 3s and a pair of Phantom Cs, a huge amount of accurate laser vomit on fast chassis. This proceeded to run roughshod over several games, as you can read about in my article. I won an award for the shortest game at the entire event and I easily could have won it had my game 2 been higher scoring.

Lynn: It was gimmick time for me again in this tourney. I brought “the Ostlist,” an all-Ostmech ilClan-era FWL force: Ostscout 12R, Ostsol 7M, Ostwar 3M, Ostroc 6R, Ostroc 2Cb, and Ostsol 8M, all at 4/5 except for the Ostsol 8M at 4/3, totalling 10k BV exactly.

This was, uh, another 1-2 outing for me. Unsurprising with the silly-season list, but a bit disappointing; my games were close, and I felt like I might’ve done much better with just some small adjustments and a tiny bit more luck. Ah well.

Simonson's Cutthroats for Cascade Clash 2026. Credit: Jack Hunter

Cascade Clash

The one west coast tournament any of us went to, Cascade Clash is a mid-sized 40k event that's been running for a few years in Mount Vernon, about an hour north of Seattle. This was the first time they were running Battletech, with a team tournament where each player had 4,000 BV.

Lists

Jack: My friend Dustin and I teamed up, with some back and forth testing to settle on a list that would work with the event restrictions - mechs had to come from Clan Invasion through Jihad eras, and we couldn't bring anything with jump 7, pulses, and a targeting computer. We ended up with a pretty simple list of good stuff that we could pull from the Mercenary availability list - Kingfisher E, Gargoyle Prime, Flashman 9M, Venom 9KA, Locust 1Vb, and a squad of Achileus Light Battle Armor. Being restricted to two non-interactive 4k chunks made list building a bit harder, but I think we had a pretty solid list. This was one of the rare gaming instances where both us and our opponents agreed that the dice were tilted significantly against us - in every game either the Kingfisher, Gargoyle, or both took early TACs and head hits that took them out of the game far too soon.

The 1-2 outing was disappointing, but it was a well run event, we took home best painted, and I'm looking forward to them running it again next year.

The Command Star rides on under the watchful eyes of the King of Crows, Credit: Liberty

Viking Funeral

A wonderful memorial event for a man who left an obviously glowing mark on his local community. The tournament was run impeccably, the vibes were immaculate, and all the competitors had great stories to share about their lost friend. It’s the kind of thing that makes you really appreciate the people in your life.

Lists

Liberty: My newly minted Raven Alliance 14th Raven Battle Cluster of the Gamma Galaxy steps to the field in a great time of an event. This was grabbing a bunch of weirdos that felt very raven, or at least from what I knew of them at the time. An Omen at 4/5, a Warhammer IIC 4, a Nova G, a Phantom C, and a Howler 2. Backing them up is a wonderfully funny 4/2 Shamash Interdictor Hover Craft from the Long Road Legion! This was meant to be my Command Star with an attached scout and made for some great thematic fun!

This list worked well and my love for the Omen and Warhammer IIC were cemented all the further. As well, I quite like that Nova, and the new Core book is only going to make it all the funnier! The tournament itself was great, well run, and a touching tribute to a man that I never knew but can only hope that I leave as lasting an impression myself.

Peri: I have complicated feelings about this one because it felt weird to show up and almost win a memorial event for someone I hadn’t met, which is something I hadn’t really realized the implications of until I got there. I think Liberty puts it pretty well in his article about this event.

List wise, my main takeaway is that the Black Hawk OG and Blood Asp B are both quite good, and that Black Hawk in particular really shined as a utility piece with all that EWAR.

Clan Sea Fox Spina Khanate Tiburon. Credit: Lynn B.

Richmond Open

I’m a strong proponent of the idea that things should end right where they began.

Lists

Liberty: Have you considered… ATMs? I have. I considered them a lot. I also made all of my opponents consider them a lot. This list was named the Two Shoe Beating. Trussed up as a new star that Star Colonel McKenna was shadowing to assess their performance this star comprised a Nova Cat E, a Loki Mk II C, a Phantom C, and a Dasher II at 6/3. Additionally they had an attached point of Salamanders at 4/3.

List wise the sole goal here was to apply significant hammer falls onto anything that gets too close to them, while sending a Phantom C to go fuck up anything in the backline that decides it wants to sit there and be an issue while dropping off the asshole brigade of the Salamander point. It worked very well. Even if I lost to Peri I still came out on top in the end which was far and away the funniest thing of any tournament we’ve had this year.

Peri: A bit of a return to form here, I brought a Blood Asp B, an Iron Cheetah C, and a pair of random fireballs or something. I ended up with a 3/2 finish which was fine and all but I am mostly just mad that I beat Liberty and he still won this one. The two day nature of the event was a big change here, and I did quite like it, but I really need to stop running the double assault double light thing, it keeps going very poorly for me.

Lynn: Sea Fox Aggro was the name of my game at this tournament, running a Gargoyle G, Mad Cat III 2, Tiburon 2, Phantom E, and a Salamander BA point, with the BA at 4/3 and everything else at 4/5. This list did well in testing, but in practice just didn’t have the staying power to keep up momentum through RO ‘26’s missions. I ended at a dismal 1-4 record.

We've been trying to reach you about your HPG's extended warranty... Credit: Lynn B.

WarZone Atlanta 2026

Lynn: With all due respect to Liberty, the beginning of his year wasn’t the beginning of my year, and I want to end my recounting with this event (while acknowledging that it takes me well past 12 months of retrospective, given that WZA ‘26 was in late May, as opposed to WZA ‘25’s early February date).

This followed MRC guidelines, 9.5k BV, Late Republic, faction-locked, and scaling PSR penalties finally were NOT part of the packet! Vibing off the unusual Late Republic timeframe, I ran one of the most niche factions possible for this tournament: The Blessed Order, the abortive attempt to revitalize the ComGuard post-Jihad. Its MUL availability for Late Republic includes less than 112 units which were legal for use at WZA, by far the most restrictive list-building requirements I’ve ever faced! I ended up running a Mad Cat BLO (4/5), Avatar OE (4/5), Mercury 102 (6/5), Dasher II (5/3), Avatar OA (4/5), and Atlas K2 (4/5), with the first three in a C3i Network.

I shocked myself with an extremely solid run through this tournament… my wins were close and driven by objective play over kill count, but I ended at 4-1 and placed second! Not bad for a gimmick list which spent a mech’s worth of BV on a mostly-useless C3i network!

Final Thoughts

Liberty: My final takeaway from the year is that just about anything can win a tournament. You may have to play it damn near perfectly and get a lucky break sometimes to make it do it but if you focus on your two prongs of the game you will quite rapidly find yourself winning more games than losing:
  • Damage
  • Objectives.

No game can be won by just one. Part of your list needs to be doing Objectives, preferably on a budget, and part of your list needs to be shithammering its way through the opposition as fast as it possibly can while not dying on its own end. Be it from having 3 low grade cheapo assaults that hammer alright damage into something, thus tying up the enemy list for long enough to do what your Objective dorks want to do, or by having a pair of horror machines that run up and rip things off the table as fast as they possibly can.

Sometimes the other list will just be able to out do you, and that’s ok, but even in those times you’ve got to knuckle down and push through. There are more than enough ways any given list can put up a fight against something that outdoes it. Pick a target and hit it. Don’t break fire, don’t take bait, and don’t take the chew toy the opponent tries to hand your list. If they’re shuffling something you’ve been shooting back and handing you something fresh and new just don’t bite.

Now… what about the next year? It’s not like I’m gonna stop going to tournaments, but I’d like a bit of a challenge this year. I want something odd that I’ve got to deal with and build around.

Well, I’ve got just the thing, and I think it’s a fun one. See, I’ve got something to prove. Something to prove about not needing the fanciest of toys to do well. So, what is it? Here, take a look:

  • Fronc Availability only. If it’s an era locked tournament outside of the existence of the Reaches I must use one of its OG parent sources, IE Taurian, Canopan, or Capellan. Those units must at some point later still show up on the Fronc Avail.
  • No Repeated Units. Once I use a ‘Mech, Vee, or BA unit I cannot use it again.
  • There is one exception to this Rule: I am allowed to have the Marshal 6FR and Tinstar Originals at any rate that I wish.
  • The repeat Marshal 6FR is me. Command Marshal Liberty McKenna. Therefore it needs to raise stakes every passing tournament. Starting with the Atomic Empire tournament at 4/5 at the next one it will thusly advance ot 4/4 at the next one, 3/4 after that and so on and so on.

The goal here is to force me to fully expand upon and explore as much of the Fronc TO&E as I can manage to play with over the year. I need to use as many varied units and strategies as I can manage with the Fronc TO&E while exploring the units that are available that I may otherwise not think, or deign, to use.

This should be fun!

The 1st Expeditionary Marshals Round, Credit: Ascii Squid as Commissioned by Liberty

Peri: My final takeaway from this year is that tournament BattleTech highly incentivizes damage almost above all else. You need to be able to do damage quickly, because unlike in pickup games, the time and turn constraints put on you at an event don’t let super high durability, low cost strategies excel. You need to be able to fairly consistently point at an assault mech and kill it dead in two turns, anything less than that is way too slow and low damage. If you had 20 turns a strategy consisting entirely of SFE IS mechs could work, or a strategy hinging around marginal value with jumping/high TMM pulse laser mechs. On the 10 term timescale that is more common at events though you simply do not have time to kill through an opponent’s list like that and you need to optimize for doing as much damage output as you can squeeze in to a format while still being able to do objectives.

For me personally I think I probably overused assault mechs this year, and the events where I had a greater percentage of mediums and heavies (Brawl, Viking Funeral) are the ones where I did much better and actually managed undefeated records. I am intending, for this next year, to use as few assault mechs as possible and really lean in to trying to make what was my least used and least favorite weight class, medium mechs, work at tournaments more consistently.

For me as a player, I absolutely improved greatly over the course of this year of events. I got vastly faster at resolving the game and memorized pretty close to everything I needed to to play games quickly and effectively. Mech selection got much better, and my playstyle has shifted heavily in a very aggressive direction, as the sort of grindy value town IS lists that I had been playing prior to this tournament run simply do not have the ability to function in a competitive environment, they are simply too slow.

I guess my main recommendation is to not be afraid to have your mind changed, and to play much more aggressively. I won many games this year on the back of a cagey or noncommittal opponent, do not be afraid to jump someone up close with some fast moving mechs, it will work out more often than it won’t.

Lynn: I think my main takeaway, looking back over my ‘25-’26 season, is that the style of play I’m most comfortable with favors a strong focus on scoring objectives with fast-ish, durable mechs, with killing as a secondary concern, and consequently I do much better in tournaments which weight scoring more towards primary than towards combat score. I can also see that I haven’t historically made melee mechs work consistently in a tournament setting. That said, I have acquitted myself surprisingly well with some very silly gimmick lists, and I’m proud of how close I can generally keep my games even in defeat.

My speed of play was a real bugbear for me at the start of my “year”, and game pacing can still be inconsistent for me, but I feel I’ve improved greatly, and I feel confident I’ll continue to tighten up my gameplay as I get more experience. (I’ve only been playing since August ‘24!)

While Liberty is embarking on a particularly limiting self-imposed listbuilding challenge for our ‘26-’27 season, I have decided to forgo his challenge to me to do the same. He’s already thoroughly proven his ability to win tournaments, and I’m not particularly surprised he’s entering his “Nuzlocking” phase of BattleTeching, but I think the best thing I can do for myself, to continue my own growth and improvement, is to put gimmick lists behind me (to some extent, at least!) and see what I’m capable of when I lock in and try to really play to my strengths.

That is… not exactly what I’m doing in the next tournament I’ll be playing in (“Atlanta BattleTech at Challenges” on August ‘29th), but I’m definitely looking forward to the ‘26 outing of the Toys for Tots charity tournament… thus far I have made it on the podium at every Toys for Tots tourney (all, uh, two of them!), and I plan to do my very best to score a threepeat!

I’m certainly looking forward to another year of cheerful competition, and I hope to share it all with you, our readers!

Remember, even with this copious talk of tournaments, our adoration of competition, and gunning for a win, what matters is having a good time and doing it all with a smile on your face. All the ‘mech reviews, the pretty plastic and the shiny mathrocks aren’t worth a damn if you don’t use them to have fun with friends! So go grab a drink, a good pack of snacks, your favorite game partner(s), a box of funny ‘mechs, a cozy spot, and a fun map then get to making some good memories, at home or at a tournament!

Until next time! Fair weather, good seas and happy gamin’! Go tear shit up out there and have fun!
