A Brush With Greatness 11: Finishing Cormac - Woven Cloth, Wood, Tattoos

October 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, my lovelies, to another instalment of A Brush With Greatness, I’m keewa, your intrepid voyager into the unknown and unexplored parts of the realm of painting. We’re back once again with our ole fella Cormac, very much a druid sort of guy - he definitely consumes questionable mushrooms and listens to the Grateful Dead. This time we’ll be painting his attire, equipment, and friends. In particular, using some techniques to increase the impression of fabric and create illusory texture where there is none.

Loading...

To start, I basecoated the tunic/robe with Vallejo Game Color Abyssal Turquoise to maintain the cool shadows that I established with the skin. Since Cormac is a druid, it seemed only appropriate that he should have a green and brown scheme for his clothing. The eagle-eyed among you will notice that in this stage I also painted the basecoat of the leaves in his hair and beard with Pro Acryl Dark Camo Green at the same time. 

For the main colour of the tunic, I chose Vallejo Game Color Dark Green, covering almost everything, leaving some of the Abyssal Turquoise in the deepest recesses. Cormac’s ensemble features a roughspun apron on top of his tunic; I decided that this should be a sort of hessian colour, which I basecoated with Vallejo Game Color Gorgon Brown.  

More basecoats! For the wood of the staff, I used Pro Acryl Black Brown, one of my all-time go-to paints. You might think, “It’s a dark brown, so what?” but this paint is good for shading so much stuff. If Pro Acryl primer were sturdier (a topic for another time), I’d use their Black Brown primer for so many things. For the feathers dangling from the staff, I painted one with Vallejo Game Color Turquoise and the other with Off-White. The staff top is covered with snails, which is charming and appropriate - I painted them with Vallejo Game Color Charred Brown. In order to add a sort of cusp-of-season-change feeling to the leaves in his beard, I painted some with a glaze of Pro Acryl Burnt Orange, especially towards the outer edges.

Cormac has a fun trio of critters on his model (the hand-bird made an appearance later); these little guys are a great addition to the model and, though they be small, they add tons of character. I basecoated the black mouse with Pro Acryl Dark Neutral Grey, and decided that little chick on his head should be a robin, so I basecoated it with more Black Brown and the chest with Burnt Orange. For the rope belt around his robe, I used some Pro Acryl Orange Brown, making every effort to try and stay out of the recessed rope-braid texture.  

Loading...

I highlighted the leaves a bit with some Pro Acryl Camo Green, then applied a further highlight focusing towards the edges by mixing in some yellow. I’ll do more to the leaves later, don’t you worry. I also decided to try wet blending a bit of bright orange into the turquoise feather, giving it a bit of a Kingfisher vibe.  Since we’re going with British birds here, it seemed appropriate.  After all that, I did the first stage of highlighting and texturing on the apron, mimicking the pattern of woven cloth by painting lots of thin parallel lines, first vertically and then horizontally, perpendicular to the original vertical lines, using the original Gorgon Brown mixed with some Vallejo Game Color Parasite Brown. Leave the shadows as their original colour, there’s no need to highlight the threads there. This crosshatching really boosts the appearance of woven fabric and helps to sell the illusion; just remember that woven fabric has horizontal and vertical lines and that they’re very often perpendicular to one another - lots of crosses! 

Once the first set of cross-hatches is done, what’s left is to highlight the woven apron, adding even more Parasite Brown into the previous mix. It’s important to try to highlight the hatches inside the threads already done, rather than creating new ones. This is pretty tricky, and I messed up in a few places, but ultimately it doesn’t stand out too much. I highlighted in patches, starting from the edge of each highlight and glazing towards the centre of each highlight area. I aimed to make the highlight the strongest at the top of each crease, the outside edge of the apron, and the top of the shoulder. I also took the opportunity to start highlighting the snail shells, which I did with some Vallejo Game Color Parasite Brown.

Loading...

I highlighted the green robe next, using exactly the same techniques and method as for the apron, carefully crosshatching with Vallejo Game Color Angel Green to start, focusing on patches that would receive light.

Rather than continue highlighting with a really strong green like Scorpy Green, the next on the Dark Green/Angel Green triad, I sidestepped and highlighted the green by mixing some Pro Acryl Ivory into the Angel Green, giving the fabric a more natural look, while also avoiding the possibility that the highlights on the robe would be too bright and acidic. For the final highlights of the dark green robe/tunic, I just mixed in even more Ivory.

Cormac looks like a man of the world who’s very committed to nature; it stands to reason that he’d have some nature-related tattoos on his bare arms. To paint the designs, I used Vallejo Game Color Abyssal Turquoise, thinned down a lot. I needed the paint so thin that it flowed easily and semi-transparently. Since I’d already finished the skin, this was a little nerve-wracking. I decided that since Cormac is a druid, patterns of vines on his arms seemed appropriate and not insanely difficult. I used the finest brush I could get my hands on, and kept a wet brush close at hand in case I needed to very quickly erase a mistake.

When the tattoos were completed, I glazed on a very thin layer of the original flesh tone highlight, midtone, and shadow to simulate the tattoos being underneath the skin. This step is the most important in making a design look like a real aged tattoo beneath layers of skin, rather than body-paint.

Loading...

Onto the staff, I painted some broad highlights on the upward-facing parts with Pro Acryl Mahogany.

I further enhanced the highlights of the wood by painting the grain with Pro Acryl Drab Brown, following the sculpted grain down and avoiding the areas that would be cast into shadow.I further highlighted the wood grain by mixing some Pro Acryl Ivory into the Drab Brown, focusing on the sharpest parts of the grain. Finally, I highlighted the grain with the brightest tone, using pure Pro Acryl Ivory. With all the highlights taken care of, I glazed the whole staff with some Pro Acryl Transparent Brown; this does squash the contrast a bit, but everything is really nicely tied together, and the saturation is given a nice boost. Using some thinned-down Scalecolor Artist Spring Green, I glazed some green into the shadows of the staff to give it a somewhat mossy look. I think the colour contrast here gives it a big boost as well. I continued to highlight the snail shells, using Vallejo Game Color Bronze Brown. I want them to look appropriately shiny, but not so shiny that they appear metallic. I finished off the snail shell highlights by mixing a bit of Off-White into the Bronze Brown. I also highlighted the fur of the mouse with some Pro Acryl Neutral Grey, following a natural fur texture to make it appear more realistic (I know the whole setup is quite cartoony, but I still want a bit of verisimilitude here!). For the robin chick, I highlight with Pro Acryl Warm Brown and a bit of Ivory mixed into the Burnt Orange of the red breast. I put catchlights in the eyes of the critters using some Bold Titanium White.

I painted some fine line patterns on the snails’ shells using Vallejo Game Color Charred Brown, mixing in a little white for the shiny highlight areas.

Loading...

I finished off Cormac by adding the Blue Tit (more British birds!) sitting on his hand. For the dazzlingly blue plumage, I used Pro Acryl Blue and Pro Acryl Sky Blue, while for the yellow breast and belly I employed Pro Acryl Warm Yellow, before shading with a very thin glaze of Vallejo Game Color Midnight Purple.

Cormac the Druid - Credit: keewa

So there he is, Cormac the Druid; he certainly looks like he could be found wandering the ancient British forests, using his sickle to harvest mistletoe, taking “natural” substances, chatting with the animals and birds - a veritable Iron Age Percy the Park Keeper.
