Kings of War: Assembling Your Forces (of Nature), Part 3

November 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

G’day Tabletop Battles Readers! Welcome back to the third part of this series, focusing on building a Force of Nature army for Kings of War. If you’re not up to date, you can check out part two here. I’ve gotten quite a bit done since last time, so read on and see!

Painting Ydrin’s Company

Last time, I had the whole Champions box primed and had drybrushed the zenithal highlighting on, and was just about ready to start with the colours. I had one test model planned and painted, and I was on such a roll, went ahead with the rest of the unit.

The Swift Shots. Credit: Urr

On the whole, they were very fun to paint, and not hard to get up to a good standard, even when trying to go quickly. They have the right amount of detail to look good, and take the paint easily, without the need to paint many different decorative bits across the whole unit. I did find a few mould lines I had missed, but none in any important places.

I decided to keep a natural feeling paint scheme, with a lot of different browns and muted colours for the cloth. I’ve avoided metallics where I can as well, only the decorative earrings have any shine on these guys. Some of the weapons on the rest of the models will be metallic, but I’m thinking of it as a status symbol, so none for the slingers.

While I’ve kept the colours rather muted, I've played around with the browns. I’ve tried to keep all the models from becoming brown blobs, and kept the brown parts visually distinct. I’ve stuck with Citadels Snakebite Leather contrast paint across all the units’ leather equipment, slings, belt, straps etc. while switching between a few other browns for the wood that is on the models. Hopefully, the units across the army wouldn’t look like they’re in a uniform, but will still look cohesive as a whole.

Warriors of the Oak and Ash. Credit: Urr

I do think I went a little desaturated on some of the highlights, particularly on the cloth on the Swift Shots. These were the first unit I did, so I took that into consideration for the next units, and tried to keep things a little brighter.

After finishing the infantry, I just kept on going, rolling right into the Centaurs. It's been years since I've painted a horse, so I took inspiration from our own How to Paint Horses article. I adapted the infantry’s colour palette to the Centaurs, and matched it quite easily. So far, the units definitely have a cohesive design, and it makes it very easy to stick to a palette and style.

Painting Ydrin

I had more painting time this month than I thought, so as a reward to myself for finishing the rest of the Champions box, I finished Ydrin. I took the lessons from the rest of the infantry on board, and I’ve brightened him up in comparison. As most people do with their heroes, I've put a lot more work into him, even doing the eyes, which I usually never do. He was surprisingly quick to actually finish, as there’s not a whole lot of stuff actually on him, 90% of the model is wooden armour, flesh, leather and cloth, with no extra bits and bobs to painstakingly pick out.

He was a fun little project, and I’m very happy how he turned out. Hopefully he’ll be on the board soon.

Ydrin's Company. Credit: Urr

Finishing the Bases

As you can see above, I've also finalised how the bases will look, which I am also very happy with. There is a bit of work to do for them, but nothing too crazy.

They are done with a mix of Vallejo diorama FX Russian Mud and AK Terrains diorama series Dark Earth, slopped on the bases all over. I do this after adding the rocks I printed and painted, to make sure all the nooks and crannies are filled in. Some parts are left purely as one of the texture paints, but mostly they are mixed while still wet. This gives a differing colour gradient and texture mix across the bases. Once dry, I give it a black wash, and once that is dry, a drybrush of Vallejo Game Colours’ Beastly Brown. Then I add a variety of different tufts to the base. I’ve got three different manufacturers and six different styles for variety. Once they’re on, it’s snow time! This was the trickiest to get right, hence all the test bases. I’m using Green Stuff World’s Terrain Series Snow Powder. It works great once you read the tutorial on how to use it.

Building a Battalion

On top of all the painting and basing, I have been building. Mantic very kindly sent us the Forces of Nature Battalion box, and it is full of models! 50 of the Feraun infantry, eight Centaurs, three Earth Elementals and a Gladewalker Druid. The Feraun and Centaurs are the same sprues as in the Champions box, so they can be built into any of the units you are after.

I've switched it up and built half the Centaurs as the ranged version, and there's so much infantry, I've built some of everything.

Forces of Nature Battalion. Credit: Urr

On the back of all this building, I’ve been thinking about what I actually want to have done and ready to take to Clash Aus.

I’ve come up with this as a goal list (among many others):

1 x Chieftain

1 x Druid - Bane Chant (2), Heal (4)



1 x Centaur Chief - Banner of Command



2 x Tribal Warriors Horde - Spears



3 x Critters Troop



2 x Centaur Bray Striders Regiment - Sir Jesse’s Boots of Striding



1 x Grymbrocks Regiment



1 x Beast of Nature



2 x Guardian Brutes Regiment - Blessing of the Gods

Theoretically, I’ve got the hammers, anvils and chaff I like, but no shooting puts me a little on edge. There’s a few points floating for items, and wiggle room around all of this. You’ll note I have not built or painted essentially any of this, so there’s a very large amount of work to go. I don’t want to lock myself into too much of this, as Arrogance is coming out very shortly, and promises to shake up the whole game, but at least I have a goal!

So, with that hefty list of models to go, where does that leave me? Well, with a lot more building and painting to go! And even some games! Just in time for Arrogance to come out and throw it all up in the air. More on that shortly...
