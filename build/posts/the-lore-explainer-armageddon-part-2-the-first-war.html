The Lore Explainer: Armageddon, Part 2 - the First War

May 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer
As we near the release of the new Battle for Armageddon Box marking the 11th edition of Warhammer 40k, we've been digging into the lore of Armageddon. We kicked off our series with a look at the overall lore; today we're diving into the lore behind the First War for Armageddon, a much less famous - but no less important - note in the history of the Imperium.

Rogue Trader Origins

Despite its importance, there's surprisingly little written about the first War for Armageddon. Its origins are in the 1992 Battle for Armageddon boxed game, a board game using cardboard chits to represent units and territories on a map (we'll talk more about the board game in a future article). The original Battle for Armageddon detailed the conflict between Blood Angels and Orks which marked the second battle. However the game's first and only expansion, titled Chaos Attack, released later that year, included additional rules for the game and detailed the first battle for Armageddon - The Chaos Wars.

These rules were later revised across a number of sources. First in the 1996 publication of Codex: Chaos, which reprinted large chunks of the lore from that initial rulebook and added some additional notes on both the Grey Knights and the Space Wolves, creating new lore around the aftermath of the conflict and setting the stage for a long-standing enmity between the Space Wolves and the Inquisition.

Since then, there have been a number of additions to the lore, the most notable of which was in White Dwarf 279 (March 2003), with a segment called Codicium Imperialis, which detailed the First War for Armageddon, giving the lore an overhaul similar to what the Index Astartes series did for the Space Marine legions and bringing the lore in line with the more modern feel of the 40k lore.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

The First War

Armageddon is a pretty shitty place to live by most accounts. It's covered in toxic wastes created over millennia of industry and marked by overcrowded hives filled with exploited workers. Most of its foodstuffs are imported from off-world and the noble families on top of things keep the workers in a state of brutal subjugation. It's a recipe that screams for armed rebellion and as a result the planet's defense forces spend a majority of their time putting down various worker revolts across the planet's hives.

As a result, it wasn't a particular cause for alarm when armed rebellions broke out across a number of hives simultaneously. Many of these revolts were quickly put down but some were more difficult and their outbreak meant that Imperial forces were caught somewhat by surprise when the space hulk Devourer of Stars appeared in the Armageddon System. The hulk was host to a massive army of World Eaters and daemons and was led by Angron himself and naval forces were scrambled to try and intercept the hulk on the outskirts of the system. They ultimately failed to destroy it, but did manage to inflict heavy damage on the hulk, slowing its approach and delaying its arrival by about one month.

One interesting quirk of this lore is that, once the Chaos forces arrived and their presence felt, a full half of the planet's defenders immediately turned to Chaos. That helps justify a bit what happened later in the lore, but also feels out of character for the more modern approach to Chaos in GW lore. The revision in White Dwarf 279 suggests that a number of the leaders of the dissident factions were revealed to be Chaos cultists operating covertly, who now shed their guises, revealing that fully half of the Planetary Defense Force had turned to the worship of Chaos. They seized the planet's defenses on Armageddon Prime, allowing Angron's forces to make planetfall largely uncontested.

The remainder of Armageddon's loyal defenders were overmatched and forced to fall back. While they fled, the forces of Chaos set up makeshift abattoirs and temples to the Blood God, choking the planet's rivers with blood spilled for Khorne. Warp storms of terrifying power surged around the planet, lending power to Angron and his daemon hordes - for a time, anyways. As the storms' power diminished following the initial assault, so did the power of Angron and his legions. He needed to establish a permanent link to the Immaterium to sustain his army if he wanted to retain the planet.

This is ultimately what helped the loyalist forces escape through the equatorial jungles to regroup on Armageddon Secundus. While they fell back, Angron ordered the construction of a massive monolith to draw power from the immaterium to sustain his armies. This process took weeks and cost thousands of lives in the consecration ceremony, buying the Imperium more time to respond.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

The Space Wolves and Grey Knights

All hope seemed lost, but unknown to the forces of Chaos, the Space Wolves were already moving quickly in the planet's defense, arriving to assist imperial forces on the banks of the river Chaeron. These forces were led by Chapter Master Logan Grimnar, some two hundred years old at this point but only in his first year as Chapter Master. Armageddon was the first real test of his abilities and when Angron's forces crossed the river Chaeron they found the imperial defenders well dug-in and reinforced by the Space Wolves. The forces of Chaos broke against the defenders' lines, handing them their first real defeat in the campaign.

This was only one front, however - in the west Angron was personally leading the attack on the Helsreach and Infernus hives, smashing their defenses wide open. Berserkers rampaged through the Imperial lines and Angron prepared to deliver the decisive blow to the beleaguered defenders in each hive.

That was when Chapter Master Logan Grimnar played his trump card: he called in the assistance of the Grey Knights, having done so the moment he learned about the danger facing Armageddon. It may have been Grimnar's first real test as Chapter Master but it wasn't his first rodeo against Chaos and he had called for their help as soon as he'd learned the nature of their foe on Armageddon.

The Grey Knights for their part dispatched an entire Terminator company to the front - some 100 terminators - a force the size of which is rarely seen and only against the most dire threats. The Grey Knights arrived just as Chaos forces surged toward the hives, teleporting directly into Angron's presence at the center of his host. The primarch was flanked by a dozen bloodthirsters, forming a bodyguard around him as the Grey Knights, led by Brother Captain Aurellian, fought their way to Angron.

After a desperate fight they managed to defeat Angron, bombarding him with psychic energy that cast him back into the warp. Aurellian died in the process, while Angron was banished for a time. Fewer than a dozen of the hundred or so Grey Knights who undertook the mission survived. With their leader vanquished, the remaining Chaos forces fell into disorder and were routed, retreating back to their space hulk as they were pursued across the Ash Wastes by the Guard Steel Legions.

The Imperial forces had triumphed, though almost all of Armageddon Prime's industrial infrastructure had been destroyed. Angron was dead but his monolith still stood at the heart of the equatorial jungle, exerting a malign influence.

and leaving him unable to return for more than one hundred years. While they were doing this, the Space Wolves launched a massive counter-attack around the Chaos flank, routing them and forcing the World Eaters to retreat back to their space hulk.

A Final Betrayal

After Angron and his hordes were defeated, there was one question left: What to do about the loyalists on Armageddon? The Administratum began a campaign to ruthlessly suppress all information about the war and the diabolic forces which assaulted the planet. They purged records and comms but no amount of wiping would erase the events from the minds of the survivors. Armageddon was too important to the Imperium's industrial machine to just declare Exterminatus on the planet so instead the Inquisition set in motion a new plan: they rounded up every man, woman, and child who fought against Angron, sterilized them, and then put them in forced labor camps on the south end of Armageddon Secundus. Millions lived out the rest of their lives in horrible slavery as the reward for their loyalty and effort in the war.

Once the hive cities had been scoured of all but their elite ruling class, the Administratum relocated millions of workers form far-away sectors to replace those who were displaced. Through this process, the lone dissident was Logan Grimnar, who considered this a grave betrayal of the people of Armageddon and their sacrifice. This would set the seeds for later conflict between the Space Wolves and the Inquisition, something which would come up again in the Battle for Sanctus Reach.

This betrayal of the loyalist forces is absent from the original Battle for Armageddon game but was introduced in Codex: Chaos in 1996. It helps establish the Imperium as a villain willing to betray its people and throw them into the meat grinder to maintain control, and positions the Space Wolves for additional conflict within the Imperium itself. Later lore explorations of the war's aftermath would make the Inquisition's actions even more horrific.

Where to Read More

There isn't a lot to read here. The original rulebook excerpts on the first war for Armageddon are pretty sparse. The most notable lore rewrite is in White Dwarf 279, in which they flesh out that one-page excerpt into a full ten or so pages with a detailed depiction of the battle between Aurellian and Angron. The best account you're going to find in the novels is The Emperor's Gift, by Aaron-Dembski Bowden. It's a highly regarded novel that details the events of the First War for Armageddon as experienced by Hyperion, a Grey Knight of Squad Castian.

After that you can find small bits and pieces here and there in different Chaos Space Marine books, the Codexes for Grey Knights and Space Wolves in 5th edition, and Codex: Khorne Daemonkin from 7th edition. Not of it is super current, but the lore doesn't seem to have changed.

Final Thoughts

The first War for Armageddon is an interesting footnote in the planet's history. It's not a terribly complicated conflict or one rife with details, but it does give the planet a cool mystique - "oh hey lemme tell you about the *real* first war for Armageddon - the secret one - is just a cool concept to bring up. The Chaos incursion has also acted on occasion as the inciting factor in other books and lore, as the influence of Angron's invasion can still be felt half a millennia later.
