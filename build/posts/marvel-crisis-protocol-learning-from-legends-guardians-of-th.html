Marvel Crisis Protocol Learning From Legends: Guardians of the Galaxy With Zach Cohen

November 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to Tabletop Battle’s Marvel Crisis Protocol: Learning From Legends article series! In these pieces, we interview top-tier MCP players to learn what they’re playing and how to get the most out of their chosen affiliation. You can read our other articles in the series by clicking the links below:

  • Winter Guard with Ludo
  • Web Warriors with Zach Cohen
  • Wakanda with Mike Whalen
  • Convocation with Zach Cohen
  • Dark Dimension with Brady Schroeder
  • Midnight Sons with Jeremy

It's time to grab your Walkman and queue some 80s beats, because in this article we're taking a look at Zach Cohen's Guardians of the Galaxy roster. At the time of this writing, Zach is the fourth-ranked Guardians player worldwide on Longshanks, and he's seen consistent success with the affiliation at several big events, such as taking down Chicago's 61-player Second Wind 5 and, more recently, going 3-1 at the 18-team Quad Goals 2026.

Guardians are one of the older affiliations in MCP, and with that age, they've evolved from awful to pretty darn good and seen fairly consistent additions of new, powerful models. That said, there's more to Guardians than chuckin' buckets of dice (though they do that pretty well), so let's jump into Zach's list.

Image courtesy of Atomic Mass Games

Zach! This is the third interview you’ve done with me. Part of that is because we frequent one of the same MCP communities online, but also because you’ve had success breaking into the leaderboards for a whole bunch of affiliations.

I’ve seen you say a few times that you don’t really meta-chase, but you happen to like things that keep finding themselves well-situated in the meta. Is that the case for Guardians? What makes them so appealing to you?

I’ve loved these guys outside the game since the first trailer for the movie came out back in 2014. It got me so interested that I read Annihilation, Conquest, and the Abnett GotG run to figure out who these guys were before the movie, and those were the first comics I ever read.

In-game, I was really disappointed with them when they first released, as I’m sure most people were. After the first round of updates made them a real affiliation, they just clicked as such an amazing toolbox that could be played in lots of different ways and could both ask and answer a ton of different questions. I find that sort of team very fun to play.

Expanding on that, you’re playing Guardians as your main thing right now, right? Why them right now over some of the other things you’ve dabbled with? Is it a good meta for them, or are they just what’s interesting right now?

Some of it is just my natural rotation through my teams, but I think that flexibility I mentioned above makes them really stand out right now. People often think of them as a purely fighty team, but with models like Moondragon, Bill, and Nova, they play a mean scenario game as well. And you can build them with models like Nova and splashes like Ultimate Spider-Man and Hulkbuster, where you can very effectively pursue your attrition and scenario games with the same models at the same time. Also, Angela just got her big glow-up recently, and she’s always been a favorite model of mine.

Image Courtesy of Troy Alanis

Focusing just on Star-Lord, since he’s the only leader you run in your list (which we’ll get to shortly), is there an elevator pitch for what Guardians want to do to win games? I know all lists have some level of flexibility depending on the Crises or matchup, but what’s plan A, if there is one?

It very much depends on the Crisis draw. I think GotG are one of the best teams at Power Overload and Wedding Party and can score out really fast on those with heavy displacement, but they also excel at Crises like Assault Ships or Survivors where you’re going to fight your way to the end. A good roster will let you flex both ways and play different model counts to accommodate both playstyles.

The flexibility definitely seems like an incredible attribute, and it's the people who leverage that instead of going all-in on attrition who seem to have the most success with Guardians. With that lead-in, let's take a look at the list you're running right now:

Obviously you’re running mono Star-Lord here. You’ve historically been pretty vocal about Adam Warlock not being for you. Could you expand on that a bit? Do you not think he pulls his weight for 8-threat, or does he just have a different game plan/list that’s hard to, or not worth, fitting in with Quill? Or is Star-Lord and his leadership just good enough that he doesn’t have a downside worth trying to “cover” for?

I just don’t really find 8-threat leaders to be super fun. You’re putting so many of your eggs in that one basket, and while Adam’s spender absolutely rips, his builder is just ok, and he has no third source of damage, so he’s not reliably doing the kind of work I need from a model who’s half my squad. I prefer a Star-Lord list where I have my work-doers spread more evenly amongst the models on the table so one bad activation doesn’t put me in as deep a hole. That said, Adam’s leadership is maybe my favorite I’ve ever played with; I just wish it were on a model I liked more. But I definitely think Star-Lord can cover all of your bases, and you don’t need another leader.

Image Courtesy of Troy Alanis

You were also quite vocal about being a proud Nova hater for a long time, but he’s obviously in your list now. What changed, and what does he bring to your list?

Short answer is the Crises changed. On the old set, I wanted more from a 5 than a kinda okay turret or a guy who made one good attack. This set feels like it rewards mobility and displacement a lot more, so the tools he brings to the table have become more valuable to me. I mentioned Power Overload and Wedding Party as big strengths for the roster, and he’s exceptional on both of those. Having a set where the secures are more important scoring elements than the extracts means Nova has more of a place.

Is he a model that’s hitting the table a lot for you, then? You’ve mentioned a few times now that one of the Guardians' strengths is that they can pivot well between attrition and scenario-first play. Do you have a “core” you usually start with for each playstyle, or is it really a true toolbox that you customize pretty fully for various styles of play?

I’m customizing a lot, yeah. I’ve got some fairly set loadouts for different crises, but it’s not a situation where “every list starts with x, y, and z and finish for threat.” Moondragon obviously gets played more on the scoring setups, and Angela on the fighting ones. I am playing a lot of Nova in both modes, though, because I’m playing a lot of 4-wide Squads, so having two good affiliated 5s helps make that happen.

We’ll talk a bit more about who is here, but I want to touch on who isn’t. Rocket and Quasar are two models that I do see pop up in Guardians lists from time to time. Have either been a consideration? What’s keeping them out?

I’ve played a ton of Rocket over the years, and I think he’s a good 2 in a vacuum, but I already have Star-Lord as a sit-back-and-shoot guy and don’t need another model doing that job. Add to that the fact that I play a lot of models that want to start making attacks round 1 if possible and not just walk to the midline and grab an extract, and Toad fits that 2 slot much better for my squad compositions.

I’m not a fan of Quasar much at all. I was really high on her at first read, but after playing her a bunch, I found she often just made two attacks, and that’s it. Her builder is good, but it’s not lighting the world on fire. Her spender does that, but it’s very expensive, and if your builders aren’t spiking and you need to be charging, it can be really hard to use it. She’s decently durable, and the power drain thing is nice but hard to afford if you want to hit her beam, but it doesn’t all come together into a 4 that will reliably win me games like the other 4s I’m running.

Nebula is the other one probably worth mentioning that you’re not running. Do you just already have enough murder that giving up the scoring body is hard to justify - particularly with Flips and Sources so popular right now? Are there any other 2s that you’ve considered besides Toad, who ended up making the list?

I do like Nebula a lot, but I can’t really justify more than one 2 threat, and Toad does the job I need there better than any of the others, so no, there aren’t any others I considered. If my plan is primarily murder, I’m typically just playing narrower and not bringing a 2 to the table.

Image Courtesy of Troy Alanis

Hulkbuster is an interesting addition. He’s the 6 that I see most often right now, but I’m also just seeing fewer 6-Threats in general than I used to. What threat levels or Crises are you looking to bring out Tony on?

He’s a staple of my Wedding Party 20 for sure (Star Lord, Moondragon, Nova, Hulkbuster, Toad). He’s also great at Power Overload if I get put on 20 threat there, and he makes a great inclusion in a lot of 4-wide squad options at 18 and 19. Like Nova, he’s a guy who very effectively controls secures while also doing respectable damage, and his spender absolutely rips. Denying power on big attacks can be huge for controlling the game's tempo.

I don’t think USM requires much discussion. He’s a cool dude who does cool things, and I’m sure he loves the consistency of a Winging It token when he gets on. Proxima, though, is less common as a splash. What’s the sales pitch for her?

She’s a unique combination of fast, durable, and decently hard-hitting. This spot is the most flexible. I was trying out Man-Thing here for a bit, but it’s mainly for when I’m at higher threats and want to go 5-wide but would rather have two action models than Hulkbuster and Toad, for example. So I might instead do a 5 and Proxima in that pairing. She’s just sort of an all-rounder I can throw at a problem and be pretty confident I’ll get her threat’s worth of value out of.

So you generally prefer to be four-wide, with exceptions. Again, things are obviously contextual, but are there any combinations of characters that you like to use together, as you mentioned above for your Wedding Party 20?

I play in a pretty fighty meta for the most part, so that certainly contributes to my 4-wide preference, and it does depend on the crisis. Like Power Overload or a P2F, I'm 100% going 5-wide. In general, Nova and Moondragon are a great pair for reliable displacement, and Star-Lord and Agent Venom are a great pair for Foreign Assignment. The team doesn't have much in the way of direct synergy, though. My characters don't really interact with each other.

It’s tough to discuss mid-game tactics in a game as dynamic as MCP, but are there any particular lines you’re looking for in Round 1? How are you balancing scoring vs. getting a few extra attacks off early with your long-range guns and leadership?

I'm often happy to go down 1 on Extracts if I can bait people taking one in front of Angela, for example. I don't like to use her as a safe grab; it feels like a waste of her potential. Otherwise, I play hard for the secures, using my strong displacement to control the midline. The Crisis itself dictates how much I care about coming ahead round 1; for Wedding Party or Power Overload, I usually want to try to get a big score cushion Round 1 and close the game out fast, but if I'm on something like Lockdown and Alien Ship, I'm happy to go down a few points and get an attrition advantage.

Touching on the leadership briefly, are there any models you put the Winging It tokens on most often that appreciate the reroll more than others, or is it just based on board state? Do you ever drop a token on someone with the intent to use the rerolls for dodge or defense, or are they attack-first and defensive in a pinch?

Generally it's the people with the best attacks. Angela, Nova, Hulkbuster, USM. Agent Venom needs them to build power. Later in the game, it becomes more contextual based on who will get to use them, depending on positioning and likely order of activation. So, like if Star-Lord is in a position where he needs to lead off the round to Hit and Run to safety, he's likely to get a token to give that first activation a little more punch, but if he's sitting pretty in the back and is probably going to be a late activation that just takes a potshot, that's less demanding of one. If I don't have prio, I'm probably not giving it to someone on 1 HP. Using it on defense or dodge is never plan A; the rate of return just isn't as good as using it on attack, so they only really get used on defense if it's life or death.

Image Courtesy of Troy Alanis

Again, I know the answer is likely “it depends,” but do you have a template for which TTCs you’re bringing most often? Guardians have a few affiliated cards, but none really seem like automatic slam-dunks every game like, say, Avengers Assemble or Wakanda Forever.

Guardians cards aren't great, with the exception of Foreign Assignment, but that obviously isn't an every-game card because Agent Venom isn't an every-game character. I'm pretty much always taking my two restricted. I usually only take Call for Backup I on Wedding Party or Power Overload. I only really like it when the Secure I'm clearing is worth more than one point. I end up playing a lot of Recal and Galaxy's Greatest, which is fine but not super exciting. I do generally like Loveable Misfits, but only bring it if I have 4 or more Guardians in the squad, which doesn't happen all that often. Survival gets played pretty often against other fighty teams.

Guardians are one of those affiliations that I’ve just really struggled with historically. For whatever reason, they always have my number, and given their model diversity and quality, it feels hard to go into a game against them feeling advantaged in turn 0. From the other side of the table, are there any affiliations that feel like an uphill battle?

X-Men on P22Fs is a tough one. That's the only one that really gives me pause these days. Everything else I feel pretty confident in it at least being a solid game, but that matchup I've got to work a lot harder at.

How about Crises? The new (well, I guess it’s not new anymore after a year) ban system lets you nope out of whatever you like least. Are there any Secures and Extracts you’re really looking to avoid, or do you feel reasonably ready for anything pending the matchup?

I generally try to avoid Strike Team, Mutant Madman, Senators, Spider Infected, and Supplies (that last one might be ok for the team, but it's a god-awful crisis, and I plan to go the whole rotation without playing it - the awfully designed Crisis selection process lets me). I don't really enjoy playing Jailbreak much, but it's generally fine for the team, and I'll pick it over the other extracts I listed.

To start wrapping up here, I like to ask a non-roster-specific question. There are still lots of Guardians-related characters from Marvel Comics missing from MCP. Which one would you be the most excited to see announced?

Cosmo is number one with a bullet. I want my psychic Soviet dog.

Finally, is there anything you’d like to promote? Any events, social media channels, or anything else? If people want to chat about Guardians with you, what’s the best place to reach out?

Don't Table Flip is coming up soon, MLK weekend (January 18019, 20207) in Austin, TX. It's sold out, but there's a waitlist, which means my YouTube channel will come out of hibernation, and I'll be posting games with commentary from that event. I'm on Discord as Zach; I'm in a lot of the MCP ones but only really active in Taco Truck. Feel free to tag me or DM me, though, and I'll see the notification; I'm always happy to chat! Thanks for having me, these are always a lot of fun.

And that's a wrap! Join us next time as we break down another MCP roster.
