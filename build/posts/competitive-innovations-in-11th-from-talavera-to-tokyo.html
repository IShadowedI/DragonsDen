Competitive Innovations in 11th: From Talavera to Tokyo

November 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

I...didn't think this could happen any more.

Somehow, we've hit a mystery gap in the event schedule across most of the world, with but a single GT+ singles event, logged from Australia. The UK is, perhaps, recovering from the Coventry UKTC double header. The US had the midterms yesterday, so I assume booking venues for the weekend would have been prohibitively expensive, because politicians needed those for rallies and/or baffling crashouts.

In Europe, it's a slightly different story - lots of players were gathered at the massive Talavera team tournament, which normally we'd have to um and ah about finding space for, but due to the extremely light schedule of regular events, we can happily give some proper attention. Finally, GW ran an official super RTT at the Tokyo Warhammer cafe, so we'll bring that in too. That gives us something that looks enough like a column to be getting on with. For Friday, we'll put together some sort of editorial.

Everyone happy? Let's go.

Thoughts

New Custodes make their first foray into a Showdown, facing off against a daunting challenge from Dark Angels, who are one of the most powerful forces in the current metagame.

On the Custodes side, we have what is quickly emerging as the One True Custodes shell, at least with the points as they stand. The consensus is that those points are a bit high (certainly on the non-Dreadnought units), so you need to build a list that 1. has Dreadnoughts, and 2. squeezes maximum value out of every other model it puts on the table. 2x Allarus squads, Dreadnoughts and support as Lions/Moritoi and your choice of Solar Watch/Emperor's Chosen/Dread Host is the consensus route to achieving that. Lions provides your Terminator squads with high mobility, and allows the second unit to pivot to scoring duties in an emergency via Unleash the Lions. It also provides some defences against highly mobile high threat armies, as Vigil Unending can blunt a key charge, witha  devastating counterattack following. Allarus are also great all-purpose killers thanks to +1 to W against anything they're not already going to mince into a fine paste.

Those are supported by Dreadnoughts, which are just plain good. The Galatus is a phenomenal profile into the current metagame, and the Memento one is a particular nightmare, while a Telemon with a Feel No Pain is good as ever, especially because of how good the overwatch from dual fist flamers is. Finally, a Caladius provides some ranged anti-tank, and is especially strong in variants running Emperor's chosen as here.

What are they up against? Dark Angels. Riding around on their bikes, playing ominous music, and generally making a nuisance of themselves. Vengeances are definitely not what the Custodes want to see, as they are pretty much perfect for chewing through Allarus (and can at least threaten everything else), while the spike damage of the big melee threats also isn't ideal (but a bit dicier thanks to aforementioned Vigil Undending being able to throw off the maths).

I think this is a tough game for the Custodes but not unwinnable - they have more good threats than the Dark Angels can kill fast, and the mission suits them - skulking 12OC of Trajann and Allarus toed next to a ruin on the near side of the central objective forces the Dark Angels to commit pretty hard to shift it, and if that goes even a little bit wrong, things could go very rapidly south for them. They're also distinctly short on things that reliably kill the Telemon - just one Eradipulsor is extremely in the range that can bounce off invulns and just not really do anything.

I think the Dark Angels have to cede the centre for one turn and try and soften the Custodes up a bit, and then go for a big dunk on turn 2 or 3, aiming to close the game out. It helps how good the incidental shooting on the bikes is at clearing out stuff like Sisters of Silence, which can happen during the go turn as almost an afterthought. Legitimately, things could still go either way at that point, and it seems plausible the game could swing hard one way or the other. It would appear that for now, the Unforgiven remain unmoved.

Archetype

Pressure Aeldari

Thoughts

Undefeated elves! Love that for us.

This list is also fully sick, going for proper lightning fast aggression thanks to the speed provided by Eldritch Raiders, and packing in a bunch of units that hit really well into the current metagame. Small squads of Shining Spears and Banshees are both in target-rich environments right now, and with this many units flitting around it's tough for opponents to avoid providing favourable trades. Having access to Jain Zar's unit also provides a bit of a sliding scale of commitment. When you need something really heavy dealt with, both the big Exodite squad and Yriel's unit provide options. The Exodites give you lots of D3 outut (especially if teed up by Lhykhis), which provides more of a deterrant to enemy deathbricks than the eldar can usually muster. Yriel's squad, meanwhile, has the always desireable option of accessing D4 melee on the prince himself, plus can create a huge swing if the foe undercommits on dealing with them and they can Withdraw and Reinforce. Don't sleep on the option of using the -2" charge stratagem to increase the chances of that either - that can be a big spanner in the works for melee enemies.

The Avatar and Eldrad round things out, the former forcing even more caution from foes thanks to the risk of him suddenly zooming 18" and Charging, while Eldrad keeps the army supplied with the copious quantities of CP it needs to power all the nonsense. Completely different to almost anything else out there, and super cool, congratulations Rhys!

The winning team brings together a pretty cool mix of lists, with a few specific synergies to call out. The list uses the sixes format to ensure that even after putting down its first Defender, it can have access to two pairs of builds with shared attributes, which can really limit what foes can safely defend with. The Knights and Orks can both go hard over the top shooting-wise, especially into hull-centric builds, while if enemies cannot handle a big unit of quality Terminators backed by a Primarch, both the Dark Angels and Thousand Sons can bully them. There's even a third sub-theme which is builds where all the key pieces are hulls/monsters, seen across all of the Necrons, World Eaters, Orks and Knights, creating a really uphill challenge for any composition without enough anti-tank.

That should ensure the team gets at least some very favourable matchups each round, and my instinct from some of the details of the lists is that the plan on paper was to set up 2 very favourable games in each round, and then hold the line with the rest. I say on paper because they obviously did way better than that, but several of these lists are very good at conserving points. Triple spawn in Thousand Sons maximises their PA capability, helping them eke out one more turn of scoring if things go south, while running shooty Orks as your Disruption choice is a great way to minimise the bleed on that Disposition. I also really like the use of Khorne Daemonkin for Reconnaisance - the Angron/Defiler/Skarbrand triumverate is so terrifying that foes can't go for a counterplay option of trying to rush down the army till they're dealt with, guaranteeing at least a few turns of free reign on scoring.

About the only thing here I'm not sold on is Inner Circle Task Force as a pair with Wrath instead of Assault Brethren. It has some cool tricks, but opening up a vulnerability to massed AP-1 weaponry (as Wrath doesn't get AoC) seems unnecessary, especially as the role of that build feels more like "don't die" than "do damage". Nitpick though - this setup seems really good, and earned a well-deserved win for the team, congratulations!

Analysis

My first question, looking at the third team here, is whether the Spanish know something about Starshatter Arsenal that the rest of us don't. It's a good build, but seeing it so universally present at the top is quite different to the metagame everywhere else.

I guess it does line up well with a plan of going heavy, and this sure is another team doing that, but with a different cast. Grey Knights provide mobile Dreadknights with reliable D3 firepower, a great tool for plenty of games, while anyone lacking anti-tank is going to have a miserable time into that guard build. Two big tanks and a full Bullgryn brick. Delightful. Also exciting here is the inclusion of Custodes, using the Dread Host version of the One True Build we discussed above. Dread Host feels like a really fantastic choice for Teams specifically because of the toolbox it offers. Being able to set up 7" re-rollable charges out of Ingress is pretty unique, especially with stuff as nasty as the Trajann squad, so it's a good spoiler to stuff like Knights that can't screen effectively. I appreciate leaning into that with the use of a Shield Captain in the second squad, as it means on-board Ingress is always a threat. The Host also pulls double duty by being super good into any sort of melee-skewed horde, as the boosted version of Dacatarai is essentially a "no thank you" button for that kind of game.

Also clearly popular in Spain is CSM melee, as we see a second usage of that here to cover off Disruption. If you're running a bunch of Possessed and Mulilators at the enemy you're definitely going to get some points out of the game, with the upside potential of just purely flattening them, so this is solid.

Last, and not least, this setup has a much more out-there usage of Ultrawatch to take on Recon. Deathwatch Terminators and Eradipulsors provide a solid core of killing power, and then lots of Intercessors and Outriders provide excellent board presence (and a bit of move blocking if needed). There's even a Rhino for two squads of Intercessors to ride around in, which is cute. Being able to go this wide with the floor of your durability being "an Intercessor" is quite unusual, and there's clearly something to it, as this was the second best performing list on the team (after the Custodes). Some nice outside the box thinking in evidence there and on several other fronts in this setup, well done to the team!
