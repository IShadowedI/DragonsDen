TheChirurgeon's Road Through 2026, Part 42: The Las Vegas Open

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I talked about the point drops and how they affect Death Guard, and I tested out a new list that focused on making the most of said drops, bringing an assload of beefy, fast units to the table to overwhelm opponents. I'm still mulling over that list, but I've been traveling for the past week, including a trip to the 2026 Las Vegas Open... which included no games. But there's still plenty to talk about, so let's dig in.

Why Did I go to LVO?

This is ironically the third time I've been to the Las Vegas Open's main event - I went to the Teams Tournament back in January and plan to go again - but also the third time I've gone without actually playing in the event. Each of the last two times I attended, it was to play secret preview games of Spearhead and 40k tenth edition, respectively. So LVO is always a weird experience for me, a convention where I'm attending but not playing and also it's the biggest 40k tournament in the country.

What I'm typically there to do - besides playing secret games - is business development for Tabletop Battles. I'm there to shake babies and kiss hands, or something like that. I'll meet up with the amazing staff at Frontline Gaming (shout out to Jake, who's always fun to hang out with), but I'll also use it as an opportunity to talk to the other companies at the event, connect on what they're doing, and talk about how we can collaborate.

Case in point: the Tabletop Titans have a new game called Wyldwood coming out that they just put out a Kickstarter for. We've been working with them on it, and may do more coverage in the future, but while I was at the event I took the opportunity to stop by their booth and talk to Adrian Phillips about the game, how it was doing, and what we might do together in the future. Sponsored content on Tabletop Battles is always an interesting process; we do not charge for reviews (and have sent back review copies when we felt games were bad), and outside of Kickstarter announcements the content we charge for tends to be the kinds of things we'd publish anyways - a tactics guide on a faction or paint tutorials. Our big ask on these is that the publisher cover the cost of writing the article for our authors. But the goal is that these end up being helpful resources with good SEO - an informative article on how to play a faction written by people who've tested and liked your game is a great resource for players.

So a lot of the work I'm doing at LVO when I'm there is stuff like that. This year, a big part of that was Bandai Namco's new game, Gundam Assemble, which features the mobile suits waging battle on a hex map with cards and dice. I played the upsized version of the game in a demo to get a feel for it, and I enjoyed it. I need to see how list building and deck construction works in the game before I make a final decision on it but I picked up a starter kit for it because it was only $35 (insane value) and it came with Gundam Wing models. Easy sell.

The other reason I was at LVO was to help Norman run the Sunday StarCraft tournament we'd signed up to run. We've been doing a lot of work to bring StarCraft support to the Tabletop Battles App, and we're also really interested in running tournaments for the game. It's got a wonderful, tight ruleset that lends itself well to competitive play at the tournament level. I'm not here to actually help Norman run the event so much as just be around while he does, so he won't be bored. Which is great, because Norman and I can talk about anything, forever - see our Patreon-only Podcast - and in a similar fashion to Greg, he and I can just sit around making each other laugh for hours.

Friday night we decided to grab dinner together as my wife, Mrs. Niche Tea, was out grabbing dinner with her parents and our schedules just weren't lined up. On the recommendation of a friend, we went to a steakhouse off the strip called Primal. It's got medieval theming - think suits of armor and wax candles - and some solid selections. It also had these fucked-up angled water glasses that made me feel like I was losing my mind whenever I looked at them:

Norman and I ended up splitting a 32oz ribeye Steak and each getting a baked potato - these were ridiculous 1-pound monsters, plus some mushrooms and brussels sprouts to ensure we weren't just eating meat and potatoes. The steak itself was excellent and the potato was solid. We left Primal properly sated, heading back over to the venue for a bit before calling it a night. I didn't take a picture of the steak before we ate it, so enjoy this photo of the bone from the thing:

I met up with Mrs. Niche Tea back at our hotel shortly after dinner and made our way back to the hotel room to settle down. She was unfortunately not feeling well the entire weekend, which freed me up to do more LVO stuff but also meant she was struggling through our group activities. I was doing fine.

Well, kind of. I woke up to go to the bathroom at 3am.

And again at 4am.

And 5am.

And 6am.

And seven. I was starting to suspect I had contracted whatever my wife had and she had more than just traveler's sickness but then I opened up Discord to coordinate with Norman and it turns out he was feeling it too:

And like, I wasn't happy Norman was shitting constantly, but at the same time I also was a bit relieved we could immediately pinpoint the cause. Makes it way easier to weather that storm if I know I can just eat a banana and down some Immodium and try and get through the day. it was... challenging, though the Immodium did its job for me pretty well through the afternoon and I ended up stabilizing, especially after eating a lot of rice at dinner.

The big challenge we faced on Saturday was terrain: Thanks to an honest mix-up between us and FLG, we had no terrain for the StarCraft event. So we had to scramble a bit - we borrowed some sets from our players for some of the tables, and I bought every starter kit FLG had at their store to hastily assemble. Shout-out to The Army Painter for lending us some clippers to get it all clipped off the sprues.

We made good time with the process, getting three starter boxes clipped and assembled in about an hour, and using FLG's leftover 40k ruins and Halo terrain to build the other tables. All total, we had three full tables of StarCraft terrain and three that worked well enough using other terrain kits. Scrambling to get things done at the last minute isn't my favorite way to do things, but it's also something I kind of enjoy - that kind of hectic energy motivates me and gave us purpose and the stress was relatively minor.

We'd originally planed to go to Area 15 and visit Omega Mart - Meow Wolf's Vegas installation - around 2, but thanks to our detour in terrain sourcing and building, we showed up two hours later, giving us only about an hour and a half or so to wander through the place. It was really cool, and I'd like to go back at some point and spend real time in there and do more with the story.

The reason we didn't have much time was because we had a room to escape - on the recommendation of fellow Tabletop Battles author and escape room reviewer Theresa Wagner, we did the Blair Witch Escape room off the strip. It's definitely one of the most involved rooms I've done, with multiple rooms and live actors, and there were some legitimate jump scares in the thing. Which is not great when you are afraid of shitting your pants at any moment because the steak you at last night has been wreaking havoc on your innards all day.

The good news is that we made it out of the thing under time and with our crew - Me, the missus, Norman, RagnarokAngel, and "Contemptor" Kevin Stillman - with time to spare. We parted ways after that, as I had to head over to the exclusive Gundam Assemble content creator event, which was back at Area 15.

I wrote about this already this week, but the event itself was a cool hangout, with free drinks, demo tables paint tables, and previews of upcoming sets. We got some cool swag and took photos, grabbed drinks and passed on the snacks, and I sat down and talked to Travis cheng (HappyRaccoon) and John Rees (Can You Roll a Crit?) about the game for a while as Travis played through a game against his friend, taking the opportunity to actually stretch and play tactically. The demo games Bandai Namco had set up were fine, but they're geared more toward beginners and people who don't play wargames. I wanted to dig into the mechanics more and really play with the cards and the respawn mechanics.

The lighter load made sense for the event in that it seemed like a 70/30 mix of gundam/anime influencers vs. tabletop/wargames people while I was there. You could kind of tell who played lots of games because the mechanics are simple enough it took about five minutes to "get it" - the hardest part of the game to me is line of sight mechanics with hexes but I also figure those will take one game to iron out and internalize. Mrs. Niche Tea and I stayed at the event for about an hour, then went back to our hotel.

Sunday was a mix of two things: StarCraft (run by Norman) and the final day of the 40k singles event. I was personally rooting for fellow Tabletop Battles writer Innes Wilson in the event, and he had a stellar run with his Adeptus Mechanicus army, taking them all the way to the finals before eventually falling to Kit Smith Hanna. At one point Norman and I stopped by Innes' table during the semi-finals to check on his game against Jack Murphy and although we tried to stay out of the camera shot it turned out we were very much in the shot, leading to Andrew sending us this screenshot.

It's my favorite shot of the event, because it has such perfect "hanging out and chilling next to the table" energy. You can practically hear us going "fuck 'em up" Innes. At this point Innes was firmly in the driver's seat, having put a beacon unit of chicken walkers on Jack's home objective. Innes is always fun to talk to and hang out with, and I was sad to see him go down in the finals, though there's no shame in losing to Kit.

Sunday night we did all you can eat Korean BBQ with Alice and Kevin and called it a night shortly after. Our flight back was Monday morning and Mrs. Niche Tea was still dealing with her own stuff, though I'd more or less recovered by Monday morning and was eating fine. Norman and I once again ate way too much, but this time no one paid the price for it the next day - at least not to my knowledge, anyways.

The big takeaway this weekend? I want to play at Worlds. I kind of felt the itch being around 40k without playing in it all weekend, and I decided that if I'm going to the WCW in December, I'm going to play. So it's time to gear up for that.

Final Thoughts

That's it for this week. I've been in New York all week on other business but I'll be back with more games and hobby progress next week, making headway on my Emperor's Children and planning for the final quarter of the year and building my plan to take on the World Championships.

See you Thursday.
