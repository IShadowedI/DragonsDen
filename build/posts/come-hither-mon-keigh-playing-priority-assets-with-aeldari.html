Come Hither, Mon'keigh: Playing Priority Assets with Aeldari

September 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
In today's subscribers-only piece, Lowest of Men takes you through how to exploit the Priority Assets disposition with his beloved Aeldari. Expect tips, tricks, and tales of elf glory (and of course, Shining Spears whiffing before immediately dying). Enjoy!

Gather round, to hear of the whispers of returning Aeldari strength. After yet another run of (small) buffs in the dataslate, the army is beginning to nudge towards being competitively viable once more. I have spent rather a lot of time in the Aeldari tank as a huge fan of the faction, with a bunch of games played on Aspect Host, Ynnari, Windrider and Seer Council in recent months. Today I am going to draw on several of those experiences to look at how you can utilise Aeldari to play the Priority Assets disposition in particular, as I believe that it is here, and especially in teams, that the faction can enjoy a resurgence in the coming months.

Why Priority Assets?

Aeldari Rangers - Credit: RichyP

In a meta of wildly agressive and expansive skew lists operating under Take and Hold, Recon or Purge, Priority Assets sits out as a slightly more passive and defensive option. You score exceptionally well from the safety of your own half, with many missions asking you to perform some actions on objectives or terrain pieces (often central ones).

Some missions also have an end game reward for stealing the opposing home.

Well, damn. If it isn't two things the Aeldari love to do! The army isn't broken at this moment in time, and as such has to stick to it's traditional play style - utilise speed and trickery to leverage a gradual advantage, before unleashing the decisive blow to cripple the opponent and roll to victory. Priority Assets massively suits this - the opponent is forced to engage you in order to stop your generous passive scoring. You bide your time, retaliate hard, and take all the marbles, cackling as you soar back to the craftworld.

Battle Focus also makes Aeldari quite uniquely suited to scoring Priority Assets- we have a number of buffs that enable us to reach the central locations we need to reach to score from far away (+2 move on already rapid flying units), we can switch off overwatch to frustrate enemy efforts to kill our scoring pieces, and we have a reactive move after being shot to potentially protect a scoring piece to try again next turn (one in particular unit has been great at this for me, but I will talk about that later!).

Some of the other dispositions are entirely viable for Aeldari, but my gripe with dispositions like Recon or Disruption in particular for Aeldari was that they often placed quite extensive demands on your unit count and positioning - the army remains very expensive per-wound relative to it's competitors, and you simply don't want to be overextending until it is literally time to kill the opponent.

When the 6++ youtube community chose Aeldari in an online vote to be the army I took to Manchester Supermajor, I knew that Priority Assets would be my disposition of choice. In the end I took a highly experimental toolbox Seer Council list complete with dinosaurs and Yriel to a strong 4-1, narrowly losing to Orks, confirming many of the above theories. So let's talk about some fundamentals and how you can exploit them in list design and play.

List Design Ingredients

Eldar Farseer Skyrunner. Credit: Rockfish
  1. Scorers: Playing Prio you need units to run the middle and push the buttons for your additional primary scoring. Aeldari have a wealth of options for cheap, throwaway button pushers- Farseer Skyrunners look very nice at 60 points. Corsairs have good OC to compliment the task and can exploit hidden to draw the opponent closer. Rangers can threaten a reactive move to make responding to them more complicated. On the... tougher end of the spectrum you can also utilise the Avatar of Khaine here. In certain match ups he is near impossible to hurt for some armies without committing a lot, and he is especially nice for Battle Focusing back behind the plate after the enemy shoots, or forwards into a Heroic Intervention if they've undercommitted.
  2. Anchors: Alongside scoring the centre you need to hold your expansion, including against very agressive pushes from the opponent. I love the Avatar here in certain games, but you can also utilise Storm Guardians or a full brick with a Conclave attached (especially good in Seer Council). This should be coupled with another key Aeldari tool...
  3. Projection: Pushing out beyond your expansion and disrupting the opponent and screening space is very important for Aeldari - it avoids allowing the enemy to push you in your territory and ensures the game happens on your terms. Swooping Hawks and Warp Spiders remain excellent at this, and Jain Zar and Lykhis can also serve in the role when the battle heats up and real units get committed. It is often better to simply ensure the fight happens beyond your lines than to try and take a punch on the expansion itself. Be careful that the projection you apply doesn't accidentally spring board an enemy counter-attack - a sloppy or ill conceived Shining Spear or Lykhis push can result in their sudden death and a lot of extra enemy movement in their turn, so pick your battles carefully and stay outside of Heroic ranges if needs be!
  4. Linebreakers: With the reward for stealing the opposing home in some missions, units that can reach beyond into the enemy deployment are very valuable. Lykhis and Baharroth both can serve here in Aspect Host in particular, Seer Council can turn any good infantry units into late game objective thieves, and the Yncarne can be set up to do the job with certain Ynnari lists too. Don't over-invest in this, but have units that are ready to take advantage if possible.

Detachments and Playstyles

There are a number of Aeldari archetypes that can play Priority Assets, and each will do so in a slightly different way:

Old Faithful: Aspect Host

Asurmen. Credit: Wings

With the change to Priority Assets, Aspect Host can look to play the distances, hiding a fair way back and utilising Aeldari speed to make it impossible for the opponent to stage on you without you getting the first hit in. You can stash fast and deadly units behind your expansion and safe home ruins, ready to punch out the moment the opponent commits to mid-board.

Aspect Host typically plays passively before erupting into an 'all-in' turn - this may involve Asurmen, Lykhis, Jain and one or two Swooping Hawk bricks pushing out at once, combining together to bring down a wide range of different targets. You can bait out some enemy pieces first with Rangers, Corsairs or other cheap units before pulling the trigger and looking to cripple the opponent. Aspect Host still boasts the best damage tools in Aeldari, with Ignore Modifiers and on-tap Lethals or Sustained to help your units do damage into almost any kind of opposition. What it lacks is staying power - so time that strike carefully...

Slow Burn: Seer Council

Aeldari Warlocks of Craftworld She'enshar - Credit: Colin Ward

Seer Council plays Priority Assets exceptionally well. The combination of fast psyker options (via jet bikes or teleportation) and a wonderful stratagem suite that includes mortal wounds when the enemy moves near you and battle-shocks at -1 in the command phase can force the opponent out and into your damage as they try to stop your scoring - the threat of additional objective primary via battle-shock is especially good in the Take and Hold game, where the opponent needs to then commit more to stop you or risk you getting an additional 4 for holding three objectives when that lone Farseer Skyrunner gives their denial chaff a panic attack.

Broadly, you want to gradually draw out and defang the opponent in Seer Council, with teleporting Warlock / Guardian / Psyker bricks relocating every turn to wipe out the enemy in key areas in combination with fast Aspect Warrior pieces. You can then push out from a secure area to cripple the opponents hold on their territory, and they will usually run dry of screening options as the game goes on opening up even more punishing spaces to teleport into and apply damage. Don't play this Detachment too 'fast' - keep the key damage dealers (the Guardian bricks) safe, and use your other pieces to protect them. If you can activate Guardian bricks for the entire five turns, you will have whittled away most of an opposing army.

Elf Nonsense: Devoted of Ynnead

Yncarne: Avatar of Ynnead. Credit: James "Boon" Kelling

Ynnari are probably still hard mode for Aeldari at the moment after many nerfs, but they have a fair few really nice assets to bring to bear when playing Priority Assets. Ynnari Wyches split by Venoms offer you dirt cheap action monkeys that can launch from safety and apply their OC, cycling through the Venoms and encouraging the opponent to push into your threat ranges. You have the threat of a sticky on death stratagem to steal an additional hold if they don't commit real units onto the points to remove you. In addition, Lethal Intent, the Ynnari 'move at the end of the Shooting Phase' jank, can allow you to suddenly sprint a second unit onto an objective in place of the one that was killed. With a D6+1 it's not consistent, but the threat of it is always there, and this combination of classic elf nonsense tricks make it very hard for your opponent to stop your Prio scoring at arms length.

The Yncarne is also really valuable here- it enables you to punish undercommitment even more by teleporting onto a point if the enemy hasn't placed adequate OC there, and has the potential to get behind the opponent late game (keying off a throwaway chaff piece or equivalent that has snuck into the backlines via it's arrive after unit death rule). Yvraine and her Storm Guardian / Warlock brick also makes a wonderful anchor piece here- she can regrow the unit and it's very hard to get through all the Guardians and Warlocks in an activation to stop it before you battle focus away. This combination of tricks leaves the opponent with no choice but to push you more aggressively, at which point you can deliver the counter punch. This Detachment needs the most careful set up to get a song out of, but by goodness it's fun when it works!

Wrapping Up

There you have it folks! Another guide to elf shenanigans for your eyes. I'm having a fantastic time writing these- if there is anything Aeldari specific you'd like written, do let me know in the comments of via Discord. Thank you for your support.

Lowest of Men, out.
