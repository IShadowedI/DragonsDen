Tabletop Battles Reviews: Arcane Journal March of Chaos

August 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello my beautiful Old Worlders! Hot on the heels of the release of a new Core Rulebook and a big confusing FAQ, Games Workshop drops a new Arcane Journal with all of the seasonings we need to spice up our games with a new (completely optional but Matched Play legal) upgrade to Magic, a plethora of 'corrupted' core magic items available to every faction in the game (except Dwarves because they're not allowed to have nice things), and a brand new Army of Infamy for the Forces of Chaos allowing you to mix and match a portion of your Warriors of Chaos and Beastmen army collections.

With the forces of the dark gods converging on the lands of elves and men both sides are pulling out all the stops, so let's dive in and let the chaos begin

A New Way To Make The Magic Happen

The first big reveal in the March of Chaos is the return of the Winds of Magic. In previous editions of Warhammer Fantasy Battle, the Winds of Magic were a dice pool that was generated by each player every turn and it was those dice that players would use to cast and dispel spells. It is a part of the game that at least I personally miss when compared to how magic is handled in the Old World so color me happy that it is kind of coming back. In the before times while some rounds were just determined by who was able to toss the most dice at any given spell, many more were layered and deeply strategic. While these new Winds of Magic rules are not all of that, they do add a new layer of interactivity to your games that I've been excited to see play out.

The Winds of Magic in March of Chaos can be played in one of two ways; either Arcane Magic or Channeling. Games electing to use Arcane Magic gain access to the all new Arcane Ploy deck (available to purchase from your Local Game Store or Papa James themselves). During your pre-game setup, both players draw 6 arcane ploy cards from their deck or, if they do not have access to a deck, roll 6 times on the chart provided in the Arcane Journal to generate them. These ploys can be used by any unit in your army that can cast or dispel spells or use bound spells making them extremely powerful additions to your respective magic 'phases'.

There are 11 different Arcane Ploys to choose from with a range of strengths, though the most powerful are limited to 1-2 per player to avoid one person getting overly lucky and completely upending the game before it starts. The rarest ploys are Towering Conjuration which allows a wizard to target any unit on the board with a spell regardless of line of sight or range and Absolute Power that makes your next spell a perfect invocation on any roll of a double so long as you meet the casting cost. Other ploys grant you free rerolls on casts and dispels, toss extra dice at casting/dispelling attempts or simply automatically dispel a spell with no roll required. In general, the ploys are neat add-ons to the game but so much of the magic has been neutered these days that these only go so far to adding interest to what we already have.

If playing with a deck is a bit to random for your liking (like it is for me) then Channeling may be more appealing. Channeling grants each player pools of dice for each of the wizards/rune priests you have included in your list with higher level caster generating more dice than their weaker counterparts. Each level 1 wizard in your army generate 1d3 dice, level 2s generate 1d3+1, level 3s 2d3 and level 4s get you 2d3 +1 dice for your pool. These channeled dice can be used for 5 different effects throughout your games: A channeled die can be used to reroll a generated spell at the beginning of the game, to add an additional die to your casting and dispelling rolls (discarding the lowest), add range to a casting or dispel attempt, or 2 dice can be used to completely reroll a casting or dispel roll (except miscasts). There is no limit to how many times you use your channeled dice in your turn, other than the dice remaining in your respective casters' pools.

This is my favorite of the two ways to play the Winds of Magic and has been a novel addition in the small handful of games I've gotten to try it. The abilities granted by the dice are just enough to make things interesting. Especially because of the downsides. Did I mention using the Winds of Magic has a downside? Because it does and it is wild. Should you and your opponent choose to add the Winds of Magic to your game, you have to also use the all new, extremely punishing, Disastrous Miscast table rather than the old Kinda Meh Miscast table that is sometimes actually good for you. Disastrous Miscasts are extremely punishing, with effects reminiscent of the old 8th edition miscasts amped up to 11. Results on this table range from Str 10 AP 4 flame templates launching out of the wizards belly before it gets sucked into the void and being immediately killed, to being permanently reduced to lvl 1 or having your casting and dispelling rolls reduced by 1 or more for the remainder of the game. Just an absolute bundle of fun that I highly recommend. Having my little spellweaver try to dispel an enemy's attempt at hammerhand only to vomit pure unadulterated violence onto the offending unit before being deleted by the gods was a glorious thing to behold.

The New Winds of Magic make even the lowliest wizards a potential threat. Until they get bodied by the void. Credit: Charlie Brassley

Whether you choose to play Channeling or Arcane Magic, the book also opens up another addendum to magic use in the Old World; Sorcerous Ruses. These are 25 point, random, single-use 'special abilities' (they do not take up any magic item allotment) that give your wizards access to potentially powerful effects...if they go off. If you are addicted to the lottery effect, Sorcerous Ruses are for you. For every wizard that you choose to purchase a Sorcerous Ruse for you roll a d6 at the beginning of the game and gain a special triggered ability. Escapology brings a wizard that just died back to life with 1 wound remaining on a 4+ dice roll, while the remaining 5 Ruses are special dispel effects with potentially big upsides. Daemonic Assailants, for example, has your wizard roll a d6 and add their wizard level while your opponent must roll a d6 and add their wizard's toughness. Should the results be equal, you simply dispel the spell, should your roll be greater, the dispel is dispelled and the caster suffers a single wound with no saves of any kind allowed. Spell Eater is by far the strongest possible ruse, automatically dispelling an opponent's spell or  letting you attempt to dispel a perfect invocation AND the next time your wizard casts a spell they may choose to cast the spell they ate the previous turn with an extra D6 added to the roll. While cute, I wish there were a few more Ruses options that were not just variants on dispels and the randomness factor probably means Ruses see little competitive play. Still, I don't believe they are particularly game-breaking and I'm always a fan of adding new options to your games.

You...Are...Cursed

In addition to the newly upgraded ways of wizardry, March of Chaos adds 17 new 'corrupted' core magic items to the game that, as stated previously, can be used by any faction that isn't stunty. As always, while most of the items here are more narratively focused than useful particularly given how they all have negative effects built in, there are a handful here that will definitely see play in the highest levels of play, including one of the best weapons we've seen to date.

Of particular note and in no particular order:

Daemon-Blooded Steed is a very good Enchanted Item that I imagine Warriors of Chaos and Wood Elf players will try to slot into their lists as it grants cavalry mounts +1 movement, strength and attacks. This is a sweet perk for the recently buffed Daemonic Mounts and Great Stags at the minor cost of gaining the Impetuous rule.

The Amulet of Spell Attraction is a new 25 point talisman that savvy players can employ to gain further control of their opponents' magic phases. Once per turn, the wielder may make a Leadership test when a friendly unit within their command range is targeted by a Magic Missile or Hex and if the roll is successful they may have the spell redirected to them instead. Given that this happens during targeting, you can do some really interesting things here, most obviously forcing your opponents biggest/scariest spell onto a unit with high Magic Resistance or just severely limiting a 'go' turn by forcing a devastating hex onto an unchargeable or hidden target.

The Flail Of Wrecks is an incredibly strong weapon at 45 points. As a flail, it grants the user Str +2 and Armourbane(1) on any turn it charged and always has an AP value of -2. Additionally and most importantly it has the Multiple Wounds (2) special rule. The only downside to the Flail is any roll of 1 to wound reduces the wielder's initiative by 1 which is a very minor annoyance, particularly on elven lords with Strikes First or Chaos Lords that are making their enemies initiative 1 anyway. This is arguably the strongest weapon in the game for the vast majority of factions and I would expect to see it everywhere.

Finally, I need to give a special nod to the Mantle of Drifting Shadow. This 20 point piece of Light Armor is for infantry only and makes its wielder untargettable by shooting or spells outside of 6 inches at the cost of gaining the Loner special rule. While being untargettable is a pretty neat effect in and of itself and would have been incredible on the Daemon Prince before it became a Monster, the Loner downside here is actually all upside for a couple of factions. Chaos players will have a field day being able to add a Sorceror Lord or BSB to a unit of Forsaken of Khorne ramping an already fantastic unit up to 11 with access to the Taskmaster Scourge, Daemonlogy spells, Brazen Collar or any number of banners and I'm sure Wood Elf players would love to be able to add a Spellweaver or Glade Lord to their units of Wardancers. As a lover of jank, I've been brewing combos for these guys for a couple of days now and absolutely been reveling in it.

The Daemon-Blooded Steed is a great upgrade for the already powerful Daemonic Mount. Credit: Falcon40k

The Hordes of Chaos

Last, and possibly least, the followers of Chaos get themselves a brand spanking new Army of Infamy and the option to create Champions of Ruin, though the latter is for narrative play only as Games Workshop are notoriously cowards.

The Hordes of Chaos army of infamy is designed to allow chaos players to mix and match units from their Warriors of Chaos and Beastmen collections into a cohesive force vying for the attention of their Gods.

As far as the composition list goes. The Hordes of Chaos allows for 1 Daemon Prince, Beast Lord, or Chaos Lord in your list regardless of points, 1 Exalted Champion, Great Bray Shaman, or Sorceror Lord per 1000 points and any number of the other character options from Ravening Hordes minus the Doombull who has been replaced by the all new Cult Magister.

Core units in Hordes must make up 33% of your list and you are forced to include at least 1 unit of Warriors of Chaos or Marauders and 1 unit of Gors or Ungors. Additionally, you may take any number of Chaos Warhounds or the new Chaos Cultists. If your army contains a Chaos Lord/Exalted Champ or Daemon Prince as your General then you may take a single unit of Chosen Chaos Warriors as Core, while if you elected to take a Beastlord then you may take a unit of Bestigors in this slot instead.

At special, we see the addition of Marauder Horsemen, Chaos Knights and Chaos Chariots. If your general is a Chaos Lord/Daemon Prince or Exalted Champ then you can also add a unit of Chosen Knights here. While at Rare (which is up to 33% instead of the normal 25) Hordes players can slot in a Shaggoth or any number of Chaos Spawn, Minotaurs, Dragon Ogres, Chimeras, Chaos Giants, Cygors or Ghorgons.

The Option to take Chosen Warriors in Core is a tantalizing one. Even if it probably isn't a particularly strong one.

Unfortunately, as you can see, a lot of faction favorites are missing from this army list. There are no Razorgors, Harpies, or Forsaken to be seen and no Gigantic Spawn or Warpfires either. The moving of all the normal core cavalry units to special is also a huge blow to list building if you're someone playing with objectives in your games. That said, there is still some combo-potential here that you can't get anywhere else and the idea of a Chosen Warrior deathstar is probably appealing to someone.

In terms of special rules, this composition list comes with four. An Unholy Union is the most powerful of the effects, though it is clear that someone got hurt somewhere along the way as the riders on it make it less appealing than it could be. Whilst within 6 inches of a friendly Beastmen unit, Warriors of Chaos units gain rerolls of 1 to hit, while Beastmen units gain rerolls of 1 to wound when within 6 of any Warriors of Chaos. In addition, while within 6 of each other, both factions may choose to apply a -1 to their Flee rolls. It must be noted that these effects are not triggered by and do not effect any characters or units that are shared by both factions in the Ravening Hordes army book. So no juicing up your Dragon Ogres or Spawn with this effect. The lack of character synergy here is the real bummer, particularly given the limited army options in the composition list. That said, rerolls are always welcome and smoothing out a combat phase for your expensive Knights or Warriors just by having a dozen ungors chilling in the back is a small price to pay.

The second special rule and the only other one with a positive effect is For the Dark Gods! Every time a character in your army kills a model in a challenge they must immediately roll on the Gaze of the Gods table and your army general gets a free reroll when using the Gaze. Free Gaze rerolls are always welcome, even if they are a 5 point upgrade option otherwise, and it is pretty common for your Chaos Lords and Champions to have to roll through multiple challenges over the course of the game. This is a pretty flavorful special rule and has the potential to turn your best combat veterans into insane blenders by the end of a game with some lucky rolls.

The final two rules for the Hordes of Chaos are Untrusting Allies and The Shadow Grows. Untrusting Allies simply states that Chaos Characters cannot join Beastmen units and vice versa and neither faction can use the leadership of a character from the other one. The Shadow Grows is a rehash of the same rule in the Heralds of Darkness army of infamy and forces you to have a rank and file unit with a specific mark of chaos for every character you have in your list that has one which further limits your army customization options though here at least it isn't as big of a loss as you'll rarely have more than 2 characters with special marks anyway.

New Units

The Hordes of Chaos introduces 2 new units exclusive to the army and both are very solid additions to your lists.

The Chaos Cultist costs 6ppm comes in squads of 5-20 and has a standard human stat-line with 3s in all the important places, 4 inches of movement and a leadership of 6 (7 with their Apostle champion in play). They come stock with extra hand weapons and throwing weapons though they may swap them for short bows should they wish for free. They come with the Open Order, Skirmishers, Warband, Mark of Chaos Undivided and Ambushers special rules in addition to Dark Pawns and Unseen Threat. Dark Pawns means that friendly units do not have to worry about panicking should a unit of friendly cultists breaks, runs through them, or explodes nearby. Unseen Threat is the real sauce here as it grants the unit a +1/-1 to their rolls to come in from Ambush AND allows them to deploy within 4 inches of enemy units when they do instead of the normal 8 which is really spicy for things like threatening baggage trains or overwhelming smaller backfield units.

The Arcane Journal recommends using a particularly spoopy looking Empire Wizard for your cult magister. Like this Amethyst Wizard absolutely force choking her uber driver offscreen. Credit: Charlie Brassley

The Cult Magister is a 55 points lvl 1 wizard that has been hitting the gym. It is an incredible stat line for its points being WS 5, Str and To 4 with 2 attacks at leadership 8 just like a Sorceror Lord. Given that for whatever reason this bozo can be equipped with a flail for just 3 more points, we're already cooking with gas. They may take a spell from the lore of Dark or Daemonology but unlike other wizards it has no option to upgrade to level 2. This is fine because The Summoning is a very good spell if you don't hit the Steed of Shadows lottery roll. The Magister may pay for ambushers for 5 points and may take up to 25 points in magic items. They have the Mark of Chaos Undivided and two unique special rules; Sacrifice and Thralls of Chaos. Thralls of Chaos simply locks Magisters to units with the Dark Pawns special rule (so just cultists for now), while Sacrifice gives every Magister a free Jagged Dagger. Every time they choose to cast a spell, the Magister can choose to kill a model in its unit and gain a bonus to their next cast equal to the wounds characteristic of the dead. I can definitely see a world where players toss a couple of Magistrates in a unit of Cultists just to sling a little more murder from afar, something the Warriors of Chaos generally lack.

382 - Chaos Lord, Shield, Mark of Nurgle, Extra Arm, General, Daemonic Mount, Flail of Wrecks, Daemon-Blooded Steed, Crown of Everlasting Conquest

175 - Aspiring Champion, Flail, Shield, Mark of Chaos Undivided, Battle Standard Bearer, Monster Hunter's Tapestry, Talisman of the Carrion Crow

300 - Sorcerer Lord, Mark of Chaos Undivided, Wizard Level 4, Daemonology, Taskmaster's Scourge, Spell Familiar, Tome of the Dark Gods

58 - Cult Magister, Flail

58 - Cult Magister, Flail

498 - 18 Chosen Chaos Warriors, Full Plate Armour, Shield, Drilled, Mark of Nurgle, Enhanced Reflexes, Champion, Brazen Collar, Standard Bearer, War Banner, Musician

72 - 12 Chaos Cultists

72 - 12 Chaos Cultists, Shortbow

77 - 12 Ungor Herds, Shortbow, Ambushers, Musician

65 - 10 Ungor Herds, Shortbow, Musician

65 - 10 Ungor Herds, Shortbow, Musician

198 - 3 Dragon Ogres, Heavy Armour, Great Weapon

Here's a quick and dirty list that's far from optimized but tries its damnedest to make Chosen Warriors a thing while holding to the max 25% of your army rule for any individual unit. The goal here is pretty simple, BSB and Sorceror Lord join the Chosen Warriors, hope you get Steed of Shadows and then abuse it and drilled along with the fleet-footed and ambushing Ungors giving them rerolls to bully opponents with a flying wall of heavily armored meat. Your Chaos Lord's job is to be an ICBM and your Dragon Ogres keep your opponents honest when they try to flank your beautiful chosen boys and girls. The Magisters join the Cultist unit with the shortbows to fling a handful of arrows and the summoning spells at lighter targets while the Cultists with throwing weapons and extra hand weapons hunt war machines and baggage trains.

Thoughts

The March of Chaos is certainly an interesting Arcane Journal with a number of welcome additions to the game that may just reinvigorate folks that have been at this since launch. The new Winds of Magic effects are a fun addition that I hope at least occasionally get picked up for tournament play and the new magic items may actually shake up the meta in some form or another which I'm always grateful for. The only real downer here is the Hordes of Chaos army of infamy, though I can understand why everything about it seems reined in to one degree or another as it is definitely an idea that can get out of hand fast. That said, in trying to work through army lists for the faction I find myself really annoyed by all the restrictions and the actual lack of synergy when trying to put together something with a bit of optimization or even with just a good mix of Beasts and Warriors. This may also be because of the recent nerfs to Hagtree and Viletide make Great Brays a lot more disappointing an option. This AoI would have gotten a big shot in the arm for me if at least razorgors and harpies had been options as right now I find it hard to slot in anything more than a handful of ungors into my lists.

Regardless, I think Old World players should be stoked about what we've gotten here, the whole package gets a solid B+ from me.
