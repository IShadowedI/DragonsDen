Conquest Faction Focus: W'adrhŭn - Characters

May 26th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to the second of our W'adrhŭn Faction Focus series! This article will cover all our Character options on the tabletop and give some ideas where you might want to use them in your games. You can also find our first article here talking the background and War Chant mechanic in more detail.

Characters

The W'adrhŭn Character stable is quite broad, but they all have solid defensive profiles and respectable offensive output across the board. Almost all of them bring notable bonuses to the army and a few have some very dangerous damage output when combined with Artefacts, Masteries and Chanting.

Your characters will pick a Cult from Famine, Death or War to apply to them and their Warband. This also can unlock some specialist artefacts and masteries tied to that cult, so you'll need to consider if there's any specific combos you want to focus on. Your choice of Cult also allows the Warband to take the Cult specific Chosen Units as Restricted options, which is a nice way of accessing some specialist units. Finally, your Warlord may choose to follow the Cult of Conquest for their warband, letting you generate those wildcard tokens to use as you demand.

Matriarch Queen

W'adrhŭn Matriarch Queens Conquest Credit: Magos Sockbert

The Matriarch Queens are the leaders of the W'adrhŭn tribes in the lore and are the closest to a generalist character in the faction. She has the widest number of Regiment choices (tied with the Predator at 9) and her abilities aren’t tied to any of the three Battlefield Roles. Her rules lend to a more defensive role including an elite Resolve 4, Stoic and her Draw Event to heal a nearby non-Monster unit 4 Wounds. The latter stipulation sadly makes her Apex Mastery a lot worse as she can’t heal her own mount. W'adrhŭn doesn’t naturally lean into defensive units but if you’re going to build a brick, a Matriarch Queen is the best character to support this.

Her Supremacy The Matriarch Walks always allows you Rally when Chanting, and once per game lets you draw another Command Card immediately after finishing your first Activation. The first part of the ability is pretty situational as W'adrhŭn doesn’t tend to take units big enough to be effective after being Broken anyway. Activating multiple Regiments can be very powerful, especially as they stipulate that this allows you to do Entourage activations too, and it encourages a high-risk playstyle to set up multiple aggressive charges into enemy units. The downside is that if you play all your Command Cards too fast in the Round, your opponent has free reign to move around the board free of reprisal with whatever they have left. in cases, this ability would be stronger if it allowed you to ‘pass’ on an activation rather than activate twice.

The Matriarch is a great utility character who slots into almost any army easily. She brings a diverse mix of Tribes and Speakers units to synergise with the rest of your army well with Mainstay Warbred being particularly attractive. She also can sit in a huge range of Regiments well, whether with some Chosen to boost the durability or with Raptor Riders for a highly mobile early game unit. She can be played as the Warlord but she doesn't lever as much utility or raw power as some of the other options available. Overall, one of the better characters in faction.

Example Builds

  • Matriarch Queen in Chosen of Death - An expensive but synergistic combination; the Queen buffs the staying power of the Chosen and heals them whilst they increase her damage output with Flawless Strikes. You can add Beads of Death too for extra threat extension, just remember you can only activate one Draw Event per activation so no immediate healing that damage taken.
  • Matriarch Queen with Brood of Omgoroh in Raptor Riders - The queen can heal the Raptors to protect against chip damage, and the artifact's Impact hits allow the unit Raptors to one-shot objectives with Mounted Hunters. The Raptors can also ferry her around to heal other units as required, giving you a fun mobile 'ambulance' service for your army.

Chieftain

W'adrhŭn Chieftain Conquest Last Argument of Kings Credit: Magos Sockbert

The Chieftain is a melee powerhouse with some potent aggression focused buffs on the Tribes Battlefield Role. 6 Cleave (2) attacks with Flurry is a strong output before any upgrades or Chant bonuses, making him a great addition to raise the damage output of a unit. Tribe’s Pride adds to this further, granting Flurry to the Regiment itself for some serious damage potential with elite units like Veterans and Chosen of Conquest. His other two abilities both support the Tribes keyword, granting Vanguard (3) to all Tribes units in his Warband and Trample (1) to any Tribes unit within 8”.

His Supremacy Concentrated Assault takes his local buffs to Tribes units and applies them army wide to that same keyword. Notably, it also increases the Vanguard to 4, allowing our Move 5 infantry to march onto objective zones on the turn they arrive and score them. You can build a potent Tribes focused list with this Supremacy, allowing units like Slingers and Warbred to position aggressively whilst providing a little extra chip damage with Trample here and there.

It's worth mentioning the Tontor Rider Mastery, allowing the Chieftain to mount a Tontor from his Warband. The two work well together with the Chieftain adding some reliable damage output to the Tontor and the Tontor being one of the best units we have in extended combat. Most importantly though, the Chieftain exchanges granting Flurry to the Tontor for Vanguard (5), hugely important for getting an expensive Heavy into position when it arrives. The combination is pretty expensive at 410 points before artefacts but you get a lot of bang for your buck.

An excellent character for both his personal output and the bonuses granted to his force, the Chieftain is a great choice in most army builds. Whilst not as versatile as the Queen, he does bring a good mix of Regiments whilst having a use case to join most of them. You can absolutely build an army around his Supremacy ability too, creating a highly aggressive infantry centred list with excellent damage potential.

Example Builds

  • Chieftain with Beads of Death in Chosen of Conquest - These units complement each other perfectly, with the Chosen providing a fast and durable bunker whilst the Chieftain greatly improves their damage output. Combining Beads and Chanting gives an absurd 10+d6” Unstoppable charge threat.
  • Chieftain with Death’s Gaze in Braves. - Don’t let your memes be dreams! For the same cost as a Tontor you can get 8 Stands of Braves... That's a 40 Wound unit that does 45 Impact attacks on the charge, 21 attacks with Flurry and Deadly Blades (plus the Chieftain) and 9 Trample hits just in case they thought you were sorry.

Thunder Chieftain

Wadrhun Thunder Chieftain Credit: Magos Sockbert

The Thunder Chieftain is very similar to the regular Chieftain, bringing an excellent damage output and giving substantial bonuses to his favourite type of units: the Speakers. He exchanges Flurry for Impact 4, Linebreaker, and Trample (2) to represent that he has a triceratops now. With one of the coolest named abilities in the game “Ride the Lightning” he grants Thunder Riders in his Warband Flank, an outstanding ability that guarantees your somewhat slow Heavy cavalry will arrive on Turn 3 and be able to impact (pun intended) the game. His Warband also features a solid selection of Speakers and Tribes units giving more options than you’d expect for such a focused character.

His Supremacy Thunderous Assault is as powerful as it is simple: all Speakers gain Shock whilst he is on the table. For a very long time, this has made the Thunder Chieftain the most popular Warlord choice in the faction. He plays into the strengths of W'adrhŭn’s aggressive playstyle whilst buffing many of the best units in the faction in the process. Raptor Riders and Hunting Pack become genuinely dangerous units whilst Thunder Riders are turned up to 11. There’s nothing subtle about this character; take him and as many dinosaurs as you can fit into the list and charge everything you see until its gone. The downside is that the Chieftain is the most expensive character in the faction that isn’t a Monster and you really need to lean into his kit.

Since release, the Thunder Chieftain has been a competitive staple for W'adrhŭn for some time now. He'll almost always be your Warlord because Shock synergises so well with what his units (Thunder Riders, Raptor Riders etc.) want to do, and you'll want to maximise how much return on investment you get. A full Speakers list is an incredibly fast army that hits well above its weight class. It takes practice to be able to use effectively, but there's a good reason it's been the most popular competitive archetype for W'adrhŭn for years now.

Example Builds

  • Thunder Chieftain with Resilience and Thunder Riders. The most popular build and for good reason. Hardened (1) on both your Thunder Riders (because lets face it, you are taking two units) is a significant upgrade to their durability at low cost. You can take Horn of Ronan to turn this unit into a complete Deathstar by doubling the Trample output, but this is a lot of eggs in one basket.
  • Thunder Chieftain with Raptor Riders - Whilst this may seem odd, running the Chieftain in Raptor Riders allows you to apply his Supremacy from Round 1 and lets you make aggressive plays in Turn 2 or 3 with other Light Speakers units benefiting from Shock. Like the Brood of Omgoroh Queen, the Chieftain also grants impact attacks to the Raptor Riders to let them one-shot objectives. Just assume the triceratops can run really quick to keep up with the raptors!

Predator

Predator by camel_wrangler

The first of four different units with ‘Predator’ in their name, the Predator is a ranged specialist with a focus on Light units. Though 6 attacks in melee is decent, his Barrage (4), Armour Piercing (1), Deadly Shot Volley attacks is what he wants to be using. In either case Fiend Hunter makes him significantly better into Brutes and Monsters than Infantry and Cavalry. He grants his unit Fire and Advance, though as this is restricted to only moving forwards this is better at keeping a unit moving down a flank rather than evading a threat.

His Supremacy has been through several underwhelming impressions. The latest of these, The Big Game Hunt represents the army hunting down targets of opportunity. In essence, you select an Enemy Regiment to be your ‘Prey’ at the end of the first Supremacy Phase and for the rest of the game your army will always benefit from Inspired or Take Aim special rules. When the Prey is destroyed you immediately choose another to be the new Prey. Whilst it's rare that free Inspired will be particularly useful, units such as Hunters and Slingers will get significant value out of Take Aim. You’ll need to be careful with your initial Prey choice for a unit your opponent has to commit forward and that you can destroy relatively easily, but you should be able to move it to your targets easier once the game is underway.

The Predator is more specialist than the previous characters discussed, with a warband focused on ranged units and a few Speakers options. His access to Mainstay Raptor Riders and the somewhat limited Slingers can be useful if the units you are after a blend of Speakers and Tribes units with early game presence. Sadly he's probably the weakest option for Warlord, but can be a utility choice for a second or third Warband especially if you want to have him boost the damage of Slingers or an Apex Predator.

Example Builds

  • Predator with Hide of the Silent Hunter in Slingers - A classic combination, the Slingers Singing Bullets complements the Predator’s quality firepower well whilst his own shots are more dangerous. The Artefact for Opportunist isn’t something you’ll get value from easily but when it does trigger this unit becomes horrific.
  • Predator with Death’s Reach on Apex Predator - Another iconic combination, this is less of a glass cannon as much as an artillery piece made out of tin foil. With a staggering 17 high quality attacks (plus 4 for the free Volley action granted by the Apex Mastery), this model hits hard into most things but is one of the best Monster hunters in the game thanks to both character and monster having Fiend Hunter.

Mounted Predator

Mounted Predator - Para Bellum

A much maligned character has finally been given the love that she deserves rather than being the exact same as the foot Predator with ‘Cavalry’ type. The Mounted Predator is a ‘super Raptor Rider’ that significantly buffs her only Regiment she can join, and does a pretty good job of it. Her own ranged and melee output matches the Raptor Leader model exactly, but with the added bonus that she grants her whole unit Deadly Shots, turning their chip damage ranged attacks into legitimately respectable short-ranged firepower when combined with their melee output.

Her ability Expert Hunters grants all Speakers units in her Warband effective immunity to terrain effects (except water). Not a bad ability when playing into other units that rely on Impact attacks as you can hide in the trees, but unless your local meta is themed around the Forests of Fangorn you’re probably not going to be using it much. Her Supremacy is the same as the regular Predator with the same strengths and weaknesses. It’s worth noting that as Raptors get Take Aim when they chant she gets a lot less value herself from this ability.

A Mounted Predator naturally compares to the foot characters using the Brood of Omgoroh artefact. If you want to boost the offense of Raptor Riders, the Mounted Predator gives some powerful artefact combos like Death's Gaze that are normally lost to Brood being required. You do have to weigh her up against the Thunder Chieftain for offensive power, or an infantry character with Brood of Omgoroh for utility like a Matriarch Queen. As with the foot Predator, her Supremacy is too niche for the Warlord option especially in a relatively fragile unit like Raptors. Take her with a Thunder Chieftain Warlord and live the Raptor Deathstar dream instead.

Example Build

  • Mounted Predator with Death’s Reach and Cure in Raptor Riders - The Predator exclusive artefact is fantastic for the Mounted Predator giving her some real teeth in melee for a bargain price. Cure is meta dependent but Raptor Riders really suffer from Lethal Demise and Aura of Death, and this cheap artefact can let them go after some annoying Light and Medium units that try to trade up with these passive damage rules.

Winglord Predator

Winglord Predator - Para Bellum

Unlike ridden monsters with other characters, the Winglord is a single unit that behaves more like a Monster with some character interactions than a character itself. Whilst it can’t take any artefacts, the base statline is very respectable with more Wounds than an Apex Predator and native E2 to give some surprisingly staying power. Move 9 makes it one of the fastest units in the game and as a Medium regiment it can score some centerline zones if Raptor Riders provide a Reinforcement Line.

At first glance, 11 Cleave (1) attacks is underwhelming for a Monster of this cost. When combined with 6 Impact attacks (with Brutal (2) if you get into the flank) and 7 free Armour Piercing (1) volley attacks (when you chant to trigger Mounted Hunters) the Winglord has a much higher damage output than you’d expect. The Winglord also brings the Predatory Shriek chant rider, granting Terrifying (+1) to all Speakers Monsters in the list. When you combine this with Chanting, and Shock from the Thunder Chieftain you have one of the fastest and hardest hitting units in the Faction. The only lacklustre aspect of the Winglord is that it still has the same Predator Supremacy that simply doesn’t synergise well with the Speakers style list that it promotes.

Winglord Predators are arguably the best unit in the Faction and well worth their points cost even after multiple nerfs. They are excellent at scoring objectives with their speed and ability to one-shot objectives with multiple attacks. If they can use their Volley and Impact attacks they hit very hard into almost any target, and as the only Monster with native Evasion in the faction they’ll often take multiple activations to kill. The icing of the cake is that a Winglord is like a ‘free’ Warband slot as they are their own Regiment and bring other great Speakers units along too. Almost any list will be happy to bring a Winglord for raw damage and utility options. A Speakers-focused list will usually take two!

Scion of Conquest

Scion of Conquest - General Cross

The first of the Cult characters, the Scion of Conquest is our spellcaster character with a strong focus on the Chanting mechanic. Unlike most Wizards, the Scion of Conquest justifies his three weapons on his model (seriously what wizard needs a scythe, a broadsword and a flaming brazier all at the same time?) with 5 Cleave 2 attacks giving him surprisingly good offensive output. Like all the Scions, he must always take the Cult of Conquest (though doesn’t have to be the Warlord fortunately) for himself and his warband.

His spells are a mixed bag cast at a reasonably reliable Priest (6). The stand out spell is Discordance that removes the ability for an enemy to score for the Round. In the ‘solid’ category, Cacophony is a passable offensive spell for on average 4 hits whilst Crescendo adds a Conquest token and allows you to rearrange your top three Command Cards for some utility choices. The remaining two spells are strong but need specific sequencing to work. Resonance applies the powerful Decay effect to the enemy, but it does require your opponent to be in an objective zone and not have activated yet in order to meet the trigger timing. Dissonance acts similar to Dread by preventing an enemy from gaining Inspired benefits, but it requires your unit to Chant whilst already engaged with that unit. In both cases, these effects are good but have probably one too many conditions to be reliably useful in games.

His Supremacy, Vision of Conquest has three effects together. The first simply makes all chant tokens you generate Conquest cult, meaning you will always get your pick of the four Cult benefits whenever you chant. Secondly, when chanting Conquest cult your unit also counts as benefiting from the Dissonance spell, which whilst still being a little situational does let you stack the effective Dread from chanting Famine with the Evasion bonus of Conquest. Finally, once per game you may choose to disregard your Command Stack and activate your units in any order you like. This can be an incredible ability especially when you are looking at Supremacy Chicken (see this article for more! https://www.goonhammer.com/the-command-stack-and-psychology-in-conquest-last-argument-of-kings/) as it lets you react to your opponents actions however you see fit. Timing is everything here though, and you may consider wanting to set up the supremacy turn by rushing your units forward to ‘complicate’ the board. By forcing lots of difficult choices on your opponent’s activations, you can capitalise on the confusion created with the complete freedom to act how you want.

The Scion of Conquest is our jack of all trades character. His spells, personal output and supremacy ability all sit in the 'effective but not outstanding' space so you'll rarely feel bad with him but also won't miss him much if you're optimising a list. That being said, the Scion of Conquest is a very dynamic character with lots of options available each activation. He's also perfect for learning the faction with as a Warlord, giving you complete flexibility for both chanting and activations to explore ideas.

Example Builds

  • Scion of Conquest with Roar of the Awe’khwge in Chosen of Conquest - Similar to the Chieftain build, this gives you a fast and (conditionally) durable elite infantry unit. In this case you trade the offensive power of the Chieftain for the utility of the Scion’s spells and the options created by Blessed from the artefact. Just be wary, the resilience of this unit is dramatically different if it gets attacked before Chanting for E3 and Blessed or after.
  • Scion of Conquest with Kiss of the Dilosaur on Drum Beast - The mounted option, a Scion on Drum Beast gains a very durable Regiment to deliver his spells and effects from a central position whilst bringing its own layers of army support. Most notably, the Drum Beast extends the spell range of the Scion’s spells by 4” which opens up the possibilities of the more niche spells in his list. The artefact also helps the Scion with augmenting the offensive output of the Drum Beast, its known weakness.

Scion of Death

Scion of Death - Para Bellum

The Scion of Death is a melee powerhouse that epitomises the glass cannon nature of W'adrhŭn, with her own high damage output and her warband choices of hard hitting but fragile units. Her own output is amongst the best in faction with 7 attacks, Flurry and Flawless Strikes setting an impressive baseline before Chant bonuses. With her excellent Evasion 3 and Resolve 4 she’s more than capable of holding her own in duels with most characters in the game too. For those wondering why she has the Priest rule with no spells, she brings an aura of Interference to disrupt enemy spellcasting, which whilst meta-dependent can be very effective. Her unique special rule Cultists of Death means she must always follow the Cult of Death, but that all infantry in her Warband increase their Evasion by 1 (capped at 3).

Her Supremacy Vision of Death leans into the infantry focus further, granting the command stand of infantry an extra three attacks when the unit chants Death. A simple but effective rule, this is most powerful on multiple small units of infantry like Blooded and Chosen of Death that hit well above their weight class now. There’s probably one too many restrictions on this ability to make it great, as allowing units to chant other cults or expanding the range to include Cavalry and Brutes would make it significantly more appealing. You’re probably better off with the broader utility of the Chieftain or Matriarch Supremacy for supporting your infantry instead.

As a side note, the Scion of Death is oddly not very efficient with her own Chosen of Death. They can’t get the Evade boost from her Warband ability as they are already at the cap, and she already has the Flawless Strikes that they grant from their Chant effect. When compared to the Matriarch Queen, the Scion of Death seems more at odds with the Chosen of Death than synergising with them.

Due to this, the Scion of Death is less likely to be a Warlord choice, but can be an effective second or third Warband group, sitting at the same cost as a Matriarch Queen. In this case you trade off the defensive buffs and warband flexibility for an absolute powerhouse of a character to turn one of your units into a seriously dangerous threat.

Example Builds

  • Scion of Death with Beads of Death in Blooded - The Scion primarily exists to raise the damage status of the unit she is with, and Blooded appreciate the boost. They’re cheap enough to bulk out into 5 Stands and appreciate the bonus to their Evade characteristic and Resolve. Beads of Death stacks with the Blooded’s +2” to Charges when chanting, giving this unit an excellent threat range and will stick around to keep the Scion fighting round after round.
  • Scion of Death with Kiss of the Dilosaur in Chosen of Death - Whilst caveating this with the above comments about efficiency, this unit will demolish whatever it touches. Kiss of the Dilosaur stacks incredibly well with the Scion’s damage output and whatever she can’t deal with, the Chosen of Death certainly will.

Scion of War

Scion of War - Para Bellum

The latest of the Scion characters, the Scion of War competes closely with the Scion of Death for the strongest offensive character in W'adrhŭn. The Scion of War trades Flurry, Flawless Strikes and attack for Impact (3) and 6 attacks with, Cleave (2), Linebreaker and Relentless Blows, setting him up as the armour cracker of the two characters. He is also one of the most durable characters in the game for dueling with an astonishing Defence 4 and 7 Wounds, letting you safely combat anything short of a Fallen Divinity. Similar to the other Scions, he must follow the Cult of War and is Priest (1) for some Interference presence.

His Supremacy is a rarity in W'adrhŭn by being defensive in nature rather than offensive. Vision of War grants Tenacious (1) to any Regiment that chants the War cult, and once per game you may grant Hardened (1) to all regiments in your army. This is a great supremacy and lets you build into a very different style of W'adrhŭn should you so wish. Tenacious is one of the best rules in the game and often means your opponent needs to commit an extra activation to kill something they would otherwise have done so. The W'adrhŭn Monsters particularly enjoy this as surviving on a single wound can be devastating if you get another activation from the unit. The downside of this Supremacy is that it has the 'Bastion' problem - it only works once you’ve activated the unit. This means you you need to have acted ahead of the opponent’s attacking units, and that you may have had to commit the unit before you wanted to in the Round.

The Scion of War is an expensive character at 130 points, but you do get a lot for your money. You'll generally bring him for Mainstay Warbred options in a Tribes-focused list if you want more offensive output than the defensive utility of a Matriarch Queen. He's also a compelling Warlord option, especially with units like Winglords, Quatl and Chosen of War, which offers some interesting ideas of playing W'adrhŭn with more durable profiles. The Scion of Conquest can be a great way to change up your experience of the faction if you want something different from W'adrhŭn's traditional playstyle.

Example Builds

  • Scion of War with Kiss of the Dilosaur in Chosen of War - Much like the Chosen of Death version, pairing these two units will be devastating for whatever they tough. The War version here specialises in armoured targets that lack evasion, and combining the Terrifying (2) from the Chosen with the forced rerolls of Kiss of the Dilosaur makes the Scion exceptionally powerful with, whilst bunkered in the most durable unit in the faction.
  • Scion of War with Beads of Death in Warbred - Whilst significantly more fragile than the Chosen of War, Warbred are much more reliable at delivering the heavyweight Scion into combat. The ability for Warbred to guarantee a 12” threat range with Juggernaut and Beads of Death when chanting is a fantastic delivery mechanism. Warbred also lack the Cultists of War rule, meaning you can potentially chant other cults if you want the Scion to use Death, Famine or Conquest effects instead.
That wraps up our review of the W'adrhŭn Characters. Let us know what you think and look forward to the next article where we'll discuss the Regiments available to the faction, as well as some sample lists!
