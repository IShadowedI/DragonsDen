Turn Order TTRPG Overview

June 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello and welcome back to Turn Order. This article is intended to serve the dual purposes of providing an overview of past "TRPG Essentials" articles, to help readers better navigate and understand what each of the first nine have to offer, as well as serving as a bridge between past coverage and the next wave of articles in what we'll be calling the "TTRPG Advanced" series.  As articles are released, this overview will be updated with links and summaries.

Each article in both series is intended to better immerse you in the tabletop roleplaying game hobby, but whilst the Essentials series was intended as an introduction, the intent of the Advanced series is to offer a more nuanced view into the hobby, including advanced skills and tools to bring to bear at your home table, a view into the various subcultures and artforms that have emerged within the hobby, and an exploration of the sheer breadth of games available to you and your table.

Essentials

Want to know where to get started with the ttrpg hobby? Look no further than the proceeding articles, each of which is written to welcome you into the immersive roleplaying tradition. Each is written with no expectation of a pre-established base of knowledge (beyond concepts introduced in those preceding them), and works to introduce concepts, skills, and knowledge to the reader.

There might also be a meta-narrative about a wizard and a homunculus they created, but really, how could you know for certain without reading every single article to check?

There are eight articles in this series, each of which will be linked and summarized below.

Nothin' to be afraid of, Billy, the old wizard is long gone. Yeah, I know you can still see movement in there but that's why you gotta stop lookin' in those windows!

Introduction to Tabletop Roleplaying Games

This article is intended to introduce you to some of the more basic concepts surrounding ttrpgs, including some simple terminology and definitions to get you up to speed. It’s by no means exhaustive, but it is intended as an invitation to further explore this wonderful hobby. If you have a friend that’s interested, but doesn’t know a DM from a d20, this is for them.

Building Your table

When looking towards exploring this hobby, your options are cleanly delineated between either building your own table or seeking out membership at a pre-existing one, and both have their strengths and weaknesses. This article lays out methods for exploring both, as well as the importance of establishing a safe space for play.

Your First System

Selecting your first TTRPG can feel a bit overwhelming. This article is intended to ease this process by options that run a gamut of gameplay styles, themes, and accessibility. Nowhere near exhaustive, this is intended to open the reader's eyes to some good entry points. Introducing systems like the ubiquitous Dungeons and Dragons, Powered by the Apocalypse, and FIST: Ultra Edition.

Tools & Tricks (DM & Player)

These two articles are focused upon introducing new Games Masters & Players to some of the many skills and tools that can aid the Tabletop Roleplaying experience, with the intent of focusing on a breadth of skills versus any particular depth. Covering topics like organization and scheduling, boxed or module play, community resources, and the most important tool of all; communication.

Theatre of the Mind vs Traditional Gameplay

In this article, we cover the basics of two of the most common forms of gameplay in the ttrpg world; Theatre of the Mind & Traditional or Granular gameplay. As with any of those that preceded it, the aim herein is not to champion one option over the other, but to highlight the strengths and weaknesses of both options to better aid you and your table in identifying what serves your needs best.

One Shot and Campaign Play

Unsure of how long you want your first ttrpg experience to be? This article has you covered, by discussing the strengths and weaknesses of both One-Shot and Campaign Play, whilst pointing directly to systems that best embody the highs of either option. Using the lenses of character pathos, lethality, spectacle, and ease, the emphasis here is to help you and your table know your options.

Wrapping Up Your First Campaign

An article on aftercare! Specifically, the sort of aftercare that a table full of ttrpg players and their GM can need after spending days, weeks, or months exploring, building, and perhaps even ending a shared world or story. The intent is to ensure that your first campaign is not your last, and that every campaign to follow is even better than those that preceded it. Though nearly every entry before this one has emphasized the importance of communication, this is the article most focused upon its application and essential nature.

Picking Your Second System

What is currently the final article in the Essentials series covers the joyful task of selecting your second ttrpg utilizing the learned experiences (both good and bad) of your first. It also happens to cover the widest breadth of ttrpgs highlighted thus far across this series, using the framework of those experiences or themes your table might be most interested in exploring. Whether your table is seeking their next experience to highlight themes or narratives with an emphasis on Exploration, Relationships, Investigation, Modular Action,  or a more Visceral experience, this Essentials article presents at least two ttrpgs to investigate for each! In addition, we also introduce the ubiquity of the One-Page ttrpg, as well as supplementary reading to further develop your relationship to the hobby.

Let's see, we got uhhh skeleton war, a bunch of golden crusaders, statuary coming to life, and it looks like the heavens themselves are about to open up? Ah yeah, yeah you're right we must have taken a turn into OSR territory. Just uh, back it on up and hopefully they let us leave this hex without any issue. No eye contact, you hear me?

Advanced

You've read our past articles, you've run your own ttrpg campaign, you've gotten immersed in this wonderful hobby and you want more! Sick. The ADVANCED series is for you. Each article is intended to provide a window into the weird and wild extremes open to you in this hobby, in the hopes that you only get weirder and wilder with your own experimentation to match. In short, TTRPG ADVANCED is written not to welcome you in, but to encourage further development of your skills, interest, and investment within this wonderful hobby.

The first few articles in this series will explore the weird and wonderful world of one-page ttrpgs, the best places to find your favorite new ttrpg, and how and why you as a game master are closer to a game DESIGNER than you might expect, as well as how you can leverage that understanding into running a campaign like no other.

Other great TTRPG Coverage on TTB

Vrekais is a New DM

Generalized or even Specialized advice for new Game Masters might be great, but the chance to follow along on a brand new DM’s journey into the hobby and the role? Unmatched. This sequence of articles written by the titular Vrekais is truly a great read from start to finish, and it’s advised that anyone that wants more anecdotal and/or experiential writing about the hobby should check these out.

Turn Order’s Favorite “Specific” TTRPGS

A collaborative article with contributions from six separate writers for TTB, this article offers a peek behind the curtain at some of our contributors favorite TTRPGS that double down on specificity. Whether such specificity be Mechanical, Thematic, Somatic, or Experiential. Take a look, and you might just find your next great system you’ve never heard of.

Turn Order’s Guide to Making a TTRPG Character as Cool as the Baldur’s Gate 3 Party

Want to focus on the less mechanical aspects of character creation? This article by Luke Taylor brings the reader through a multitude of aspects to consider and tools to approach them with. As this is a medium of collaborative storytelling, this article focuses upon one of the very same pillars that the Essentials series has worked to emphasize: Communication.

A Player’s Review of Daggerheart

This article by Jefferson Powers covers one of the more famous and recent D&D “Heartbreakers”; Daggerheart, from the boots on the ground perspective of a player. Focusing on details like Mechanical differentials between it and D&D, the physical objects that enhance gameplay, and the system’s focus upon teamwork, this article works to give the reader a personalized view into Daggerheart and what makes it tick.

What Else?

Alright, so you've seen what we've done so far, and you've read what we're intending to bring your way soon. Guess what? That doesn't have to be it. You, the reader, can influence the topics to come. All you have to do is leave a comment, telling us what you would like us to explore. Maybe you want coverage on a specific niche ttrpg, an interview with a specific game designer or dev, maybe you want us to dig deeper into ways to enhance the play experience at your table, or maybe you've got something weirder for us to talk about. Regardless of what your hopes and dreams for this series might be, the only way it happens is if you drop a comment and share your thoughts.
