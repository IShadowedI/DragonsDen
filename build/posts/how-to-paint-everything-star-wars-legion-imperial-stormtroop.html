How to Paint Everything: Star Wars Legion - Imperial Stormtroopers and Scout Troopers

November 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Greetings Legionnaires! In today's installment of How to Paint Everything, we are going to be taking a look at how to paint the iconic White Armor that is a staple of Imperial armies across the galaxy. Whether your preferred unit is the Imperial Stormtrooper, Scout Trooper, or Snow Trooper, this article will have you covered.

A huge thank you to Atomic Mass Games for providing the models!

To begin with, similarly to my Rebel Commandos and Rebel Troopers, I had to come up with something that I could paint relatively quickly because I knew I would have to replicate it across several units of models, and didn't want to spend too much time on my basic troopers. Without further ado, let's dive in!

Stormtroopers/ Scout Troopers

Normally, I would say the key to painting White is to not actually paint White. However, in this case, I wanted very few steps, and I wanted high contrast, so I did use white in the recipe.

  1. Like most “white armored” Star Wars troops, I started them with an all-over airbrush of Pro Acryl Bright Warm Grey as a base to build the white upon. I really like this bright shade of grey as a base for any paint job where I am going to have to highlight up to white, or give the illusion of highlighting up to white. It also has really good coverage over my black primer coat.
  2. Next, I gave them a focused highlight of Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White. This is, by far, my favorite white. Usually, the pigments are too big in white paint to work through an airbrush, but somehow Monument has figured out how to make it work. It also has great coverage and is extremely bright, so it can give that great contrast on the armor in just two different colors.  
  3. I painted all of the connecting materials and blaster with Pro Acryl Coal Black. This feels like a pure black paint, but it has a little more depth to it than other black paint I have used before. At this point, they really started to look good enough to put on a table. I did a few selected highlights with Pro Acryl Brown Grey on the connecting material, so it didn’t look like primer. I have used this paint as a base for all of my comic-style non-metallic metals for my Marvel Crisis Protocol minis, so I felt really comfortable using this as a highlight color. It dries much darker than it looks when you apply it, so the highlight is a little bit more subtle than it may initially appear to be when you are putting it on. I absolutely love the little hint of brown in this color, too! It adds so much to the finished paint job! I also chose one color to paint onto the models to help with squad identification. You can really choose whatever color you want for this stage; just keep it consistent for the entire squad.  
  4. At this point, the bulk of the work was done, and it was time to apply a coat of gloss varnish to lock in my progress up until this point. Once I had given the varnish plenty of time to dry, I mixed up odorless spirits and black oil paint to get my usual black oil wash.  If you've never tried this technique before, I highly recommend it.  Because the vast majority of the model was white armor, I really needed this oil wash to do a lot of the heavy lifting when it came to shading the recesses and bringing out the details. It can also give the models a little bit more of a "they've actually been in the field" look than traditional shade washes can (at least in my experience). Then I finished off with a stippled line of Scale75 Black Metal on the blasters so they had a little metallic sheen.
  5. The finishing touches for these models are the same as all of my Legion miniatures. I seal the model with a matte varnish through my airbrush after I paint the black rim around my base. After that, I use Beach Sand, Concrete, and Pacific Blue Water Gel from AK Interactive to build my Scarif-themed bases. After they have had time to dry, I will stipple on some Bold Titanium White across the water to simulate the foam, but I am very tempted to try AK's Water Foam for a more realistic look on my bases. I'll have a tutorial on how to make those bases specifically in another article.
