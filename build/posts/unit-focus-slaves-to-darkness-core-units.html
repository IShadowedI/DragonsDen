Unit Focus: Slaves to Darkness - Core Units

November 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

In this article series we’re taking a look at the various unit options available to each faction, looking at what units they pair with, and talking about the combos available to those units as well as tips for running them solo. You can find the rest of our Start Competing Slaves to Darkness Articles here.

Slaves to Darkness have a pretty diverse set of unit options, but a lot compete for similar roles. A lot of unit choices will come down to what specifics make sense for your list and how you want to actually approach the two main goals of this army - scoring points and killing things. Heroes often provide the majority of support for the army, so a lot of the units have a pretty clear cut role. It’s also worth noting that outside of Darkoath and “screening” units, the army runs pretty expensive. It’s not uncommon to run a handful of powerful units and have some extra dorks just to help do jobs.

Before we get into this, it’s worth noting that I’m going to be assuming you have a base understanding on how each unit works, and won’t be going through their whole kit in detail; this will more be a focus on how to use each unit.

Chosen

We’re starting with the heavy hitters here. 3 wounds each, 3+ save makes them pretty durable, especially with a ward from a Sorcerer Lord. They’ve got a pretty stellar combat profile, and with a once per game fight twice/fight last ability, they’re going to hit whatever they get to very hard. The trickiest part of this unit is the movement - base movement of 5” means they aren’t exactly going to get to where you need them without help. Relying on Daemonic Speed, Soulscream Bridge, and Pledge to Slaanesh in a pinch means they can put themselves in a very scary place when they need to be there. Notably, they’re a premier carrier for a Ensorcelled Banner - The Dread Banner is typically the go-to here, but there can be use cases made for the other aggressive options.

This big hammer costs a quarter of your army to pack, so let’s talk a bit of tech to make sure you use them to the best of their ability:

  • These guys are very vulnerable to getting screened out - this is a general issue S2D has as a whole, as they don’t have many ways to clear screens outside of combat. There are times when you just need to send them to clear a screen and rely on getting two pile-in moves to get into a better spot for the second fight.
  • While they are a hammer, they aren’t a glass cannon with the right support. If you get them warded up, they can brawl really well and often want to tag a lot of the other army just to keep smacking things. Just be careful if you send them alone.
  • When marking them, always remember to cast your support spells first. If you fail Daemonic Speed but you need the charge reliability, Mark of Slaanesh is worth it. Better to make the charge you need and be a bit weaker than be a sitting duck.
  • Position your banners towards the center of your unit if you have an Ensorcelled banner. If your standards go away, you lose the buff, and that often is much more valuable than the extra control point when you toe an objective.

Overall, they’re one of the game’s top shelf options for true hammer units. When you need incredible violence delivered to a place on the board, they won’t let you down. It’s one of the most popular units in the faction for a reason.

Furies 

Furies are a fast screening and scoring unit. The ability to bounce out of combat makes them really annoying to clear for melee units, and a 12 inch move means they’re pretty swift. The downside is that they fold to any shooting or hero phase damage, as well as having really awful combat profiles. That’s not why you take them though. These are great for secondaries, and for the cost they’re a steal at the time of writing. While other units might perform better as a roadblock, these are definitely one of the best choices for tagging objectives and scoring battle tactics. Don’t forget you can Pledge them as well: Tzeentch on these guys late game gives some real scoring threat.

Chaos Legionaries

One of your options for very cheap foot screens. At the time of writing, these are the same price as Marauders, and I think the viability of them is mediocre. They don’t fight super well, but they do have a command disruption ability that goes off on a 4+. It’s inconsistent, but just real enough that people often can’t let them sit there. Right now, I often lean towards Marauders, but if you only are fielding one MSU as a small chaff unit, the utility from them is solid. They also gain a slight bonus with Eternus, which can be pretty clutch if you already pack him.

Slaves to Darkness Chaos Warrior. Credit: Loxi

Chaos Warriors 

A very efficient brawling package that gets better on objectives you control. While running 20 is a valid option for an anvil, I often prefer them as MSU that can punch up surprisingly well and operate well on their own. If you aren’t committing to an “iron wall” list of like 40 of these guys, they’re great to run around and hold flanks, since they will absolutely out-fight a lot of similarly classed scoring units. Note that a 20 block holds an Ensorcelled Banner really well too.

Chaos Warriors (Scourge of Aqshy) 

The new hotness, and my current favorite unit in the game. These guys get scaling buffs depending on how many fury levels you have, starting the game with a 6+ ward at level 1 and gaining Anti Charge (+1 Rend) and Strikes First as long as they didn't charge at levels 4 and 7 respectively. These guys with a Nurgle pledge are an insanely good brawling unit, and with Brand of Apoplexy, if you can get to 7 Fury they will clean house in any prolonged fight with a lot of things in the game. Right now, they often get picked in Despoilers to get the early extra Nurgle Pledge, so often you don’t see them with an Ensorcelled Banner. They function as a great anvil/brawling unit - even though they don’t have any recursion, they will absolutely brawl really well if you’re continuing to build Fury levels. As long as you have enough heroes to farm Places of Power, these will always be a fantastic choice.

Darkoath Marauders. Credit: SRM

Darkoath

Darkoath Marauders 

The cheap, bountiful infantry of the Darkoath. They get better rend after nailing a charge roll of 8+, which isn’t as unreasonable as you think as an army with 3D6 and 3D6-drop-1 readily accessible. Dealing mortals on 5+ rolls makes old Blades of Khorne players jealous, but more importantly makes them a great space filler and screening unit. They aren’t super fighty nor are they super durable, but with the rest of the Darkoath toolkit they’re going to be a great anchor unit to just walk forward and stand on objectives. You’ll often see them reinforced in lists that run Darkoath Hoard to bring them back, but MSU are great in many lists that have 80 points to spare and need a cheap screen.

Darkoath Savagers 

Now a reinforceable unit, these are the dedicated scalpel/skirmishing unit for Darkoath. They’re as squishy as Marauders until they finish their Oath, which involves starting a hero phase on an objective outside of your territory to grab a 5+ ward for the game. This doesn’t make them durable, but it makes them slightly thinner than paper. Where they really shine is in combat - Pledge to Khorne gives these a silly amount of attacks with crit mortals. You can’t use this like a true hammer, but they will throw a lot of dice for a very cheap unit. They’re staples now in Darkoath lists as the main melee damage dealers, but they also can have some utility in mixed lists as a little missile once you get them Pledged and positioned for combat.

Ogroid Theridons 

Chaos Ogor-type beasts that have a big chunk of wounds and hit pretty hard, especially against infantry. Once per game they get mad as hell and can bump up to damage 3, making them genuinely terrifying against infantry. They really shine with the +1 to hit chain fight from a Myrmidon, but they can be a decent scrapping unit aside from that. They have the damage to be a main hammer, but they’re pretty squishy outside of a pretty large amount of wounds with a 4+ armor save and a conditional 6+ ward from Nurgle pledge. Overall, I like them a lot as a secondary hammer, but I think they lack the durability and versatility to take the main hammer role. The good thing is that they’re not crazy expensive, and even part of their value is how good the Myrmidon is to pair them with.

Chaos Knights

The other core unit I never leave home without, Chaos Knights are a great flex roleplayer across a lot of lists. MSU, they are great for skirmishing, pinning, and flanking around to kill backfield characters on exposed board edges. Paired with a chaos lord, they make a great secondary hammer, especially when you get them Pledged to Khorne. As a reinforced unit, fast lists can use them to just delete things with a boatload of attacks. They pack a ton of wounds for a fast unit with a good save, so they often can take a hit and still do a decent chunk of damage on the charge. The catch is that they hit like a pool noodle off the charge, so be very careful not to get tagged.

Darkoath Fellriders 

Pretty much the complete opposite of Chaos Knights, Fellriders are squishy but fast cavalry that are harder to clear with shooting. They can fight OK with the right support, but they aren’t ever going to fill a true damage role. Their main roles are being a very fast and very annoying unit in Darkoath lists that can benefit from their recursion rules. They make a decent fast screen outside of darkoath lists if you have the points to spare over something like Furies, but most of the time they tend to show up in Darkoath Hordes to come back and snag an objective or battle tactic. They are one of the few shooting attacks in the army on a full size unit, which can be handy for secondary scoring.

Varanguard. Credit: SRM

Varanguard

The other main hammer unit in the army and one of the game's scariest fighting cavalry units, they share the “Fight Twice/Fight Last” package with Chosen and fill a similar role, so it’s pretty reasonable to compare them since the majority of lists won’t run both of them. Compared to Chosen they are faster, and due to this are a bit more self sufficient since they don’t need a delivery system. They have generally a bit higher damage on the charge, but a bit lower damage off the charge. Interestingly, the math works out that both units utilize Pledge to Khorne for a similar increase in damage. They also have the same wound density, which means they’re defensively the same for all intents and purposes. The main drawbacks are that Varanguard can’t take an Ensorcelled banner, and are going to run you ~80 extra points over chosen.

Overall, one isn’t necessarily objectively better than the other, a lot of it depends on what your list needs more. Varanguard don’t need as much support and have a more explosive go-turn if you can get a lot of value off both fights for them. Not being able to take a banner is typically a drawback, but if you want to use an Ensorcelled Banner elsewhere anyway, it’s not a huge deal. In general, more lists can use the brawling power of Chosen a bit better, but when you want to come in fast and swing for the fences, apocalypse ponies will never leave you wanting. It’s worth a last shout that these are also decent MSU, but require a bit of finesse to make work properly.

Chaos Spawn

This unit is bad. Only take this unit if you’re absolutely settled on a list and have 60 points to spare. It has a use case in being extremely cheap, but that is it.

Darkoath Wilderfiend

The Wilderfiend is one of the core things that makes Darkoath work as a subfaction. They even shine pretty well in mixed lists, providing a way to project damage that this faction lacks. They have a few different utility abilities that are juiced up by nearby models dying - most of the time they’ll be using the mortal bomb ability to throw 6D6 rolls at an enemy within 12”, with every 3+ being a mortal. They also can opt to heal themselves or attempt to shut off commands on an enemy unit instead - these are nice but they only come up in niche scenarios. In a dedicated Darkoath list you can easily power up a whole bunch of these and just laser things in every one of your hero phases. Just sit these behind your infantry blocks and watch them go crazy. Just be careful of them getting sniped by shooting and spells.

Raptoryx

These are a true screening unit, through and through. At the moment, they’re the same price as Furies, and they’re a bit of a rough comparison. What you gain over Furies here twofold; double the wounds, and significantly larger bases. The wounds don’t really matter because their save is abysmal, but the base sizes are handy to daisy chain for a super long screen. If your goal isn’t explicitly screening your cavalry advance, I’d look towards Furies, but for that use case, these aren’t a bad option. If these get cheaper I’d wager they’ll hit the board more.

Mindstealer Sphiranx

A supportive monster to hang out behind your infantry blocks and hand out strikes last while trying to avoid combat. This is a great tech piece but is pretty vulnerable to shooting. Not every list needs this, but it can be great for running out behind Chaos Warrior anvils and making a frontline of Chosen a lot sketchier to engage. 10” movement also means it’s pretty speedy, and it’s a bit more reliable of a combat trick than Bel’akor against many targets for a much lower investment. It’s a bit useless against really big monsters, but reliable Strikes Last is great for a combat army and saves you from relying on spellcasting to hand out the effect.

Slaughterbrute 

There’s a unique use case for running this alongside a Centurion Marshall and bonding them, since the Marshall can chain fight with this guy while it can benefit from the hit bonus since it will lose Companion while near its bonded hero. All that said, it’s still a bit expensive at the moment for what it is. It’s comparable in stats to a block of knights with a hero, and those often outperform it by quite a bit for only a slightly higher investment. If you aren’t using that synergy, I would avoid this model.

Slaves to Darkness Chaos Chariot. Credit: Loxi

Chaos Chariot 

The chariot is fast, cheap, and has a big base. It’s very handy for scoring tactics, screening your backfield and fast cavalry, and tagging side objectives. It’s overwhelmingly fine, but it’s the cheapest fast unit you can get. I often prefer Furies to this in most use cases, but the reality is that being more cost efficient does earn this a spot on the roster pretty frequently. It also is one of the few sources of charge phase damage in the army, which can come into play for battle tactics.

Final Thoughts

First, you might notice a few things missing: Fomoroid Crushers, Mutalith Vortex Beasts, and Gorebeast Chariots are not units I consider competitively viable at the time of writing. Even the Chaos Spawn is realistically an incredibly niche use case, but these warscrolls are all pretty underwhelming and quite inefficient.

Overall, S2D has a really solid roster to pick from. A lot of units share similar roles, but provide enough distinct differences that you can adapt to what your list needs, what battle tactics you want to focus on, and what your playstyle preferences are.
