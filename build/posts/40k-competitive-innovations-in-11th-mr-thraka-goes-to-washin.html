[40k] Competitive Innovations in 11th: Mr. Thraka Goes to Washington pt.3

July 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

It's part three, and today it's Chaos time. As sometimes happens, the sorting algorithm has grouped a bunch of triumphs for the dark gods into one part. Obviously, various Chaos toys did take some hits in the Balance Update, so who knows how things will look going forward, but for now the dark powers (and some other factions) are pleased.

Archetype

Veteran Defilers

Thoughts

More Chaos! Even more! This time, the Veterans of the Long War detachment, which is low key a serious sleeper at only 2DP, brings the Defilers to town. Hold onto your hats for this one, but Defilers with hit re-rolls and access to Ignore Cover on demand are pretty lit. After the updates, you almost certainly cut down to a single Defiler, but Veterans also has strong synergy with things like Obliterators, providing alternative sources of extreme violence. The list also gains the option of playing Recon instead of Take and Hold thanks to the change in the Murdertalon Raiders Disposition, providing more angles to re-tune. Cool seeing different detachments getting some work, well done to Victor.

Archetype

Dreadknights and Purifiers

Thoughts

Someone said Chaos too loud, and the Grey Knights are here to solve that problem via extreme violence. This is an incredibly unsubtle list, but obviously super deadly, and hard to pin down. That means the balance update is a mixed blessing for it - on the one hand, restoring the ability to reposition turn one is great for Grey Knights in general, but on the other hand this is extremely a Purge the Foe build, and Warpbane has swapped to Take and Hold. I could certainly imagine trying something like this as Brotherhood Strike, but it's not really set up to benefit from 1DP detachments at all. Will be interesting to see where this goes next, but for now well done to Zach for ensuring Chaos don't have things entirely their way.

Archetype

Boyz Horde

Thoughts

No real introdution needed here, since this is pretty similar to the winning build from Tacoma we covered on Wednesday. Many Boyz, much krumpin'. The balance update, obviously, takes aim at this build in particular, upping the cost of spamming Boyz, and taking away the 1DP add-on. I do wonder if it's still worth going Tide at that point, or if you just pick War Horde, which gives you the same Dispositions, but a bit more flexibility in the use of units. There's still plenty of fight in Da Boyz either way, and I don't expect them to go anywhere, especially as their closest competitor for top of the metagame (Thousand Sons) got hit hard as well. Congratulations Justin.

Thoughts

The Aeldari make a play for a top finish here, using one of the increasingly popular Corsair detachments to set up a nasty scoring build. Enough small units to keep the Priority Assets points flowing, but also some really nasty threats to punish foes who try to disrupt that.

The thing about Coterie that's especially notable in this matchup is the detachment rule, which is extremely good into the very MSU-centric Salamanders list. Sabotage necessarily creates a tug of war over objectives, and every time the Marines push the elf menace back, it'll cost them. They do at least also have lots of units to keep the PA points rolling, but also only really one unit that can properly tank the more vicious Aeldari threats (the super stack of Bladeguard).

That's not to say this will all go the Aeldari's way, because they're definitely out-gunned, and a few foes here are a particular problem for them. The good news is that the Marines are a little short of ways to reliably kill the Avatar, unless the Lancer gets lucky. The bad news is for everything else - Fire Prisms and Wraithlords are super vulnerable to even the MSUs of Eradicators, and it would be very easy for a single bad turn to cause a cascade failure on the Aeldari's side. Other stuff like the Sternguard and Assault Intercessors being in small units is also a pain - yes the Corsair Detachment rule is going to tax the Marines for some models, but even three or four Assault Intercessors/Sternguard can do considerable damage if allowed to. I think the biggest help for the Aeldari is that the small  central objective in this mission makes parking the Bladeguard on it a much more dangerous prospect than it would be on some of the Take and Hold maps - they really will get hit by everything in that case. The maps also have good Infiltrate spots for the Troupe, while the Marines only have a combi-Lt with Infiltrate.

I can see a real argument as the Aeldari to play assuming they get the first turn, aim to frontload scoring and taxing with the Detachment rule as much as possible, and trying to hold on to enough units to sustain that through to at least turn four. They had a good go at it too - the Marines took them down in the end, but only just.
