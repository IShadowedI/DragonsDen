Getting Started: Eldfall Chronicles

November 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back to another article about Eldfall Chronicles, the combination skirmish and dungeon crawl game from Freecompany. You might have seen some of our recent ongoing coverage here at Tabletop Battles and wondered what exactly it would take to try this game out. Good news, that's exactly what you're reading right now.

The first thing I'd suggest doing is figuring out how you want to play the game. I touched on some of the many ways to play Eldfall Chronicles earlier, but it mostly boils down to whether you want to play the skirmish wargame or the cooperative dungeon crawl. All of the models in the game can be used in either form of gameplay, but there definitely are some entry points that are smoother than others.

Eldfall Chronicles Imperial Banner. Credit: Blake Law

Also, as a reminder, Freecompany has all of the rules for everything available for free on their website. Whether you want the core rules for the skirmish wargame, dungeon crawl, or any other additional rules (like behavior cards, FAQs, competitive play quests, etc.) then you can find them on their Resources page. Additionally, there's an app called Guild Hall which is used for building forces in the skirmish game. Through this, you can view every model's rules, all the spells and upgrades in the game, and save your forces to be referenced later.

Enough website stuff. Let's start by looking at the dungeon crawl options because they're both very simple and quite timely!

Dungeon Crawling in Eldfall Chronicles

If you want to play one of the dedicated dungeon crawl experiences, it's pretty easy to make the recommendations for what to look for. There are currently two core boxes, each of which contains everything you need to play.

There are a lot of moving parts in the dungeon crawl games. Beyond the normal mechanics of playing the game, there are various decks of cards that have to be curated between games, some form of time tracking to ensure your party isn't lollygagging during their delving, and various other miscellaneous things to track that aren't present in the skirmish game. However, the structure of both campaigns starts out with some very stripped down tutorial missions that gradually add on more mechanics. For Northern Wind, you work through five different quests that each build on each other before having the full suite of mechanics in play. We don't have as many details about Apocrypha yet, but I can confirm there are at least two of these tutorial style missions that help ease you into everything.

An early quest in Northern Wind. Credit: Jake Bennington

Northern Wind

First up, and the only option if you want something right now, is Northern Wind. If you want a deeper look inside the box, Tabletop Battles has your back because we have a review of Northern Wind that covers the gameplay, contents, and more. We've also been playing through the game and recording our experiences in a series of battle reports, so check those out for more Northern Wind thoughts.

At $217, it sits at the price point for premium board games or things like Warhammer 40,000 Battleforce Boxes. For this price, you'll get a packed box that includes 15 miniatures (4 heroes, 6 undead, 5 oni), 30+ dungeon tiles, and a boatload of cards and tokens plus the various rulebooks to play. This is more than enough for hours of replayable gameplay as you take different routes up the mountain, make different choices, and (potentially) include different members of your party.

All of the included models also have their relevant skirmish profile cards included, but the only fully playable force you'd have from this box would be the oni. The heroes themselves are members of different factions, so while you can't necessarily field an opposing force for the oni, you would have some models that you could add to any of the other packs that are out there.

Goblin Wartribe. Credit: keewa

There are various expansion packs available, but they only expand what's already a very hefty amount of game. They range from single model releases that replace the cardboard standees (Oni Pariah, Oni Yaksha, Nekomata & Komainu, Kogetsu, Amazon Gladiatrix, and Guildsworn Knight) to expansions that add entirely new encounters or hero options. The Goblin Wartribes expansion in particular feels like a nice value because it provides a new set of enemies for Northern Wind, a fully playable faction for the skirmish game, and also an alternate hero option with Chanra the Goblin Shaman. The Blighted Riftspawn expansion serves as a massive final boss to be the capstone of a Northern Wind playthrough, so you could wait until you're a handful of sessions into the game before snagging this terror.

Apocrypha

The next option is Apocrypha, the latest release for Eldfall Chronicles. It's so new that it's still in crowd funding status over at Gamefound for the next two weeks. If you want to see what Apocrypha is like, we had a review go up just a few days ago. The cheapest point of entry is the Core Pledge level that is around $100 USD – this nets you the entire game including four hero miniatures and a miniature for the final boss with all the rest of the enemies being represented by cardboard standees. The astute among you will have noticed this price point is quite a bit lower than Northern Wind's. It's partly due to a crowd funding discount but also because this version of the core box was intended to be more of a board game feel with fewer miniatures and more standees which keeps the price down. The stretch goals have also been getting blasted through, so by the time this article goes live there are probably at least a few extra goodies that will be included.

Delving through the first mission in Act I of Apocrypha. Credit: Jake Bennington

There are, of course, different levels that offer different amounts of rewards. The Deluxe Pledge replaces all of the standees with miniatures (13 models) for around $230 USD. The Collector Pledge is about $290 USD and adds on an expansion pack of particularly cool looking undead that, like the Goblin Wartribes, are also going to be a playable faction in the skirmish game. There are even higher tier options that include everything plus a bunch of fancy swag like acrylic tokens, card sleeves, storage options, and the like.

And if you're a complete sicko (positive connotation), you can do the Dungeon-Crawl All-in and get everything ever released for Northern Wind and Apocrypha at once. If you do, let me know because I want to be your friend.

Dungeon Crawl Summary

If you're interested in playing the dungeon crawl format for Eldfall Chronicles, the short version is to either pick up Northern Wind or Apocrypha. Northern Wind is available right now and has a ton of gameplay to keep you occupied but even the cheapest price point is enough to make some folks pause. I think it's fully justified given how much stuff is in there, but it's 2026 and all. Apocrypha has an entry point of less than half the price, but it won't be delivered until approximately Q3 2027. Both games have a variety of additional add-ons and goodies you can pick up to further enhance it if you find yourself enjoying the base game.

Skirmishing in Eldfall Chronicles

A battle upon the ruins of the Sand Kingdoms. Credit: PierreTheMime

The skirmish game currently has a few different entry points, but that is going to be changing at some point in the near future. Let's take a look at what exists right now.

Two-Player Starter Sets

There are currently two different versions of the Two-Player Starter Sets. One contains models for the Empire of Soga (think samurai, Kitsune, and overall martial competency) vs Helian League (synergistic "elves" with a tendency for fire and high quality armaments). The other set pits the Coalition of Thenion (individualistic "elves" who specialize in hitting hard and fast) against the Sand Kingdoms (a desert-dwelling faction composed entirely of magic users). Both of these boxes contain most of the other components you'll need to play a game – rulebook, unit cards, tokens, and dice. There's even a bonus water elemental in each of these boxes. The only thing you will need in addition to these boxes is some type of terrain. Grab some existing terrain or just use some glasses, the boxes the models came in, and whatever is lying around to create an impromptu battle to get a feel for things.

I absolutely love that Freecompany shows stuff like this in their Quick Start Rules because it's absolutely how a lot of people get into games. Not everyone has a closet full of terrain! Credit: Freecompany

One nice thing about these two sets is that there are corresponding Quick Start Rules (available on the Resources page linked above) for each of them. These documents are just nine short pages each, but they walk you through a simplified introduction to the skirmish game involving just two models per side and only a handful of the rules. It's enough to get a feel for the game and prepare you for the full thing afterwards. I like how, despite this being Baby's First Eldfall, it isn't just a straightforward death match like a lot of games have. There's still an objective that both players are fighting over.

Single Faction Starter

The faction pack bundles from the two-player starters are also available separately – if you only want the Empire of Soga models but not the Helian League, you can pick them up by themselves. However, the price point makes a lot more sense if you can just split with someone interested in the other faction because all the tokens, dice, rules, and water elemental wind up just being free.

However, there are some factions that aren't available in those two player sets. These include the Goblin Wartribes and Oni Clans, so if those are the factions who speak to you (and you don't pick up the Northern Wind box which includes the Oni), then this is an option. As if those aren't enough options, each of the four core factions (Soga, Helian, Thenion, Sand Kingdoms) have what's called a Reinforcement Pack. These are 3 models for the faction and each contains two brand new units along with one resculpt of an existing model. It's handy because each of these alternate sculpts happens to be a model that can be taken more than once (and from my understanding are popular to do so with), so you can get some variety in your army lists even if taking duplicate models.

Eldfall Chronicles Sunspear Reinforcements. Credit: Blake Law

Future Starter Options

I mentioned the two-player skirmish introduction point is going to be changing in the future. This was straight from a community milestone Q&A video that came out a month ago. Instead of having two separate starter boxes, they're going to have a single entry point for skirmish. This is to have a unifying new player entry point and likely also reduces SKU bloat which is a problem all games face when stores start stocking them on shelves. It will be different from the ones currently on offer and will feature two new sub-factions (no, we don't quite know what that means yet). The existing models will stick around in their own, single faction releases as mentioned above.

Skirmish Summary

If all of the factions are equally interesting to you, I think the general consensus is to go for the Empire of Soga vs Helian League box. Those two factions are a little more straightforward than the squishy glass cannons of Thenion or the "everyone is a mage so I hope you like looking at the spell list" of Sand Kingdom. The Oni also just have so much raw power that they can carry a newer player through some learning games based solely on their stats.

There's nothing stopping you from using the Quick Start guides with other factions if those are the ones that you favor. There's also a Game Night Guide which is intended for someone running demos of the game, but could easily be used by two players learning the game themselves.

Blue and Red Rasetsu. Credit: Justin Crawford

Welcome To Calad!

Eldfall Chronicles is a crunchy game with a lot going on at any one time, but there are baby steps you can do to get familiar with it without having to jump into the deep end straight away. It's also a pretty neat feeling to know that if you're learning the rules to play in one format, they're basically identical to what you'd do to play the other ways and all of your models can freely swap between dungeon crawl and skirmish. How do you plan on exploring the world of Calad?
