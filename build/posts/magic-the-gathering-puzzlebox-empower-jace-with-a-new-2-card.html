Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Empower Jace with a New 2-Card Combo

September 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The role of Planeswalkers in Magic have charted a course towards normalcy, even banality, in their 19 years of existence. First debuting in Lorwyn, this was a card type whispered about with excitement since their first teaser on Tarmogoyf, with each adding to the others’ mythos. Here was one of the most powerful Creatures printed in years, mentioning with blasé that yes, the most powerful characters in the game’s history would soon be playable in your deck, not just in the pages of the game’s novels. We take for granted how utterly cool this all was, yet as the years have gone by, the narrative of Planeswalkers as a game piece has been that of an albatross around the designers’ necks. Essentially all Universes Beyond sets decline to include them, as Magic is the only story to contain the concept at its most literal, and the most popular format is hostile to their play—with three opponents, and being locked to once per turn cycle, Commander isn’t a happy home for most Planeswalkers. Yet, in spite of this changing landscape, Magic’s second real Planeswalkers-matter set is dropping this fall, and in Reality Fracture, a new innovation is emerging: Planeswalker tokens. Heck, OG Tarmogoyf is even back in Standard! Does this crossroads of past and future fix the issue of the card type, or make it all the more mundane? Let’s talk about getting the most out of Empowering Jace.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Planeswalk the Walk

Fittingly, Empower Jace X is akin to Amass X, being a spin on a mechanic introduced originally in the first Planeswalkers-matter set, War of the Spark. If you lack a token Jace, first you create one: A non-Legendary Blue Planeswalker with no mana cost, and no means of innately increasing his own Loyalty. Then, however many Loyalty counters noted on the Empower are placed on a token Jace you control. Like with Amass, any means of doubling the number of tokens you create will still require you place the noted number of Loyalty counters on just one, meaning the other immediately dies, but in a similar vein, ways of duplicating the trigger or number of counters do indeed work to grow Jace’s loyalty to outrageous levels. While innately this means you just get a fancy Phyrexian Arena, there’s a host of cards which afford new abilities to Planeswalkers, and more arriving this set. Two early standouts are ">Ichormoon Gauntlet (whose price has already skyrocketed) and Kasmina, Enigma Sage...who is actually the better of the two, funny enough. If you resolve a Jace’s Machinations with her onboard, congratulations on the Instant-speed Enter the Infinite (or whatever big Blue spell, really).

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Pending additional spoilers, the other option at time of writing is Rewrite the Multiverse with 8+ Creatures onboard, which is a bit more expensive, but better on its own. Kasmina herself is fairly easy to find, and tutoring for a 3 mana Blue Instant is even easier, so this package is definitely in the cards for a competitive list. It’s a compelling 2-card combo, even if the addition of other colors makes finding pieces all that much simpler, on top of redundancy for other means of Empowering Jace 8+ in a single instance. If you’re willing to set up an engine, however, Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes can probably get a Jace with the necessary loyalty over the course of a few turns, and is already in the same colors as Kasmina. If you play Land ramp, going from a fetch into two drops in one turn with the Avatar already onboard isn’t out of the question. The early frontrunner for Kasmina lines is, in my eyes, The Eighth Doctor+Susan Foreman, with resilience via 8th, and Susan providing early mana and a body with which to do Birthing Pod/Eldritch Evolution lines via Academy Rector. Plus, with Susan being your ramp on 2, you can jam a comical number of 4-mana ramp spells which find 2 Lands, in turn helping to make Avatar a better means of hitting our critical 8 loyalty.

The Jace-stice League

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Going one by one, let’s start with my favorite sub-archetype in Commander: Scramble. Named after Reality Scramble, this set of ~3 cards (also including Chaotic Transformation & Reweave) turns one permanent into another random one of its shared type, directly from your Library. This is the first time we’ve had access to token Planeswalkers, though, so while Scramble has existed for Commanders like Jeska, Thrice Reborn & Dihada, Binder of Wills, never before have you been able to do it outside of the Command Zone. If you Empower Jace (available in all five colors, and Colorless), that gives you the capacity to flip it into another Planeswalker of your choice, with reduced room for countermagic. If you’re all-in on Empower Jace, this can just be Kasmina, but options like Ugin or the new Feroz are likewise tempting. Essentially, by playing solely 1-2 Planeswalkers in an Rx deck with Scrambles & Empower, you can hedge deckbuilding opportunity costs to access powerful soft tutoring. White meanwhile can just directly tutor Planeswalkers in a number of ways, including the brand-new Loyal Tutor, which means turbo Kasmina decks really want to be Bant, bare minimum. Black can do a bit of both of the above, Entomb-ing your Reality Scramble (they put Retrace on this card, can you believe that?) or directly tutoring relevant pieces, depending on your access to mana.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Hold on though, Empower Jace doesn’t say “that token”, it says merely to put Loyalty on a Jace token. Sure Kasmina’s a 2-card, but we can’t do that outside of a high Bracket 3 list—what nefarious lines might we use in a jankier Jace list? There are two Jaces which already make token copies of himself, but those are milquetoast, not worth funneling our Loyalty onto. Instead, we need to somehow copy him ourselves, ideally via our Commander; to that end, three candidates emerge, being Saheeli, Radiant Creator, Narset, Enlightened Exile, and in distant third, Nashi, Moon’s Legacy. Saheeli & Narset each have Red, meaning Scramble is on the table, but whether you prefer the mana efficiency of not needing to cast Jaces first with Narset or Saheeli’s UGx identity providing Kasmina is up to you. In either case, the two Jaces I’d look to turbo-tokenize are Jace, the Living Guildpact & Jace, Unraveler of Secrets. Each puts you insanely far ahead, and both have ults accessible via the magic number of 8 re:Jace’s Machinations. There are other Jaces you could consider, but for how invested you need to be to tokenize a Jace, it’s worth honing in on the best possible options. Empower Jace is a fantastic new mechanic to dive into, and between a powerful 2-card combo, Scramble synergies, and even enough jank to impress a Johnny like myself, there’s a lot to look forward to in Reality Fracture! I’m curious as to whether the Kasmina line actually makes it into fringe cEDH decks—Simic can always use smaller packages for wins, and without Black required it may end up as a worthwhile consideration for UGx, specifically Bant.
