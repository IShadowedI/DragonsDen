StarCraft TMG FAQs and Organized Play Roundtable

October 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With the official release of StarCraft TMG finally released and wave 2 up for hitting shelves at the time of this article, things are looking good for the Real Time Strategy game turned Tabletop Miniature Game. Archon has decided that now is the time to release the first FAQ and Organised Play Guide and as such we here at TTB decided it would be a good idea to do a roundtable discussion on what our thoughts are.

One thing to disclose, as team members of Tabletop Battles did assist in the writing of the Organised Play Guide: None of the contributors to this specific article had any input on the development of the guide.

Boom, baby! - Starcraft Terran Marauder by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

FAQ

Pox: There were a few things I am really excited the FAQ clearing up and the first of those things is the Measuring & Movement section. The question about models being placed on the other side of the enemy during a Charge or Close Ranks action was the exact question I asked when I played the first round of the Goonhammer Open. Between that response and the following one about Coherency Link's being based on what the Leading Model can legally traverse (the FAQ specifically calling out the Zerg Raptor, its just... Chef's Kiss), it makes me excited to try to run another mass Zergling army again.

At the same time, like any FAQ there seems to be a lot of very simple questions that were answered or seemed pretty clear in the rulebook. So those types of questions I will always get a good chuckle out of.

Overall I do think it was good and necessary and can't wait to utilize it in my future games. Which I can start planning soon, since I did manage to get all my Terran built this past week. Also who doesn't love some cheeky hints of upcoming rules for abilities that don't even exist yet.

AshC: I’m really pleased with the FAQ. Pretty much any question I’ve had or been asked during a game was addressed, as far as I could tell. I’ll echo what was said about Coherency Links. I also like how it does directly answer that yes, if you and your opponent both picked the same mission for draft, the one removing options can just force a specific mission.

Additionally, the Omega Worm being a Friendly Entry Edge, thereby allowing you to Burrow Ambush off of it is peak nonsense and I’m here for it. I’m personally not a big fan of how they decided to resolve Place (0) for Artanis, which is basically carve a special exception out for him that goes against how the ability normally works, but it’s probably much less trouble than stuffing more words on his card. 

StarCraft The Miniature Game Terran Marinesy by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Organised Play Guide

Pox: A document like this is always something that any sort of tabletop miniature game should plan on releasing early on in the games life cycle. People want to run events and people like to have some sort of standardized organization (notice the use of z here). The first thing I really noticed when reading this, was that Archon somewhat separated events by either a Community Event or a Competitive Event. I think this was a really nice touch since there are a lot of people who are getting into their first tabletop wargames via StarCraft TMG, and may be somewhat intimidated by the idea of running "serious" competitive events. So it was good to add a section that basically says that a community event is just the same as competitive, but in a more relaxed environment.

One of my favorite things of note within the Organised Play Guide is the Dual List Format. Having done this at a Dropfleet Commander tournament and at a couple Middle Earth Strategy Battle Game tournaments, I think the move to go Dual List is the right choice. This allows players to sort of tailor each of their lists to do separate things. Whether that be a swarm list and a more balanced list or something like a heavy Core list and a heavy Elite list. Either way I think it is a great choice. BUT it also states that a player may submit up to two Army Rosters which I think is another great addition, so that some players do not feel like they have to have two different builds.

AshC: I asked about this document back at UKGE Birmingham, and was told it’d be coming around October. It’s a bit sooner than that, but that works even better, especially since my FLGS is probably going to start running StarCraft soon. Having clear rules regarding every step is excellent guidance, especially when many people won’t have had experience running events beforehand. I concur that splitting Community and Competitive events is great. 

The Dual List format works on multiple levels. First off, you get more variety. You also get to feel a bit more of that RTS spirit, where you’d set your build depending on the opponent’s faction (unless it was Random, then you’d howl into the void at being matchmade into someone who could play all three factions at Diamond/Masters level). It’s also great that they explain what happens at Dice Down, because when it comes to a TO call you can always try to argue them down. If it’s in the official rule pack, the TO can just point to it and say that’s the game designer’s call to make.

Cody: I consider myself a competitive gamer so Organised Play is always important to me. The factors I look for in the health of a competitive game are Clarity, Intention, and Balance. For StarCraft, I have some gut reactions to what the packet represents.

For one, they seem to be leaving themselves room to adjust and while that’s not a bad thing inherently, it can lead to a lack of clarity and balance. Archon is trying to not step on toes with the direction of this packet by allowing it to be adjusted to fit your event. They even state that if you want the event to run like theirs, you should follow the packet which makes me wonder how many major conventions will ignore that advice and run it however they feel is best. It could lead to different competitive experiences across the board but I digress and understand this is a nit pick at this point. I’m just happy to have a packet!

For the event itself, the intention seems clear! They want the game to be easily accessible and fair for everyone. I love that painting isn’t a requirement and it only really works here because the models come in colored plastic making it rather easy to determine what is what. I do think there will be issues with figuring out upgrades and unique models such as Zealots vs Praetors if things aren’t painted. It certainly opens up the playing field though since some people just don’t have that hobby desire!

Archon has done a great job with clarity overall but including situational concepts for illegal lists, placements of objectives, and a detailed explanation on terrain layout. I was pleasantly surprised at the coverage of often overlooked situations. 

Timing rules are so incredibly important for the event scene. Nothing feels worse than being rushed all day and leaving with a hurt head and potentially hurt feelings. This packet is pushing for 2 hour 30 minute round timers but this includes the drafting time at the beginning which they estimate should take 15 minutes. That means the standard games are going to be long but if you are playing StarCraft, this shouldn’t be surprising. They have a rule on not starting a new round if only 15 minutes are left which I find interesting. I do think it’s smart but I can’t help but wonder how many people will follow that or simply not know they are under 15. I know if I’m behind, I’ll want to speedrun another round in those 15 minutes but will the head judge call me out? They also have a strict ruling that rounds don’t score if time is called before they finish. This is going to result in frustration amongst anyone who can call out their opponent for slow play. I’m not saying this rule is a bad rule, but it will need to be enforced properly and TOs and HJs will need to be strict on gameplay. No one wants to lose to slow play. Ever.

Tournament scoring is interesting and I highly recommend all players go over it. This game’s tournament scene will be brutal and I mean that in a “no pulled punches” kind of way. You are rewarded not just for winning, but winning with a vengeance. The higher your point differential is between your opponent, the more Tournament Points you get. Winning a game will give you Match Points which are the primary way to determine score but Strength of Schedule is surprisingly not the second determination, it is Tournament Points. I’m actually a little nervous about this because this ruling means a skilled player will likely not take it easy on a new player as you have to beat a threshold to get the maximum amount of Tournament Points. I would also be very careful as a TO to watch who concedes and when they do it. Concessions give the maximum amount of points which is kind of absurd considering they might have not gotten that if the game was close. This is especially true if the two players were going to stalemate (which is not necessarily a tie, it just means a very close game). There will be situations when a game would be a stalemate but one opponent might be out of the running and decide to just concede the game. These moments can potentially skew tournament results. 

Final Thoughts

Pox: I think that both the FAQ and Organised Play Guide are great resources and organized in such a way that I think they will benefit players games for the better. I will add that I really do love the Organised Play Guide and I can not wait to begin running events in my local area and using this as a starting point!

AshC: Loving both documents. I’m looking at an event next month maybe, and possibly running a community event of my own at an FLGS!
