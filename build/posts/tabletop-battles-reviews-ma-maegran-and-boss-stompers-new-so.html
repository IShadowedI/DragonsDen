Tabletop Battles Reviews: Ma Maegran and Boss-stompers, New Sons of Behemat Models

September 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer
A mandatory thank-you to Games Workshop for sending us the new Sons of Behemat kits for review purposes. That's what I'm doing here, I'm telling you about the new kits, you knew that, that's why you clicked on the article. 

The Sons of Behemat have long been an army made up of an old Warhammer Fantasy Battles Giant (excuse me, Gargant) kit that came out when I was a child and at the time thought was the absolute coolest set to ever exist despite being a dwarf player and a far more recent Mega-Gargant kit that outshone it in every way. There have technically been a couple iterations of the latter kit but that's not worth talking about. I've spent the last days building and painting the brand new Gargant kits, essentially a second wave of releases for the Sons (and now Daughters?) of Behemat. In short: these kits are good, and you probably already suspected that.

Boss-stompers and Rock-hurlers

The first of the two new sets I'm talking about and that's because, frankly, it's the more interesting kit between the two because this one has options in it when you're building them. This is a single box set not two different sets and uses the same body and arms. The sprues are laid out in a way that this was clearly designed to be a box of three always, the Gargants are not split evenly between three separate sprues but pieces are jumbled all over the place for reasons that likely make production sense. This means if you were hoping to split the box down just for one Gargant then you'll need to cut out all of the appropriate pieces to do so not just take one sprue out of the box.

The first few pages have you build these models identically to each other regardless if you're building the melee-focused Boss-stompers or the ranged Rock-hurlers; there are no options whatsoever up to the point of the photo below, which is why I took it at this stage. Two of the bodies are visibly male coded while the third is female coded. The only parts that are sort-of optional are the necklaces on the two male bodies, you'll notice that I left off the one from the model on the left, something that I only noticed now while writing this article (which is well after having painted the models). I'll pretend that was a deliberate choice when asked.

Rock Hurlers and Boss-stompers assembly; bodies must be built up to this stage like this with no other options so far.

After this stage you then choose between creating the Rock-hurlers or Boss-stompers and I've gone for the melee Boss-stompers, mostly because I think they look very cool and I like melee. Love to stomp on a boss. In either case at this point you do have some variation in the kit, there are six heads total with each body being very specific as to which of the two optional heads each will fit it; with a bit of greenstuff though the world will be your oyster I am sure if you really want to put a different head on a different body.

There is a little choice past this as well, the Boss-stompers can elect to build a Champion (which I've done) with the massive club with wheel attached at the end, or just another guy with two melee weapons. That's up to you and in-game it makes zero difference since the unit can't be reinforced anyways and you'll get a Champion's extra attack regardless.

Much to my disappointment there is no different feet or footwear options to be found. Other than that my only real complaint is that some of the seams are a bit obvious, I fixed that over with a bit of extra plastic glue to melt the seams over which worked well but I'm not as picky about this as some.

Size Comparison

I didn't own a Mancrusher Gargant so a thanks to my friend Laura for lending me one to bring home for this photo, you now know what it was for Laura and I appreciate your lack of hesitation or questions when asked if I could borrow it! You'll get it back soon, probably; it doesn't match my colour scheme anyways.

Boss-stomper and Mancrusher Gargant size comparison

Ma Maegran and the new unit of three, regardless of how you build them, come with 90mm round bases and are not actually larger than the older Mancrusher model much to my surprise! The Games Workshop points dock claims that they should be mounted on 80mm bases but I'm choosing the believe the sealed box I received that came with 90mm rounds and not a likely typo on a PDF. The Mancrusher here looks a little taller and that's mostly the angle and that he's stood on a giant skull.

Ma Maegran and Ancient Gyrochs

Similar to the unit of three Gargants I talked about above this kit is split across a couple of sprues, clearly designed to only be sold/used together without any easy splits happening. That's then matched in how you take these units in game with Ma needing to take the unit of two Gyrochs who can't be taken by any other means and can't be reinforced either. This is a unique, named, hero which a generic wizard option in sight to build the kit like we've seen with other monstrous hero kits. That's a bit of a shame and a missed opportunity for me, since Ma is the only wizard in the army it would have been nice to have a generic alternative option, especially for you narrative gamers wanting a non-Mega-Gargant hero for Path to Glory. There's also no options whatsoever in the kit.

Ma Maegran and Ancient Ghyrochs, Sons of Behemat - credit Bair

The models themselves go together very easily, some of the nicer and simpler GW kits I've put together actually especially for modern kits, with minimal gap filling needed, less than with the Boss-stomper kit. The Gyrochs are just a few pieces and feel nice and sturdy, no complain on these as they're simply beasts and don't need to be any more than this!

Painting the Gargants

I've gone for a very simple scheme that started with the idea of frost giants and moved away from that slightly by adding some warmth in metallics and fabrics and not doing snow basing (because that's normally quite boring for me).

Paints used include:

  • Colourforge Wolfkin Grey spray (for the Gargants)
  • Colourforge Raven Black (for the Gyrochs)
  • Pro Acryl (PA) Faded Ultramarine
  • PA Mahogany
  • PA Dark Golden Brown
  • PA Drab Brown
  • PA Tan Flesh
  • PA Bone
  • PA Dark Flesh
  • PA Burnt Red
  • PA Camo Green
  • PA Steel
  • PA Bronze
  • PA Copper
  • PA Faded Purple
  • PA Dark Ivory
  • PA Dark Neutral Grey
  • PA Warm Grey
  • Citadel Corax White
  • Citadel Thunderhawk Blue
  • Citadel Targor Rageshade
  • Ammo by MIG Acrycl Dark Mud Ground
  • Ammo by MIG Ultra-Matt Lucky Varnish (brushed on)

Starting with the Gargants here it's a very simple process of spraying them in Wolfkin Grey and using a beauty blender (one of those egg shaped ones) to sponge on Faded Ultramarine all over, allowing natural shadows to form where it couldn't quite reach. Then blocking in all of the other colours, starting with the cloth in green, red, and browns as you like and then working your way from the inner most details to the outer most to create the least amount of clean-up work later on. That means if it's harder to reach you paint it first, any details that sit on the top-most part of the model are done last.

The faces and joints were done by mixing about 50:50 tan flesh with faded ultramarine to give more of a living look to the models, something I've tried for the first time here, and then shaded over with Targor Rageshade, a slightly purply brown shade. That shade is also selectively applied to various indents and fold creases of the Gargant skin like the naval, joint creases, over the knees and across fingers and toes after the nails were painted in bone. After all of the other details are blocked I gave everything that isn't skin an all-over shade of Rageshade to soak into the details.

Basing material is applied before varnish since this stuff dries shiny and needs it as well. I painted the bases brown first in case of any gaps, spread the texture paste all over and left it to dry overnight. A quick drybrush with drab brown and then the whole model and base gets a nice brushed-on coat of matt varnish.

Ancient Gyrochs were even easier than this! Sprayed in Raven Black before a drybrush with Thunderhawk Blue and a lighter drybrush with warm grey. For the rocky bits on their back to give a textural difference I stippled (with an old, small, drybrush) Bone, Dark Ivory, and a little bit of Corax White on so that it looked flecked and rocky instead of the same drybrushed look on their fur. Similar to the Gargants I mixed 50:50 Thunderhawk Blue and Dark Flesh for the faces, knees, and fleshy area above their knees. Once all of the ropes, metallics, and horns/teeth/hooves were done they get an all-over wash with rageshade before the same basing and matt varnish step above.

Then the fun part: tufts! I love tufts. These are all from Gamers Grass (not a sponsor, just really, really, good product) and using a mix of colours and lengths gives a very natural look. I wanted these to look like they've been roving in Ghyran, lots of untamed growth, and I think the tufts really brings the whole model together, like a good rug.

Boss-stompers, Sons of Behemat - credit Bair

Final Thoughts

I really do think these are great kits and that the lack of variation/options where there could have been more is helped by the fact that realistically very few people are going to buy more than one or two boxes of the new unit and only even just one Ma for gaming purposes. Is there an absolute sicko out there that's going to want to run Ma with five units of these new guys? Probably! If you're that sicko please let me know, I want to hear from you and want to know once you've done it for proof.

Being the same size as the existing Mancrusher that's also still in the army feels a bit odd and this feels like a stepping stone more than anything of losing that single model to the great Old World range in the sky at some point later on.

Now that I've painted these all I need is three Mega-Gargants (including Brodd, obviously) and I've got a full army. That's a lot of blue.
