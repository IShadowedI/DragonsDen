11th Edition Detachment Focus: Hexwarp Thrallband (Updated July 15, 2026)

July 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In our Detachment Focus series we talk about the game's Detachments, covering what’s in them, how they play, and how they’ll fit into the broader meta and your games. In this article we're covering the Hexwarp Trallband for the Thousand Sons.

The Thousand Sons have been riding high for almost all of tenth edition, bolstered by some incredible datasheets, a bonkers detachment, and a borderline busted army rule. Coming into Grotmas they’re hot off the heels of a series of points nerfs designed to balance them… to limited success. Grotmas gives them a new Detachment aimed at changing their play style to one more focused on board control, also to limited success.

Changelog:
  • 2026-07-14: Updated for 11th edition.
  • 2024-12-04: Initial publication

Detachment Cost: 2 DP

Force Disposition: Take and Hold

The Hexwarp Thrallband is all about your Psychic characters, giving you buffs to Psychic Attacks and powering you up based on your control of objectives in the middle of the table. To that end, the Detachment features a rule similar to the Chaos Daemons’ Shadow of Chaos, giving you re-rolls on 1s to wound normally with psychic attacks and +1 to wound instead if you control more than half of the objective markers in No Man’s Land. You'll also get bonuses to a number of your stratagems if you use them on units which are in your Flow of Magic.

This Detachment was a bit of a dud on its initial landing; it was forced to compete with the far superior (and outright busted) Cult of Magic Index Detachment in tenth edition. And it also just has some real stinker rules which don't make sense given what's on the army's datasheets. But in 11th edition it has new life, primarily because it is a 2 DP Detachment which can still give you some great Stratagems that the army needs while still giving you access to the Take and Hold Disposition and the ability to add the Sekhetar Cohort or Ritual of Regeneration Detachments for extra power.

Detachment Rule: Flow of Magic

Specific areas of the battlefield can be within your army’s Flow of Magic. Your deployment zone is always within your flow. At the start of any phase, if you control at least half of the objective markers in No Man’s Land, until the end of the phase, No Man’s Land is in your flow of magic. At the start of any phase, if you control at least half of the markers in your opponent’s zone, then until the end of that phase, your opponent’s zone is in your army’s Flow of Magic.

When you make a Psychic attack with a Thousand Sons model in your army, re-roll wound rolls of 1. If that model is wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic, add 1 to the wound roll instead.

At first glance, you’re likely wondering why you’d even want this rule - after all, your characters attached to Rubrics already have the ability to re-roll 1s to wound. And getting +1 to wound is neat but not as good as getting full re-rolls to wound, particularly when many of your good Psychic attacks have either ANTI-INFANTRY 4+ or ANTI-MONSTER/ANTI-VEHICLE 4+, making +1 to wound kind of pointless against them. There’s some marginal upside here for Magnus, who wants to be able to re-roll 1s to wound, but that’s pretty small and as soon as he’s within your Flow he loses any benefit from this Detachment. And note that the +1 to wound is instead of the re-roll 1s, not in addition to.

When this Detachment first released, we were not kind to this rule, and with good reason: It's utility is pretty marginal outside of Magnus and Daemon Princes and you'll usually want re-roll 1s to wound over +1 to wound unless you're taking on knights. But it does have some use. That said, your main value here is going to come out of the Stratagems.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Enhancements

The Enhancements here are okay (except for the Noctilith Mantle), but only okay. You're not likely to end up taking them in most games.

  • Arcane Might (20 points) – Add 1 to the Strength characteristic of Psychic weapons equipped by models in the bearer’s unit, or add 2 while the bearer’s unit is wholly within your Flow of Magic. This only really matters on your Daemon Prince, in part because his melee attacks are PSYCHIC, and this can get him to S10 - a powerful break point. It's not so much worth it on the other units, where most of the psychic attacks you're throwing out rely on ANTI-whatever. It could be cute on an Infernal Master, where you'd be tossing out S8 Fires of the Abyss.
  • Empowered Manifestation (20 points) – While the bearer’s unit is wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic add 6” to the Range of Psychic abilities the bearer has that specify a range (including Rituals), and each time the model takes a Hazardous test for a psychic weapon, you can re-roll the result. In terms of unit abilities, this is an extremely narrow upgrade - by my reckoning it only does anything on a Daemon Prince or a Terminator Sorcerer. On the latter it lets you get your re-rolls to hit at 24” instead of 18”, which is OK. On the former however, it lets you extend the range on the Daemon Prince’s Stealth aura to 12”, which used to be great but is a lot more trivial in 11th edition. Instead the value here is +6" on Rituals, which means 24" Doom Bolt and being able to launch things from the Sorcerer on Disc without being shot back.
  • Empyric Onslaught (25 points) – While the bearer’s unit is wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic, add 3 to the Attacks characteristic of Ranged Psychic attacks equipped by the bearer. This is a solid upgrade on a Psychic character but works particularly well on the Exalted Sorcerer, where moving to D6+3 attacks is a solid upgrade on a unit with a D3 damage Blast attack with Devastating Wounds. But the requirement to be within your army’s Flow of Magic means it’s not worth 25 points.
  • Noctilith Mantle (15 points) – While the bearer’s unit is on the battlefield, it is wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic. Models in this unit cannot be selected to use any Rituals. That’s a hell of a downside for a unit, and absolutely brutal in most cases, and you’ll need to do some careful tricks to make sure another unit can hit you with a ritual so you can still Temporal Surge or get a free Stratagem. And then you ask yourself if all this is worth +1 to wound on your psychic attacks, and you realize that no, no it isn’t.

These are some interesting Enhancements, but on the whole their uses are just a bit too narrow to make them "keepers."

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Stratagems

Here’s where the real power is in this Detachment. There are some very good Stratagems here, with some solid utility and defensive buffs. They’re all set at a cost of 1 CP, meaning you can get them free with Echoes of the Warp Cabbalistic Ritual.

  • Warding Hex (Epic Deed, 1 CP) – Used in your Command phase to sticky an objective in your army’s Flow of Magic that is within range of one of your PSYKER units. The hoop to jump through here is rough - most of the time it’ll mean holding two objectives midtable before you can sticky anything in No Man’s Land, but you can use this to sticky your home objective early and leave it behind on the few missions where that matters. This can be helpful if you're playing Take and Hold and know you're going to get killed off an objective.
  • Wrath of the Doomed (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in the Fight phase after an enemy unit picks targets. Choose one unit they picked as a target and until the end of the phase, every time a model in your unit dies, roll a D6 and add 1 if the unit is wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic. On a 4+, they can fight on death. This is cute but the reason you’re not charging into combat with your models is that they’re generally bad at it. This is best used on some Scarabs but most of the time you’re better off avoiding combat or dying since losing multiple Scarabs in melee is going to ruin your day and they tend to be disappointing when they fight.
  • Strands of Time (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in your Movement phase, after a unit Falls Back. They can still shoot or Declare a Charge this turn. If they’re wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic, they can do both after falling back. You’ll almost never want to charge back into combat after Falling Back with a Thousand Sons unit, so that second rider isn’t super important, but being able to Fall Back and shoot is a key ingredient missing from the Grand Coven and it’s super useful to have for a unit of flamer-toting Rubrics or Scarabs, neither of which want to be tied up in combat when they could be shooting things to death.
  • Through the Veil (Epic Deed, 1 CP) – Used at the start of the Reinforcements Step of your Movement phase. You can take one unit of Rubric Marines or Scarabs in reserves and Deep Strike them (the Rubrics gain Deep Strike). If you opted to use this on a unit of Scarabs they can be set up anywhere wholly within your army’s Flow of Magic but more than 6” from any enemy units. If you do this they can’t declare a charge this turn. This is big, and can really help you fit a ten-model unit of Scarabs into some tight spaces. That said, it's important to note that Flow Control checks at the start of the phase, so you can't move onto and steal objectives for Flow, then use this to teleport in closer - you won't be in your Flow of Magic until the Shooting phase.
  • Scouring Warpflame (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in your Shooting phase on a Psyker unit in your army’s Flow of Magic. Until the end of the phase, ranged weapons in that unit gain [IGNORES COVER] and after they’ve shot, pick an enemy unit they hit and that unit can’t get the Benefit of Cover for the rest of the phase. This is extremely good with the changes to 11th edition rules, and its primary uses are stripping cover for a unit of Scarabs or a Forgefiend to put in work with their guns. Rubrics already ignore cover with an icon, so that part is just kind of whatever here, but this can be a big help taking down larger threats.
  • Kaleidoscopic Tempest (Battle Tactic, 1 CP) – Used in your opponent’s Shooting phase, after they pick targets. One PSYKER unit they picked gets Stealth until the end of the phase, and if they’re wholly within your Flow of Magic, they get the benefit of cover. This is just wholly broken in 11th edition and doesn't do much beyond give you cover in the open. If you get caught fully in the open it can be useful, but you should avoid being in that situation.

Look, these are some solid stratagems. The ones you're going to make the most use of are Strands of Time, Scouring Warpflame, and Through the Veil. Being able to fall back and shoot with Scarabs is huge, and they're one of the game's more deadly shooting threats when they can stack buffs.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Playing This Detachment

The reason you're playing this Detachment is that it's a Take and Hold Detachment which costs 2 DP, allowing you to combine it with either Ritual of Regeneration if you want to go heavy on Scarabs, or Sekhetar Cohort if you want to run lots of Sekhetar Robots. Right now the vast majority of good Thousand Sons tournament results come from either Hexwarp Thrallband or Warpforged Cabal, paired with Sekhetar Cohort. 

If your running Sekhetar Cohort with Hexwarp, you'll likely be on Take and Hold. Those lists typically want a ten-model unit of Terminators to act as both a nasty shooting threat and also hold large objectives at mid table. Those will want a Terminator Sorcerer leader - a natural add-on who helps them punch up into bigger threats - and support from Rubrics, Tzaangor Enlightened, and Sekhetar for holding objectives and moving around the board. If you're going that route, Priority Assets is a decent choice, and you can focus on using Scarabs to hold the central objective while smaller, faster, cheaper units perform your actions.

Strengths

  • Some good Stratagems which give you new tools for playing with Rubrics - especially Deep Strike
  • Gives Magnus the ability to re-roll 1s to wound when he’s not in your Flow of Magic
  • Only costs 2 DP and gives you Take & Hold

Weaknesses

  • The Detachment Rule is already something you get from Rubrics
  • Detachment Rule only works on characters and champions, with no ability to give unit attacks the [PSYCHIC] tag
  • Power level pretty low overall and the Detachment Rule doesn’t give you re-rolls and +1 to wound at the same time
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Sekhetar become much more useful when they can hit on a 3+ in melee and the ability to have them sticky objectives, shoot an action, and give opponents -1 to wound rolls makes them outstanding utility pieces for your army. A unit of four has quite a bit of beef thanks to a 5+ invulnerable save and a T6, 4-wound profile, and so these can credibly help support and protect your Scarabs at midtable while you rack up scoring for Priority Assets.

Final Thoughts

Hexwarp Thrallband isn't a great detachment on its own; it has some decent stratagems but the Detachment rule is pretty lackluster and the enhancements aren't what you'd want to see compared to other detachments. That said, being a 2 DP detachment means that it doesn't have to be: Its power + another Detachment just need to be higher than a 3 DP Detachment and that's where it succeeds. Grand Coven and Rubricae Phalanx may be better, but being able to take a second Detachment for more power pushes Hexwarp over the top.
