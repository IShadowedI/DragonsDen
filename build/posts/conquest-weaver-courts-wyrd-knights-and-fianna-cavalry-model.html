Conquest: Weaver Courts Wyrd Knights and Fianna Cavalry Model Review

October 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

I've been really enjoying lately the amount of dual kits from Parabellum that allow you to make on of two, quite vastly different, regiments. Earlier I reviewed the Gallowglass, letting you make either a Medium ranged unit, or a Heavy melee variant. Now we come to the funkiest, most little shop of horrors-esque cavalry you ever did see. From, I can only assume, the mind of HP Lovecraft come the noble knights and sneaky ambush hunters of the Weaver Courts.

Wyrd Knight by Trout

The Kit, the Vibes, and the Building

Now, I have and will continue to sing the praises of the unique play styles of these two regiments; the same cannot be said of the design. I LOVE the look of these units so much, the sculpt is simple yet detailed, sleek yet active, and the unique mounts make a formidable sight on the battlefield... But for the love of bugs, I cannot tell which is which. If one committed a crime I would pay you well if you could pick them out in a line up! The mounts are (even more so than my BFFs the moths) identical. You have no options in pose either really so multiple units of these will start to look very same same (I'm looking at you Chios people, be more interesting.) Now, as long as you paint things uniquely and tell your opponent in good time, I see no reason this should be an actual issue in a game; I mean I run all my bugs with no rider on top and I've not had any issues, so can't see this being any more than me being a lil petty that the pretty lion plants aren't unique enough. (Isn't my lobster too buttery?)

As said, the build is super simple, so good work again, Para Bellum. The riders can be a little flimsy so a little care should be taken there, but they don't break nearly as much as the damn moose antlers so that's a win in my books.

Golden Daemon?

For my unit as seen here, I went in on the lion vibes with a bright orange skin, pale mane and some black stripes covering the body. The, almost, sinew like skin made contrast painting very effective here so they were up and ready with a good Zenithal base (Black with grey then white "highlights" dry brushed on,) contrast, and some little layered highlights to finish up. I used mainly the Games Workshop contrast range for this as I enjoy the vibrancy of the griffon hound orange. I added a splash of purple into the mouth for some differentiation in colour and added some fun bushes form Green Stuff World to the base to add to the pouncing and sneaky poses of the models which I think (even with the lack of rider) gives these kittens a really great battle presence!

Wyrd Knight by Trout

Git Good Gaming

Now, both these units give you a similar base stat line with a solid March 8, Attacks 5, Wounds 4, and Evasion 2 to keep them safe from some light to medium sneezes. However both these units are very set apart by their unique special rules. Like most of the roster these days, they both sit in the same season as your warlord giving that flex in play. This works nicely for both as being predominantly melee fighters and harass pieces, all four seasons offer up some nice advantages.

The Knights! The most proud and gallant of elf bugs, these solid Resolve 4 Cav units hit damn hard for their points and with a solid range to boot. For the cheap(ish) price of darts favourite score you get a fast moving, Fluid Formation, unit with a good number of attacks and some flexibility of extra rules. If you can get them a seed pre combat (not hard but very needed) you can drastically up their hitting power. At a glance five attacks with no cleave at Clash 3 is fine, but not ground breaking. However, with one seed, you can go from 16 attacks with nothing, to 18 attacks with either Flurry, Cleave 1, or Deadly Blades. This upped hitting power is just insane really. I may sound like a broken record, but the flexibility given by this rule allows you to optimise for the opponent you're engaging. Need a lil AP? Great, you got it! Or just roll terribly and need a mulligan? Amazing! I've seen these cats one shot so much with a Charge-Clash to up-trade very efficiently if used well. The fluid formation allows you that extra threat range, letting you pop into line of sight, or sneak a flank charge in; and the 3 impact hits each at Clash 3 do really add up! Now, you cant expect them to take much of a hit. Wounds 4 and only E2 with no other defensive rules make them disappear to most counter punches. Resolve 4 is nice, but really the damage is done already... But, they're fast and flexible, you should be getting the first strike off allowing you to make the favourable engagements most of the time. A top tier unit I would expect to see 1 (or 5 - really, look at this year's Chios lists) in every Weaver army

Wyrd Knight by Trout

The younger sibling comes in at a budget 160 and a similar stat line. Notably though only Clash 2. Now, the story and lore of the ambush predators, stalking their prey. Striking from the shadows and disappearing into the wild... is amazing and I love it. To represent this, the Fianna Cav have a fun set of special rules called Prowl and Pounce. I think, this is the first rule in Conquest that gives stealth to a unit. The opponent cannot draw line of sight to them should they be outside of 8". So no shots, no magic, and no long bomb charges. This is a really big survival boon for the Fianna's over their knightly counterpart. They do loose this rule until the end of the round should any enemy get within 8" so they can be vulnerable to a March-Charge, or the enemy sending in a fast moving scout unit. But this is fun, balanced, and thematic so I really love this rule!

A fun note to add, the unit is still size 2 and can block line of sight to things behind it. So in theory you can hide your main army behind these lions and have everything immune to line of sight based attacks until you get within 8"! Whilst prowling you do loose scoring potential and 2" of your March so, even with your Vanguard 4, you wont be guaranteed those midboard objectives. You can, during your activation, choose to loose the Prowl rule (so you can be seen) but gain Linebreaker and Terrifying 1 until the rounds end. Stack this with Opportunist, you can get some really spooky flank charges with them. However, the Clash 2 really hurts them and, with no fluid, having to be over 8" away, its not easy to get the perfect flank charge off. Even if you do, your Impact hits are much less effective at C2 and no extra rules like Cleave or Flurry mean they really don't punch up like you'd want them to for the amount of effort you no doubt put into the attack. These are a unit that really do enjoy a Summer based army. The shock helping them dish out a bit more serious damage is a really good percentage increase in output so I think I'd recommend that. But for only 20 points more, I think the Wyrd will see a lot more play in the competitive scene.

Final Thoughts

As for the Season, again it will depend on your play style and the rest of your army. Shock and Summer work well for both, that crunching charge is always a good thing to pounce (heheh) onto your opponent. I'm a sucker for Spring personally, I like the speed; 10" with Fluid on the Wyrd gives them really scary attack range, and Clash 3 innate already gives them the high average hits (with potential rerolls) that I do love. Autumn is Autumn; I do like the Mhor Sidhe Cleave Bloom to go alongside then Flurry on the Wyrd to hit very hard, and the culling rule is, as usual, fine in addition. Dread and Winter is probably the weakest here. With the units being low Wounds and usually MSU (Don't wanna give up fluid/ it's hard to sneak a large unit around) that means most attacks into you will remove the unit anyway, the lack of Inspired isn't usually enough to save them.

I've seen talk about adding heroes into the units but I personally like them to run alone, they're cheap enough to slot into most armies and quick and killy enough to win most 1v1s on the flank, and with good speed to quickly relocate should you need them. All in all one very interesting and decent unit, and one top tier sees this most become a staple of most tree-lover's army collections!
