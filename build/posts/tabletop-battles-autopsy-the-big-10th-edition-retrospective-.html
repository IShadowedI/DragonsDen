Tabletop Battles Autopsy: The Big 10th Edition Retrospective – Top 10s

May 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer
We come to the conclusion of our big retrospective on 10th Edition's suite of Codexes with, fittingly, some top ten lists.

As you'll recall if you've been reading the series so far, we've rated each Codex out of 5 on three metrics:
  • Power: What was the power level of the Codex at launch?
  • Merits: How highly would you rate the good aspects of the Codex as initially released, from the point of view of someone who plays the faction?
  • Mistakes: How bad were any impacts on the game for everyone else from the Codex as initially released? Were there any complete disasters that needed to be rewritten?
We then looked at each of the books grouped by superfaction, and you can find the earlier parts below:
  • Tabletop Battles Autopsy: The Big 10th Edition Retrospective - Space Marines
  • Tabletop Battles Autopsy: The Big 10th Edition Retrospective – Imperium
  • Tabletop Battles Autopsy: The Big 10th Edition Retrospective – Chaos
  • Tabletop Battles Autopsy: The Big 10th Edition Retrospective – Xenos
Our writers have given our thoughts on what made all the books good and bad, but which was the best? Or, worst if we're talking about mistakes?

In this final part, we've put together top and bottom ten lists for each of the three metrics. Which books will be fondly remembered? Which will live in infamy? Will some do both? Let's find out.

Notes on Scoring

When assembling the previous articles, we rounded the scores to the nearest 0.25 because there was no need to go more granular than that. Here, we've used the exact scores to compare the books, which is why there are fewer ties than you might otherwise expect!

In terms of ordering - for the highest score lists, #1 is the highest score, #10 is the lowest, for the lowest score lists, #1 is the lowest, #10 is the highest. OK? OK.

For those wondering there are also 28 total books, so every category has 8 books that were perfectly, beautifully middling.

Thoughts

Probably no huge surprises as to which factions are on top here, but it's interesting that the reasons for Death Guard and Aeldari being busted were pretty different. Death Guard was just a raw numbers game; we'd hit the part of the Codex cycle where every datasheet was somewhere between good and great, and it was costed hyper aggressively (maybe spurred on by being in a similar release window to Knights?). The lists you could put on the table just brutally steamrolled almost everything else, even accounting for the other horrific stuff in the metagame at the time.

Aeldari was a different beast - it was also undercosted at launch, but what pushed it wildly over-the-top was two layers of busted Detachments: Ynnari to start with, then a second round of Aspect Host and Warhost builds that could inflict massive damage with way too much reach and impunity. This was supported by Warp Spiders and Swooping Hawks being way too good at locking in move blocking and scoring if you just jammed three units of each into every list, giving a level of reliability that no other army could really equal. The value of units changing when you spam them has been a persistent problem in the last few Editions, and Aeldari really pushed that to the limit.

Necrons is the other one to shout out at the top, because what a slam dunk that book was. It was an early Codex, coming off two Editions where their books sucked, so the omens weren't good, but the book has so much going on that it's lasted as a real force ever since; even now, two very different Codex Detachments routinely rack up two finishes, despite many things in the book having been toned down over the years (admittedly, new C'tan help).

At the other end of the scale...I mean no real surprises. The Adeptus Mechanicus book was awful, and essentially stayed awful through many rounds of buffs till it was handed a second Army Rule. Those alongside it also needed a lot of love to get onto the tables as a meaningful force (though several definitely did get there), but I guess it is reassuring that from around Votann upwards, you start seeing books that were at least fine in their initial incarnations. There were some real duds

Thoughts

Elves make the double, but while there is some significant overlap at the top, there are some other books that punch above their power weight here, most notably Orks, Chaos Space Marines and Emperor's Children. Orks and CSM both stood out via the Detachments - just really deep sets of rules providing a lot of variety, and it's really just a shame that Orks got gutted so quickly (whereas CSM has really lasted). I'd say the snap overreaction to Orks probably sits alongside letting Ynnari slide through a balance Dataslate as one of the biggest mis-steps with nerfs, because Orks was a really joyful book, and deserved to be allowed to run free for longer.

Emperor's Children also make a really strong showing here despite a very constrained roster; the things it does well, it does really well, and it was very impressive that it managed to carve out a distinctive niche as a melee Chaos Marine army when World Eaters already existed. The last one to shout out at the top is Adepta Sororitas, which is maybe the book with the highest average level of rules quality through the whole Edition; it's clear that the designers went through it with a fine-toothed comb to make sure every single unit and Detachment was exciting. The initial points were a lot more reasonable on them as well; hulls ended up needing adjustment, but in general it was a much better example of costing to the rules that had been written.

At the bottom, it's mostly the usual suspects again, though with a bit of shuffling to reflect the fact that Custodes, in particular, felt actively hostile to the idea of the army being good. Adeptus Mechanicus was a weaker book, but that seemed like more of an artifact of the army adjusting very badly to the new way Characters and buffs worked in 10th, whereas Custodes was a damnatio memoriae for all the (many) crimes of the Index. Once again, however, it's very striking that it's really only the bottom 5-ish books that were properly miserable.

Thoughts

That's right baby, space elves number 1, undefeated 10th Edition champions, never bet against touchdown Fuegan.

Unsurprisingly there is, indeed, a lot of overlap in the three lists, which does bring us to the main conclusion of this series - taking risks and pushing the boat out power wise does tend to make Codexes pretty memorable, they just also tend to cause problems. The alignment isn't quite one to one, and if the studio are reading, the takeaway should be that following books seem to have done the best job of landing something hype without causing big problems:
  • Chaos Space Marines
  • Orks
  • Emperor's Children
  • Adepta Sororitas (ish)
For me, this list does create one slightly awkward takeaway - 10th Edition is by far the most fun I've had with an Edition of Warhammer, and the two armies I play are sitting right there at the top of the rankings on all three metrics. Apparently I have been part of the problem, but my goodness has it been enjoyable. I hope everyone else has as good a time as I did wherever their armies fell in the rankings, and the inexorable rise in tournament participation throughout suggests a good reception. Here's to more of that.

Wrap Up

And...that's a wrap. We've really enjoyed putting this together, and we hope you've enjoyed reading it too. See you in three years, we guess.
