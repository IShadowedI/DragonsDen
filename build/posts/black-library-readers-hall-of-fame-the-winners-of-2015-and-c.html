Black Library Readers’ Hall of Fame: The Winners of 2015, and Class of 2016

August 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Black Library Readers’ Hall of Fame is Tabletop Battles’s long-term, reader-participation initiative to recognize those novels that have “cultural, historical, or aesthetic significance” to the Black Library. Through the votes of readers as well as our community panel of community voices, we’ll examine the entire history of the Black Library, induct the latest winners, and vote for the next round’s candidates every other week.

And here we are at long last, the end of Warhammer Fantasy. While we stand on the verge of Age of Sigmar1, the Class of 2015 was (almost) the last hurrah of the world that was. I say almost, of course, because in 2024 Graham McNeill's Lords of the Lance proved that nothing ever quite stays dead. Sadly, while McNeill has telegraphed that he'd love to do more, it appears to have been a one-off.

So soak it in, folks, the end of an era... and the beginning of a new one.

As a reminder, if you're really enjoying this project and want to see it from a different angle, the companion piece, The Hall Monitor, runs over at my Substack, The Warhammer Wordforge. A cross between a director's commentary and behind-the-scenes look, each installment we dive a little deeper into a particular element of the Readers' Hall of Fame project. Hope to see you there!

The Envelope Please

Now, let's get to the stars of the show, the four books inducted for 2016!

Michael, Track of Words: "It’s the way Josh Reynolds captures the voices of these characters that makes this such an enjoyable read; the events taking place are so jaw-droppingly epic that it could easily have ended up as a glorified battle report, but instead there’s a real sense of pathos in so many of these characters, from the strangely human vampires to the stoic acceptance of the dwarfs; even those on the side of Chaos come across as much more than just one-dimensional villains. At its heart this is just a brilliantly entertaining novel."

AJ, The Paladin Journal: "It couldn’t have been easy to write the book that closed out an entire game system that had survived for almost 30 years. Josh Reynolds had the distinction of writing the final novel of Warhammer Fantasy, and expectations for it were certainly high across the board. And that I think is why The Lord of The End Times is actually a pretty great novel. Josh wrote the best possible version of this story and he brought to a close the stories of several key characters from across the setting as we traveled the length and breadth of the Old World. Even after all these years now, its still hard to process because there are so many emotions and memories attached. This novel was always going to be an uphill battle, and Josh was at his best here."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "Here it is, the obituary of the Old World. Reynolds was given an almost impossible task here, taking the last survivors of the nuking of the Old World and giving them suitably notable last stands. As the ultimate book of the End Times series, the "last one out turn off the lights" entry, the historical weight of this one is immeasurable- and so likely was the pressure to get it right. Reynolds understood the assignment and delivered accordingly."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "One of the most consequential books of the Horus Heresy, it offers some closure to the Imperium Secundus subplots, features a very popular dynamic in the relationship between two brothers of the Iron Warriors and Imperial Fists, and sets up the Tyranid menace that would come 10,000 years later. All this, and with Haley at the peak of his powers behind the pen, too!"

Michael, Track of Words: "As Guy Haley’s first novel-length contribution to the Heresy series it doesn’t disappoint. On top of the great characters and the well-paced plot, it’s the first Heresy book in a while to capture the sense of tragedy around the whole episode, and it’s got some really powerful moments. Essentially it’s everything a Heresy book should be."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "David Annandale's second novel featuring Commissar Yarrick, Pyres tackled the telling of the Battle of Hades Hive. This had been a significant lore event from the Codexes, and bringing it to story form would have been a high bar for anyone. It's here that one of 40K most enduring rivalries- Yarrick and Ghazghkull Mag Uruk Thraka- was born, and as such it has earned its place in the Hall."

Gus, The Black Library Subreddit: "This is the Yarrick novel. This is where he becomes a legend of legends, both to humans and Orks. It also has one of the most heinous villains in 40k, spoiler, it's not an Ork. David Annadale wrote an action movie with a little bit of everything: over the top action, political intrigue, larger than life characters (even for 40k), and all sorts of fun encounters. This is one of the most underrated novels in the Black library in my opinion and it is a must read on any Black Library essentials list."

Michael, Track of Words: "It must have been a tricky balancing act to remain true to the series while also fitting in with the End Times narrative. Thankfully, when the real essence of Gotrek & Felix does come to the fore, it’s everything you want to see and a powerful reminder of why these characters are so popular."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "Gotrek & Felix was one of the Black Library's most iconic, longest-running series in its entire history- and Slayer is the book that laid it to rest. The partnership that helped define Warhammer Fantasy for a generation of readers was given a deeply emotional, epic farewell. As with Reynolds, David Guymer had an extraordinary challenge to do justice to the series, and cleared the bar."

Congratulations to all the worthy winners, and a very special welcome to first-timers David Annandale and David Guymer!
