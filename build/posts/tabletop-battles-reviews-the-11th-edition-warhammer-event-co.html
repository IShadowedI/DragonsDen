Tabletop Battles Reviews the 11th Edition Warhammer Event Companion

June 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Today Games Workshop released another key document in their slow rollout of 11th edition assets, dropping the Warhammer Event Companion, the updated 11th edition version of the Tournament Organizer's Companion from prior editions, along with guidance for Teams, Dominatus, and Doubles events in other documents.

If you're wondering where you can get a copy of this document, you can find it here:

The 11th Edition Warhammer Event Companion Version 1.0

The other docs are here:

  • Dominatus Companion
  • Doubles Companion
  • Teams Companion

That's a lot to cover, and if you're interested in the Cliff's notes of what changed and what's in each document, well - good news! We've got you covered:

The Event Companion

The Event Companion gives the basic rundown of what you need to play competitive games. That includes:

  • The pregame sequence
  • Additional Errata and FAQs for Chapter Approved Missions
  • Mission and terrain layouts for every mission combination
  • Base size guidelines for every unit

There are some key changes to be aware of here, and some important rules that are going to be important for every game - not just tournament games - which are only found in this document.

The most important of these is that players select a force disposition when they muster their army. Unlike the rules laid out in the Chapter Approved pamphlet which let you do this at game time, this makes it clear that you'll be playing the same disposition every tournament. This means you should more or less always be building for the single disposition you plan to run, and not worrying about any of the others, unless there's a bad matchup you see in the mix. We've talked at length about the different dispositions, but whatever one you like the most, you'll need to make sure your Detachments make that an option for you.

The Layouts have also been finally revealed publicly here, and there are three sets for each matchup, for a total of 45. They're not all unique, but close enough that you likely won't notice the difference. These have been split into A, B, and C groups, and you'll rotate through them during an event as a way to keep things from feeling stale. You might play Purge vs. Purge four times, but at least you're likely to do it on two or three different layouts.

There are some Designer's Notes in here, specifying what terms like "Cumulative," "or," "leaves the battlefield," "When drawn," and "Up to a limit" mean. And the Errata and FAQs section has some helpful clarifications for the scoring on Death Trap, Surveil the Foe, and Vital Link.

The Teams Companion

Curie: Today we also saw a first from Games Workshop - official rules on how to run and participate in Team events! Veterans of the format will notice that much of what was used in the previous edition’s World Team Championships (WTC) format was carried over to here, but we’ll walk through all the key points here.

Team Composition

The first thing is on team composition - they have put forward the restrictions on armies as well as dispositions. In each team, you can use each codex only once per team - either as the main faction or as allies (e.g. taking Chaos Knights with Chaos Daemons allies would use up both choices for your team). In addition, you can only take each disposition once for each 5 players on the team (rounding up). What this means is that in 5-player team events, each team will have exactly one instance of each disposition, guaranteeing an element of diversity not just in armies but also in the matchups that need to be planned for. In 8-player team events (the largest team format currently used, and the standard for large events such as the WTC and Challengers Cup) this would mean you can take up to two of each disposition in your team.

Team Pairing

Now that you have your team, we have to figure out how to pair the teams off. In team Warhammer, each player will have a single opponent, and this is how we do it. GW has done a good job of giving broad guidance on how to do this pairing process for every size team from 3 to 8 players. There are 3 broad sections that they divide pairings into: Initial Skirmish, Main Engagement, and Champion.

In Initial Skirmish both teams secretly select a defending player, and then the defenders are simultaneously revealed. Each team then secretly puts forward two attackers to challenge the opposing team’s defender, which are then revealed simultaneously, and the defender from each team then secretly chooses an attacker, rejecting one of them back to the roster. At this point each defender announces their layout choice and you have the first set of pairings. This segment is used once in 5-player & 6-player teams and twice in 7-player & 8-player teams.

Main Engagement is the section that is used to finalize the pairings between the teams. In it, you follow the same process as Initial Skirmish, with the exception that the two rejected attackers are then paired into each other. The refused attackers will play on a layout based on the event round - in Round 1 they use Layout A, Round 2 they use Layout B, Round 3 they use Layout C, and so on.

The Champion is named for the one player left on each team in 4-player, 6-player, and 8-player team sizes. The Champion is the player from each team that is left over after the Main Engagement step and they play each other. They follow the same layout pattern as the rejected attackers did in the previous step.

Playing the Round

From here, the games are played like any other game of Warhammer, with one small tweak to the pregame sequence - the Defender from the pairing step is the Defender in-game, which means that they will have first deployment in game.

Team Scoring

Team scoring keeps the format that was widely accepted as the norm for team events across the last several editions - differential scoring. Each player received a number of Battle Points (BP) for their team based on the difference between their respective Victory Points. If the two players are within 5 Victory Points of each other, the game is a draw and each player scores 10 Battle Points. From here for every 5 victory points of difference, the winner gains 1 Battle Point and the loser loses 1 Battle Point. As an example, let’s say Rob scored 95 points and I scored 76 points that is a difference of 19 points - which is 3 intervals of 5 points and so he will score 13 BP while I will score 7 BP for our respective teams.

To win a round there is a final check on the difference in points between the teams - known as the win margin. For 3-player teams the differential required is 4 BP - so a score of 32-28 is a win for the team scoring 32 - this difference then increases with each player added to the teams by 2 points, capping at 12 BP for 8-player teams. If the teams are within this range (e.g. 84-76 is 8 BP difference), then the round ends in a draw.

Pairings and Placings

Similar to the individual tournament guide, there are some recommendations made for team pairings and placings - these do differ from what’s been used historically and some tournaments may opt to not use them as a result.

Pairings are recommended to be done by Round Wins, then Game Wins, then Random while placings are recommended to be done by Round Wins, then Game Wins, and finally by Battle Points.

Closing Thoughts

The team pack has one big surprise for folks - that the defender in pairings is now the defender in-game. Planning around armies that benefit more from reliable Infiltrators options will become more valuable as a result of this. Otherwise, this pack codifies a system that’s been widely used for years now by the larger competitive community. Go find 2-7 other friends and have some fun playing some teams!

Doubles Companion

Here's an entirely new one - rules for Doubles events in Warhammer 40k. And it only took the better part of four decades! These rules specify that when you're building your doubles armies, you're operating in many ways like you have one big army comprised of two components - you cannot double up on EPIC HERO units, enhancements, or any other once per army items across your team. Any particular Upgrade can be included more than once in a player's army, but can't be included in both armies in a team. Both armies have to have a CHARACTER to be their WARLORD, and once you've built your lists you pick one Force Disposition for your entire team.

Core Rules Changes

The big thing here is the Core Rules Changes, which are designed to make doubles play work. There are a few big ones here:

  • In a Unified forces, both armies share the same faction keywords. In these armies models and units are friendly models/units for rules purposes, and things like auras. You also share your army rules, so for example Drukhari share a pool of pain tokens, and if you did Death Guard, I believe you'd have a single contagion to choose. You can also hop in each other's transports.
  • In Forces of Convenience, that isn't the case, and other models/units on your team are neither friendly nor enemy models/units for rules purposes. Note that IMPERIUM teams fall into this category, so Marines + Sisters is a force of convenience.
  • Teams gain 1 CP per round and can't generate more than 1 extra. Players share CP for Stratagems, and those are limited to a force rather than each player's army, so you can't double up on Insane Bravery.
  • Your Detachment rules only apply to your armies, however - you don't get to benefit from your ally's detachments.
  • Point limit restrictions are by team, not player. So if you wanted to bring Daemon allies in a 2000 point teams list, you could still have up to 500 points.

These are... pretty good! They satisfy the minimum requirements of being questions you need to answer for doubles events. They're good to have, and we'll be using them.

Dominatus Briefings - Credit Warhammer Community

Dominatus Companion

Finally there's the Dominatus Companion, and it's pretty short. Dominatus is already set up to run five rounds of games. This gives you some guidance for pairing players, how to use the Relics, Honours, Skills, and Agendas within that event, and how to handle phases. There are some timing changes for the ends of each campaign phase to smooth things along, but otherwise no major rules adjustments. When you declare battle formations you'll pick up to 3 upgrade cards you've earned from the campaign to apply to your units.

Final Thoughts

There's a lot here, and it's good stuff. Games Workshop have been doing better work each year with their Tournament Companions, and it's good to see them finally tackle Doubles events and Narrative events with Dominatus. The teams development is a big deal, and a huge step to finally going to that grand, unified Warhammer Tournament circuit GW has been building towards. I'm not sure if that's good or bad, but I think everyone playing the same Warhammer is a good development regardless.
