Trench Crusade Procession of the Sacred Affliction Model Review

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With the release of the Carcass Front box comes two new plastic kits for Naval Raiders and Sacred Affliction. Considering these are the second and third plastic releases for TC, this will set the tempo for what we can expect going forward. We'll obviously be starting with the cooler of the two factions (unless you read our Naval Raider article first), the Sacred Affliction.

Thank you to Factory Fortress Inc for providing us with advance copies for review.

What’s in the Box?

  • Lazarist War Prophet 
  • Lazarist Castigator
  • Leper Pilgrim (32mm base) x 2
  • Leper Pilgrim (25mm base) x 3
  • Stigmatic Nun
  • Ecclesiastic Prisoner

Building the Models

Dandy: Having built models from Archon a few times now, I can safely say that the quality remains high, as you would expect from them. Many of the models come with two different options, offering easy mix-and-match potential and lots of opportunities for kitbashing. One particular aspect I appreciated is the way pieces fit together to avoid obvious gaps. For example, on one model, the cloak's scroll overlapped the other connective piece, completely hiding a line cap and reducing cleanup time. It’s definitely a well-designed kit that shows a lot of thought went into it. 

The sculpts themselves are phenomenal. When you look at the regular Trench Pilgrim lineup, there aren’t a lot of details on all of the models. The Castigator and even the Prisoner are probably up there for having the most little details and varied textures. These though? Basically, every model I painted has a good chunk of small, differing details. I think of the four I painted: the Castigator was actually the leanest, with the remaining three having multiple layers of bandages, cloth, rope, and even wood. 

I did have some issues adding these models to my current Trench Pilgrim force; however, that was simply due to the added metal bars and basing. I already have a set base scheme for my Trench Pilgrims, and I wanted these models to expand that range. I found it a little difficult and awkward to snip off the unwanted parts to make it work in my trench scheme, leaving a couple of models in precarious poses and requiring me to re-support their poses. If you’re planning on adding these models to an existing force and the base scheme isn’t wanted, you will need to bring out the pliers and snip away. 

Bre4d_: I agree with Dandy, the models have a solid amount of customizable options as well as brilliant sculpts all the way around. I will never complain about being spoiled with cool choices! My complaint about the customizations is that each model has at least one optionable part, except the Ecclesiastic Prisoner, who is my favorite small adult son. Fortunately, the model is cool looking enough and I don’t have to settle for something that looks bad. 

The different bits are separated on the sprues by not only numbers but also letters, which I think is a neat idea to help have the different models as part of the same sprue. I have absolutely no stake in Big Sprue™ but I felt like these were very congested with bits and it was really difficult for me to find the next bit I needed for each model. I’m sure cost, logistics, and a bunch of other boring reasons behind the scenes go into how these sprues are laid out, but I felt particularly frustrated trying to find ‘G13’ when there are a bunch of other ‘X13’s that all have similar dimensions and bandages everywhere, as well as the sections of the sprue being all over the place and different bits for each model not being near each other. After the scavenger hunt of finding the bits, everything slotted into each other nicely and these amazing looking models make up for it. 

Painting the Models

Credit: Dandy

Dandy: When it came to painting, I wanted to stick to my regular Pilgrim scheme so I could interchange the models whenever I wanted. Overall, it was easy to stick to my previous scheme, and there were matching elements across both model ranges that made them blend together. Armour for the Nuns, Castigator with long swathes of cloth, and Pilgrims with ragged clothing. The variety within the Leper-Pilgrim sculpts themselves has unifying elements, whilst still retaining their own uniqueness. The highlight for me is easily the Leper-Pilgrim swinging the Millstone. On the back is a statue of someone praying, which also serves to carry some gear. It was a joy to paint, and it was refreshing to have this other element to contrast with the front of the model.  Coming back to my previous point about all the differing details. All these details that overlap with one another obviously need to be painted, and well, there are a lot of bandages. Like, a LOT. It’s not just that, but given the ragtag nature of the faction, the bandages of the models leave skin exposed, which can be a little fiddly to get to when it comes to painting. Likewise, Models like the Nun have a few layers of clothing you may need to differentiate between, all with flowing bandages. As long as you go in with a bit of a plan, it’s not difficult, but it is a clear step up in complexity compared to the leaner sculpts of the Trench pilgrims. 

Bre4d_: For my painting, I tried to also match my existing Trench Pilgrim force which has slightly drab greens and khaki and is weathered heavily to look like they have been dragged through…the trenches. This is advantageous to me since I will try really hard to do something really exciting paint wise, realize I would need way more time to actually execute said idea for a deadline, so then I get to hide how the sausage looks(?) with some grimy and gunky sponging. 

Sacred Affliction War Priest - bre4d_

As Dandy said, it is A LOT of bandages with little bits of skin showing in between. This challenge presents a lot of good opportunities to show some painting prowess that I simply do not have the patience to implement, so if you’re trying to bang these guys out quickly, the insane amount of textures make these models fantastic recipients of slap-chop like techniques. 

STLs

Norman: In addition to the box itself, there are a set of STLs accompanying the release of Carcass Front. There are two STL sets, a multipart Leper Knight kit and an upgrade set for the Anchorite Shrine.

Leper Knights

The knights are interesting as they're a fully customizable multipart kit. There are three bodies to work with and each has a slue of ranged and melee options as well as a set of accessories to go on their their backs. The models themselves look great, and will feel right at home next to their Nun sisters. If you fell in love with art of knight helms with gas masks integrated into them, this is a great kit to mess around with.

Anchorite Shrine

I didn't have time to finish this guy but he's very sick. There's options for a wheel with and without the prisoner and an alternate mace, head and joint coverings. If I have any issues with this guy it's that it demands a lot of freehand; the leg and shoulder reliefs look very odd as just bare wood so you'll want to put something there even if its just vague Christian imagery. I couldn't get the candles to sit flush on the armor personally, although I'm fully willing to chalk that up to a me problem.

Final Thoughts

Dandy: Absolutely love the models, and it was a hell of a good time to paint. You can see that the team is taking designs to the next level with this jump to plastic. They’re not sitting on their laurels, but are inventing unique design ideas for the factions, as well as innovating on the sculpts, to make them both easier for people to build and reduce cleanup, meaning you can get straight to painting. I haven’t dipped my toes into the Heretic side yet, but even just from a quick glance, I'm excited to build them up and have a go at painting those next. 

Bre4d: The endless creativity of FFI continues to come to fruition nicely with everything in the Carcass Front box. I’m so excited to see how they move forward with the rest of the plastic kits, between both how they are going to be distributed as well as what other awesome model designs they will come up with.
