[Kill Team] Tactical Focus: Scout Squad

October 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer
In this series of Kill Team articles we take a deep dive into one of the available teams, analyzing each team's strengths and weaknesses, how to set up important plays, and ultimately what to consider to pilot the team to victory. Note that this article assumes you already have knowledge of the basic rules for each team, and will reference abilities and ploys from that team throughout. If you want to learn more about the rules for each team you can find the most up to date version of the rules on the Warhammer Community Website here.

The klaxon of your trip alarm signals the enemy presence. New dots light up your auspex screen as you engage your booby trap to slow the oncoming forces. Your Sergeant radios in on the comms, it's time to move out. Readying your shotgun, you relocate to your next position. The enemy is here, engage and eliminate your targets and complete the mission. Will your tactical forward scouting be enough to control the killzone and accomplish your mission or will you and your battle brothers in training find failure in your journey to become Astartes? 

Welcome, dear reader, to our tactical focus on the superhuman war machines-in-training, Scout Squad. The Scouts Squads that make up the various Astartes chapters of the Imperium of Man represent the fresh initiates of the Space Marine ranks, though their bodies are still not yet ready to take the final step into donning the iconic power armor. What they may lack in brawn compared to their elder brothers, though, they make up for plenty in guile, relying on predator cunning to turn any battlefield into a well-laid trap for their foe, overwhelming their opponent before they even know what's going on. Their tabletop incarnation in Kill Team is no different, with a complex but rewarding playstyle that's basically the closest you'll get to putting the A-Team on the board. It's not easy, but learn to master this team and you'll love it when a plan comes together.

Imperial Fist Scouts. Credit: Jack Hunter

In this edition of Tactical Focus, we will teach you how the deep toolbox of Scout Squad and all of their various tricks and traps, how to best pilot their operatives and equipment, and what tactics and teams you need to stay alert for during each game. Let's get right into it then, we've got an ongoing operation to win here, and have got no time to waste.

Overview

Kill Team is a game of careful planning and precise measuring. The Scout Squad kill team personifies this with their Forward Scouting faction rule, allowing them to potentially modify the killzone and their operatives after both players have finished deploying their equipment and operatives. Combine this faction rule with Strategy Phase movement provided by Stealth Relocation and Scouts can exert early pressure on their opponents that can catch players off guard.

Scouts require their pilot to make many pregame decisions, starting with what operatives you want to take. You get a 3 APL Sergeant and eight 2 APL Scouts for your team selection. Aside from your leader who has three optional loadouts, you select the rest of your team from up to five of your specialists and as many of your three warrior variants as you need to fill your remaining spots. This leads to a good variety of team loadouts game to game and always having options for what you are playing into. Nine operatives with a 4+ armor save and 10 wounds on average (your Sergeant is a full fledged Astartes and has 11 wounds) makes you reasonably tough against smaller arms fire, but heavier weapons still remove you from the killzone effectively. 

Credit: Warhammer Community

Strengths and Weaknesses

Your Faction Rule is one of the key strengths of the Scout Squad, understanding what your scouting options do and when to use them is imperative to your success on the killzone. You have nine options to choose from: Relocate, Reposition, Trip Alarm, Booby Trap, Tactical Manoeuvre, Diversion, Devise Plan, Designate Target, and Spy. From that list you can select Trip Alarms and Reposition up to two times. Relocate allows you to redeploy a third of your team. Use this if you make a mistake in your deployment (it does happen from time to time) or use it to show one threat and shift to a different one if your opponent deploys in response to yours. Reposition lets you perform a reposition action but you must finish that action within 3” of your dropzone. Using this in combination with a reposition, dash catapults you up to 12” forward Turning Point 1; who said turn 1 violence was dead? Trip Alarm has you place a marker 6” away from your opponent’s dropzone that grants a 2” radius of Seek. These tokens disappear at the end of Turning Point 2, but while they are in the killzone, they are vital zoning tools that you need to utilize. Remember, you can place these on Vantage terrain or near access points, and keep teams off critical staging points like Volkus Strongholds and Tomb World Doors.

Booby Trap is a little finicky with its placement restrictions, requiring you to place the marker 6” from the opponent’s drop zone, more than 2” from other markers, access points, and accessible terrain then stopping any movement and dealing 2D3 damage. These are best used to shut down approaches from strongholds and lanes where you know they will be going. You can put them in front of light barricades to dissuade enemy charges. If you suspect the opponent’s Tac Op or know it from your Spy scouting option, a mine in their pathway can disrupt an opponent’s otherwise good plan. Devise Plan is simple, 1 free CP. You take Tactical Manoeuvre and Diversion to modify your own APL and your opponent’s APL. Diversion requires the opponent’s operative to be within 6” of a board edge, but you can stop some telegraphed plays or make a first activation play on your opponent’s home objective. Designate Target exists as a source of a reroll in shooting and combat against one target, but there are other options you will want to use more often than this.

Scouts. Credit: Saint

After several rounds of buffs scouts now reliably output enough damage to threaten power armor. Using your Targeting Oculars equipment, Ambush, and Emboldened Aspirant ploys helps confirm critical kills. Scouts killing power shines into 8 wound and lower operatives but do not underestimate their damage output into Elite kill teams with access to a Heavy Bolter,  Missile Launcher, and Sniper operatives. You can also play a little aggressive with the ability to change orders in the strategic phase using Adaptable Training. Scouts excel at scoring double kills into horde teams or double tapping important elite targets, leveraging their Astartes Training firefight ploy which allows them to double fight, double shoot when selecting at least one Bolt weapon, or shoot a second time with one of your gunner operatives for 2 action points. Having the ability to double shoot a Shotgun, Sniper, Heavy Bolter or Missile Launcher threatens damage that every opponent has to respect, especially when you out activate your opponents.

Certain weapon profiles present less than ideal breakpoints. Power Weapons, Plasma Guns, and Meltas all do damage that Scouts do not like to see on the other side of the table. Power Weapons kill them with a crit and a hit. An overcharged Plasma Gun guarantees a kill with 4 normals and a Melta only needs 3 hits to confirm a kill against Scouts. Missile Launchers are threatening and cannot be ignored, but they have a heavy keyword so you can plan for their threat ranges. Against bolters and similar profiles you can often live, especially when paired with your free smoke grenades from your warriors.

Black Templars Scouts. Credit: SRM

Tactics

Capitalizing on your +1 APL buffs is key to success with Scouts. One of the best ways to get the most out of the APL buffs from your Sergeant is to Stealth Relocation forward 3 inches and then reposition shoot shoot, or reposition dash shoot. You can do this in combination with your Targeting Oculars to shoot with a balanced or ceaseless, lethal 5+ Krak Grenades to send a missile on a one way trip for a key target. 

Doing this with your Sergeant and a Tactical Manoeuvre results in a 4 APL beast that can strategy phase dash then move dash 12 inches forward or charge fight fight shoot. You can boost that one inch using a Raw Physiology and ignore injury. Speaking of your Astartes Sergeant, he natively has 3 APL, gives out +1 APL to another Scout in the killzone during his activation, and can shoot or fight twice similar to other Astartes. He acts as the team’s Comms operative and is critical to keep alive to make the team operate at its full output. The Scout Squad kill team has access to +1 APL up to six times a game if your leader stays alive the whole game and you select the Tactical Manoeuvre Forward Scouting option.

Another strategy that utilizes your gunners is to throw a smoke at the feet of your sniper and to use his Optics ability to ignore obscuring and going on guard. While going on guard only works on close quarters maps you can use this same technique on open as your last activation with a regular shoot action. Doing this behind cover with his native camo cloak can make him deceptively tanky.

Using your Hunter for a Krak or Frag Grenade vehicle on Killzone Volkus is a great way to establish asymmetric threats. The Hunter treats the vertical distance of climbs as 2 inches. Use that to climb ruins and strongholds and throw your supercharged Kraks at an operative your opponent mistakenly thought was safe.

Learning when to sit and wait patiently is a skill every Scout Squad player needs to hone to a point. Activating and passing to prevent unnecessary deaths and losing games on Kill Op. Getting behind on Kill Op is easy to do but hard to claw back from.

Scouts. Credit: Corrode

Tac Ops and Crit Ops

Scouts have Infiltration and Recon as their Archetypes. Starting in Infiltration, Track Enemy requires you to throw someone forward within 6” of your opponent, you cannot do this against horde teams or you will be punished so the best time to take this is into teams you out activate and even then you have better options available. Steal Intelligence is effective against teams you are confident you can kill early. Holding one token for three turns nets you 4 points on your tac op; two kills and pick ups nets you 5. Sometimes you’re going to get these chances and this tac op is effective when they come up. Plant Devices is viable into crit ops that require opponents to stay on their home objectives, think Transmission, Orb and Energy Cells.

Moving on to Recon, if you are planning on taking knife or shotgun warriors and pushing forward into the opponent’s territory, Flank can work but will throw operatives forward to likely perish if they do not go first. Your sniper can help you threaten shots and score Scout Enemy Movement. Either your opponent stages the scouted operative out of your vision or they give you easy tac op points. As easy as this sounds to score it can lead to very low scores on your tac op as your opponent can directly influence your score. Retrieval is the Scouts bread and butter. Scouts can reach the opponent’s objective quickly turn 2 with their aforementioned strategic phase dash if they go first and the opponent does not defend against Retrieval. If you do this on your Home and Middle objective you score 4 points very safely as long as you keep those operatives alive. Pairing this with your primary op is a reliable way to score 2 points on primary with you often scoring a 3rd at the cost of a sacrifice.

Scouts appreciate crit ops such as Loot and Stake Claim where you can leverage your bonus APL early on to get ahead on points. For stake claim a trip alarm on the center objective and cover will dissuade most teams from approaching the center point making it easier for you to control. Loot pairs well with Retrieval to move onto an undefended point, loot and retrieve for a 2 Victory Point activation. Repeat that on your home objective and you’ve netted yourself 4 points on two activations.

Match Ups

Scouts like being the controlled aggressor; if you overextend, you will lose too many operatives to be all that effective late in the game. With your 4+ armor save you are going to make some critical saves but you will also miss some of those same saves too. You live by the 4+, you die by the 4+. Scouts thrive into 8 wound teams due to their weapon breakpoints. Anytime you only need two shotgun hits to go through to ensure a kill is a nice time for scouts. Elves, Dwarves and Guardsmen are all prime targets for your shotguns to achieve this. Look for opportunities to set up double kills using your APL boosted warriors in conjunction with your strategy phase dash and Astartes Training double shoot.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Celestian Insidiants are a challenging match up for Scouts; their 3+ save and 9 wound breakpoints make your shotgun math significantly worse. You can reliably trade one for one using your double shoot, but they can riddle you with silent crossbow fire in response. Pair that with their damage reduction in melee combat and Scouts always need 3 hits to kill an Insidiant. 

Marines are still Marines at the end of the day, and they can present a problem match up for Scouts. 3+ power armor and 13+ wounds make Marines tough nuts to crack without using piercing weapons, which Scouts have as well as an activation advantage. However, Marines all have access to innate double shooting or double fighting, so you have to be very careful with your positioning or you can and will lose two Scouts to one Marine fast.

Imperial Fist Scout with auspex. Credit: Jack Hunter

Piloting the Team

Scout Squad’s simplicity is a strength as much as it’s a weakness. You only have five specialists to choose from so the mental load for learning the team is low, but that also means your tricks are easy to learn for your opponent. After selecting which of your specialists you want to take, you need to fill the rest of your team with regular warriors. You can select from warriors equipped with Astartes Shotguns, Bolters or Bolt Pistols and Knives and each turn a warrior can throw one free Stun and Smoke grenade. There are cases for taking each warrior type, with shotguns getting fielded the most. The knife warriors excel at threatening multi kills into horde teams without needing to invest into Astartes Training, although you will likely still bring shotguns as well. Bolter warriors don’t see the field often, but have a niche match up into Vespids due to the break points that bolters hit into 9w operatives. Shotguns are a good take every game. So much of the team’s shooting efficiency comes from your shotguns and you will be happy you have them.

Scouts pressure early to jump out to a lead and hold that until the end. Using your Trip Alarms on the middle objective to exert control over the mid-board is key to approaching this strategy, just be wary of losing too many operatives in doing so. Stage warriors to make runs at the middle and enemy objectives if you are running Retrieval, and stage them to assault the opponent if you are running Steal Intelligence. Your warriors have stun grenades and smokes for free so you can use them to stun the enemy clusters turn 2.

Your specialist operatives each serve a unique purpose on the team. Your Tracker can give your team Seek Light against an enemy within 8 inches of him using his Track Enemy special ability, and use his Auspex Scan to remove obscuring within the same radius. Your Sniper can use his optics rule to ignore obscuring as well, and if he makes a shot against a target under the effects of your Auspex, he gains Seek Light for that shot. Your Heavy Gunners have the Heavy Weapon Bipod special ability, giving them Ceaseless for attacks where they have not moved when they shoot, and Relentless if combined with Ceaseless from Ambush. You won’t take every gunner every game. The Heavy Bolter is great into elites but less effective than a regular shotgun warrior into hordes. The Hunter is fantastic on Volkus for his Lethal 3+ ability that can bully teams in melee. On Tomb World, there are no vantages to run under or drop from, so it is harder to use his special ability.

Imperial Fist Scout with Heavy Bolter. Credit: 40khamslam

Get your points and get out of Dodge! You can reliably take an early points lead at the end of Turning Point 2, but you need to set up with the intention of getting to safety when you activate or through the use of your strategic phase dashes. Getting out to an early lead is doable, but holding it is harder to accomplish if you start to give your opponent too many chances to respond. Only commit what you need to commit for your points. In order to do this effectively comes down to getting the most out of your bonus APL. The primary source of this is your Sergeant and as tempting as using him early to threaten early kills, he can give out up to 4 bonus APL a game to other operatives. Ensuring his safety is paramount to making sure you can buff your other operatives and get the most out of who needs to score you points.

Credit: Warhammer Community

Final Thoughts

Getting the most out of your Scouts requires strategic planning and proactive thinking for where and when you want your operatives to be with their bonus APL. Understanding the tempo of your game, and if you're the aggressor will also dictate when you make your plays. Sometimes the answer is not to just rush the opponent’s home objective with Retrieval, but rather to wait for your opponent to show you what they are planning.

Be wary of over committing your limited resources for low reliability points. It's okay to sacrifice a warrior on the enemy home objective when he can Plant Devices or score you Retrieval. You just cannot lose more than that on your flank. Losing three spread out models is a comfortable spot for scouts to be because your opponent is only at Kill Grade 1, but losing more than one operative on a flank can crush your carefully constructed gameplan.

While Scout Squad can't just overpower most enemy teams with overwhelming numbers or raw muscle, no team offers quite as many ways to alter a battlefield and determine just how a scenario is played quite like them. They reward careful planning, map knowledge, and patient play, and a misstep in any of these can spell disaster. Keep walking on that razors edge without slipping though, and you'll spend most of the game one step ahead of you opponent, forcing them to spend the game on the backfoot while you waltz on in and win.
