BattleTech IlClan List Overview: Federated Suns

November 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Greetings hereditary nobles and welcome back to List Overview! Last time we took a look at a strong Clan faction, the noble and bird-infested Clan Jade Falcon. This week we will swing to the other end of the cultural divide and look at an Inner Sphere power, the whipping boy of the IlClan period, the great, the mighty, the extremely delusional, Federated Suns!

When looking at a faction, it can be hard to figure out what sorts of things their availability wants you to do, and it can be harder still to make a force that really feels like it belongs to that faction. IlClan has made some significant strides compared to previous eras, with a good deal of faction specific mech variants and chassis that you can use as tentpoles to prop up a general conception of how a faction wants to fight, in ideal circumstances. While it can be fun to deviate from that here and there, this week we will take a look at what a bog standard stereotypical Fed Suns list would look like, and how you would go about playing it.

Lore Primer

Davion Heavy Guard force for an event. Credit: Jack Hunter

So, the Federated Suns. Once the most powerful state in the Inner Sphere, for a long time both in and out of universe it looked like the Federated Suns, led by the noble House Davion, were going to inevitably unite humanity under their "benevolent rule". However, compared to their height during the Clan Invasion, the Federated Suns are a shadow of their former selves come IlClan. The repeated hammer blows of the collapse of their personal union with the Lyran Commonwealth, the resulting civil war, the Word of Blake Jihad, and the HPG blackout badly weakened their state. This left them in a fragile position when their system of absolute rule by a single family (nigh universal in BattleTech's setting, to be fair) produced an insane lunatic who very nearly led the realm into ruin, culminating in the capture of their capital planet by their historical rivals, the Draconis Combine.

While the Federated Suns have recaptured New Avalon and are finally under semi-competent rule again, they are still in a battered state and desperate situation, with enemies on all sides sharpening knives to finally finish off the Davion devils. This is a large reversal from the historical trend for the Federated Suns to be the hegemonic heroes of BattleTech, knights in shining BattleMechs come to liberate the Inner Sphere to a slightly less repressive autocratic system. That shining armor is dented and battered, but you do have to admire the spirit to keep kicking, screaming, and biting the whole way down no matter how awful things have gotten for them.

In terms of how their military is said to fight, there is a heavy focus on Autocannons, with nearly all Fed Suns specific mechs mounting at least one of them on their primary configuration. In addition, they are rich, well equipped, and for the longest time they were at the bleeding edge of Inner Sphere technology. While that technical lead has all but disappeared by IlClan, their love of autocannons refuses to be extinguished and they still have a large amount of former testbeds in their inventory deployed as line units.

You might want to play the Federated Suns in IlClan if you love autocannons, special munitions, and the fantasy of bitter resistance while surrounded by enemies looking to get even with you.

Availability

The Federated Suns availability is quite fun come IlClan. They have a large amount of faction specific units that are extremely strong under Core Rules. As the inventor of Precision and Armor-Piercing Autocannon ammunition, they maintain a large amount of basic and light autocannons in their mech inventory and can make fantastic use of those special ammo types. Their faction specific mechs, in particular the Templar, Templar III, Argus, and Inferno, are all priced to move and extremely mean for the cost. Faction specific variants are also strong with many of them originating as Civil War/Jihad era experiements that have reached mainline production, such as the Longbow 13NAIS, Thunderbolt 9Nr and 9NAIS, the Enforcer III NAIS, honestly anything with NAIS in the name.

There is a through-line with most of these mechs, which is BV efficiency. Davion mechs tend to come cheap. Autocannons are dirt cheap in BV and their faction specifics tend to be slow and extremely durable, which BV is kind to. In addition, they get access to the Blood Asp and Regent, two of the game's better Clan assault mechs for a given amount of BV. While you can go fast and light, the Davion Availability tends to push you towards their BV efficient 4/6 and 5/8 heavies and assaults. These mechs are extremely reliable and rugged, and this makes the faction fairly merciful to players of moderate experience. You have to make some positioning calls and deploy smartly due to that low speed, but you get enough armor per BV that it is unlikely you can make a minor mistake bad enough to be game ending.

Anyways, enough about the broad strokes, lets get specific and go over the list I built to bring as much Davion character and charm as possible while still putting your opponents in the ground.

The List

This Scale 4 list consists of the following mechs, all at 4/5 skills

  • Nightstar NSR-9FC, 1929 BV
  • JagerMech JM7-DD, 1690 BV
  • Axman AXM-4D, 1222 BV
  • Argus AGS-6F, 1524 BV
  • Thunderbolt TDR-9Nr, 1635 BV

This comes in at exactly 8000 BV on the dot, which pleases me. In addition, every mech here save the Thunderbolt is primarily armed with autocannons. They also all are faction specific to the Federated Suns in IlClan and aren't on any other major power's availability. The Nightstar and Thunderbolt do show up on Mercenary, but that is it. This is as Davion of a list as it is possible to build, and a list you genuinely can't build anywhere else.

Nightstar NSR-9FC

Nightstar. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Nightstar is, as you might know, one of Liberty's favorite mechs. While this is not the Nightstar he prefers, it is still an exceptionally strong assault mech. Moving 4/6, it carries an effective weapon load with 2 LB 10X autocannons, an ER PPC, and 3 medium lasers. With a solid firing pattern of "Everything but one laser", the 9FC is your centerpiece in this list, able to lean on its thick armor and sheer mass to bully smaller mechs to death while still coming in at less than 2000 BV. For deployment and use, identify the central objective and run towards it, drawing fire away from the rest of your list and outputting the same damage as an Awesome 9Q on a much faster, meaner frame.

For the LBs, you have way more than enough ammo for a normal game. Fire solid shot until their are open locations on your target, then swap to cluster to exploit the great big holes the rest of your list is opening up for you. This is your main crit-seeker and source of cluster hits, so make sure you are keeping that in mind and taking advantage of that flexibility.

JagerMech JM7-DD

JagerMech. Credit: Rockfish

Delta Delta here is a mech I have been absolutely in love with lately. Turning the usual JagerMech/Rifleman script on its head, the JagerMech is a tough, mean motherfucker of an all rounder. Mounting a pair of Clan UAC/10s as its primary weapons alongside a pair of LAC/5s and ER medium lasers, it outputs a genuinely fantastic amount of damage for the BV. While Ultra Autocannons are not terribly reliable, when they work they really work.

In addition, while the DD is full of ammo, CASE II goes a long way towards making that almost a non-issue. You also have enough ammo to carry both precision and AP in the LAC/5s, and you should. Having access to both ammo types is strong, and AP can absolutely save your ass if you come up against Hardened or Ballistic Reinforced armor.

For use, the DD wants to move up alongside the Nightstar and Axman, focusing its fire with them and generally being a max armor 70 tonner at your enemy. Normally a JagerMech would want to hang back and plink, but Delta Delta is a strong and independent fire support mech that doesn't need a frontline, it is the fucking frontline. The JagerMech as a chassis has a reputation as a poorly armored shitbot, and you can use that reputation to keep fire off of it as it pummels the enemy's frontline the fuck to death. It can also flip its arms and put UACs and Precision LACs behind it, which will make any enemy Fire Moths very, very sad.

Axman AXM-4D

Axman. Credit: Rockfish

The 4D is one of my two favorite Axmans, and I am a connoisseur of Axmans. Costing basically nothing and carrying quad LAC/5s, a pair of medium lasers, just enough ammo, and a targeting computer, the 4D is here to dispense 5 pointers and axe murder its way through the enemy's list. With a reasonable heat build of movement heat on an alpha, you do need to be careful around Infernos due to the single heat sinks that this mech carries. For 1222 BV it is almost impossible to get more mech though, and if you can bait correctly you can spread damage out between this, the JagerMech, and the Nightstar, keeping all 3 of them alive for much longer than their XL engines would otherwise imply.

In addition, if a mech has to stand on an objective or draw fire, the Axman is your main guy for that. It is tough enough to stand up to a Kodiak or other nightmare DPS machine for a couple turns and buy time for the rest of your list to hopefully pummel said nightmare mech the fuck to death. It is hard to trade down with a 4D, any heavy or assault mech you can trade its life for is going to be a positive value trade for you. You should be looking to trade it aggressively, and in all honesty AP ammo is great for this. Most opponents will get extremely scared when they start taking 4 TAC attempts a turn all at a -1 to hit bonus, so you should almost always be able to draw fire onto the 4D. Overall a solid piece of your frontline and one of the game's absolute best heavy mechs for the BV spent.

Argus AGS-6F

Argus. Credit: Rockfish

Another absolute all-star, the Argus 6F is part of an extremely elite club. It is one of the only 3 basic AC/20 mechs in the game that goes faster than 4/6, and a 5/8 AC/20 is a scary goddamn thing. It only competes with the Thunder 1L and a random Dragoon variant that only exists during the Star League Civil War era. Use case for the 6F is extremely simply. Load half the bins with Precision, the other half with AP, and run next to people and murder them. While your 3 4/6 mechs are pushing up the mid field to secure an objective, the Argus and Thunderbolt should be looking to do one of two things.

If there is a second objective you need to seize, the two of them should try to take it. If they run into trouble at that second objective, you have the mobility to flee back towards the main body of your force. Compared to many other list's flanking/second objective forces you will usually have a hell of a lot more mass than them to bully them off of an objective, and a Precision AC/20 acts as a phenomenal flyswatter for any Locust IICs, Fire Moths, or Phantoms that decide to come fuck with you.

If there isn't a viable second objective to fight over, the Argus and Thunderbolt just need to hang back a bit until the frontline engages and then run in to pull aggro and pull some nasty flanks, piling damage onto damaged targets and exploiting any back arcs they can find.

Thunderbolt TDR-9Nr

Thunderbolt. Credit: Rockfish

The perfect companion to the Argus, the 9Nr is another fantastic 5/8 Davion cavalry heavy. Mounting a large VSP laser as its main weapon, it backs that up with a trio of ER medium lasers, a Light PPC, and a Streak 6. It also carries a full EWAR suite with a Bloodhound probe and an ECM. There are also B-Pods in it so that you can clown on Battle Armor, which is a lot of fun.

The VSP and ER lasers are your main firing pattern, and it is safe to chuck the Streak on every once in a while if you are willing to go to 4/6 for a turn. You just want to follow along with the Argus and do all the same flanky things, but there is a slight difference with the Thunderbolt. If the enemy has ATMs or a large amount of Artemis missiles, you should make absolutely sure that you keep your entire list within 6 hexes of the Thunderbolt. That ECM can shave huge amounts of damage off of an ATM or Art Missile volley, and it will pay for itself rapidly if you can make use of it.

Gameplan

Bits and pieces of this have been scattered throughout the mech sections, but as a whole this list wants to use its mass and armor to bully your opponent. You will almost always be heavier, nastier, meaner, and more heavily armored than any given opposing force. Your damage up close is strong, but this list still has some substantial teeth at medium ranges and falls off gracefully. In general you would love to be able to make a big old ball of mech in the middle of the board and slam forwards, making tons of physical attacks and drowning them in nasty autocannon hits. Many missions though are going to have objectives spread across a map, and those can be a bit of a struggle. My advice for missions like that is that you can only really fight to contest two objectives, one with the 4/6s and one with the 5/8s.

If your opponent goes wide to every objective, you should have local superiority of firepower and mass on both of the objectives you are fighting over, letting you secure the points from both of those and try to make up the difference on the objectives you can't contest by murdering anything that comes close to you. You will frequently be outmanuevered but rarely outgunned, outnumbered, or outmassed. Do not be tempted to peel individual mechs off to do side objectives or anything, you need to stay together.

For Core Missions, you want to play the various Deathmatch missions (Ambush, Annihilation, Dominate, Extraction, Focal Point, and General Melee) by just making a big ball and running forwards into your enemy. The other missions are a little rougher for this list than one that has a heavier light mech presence, but the Thunderbolt's electronics and the fact that 3 out of your 5 mechs have hands should give you sufficient tools to play with. You want to, as mentioned, try to split into two groups to hold 2 objectives. You really can't afford to go wider than that. If you need to have a mech hang back on an objective away from the fight, the Axman is probably your best choice for that. It has decent enough range on its LACs and it is your cheapest unit. If there are objectives you can pick up and move, use the Thunderbolt to pull them over to the rest of your list..

Final Thoughts

The idea of this list was to use the unique, strong Davion exclusive mechs to go wide with heavy mechs and bully people. You can easily survive the death of any single one of your mechs and keep going, and nothing is an irreplaceable lynchpin. While you won't win a 1v1 with a Kodiak with anything in your list, you should never, ever be fighting a 1v1 if you can help it.

At Scale 4 the Fed Suns are a very strong faction in general, they have a huge amount of very good 1000-2000 BV mechs that do a whole hell of a lot for the price. Most factions need to concentrate BV in some more expensive units at Scale 4, and end up with some medium and light mechs to make up the difference. The Fed Suns have a very strong heavy mech spam archetype, and this is only one example. Building around the Templar III and Inferno is a very valid alternative, and you can get up to some really goofy shenanigans with an AC/10 maxxing strategy.

This list is fairly beginner friendly and simple to pilot, the main thing to keep in mind is that you absolutely need to keep your mechs together in at most 2 groups. Individually you are mediocre, but through the power of teamwork you can equal or surpass any opponent.

Also I just love autocannons so much. Shoot people with autocannons. It will do you a lot of good. Its therapeutic and good for the soul.
