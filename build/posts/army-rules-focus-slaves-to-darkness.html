Army Rules Focus: Slaves to Darkness

November 2nd, 2026 via Goonhammer

In this series of articles we take a deep dive into the Army Rules, or an Army of Renown, for a faction. We’ll cover the faction’s battle traits, spells, Manifestations, and upgrades as well as talk about how to build around that faction for competitive play. In this article, we’re covering the Army Rules for Slaves to Darkness.

Rules Overview

Slaves to Darkness were one of the earlier Battletomes added to the roster with the current edition, so their rules can be found readily available there or respectively in the Age of Sigmar app. All of the rules besides the seasonal Scourge of Aqshy rules are available in the tome; those can be found on the Warhammer Community website. I’ll be briefly covering those, but it’s worth mentioning that those may not be matched play legal in the future.

Battle Traits

There are two main parts to the battle traits for Slaves to Darkness (S2D): Pledges to Chaos and Eye of the Gods.

Let’s start by covering Pledges. These are the current iteration of “Marks of Chaos,” which is thematically showing each unit’s devotion to fight on behalf of a specific patron god, and being rewarded for their fervor. Once during each of your hero phases, you can select a single non-unique unit that does not already have a “Pledge to Chaos” keyword and give it one of the following abilities.

  • Pledge to Nurgle: The unit gains a 6+ ward save. If it already has one, gain a 5+ ward save.
  • Pledge to Khorne: The unit gains +1 attack to melee weapon profiles.
  • Pledge to Slaanesh: The unit gains +1 Dice to charge rolls, but must remove one dice of their choosing after the roll is made.
  • Pledge to Tzeentch: The weirdest one, fittingly. The unit gains a movement ability - Once per turn (army) in your movement phase, pick a point within 2D6 inches of your unit. Mark where that point is (this is not part of the rule, but you should do it), then pick up your unit and set it up wholly within 6” of that point and outside of 9” from enemy units.

These are all incredibly useful abilities across the entire army, and it’s important to remember that anything that isn’t a unique unit can access these, which has a lot of influence on the internal balance of the roster. There isn’t a strict priority or anything on these - we’ll cover use cases as they come up, but in general, it’s good to assume you want to try to get Khorne on your hammer units since it’s just going to be a significant damage increase on pretty much every damage unit in the army. Nurgle is great for units that already have a high wound density, like reinforced Chaos Warriors or even Ogroid Theridons. Slaanesh is interesting as it’s often more of a backup plan for when your other delivery methods fail - we’ll cover this down below when we talk about spells, but for now just treat this as a contingency plan, as it’s way better to have a unit actually succeed getting into combat and not have the Khorne pledge than the alternative. Tzeentch is great on screens or units that need a speed boost - note that it’s not a CORE ability or a Move ability, so you can pull shenanigans like using this after you move, or even using it to pseudo - retreat from combat as long as you don’t roll too low on the 2D6. This will most often be used later in games to give some more reach to your slower units or let your faster units zip across the board to a hard to snag objective.

The second part of the Army Rules is Eye of the Gods, which signifies your army leader’s attempts to earn the favor of the gods throughout the battle. During deployment, pick a single non-unique Warriors of Chaos or Darkoath hero (note this does rule out a few options, such as Ogroid Myrmidons, Centurion Marshalls, and Daemon Princes). For the rest of the battle, the unit gains Dark Apotheosis points for doing specific actions: D3 whenever they use a fight ability, and D3 at the end of each of your turns if they’re contesting an objective wholly outside of your territory.

When your selected unit reaches 8 or more Dark Apotheosis points, they can use the Dark Apotheosis ability, which is restricted to Once Per Battle (Army). They then can pick one of the following options:

  • Aura of Chaos: Heal all damage points allocated to them, then gain a 5+ ward for the rest of the battle.
  • Daemonhood: Ascend to a Daemon Prince - this has a bunch of extra stipulations, but in short, you replace the model with a Daemon Prince that gains any enhancements your original model had and must be assigned a Pledge of Chaos if the model it replaces didn’t have one. It’s worth mentioning that damage points don’t carry over to the Daemon Prince, so it effectively heals the model as well.

More often than not you’ll see this used for the Daemon Prince, but both options are solid. It’s not something that will come up naturally in every game as sometimes you just won’t hit the threshold due to dice rolls or fast combats, but it’s very handy to have when it comes up. Due to the requirements for how these points are earned, you’ll pretty much only see this on combat-oriented characters and often not on things like Sorcerer Lords.

Slaves to Darkness Chaos Lord. Credit: Loxi

Battle Formations

To be transparent, the battle formations for this army are not anything to write home about. Often you’ll be defaulting to one of them because it’s just what fits your army best, not necessarily something you’ll intentionally build around.

Godswrath Warband is what you’ll most commonly see for lists, as it’s pretty generally applicable even though it’s not super impactful. Once per turn at the end of your turn, pick an objective contested by any friendly units and put a Defiled token on it. Then do roll a D3 for each enemy unit contesting any objective that’s defiled. On a 2+, they take that many mortal wounds. It’s not a stellar ability or anything, but it’s basically just the free opportunity for extra mortals just for playing the game normally. Most importantly, it doesn’t require you to soup in anything with a Warriors of Chaos focused roster.

Darkoath Horde is arguably the best one, but is locked specifically to Darkoath heavy builds. Once per turn in your movement phase, you can activate this ability as long as you have a Darkoath hero on the battlefield. You can pay a command point to bring back a destroyed unit at half strength, wholly within 6” of a board edge outside of 3” of enemy units. This gives a ton of extra staying power to Darkoath, and ever since it became consistent (it previously was free to use, but only triggered on a dice roll) it’s become a staple of how you want to actually keep Darkoath on the board.=

Despoilers has been recently updated, and is now a very potent selection if you want to run a Daemon Prince. At the end of your turns, your Daemon Prince can pick a unit ww 12” to give them his Pledge to Chaos, replacing an existing one if they have one. This is a great way to boost your pledge economy and can make him a great pairing with anvil units like the new Aqshy Chaos Warriors, handing out Pledge to Nurgle without sacrificing damage elsewhere.

Legion of Chaos is the soup formation. While there is both at least one Warriors of Chaos hero and one Daemon or Darkoath hero on the battlefield, add 2 to the control scores of friendly Slaves to Darkness units contesting objectives wholly outside of your territory. This isn’t worth the formation slot, 2 control is just not enough to matter.

Heroic Traits

These are actually some decent options, all of them have a solid use case and will find a home in certain lists.

Favored of the Pantheon lets a unit start with 3 Dark Apotheosis points if you give it Eye of the Gods. Solid stuff, I’d say the least common of the three but it makes the threat of pulling a Daemon Prince out of nowhere much more real. If you aren’t sure what to take, this can be a safe and easy pick.

Deathmonger is my personal favorite. Gain the ability to fight a second time at strikes last, activated once per battle in the combat phase - just like Chosen and Varanguard. This can give a crazy amount of punching power to Ogroid Myrmidons, Karkadraks, and Lords on Daemonic Mounts. Basically any combat character can use this to make your turn that much stronger, and that’s a lot of what this army does well. It doesn’t always fit as well into brawler lists that don’t have as strong of an alpha strike component, but very often you’ll find a home for this.

Radiance of Dark Glory is the most important one to know about as it’s absolutely crucial to helping some lists function, but many other lists won’t want it. In ANY hero phase, pick each damaged friendly unit wholly within 12” of the holder. On a 3+, Heal (1) the target. If it’s a Monster, Heal (3) instead. This is essential to keeping big boys like Archaon and Belakor alive from shooting and chip damage, and combined with the occasional rally can make them absolute nightmares to remove. It costs 20 points at the time of writing, so you often won’t take this if you don’t have specific targets in mind for it, but I’d argue that every Archaon list and most Belakor lists want a dedicated caddy for this.

Artefacts

I’m going to be blunt - the core book artefacts for this faction are just not that great. The Scourge of Aqshy options are pretty great though.

Battletome

Conqueror’s Crown subtracts 5 from the control scores of infantry while in combat with the holder. 5 is at least a decent chunk, so if you think you’re going to be brawling around objectives it can be a good contingency if you aren’t killing what you get in combat with. Typically, I’d say we’d rather just be killing what we charge, but none of the options here really help in that department anyway.

Infernal Puppet gives you a once per battle ability you can activate at the start of any turn. Pick a visible enemy wizard within 24” of the holder. Each time they use a spell during that turn, inflict D3 damage points on them. This isn’t awful and there’s always the dream of killing a squishy 2-cast wizard with this alone, but most of the time this realistically just means “deal 1-2 D3 of damage to a wizard once per game” since most people won’t not cast the turn you pop this and will instead just burn a rally beforehand if their wizard is really at risk. Also, doing nothing into matchups where your opponent is wizard-less feels rough.

Realm Warper’s Twist Rune is a weird once per game ability that lets you pick a terrain feature within 12” - if it doesn’t have obscuring, it gains that. Then, roll a die for every model (friendly and enemy) within 1” of it, and it does a mortal on a 5+. This is interesting, to say the least. What I actually think the best use-case is for this is clearing out obnoxious infantry blocks, but it’s still pretty niche. You can technically use this to make something obscuring and then walk up to it safely during your movement phase, which is neat but likely not worth your artefact spot.

Scourge of Aqshy

Rune of Murder is a once per game ability activated in any combat phase. Pick a weapon and select one of the following options:

  • Add D6 attacks to the weapon until the end of the turn.
  • The unit takes 3 mortal damage, then double the attacks of the weapon.

This pairs incredibly well with Deathmonger to make pretty much any hero that wants to be in combat that much better at it, and will often be the default choice if you’re running a combat focused character with Deathmonger anyway.

Darkflame Pendant provides a passive ability that weapons targeting the unit have a maximum damage of 1. This is great as a survivability boost, and goes a long way on things like Daemon Princes that already have a higher amount of wounds and a ward save built in. It’s a good choice for other choices like Chaos Lords on Daemonic Mount as well, since they might not always be swinging first if they’re a secondary hammer with Knights.

Aelfskin Scroll is a once per battle reaction when an enemy wizard casts a spell within 18” of the holder. You can immediately cast a spell with a value equal to that roll without using up a power level. It’s a pretty nifty trick and has some good utility, but compared to the other options it’s a bit lackluster.

Brands of the Dark Gods (Scourge of Aqshy)

These are point-costed unit upgrades that can be put on one Non-Hero Infantry unit in your army.

Brand of Apoplexy is your most common option here, granting +1 damage to all weapons at 7 Fury levels. If you have any potential to farm fury in your list, which you should, this is going to bump a lot of extra damage out of a lot of units and has crazy synergy with Scourge of Aqshy Chaos Warriors.

Brand of Unbreakable Bonds is a once per battle ability that you can use in your movement phase to teleport your unit wholly within 3” of an objective you control and not in combat, but you can’t use charge abilities for the rest of the turn. Notably this can pull you out of combat. This is nice as a trick to get a unit out of a sticky spot, but often you’re wanting to be the one getting stuck in, so this sometimes feels a bit unnecessary. It can be a neat way to fling a large block of a durable unit up the board and teleport them back to the middle after to help hold down the fort.

Brand of The Unaligned lets you remove a unit’s pledge in your hero phase and select a new one, only once per battle for each pledge option. This only works in your hero phase, but there’s definitely utility to being able to swap between Khorne/Slaanesh  when you want to be aggressive and back to Nurgle when you know you’re going to take a punch. Do note that it has been FAQ'd so you cannot take this with an Ensorcelled Banner to get cheeky and take an ensorcelled banner of one god then switch to another to try and mix and match buffs.

Ensorcelled Banners

A unique system specifically for Slaves to Darkness, these are an additional enhancement you can attach to any unit that has at least one standard bearer, and the bonus applies as long as at least one of them is alive. There are 5 total options here - one for each Chaos god, and one representing Chaos Undivided. The god-specific options will give whatever unit you assign this to the respective Pledge, meaning you can have a unit start the game with a Pledge bonus if you choose to do so. This can be really useful if you have multiple units you want to get pledged early off in the game, so keep that in mind when assessing these.

The Dread Banner is the undivided option, and also what’s currently most commonly taken. Enemy units in combat with a unit that has this banner can’t use commands. Boom. Simple, but brutally effective. Even aside from all of the other commands that can end up throwing a wrench in your plans like Power Through, just being able to turn off All Out Attack/All Out Defence is a huge power spike for both hammer and anvil units alike. Pretty much anything that can take a banner will benefit from this, and while you don’t get the benefit of Pledge economy that you do from the other options, this opens up the flexibility to not be locked in to a specific Pledge from the start of the game.

The Banner of Rage is the Khorne option, giving the unit Pledged to Khorne as well as +1 to wound rolls in melee. If you have a unit you know you want to be juiced up with a Khorne pledge anyway, this isn’t a bad choice for very “full send” oriented lists that might want to mark a lot of things early. Often turning off all out defence for effectively -1 save on your opponents can pull more weight as it’s easier to get wound bonuses in the army than rend bonuses, but this is still a reasonable option for hammer units.

The Banner of Screaming Flesh is the Slaanesh option, giving the unit Pledged to Slaanesh as well as +1 attack to melee weapons if the unit charges. While this ends up amounting to making your unit less effective in combat than just taking The Banner of Rage, it does mean you can make a really effective self-sufficient unit. With Slaanesh helping secure better charges and still getting the +1 attack you’d be missing out on from Khorne, you can throw this banner on a unit you want to drive up a flank and give them little extra support. The most common use case for this is reinforced Knights, as they can rip things up pretty well with this and nothing more.

The Eroding Icon is the Nurgle choice here, granting Pledged to Nurgle as well as making all critical hits against the unit count as normal hits instead. Ignoring Crit (Mortal) is really devastating for some units, so often this is something you take as a meta call when there are a lot of scary threats that have critical abilities. Beyond that, Nurgle is quite a good pledge to start with if you have a big reinforced anvil of some kind, as it means you can still get your combat bonuses on other things without missing out on this early.

The Blasted Standard is the Tzeentch banner, and by far the most niche one. It grants Pledged to Tzeentch as well as a 4+ ward against shooting attacks. You don’t often want big units stuck on Tzeentch from the start of the game, so this is really reserved for scenarios where shooting is a serious problem for your important stuff.

Slaves to Darkness Chaos Lord. Credit: Loxi

Spell Lore

Our spell lore is categorized by having some very powerful spells with pretty restricted casting ranges. Everything has a 12” range, so be ready to get pretty close to what you want to target.

Spite-Tongue Curse is our unlimited spell, it casts on a 5 which is actually really consistent. Pick an enemy within 12”, then make your casting roll. If it’s successful, 3 mortals to the target, no extra dice rolls needed. If it’s unsuccessful or unbound, the caster takes D3 mortals. This spell is alright, in some matchups the unbinds can hurt quite a bit and really end up adding up in chip damage. The plus side is that it’s a very consistent mortal output spell, and even though the range is short, it’s one of the few ways we have to project damage outside of melee range. It’s not the reason you bring wizards, but it’s helpful to have in your pocket.

Daemonic Speed is your bread and butter buff spell, and one of the huge benefits to taking a wizard here. Casting on a 7, target a unit wholly within 12” of the caster. If successful, add 1 to the number of dice rolled when making charge rolls. This is the juice. This is how you can more reliably deliver things like Chosen, Theridons, and other things that are a bit slower than the rest of your force exactly where you need them. You can also use this to just give your already fast units a truly absurd threat range.

Binding Damnation is your last trick, casting on a 7. Select an enemy unit within 12”, and if the cast is successful they get Strikes Last until the start of your next turn. Powerful, but as with the other spells here, the range means you do have to be reasonably close to get this off. That being said, if you’re casting this on your own turn and don’t take a double, this is two full combats worth of Strike Last goodness. This is a combat focused army, so being able to mess with combat order can be great - the biggest issue here being that unlike other similar combat tricks, this happens so early in the turn that you won’t really be using this unless you’re already pretty stuck in.

Manifestations

Friends, I have terrible news here. Manifestations of Malevolence, the faction-specific manifestations for S2D, are just really bad. You gain an inconsistent shooting attack that can’t even shoot the turn you place it because the range is too short, an extremely conditional and finicky attack bonus aura, and a mediocre hoard clearing piece. Unless your local meta is just loaded with chaff lists, these are absolutely not worth considering at the time of writing.

There is great news however - we make fantastic use of the other manifestation lores. While some of them cost points to include, these costs are absolutely worth it and should pretty much always be prioritized over the default faction choice.

Forbidden Power is often the default choice, as it’s extremely useful across the board. Soulscream bridge gives us some much needed mobility for our infantry, and paired with Pledge to Slaanesh or, better yet, Daemonic Speed, this can give some serious reach to units that are stuck with slower movement. Shards of Valagharr provides some great backfield screening, and Horrorghast can give a little extra shooting punch to a roster that doesn’t really have much of that. Lauchon isn’t always the most useful, but it can be handy to have in a pinch.

Primal Energy is a great pick if you’re relying on any of the centerpiece unique characters or if you aren’t really going to utilize Bridge as well from Forbidden Power. The healing is great for our monster heroes from Emerald Lifeswarm, and Jaws is always handy for extra screening and mortal damage output.

Aetherwrought Machineries is good if you’re really strapped for points or running Archaeon, as he makes fantastic use of the -1 to hit from Chronomatic Cogs. Pendulum is handy for creating space on the board, and Quicksilver Swords can be some helpful damage for assassinating targets relying on ward saves.

Twilit Sorceries is a mixed bag for us: we already have a great way to turn off commands in The Dread Banner, and protection from shooting isn’t as important for an army that plans to close the gap very fast anyway. That being said, Geminids is nice if you aren’t taking The Dread Banner, and the Umbral Spellportal can be quite helpful to mitigate our generally lower ranges for spells.

Krondspine Incarnate and Morbid Conjuration both are fine in their own rights, but don’t bring as much to the table that the other lores offer. We don’t really need more help in the fighting/damage departments, it’s getting there and staying there that is often more helpful to gain from these.

Faction Terrain

The Nexus Chaotic is quite a contentious piece of faction terrain, as it’s not bad by any means, but it’s also completely non-essential for the faction to function. 12 wounds on a 4+ save with a pretty big footprint is pretty typical, but it does mean it won’t die to chip damage and often does require at least some degree of commitment to kill. In each of your hero phases, you can roll a die and add that many power points to it, up to a maximum of 12. You can then spend those points on the following abilities:

Infernal Sorcery costs 3 points, and gives a friendly Slaves to Darkness wizard within 3” of the terrain feature +1 to casting rolls for the turn. Great for setting up Bridge/Daemonic Speed on early turns, and even better on 2-cast characters like Archaon, Belakor, and the Gaunt Summoner.

Corrupt the Realms costs 9 points, and requires you to select an objective or terrain feature within 24” of the Nexus. Any number of enemies within 3” are the targets. Roll a D3 for each unit, and on a 2+ deal that many mortals. This is fine, it’s basically free damage to midfield targets that will happen semi-randomly since you can’t really always rely on having the power points.

If you’re able to make use of both of these, it’s a solid piece and definitely worth deploying. You won’t really be getting the casting bonus after the first turn or two in most matchups, but setting up your early spells better is pretty handy, even though it’s not 100% reliable. The mortal wounds can be helpful, but it’s not often going to make or break your gameplan. As a good rule of thumb, if your opponent has a ton of teleporting/high mobility shenanigans and you aren’t making use of the casting bonus, you can consider leaving this off the board to not give a stationary target for people to charge off. It’s 0 opportunity cost to bring in a list though, so it’s worth always packing it.

Chaos Warriors. Credit: SRM

Tricks and Combos

There are a whole ton of tricks and synergies to cover, so I’m just going to rapid fire mention a bunch of them. Keep in mind this is not a comprehensive list of every little thing with the army - these are just touching on some of the topics covered above.

  • Sequencing your abilities is one of the most important things you can do here. Always try to cast Daemonic Speed and/or Soulscream Bridge before you assign your Pledge to Chaos - if you successfully get one of these buffs on a unit, you can opt for something like Pledge to Khorne to get more damage, but if you’re unsuccessful and still have a sketchy charge, you can instead choose Pledge to Slaanesh to make sure they still get in.
  • Magical Intervention is a very useful tool here: setting up Soulscream bridge can be handy, and even if your opponent wastes time walking up to destroy it, that can often be just as helpful to close the gap more.
  • This is more generic to Soulscream Bridge, but for those unaware it can be very useful for movement blocking, as you can move through manifestations but can’t end up on top of them, so it can take up a lot of space on a unit’s flank to make charges awkward.
  • Varangard can’t have a standard bearer, so if you’re running them and another big hammer unit that you want to send out early, having a banner that conveys a Pledge to Chaos can be invaluable as you really want to make sure Varangard are marked when they go to fight.
  • Your Daemon Princes that come into play via Dark Apotheosis get to pick if they have wings for FLY and +2” movement or a Trophy Rack for +1 rend against heroes. Don’t sleep on the trophy rack here, a Nurgle Daemon Prince with a Trophy Rack can brawl with a lot of tough cookies.
  • Radiance of Dark Glory isn’t that big of an aura - 12” can be finicky if you’re trying to heal something with a big base like Archaon. To help with this, try to put it on something else with a big base if possible - Chaos Lord on Daemonic Mount and even the Centurion Marshall are good choices for this purpose alone.
  • The Dread Banner often makes a lot of sense to put on a unit with a lot of staying power like Reinforced Chaos Warriors if you’re playing a more traditional “hammer and anvil” playstyle, as your Hammers will frequently be fighting near them anyway.
