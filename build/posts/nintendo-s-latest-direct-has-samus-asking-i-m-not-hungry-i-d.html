Nintendo's Latest Direct Has Samus Asking: I'm Not Hungry? I Don't Take a Slice?

September 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

As we draw closer to the end of the year, that means it is time to start winding down on 2026 gaming news for upcoming titles and start looking forward to the nebulous "2027" release dates, which means it is time once again for a Nintendo Direct recap! This time, Nintendo primarily spent the day trying to sell the Switch 2 as a thing you absolutely need to have or upgrade to, and... maybe did an okay-ish job of doing so, depending on what you're looking for. The console gaming market is looking pretty dire overall, with Sony and Xbox racing to the bottom of a "Duck Season - Rabbit Season - Fire!" routine, but Nintendo has also spent the year spinning their wheels and occasionally sticking their own hands into them. If you missed the Directs (yes, two of them), then we've got the recap to help you on what news you can use for your gaming purchases; we won't be covering everything, so if you want to see the entire directs, we will link both of them here, and focus instead on the things that primarily caught our attention or felt like the biggest news of the day.

Edit 9/10: This article has been updated to include coverage of Level-5's AI made direct, as relevant to discussion of the Professor Layton game below.

Legend of Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct

The first direct actually aired the day before, and was devoted entirely to Legend of Zelda. You probably saw the memes and reactions already, but if you didn't, Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time's remake was shown off and looks, uhm... unique? The game has taken a direction that hews especially close to the artistic vision of the directors and stays in line with Zelda's often artistically leaning aesthetic directions, but frankly the game just looks glistening and weird. Link, Zelda, and Ganondorf all look terrible, and I have to admit that I was already kind of on the fence of picking up the remake myself, with this pushing me pretty firmly into "no". Your opinion may vary, so take a look at the trailer yourself and develop your own opinion.

Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity

Age of Calamity didn't really do very well on Switch, but perhaps this time, on Switch 2, things will be different...! Well, it does look better, and if you own the original, an update will be available instead of having to buy the entire game over again. I'm likely going to pass, as even though I enjoy Dynasty Warriors/Musou Games, I didn't really vibe with the Hyrule Warriors games. If you're interested, Age of Calamity Definitive Edition will release on Feb. 25th of 2027.

Nintendo Direct 9/9/26

The other Direct was yesterday's Direct on 9/9, which showed off some of the above Zelda material, as well as a pretty large host of games, with some cool surprises. Overall, I would say the Direct had a somewhat mixed bag in terms of selling the Switch 2; there were some cool games that we'll get to, but nothing that has really been knocking it out of the park in terms of continuing the idea that the Switch 2 is a necessary upgrade from the Switch, except through the fact that less and less games are getting released across both platforms. Since the Switch 2 also has (or attempts to have) parity with other consoles and PCs, there are a lot more multi platform games here that are providing a similar problem that Sony and Xbox are facing, namely, "why do I need this if I have a PC?"

Meccha Chameleon

Developer: Lemorion

Platforms: PC, Switch 2

Release Date: Now!

Meccha Chameleon was all the rage on Twitch a few months ago, racking up a huge amount of sales on Steam as the big flavor of the month game for large friend groups. Essentially hide and seek, Meccha Chameleon tasks players with hiding in various locations and using a fairly robust painting mechanism to try and blend in with the environment. Players accrue points for how long they can hide in view of the hunters, and hunters win by tracking down all of the hiding players. It is very similar to Prop Hunt, and the Switch 2 release was quite a surprise; if you have a large enough friend group of Switch 2 players, this game can be a really fun party game, but you may have better luck on PC.

Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave

Developer: Intelligent Systems

Platforms: Switch 2

Release Date: September 17

Fire Emblem returns with another huge "main system" installment, following up on 2019's Three Houses and 2023's Engage. Fortune's Weave has players taking on one of four protagonists: Cai, Theodora, Dietrich, or Leda, but don't worry: you still get to create your own avatar (Default named Eshmel) that you can select the gender of before the game begins. Fire Emblem has always been a strong tactical RPG game, and Fortune's Weave is kind of interesting in that it seems to be reviving dungeons, a feature that hasn't been in the main series since Shadows of Valentina. Fire Emblem really tends to attract people for two reasons: the fun rock-paper-scissors strategy combat, and shipping characters together to see what happens. In both cases, I'm ready to play when it comes out next week.

Professor Layton and the New World of Steam

Developer: Level-5

Platforms: Switch, Switch 2

Release Date: Dec. 10, 2026

This one breaks my heart for a simple reason: Developer Level-5 has gone HEAVILY into supporting, promoting, and using AI in their games. Unlike a lot of other nebulous examples, Level-5 also (unhelpfully?) continues to publish their examples for doing so, such as in character art, background art, concept art, programming, and more. So, realistically, I have a hard time supporting their games, which makes the announcement of a new Professor Layton game even more painful for me. Layton is one of my all time favorite series, one that I've bought and played every game in the series for, watched and read just about anything else I can get my hands on from it, and even own a Professor Layton Revoltech that game out years ago. Sadly, while some of this looks great and a return to form, I will need to know what and how AI was used before I can purchase it. The game releases on December 10th, so hopefully we'll find out (and maybe the answer will be it won't have been used, but... not really crossing my fingers). Oh, they also said that Yo-Kai Watch 2: Haunted Domain is in development, if you even care.

Edit 9/10: Man.

So, ignore all of the above. Today, Level-5 held a completely AI made "direct" of their own to talk about the future of the company and show off their CEO's delusional obsession with AI, which was illustrated primarily through the soulless AI created and animated avatar of him that spoke throughout the entire presentation. If you think I'm being hyperbolic, here are some stills of what it looked like:

Anyway, as you can see, there's really no hope for any Level-5 games to be unaffected by CEO Akihiro Hino's obsession with AI, and for that reason, you can expect to never see games by Level-5 covered here. It is incredibly disappointing to have to trash Professor Layton, Yo-Kai Watch, and at least personally, Inazuma Eleven, but if you can't even guarantee that your flagship series gets some treatment that doesn't have weird AI mish-mash spellings like BIG FEERY in it, what are we even doing here, man?

You'd think as the maker of Inazuma Eleven, one of THE biggest franchises in Japan, you'd try to ensure all the text actually made sense and not INAZIMA BIG FEERY.

This future sucks man.

Onyx: The Dark Grip

Developer: Bloober Team

Platforms: Switch 2

Release Date: 2027

After Silent Hill 2 Remake, I'm willing to give Bloober a bit more of a benefit of the doubt with games, and their new game Onyx certainly seems interesting. However, the game is being touted as "unplayable" on a television, as it requires use of the touchscreen and possibly motion controls that sucked me out of what otherwise looks like a cool horror puzzle game. I do like the aesthetics and I really do want to give the game a shot, so I hope that the touch and motion mechanics are maybe optional or not that intrusive; while I know these are key tenets of Nintendo systems lately, I've always hated them and find them kind of uncomfortable. We have a while to find out, since Onyx is slated for release some time in 2027.

Ondeh Ondeh: Kaya's Tasty Tale

Developer: Metronomik

Platforms: PS5, Xbox Series X/S, PC, Switch 2

Release Date: 2027

A rhythm platformer, Ondeh Ondeh caught my eye for that reason and the fun aesthetics, and I'm pretty excited to give it a shot when it releases in 2027. Ondeh ondeh is a dish from Southeast Asia, and the game has a lot of that influence present in the presentation overall. It was one of the more original standouts in the presentation, with lots of vibrant colors and an exciting looking world to explore to the music. Also, this one is multiplatform, so almost anyone can pick it up when it releases in 2027.

Metroid Ravenous

Developer: Nintendo

Platforms: Switch 2

Release Date: January 28, 2027

I initially wanted to call today's article "Metroid Prime Vore" but was told no. Anyway, Samus returns in a new 2D game that has her hungry for action as she devours her enemies along the way. If the latest 3D Metroid didn't excite you or interest you, this one seems to be far more along the traditional lines of classic Metroid games, particularly Fusion. The new mechanic presented in Ravenous has to do with the titular adjective: Samus absorbs her enemies powers, adding them to her own arsenal, and in her own words, "It's eat, or be eaten." Judging by the reaction on my personal timeline, everyone has been extremely normal about this.

Dangan Ronpa 2x2

Developer: Spike Chunsoft

Platforms: PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 1 and 2, PC

Release Date: January 14, 2027

Dangan Ronpa 2x2 is back, bringing the original Dangan Ronpa 2 kicking and screaming into modernity by also teasing new story paths and gameplay experiences that weren't included in the original, including a brand new character who seems to be a potential new protagonist. If you're not familiar with Dangan Ronpa, the game is essentially a murder mystery and trial game with absolutely bonkers scenarios and a fixation on "Despair" and "Hope" as two driving themes in all of the games. If you've never played them, they can be a lot of fun to give a try, although I'd probably just recommend The Hundred Line: Last Defense Academy.

Romancing Saga 3

Developer: SquareEnix

Platforms: PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 1 and 2, Steam

Release Date: February 16, 2027

This is probably the biggest surprise for me, and I'm honestly so excited to get to play Romancing Saga again. If you're unfamiliar with the Romancing Saga series, they're sort of like a pervert's final fantasy, in that they have really strange systems, sprawling stories, very unique characters, and a flavor all their own that have mostly been totally impenetrable to Western markets since release; Romancing SaGa 3 came out in 1995, for example, only getting a Worldwide release in 2010. SaGa was a spin-off of Final Fantasy II, which explains why it has so many seemingly esoteric choices in how the games play and how things work; in fact, the very first games in the series were released on Gameboy as "The Final Fantasy Legend" series, which did actually get released in the West. You may also have played SaGa Frontier and SaGa Frontier 2 on PSOne, all of which were strange, weird games. I love SaGa, and I really recommend giving Romancing SaGa 3 a chance when it releases next year; unlike many series, the remasters have all been very good.

Atelier Karia: The Night Kingdom & the Guide of Memories

Developer: Gust

Platforms: PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, Steam

Release Date: February 25, 2027

Another series for RPG perverts, the Atelier series is a long-storied one in which players are tasked with combining turn-based combat with business management and collection sims to work through an overarching story. Atelier Karia has received a lot of attention recently from a survey in which developer Gust asked players if they would like Karia to get chubby if she ate too much, which resulted in an overwhelming response of "Yes" (80%), proving that good things are actually possible in gaming. Unlike the SaGa series, Atelier has had a pretty wide release in the West, with the latest titles helping the game breach larger audiences thanks to releases on Steam. If you like the idea of a sim-rpg with combat and colorful characters, the Atelier series will fix you right up; if you want to dip your toes into it, consider trying the Ryza series that preceded Karia.

The Apothecary Diaries: The False Imperial Brother

Developer: Gust

Platforms: PS5, Switch 1 and 2, Steam

Release Date: 2027 (Q1)

Speaking of Gust, they also showed off a trailer for their game adaptation of hit period drama and mystery series The Apothecary Diaries, which published originally as a series of Light Novels in Japan before spawning a manga, anime, and live action set of adaptations. Gust makes a lot of sense for a developer to work with Apothecary Diaries; as I mentioned above, they have a big history with simulation and exploration games that are mixed with great stories, and Apothecary Diaries is all about protagonist Maomao's ability to gather and create medicinal solutions or discern the root cause of all sorts of palace intrigue through her mastery of poison and other materials, all while being vexed by the beautiful eunuch Jinshi, who she definitely does NOT have feelings for.

Kirby and the World Beyond

Developer: HAL Laboratory

Platforms: Switch 2

Release Date: Spring 2027

Closing out with the other Nintendo character known for eating people, Kirby is back with a brand new adventure in Kirby and the World Beyond! The newest mainline adventure game brings about Kirby's 30th anniversary of sucking up enemies and stealing their powers, all while exploring cute worlds that also somehow contain horrific secrets and implications. Kirby's newest game seems to follow that tradition, as the lovable and seemingly unbothered cute pink ball is pulled once again into a possibly universe shattering adventure with new characters and a big 3D world to explore, and the trailer shows Kirby running into a new character who he helps by literally cleaving the fake sky entrapping him and others to reveal the world beyond it. I'm absolutely here for it and will be seated in 2027.

Wrap Up...?

There were a lot of other notes and updates in the Direct, namely that Witcher 3 is coming with the updates promised to other platforms, Kingdom Come Deliverance II is getting a Switch release, Monster Hunter Wilds is also on the way with DLC included, and much more. If you'd like to read the nitty-gritty on all of the things announced, you can head over to Nintendo's site and check out the full list of smaller titles and updates, but, in Nintendo Fashion, I have one more trailer I wanted to shout out...

Torneko's Mystery Dungeon Classic HD is out NOW.

Developer: Square Enix

Platforms: PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 1 and 2, Steam

Release Date: NOW.

Are you someone who likes the term "Roguelike?" Perhaps you've played various "Mystery Dungeon" games from the likes of Pokémon or series progenitor Shiren the Wanderer? Well, Dragon Quest IV's Torneko is here to explore his own adventure into these styles of games with a game that absolutely vexed a younger Marcy. I absolutely LOVED this frustrating and difficult game's sequel, Torneko: The Last Hope when I played it on the PSOne back in 2000, and although I've played the original since, I had to say this was my favorite announcement of the day, primarily because Romancing SaGa 3 won't be out till next year, and this is out right now. Having already downloaded and played it a bit since the release, I'll tell you that you need to remember how a game from 1993 is going to play, which is to say, slowly and kind of clunky, and I absolutely love it for that. If you want to try a game off my recommendation today, grab yourself a copy of Torneko's Mystery Dungeon. You might regret it at first, but I bet you'll keep playing it till you beat it.
