Magic: The Gathering Star Trek Review, Part 1 of 4 - The Mechanics

November 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Space, the final frontier. These are the voyages of the starship Enterprise...as well as around 34 other new Spacecraft. Our continuing mission: To explore in detail the ramifications of newly-printed cardboard; to speculate upon their effects on formats such as Commander, Standard, & Modern; to boldly joke about the 43 new Creature types printed in this set unlikely ever to be seen again! Yes, Star Trek has arrived to Magic, and with it a host of new cards sure to shake things up. Most impactful is the full realization of Spacecraft as an Artifact subtype, more than doubling the number thusly printed. This week, we're covering the set's mechanics, with thrilling new abilities like a new spin on Flashback, and 'Creature types matter'. Jokes aside, many of the mechanics are quite dense for what is ostensibly a new-player-friendly set!

Star Trek officially releases to tabletop on November 13th, and on Magic: The Gathering Arena on November 10th.

New Mechanics

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Beam Me Up

Beam me up is a new spin on Flashback, appearing on Sorceries as an alternative cost. By returning a Creature you control to the hand as part of the cost, you can cast the Spell from your graveyard, with it being exiled as it leaves the stack. Importantly, like Web-slinging, the Creature is returned as soon as the Spell hits the stack, meaning this mechanic can be used as a way to protect or reset bodies without fear of interference.

Saffgor: Importantly, no Instant was printed with this mechanic in the set, and that's for good reason—if you could cast any of these in response to a removal Spell, they're essentially protection from the yard. That means they have a massive upside in any deck which can enable Flash, or a dedicated Scotty, Miracle Worker Commander deck using Instants. Including a couple of these in your list also makes you far less vulnerable to cards like Darksteel Mutation, with an inbuilt way to 'reset' your neutered Commander, and it appearing across all five colors introduces self-bounce to archetypes which would otherwise lack it. Neat stuff, though the name could have easily just been 'Beam Up' to make it readily reprintable.

BPhillipYork: I don't really like mechanics where what is happening doesn't really make any sense, sort of logically. In terms of balance this is just a flashback variant, it is decently set up so that you can build around it with creatures that get a lot of value from their ETB, or do weirder things like steal creatures then return them to your opponents hands somehow. Returning a creature to your hand is perfectly in synch with "beam me up" but then getting extra value from a spell is... weird? Anyway if you can get flash, or run Scotty, Miracle Worker this can get pretty abusive as a way to dodge removal, and all in all it's a fun mechanically slightly different flashback.

FromTheShire: We love a good flashback spell, and tacking on a way to bounce a creature back to your hand is very solid with the current state of enters triggers. I agree it's a bummer it got printed with a very parasitic name though.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dilemmas

Dilemmas are a codified game action like Crimes, and you face a Dilemma when you choose one or more modes for a Spell or ability. That boils down to cards which have the 'Choose one —' syntax, followed by a bulleted list, but you can see a deeper breakdown in our article on Dilemmas from last week. Triggers that occur when you face a Dilemma go on the stack as soon as the modes are selected, above the triggering Spell or ability.

Saffgor: Dilemmas are substantially harder to face repeatedly compared to Crimes, but the fact many of their triggers lack guardrails like 'once per turn' does cause one to wonder. I've unironically been considering Pyramids in the big '26, as a mana outlet that faces a Dilemma, so I hope they print some more repeatable triggers soon. There's just not a ton of cards in the set which want you to face Dilemmas, and they're all either 5c or within the Blue and/or Black color identity; this means you're restricted from really paying off mass dilemmas unless you choose one of 3-4 middling Commanders.

BPhillipYork: I really like the idea of a trigger off of modal spells, because I think modal spells are neat. With only 10 dilemmas unless you are building around one of the two legendaries, though Captain Benjamin Sisko is actually pretty abusable and is easily the default dilemma commander. I hope they keep this as a deciduous or evergreen mechanic since I think modal spells help ease some of the built in frustration of magic, and it's a solid but balanced mechanic with a lot of potential design space.

FromTheShire: I'll be honest, I would have sworn this was already a keyword that existed. I'm sure I was just mashing up Riku of Many Paths and Villainous Choices in my head, but yeah, this is an easy space to fill. Thankfully it also doesn't have a Star Trek specific name so we can actually see this get expanded in the future. While there isn't a ton of support currently, this is one of those mechanics that has the potential to spike prices and become a whole thing years down the road.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Assimilate

Assimilate targets a Creature card in a specified zone, and moves them to your battlefield as a Borg Artifact Creature that enters/gains a +1/+1 counter, losing all other creature types. Recall that this all occurs on Layer 4, as type-changing effects, with the Assimilated quality being a single effect that alters both type and subtype. Assimilate cards in the set do so from the opposing battlefield, graveyard, and even exile, meaning resistance to the mechanic really is rather futile.

Saffgor: When I see a type-changing effect I know I'm in for a treat, being the resident Princess Yue guy here on Tabletop Battles. Assimilate notably only overwrites solely subtypes, and not supertypes, meaning if a card is Legendary it remains Legendary, or Land (e.g. Dryad Arbor) remaining a Land...Artifact Creature — Forest Borg. There's not near enough of these cards printed to concoct a deck which abuses the mechanical intricacies to the fullest, but even so we can get up to a bit of nonsense in turning things into Artifacts, or effects with specify 'non-[blank]' Creatures. There's been a few sets like Eldraine which care about non-Humans, and Assimilating something robs it of its humanity, so keep that in mind.

BPhillipYork: This is a neat way of handling the Borg, I still sort of wish they'd have a more Sliver like effect, or else something like Plague Rats. Nonetheless it's potentially a really strong reanimation type effect.

FromTheShire: Coming back from the dead as a stronger, mechanical, no longer human creature? Seems to capture the vibe of being Borgified to me. Unless you want to mess around with creature types there's nothing crazy here, and even if you do I'm not sure the juice is worth the squeeze, but at least you have the option.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

New Ability Words (Federation, Strange New Worlds, Battlefront, & Seek Out)

There's a host of new ability words appearing across the main & Commander sets, which delineate things such as drawing your second card, the number of differently named Lands you control, and more. Ability words don't affect gameplay, but do help guide players towards their specific payoff or theme, with Landfall being the gold standard for this text type.

Saffgor: Calling out differently-named Lands in the same set as a new type of Land token are introduced sure is a choice. Federation rewards you for tracking types in a way that flavorwise is a bit weird in Magic, where something like a Human Wizard Artificer is three types, and therefore the 'type=alien species' thing breaks down. That said, flavor is free, and I'm totally fine with them building on these shared conditions as the baseline of a set.

BPhillipYork: Federation is neat, though it's a bit strange that creature types like Klingon, Romulan, and Cardassian benefit Federation; I think part of the vision of Star Trek was that it wouldn't end up being a polar world with a continuum from good to bad. I think they did a good job with Federation matters and the Borg sub-type, and I think the way Federation works, benefiting from diversity essentially, is an example of a mechanic that fits the lore really well. I do think it's sad that they fixed the Werewolf Pack Leader thing. If you take that many steps you should be able to abuse Federation. Strange New Worlds to me really seems like a huge buff to green, which is the color best at ramping lands, and due to meta reasons it's already allowed a sort of unrestricted ramp that other colors are often not allowed. So far most of the cards that key off of it are pretty tame, but if they put too many ramp or extra land abilities keyed off of it it has the potential to get broken fast. Battlefront is an ability I really like, it encourages an active interactive game. It also pairs dangerous well with melee and myriad, and red+ decks running lots of these keywords will get aggressive really fast and be in your face, chipping down life totals very effectively. Which is good. Games should end. Seek Out is mostly a win more ability, though for red it's fairly easy to get additional draw that includes some discard, or else just run blue and draw a lot. However, I think Seek Out is a bit dangerous in that it benefits you even more from doing something that you basically want to do. It'll get pretty gross if you're able to trigger it on every turn, though like Ro Laren, Double Agent, you're dealing like 8 damage per cycle of turns, which isn't that impactful. I do think it's kind of interesting that every Seek Out card is a legendary creature.

Returning Mechanics

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Spacecraft & Station

Spacecraft is an Artifact subtype that allows you to tap Creatures you control as an activated ability to funnel their power into Charge counters, gaining cumulative effects based on the number of Charge counters accrued. Notably, Legendary Spacecraft (much like Vehicles) are legal as Commanders, something that we touched upon in last week's Commander Focus on the H.M.S. Bounty.

Saffgor: I've been in love with Spacecraft since the moment they debuted, having a Monogreen Spacecraft-matter deck in paper and now one helmed by the genuine article on the way (Happy ending, I bought the Bounty deck in paper). Spacecraft just succeed on every level, from flavor to function, and make for a compelling way to leverage tempo now for evasive power later. They play well, and it's the only reason I was remotely excited for Star Trek prior to its reveal. I'm glad that hype was paid off in full!

BPhillipYork: I feel like they did the whole Edge of Eternities set and the Spacecraft and Station abilities in order to make this set more palatable. Honestly. Nonetheless, these are fine and interesting mechanics, especially the way they dovetail with Commander, spaceship commanders ability to not be creatures then become creatures is really neat. I do think officers should be able to station better or something like that, missed opportunity.

FromTheShire: Behold the power of our fully operational subtype! I'm still not stoked about these existing in Magic from a flavor perspective, but I think they have done a great job with them mechanically and as expected we're getting a bunch of them here.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Planets & Land tokens

Planets originated as a cycle of 5 cards in Edge of Eternity as Lands with the ability to be Stationed, and return in Star Trek as...just another Land subtype, like Cave or Desert. Many cards in the set create Planet tokens instead of ramping normally, a new way to enable Landfall & token strategies, and pull upon a few of the Planet-matter cards in the set. Of note, the only Planets to have existed prior are the EOE cycle, and Planar Nexus.

Saffgor: A lot of the subtype's uniqueness has been robbed from it by not making these new Planets Station-able, but I get the concern over complexity. Landfall triggers are easier than ever now, and with quite a few of these Planet token producers being exceptionally playable, I think it's something we'll see a shift towards moving forward. After all, no searching means no shuffling, and that leads to better game pace.

BPhillipYork: These are also like, invented for this set and released in Edge of Eternities to keep the bitching about Universes Beyond to a minimum. Which is fine. They're fine. They won't be overly impactful in this set, planet tokens aren't overly useful with Strange new worlds since they are all named New Planet. Still they are kind of fun.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Quests

A new cycle of quests arrive in Star Trek, originating in Zendikar and returning sporadically in a few recent Universes Beyond sets. With this new cycle, the number of Quest counter payoffs numbers 38, about as many Spacecraft that exist, so building around them feels within reach.

Saffgor: While I don't own it in paper, I do very often break out a Moira Brown, Guide Author deck online that's shockingly competent. With literally every release of new Quests, she becomes a better Commander, and frankly she was already pretty much there post-Avatar. Great to see these return as the 'heroic' counterpart to Marvel's Schemes.

BPhillipYork: This ability seems like a logical pairing given how much of Star Trek is about exploring strange new worlds and finding cool stuff.
