The Lore Explainer: Armageddon, Part 1 - The Planet

May 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In our Lore Explainer series we take a deep look at the lore of various games, settings, and factions. In this article series we're taking a deep dive into the lore of Armageddon, the Imperium's most fought-over planet. In this first article we're looking at the planet itself.

With the launch of Warhammer 40,000 11th edition we're going back to Armageddon for a fifth war, this time with Ghazgkhull returning to the planet for a third whack at conquering it. In terms of planets in the 40k universe, Armageddon has seen more action than any other, including Cadia, with massive assaults from both Orks and Chaos and a history stretching back well before the Horus Heresy.

Welcome to Armageddon

Credit: Games Workshop

Armageddon is a Hive World in the Armageddon Sector of Segmentum Solar. It's possibly the most famous Hive World in 40k lore, with only Necromunda giving it a run for its money. Armageddon acts as the industrial hub of the surrounding sector and several well known patterns of vehicles and weapons are manufactured there. Unfortunately it turns out that tens of thousands of years of industrial activity has polluted the planets oceans and turned its surface into a blazing, barren, irradiated wasteland. Humans can't survive in these wastelands for long and instead live in the artificial environments of the planet's hive cities. Food, clean air, and water are imported form off-planet.

Armageddon is one of ten planets in its system and is fourth from its star. It is positioned as the nexus of the sector's primary warp transit routes and once supplied arms to Astra Militarum regiments across the galaxy. Its production capabilities have been substantially reduced over the last thousand or so years, owing to being embroiled in a state of constant conflicts.

Credit: Games Workshop

Armageddon's surface has three main continents and two major oceans. The planet itself provides pretty much every type of ecosystem you could want to fight over, making it an ideal world for campaigns that want to use different map and terrain layouts.
  • The Fire Wastes are located near the planet's northern pole. Most of the continent is covered by the Sreya Rock Mountain Range and scarred by volcanic activity and lava rivers. The high temperatures here make most of the land mass uninhabitable for humans, with only the coastal regions capable of supporting laborers without enviro-suits. This is also where the warp anomaly called the Red Angel's Gate is located. Due to the planet's axial tilt, this part of the planet is almost always pointed at the system's star.
  • The Deadlands are the planet's southern continent, split into the Deadlands and the Netheria Peninsula. The Deadlines are primarily covered in ice, making them the planet's primary source of water and the location of its water processing plants. Purified water from the plants are pumped through undersea pipes to the main continent, where they connect to Hives Helsreach and Tartarus.
  • The Main Continent is also called Armageddon for some reason. It's split into Armageddon Prime (the Western half), and Armageddon Secundus (the Eastern half). Armageddon Prime is home to Hive Tempestora, Hive Volcanus, the Death Mire, and the planet's Equatorial Jungle. Armageddon Secundus hosts most of the planet's famous Hive Cities - Hades, Infernus, Helsreach, Tartarus, Acheron, and Thoraddis.
Between the Main Continent and the Fire Wastes is The Boiling Sea, while on the other side of Armageddon Prime is The Tempest Ocean. Both boast a large number of oil platforms and pipelines to the main continent.

Notable Locations on Armageddon

The most notable locations on Armageddon are its hive cities, and several of these have played major roles in various stories and wars over the last forty years of 40k lore.

Hive Volcanus

One of three large hive cities on the western half of the main continent, Hive Volcanus is named such because of its proximity to the Volcanus mountains which border it to the south. The nearby mountains hold the Volcanus Space Defence Fortress, a key part of the planet's defenses. Also near Hive Volcanus is the Nemesis Island Penal Facility, which plays host to the planet's worst criminals. The Hive's location near the Boiling Sea has made it a major focus in every war for Armageddon.

Hive Tempestora

Hive Tempestora's location on the northern shores of the main continent make it a key endpoint for docks and pipelines crossing the Boiling Sea. The hive is surrounded by toxic ash wastes created by runoff from its factory complexes, and the seas nearby are littered with the remnants of Ork submarines left over from prior battles. During the Fourth War for Armageddon, the hive was overrun by cultists who successfully summoned Angron. As of Operation Imperator, it remains a shattered, overrun wreck.

The Equatorial Jungle

The Equatorial Jungle splits the main continent into its Prime and Secundus areas and consists primarily of forests and jungles populated by all manner of nasty creatures including Grope Weeds and a type of carnivorous plant referred to as the Helsreach Monster. The southeastern reaches of the jungle is currently home to feral orks who were left over from the first war for Armageddon and now live in the Plague Marshes.

Hive Helsreach

The Armageddon Hive you're likely most familiar with, thanks to the excellent book by Aaron Dembski-Bowden and the Helsreach animated fan series on YouTube. Helsreach is the first of the human settlements on the planet. The hive city hosts a massive dock and is bordered by the Balboa mountains on the East and the Valdez Alpha Offshore Oil Platform to the south. The hive also has a massive shipyard, and with the Oil platform acts as a major resource hub for the planet's factories.

Hive Infernus

One of the most heavily defended hives on Armageddon, Infernus sits atop a crucial nexus of the planet's waterways. It also acts as the seat of the planet's government.

Hades Hive

Hades Hive was a vast hive city on the northern coast of the main continent. It was the site of many of the key battles in the Second War for Armageddon and was destroyed when it was bombarded from orbit by the Orks at the outset of the Third War for Armageddon. Refugees still survive within the ruins, though the hive was conquered again by Chaos forces during the Season of Blood.

Hive Tartarus

The easternmost hive on the main continent, Hive Tartarus acted as the last line of defense against Ork forces in the second war for Armageddon.

Hive Acheron

A large hive on the east end of Armageddon Secundus, Acheron acted as Commander Dante's forward base during the second war for Armageddon. The hive notably fell during the Third War for Armageddon due to sabotage and an attempted coup from former Planetary Governor Herman von Strab, who led Ork forces into the city through secret tunnels and led to its capture without a single shot fired.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Ullanor Prime: The Secret History of Armageddon

Over the last ten thousand years Armageddon has been the target of violence by a number of factions but most famously the Orks and Warboss Ghazgkhull Thraka. You may be asking, "Why Armageddon? What about that planet makes it so important to them?"

Well the answer is that Armageddon wasn't always called that. During the days of the Great Crusade, Armageddon was named Ullanor, and was the seat of a vast Ork empire under the rule of Warbass Urlakk Urg. From Ullanor, Urg's massive Waaagh! controlled a massive region of space and threatened the growing borders of the Imperium. Determined to end their threat, the Emperor led a massive Imperial force to capture Ullanor and compromise Urg to a permanent end.

Leading a massive force of Space Marines, the Emperor and Horus were ultimately successful, luring Urg's forces away and launching a direct attack on Urg's palace that ended in Horus flinging Urg's screaming body off the roof. Urg's demise sent shockwaves through his troops and his empire fragmented, paving the way for the Imperial Forces to reclaim the entire sector.

This was a huge deal, and marked a turning point in the Great Crusade. It was Horus' greatest triumph, and to commemorate the achievement the Emperor had the Adeptus Mechanicus come in and level half of the continent to lay down a three-hundred mile parade path decorated in Ork skulls. At the end of the parade Horus was named Warmaster of the Great Crusade and his legion renamed the Sons of Horus. It was a great day for the Imperium and nothing bad ever happened to them or the planet ever again.

Just kidding. Horus turned against the Emperor and during the Heresy Ullanor would become the staging ground for the traitor forces before their final siege on Terra. Oh, and also Ullanor would fall to Orks during the War of the Beast.

The War of the Beast

About 1,500 years after Urg was hurled off the top of his palace a massive, hyper-intelligent Ork Warboss going by the name "The Beast" showed up to reclaim Ullanor as the seat of his empire. The Beast was a particularly powerful Ork Warboss, leading the largest Waaagh! the Galaxy has ever seen. He was highly intelligent and supposedly some 33 feet tall, and his key weapon were Attack Moons, which were more or less manufactured space hulks turned into Death Stars. They could recharge their weapons and batteries by eating worlds Unicron-style and incorporated subspace tunnels that could spit out Ork warships. The Beast had intended to turn Ullanor into an Attack Planet to use for wiping out Terra.

Eventually Primarch Vulkan re-emerged to help kill The Beast, and after a massive, prolonged war second only in size to the Horus Heresy, the Imperium was able to turn the tide and bring the Beast and his Waaagh to heel. Imperial forces reclaimed the planet, which had been transformed by Ork technology. An Exterminatus was ordered on the planet but Fabricator-General Kubik of the Adeptus Mechanicus wanted the Ork technology left on Ullanor and instead used it to teleport the planet to a secret location in Segmentum Solar. The planet was later renamed Armageddon, presumably after Fabricator-General Kubik's comment, "Let the Imperial fleet see us bring armageddon down on Ullanor!"

If all of that sounds pretty dumb well, the actual books are even worse.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Many Later Wars

Armageddon would go on to host many later wars after its move and subsequent renaming: Two from Angron and the forces of Khorne and three from Waaagh! Ghazgkhull. By our count, we're on the fifth War for Armageddon at this point as we head into 11th edition, though it's not currently clear if the second invasion of Angron and his Daemons is a standalone War for Armageddon or part of the third (our take is that calling it part of the third war is lame, given that everyone involved in the Season of Blood is different from Ghazghkull's first revenge tour).

We'll cover these wars more extensively in future Lore Explainer articles but the basic rundown is this:
  • First War: Angron and his World Eaters attack Armageddon and are driven off by Grey Knights and Space Wolves. The whole planet's population is executed in the aftermath.
  • Second War: Ghazgkhull leads a Waaagh! to attack Armageddon. Through the heroic efforts of Commissar Yarrick - who lost his arm defeating Ork Warboss Ugulhard in the fighting - plus Blood Angels, Ultramarines, Salamanders, and the Imperial Guard, the Orks were defeated.
  • Third War: Ghazghkull comes back fifty years later at the head of a new Waaagh!. Yarrick and Imperial forces muster for battle again, this time including the Black Templars and Chaplain Grimaldus and the Salamanders space marines.
  • Fourth War/Season of Blood: A Khornate host makes its way to Armageddon and Angron is summoned through the Red Angel's Gate during the invasion. He's defeated by the combined efforts of the Grey Knights, Space Wolves, Black Templars, and Imperial Guard.
  • Fifth War: Ghazgkhull launches a new attack on Armageddon shortly afterward, kicking off the main conflict of the 11th edition 40k boxed set.
Credit: Dan "Ditka" Boyd

Playing Battles on Armageddon

Armageddon has a varied landscape with a number of different biomes to fight over, but a large amount of conflict on the planet will occur either in the planet's major hive cities or the polluted ash wastes near them. If you're looking to fight famous historical battles on Armageddon, there have been a ton of 40k supplements around various battles and wars fought over the planet, stretching back to the game's first edition.

The First War for Armageddon

Although mentioned in detail in a number of sources, there's very little Games Workshop has put out about actually playing battles in the first War for Armageddon. The only rules published for it are the Chaos Attacks expansion for the 1991 board game Battle for Armageddon (see below), which sets the stage for Armageddon and adds the Khornate forces as a new faction.

The Second War for Armageddon

  • Battle for Armageddon (1991) - This cardboard chit board game, released by Games Workshop in 1991, lets players move strategically around the planet's main continent, attempting to claim the planet. Notably the later Chaos Attacks expansion adds rules for the Squats showing up to fight against the Orks as well, though this alliance would be retconned away.
  • Battle for Armageddon Scenarios - this booklet, released with the 2nd edition boxed set, details a short campaign modeling some of the key battles for the planet between Space Marines and Orks during the second War for Armageddon. There are three missions in the book, all of which are designed to be played with the small forces of Space Marines and Orks which came in the box.

The Third War for Armageddon

The third War for Armageddon has by far the most content around it, with a ton of different supplements and rulebooks tackling its forces.
  • Codex: Armageddon (3rd edition) - This Codex doesn't have missions or campaign rules but it does have backstory on the third war and more or less is the book which first established the third war. It has rules for Ork Kult of Speed Armies, Black Templars, Salamanders, and Armageddon Imperial Guard armies.
  • Apocalypse (6th edition) - The core rulebook for the 6th edition version of Apocalypse covers the massive battles of the third war's campaign, with an extra campaign section in the main rules to cover Ork and Marine forces and war zones specific to the planet.
  • There was a Global Campaign run by Games Workshop in the summer of 2000 to determine the outcome of the third war for Armageddon, with players in different countries assigned to major hives on the planet's main continent. The campaign ended in a narrow Imperial Victory.

The Fourth War/Season of Blood

There's really only one set of rules for the Season of Blood and that's the 10th edition Crusade Supplement for Armageddon. It's got rules for playing battles and running campaigns in the blood-drenched wastes, and while they're not entirely good, the book has a lot of fluff to go with them.

The Fifth War for Armageddon

Battles from the fifth war for Armageddon are detailed in the Operation Imperator booklet packaged with the 11th Edition Armageddon boxed set for Warhammer 40k.

Final Thoughts

There's a ton more to go over with this one, and Armageddon is a topic we could have easily split into a dozen articles. So we basically did that. Stay tuned in the coming weeks for more articles on those wars, the major characters, and the major battles of the planet, along with analysis and thoughts on how that lore was created, released, and changed over the years. And yeah at some point we'll also cover the War of the Beast.
