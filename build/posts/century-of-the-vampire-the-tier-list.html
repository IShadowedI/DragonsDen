Century of the Vampire: The Tier List

August 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The List

Thank you to this specific Tiermaker template for providing the base for this image; I added a couple things in myself. It's not on them that they didn't have a 40K animated series from 2021. They did have The Batman vs. Dracula!

Here is our master page for Century of the Vampire, with links to reviews for every movie pictured above. Below, I'll shout some out specifically and explain some of my decision-making.

The Words

Sixty-two films over a year and a half or so; hardly an exhaustive look at all vampire cinema -- you can find lists of more than English-language 200 films without even looking too hard, to say nothing of the films from various European and Asian cinemas we lightly touched on at best -- but I think we got the big ones in terms of cultural impact; the high notes and the low notes. I’ve sorted the films into tiers, as is the modern custom, from S to F. I anticipated a bell curve that saw very few S and F films and the majority of the offerings ending up in B and C; that’s almost what happened -- if you combine B and C together into a vast undifferentiated middle, then that blob does in fact outnumber the As or the Ds by itself. The way I thought about that ranking was that B films were mediocre in a positive fashion -- fun-bad or fun or almost-good -- and C films were, well, just mediocre. As I discuss the tiers I’ll talk about the film I had at the top of each tier, the film I had at the bottom, and the film in the tier whose placement there surprised me the most; we won’t go through all 16 films in the D tier, for instance, in a specific order of quality. At a certain point of granularity it just becomes completely arbitrary, and frankly I’m not willing to think about nonsense like “which Twilight movie is better than the rest of the Twilight movies” ever again.

S-Tier

Here we have three films that I think epitomize the best instincts of modern Western vampire cinema, each in a very different way. Blade remains the high water mark of vampires as action/science fiction blockbuster monsters; I’d say “often imitated, never duplicated” but no one actually seems interested in making a movie like this anymore, certainly not Marvel themselves, who remain paralyzed by their inability to shove Blade into their nice, neat Cinematic Universe typology. Shadow of the Vampire is almost certainly the scariest of the vampire films I watched for this feature; the looming horror, the creeping dread, the growing realization that while the Schreck creature is indeed a monster, he is not the only one haunting these proceedings and might not even be the worst of them. Vampire movies struggle to actually be scary in the visceral ways that gore-soaked and jump-scare addicted slashers and the like manage, so they have to make up for it in spades with both intellectual and vibes-based horror. Shadow of the Vampire is the best of the bunch at that. And Only Lovers Left Alive is at the complete other end of the spectrum; it is the best example in the dataset of vampires as social, sexy creatures -- they hunt and kill in the film, yes, but it is not a horror movie. It’s just as much hangout cinema as anything else Jim Jarmusch has ever made, and as a director he is very much a sometimes food for me. Here, it hits. This is the one category where I don’t have a best or worst; I think I prefer Blade the least of the three as I enter middle age, but it would top this list 20 years ago. Only Lovers Left Alive is the clear surprise here; I had very low expectations going in, though part of that was his terrible zombie movie (made years later) leaving a bad taste in my mouth. In every way, zombies are the inferior movie monster.

A-Tier

An expected list of vampire mainstays; the recently-released Sinners is on here, along with Coppola's Dracula, del Toro’s Cronos, Park’s Thirst, Schumacher’s The Lost Boys, both version of the Let Me/the Right One In, Jordan’s Interview with the Vampire, every version of Nosferatu we looked at, and…Dracula Untold? Yes, this is the movie that defines the bottom of our A-Tier; it’s surprising to you that it’s here, probably, but not to me. This is the closest we’ve come to a great vampire action flick since the original Blade, and it’s very unfortunate that it got caught up in the Dark Universe nonsense because I think Luke Evans does a great job in it. Unironic fan of the Bat Punch and Bat Slam. I think Near Dark is the best of the bunch here; I think Shadow of the Vampire was the best horror vampire film in the bunch but Near Dark is easily the meanest. It would probably be in the S-Tier if I was being slightly less restrictive on that grade. The most surprising entry here to me is the video game Vampire: The Masquerade Bloodlines 2, which I thought would be a disaster. It was instead merely a flop, but I still found the story, linear as it is, extremely compelling. Great vampire stuff.

B-Tier

We’ve got El Conde at the top, a bit too long and inconsistent to get into the A-Tier but with great individual images and moments, which is sort of the epitome of the kind of film that ends up in the B-Tier. From Dusk Till Dawn would likewise be up a tier if the first thirty minutes weren’t intolerable; the version of that film you have in your head from when you watched it when you were 13, where it starts out with them arriving at the bar, that’s an A-Tier bordering on S-Tier film. Its placement here is probably the tier’s biggest surprise. 30 Days of Night is a rock-solid B-Tier film by our rubric; it’s also a pretty mean film, but it’s dragged down in places by how aggressively 2010s it is. Every time I look at the original Underworld on the end of the list here I’m tempted to go back and bump it down to the top of the C-Tier. It was certainly a much more successful movie than Blade II, and it certainly has fewer weird bumps and rough edges than that movie does, but that’s about the best that can be said for it -- and is part of the overall argument that the B and C-Tiers are really expressions of the same general silo of films.

C-Tier

Blade II makes it into the B-Tier when I’m specifically thinking of the monster design on the freakish bug vampire Nosferatu creatures; then I think of literally anything else about the movie and it’s back to the C-Tier. But that’s good enough for the top of the tier! The movie I’m most surprised is down here is the original Dracula; that’s a very poor adaptation with way too much time spent on the Renfield character, and notable basically only for Lugosi himself. The ratio of interesting shots to boring flab in Nadja sadly puts it down here instead of higher up, though that is a film whose Renfield I very much enjoy. Three of the four Underworld sequels live at the bottom of this group, with Evolution mainly still up here to form a firm floor to differentiate from…

D-Tier

Cirque du Freak: The Vampire’s Assistant gets place of privilege here just because John C. Reilly is working his ass off to get leading man bookings. It doesn’t work, but he really does try. Bad film, though. The animated feature The Batman vs. Dracula is down here because I didn’t have anywhere else to put it; it’s a Batman cartoon where he fights Dracula. It won’t surprise you. John Carpenter’s Vampires? Check. Dracula 2000? Check. Vampire in Brooklyn? Check. The Nineties were great for vampires but they also churned out some real dreck (Dracula 2000 came out before 9/11, so it counts as part of the Nineties). All the Twilights are down here so I don’t have to think about them. To give you an idea of how far down D-Tier can stretch without getting to F-Tier, Morbius is down here too. The CGI vampire fighting stuff is cool enough to give it some redeeming value; I think that’s actually the surprise here, that it’s this high on the list. Ultraviolet forms the floor for the D-Tier, in a very similar place to Near Dark but in absolute inversion: If I wasn’t keeping the F-Tier exclusive, it’d be down there.

F-Tier

For a very, very long time there was only one film in this tier: BloodRayne. I kept this entire tier cleared out for this unprofessional, intractable piece of garbage, too lazy and boring to be repulsive; in this respect it is unlike the man who directed it, who manages all three with ease. There’s little I can say about it that I haven’t already said, except that in the time since I wrote my review he returned to “prominence” with a work that’s more an act of criminal incitement than a film, Citizen Vigilante. Simply one of the most contemptible to have ever done it, holistically.

Then Vampire Academy came along. I wrote in the Vampire Academy review that the film didn’t “threaten [BloodRayne’s position] directly” as a matter of craft, and I do believe that; it is at the very low bounds of professionalism, perhaps, but it was a professionally-made movie. But whereas a good director and writer can probably do something acceptable with BloodRayne -- it’s never making S-Tier and probably not A-Tier, but you can certainly see the possibilities of a B-Tier film there -- Vampire Academy is just unacceptable in its bones. It’s a soulless attempt to cash in on Harry Potter while doing a significant amount of race science along the way, disastrously conceived from the jump to be nothing but easy money from undiscerning young readers and viewers. Though the physical production and actor work is up to standard (mostly; the male lead is badly stumbling through his lines trying to act in a language he clearly doesn’t speak), the scriptwriting and plotting might actually be more incompetent than BloodRayne. In the end, the thing that distinguishes it from BloodRayne at the bottom of this list is its contempt for the concept of vampirism; “vampire” is just a brand to slap on an extremely shitty and boring private school YA story that has a little bit of poorly-thought out magic attached to it. It had the temerity to end on a sequel hook that, thank Christ, it never got. They tried it again as a Peacock show and while I’m sure they took more liberties with the material that time to make it less putrid, a bad foundation never leads to a good house. At least we’ll always have the CHVRCHES cover of Bela Lugosi’s Dead.

Final Thoughts

And that’s the wrap on the project! This leg of it, at least. Vampires aren’t just movie monsters these days. They make video games about them too. I covered one in this series because it had just come out and I needed to break up the string of Twilight films, but we’ll probably be doing a more thorough examination of their contributions to that form of media somewhere down the line.
