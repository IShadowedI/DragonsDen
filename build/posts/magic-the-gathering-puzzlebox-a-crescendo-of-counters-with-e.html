Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – A Crescendo of Counters with Esior & Reyhan

October 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

A few weeks back on The Puzzlebox, I highlighted a Commander used by someone I played against that really impressed me with its ingenuity, being Songbird, Sonic Screamer. I've wanted to use this series as a way to shine a light on strategies that aren't within my admittedly narrow purview, those which might not include such constant companions as Tezzeret, Cruel Captain. One of those lists is the one we're talking about today, headlined by a Partner pairing of Esior, Wardwing Familiar & Reyhan, Last of the Abzan. These two tag together to lead a list that achieves lethal Commander Damage remarkably quickly, but that itself is nothing new; a +1/+1 counter strategy leading to quick, aggressive gameplans is just about par for the course. No, the actual reason I'm so enthralled by this list is not the endgame, but the mechanism—this is a Clones list, which lends the strategy a flexibility and means of escalation that really makes Reyhan sing. This is true of any Reyhan Partner pairing that includes Blue, which we'll talk about below, but Esior puts in a ton of work as a cheap, evasive body that wears those counters well. That protection isn't ignored by anything in the game, courtesy of it being printed a few sets before Ward released in Strixhaven, School of Mages, which often just functionally means you have Hexproof on your Commanders. We're getting ahead of ourselves though, so join me as we soar with this odd couple!

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Majoring in Bird Math

Backing up a bit, Reyhan is a 3-mana Creature which enters with 3 +1/+1 counters, and comes with a triggered ability to 'pass off' counters when itself or another Creature of yours dies, or is put into the Command Zone. Notably, these counters aren't literally transferred, but new +1/+1 counters are placed equivalent to those on the recently-deceased. Keep that in mind for a second. Reyhan obviously works well with any replacement effect which adds additional counters, such as Winding Constrictor, in order to slowly build up counters between bodies as you churn through Creatures. Removal is made moot unless it is total—picking apart a board while Reyhan's around just means building up counters on your Commander, and that usually means someone's getting smacked for a ton of damage. A lot of the means of building up +1/+1 counters are fundamentally fair, however; you might get one here or there from opponents executing their gamplan, via Managorger Hydra or Predatory Hunter, and if that were all this deck did I'd not be talking about it. No, Reyhan merely masquerades as a meandering counters-matter midrange pile, and in fact there's little intention of the game going long enough to truly see your incremental gameplan come to fruition. That's plan B. Plan A is Clones.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Reyhan's trigger is such that it sees both itself dying/moving to the Command Zone, as well as any other simultaneous Creature doing the same, which means at a baseline, using something like a Flesh Duplicate is essentially a 2-mana Spell which adds 6 +1/+1 counters (that's 3 from the clone seeing itself die, and 3 from the remaining Reyhan). Crucially however, that's three independent instances of counters being placed: One from the clone Reyhan entering, another from it dying, and another from the remaining Reyhan. If we have any means of increasing the number of counters placed, things really begin to snowball. At worst, though, those 6 +1/+1 counters raise Esior's power to 7, meaning it's three hits away from ending someone via Commander Damage. These clones can be flexed as more than just Reyhan, however, as doubling up on most counter-matters effects gets out of hand extremely quickly. Beifong's Bounty Hunters with Reyhan represents a revolting number of counters, and a second Pensive Professor or Pollywog Prodigy cascades your advantage in a grinder game. The list correctly identifies that the scariest thing a counters-matter deck can do is isolate the most broken payoff, and try to execute it as many times as possible.

Reyhan Dies and Doesn't Come Back

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

There's something endearing about Reyhan & Esior, beyond the fact that they combine to be an exceptionally difficult-to-deal-with aggro deck. Reyhan is one of a scant few cards in modern memory to be functionally errataed, with death triggers being lumped into how Commanders work at a baseline, and Esior's text is weirdly unique without the shortcutting of Ward we'd see, were it printed today. This pairing is a mere 58 decks, but even so I've never see a Clone-based Reyhan deck prior to sitting across from it. It is however funny that Reyhan's most popular pairing, being Ishai, Ojutai Dragonspeaker, is also a Bird. She's got a type, I guess. Another facet I appreciate is the commitment to Clones which need not stick around, like via Mirror Mockery, or Dance of Many. By the time their negative effects roll around, that body is long gone, and in the case of Mirror Mockery its repeatability is frankly frightening. Playing something like a Mold Adder also showcases that the list intends to generate advantage enough such that, even if it's a dead card in a pod without Blue or Black, it remains worthwhile for those tables where it's a cheaper Managorger Hydra. One last card to call out specifically is Applied Geometry, which when coupled with Reyhan is enough counters on its own to swing out for 21+ Commander Damage (which may or may not have happened recently).

I'm no stranger to odd +1/+1 counter decks, as one of the first articles in this series was a spin on Monogreen Spacecraft with Maester Seymour. This list both is, and isn't, however; in many ways the actual counter gameplan is ancillary to the fact that your Clones are just whatever the best body on the board is, at any given moment, with a sidenote that 'you will die to Commander damage from an untargetable flier'. It aims to present the necessary threats to stop decks which try to execute their gameplan faster, and fail to fold to control players by way of how sticky your power is. Single-target removal bounces off, board wipes can end up being a negative, and very quickly you'll find yourself clawed to death by the world's largest owl if you can threaten the Reyhan/Esior player. It's a treat to play against, and has some absolutely fantastic player dynamics. This is the first time on The Puzzlebox I'm reviewing someone else's deck in the literal sense, and while I've helped in small ways to improve card quality, it's great to see a list that I couldn't have fashioned by myself. Whether I'm dying to a Fell Beast of Mordor with a billion counters, or Springheart Nantuko is making repeatable copies of Reyhan, I've never had a bad time losing to this list.
