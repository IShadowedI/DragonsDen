Games from the Crypt #16 - It's "Athelete!"

June 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

It has recently come to my attention that a quote from a review I wrote many, many years ago has been used in the box copy of a new game. Now, this is not the first time it’s happened- Portal used a line from my Neuroshima Hex review on all their big tradeshow banners, for example. I’ve always been proud and honored that publishers and designers I respect take pull quotes from my archives of over 26 years of games writing and slap them on a game that I can stand by. But I’m usually not particularly fazed by it. This time, however, was different.

On a box band, which many will likely discard, and also on the game's Amazon page there is my quote describing the great and until recently mostly forgotten Magical Athelete (yes, with an extra E- more on that coming). I stated that the game is “one of the great games of our age, embodies what it means to have fun with friends and family”. And right below, a quote from none other than my archnemesis Tom “The Dice Tower” Vasal. Then credit due to original designer Takashi Ishida. And not too far down from that, the “updated by” credit for Richard Garfield.

I was pretty struck that I made it onto a game box with Richard Garfield. And with a quote that was largely a middle finger to where game design was when Magical Athelete was a game you could only get from Japan and most gamers were focused on what I would call the Bronze Age of Eurogames back in the early to mid 2000s. I still recall my first encounter with this indeed magical game back in 2008, which was actually something like five years after it was actually published. Atlanta gaming legend Frank Branham had secured an import copy, and slated it for us to play during one of the meet-ups of the Brown Pudding Lads (long story). Frank always had the weirdest, most obscure, and rarest games. He still does.

It was a smash hit. I remember my initial assessment like it was yesterday – “This is either the best game ever made, the worst game ever made, or both.” But more significantly, that session was also attended by another gaming legend, none other than Zev “Z-Man” Shlasinger, who was in Atlanta to visit Frank and to make fun of the local pizza options. He played the game with us, would go on to get the rights to publish a US version. I have a couple of emails where he asked me for some advice about his production of the game. But when he did the Z-Man Games version, he spelled it Athlete. I was furious. I refuse to not call this game Magical Athelete because that’s what is says on the original Grimpeur Games Japanese edition.

As for the game, it’s freaking brilliant and it remains so even without the misspelling, which was AGAIN and SADLY corrected in the most recent edition published in late 2025 by CMYK with some updates and expansion concepts provided by Mr. Magic the Gathering. The new edition has some extremely funky artwork that has a very 1970s Letter People vibe that I dig tremendously.

It used to be a 4-5 player game but now it’s been updated to accommodate 2-6. Players get a little startup money to draft various characters that all have a unique special power. The ones with the most desirable powers tend to get bought up early at their highest offering prices while the remainder shift down to lower prices. Once everyone has a stable of 4 or 5 runners, the game goes to a board where you just roll a D6 and try to win the race for VP payouts. It’s really just a plain old roll-and-move and in the original version there was only one track with nothing on it. You just rolled the dice and moved.

BUT – here’s the magic. Those special powers. Like Cosmic Encounter, the game-breaking and altering abilities transform the game into something much more. It becomes wild, crazy, and unpredictable. Some abilities affect other players, some impact die rolls, some give special movement options, and wilder options change race results or even outright eliminate other racers. It’s total chaos, especially with a full table, and if there is one thing I value in game design above all else it is in fact total chaos provided said chaos is fun and provides ample opportunity to use the mayhem competitively.

What makes this game special is, like a lot of games you’ll find in my Game Crypt, that it foregrounds player agency and the mechanics serve as a medium for interaction with other players. This is what I meant by that box quote- the “rules”, such as they are, get out of the way and let you play with friends and family. Which is what any great board game should be doing, really. To be clear, this is a game for players that want to laugh and cut up, not for those who may be looking for an exercise in brow-furrowing and contemplative harumphing.

I have yet to play the new edition but I absolutely love the direction CMYK went with it. It looks completely singular and the 1970s funky weirdos thing is a pure delight. The addition of a “wild mile” track is inspired, and it offers even more chaos and nonsense so I am here for that. The production is just wonderful, with the old cardboard standees of the original manga-themed edition replaced with big wooden pawns. I can overlook the missing E for now.
