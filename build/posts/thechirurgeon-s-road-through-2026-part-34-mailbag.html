TheChirurgeon's Road Through 2026, Part 34: Mailbag

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I talked about switching to Grand Coven and running a practice game with my Thousand Sons against my friend T - which ended quickly when I got too aggressive. This time around I'm shifting to my plans for the 2026 Grand Narrative and the army I'm taking. I'm also going to talk about HeroQuest. But first, I'm going to answer some READER QUESTIONS. It's time for the first ever ROAD THROUGH MAILBAG.

These questions come directly from our Warhound Patrons, asked via Discord.

Nurgler: How the hell do you manage being a good dad and completing anything at all paintwise?

This is a good one to start with. And I'll set the record straight: I am, at best, an average dad. I try to engage with my son when he's home and steer him to make good decisions. I think the hardest part for me has just been engaging with him in ways he likes - one thing about kids is that for at least the first nine years of their lives they really want your attention all the time and will be happy with whatever you give them, provided there isn't a screen to look at instead. Once that thing goes on, you're dead to them.

So one thing I try to do is to find areas our interests overlap. It's not always going to happen - while I really like drawing and he likes drawing, my son doesn't really like sitting down with me and doing art. He really only ever wants to do art at school, specifically when he's not supposed to be doing it. So that one's out. And painting minis is tough - he was motivated a couple of years ago to get his marines painted for Armies on Parade but he's been pretty indifferent about it ever since.

On the other hand, we've had him learning to play drums in School of Rock for the past year or so, and as he's done that I've been able to help him practice by dusting off the guitar and playing with him. They do a lot of more modern songs, so when they pick something like "Big Me" by the Foo Fighters I can pick that up because it's a song I learned to play like, 30 years ago. And practicing with him gives him the kind of encouragement I didn't really have as a kid - while my father is a solid musician, he's never really had the patience for practice (I have no idea how he got good himself), or listening to me practice. When I was a kid he'd tell me to knock it off whenever I practiced trombone, partly because it was loud as hell and usually in a space near where he was watching TV. I'd eventually learn to play guitar, but that was by teaching myself in college.

Likewise, I'll sit down and play some games with him - just last night we decided to start on a big family playthrough of the original Final Fantasy VII - it's my wife's favorite game, and we're letting him take the reigns on the playthrough and just doing occasional voices for it. He has to read all of Cloud's dialog (he renamed Cloud to his own name, and named Barrett "Dad"). It's a fun family activity for us at the moment.

Then there's HeroQuest. We've been playing HeroQuest at least one weekend a month for like two years now, running through games of the main quest and First Light, alternating often between the two with some of my son's friends. I've loved HeroQuest forever and greedily picked up the Avalon Hill re-release and every expansion, plus a host of 3d printed accessories from Etsy over the years. I'm slowly painting all of it. Right now I've painted the entirety of the base set, most of the player characters (I still need to do the female dwarf and male elf), and a lot of the extra stuff from the deluxe edition of the original rerelease. I also finished painting this dragon before last weekend:

This past weekend we added a new player - another of my son's friends from school - and she took the Monk character as they battled their way through the final dungeon of First Light, defeating Qwindrak and freeing the baby dragon in the Seerscale egg. It was a cool moment, and our new player was hooked (we're already planning the next HeroQuest meetup). It's something he really enjoys doing and having the kids over to play has been great. I make them read every card and let them make mistakes.

I wrote about how to run HeroQuest with kids in this article.

This past weekend the missus was out of town so we set up the 3d printer. He's been asking me for things nonstop, and has shown an interest in painting them so they aren't just the same color as the black filament I put in the printer. That's been a fun experiment as well, and using it was easier than I expected. It's a Flashforge printer my mom got on sale a while back for my son, and it sat in my garage a long time before I finally took it out, assembled it, and started using it. It's easier than I expected but also just as pointless - unless you have something very specific to do with a 3D printer, they just kind of sit inert most of the time. So far I've printed a chaos emerald for him, the idol from Raiders of the Lost Ark, and I'm currently working on a time stone from Sonic CD. The boy likes Sonic.

Incredisteve: how often do you paint, for roughly how long?

It depends. At the very least, I paint every Saturday night. My friends and I have a weekly "hang out and paint" chat on a private Discord server. And every Saturday night starting around 7pm EDT, people start hopping on. It's not always the same people every week and I don't always paint when I'm on it, but it's a great motivator to sit down and paint something every single week. It's also just a good time. We have about 20 or so people who rotate in and out, though most weeks I'm in there along with Alice (RagnarokAngel), Jeremy (Curie), Norman, Joey (Crab-stuffed Mushrooms), and Craig (masterslowpoke), though a host of other folks are on there pretty regularly. When I hop on there I usually do so until I crash, so around 4-5 hours.

When I'm not doing Saturday night hobby hang-outs, my painting tends to focus on project-related tasks. I'm starting to ramp up now on my Emperor's Children for the Grand Narrative, which will mean adding two to three sessions per week until November, usually around 2-4 hours each. Then I'll crunch before the event.

Chris_from_IT: Less a question and more a request for more work in progress pics for the Emperor's Children

Hey, great timing! I started back up on these. Here's a guy I literally finished yesterday.

Mesa: You’re committed to Chaos. How did you get into that kind of thing? I would also enjoy Pro Painting Tips

It started with HeroQuest, where the Chaos Warriors were the coolest models in the entire box. I fell in love with those, and when Codex Chaos came out in 1996, I was hooked. You can read more about my love affair with Chaos in my Night Lords army showcase, one of my favorite articles I've written for this site.

I've had two imperial armies over the years as well - my White Panthers and my Deathwatch - but they don't hold the same place in my heart as the Chaos armies. As for pro painting tips, I've written most of what I know in my How to Paint Everything articles, but I'm working on an upcoming subscriber-only series on wining best overall/best painted soon.

Gerebell: What have you found the most important mindset shift or concept to grasp to play well in 11e compared to 10e? I know the basics of good play are the same, but what's the most significant change to compete that you've observed?

I don't really approach it much differently. I worry less about actions now, since I'm usually running Purge or Take and Hold. I treat cover differently - I tend to assume that if I'm not explicitly behind a wall, I'm going to get shot. That helps keep me from overextending into open spots on the map. Few moments in 40k feel as bad as when a unit you thought was safe is suddenly exposed by some angle you weren't expecting or because you misjudged a distance and now you're going to lose the unit and the game. You want to avoid those moments.

Otherwise, simply focus on being reactive and forcing your opponent to expose themselves or make the first move so you can get the drop on them. When I ran two units of Scarabs, the goal was always to have them drop in and kill something, never to just sit there. If my opponent had their own deep strikers, I'd always hold mine until after theirs came in.

SoylentRobot: how is it to transport your army around to these events, got any tips n tricks for making it manageable?

I talk a lot about transporting armies to events in this column. Usually I make decisions about what to bring based on what I can transport and how. I notably only use a regular citadel case for my armies, and if something doesn't fit in there, I don't bring it. I decided my Tacoma army based purely on what fit into a single, small Citadel case.

I will occasionally magnetize things to make it easier, but if I had something like a double or triple defiler list, it would probably just not be something I took with me to events requiring I fly. I also notably do not like to check bags when I travel - I hate spending more time at the destination airport - so I try to work out solutions which let me avoid doing so. This does mean I've packed socks and underwear in my minis case before.

Temporal: on average how many games do you play against a person before you're comfortable popping the question "hey I am Not A Murderer, come play at my house so only one of us has to go somewhere"

I'm usually pretty casual about having people over, in part because I'm not worried that the average person I meet will be a murderer, and you can just ask people to leave if they suck. I'm usually more concerned that they're just going to be shitty to play against, and even that's easy to mitigate. Or that they're chuds. Even living in Texas, I'm not so hard up for friends that I have to put up with shitty politics in order to enjoy my hobby. I can play those people at events; I don't have to have them in my house.

That said, I'll typically talk to someone a few times and play them once at the store before I invite them over, and there are some people I've known a while who are still only store-only folks, though that's just as often to be because they want to hang out in a store instead of coming over to my house as it is me not wanting to invite them over. I've definitely had certain people come over and not get an invite back, but that has never been because of how they acted in my house and was always about something that happened later at a store or an event. They were perfectly fine and respectable at my house.

The other thing is just getting a second opinion or reco from one of your existing friends or mutuals. I have people I trust, and if they tell me someone's on the level, then I have them over.

And I mean, that's an important aspect - when we're at my house, I'm in control. They are my guest. I'll provide food and drink and a sick place to play, but if they get out of line I can ask them to leave. If they're shitty at the game store, that's a public space and I have no right to kick them out. But I also don't have a big problem inviting someone over if they seem cool - if someone seems decent I'll gladly take a chance on them, and ideally we'll hang out and do something that isn't even Warhammer.

You don't want to end up like the supposed "casual" players who openly brag that they don't play against "pubbies" or the people at the game store. That's ultimately how the game dies; you want to help it grow and bring more people in. One of my best friends is Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson - and I invited him over during the pandemic to play a game fully masked without ever having met him in person. Those early "Road Through" articles are littered with his many losses. He's now a co-owner of Tabletop battles and we recently roomed together at the GW US Open in Dallas, where he won Best Overall for Kill Team and I won Best Painted for 40k. He's brought his wife over. They're lovely.

Final Thoughts

This was a fun one to write. I've always enjoyed answering questions about the site and my hobby, and I'll probably do this again when I have another relatively "down" week. For now it's back to the Emperor's Children grind, getting the army ready for the Grand Narrative, and prepping my Thousand Sons for Challenger's Cup in a few weeks.

See you next Thursday.
