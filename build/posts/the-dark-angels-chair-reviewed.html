The Dark Angels Chair, Reviewed

July 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Specifically this is a Secret Lab Titan Evo, Dark Angels Edition. Thanks to Secret Lab for sending me this thing that my wife constantly makes fun of me for. 

I’m the worst person to review a chair. Completely unqualified. My current office chair, and all previous office chairs in my adult life, have been stolen from hallways when a company was moving out of whatever office building I was working in at the time. Getting this for me is like asking a dog to review a car after letting them stick their head out the window a few times. Of course I think the chair is good; I have no perspective to think otherwise.

Under any other circumstances it would be (and to some extent it still is) frankly baffling for this object to be present in my home. But this isn’t just a gamer chair, it’s a Dark Angels gamer chair, and I do have a history of opinions as regards Dark Angels merchandise. Since I am qualified to comment using that rubric: This thing whips. It absolutely owns.

My usual style of seating, which you could guess from when I was tarnishing my bona fides earlier, is a box store product most at home in a cubicle farm. I sit only in ISO standard replacement-level chairs: one unit of black cloth seating per desk, no more and no less. This is something else entirely. It looks like it came out of a race car, or at least a particularly unsavory itasha (a Japanese word that roughly translates to "anime Nissan destined for a police auction"). I don’t know why a gaming chair needs side bolsters, but they’re there. It looks like it’s going to haul ass around my office, at least until the wheels get lodged in the carpet and all 70 pounds of the thing slam into my desk after I force the issue. For whatever it’s worth, I’ll bet money that if I rammed this into any other piece of furniture in my house, the chair would win.

I’m not a gamer, and I’ve never owned a Gamer Chair, but as far as I can tell the Dark Angels Throne is a normal Titan Evo (medium size) with a new and extremely cool design on the upholstery. Since none of this makes sense to me, I ran this review past my brother in law - who is a gamer, and has the regular version of this chair - and he corroborated everything I’m about to say.

As far as the presentation, the first thing you see is (obviously) the box, which is cool enough, but then you open it and are greeted with this:

Secret Lab Dark Angels Chair. Credit: Greg Chiasson

Hell yeah. Not only is this totally sick, the instructions are printed on the reverse, making this the most bad ass instruction manual ever published, in addition to the largest. There are other instructions with pictures of Dark Angels on them, such as every box of Warhammer models, but none of them are this physically imposing. I don’t know what I’m supposed to do with this thing, but it feels too powerful to throw away. If you’re thinking that the image looks familiar, I had the same thought. I couldn’t place it at first, but then I stared at the wall to help me focus, and it turns out it’s cropped from a piece about Dark Angels wailing on Death Guard, a print of which is in my office, directly in front of where the chair now sits.

As far as the rest of the unboxing, I’m impressed with how compactly Tetris-ed this is. There’s an old adage about product packaging and how you can tell whether a company respects their customers based on how much of a pain they make it to open the thing - sealed clamshells and excessive zip ties on one end, Amazon’s loose items thrown lackadaisically in a bag on the other - and by that metric Secret Lab just have a deep and abiding love for the people shelling out for these things, because this is perfect. The chair did take longer to unpack from the box than it did to assemble, but that says more about the ease of assembly than the difficulty of unwrapping it. Maybe that’s just how things are done now, in an age where fickle consumers talk wild trash on reddit about minutiae, and your sales numbers can crater if you’re targeted by unflattering unboxing videos, but purchasing gamer-oriented products is new to me so this was a novel experience. Everything was secure and arrived in good condition without requiring multiple trash bags worth of styrofoam, is the main idea.

Secret Lab Dark Angels Chair. Credit: Greg Chiasson

Here are the parts, all laid out. You see what I mean, I hope, about the foam packing around the small bits - lesser brands would have thrown those in bubble wrap and called it a day. I didn’t use the included screwdriver, because I have a drill, but the fact that they did include one, and a pretty good one at that, is a nice touch. That small box? Inside is a purity seal. Incredible.

Secret Lab Dark Angels Chair. Credit: Greg Chiasson

The other amusing item in here was an insert with a QR code and instructions to post a picture of your chair on social media, in exchange for extending the manufacturer’s warranty. I don’t actually have a social media presence, so I’m going to send them a link to this article and see if my regular media presence counts.

This thing is so cool. It rocks. It has a picture of a sword on it. This might be an exaggeration, but now that I have this I would not be caught dead sitting on anything else. I’m going to replace my dining room chairs, and the driver’s seat in my car, with these. I’ll take it on airplanes, so the FAA and all the losers sitting around me will know that a True Son of Caliban is in their presence. I’ll sit there and fiddle with the little knobs and levers on it, while they can only stare in awe at the grim reaper angel on the back of it and the little triangles (a certified Cool Shape) down the side. That's bad ass.

Secret Lab Dark Angels Chair. Credit: Greg Chiasson

The chair is rock solid, because it’s made out of metal. Strong metal. Heavy metal. It is also huge, and barely fits under my desk. Admittedly I’m not a large guy, but I feel fully ensconced in this, like a toddler in a car seat. The cover is some kind of fake leather and suede, which I approve of: it requires less murder than real leather, but also is lower maintenance, so pluses and minuses there. I do have to point out that it smelled like powerful chemicals when I first got it and for a while after, in the same way that a new mattress will, but that’s fine I bet. Some of the parts (the arms and the headrest pillow) are stuck on with magnets to make them swappable, which is how you know - along with the tags stating so - that this is An Official Warhammer Product.

The design on it - which I want to stress is cool as all get out - is almost entirely stitched, with the only printed part being the goofball da share z0ne angel motif on the back side. Even the Dark Angels logo on the backrest is embroidered. This all feels like quality stuff. I don’t know how well it’ll wear, but even if it does eventually get ratty I think that’s appropriate for the subject matter. The color is maybe a touch light for Caliban Green - albeit not to the degree to which the candle was off, that thing wasn’t even close - but the gold and bone are perfect.

Secret Lab Dark Angels Chair. Credit: Greg Chiasson

The part you sit on is a bit firm compared to what I'm used to, but that actually makes it more comfortable. A bunch of pieces move around. As usual, the flat part has uppy-downy, but it reclines as well. The arms go up and down, in and out, and also swivel. There’s even a lumbar support - which, given that I’m on the wrong side of 40, and have two kids but zero intact vertebrae, is incredible - that can be adjusted in two axes. Utterly alien to me, having a chair I need to adjust, but now that it’s configured it fits perfectly. I feel like this is too good for gamers. They don’t deserve it. The Dark Angels chair needs to be marketed to a higher class of user.

It’s been three weeks since I got the chair, and it only recently stopped smelling weird, but I do love this thing. I'm sitting in it right now as I write this, and quite possibly also as you are reading it, and I have no complaints. Aesthetically, the concept is spot-on, and the design is well executed. It’s fun, which is an odd way to relate to a chair. Slightly juvenile, yes, but I think there’s a place for that, and having it around genuinely makes me happy.

As seating, it handily annihilates any other chair I’ve ever owned except for the Aeron I stole from a guy - and was eventually stolen from me - but even then I think this might be better. As Dark Angels merchandise, this is probably the most impressive thing I’ve ever seen, and certainly the most useful. The chair, it’s good.
