Contemptor Kevin's Combat Patrol Issue 59 Review

July 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

We return to the Great WAAAGH!!! in Issue 59, which features the "alternate" Ork Combat Patrol model: the Boomdakka Snazzwagon. As always, thank you to Tabletop Battles for the opportunity to review these models, magazines, and materials. Also for putting on the Goonhammer Open Finale.

Hobby Materials

Boomdakka Snazzwagon. Credit: Rockfish

This issue gives us the entire Boomdakka Snazzwagon, a 2018 Ork model. Released as part of the 2018 Orktober refresh, the Snazzwagon is an absolutely lovely model that seems to have had absolutely no memorable competitive impact in the past several years. Back in Ninth Edition Wings described the Snazzwagon as the "least" of the Orktober buggies (the others having been ridiculously pushed from that Codex), and that seems to have been the case through most of 10th. In early 11th Edition, the Snazzwagon has been almost absent from GTs, having been brought to a GT in precisely one list that went 1-2. So why the enduring popularity for this unit? Why, it's Tabletop Battles friend Steve Conlin, of course!

Way back in 2020, when GW was having to deal with pandemic-era shipping, they had Conlin in as James Workshop describing shipping deadlines, and the example he used was the Boomdakka Snazzwagon. His hammy delivery of the Boomdakka Snazzwagon turned what was a cool but mediocre model into a legend. Accordingly, I was stoked for this issue and model.

The Snazzwagon, as befitting an Ork light vehicle, is a relatively complex but small kit. The sprue is the same size as the Dakkarig, but being an older, glue assembled kit it's a bit harder to put together. Assembling the Snazzwagon felt like building a model car, as you had to build all the internal engine and suspension parts (with all the model flash and moldline clearing). Plus there's four crew members: two Orks and two grots. One can be forgiven for wondering what GW and Hachette were thinking, putting a model this intimidating into a package aimed mostly at beginners.

But then you read the instructions in the magazine and it's clear they thought of that problem. The magazine makes clear that the model needs to be sub-assembled, and they make each sub-assembly called out as a sub-assembly and kept apart for ease of painting. The painting guide for the model explicitly states that once you have the model painted, you can scrape off paint around the connection points so the parts will bond. The magazine suggests 4 sub-assemblies, though I ultimately went with 5. I left the roof and roof gunner separate, left the bumper separate, and kept the bumper grot and the grot gunner separate from their hull assemblies. It took me a couple of hours to build the kit, which is faster than I was expecting for a model from that era and with those mold lines. This will let me reach inside the buggy to paint the extremely detailed interior, when I get around to doing so this fall. (Hey, I've got 35+ Armageddon Orks to paint this summer!)

The issue has a paint guide in place of a narrative section, and includes a guide on how and why to use sticky tack. The issue recommends using it as a mask over the areas that will later be glued together. THe paint guide goes over each area of the model, and includes helpful reminders for parts that may be forgotten about when painting such a detailed model. We get the usual basecoat, shade, and highlight sections, though the magazine does not recommend using any Retributor Armor to break up the Leadbelcher metallics. The instructions finish off with the ultimate summation of this model: "Vroom Vroom, Hahahaha!"

The Boomdakka Snazzwagon and a unit of Warbikes. Credit: head58.   If you were able to blow these models up at the 2026 GHO, please leave a comment below.

Gaming Materials

We get the datasheet for the Boomdakka Snazzwagon (which replaced the Deffkoptas in the 2021 Ork Combat Patrol), and a scenario for using this model: Scenario 36 - the Speed Freak. This mission is a race between the Snazzwagon and the Primaris Chaplain on Bike: the two models start in the bottom center of two Combat Patrol playmats, and race in a counterclockwise circle. The Snazzwagon needs to complete three laps to win, while the Chaplain needs to kill the Snazzwagon. During the movement phase, the models can choose to either move normally and shoot, or they can supercharge and roll a d6 for a movement buff (that could let them shoot if the dice roll is high enough). During the shooting phase, before the model shoots, it rolls a d3 for either a -1 to hit, no modifiers, or a +1 to hit.

The Final Verdict

This is an absolute steal of an issue, especially if you can get this at retail cost. It's $15 for a $60 model that has so many interesting Ork tech bits that you probably wouldn't go wrong buying this to kitbash into another vehicle, or a terrain wreck, or another buggy. Right now it may not be memorable on the tabletop, but that does not mean it's a kickass Ork racecar and maybe the 11th Edition Codex will offer it the glowup that Steve Conlin's delivery encourages? I assume we will find out soon!

Until next time, Combat Patrolers!
