Marvel Crisis Protocol Tactics: Mirage, Havok, Jubilee & Multiple Man

October 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Mutants make up a large, popular corner of the Marvel Universe, so it makes sense that their various affiliations get plenty of releases in MCP. However, this one is particularly interesting, as the included models not only bolster the rosters of existing X-Teams but introduce two leaders for a brand new one - X-Factor! Covering a wide range of threats and gameplay niches, the Mirage, Havok, Jubilee & Multiple Man box brings an exciting next chapter for MCP’s mutants.

Havok

Image Courtesy AMG

Havok opens the box as a 4-Threat brawler who isn’t terribly flashy (ironic, given his power set) but can do solid damage and apply helpful conditions from afar. His stat line is pretty spot-on for the average 4-Threat in the post-August-errata world, maybe even a bit under, with only 6 HP on his injured side. It’s never great when a character has Physical as their lowest defensive stat, given that it’s the most common attack type in the game (and used for dodging), and without any defensive tech, Havok isn't a guy who wants to take too many hits.

At first glance, his two attacks also seem fairly milquetoast. Range 4, 5 dice with a wild incinerate trigger for Plasma Blast is perfectly reasonable without being overly exciting, and a B4, 7-dice Plasma Wave with Pierce on a wild and auto-Incinerate is quite nice, but 5 power is a lot! Fortunately, Havok has two ways to help those attacks hit a little harder. Plasma Dispersion is an innate power that lets Havok modify a die to a Hit for every Wild he rolls. It’s dicey, but he has a 52% chance to hit the Wild on 5 dice and 65% on 7.

His other boost to output (and survivability, if needed) is his leadership, Government Sanctioned, which should look familiar. It’s a lot of text, but it’s functionally the same as Star-Lord’s Winging It tokens. Hand one out to three characters in the Power Phase, and spend them to get two rerolls when attacking or defending. However, unlike Star-Lord, Havok requires either the attacker or defender to be contesting or holding a Secure/Extract. It also can’t be used on dodge.

Will there be niche cases when neither the attacker nor defender is on/with a point, or you wish you had it for dodge? Sure, but functionally, it’ll be the same as Winging It more often than not. Fortunately, Winging It is quite good! I don’t think Government Sanctioned is the best leadership in the box, but it seems perfectly reasonable for a punchier flex when Multiple Man doesn’t seem like the right call. He’s also affiliated with the X-Men.

Overall, Havok seems like he’ll be a perfectly fine affiliated dude, and he comes with two TTCs. Mutant X is unaffiliated and lets Havok pay 5 to play himself within Range 5 and grant an ally a superpower to heal 1 damage the first time it deals damage during its activation. It’s a neat effect, but it’s realistically not going to add up to too much healing. The Range 5 place might be worth it, but 5 Power is a lot, and he drops any Objectives he’s holding.

Tactical Operation is likely better. It’s an X-Factor Affiliated card that lets Havok pay 1 Power to let an X-Factor character Advance Short and interact after an enemy is Dazed/KOed. The obvious use is to pick up an Extract that was just dropped, but you can also use it to interact with a Pay to Flip, or simply move over to a Secure or into range to attack.

Multiple Man

Images Courtesy AMG

If Havok is too straightforward, Multiple Man makes up for it with a complex, flexible kit and an exciting leadership. Jamie Madrox starts off looking pretty underwhelming, with an under-par 4-3-3 defensive line and only 13 total health at 4 threat. A range 2, 5-dice Strike also doesn’t inspire confidence, nor does a 3-cost, 7-dice Madrox Monkey Pile. Fortunately, Multiple Man has plenty of tricks up his sleeve to get more mileage out of those numbers and impact wide swaths of the board with scenario presence. If Multiple Man’s Duplicates are within 2 of the defender for either attack, Jamie gets to reroll 2 (builder) or add 2 (spender) dice to the attack, putting him up there with the hardest-hitting 4 threats.

But the grunts aren’t just dice boost bots. The grunts have a standard 4-dice Grunt attack, but they can reroll 2 dice if Multiple Man is within 2 of the defender, which is very high damage potential for a Grunt. Moreover, these Grunts can apply Slow with the attack and interact with all objective types. They need an allied character also contesting to score, but that’s still huge. But wait, there’s more! Jamie can pay two power for Duplicate Reabsorption, which moves an Extract from the Grunts to Multiple Man, KOs the Grunts, and heals Jamie a whopping three damage (RIP Sandman).

The Grunts pop out whenever Multiple Man takes damage, and they don’t come out Staggered. With a self-throw (Follow the Leads) that damages Multiple Man (and whoever he funs into), he can easily get the Grunts out.

Multiple Man also packs the second X-Factor leadership, and it’s a doozy. X-Factor Investigations not only gives allies an extra power in the power phase if they’re contesting a Secure, but it also lets any allied character reroll one die when interacting with an objective. This obvious synergy is Pay to Flips, but it also works on things like Skrulls and any future Extracts that require rolls. With leadership and Grunts that can Interact, Multiple Man seems like a powerhouse scenario leader who can also put out massive damage if his Grunts are nearby. Jamie is also affiliated with the Uncanny X-Men.

Multiple Man gets access to two TTCs in this box. Dangerous Business is somewhat similar to Warpath, letting Jamie pay 1 Power after an allied X-Factor character is attacked (before damage is dealt) to let them Advance Short. It’s cheap, and the effect is strong, so I expect it to see regular play in X-Factor. Multitasking also seems great. For no Power cost, Jamie can play the card to remove the superpower from his Grunts that requires another ally nearby to interact or provide context. Big scoring potential there.

Mirage

Image Courtesy AMG

Mirage is a 3 Threat with incredibly high potential, but hoops you have to jump through keep it in check. She’s got a perfectly average 3-threat stat line, but she’s one of very few in that band with a range 5 builder (Neurokinetic Arrows). One a wild, her arrows give the defender a Neural Connection token, which turns on other parts of her arsenal. Psionic Salvo is a perfectly good Spender at 7 dice with a two-die trigger to make the same attack into a different character.

If enemies have a Neural Connection token, Mirage can spend 2 Power to Advance them short (at Range 5!) with Figment of Fantasy. She can also force attackers within 1 to reroll 2 attack dice, but that range goes up to 5 if they have a Neural Connection token. Time will tell how easy it is for her to drop these tokens where she wants (and she can only have one out at a time), but the effects are very strong for her threat.

Mirage also packs a New Mutants leadership, Coming of Age. It’s a unique effect that grants allies 3 Power and a Self Discovery Token when they flip to their injured side; they can spend the latter to add three dice to a defense or dodge roll. Mirage is also affiliated with X-Force and SHIELD.

Mirage has one TTC, Confront the Demon Bear. For 2 Power and an action, Mirage can make a Range 5, 7-dice attack and give the target a Neural Connection token. It’s not the most impressive card, since her main attack is already range 5, but it helps when you can’t risk needing to roll a Wild to hand out her token.

Jubilee

Image Courtesy AMG

The last character is Jubilee, who is instantly interesting for being one of the game’s few 2-Threat characters. She’s a 2-2-3 with 4 HP on each side, and she’s got a seemingly now-standard 2-Threat drawback on her scoring potential.

Lumikinetic Blast is a solid gainer, throwing 4 dice at range 4 with a Wild/Hit Stun trigger. She’s likely to be perfectly happy occasionally sitting on a back Secure and fishing for those annoying early-game Stuns on the opponent’s centerpiece model. Princess of Pyrotechnics is a 6-dice spender at range 3 with the same Wild/Hit trigger for rapid fire. Not too shabby.

Jubilee also has two Reactive Superpowers that help her stay alive and/or support her team. Fireworks lets her buy defensive rerolls for herself or allies within 2, and Wolverine! thematically lets her redirect an attack from herself to an allied James Logan Howlett within range 2.

But there’s a downside. I Totally Meant to Do That! forces Jubilee to drop any objective tokens she’s holding whenever she rolls a Crit, but he also gets a Power for her trouble. She’ll be fine sitting on a Secure, especially if Logan is nearby, but picking up an Extract is risky business. She’s mono-affiliated in X-Men, and time will tell if they need her help with threat math and which leader(s) want what’s in her toolbox.

Jubilee also gets two TTCs in the box. Blood Cursed doesn’t seem overly strong, but it’s thematic and fun. She can play the card for free during deployment, and it grants her a Reactive Superpower. After being attacked, she can pay 3 Power to place it next to her attacker, roll some dice, and deal/heal for every Crit and Wild. Though it’s hard to imagine her surviving too many hits with her defenses and HP.

Light Up the Sky lets her reroll one attack die every time an enemy attacks from within Range 3 of her. It’s a solid support effect for only 2 Power that could make its way into Rosters/Squads with more limited options.

Lastly, this box also comes with a non-character-specific X-Force card called Covert Operations. If it looks familiar to the soon-to-rotate Pretty Sneaky, Sis, that’s because it is! That card lets X-Force characters pay 2 Power to require attackers to be within Range 2 to target them. Now, an X-Force leader has to pay all the Power, but it also works on unaffiliated splashes.

Overall, the Mirage, Havok, Jubilee & Multiple Man box is a bit of a mixed bag. I’m not overly sold on Havok, and Jubilee remains a question mark with potentially high highs but some important drawbacks. Mirage seems like a slam dunk for her affiliations, and Multiple Man hits the perfect balance of creative and powerful.
