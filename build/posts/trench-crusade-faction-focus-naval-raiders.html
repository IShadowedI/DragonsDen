Trench Crusade Faction Focus: Naval Raiders

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Lore Primer

While the Heretic Fleet works below the vast Heretic Legions, they operate as a separate entity with a separate command structure, led by a High Captain and a series of admirals. The forces themselves act like pirates on the high seas, mostly working to strangle the supply lines of the faithful and conducting surprise raids on shoreline positions. The Heretic Fleet maintains naval superiority over the oceans of the world, with the exception of the Mediterranean where the Faithful are most concentrated, and does so with a variety of ships from the massive Behemoth ships to the stealth submarines, but when it needs to get something done on land, they rely on the Naval Raiders.

Naval Raiders specialize in shock assaults and boarding actions. Their armor has been adapted into diving suits, their Death Commandos are outfitted with specialty weapons for drilling through ship hulls, and even their Artillery Witches (called Sea Hags) are adapted to work with torpedoes and sea mines. To be on the receiving end of a Naval Raiders assault is to hope for death. as that is the preferable outcome to being captured as a slave, or worse, being made into an example.

On the Table

Naval Raiders are generally an elite force that adapt what you’d find in a standard Heretic Legion force to work with their standard infantry focused playstyle. You’ll likely be running Heretic Raiders with submachine guns backed up by heavier Anointed with some backline support of a Sea Hag. You’ll be moving quite quickly thanks to their special abilities and have a well rounded arsenal to work through most problems as long as you tech for them. You are down a big guy (the War Wolves stink real bad when they get wet), but you’ll have enough hard hitters you won’t necessarily be missing them.

Four Defining Traits of the Naval Raiders

Naval Raider Warband Credit: Mildnorman

Speed

Thanks to your Special Rules you get +1 DICE to dashes on everyone in the warband and can make three non-Elites Infiltrators. With that, you’ll be where you want to be quick and easy. This is good since you’ll be leaning on this to get your elite pieces to influence the table since you can’t be everywhere. 

All-Rounder

The Artillery Witch is one of the best shooting platforms in the game, and with the Sea Hag being almost an exact clone of that profile but better, you have one too. You also have access to the Drowned Chorister, one of the best melee threats in the game. Your battlekit allows you to deal with any situation as well, whether it be armored targets or needing to force a lot of markers quickly. Naval Raiders will live and die by being well equipped for the situations they find themselves in

Elite

That flexibility comes at a cost. Your dudes will run high in cost compared to other warbands and you can expect to have less models than they do. In addition, your guys will die HARD, expect to lose upwards of 70 ducats on just a lost raider.

Specialist Pieces

You have access to quite a few pieces that will need practice to use effectively. Knowing where to shove your Drowned Chorister will take practice and knowing how to leverage the Heretic Captain’s Puppet Master ability will take a few games but mastering those things will make you an opposing force to play against. The Captain, Sea Hag, Chorister, and Abyssal Commando are all pieces that do one specific job and do it very well.

Units

Heretic Captain

The Heretic Captain is your mandatory leader pick and you will never be sorry to have him along. With +2 DICE to both Ranged and Melee, you can kit them out to do anything you need them to. They also get Tough, so they can easily get in there and get their hands dirty.

On top of that, you also have the Puppet Master ability which lets you take a Risky Success roll to move a model D6”. The important thing here is you can use that ability on friendly and enemy models which makes the applications almost limitless. Need to get one of your models further up the table to make for an easy charge? Puppet Master them further up. Need to get a Sniper Priest out of a commanding overwatch position? Puppet Master them off the ledge and have them take a tumble. Got a big threat in combat but you whiffed with your Chorister? Puppet Master them out of combat and give them another swing.

Abyssal Commando

A Death Commando with their scuba certification, the Abyssal Commando is your stealthy Infiltrator designed to take out elite targets. They have a nice statline of being +1 DICE to ranged attacks, +2 DICE to melee attacks, and of course Infiltrator.  Battlekit wise, they’re limited on ranged weapons and grenades but otherwise can take any armor or melee weapon. 

Their ability Mist-shrouded gives them -1 DICE to incoming Ranged Attacks which will help them get in closer to their targets without just getting shot to pieces. In addition they have the Fade ability which lets them do a Risky Success roll with +1 DICE provided they’re in range of terrain at least .5” high or composed of water (a nice touch consider the whole diver thing), and on a success they can’t be targeted by Ranged attacks or Charges unless your opponent gets within 1.5” of them. This effect ends if they move at all or make a ranged attack, but this is fantastic for threatening certain areas of the table after getting into position.

Lastly, there’s an upgrade you can take if you have Reinforced Armor that upgrades it to Abyssal Armor for just 5 Ducats. It lets you treat any water based terrain as open terrain and lets you count as in cover when touching such a piece in addition to being able to Fade on water terrain no matter how tall or wide it is. All that said, if you have 5 Ducats lying around and are playing on some thematic Carcass Front boards this isn’t a bad upgrade to get.

Drowned Chorister

Drowned Chorister Credit: MildNorman

The Drowned Chorister is a pretty straightforward model; you point him at the thing you want to fight in the mid board and let him rock. They have a miserable Ranged characteristic of -2 DICE but a great Melee stat of +2. This means it’s not really worth giving this guy a gun even though you can give them any battlekit from the armory outside a flamethrower. The ability Unholy Hymns is the headline here, giving him an 8” bubble of -1 DICE to success rolls for enemy models. This can be devastating in the right contexts and at the very least means its -1 DICE to hit in melee. This generally means you want to up armor your Chorister with Reinforced Armour and a Shield most likely. The other ability is a 10 Ducat upgrade with which you can give him Leviathan Bell which lets you avoid the Risky Action for climbing or jumping as well as avoiding the Injury roll for falling, letting the Chorister get around the table nice and fast.

Heretic Raiders

These are your basic bozos. They work exactly like their Heretic Legion brothers, sitting at a +0 dice characteristic for both ranged and melee and they have the ability to be upgraded to Legionnaires which gives them a bonus to either Ranged or Melee, but this can only be done to half your Raiders. These are a great workhorse and not a bad candidate for your Infiltrate upgrades if you can give them the armor they need to protect themselves and the battlekit necessary to make it worth it.

Wretched

The Wretched does much the same as it does in Heretic Legion and Court, giving you a budget idiot to sit on objectives or just die in necessary places. They can be sold in the quartermaster step for their full value and a nearby Elite gets a blessing marker when the Wretched dies. You can't give them gear costing more than 10 Ducats and if they somehow kill an elite they immediately leave the game having earned their freedom. In Naval Raiders, these guys get a bit more juice due to their ability to dash a bit easier, allowing them to get to where they need to be that much more reliably.

Anointed Heretic Raiders

These are your heavy weapons guys. With +1 DICE to both ranged and melee along with built in Reinforced Armor, these guys are quality picks. Grab your favorite heavy weapon and throw it on this guy for some easy use. A mistake folks sometimes make with these guys is loading them up with too much to use in both melee and shooting. While they can do both, models rarely can effectively use a heavy ranged weapon and a heavy melee weapon, so you may want to pick one to specialize in.

Sea Hag

You know her, you love her, it's the art that got you into the game in the first place but this time she’s a water type. The Sea Hag is a Heretic Legion Artillery Witch with a few extra rules. She’s got no bonuses to hit and is -1 DICE in melee but her abilities are where she shines. Instead of taking ranged weapons she throws Infernal Bombs which are 36” range Blast 3”, Scatter, Ignore Long Range and Cover, Shrapnel weapon. It also has Ignore Elevated Position and Reload so no dashing back into cover for her and she wont get bonuses to hit from being higher up. Things near cover can get -1 to Injury from the blast but if it lands directly on them the bomb gets the DEADLY keyword. It also forces everything hit to move D3” which can cause even more havoc. Lastly, you can upgrade the bombs to Infernal Mines for 10 Ducats which makes the bomb a mine that can explode if enemies end a move too close. This means you can use her to deny space without risking the model making it a well worthwhile upgrade. On top of all this, she can Levitate, meaning she can climb and fall with no penalties and she has Abiotic Life which gives her -1 Injury Dice to Gas attacks as well as Negate Gas. She’s a great staple for your list to provide constant pressure while your forward units get into position.

Key Equipment

Heretic Raider Credit: Mildnorman

Ranged Weapons

The headliner here isn’t necessarily anything specific to Naval Raiders, but they can take a Submachine Gun on everyone for just 25 Ducats. With Shield Combo and Assault 2 this weapon is a workhorse for your fast moving hybrid troops. It's expensive, but you should always consider putting this on your Raiders before deciding on another ranged weapon.

Naval Raiders also get access to the coveted Anti-Material Rifle which goes great with your Captain. This weapon is a one stop shop problem solver for armored targets, sporting +1 to Injury and Ignore Armor on a 36” Range gun. It costs 3 Glory so you won’t get one until a ways into your campaign, but either your Captain or your favorite Anointed Raider should get this one.

For heavy weapons, you get the Grenade Launcher, Machine Gun, and Heavy Flamethrower, all of which are great options for your dudes. The Machine gun in particular can be nasty with Incendiary Ammunition but otherwise is probably the weakest of these three. Another combo to look for is an Infiltrating Anointed Raider with a Heavy Flamethrower; this makes for a very powerful piece if very expensive.

Melee Weapons

There are a lot of special melee weapons here and they’re almost all worth a look. First off is the Blasphemous Staff which costs 2 Glory and is Elite only but in addition to being just 1-Handed and having Critical and Fire, it gives non Attack or Dash rolls +1 DICE. This is fantastic for your Captain, considering it helps your Puppet Master rolls as well as helping them climb and jump to more favorable positions.

Next up is the iconic Naval Raider Boarding Axe. This was present on the very first art of Naval Raiders, and is an appropriately solid workhorse weapon. It’s 1-Handed and Gives your Attack Armoured Piercing and Critical but attacks with it are Risky. In addition though, it gives you +1 Dice to climbing which can be clutch on certain tables. At 10 Ducats it's not a bad pick for any model and two of them can go crazy if you’ve got the money to splurge, just don’t miss.

The Abyssal Commando gets some bespoke pieces of Battlekit here as well. First is a returning favorite for those familiar with the Death Commando, Tartarus Claws. These Claws have Critical, Cleave 2 and are naturally cumbersome since you can’t hold two of these in one hand. It also has a special rule that lets you move up to 3” if you take a model down or Out of Action and if you use that move to move into another enemy model you can fight them too. This is neat but don’t count on your Abyssal Commando not just doing a Blood Marker and just standing there. The other piece of gear is brand new, the Hull Drill. It’s a Held 1-Handed weapon with Ignore Armor, Critical and Risky. This is a fantastic add for your Commando, giving him a great answer for armored targets and with it being 1 handed you can pair it with a silenced pistol or another melee weapon to help seal the deal.

Another new piece of gear is the Wrecker’s Torch, which is effectively a Fire, Armour Piercing, Polearm that’s exclusive to your Elites. This is a really nice piece to take on units you want to hold the line and shoot as it’ll have your opponent thinking twice about charging them.

Lastly, but not least-ly is the good old Sacrificial Blade. This is a returning piece of kit from Heretic Legion and its a very welcome addition to the armory. Its a simple 1-Handed weapon with Risky but it has +2 Injury Modifier. This is the go to for my Choristers, giving them this and either a shield or another melee weapon to make sure they can seal the deal. This thing absolutely rips through models, just don’t miss.

Equipment

Besides the Incendiary ammo mentioned above the other piece of gear here worth shouting out is the Shark Tooth Talisman. Its a Consumable that grants Negate Fear and once per game, you can use it to get +1 DICE to melee attacks if there’s something nearby with a Blood Marker. For 10 Ducats its a bit steep, but this can help your Anointed or standard Raiders seal the deal when they really need to hit their punches.

Variants

The Drowned Choir

Leviathal Chorus Credit: Mildnorman

The Drowned Choir is what happens if a few Choristers meet up and decide they wanna hang out together. By singing together, their combined Dirge causes everything around them to descend into madness, hurling themselves into the ocean to serve the Choir. On the table, this is the Chorister variant. You can take up to three Choristers and one must be your leader, upgraded to a Leviathal Chorus. The Leviathal Chorus has the same stats but costs 20 Ducats more, Leader and Tough, and replaces their Debuff Aura with an aura that forces enemy Success Rolls to become Risky. This guy gets upgraded to a 40mm base which makes sense considering there’s a free STL for those who backed the Kickstarter for this guy. If you can combine this model’s aura with the aura from the Chorister, your opponents are in for a VERY bad time.

Speaking of other Choristers, thanks to the Mounting Madness rule, your Choristers and Leviathal Chorus auras grow by 4” for each other nearby Chorister or Leviathal Chorus. This means with all 3 on the table, you can get a 16” bubble of -1 DICE to actions and those actions are Risky. This means you’ll want to create a death ball of Choristers to send to midboard and hopefully just debuff your opponent’s models such that they can’t do much against you.

To help you in this endeavour, Wretches in this warband have a melee characteristic of +0 DICE and get +1 DICE if they’re nearby a Chorister/Chorus. They cost 30 Ducats, the same cost as a Raider, but this means you can absolutely just flood your opponent with a bunch of bozos while being locked down by the backing vocals from your Choristers. 

The bad news is these and standard Raiders are the only models you can take. You probably want a couple Raiders to just have something shooting but for the most part you’ll be throwing fanatical wretches at your opponent and will be looking to overwhelm them.

The Leviathan Shoal

You ever see that show Street Sharks? If so this is a lot like that, a bunch of buff dudes high fiving and getting stuff done. For those that haven’t, picture shark men in blue jeans, and make them huge. Anyway, Leviathan Shoal is a melee focused subfaction of Naval Raiders. Your only restrictions are you can’t take Wretched, Choristers, or Sea Hag, you can’t have less Ranged Weapons on a model than melee weapons. In addition, you can’t take any Heavy ranged weapons which means no Machine Guns or Grenade Launchers. In exchange you can take up to 4 Anointed Heretic Raiders, one of which can be recruited as an Elite, and up to 4 Sacrificial Blades that can be given to Anointed Heretic Raiders as well. 

In addition you get two nifty warband wide abilities. First is Feeding Frenzy which gives you +1 DICE to hit things in melee for every model with a Blood Marker within an inch. This includes the model making the attack too, so this is nice for mitigating markers as well. Your other ability is Chosen Sons, which gives an Anointed Raider Tough after it takes out the first model in melee. 

Overall this is a really solid set of rules but the drawbacks make it a bit tough to build around. The losing access to your heavy weapons and the Sea Hag means you’ll be working without much of the ranged pressure Naval Raiders bring to the table. The 4 Sacrificial Knives shouldn’t be underestimated however, considering they’re one of the best melee weapons in the game. Overall, if you’re looking to lean into big guys and gals this is a cool way to do it, but I’d stick to vanilla raiders unless you’re a die hard fan of this playstyle.

  • Infernal Mines - 10

This list aims to give you a solid and varied start to a campaign and a good foundation to build from. The Sea Hag provides some really solid early campaign threat and the Heavy Anointed Flamethrower is meant to go around killing big armored targets while also being able to hold their own in combat. Submachine guns are just really good so no reason not to take that on as many guys as possible too so as you add raiders, always consider grabbing those. 

Final Thoughts

Naval Raiders do exactly what you’d expect; they get in fast and blast your opponent with a ton of bullets or hard hitting melee threats. They’re fairly elite, but you can kind of build anything you want out of them. In terms of their transition from variant to full fledged faction, they still kinda feel like a Heretic Legion variant with all the shared models and battlekit, but they definitely have their own niche without a Big Guy. Either way, they’re a welcome addition to the roster of faction choices.
