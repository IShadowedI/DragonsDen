[AoS] Competitive Innovations in the Mortal Realms: Onto Aqshy, the Realm of Fire!

July 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

With the General's Handbook coming out last weekend, a lot of the usual clubs took a break rather than run Ghyran again, so we're back and fully in the realm of Aqshy this week! We'll see some interesting change ups to the usual lists (and of course some returning superstars) thanks to the slew of new warscrolls, points changes and rules updates for every army.

  • 2026 Vienna Summer Tabletop Major AoS Singles - Austria

Thoughts

As the largest event of this first weekend of the new GHB, it presents a new wide field of opportunities. It's also serendipitous that the Slaanesh Battletome is not only playable but makes an appearance to the top slot, so we can take a look at what angle the army might be headed in.

Cities of Sigmar have proven themselves gaining some pretty good traction in the past few weeks since the book's debut, mostly due to the sheer power and versatility offered by Cavaliers, but also supported by some of the combat tricks of Fusiliers, Grenadiers and Steelhelms.

Slaanesh has the potential to tank a lot of hits with their Painbringers here, while still retaining a lot of mobility from other units and having a solid hammer from the allied Daemon Prince. The challenges include being able to outmaneuver COS's combat tricks and breaking in the new Paragon system.

Archetype

Cavaliers and Fusiliers

Thoughts

This list is pretty much unchanged from what Dylan has been running (and by virtue of his place in the ITC, most Cities players are running), not even running the new Aqshy toys, battle tactics excepted of course.

The Cavaliers are the star of the show here, they have the ability to charge when in combat, and while they do have to remain in combat with the thing they started in, a block of 10 has a lot of room to move around if it can find a juicier target to latch onto. This can make them difficult to pin down, able to try and go after a nearby unit, or reposition themselves in such a way that piling in is more difficult. Steelhelms stand by their side forcing the enemy to fight a far less appetizing target, as well.

Behind them Grenadiers and Fusiliers are not great damage but pretty reliable with their Rend 2 shots. Thexa also works as a solid control piece, shutting off crits and enabling a unit to fight and then duck out after, saving them from being hit and possibly grabbing an objective if close enough.

Archetype

Warriors of Chaos

Thoughts

Pretty much the same as it's always been. The Chaos Chosen are one of the strongest hammers once they get into combat, and with the Dread Banner shutting off commands that allows them to avoid getting hit with an All out Attack (or their target popping all out Defence) makes them a real problem to deal with. The new Chaos Warriors can become quite strong once enough Rage is built up, and the Artillery RoR gives them much needed ranged firepower.

Thoughts

With some of the radical changes to Kruleboyz for the season it is interesting to see where they're going to end up. Kragnos implies a much more aggressive Kruleboyz army and they're going right into a force of Bloodletters. Both armies are popping Crit Mortals like there's no tomorrow so it's likely there won't be any models left when the blood settles.

Archetype

Kragnos and Infantry

Thoughts

Kruleboyz got some radical changes to their army with the end of Scourge of Ghyran. They did spectacularly when the Swamphorde Bullies battle formation debuted, then less so once points hikes happened but they held their own. Since Aqshy didn't include battle formations this time, there was a lot of questions on what they could do now.

Probably the biggest sleeper buff is the ability to use multiple dirty tricks in a phase. It didn't make sense to both ratchet up the required dice roll each time and limit it to once per phase, especially because some of the dirty tricks are really bad. So what can you do now?

By bringing Kragnos and willing to be patient to wait for combat, you can pop Venom Encrusted weapons multiple times and slam in with multiple units doing crit mortals on a 5+.

Thoughts

We have not seen Seraphon in the showdown in a while and the week it loses its SoG Carnosaur? Incredible.

I'd think that Seraphon would have a tough go here, but they have tools for dealing with the enemy. They're tanky as hell so the lack of mortals from Hellsmiths will be a real challenge for them. Archetype

Big dinos

Thoughts

The death of the SoG Carnosaur does leave a dinosaur shaped hole in the heart of every Seraphon player. The base version isn't bad but it isn't as good either. Luckily, the Monsters in the faction in general got cheaper. Much of the heavy lifting is done by the monsters here, they're quite tanky and while not the highest damage dealer, they're still solid. The Skink Oracle on Troglodon can head in alongside them to make enemy units -1 to hit, which will help survivability a bit longer.

Solid list for people who want to just slam big dinosaurs into stuff.

Thoughts

This is an interesting matchup. Cities of Sigmar are one of the top armies and while Sylvaneth were sharing that spot, the nerfs they took for the new Battlescroll casts that in a bit of doubt.

Without Drycha, it's more challenging to deal with the Cavalier shenanigans. However, both armies are really solid castle armies, preferring to bait the enemy to charge them and pouncing. Sylvaneth, however, have more shenanigans to move and teleport around while Cities have to do more of a slow expansion. That gives Sylvaneth time to set up and put Overgrown on more terrain, giving them a bit of an advantage on home turf.

It ends with a respectable 12 point difference.

The List

See Showdown

Archetype

Big Trees

Thoughts

The nerf to Drycha makes her a lot less appealing now, and she was basically an auto take so Sylvaneth players are re-evaluating their lists.

Belthanos is still as good as he was, being a mobile hammer who makes Kurnoth able to run faster. Accompanying him are the Spirit of Durthu and the new Treelord Ancient, both rather strong hammers into the enemy. This creates a very elite list that wants to avoid getting out into the open but as long as it remains in the shelter of its overgrown tokens, is very hard to dismantle.
