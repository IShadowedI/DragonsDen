Thoughts

Hey, one of those players was our own Tom Lowman! So rather than write up a complicated blurb here on his game that's certain to miss something, I've asked him to write up an analysis of the final match himself. That way you get to read some more insightful than "Custodes win when they roll their 4+ invulnerable saves and lose when they don't, good talk."

Tom: I had done some research into Adam's list before the event, as I had a hunch the final might end up involving him. These new Allarus heavy Custodes lists are awesome - very difficult to screen, exceptionally punchy in melee and at range, and full of 4-wound, 2+/4++ save men that make the massed damage 3 in the meta a little bit awkward to use. For my part, I have the excellent scoring of Priority Assets (using my Chaos Spawn as early scoring and bait), and some damage units that absolutely cleave through Custodes units if they can connect.

We chatted before the game and our assessment broadly was that the match was drawish - playing Priority Assets my Emperor's Children have a slight passive score advantage, but the massive threat of heroic interventions and rapid ingresses means that it is very difficult for me to push into the Custodes, who get to work their way into the game and then push me.

This is very much how the first few turns of the game played out - I use my outer layer of Spawn and chaff to screen any nasty Allarus Ingresses, whilst picking off Custodes units where I was able. In turn Adam threatened Fight on Death and Intervention wherever he could to keep a secure hold on his expansion and begin to threaten mine.

In the end, we both maxed Primary with Adam winning narrowly on Secondaries. We identified that there was a moment in 3-4 where I mismanaged the screen of some Allarus after a Lord Exultant suddenly bombed a ten inch charge I hadn't been expecting him to make. This created too many engagements and allowed the Allarus to get up the board a turn early, which hurt my ability to keep them back and gave Adam a couple of cards he probably would have struggled to get otherwise. I'd played a very good passive game early on, but I needed to evolve that into pushing back at Adam more decisively in the mid to late game - getting this wrong ultimately saw me come out the wrong side of the draw, 93-97.

Adam was a brilliant opponent, and the game was collaborative, intense and hard fought, exactly like a final should be. Congratulations on your victory!!

Result

Adeptus Custodes (Might of the Moritoi/Lions of the Emperor) Victory - 97 - 93

Thoughts

This one's got video! You can see the stream from Wargames Live here:

We've talked a lot about Darkflight this week but now you get to see them in action in a game where they actually lose. Which isn't quite what Jeremy wants broadcast, but them's the breaks. Darkflight lists tend to do a great job controlling the tempo, picking of units that stray too far afield while staying out of range of the rest of the opponent's army. By keeping an opponent from interacting with anything on turns 1/2, they can control the pace of the game and scoring and force opponents to engage on their terms.

In this particular game, Jeremy's fatal mistake was getting a bit too aggressive - he put the Land Speeder Vengeance a bit too far forward, then repeated the mistake with the Lion and Azrael. The turn 2 Vengeance loss was the start of a snowball effect, giving the Emperor's Children player Bring it Down and extra forward movement while pushing the Lion too far forward opened him him up to a charge that removed him pretty quickly - Frenzied Host is a big help going into the Lion as it gives the Infractors S5 on their attacks on the charge, doubling their expecting output with their Duelling Sabres. This is more or less only possible with the changes to Fights First in 11th edition, and once the Lion goes down, Azrael is more or less undefended. Likewise, the Daemonettes removing Azrael and the Defiler taking out the Inner Circle Companions essentially left Jeremy without much in the way of strong melee pieces on turn 3, capping off a huge turn that ended up being the backbreaker.

If you've never played against the Carnival of Excess Defiler, count yourself lucky. The Reaper Autocannons on the EC Defiler come with [SUSTAINED HITS 1] built-in, making them one of the few units in the army which can immediately benefit from the 5+ crits. So your Defilers hit on 2+ against targets above half strength with Sustained Hits 1 and crits on 5+. I slept on these and then one shot Magnus to death in one activation. Don't let it happen to you.

Thoughts

I'm a bigger fan of single defiler Coterie EC these days; while you lose the redundancy you make up for it by just having more pieces to play with on the table. Carnival of Excess lists can get around this by running Daemonettes, which still cost 90 points but provide 10 bodies for the cost. They also just get better Defilers, but that's beside the point. This list runs a 10-model Infractor squad in a Rhino with an attached character, and is likely to scout that one forward while keeping the Noise Marines and another unit close by so they can hop in after the Infractors hop out.
