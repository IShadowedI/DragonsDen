BattleTech: Lore Primer

August 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Catalyst Game Labs, current publisher of BattleTech and driving force behind its recent renaissance, has done a lot to improve onboarding into the BattleTech setting recently, and this venerable game has inspired a wealth of other resources as well.

There are a lot of on-ramps a curious onlooker or a fan returning from hiatus can look towards. The new, now-arriving-on-shelves Starter Box includes the "BattleTech: Sagas" booklet, a condensed 24 page introduction to the universe, with a focus on presenting the major factions in play. The coming Core Book itself includes a wealth of lore sidebars, with each chapter doubling as a faction focus (which allows for some cute associations, like the combined-arms-focused Clan Hell's Horses being introduced in the chapter on Battlefield Support). BattleTech Universe offers a fantastic coffee-table-book style introduction to the full breadth of the setting. There's a brief run through the eras of BattleTech's in-universe history at the back of every modern publication of a BT novel, along with a glossary of terms and art of the mechs featured in that book. The impressive, if imperfect, fan wiki Sarna is available as a free online resource. Heck, probably the best introduction I've ever seen for the setting leading up to 3025, the starting point in the timeline at the tabletop game's inception, was the completely wordless intro to Harebrained Schemes' BattleTech video game.

There's a lot of ways to dip your toe into BattleTech lore, is what I'm saying.

This is the only ramble about what the ilClan era is and how we got here from one of your beloved BattleTech writers here on Tabletop Battles Dot Com, however! Hopefully the blend of in-universe lore, behind-the-scenes tidbits, passion for this setting, and unadulterated snark I have to offer can entertain and inform you on this fine day.

Star League Defense Force Javelin. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Elephant Star League in the Room Timeline

As much as I hate "It's Game of Thrones, but [X]!" as an elevator pitch, the interstellar political soap opera of BattleTech is just as vital to its setting as the big stompy robots. If "it's Game of Thrones in space" helps you understand that, then sure, bingo, you got it. I basically can't say a word about any of that political intrigue, though, without first talking about the (First) Star League.

The first bar your suspension of disbelief must climb when approaching BattleTech lore is that in the space-future, Feudalism (well, Neo-Feudalism) became cool again. As humanity spread from Terra to the stars via the Kearny-Fuchida Jump Drive, the emergent power players crystalized into a new nobility. As anyone who's met more than one human might have guessed, this quickly led to conflict.

The emergent powers of the Inner Sphere soon found out that you can be a lot more careless about using weapons of mass destruction when you're using them against a planet you don't live on. Unfortunately, using them against people on planets you don't live on might lead to those people's friends using them on your planet, so that's a bit of an escalating problem. This led to the signing of the Ares Conventions, an outline for limited warfare which helped the BattleMech emerge as the king of the battlefield despite the "Wait, wouldn't giant robot dudes be vulnerable to saturation bombing?" of it all.

It took another century and some change past the introduction of the Ares Conventions, but (relative) peace came to (the Great Houses of) the Inner Sphere with the establishment of the Star League, a coalition which was... kind of half Space United Nations and half Space Holy Roman Empire.

The glorious first act of this new union was, of course, the violent subjugation of the economically-disadvantaged states at the Periphery of human settlement, dragging them kicking and screaming into the Star League's embrace. The popular conception of the First Star League among the Inner Sphere (and the Clans, who... we'll get to) remembers its ascendancy as a golden age of peace and prosperity, but the Periphery remembers the Star League era verrrry differently.

Pride Goes Before a Fall

Stefan Amaris, scion of the most powerful family in the Rim Worlds Republic, one of the Periphery states conquered by the Star League, eventually worked his way into a position of influence which allowed him to assassinate the First Lord of the Star League, usurp control of Terra, and trigger a bloody civil war. His coup was eventually defeated by the Star League Defense Force, but the beloved commanding general of the SLDF, Aleksandr Kerensky, decided he was absolutely fed up with the politics of the Inner Sphere and was willing to take drastic measures to prevent the SLDF from becoming a pawn in anyone else's game.

In a move which must have seemed insane at the time, Kerensky gathered his loyalists - the majority of the SLDF, since his personal charisma was most of what kept them together throughout the Amaris Civil War - and led them on an Exodus into the uncharted systems of the Deep Periphery, where they'd go on to become a sort of "King in the Mountain" myth for the people they left behind in the Inner Sphere. Put a pin in this, we'll be coming back to the self-proclaimed SLDF-in-Exile.

Locust 1v2. Credit: porble

Succ Wars

With the Star League fallen, its ruling family (House Cameron) purged, the Terran Hegemony shattered, and the SLDF effing off into unknown space, there was suddenly a gaping power vacuum in the space which had previously stopped the Great Houses of the Inner Sphere from going to war with each other.

Naturally, they almost immediately went back to war with each other.

The rulers of each of the five biggest polities in the Inner Sphere (the Coordinator of the Draconis Combine, the head of House Kurita; the First Prince of the Federated Suns, the head of House Davion; the Chancellor of the Capellan Confederation, the head of House Liao; the Captain-General of the Free Worlds League, the head of House Marik; and the Archon of the Lyran Commonwealth, the head of House Steiner) all declared themselves to be the true successor to the First Lord of the Star League, kicking off the centuries-long all-against-all (or at least most-against-most) conflict of the Succession Wars.

The early Succession Wars saw WMDs and atrocities return to the fore as the Ares Conventions were thrown out the window. They forgot the promise of progress and understanding, there was no peace among the stars, you know the drill. This led to much of the Star League's advanced technology being lost, and the "Successor States" returning to Ares-Conventions-esque limited warfare out of sheer necessity as they exhausted their people's will to fight a total war alongside their war materiel.

The earliest material published for BattleTech framed it as a hardscrabble post-apocalyptic setting in which power flowed from possession of one of the few remaining functional BattleMechs and the tiny lance-on-lance conflicts of the average tabletop gaming session could decide the fate of entire worlds. This portrayal lasted... approximately no time at all. The very first novel published for BattleTech kicked off a trilogy which ended with a LosTech data cache being disseminated across the Sphere, sparking an industrial revolution as old Star League technologies began to become accessible again, and the number of mechs on the average in-lore battlefield ballooned as writers with a background in military sci-fi grew in influence, their modern-warfare-inflected mindset supplanting the original "MechWarrior as feudal knight" concept.

By the time the Fourth Succession War caught flame... within three real-world years of the first publication bearing the name "BattleTech!"... large-scale conventional war was most certainly the norm, as the Federated Suns and Lyran Commonwealth fought to erase the distance between their realms to physically join the states their rulers had already joined in matrimony. The newly-minted "Federated Commonwealth" seemed on the cusp of Winning BattleTech, as a setting...

...and then everything changed, when the Fire Furry Nation(s) attacked.

Kodiak. Credit: porble

The Clans

Long story comically short, the SLDF-in-Exile found itself squatting on some pretty shitty, marginally-habitable worlds, and they were troubled by internal division as old ethnic tensions they brought with them from the Inner Sphere began to reemerge. General Kerensky's mentally unstable son, Nicholas, decided that the only solution to this was to destroy his people's connections with their Spheroid heritage and reforge them into a wholly new society.

Naturally, Nicholas's utopian vision was a caste-based, ruthlessly meritocratic, fantasy eugenicist, warrior-ruled society whose artificial division into manufactured (and largely animal-themed) "Clans" would help channel their aggression into strictly-controlled ritualized combat rather than full-blown internecine violence.

Over time, these Clans would largely come to believe that they were destined to return to the decadent Inner Sphere as [conquerors come to end the Successor States' ceaseless squabbling by force / protectors come to guide the Inner Sphere toward peace... by force, your political philosophy may vary]. Per their traditions, while they were unified in their goal of conquering the Inner Sphere, each individual invading Clan would (largely) fight its own war on its own terms, competing for glory. The Clan to take Terra itself would make its mark on history as the "ilClan," forever honored as the foremost among their number.

...no, this doesn't lead directly to the modern "ilClan Era," there's a bunch of detours in between. We'll get there, I promise.

The Clan Invasion

Despite the fact that this is a thoroughly-fleshed-out piece of fictional history which unfolded as live metaplot developments over basically the entire decade of the 1990s, I'm going to yadda-yadda this harder than I did the "prehistory" of the setting. If you have any casual exposure to BattleTech, this is probably the part you've heard about.

The Clans invade! They have an enormous technological advantage and a higher quality of training on average! Their initial victories are slowed by their rigid honor code, difficulty assimilating captive worlds without shared cultural underpinnings, the actually-thoroughly-understandable logistical difficulties of how far the Inner Sphere is from the Clan Homeworlds, and their hard-wired inability to stop competing with each other. The tide begins to turn.

The most important developments here for the big-picture overview are basically that the Inner Sphere is forced to cooperate in the face of this overwhelming external threat, leading to a fragile, fractious alliance they over-ambitiously dub the "Second Star League," and the Clans' invasion stalls out to an agreed-upon stalemate, with their progress towards Terra halted, but much of their invasion zone intact, leaving the invader Clans in prolonged cultural contact with their subject peoples and Spheroid neighbors.

Bushwacker. Credit: porble

It Is a Period of Civil War

The next big-name "era" and marquee plot for BattleTech following the Clan Invasion was the FedCom Civil War, as Katrina Steiner(-Davion) (Yes, lore heads checking my work, I know she was born "Katherine," but I respect chosen names!) evilly asserted rulership over the nation she had, uh, already functionally been left in charge of when her wannabe-action-hero brother Victor ran off to chase Clan Smoke Jaguar back to its home planet, kicking off a civil war in which the narrative wanted us to be certain she was definitely the villain.

I said earlier that the Clan Invasion was what you probably knew if you had casual exposure to BattleTech, but if you were a video gamer in the early '00s, this was the conflict featured in MechCommander 2 and MechWarrior 4: Mercenaries. This is actually the chunk of the setting I first cut my teeth on, before I'd even heard the name "BattleTech."

It was also one of the last gasps of a dying FASA, as the company which ushered BattleTech into the world went the way of TSR before them. For a certain flavor of gamer, this is The End of BattleTech, with everything past this point a shambling zombie wearing its face, but despite some pretty abysmal PR as the IP transitioned to its new owners and new, uh, eras (yes, plural, gimme a second), there's actually a remarkable degree of continuity from FASA's BattleTech to the WizKids/FanPro years to Catalyst, with more than a few writers following the property from company to company.

Hastati Sentinels Malice. Credit: Jack Hunter

The End of History

So from a meta perspective, and in fandom consciousness, things get messy at this point. If you aren't interested in how sausage is made and how fans form Star-Wars-Prequel-Trilogy-tier opinions about things that [ruined / were foundational to] their childhood... I apologize, and you can skip to the next heading.

For the rest of us... I'm sure this is going to be in some way inaccurate, but I'm writing this past midnight on my birthday and I still need to bake my own cake in the morning, so I'm going to skip refreshing myself on research and just give it to you as I understand it, as someone who both lived through these events and has read a lot of other people relitigating this era.

Basically, late-FASA-era material had already seeded the plot hooks for the Word of Blake, a radical faction of ComStar, the semi-religious telecommunications cult keeping interstellar FTL communication running, to cause the next major interstellar crisis by throwing a massive shitfit in response to the Second Star League collapsing amidst the FedCom Civil War. Meanwhile, WizKids, simultaneously the new owners of the IP and a company founded by one of BattleTech's original creators, wanted a fresh-start relaunch returning the setting to its post-apocalyptic roots, and wanted to pursue a more streamlined, more marketable collectible-miniatures game design along the lines of its successful Mage Knight property.

Thus, two eras were born. "Classic" BattleTech, and the timeline-continuing Word of Blake "Jihad" (it was the War on Terror era IRL, I'm sorry), were placed in the hands of licensee FanPro, while WizKids jumped ahead in the timeline for their game MechWarrior: Dark Age, set in the wake of a devastating Blackout of the interstellar communications network which had been maintained by ComStar.

This is getting a bit into rumor and conjecture, but it's my general understanding that the Jihad and the Dark Age were originally conceived to follow one right after the other. MW:DA's rough-hewn BattleMechs and repurposed IndustrialMechs might have naturally followed on from the industrial devastation wrought by, and RetroTech experiments in response to, the Blakist Jihad, and the Blackout itself would've made perfect sense as the last vengeful gesture of a defeated Word of Blake. If one era flowed directly into the other, I feel the transition would be smoother, easier to explain, and might have raised less ire among fans. (Though I'm sure the doomsaying around Classic being "dead" would have still continued.)

Unfortunately for thematic consistency, a desire to give FanPro more timeline to work with / to distance MWDA more from CBT / someone getting enthusiastic about the Republic of the Sphere / IDK, something, ended up putting fifty years of relative peace between the end of the Jihad and the start of the Dark Age (and ultimately left the Blackout itself almost completely unexplained!), raising a zillion questions among fans. These are largely explicable (early expansions of the 'Clix game were basically exclusively set inside Republic territory as it began to fall apart, the shoddy state of armaments available was the result of the forces being impromptu militias hastily cobbled together in what had previously been a peaceful state with strong-for-a-wargaming-setting arms regulations, the actual Successor States and Clans never disarmed and their still-fully-functional militaries showed up in later expansion sets), but the whole situation is a headache which probably could have been avoided, particularly with closer collaboration between WizKids and FanPro.

I haven't even mentioned how the Jihad forcibly had to shift from the novel-driven storytelling BattleTech had become accustomed to towards sourcebook-driven storytelling because WizKids wanted novels to be exclusively Dark Age for consistency (which did actually work very well for branding; I was there, and I can tell you there were waaaay more MWDA books in mainstream bookstores then than there are modern BT novels now, that's just true).

Anyway. I was supposed to be telling you how we got to the ilClan era.

Atlas II. Credit: porble

The Phone Company's Religious Fanatics Get Pissy

The Word of Blake Jihad is extremely an artifact of its out-of-universe time. An insidious group of religious fanatics with influence everywhere unleashes a wave of terror attacks while indiscriminately throwing around WMDs, false-flagging and manipulating other powers to fight each other, almost all the characters you know and love from previous fiction die in pointless, brutal ways, etc., etc. It's a Universal War on Terror plotline written during the "Global War on Terror." It somehow still manages to be less xenophobic/racist than early-BattleTech Sinophobia by virtue of its religious fanatics being cyborg techno-cultists of no particular ethnicity.

Oh, also, the Clan Homeworlds simultaneously went through their own fanatic, philosophy-driven purity purge, destroying several minor-character Clans which hadn't ever really gotten out of the Homeworlds and driving most of the others to relocate exclusively to the Inner Sphere. (Or exclusively to the slightly less Deep Periphery, in the case of Clan Goliath Scorpion.)

Somehow This Wasn't a "Star League"

In the wake of the Jihad, a new government, a pseudo-Roman-Republican "Republic of the Sphere," emerged on Terra and managed to win territorial concessions from basically everyone except the Capellans to form a pretty substantial state at the center of the Inner Sphere. Despite governing with the consent of the Great Houses, serving as a buffer between them, keeping interstellar conflict at relatively low levels... in other words, despite doing all the Star League things, the Republic never declared itself a Star League. Go figure.

Savage Wolf (Mad Cat Mk IV). credit: porble

Then Came the Night the Lights Went Out (for More Than Georgia)

After approximately 50 years of the Pax Republica, the HPG network, the series of FTL communications relays holding the Inner Sphere's economy together, suddenly, seemingly-irrecoverably, crashed, almost in its entirety. This "HPG Blackout" ushered in a new age of war as long-suppressed old rivalries sprang back into the light.

At the same time the Republic of the Sphere began to decay, ferocious new leaders emerged within two of the most powerful, most belligerent Clans.

From Clan Jade Falcon, historically violently traditionalist, came the self-proclaimed "Chinggis Khan" Malvina Hazen, a ruthless, unstable leader who adopted brutal scorched earth policies in an attempt to rule by absolute fear.

From the historically pragmatic Clan Wolf, the cunning Alaric Ward emerged, the test tube progeny of the deposed Katrina Steiner (who took refuge with Clan Wolf following her defeat in the FedCom Civil War, becoming "Katherine Wolf"), former Wolf Khan Vlad Ward, and, secretly, also Katherine's brother Victor's DNA got mixed in there, too. Theoretically, Alaric lives at the juncture of Clan talent and honor and Spheroid cunning and tact, but how well he lives up to that ideal - and how well he's supposed to actually meet those expectations - definitely varies between writers.

With the Republic crumbling, and Malvina and Alaric both possessed of ambition without limits, Clans Jade Falcon and Wolf renewed their near-century-delayed advance on Terra. While the Republic was able to delay the inevitable through the use of some pretty bizarre super-technology which blocked JumpShips from safely entering their remaining territory, they couldn't keep up that defense forever.

In what came to be known as "the ilClan Trial," nearly the entirety of Wolf and Falcon's fighting forces clashed over Terra.

When the dust cleared, Alaric Ward was the ilKhan, Wolf the ilClan.

Kontio, painted by Porble, image provided by Catalyst Game Labs

The Modern Sphere

And that brings us to the current "ilClan Era," the bleeding edge of BattleTech's still-ongoing metaplot. In the current day...

Marik Militia Lance. Credit: Rockfish

The Free Worlds League

Still fairly-recently-reunited after having balkanized in the wake of the Blakist Jihad, the Free Worlds League has weathered some very strong challenges from its neighbors over the past decade or two. It currently stands at a crossroads, however. Alaric carved a "Wolf Empire" out of its flank to use as a staging ground for his push to Terra, but that territory is now only very lightly defended, with the bulk of Wolf forces pulled toward Terra itself. The time is ripe for an invasion of the Wolf Empire, and, indeed, the actions of eager nobles along the border have forced the central government's hand towards action. The FWL harbors an enclave of Clanners, however, with their "Clan Protectorate" a full voting bloc in their Parliament, and the dominant faction in the Protectorate's leadership has declared allegiance to the ilKhan, signalling civil strife ahead.

Lyran Commonwealth Wolfhound, Vedette, Crusader, and Hunchback. Credit: SRM

The Lyran Commonwealth

Beset by crisis after crisis in recent years, the Lyran Commonwealth has begun to crumble. While the Archon still retains control over much of its territory (at least nominally!), its historically-strong economy is in shambles, multiple breakaway states have carved worlds from its flanks, and it hasn't had any significant military victories to claim in a good while. Things are not looking good for the Lyrans, fueled in no small part by the emergence of...

The Jade Falcon Epsilon Galaxy forces from Battletech Aces: Scouring Sands. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Hinterlands

The Wolf Empire was left drastically weakened by Alaric's push to Terra, but Malvina Hazen never did anything by halves: the Jade Falcon Occupation Zone was all-but stripped completely clean of Falcons in her desperation to reach humanity's cradle. While basically all this abandoned Falcon territory was once part of the Lyran Commonwealth, many of its people had grown accustomed to Falcon rule over the past hundred years, and many more had grown resentful of the Archon who, at one time or another, failed them.

The Hinterlands have become one of the most interesting, dynamic portions of the setting, a volatile collection of microstates including a tiny "Clan Jade Falcon" that's trying to redeem its reputation, a deposed once-Archon trying to scam together a domain for himself, a state led by ex-Clan merchants trying to move beyond their Warrior-dominated past, a coalition grown out of the venerable Kell Hounds mercenary command, resurgences of the Tamar Pact and the Isle of Skye, and more besides.

Clan Hell's Horses

The Weird, Harley-Davidson-Riding Uncle of the Clans, the Hell's Horses sure are still present in the Inner Sphere. They're coming off a long string of being the Jade Falcons' punching bag (even taking a loss to Jiyi Chistu's tiny, underpowered remnant of the Jade Falcons!), but maybe they'll get to do something interesting soon? They did tell Alaric he could suck it when he asked for their allegiance, so that's fun.

Ghost Bear Alpha Galaxy Ebon Jaguar (Cauldron-Born). Credit: porble

The Rasalhague Dominion

Long story short, the "cites Family as often as Vin Diesel in a Fast movie" Clan Ghost Bear and the "democratic-ish Norsemen" Free Rasalhague Republic have managed to achieve a fairly stable cultural fusion as the "Rasalhague Dominion." When Alaric demanded the Ghost Bears' allegiance as ilKhan, the Dominion put it to a vote. The pro-new-Star-League side won that vote, but Alaric got pissy over the fact that 1.) they voted at all, and 2.) it wasn't unanimous support for him, so he refused their allegiance. This roller coaster will-we-won't-we caused a surge of civil unrest within the Dominion, which developed into a shockingly violent, if brief, civil war.

At present, the Dominion's leadership has successfully diverted the Bears' aggression towards an old foe...

Spider. 8Xr variant. Credit: porble

The Draconis Combine

People talk about mixed blessings, but can there be mixed curses as well?

After a dramatic series of military successes in the mid 3140s saw the Draconis Combine establish a salient deep enough into Federated Suns space to capture the FedSuns' capital world of New Avalon itself, those gains have been almost completely erased by recent Suns counteroffensives. On the other hand, the inconveniently-successful, inconveniently-influential warlord who led that conquest, who wished to use sitting Coordinator Yori Kurita as a puppet while exercising real power in the Combine himself, is now dead, with his greatest victory turned to ashes, significantly improving the security of Yori's seat.

That said, the Dragon is still deeply embroiled in conflict with the Federated Suns while the Rasalhague Dominion now presses on their other border; they're hardly in a comfortable position. But neither are...

Davion Heavy Guard force for an event. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Federated Suns

The Federated Suns' successes against the Draconis Combine have come at the price of offering a lot of landholds to various mercenaries as part of their payment, and tensions between older nobility and the newly enfeoffed sellswords are likely to become an issue. First Prince Julian Davion also has the problem that the popular Prince's Champion Erik Sandoval-Groell won more glory than he in the retaking of the Dragon's Tongue, and tensions are fairly high between the two men.

And right, also, they've additionally been attacked by...

Steve / Sol Roth's Raven Alliance Alpha Galaxy. Photo taken by Lynn B.

The Raven Alliance

The merger between largely-agrarian Periphery state the Outworlds Alliance and the displaced-from-the-Homeworlds Clan Snow Raven has not been as complete as the Rasalhague Dominion's integration, but the two aerospace-fighter-loving peoples have still built an effective partnership. The Ravens were among the earliest supporters of the new-minted ilKhan, and their current offensive against the Federated Suns is being carried out in the name of his new Star League.

Clan Sea Fox Spina Khanate Tiburon. Credit: Lynn B.

Clan Sea Fox

Another founding member of the Third Star League, the mercantile-minded Clan Sea Fox continue to play as many sides as they can get away with to their continued profit. They seem to be flexing into spaces previously filled by ComStar. They've not only become the premier mercenary bonding authority of the Sphere in recent years, they have secured the assistance of Dark-Age-era MacGuffin-Man Tucker Harwell and seem to be on the cusp of implementing a fix for the HPG Blackout.

What remains to be seen is how the internal politics of the new Star League may (or may not, but my money is on may) bite them in the tail. At the moment it seems they're still selling to, and bonding mercenaries for, enemies of their own Star League, and that doesn't seem like a status quo that anyone is likely to tolerate for long!

Speaking of enemies of the Star League...

2 lances of Capellan Confederation Battlemechs. Credit: Perigrin

The Capellan Confederation

The Capellans have neatly transitioned from being the most persistent enemy of the Republic to being the most persistent enemy of the new Star League. Having never even made a cursory gesture towards participating in Republic era disarmament programs, the Capellan Confederation was consistently one of the strongest military powers of the Dark Age, and they carried that strength into challenging Clan Wolf and their allies in the immediate wake of the ilClan Trial.

That war has seen... mixed results, and the Capellans currently find themselves with a brand new Chancellor in the form of Danai Liao-Centrella, after previous leader Daoshen Liao definitely died in a Snow Raven bombardment under circumstances which were in no way suspicious at all. The Capellans' Andurien allies have also taken advantage of the chaos to suddenly turn and stab them in the flank; this is almost certainly the result of communication breaking down between Daoshen and his, uh, sister-lover Ilsa Centrella, who's the ruler of the Magistracy of Canopus but is married to the leader of the Duchy of Andurien, Ari Humphreys, and...

...look, okay, sometimes the Game of Thrones comparison hits closer than others, what can I say?

Clan Wolf Beta Galaxy Heavy Star. Credit: Jack Hunter

Clan Wolf

The ilClan themselves are in their Suffering From Success phase. Having very nearly expended their strength in the process of becoming ilClan, they've had to bargain away increasingly more of their influence over their own Star League just to keep the lights on and the guns loaded.

Also, bucking centuries of Clan tradition, Alaric has refused to give up the title of Wolf Khan despite claiming the ilKhanship; this haaaas to be breeding resentment in saKhan Chase Vickers, who's left actually governing Clan Wolf day-to-day without the title to go with that labor.

Savage Wolf (Mad Cat Mk IV). Credit: Rockfish

The SLDF

Aaaaalso, Alaric has established a new SLDF to go with his new Star League, which is not only separate from the Wolf touman and staffed largely by Republic veterans, but is under the command of Anastasia Kerensky, a former bitter rival of Alaric who once captured him and tortured him for days, and who also personally killed one of his former lovers. Fun, fun fertile ground for drama there.

And More!

I've run briefly through the current status quo of the major players (and also Hell's Horses) here, but there's plenty more interesting stuff going on in this developing ilClan era. Periphery powers! Reborn Clans filling out the ranks of Alaric's Star League! Characterful mercenary units! We're feasting good these days on BattleTech content, y'all.

Final Thoughts

Blake's Blood, this article ran on longer than I intended! The short of that long is that I'm really excited by the point where we stand at BattleTech history, with dog-who-caught-the-car Alaric Ward struggling to find the right next steps after ostensibly Winning BattleTech, culminating both the Succession Wars and the Clan Invasion in one fell swoop. Every single major state, and honestly most of the minor ones, are dealing with both external foes and internal struggles, and there's just sooooooo much room for developing drama! I'm eager to watch it all unfold!

And now, I need to give myself the birthday gift of sleep. See y'all later, MechWarriors!
