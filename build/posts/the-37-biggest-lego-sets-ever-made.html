The 37 biggest Lego sets ever made

August 28th, 2026 via Wargamer

From conversation-starting display pieces to towering monuments, this up-to-date guide tracks the biggest Lego sets ever made. We can help you find rare behemoths to add to your collection, and we also list the newest additions to Lego's giant hall of fame.

For clarity, we're measuring Lego sets with the most pieces, not merely those with grand physical dimensions. Unsurprisingly, these ultra-large sets also include some of the most expensive Lego sets on the market - but size is no guarantee of quality: if you want our all time favorites, try our guide to the best Lego sets instead.

Lego World Map

ID code 31203
Number of pieces 11,695
Minifigures included 0

Released in June 2021, the Lego Art World Map contains 11,695 pieces, making it the biggest Lego set ever made. Much more a piece of home decor than a typical model or plaything, it's over one meter wide and is designed to be built in a color combination of your choice. You can paint the oceans of the world in pastel colors, create patterns in the seas, or build a monochrome map.

Using a nifty system of connectors, you can even choose which continent lies at the map's center. But it's not only something to look at. Mark your travels across the globe by dotting small, colored pins on the countries you've visited, or listen to the included soundtrack to take an audio tour.

Lego Eiffel Tower

Set number 10307
Number of pieces 10,001
Minifigures included 0

At an appropriately towering 59" (149cm), the Lego Eiffel Tower must be the toy firm's most imposing set yet. At 10,001 parts, Lego's interpretation of the Parisian landmark might not quite trump the Art World Map, but it certainly feels bigger in the room.

And if you're an engineering, history, or construction geek, you might be as thrilled as us to learn that the build process and ordering of this Lego set replicates how the original Eiffel Tower was put together - largely as a four-part process.

And the plastic Eiffel Tower isn't only about its scale and construction. It drips with elegant style and classy details, from the undersized trees down below to the flourishes on the spire at the top. If you've got the space and cash, few sets compare to the Lego Eiffel Tower.

Lego Titanic

Set number 10294
Number of pieces 9,090
Minifigures included 0

Another 2021 release, the Lego Titanic is made of 9,090 individual pieces. Although a little smaller than the real ship, it's no less impressive, and its 135-centimeter length makes it the longest Lego set ever released.

Aside from showcasing the Titanic's major external features - main deck, four smokestacks, and even its swimming pool - it also includes a detailed cross-section of the ship, letting you view the grand staircase and boiler rooms inside. With an adjustable anchor and moving engine, this is one of the more interactive big Lego sets.

Lego Colosseum

Set number 10276
Number of pieces 9,036
Minifigures included 0

Is any monument of history more impressive than the Colosseum? Probably not. Is it even more impressive when made out of Lego? Quite possibly. At 9,036 pieces, the Lego Colosseum only just comes under the Titanic, but its detail of the historical Roman ruin is unmatched.

Its perimeter includes several shades of Lego brick to capture the changing color and age of the amphitheater, while several sections can be removed to reveal the hidden passageways beneath the arena's floor. Just the shape of the model makes it truly impressive to behold.

Lego Millennium Falcon UCS

Set number 75192
Number of pieces 7,541
Minifigures included 0

On its release in 2017, the Ultimate Collector Series Millennium Falcon was the largest and highest priced Lego set ever produced. Now, its 7,541 pieces might pale in comparison to the sets above, but its detail is no less impressive. This is Han Solo's YT-1300 light freighter like you've never seen it before.

We called it one of the best Star Wars Lego sets ever produced, and it's not hard to see why. Every inch of the model is packed with detail, whether it's the removable hull panels, the interior engine room with added hyperdrive, or the main cockpit. This isn't just a large Lego set, it's an homage to perhaps the most iconic spaceship in all of science fiction.

Lego AT AT Walker UCS

Set number 75313
Number of pieces 6,785
Minifigures included 9

Since The Empire Strikes Back came out in 1980, the lumbering grey AT-AT has plodded from the icy plains of Hoth and into our hearts.

At 6,785 pieces, Lego's Ultimate Collector Series AT-AT Walker is quite a bit smaller than the Millennium Falcon in terms of sheer bricks, but a lot taller. Standing 62cm off your table, it's also fully poseable, making it one of the largest and, surprisingly, most agile sets out there. It's also one of the highest priced kits available - but if you were after cheap Lego sets, you probably wouldn't be perusing this guide.

What's most impressive about this set, however, is its relative scale. It's a perfect fit for the ordinary Lego minifigures, and the crew of Snowtroopers and pilots that come with the model only reinforce its gargantuan size.

To see Luke Skywalker tangle below the AT-AT's hull on a cable makes you realize what the Rebellion was up against. 40 whole minifigures can fit into the walker's hull, making it a real miniature mover!

Lego Razor Crest

Set number 75331
Number of pieces 6,187
Minifigures included 5

That's right, the Lego Razor Crest is the third Star Wars set in the top ten. That's testament to the fact the Star Wars Lego theme is thriving; popular enough to have plenty of fans willing to invest in sets costing hundreds of dollars. You'll need plenty of cash to invest in the 6,187-part Razor - but what a remarkable set.

It's certainly large. In fact, it's considerably weighty. It captures the Mandalorian vessel in a strikingly stylish way, and it drips with detail both inside and out. Get under the cover and it's riddled with themed rooms and nods to the TV show realized through smart building techniques.

This giant really does hit all four of the key Lego offerings: a riveting build process; loads of playability; handsome display chops; and a part supply to let you build so many other wonderful things.

Lego Rivendell

Set number 10316
Number of pieces 6,167
Minifigures included 15

As Lord of the Rings locations go, very few can convey the beauty, intricacy, tranquility, history, and otherworldliness of Tolkien's elves quite like the House of Elrond at Rivendell - and 2023's Lego Lord of the Rings Rivendell set does a surprisingly good job of capturing it in brick form.

Its sheer size and scale are part of the reason it's so beloved. It's made of several detachable sections, and each is loaded with little references that'll make LOTR fans' hearts sing.

From Bilbo's writing room, to Frodo in bed after being stabbed with the Witch-King's Morgul blade, to the statuary chamber where the shards of Narsil were kept, to the forge where they were remade into Andúril, Flame of the West… it's a lot.

A generous helping of 15 Lego minifigures sweetens the deal further, including all nine members of the Fellowship of the Ring, plus Elrond, Arwen, and Bilbo (and some no-names). The minifigs have detachable legs, too, so you can sit them around the table, re-enact the Council of Elrond, and say 'One does not simply walk into Mordor' in a Sean Bean voice! You know, er, if you want.

Ninjago City Markets

Set number 71799
Number of pieces 6,163
Minifigures included 21

After the 2021 Ninjago City Gardens set proved so popular (read about it below), it seemed inevitable we'd get another gigantic playset bringing part of Lego's futuristic East Asian-style setting to life - and so we have!

Released in 2023, Ninjago City Markets is that set, and it's both big and beautiful. Designed to complement the Gardens, it trades that set's bewildering verticality for more breadth, overall volume, and interactive doodads.

This thing has a moving cable car, elevators, various teeny room environments built into multi-level buildings - and a toilet that you can flush poop down, and the poop comes out into the river. And, to populate this veritable utopia of plumbed-in pleasures, the set includes an incredible 21 minifigures - more than any other set on this list.

At $369.99 (£319.99), it still ain't cheap, but it's among the more affordable here. If you're into the Ninjago aesthetic, and like having a lot of in-universe minifigs to play out scenarios with, this is a brilliant pick.

Lego Hogwarts Castle

Set number 71043
Number of pieces 6,020
Minifigures included 4

Although packing 6,020 pieces, the most interesting aspect of the Lego Hogwarts Castle isn't how many bricks are in the box, but the scale of the model they're used to build.

A micro-scale model, that's built to fit tiny figures instead of the usual minifigure size, Lego's cottoned on to a neat trick here; rather than a humongous Hogwarts that no den could fit, we get a medium Hogwarts and with a cast of scaled down wizards (plus some bonus minifigures for good measure).

It pays off, though. The set captures all the majesty of the famous fictional castle, containing several classic locations of the franchise: the Great Hall, the Whomping Willow, Hagrid's Hut, and the Chamber of Secrets.

Lego Taj Mahal

A huge, iconic Creator Expert set.

Set number 10256
Number of pieces 5,923
Minifigures included 0

Between 2008 and 2011, the Lego Taj Mahal was the biggest Lego set of all time. Packing 5,923 pieces, it's still a mammoth build even now. It's got symmetry, majesty, and an absolutely cracking dome at its center - the Lego design team sure know how to pick them. The Taj Mahal works brilliantly as a Lego set, and vice versa.

You won't find it in any stores, however. After briefly reappearing in 2017, the set was discontinued. Look for this one in second-hand stores or online Lego marketplaces and trading platforms, and expect to pay a real premium!

Lego Santiago Bernabéu

The biggest sports set ever.

ID code 10299
Number of pieces 5,876
Minifigures included 0

Lego Real Madrid - Santiago Bernabéu Stadium is a must-have for soccer fans. It's a huge, detailed recreation of the iconic stadium, and viewing it gets even better when you remove the top and check out the interior.

Like many symmetrical Lego sets, building the stadium can get fairly same-y. However, if you're the kind of person who finds repetition relaxing, this will be a soothing build, without too much complexity. And the final result is well worth the work.

Ninjago City Gardens

ID code 71741
Number of pieces 5,685
Minifigures included 19

Released in early 2021 in celebration of the show's tenth anniversary, Ninjago City Gardens is a three-storey slice of Ninjago City that contains many of the characters and locations fans will be familiar with from the Ninjago cartoon. And that's the main draw that makes this one of the best Ninjago sets out there. Ronin's pawn shop, Chen's noodle house, and even a history museum are collected inside.

This set is really a love letter to the Ninjago concept, setting a new standard of detail and build intricacy. And it also pairs with the other modular Lego sets in the series, connecting into one, massive street.

Lego Diagon Alley

ID code 75978
Number of pieces 5,544
Minifigures included 14

JK Rowling's boy wizard gets another entry on the list, this time at a normal Lego scale. Harry Potter Diagon Alley's 5,544 pieces combine into six modular wizarding shops: Ollivander's Wand Shop; Weasley's Wizard Wheezes; Florean Fortescue's Ice Cream Parlor; Scribbulus Writing Implements; Flourish & Blotts; and Quality Quidditch Supplies. Better yet, they're all fabulously detailed.

Windows are decorated with displays, every nook is full of color, and minor details pepper the buildings' interiors. There are plenty of optional ways to construct the set, including creating an actual alley or fitting the shops alongside other modular Lego sets to add a slice of the fictional to your dioramas.

Lego Camp Nou

Set number 10284
Number of pieces 5,509
Minifigures included 0

:As our second sports set on this list, Camp Nou - FC Barcelona makes quite the model. Coming in at 5,509 pieces, it's no small fry, but includes every possible detail of Barcelona Football Club's stadium you could think of: the stands, the press room, the player tunnels, and even a miniature team bus.

Not the only football stadium Lego has created, but definitely the largest, Camp Nou is a sight to behold.

Lego Barad-Dûr

Set number 10333
Number of pieces 5,471
Minifigures included 10

Lego Barad-Dûr immortalizes Sauron's imposing stronghold as a 33-inch-tall fortress of black bricks. Its four floors conceal a range of interactive details and gorgeous minifigs that help bring this Middle-earth monster tower to life. At the top, shining like the star on a spooky, dead Christmas tree, is the eye of Sauron, which is illuminated by a light brick.

At 18 inches wide and 12 inches deep, Barad-Dûr is most impressive for its height. Its value for money will also wow, with around 20 hours of build time needed to set this big boy up. Expect a fairly complex but satisfying build experience (with lots of similar color bricks to sort).

And should eighteen inches be too small for your tastes, you can even stack multiple Barad-Dûrs one atop the other, thanks to the modular design of the levels.

Lego Venator Republic Attack Cruiser

Set number 75367
Number of pieces 5,374
Minifigures included 2

The magnificent Lego Venator-Class Republic Attack Cruiser was the hottest thing in Lego Star Wars in 2023. Though the Venator is only a minor player in the mainline Star Wars movies, it starred in many episodes of the Clone Wars animated series, developing a cult following among fans.

A bit smaller than its original trilogy-era variant, the UCS Star Destroyer, the Venator comes in at 43 inches long, with 5,374 pieces total. A measly two minifigures come with the set - a slight disappointment to some - but the choices of Captain Rex and Admiral Yularen will please Clone Wars superfans.

Lego Avengers Tower

Set number 76269
Number of pieces 5,201
Minifigures included 31

The Lego Avengers Tower stands at a heroic 36 inches tall. Most of this height is made up of glass windows, which are a real chore to attach, but the final result is stunning. Inside the tower itself, you'll find lots of  Marvel Cinematic Universe Easter eggs. Combine this with a small army of minifigures, and you've got a wonderful bit of fan-service.

Yes, we've seen many of these minifigs before. And yes, the set is pretty pricey. But its staggering size and fabulous display value make it a worthy investment for any Marvel fan - or Lego fan.

2007 UCS Millennium Falcon

Set number 10179
Number of pieces 5,197
Minifigures included 5

The 2007 UCS Millennium Falcon set was the last word in Lego Star Wars for a full decade. When it launched it was one of the biggest sets ever made, and its still one of the largest Lego sets to this day. With just shy of 5,200 pieces, this is a long yet engaging build. The final product is a stunning display piece that's packed with detail (though it's way too big to be played with).

We're not sure we'd recommend buying one - the newer model is just as pretty and widely available, while this version can only be bought second-hand for at least three times its original price. But if you're a hardcore collector, this will be one of your holy grails.

Lego Hogwarts Express Collector's Edition

Set number 76405
Number of pieces 5,129
Minifigures included 20

There have been many brick-based renditions of the famous train from Harry Potter, but the Hogwarts Express Collector's Edition is the largest by far. Never before has Lego put so much detail into this steam engine.

While there's not much of a track to push the train around, the Hogwarts Express is surprisingly functional. Sliding doors, light bricks, windows you can see through, and snack trolleys that can comfortably move through its corridors are all part of the set. Also inside are mini set pieces from various Harry Potter films, with quote plaques and accurate minifigs to match.

Size often comes at a price, and this collector's edition is no exception. If you're on a budget, there are plenty of smaller versions of the train, each of them a worthy display piece in their own right. But if you want the ultimate Harry Potter display piece, this is a serious contender for the top spot.

Lego Ninjago City

A massive, modular building set.

Set number 70620
Number of pieces 4,867
Minifigures included 16

Lego Ninjago City is now only the third biggest set in the Ninjago line. However, it's still impressively large, and it's stuffed with minifigures, detail, and value for money. The build is long but rarely repetitive, and once this satisfying stage is over, you're left with a modular building that's just as fun to play with as it is to display.

Ninjago City is split into three floors, which can be lifted for easy access. The bottom level includes a bridge, sewer outlet, stream, fish market, house, service station, and taxi stand.

The functional sliding elevator then takes you to the second floor, where you'll find a fashion store, construction site, comic book store, crab restaurant, and ATM. At the very top is a radio tower, sushi bar, and apartment. Many of these come with moving parts and intriguing details that bring the location to life.

The Old Town 15th Anniversary

Set number 71861
Number of pieces 4851
Minifigures included 23

The Old Town 15th Anniversary is a celebration of fifteen years of the Ninjago TV series, and the newest Ninjago entry on this list. Packed with references and a huge cast of minifigs, this continues the Ninjago tradition of being both utterly gorgeous to look at, and incredible fun to play with.

That means it has the same weakness as the rest of the Ninjago line - this is a set that every kid will want to build, but which has some fine detail and fiddly parts that may fox some youngsters.

Lego Disney Castle

Set number 43222
Number of pieces 4,837
Minifigures included 8

The Lego Disney Castle is an updated version of the 2016 71040 set, and it's the largest Disney Lego set to date. This also means it's one of the highest priced Disney sets, but the size and detail on display help justify the high cost. Disney's most iconic building is enormous in brick form, with a regal gold-and-blue exterior and inner rooms filled with Easter eggs.

Some of Disney's most iconic couples make up the minifigures, and most are exclusive to this set. Princesses Tiana, Rapunzel, Cinderella, and Snow White appear with their beaus, and each is printed in delightful detail.

A spinning ballroom floor adds some neat functionality to the inside of the castle. With nods to Beauty and the Beast, Fantasia, and more found within, this is an ideal collector's item for any adult Disney fan. The castle is a staggering 80cm tall, though, so make sure you've got plenty of space to display it.

Lego Gringotts Wizarding Bank

Set number 76417
Number of pieces 4,801
Minifigures included 13

Gringotts Wizarding Bank is a huge multi-story Lego set, recreating the famous set piece from the Harry Potter universe. This is an enormous set in its own right, but it can also be combined with Diagon Alley. The top half of this set slots seamlessly into that diorama, creating an enhanced display piece.

The top half of the kit recreates the striking white exterior of the bank, as well as the tiny Magical Menagerie shop next door. Inside, a range of goblin minifigs are on hand to show Harry Potter and his disguised allies through the bank's marble interior. The level of detail here is excellent, and there's just enough room inside for play.

If you display Gringotts Wizarding Bank on its own, you can also attach the lower half of the set. This recreates the underground vaults of the bank, complete with a functional mine cart that minifigs can ride down to the bottom. The vaults are small, but they still conceal engaging surprises within.

UCS Star Destroyer

Set number 75252
Number of pieces 4,784
Minifigures included 2

You knew you'd find the Star Wars UCS Imperial Star Destroyer on this list somewhere. One of the most famously huge Lego sets ever made, this classic set perfectly depicts the terrifying, grey, wedge-shaped space warship that first captivated nerds in the opening seconds of A New Hope.

And it's a behemoth, too - more than three feet long, two feet wide, and over a foot high on its display stand, the powerfully gray UCS Star Destroyer takes up more than enough room on your table to honor its movie counterpart.

Sadly now retired from production, this set can be pretty pricey to pick up used. But, for serious Star Wars fans, it's probably worth it for the sheer scale and detail - from the independently poseable turbolaser turrets, to the massive, intricate engine outputs at the rear, to the adorable mini version of Leia Organa's Tantive IV that lets you act out Episode IV's opening chase (sorta).

Lego Mosaic Maker

Set number 40179
Number of pieces 4702
Minifigures included 0

What if you could turn a family portrait into a totally unique piece of Lego wall art? You can, thanks to the Lego Mosaic Maker kit!

This kit comes with 4,702 single-stud flat bricks in black, white, light gray, dark gray, and yellow, plus a 48 x 48 Lego baseplate. By uploading a picture online, you'll get a custom pixel image that shows you just how to lay out your studs on the baseplate to create your own pixel portrait.

Lego Ghostbusters Firehouse

Set number 75827
Number of pieces 4,634
Minifigures included 9

When there's something strange in your neighborhood… well, the gigantic Ghostbusters Firehouse Headquarters Lego set really won't help at all. But it is a lavish and spookily detailed recreation of the 1984 movie's classic fire station hangout.

At over a foot tall, this plus-sized, 4,634-piece playset starts out just nine inches wide, but opens out like a book until it's a full 18 inches across, exposing  a wild cornucopia of movie-accurate details inside, from toasters to toolboxes. It's very playable.

And the minifigures are the star of the show: original Ghostbusters Peter Venkman, Raymond Stantz, Egon Spengler, and Winston Zeddemore are all here, along with five other character minifigs, bit-figures for Slimer, and two colored ghosts. Now retired from production, this set will cost you a pretty penny to get a hold of.

Lego Lion Knights' Castle

ID code 10305
Number of pieces 4,514
Minifigures included 22

The Lego Lion Knight's Castle is inspired by a classic set from 1984, but that hasn't stopped it from being innovative and appealing to modern fans. This huge stone keep is stuffed with hidden details, vibrant minifigures, and seamless functional features.

This set stands at a regal 38cm high, and it's 44cm wide when closed. The two halves of the build are connected with clips, so they're easy to spread out and separate if you wish. However you wish to show it off, this is a commanding set piece from all angles, with as much detail on the inside as on the out.

The large number of minifigs and functional features make this an ideal set for play. However, the high price may put parents off gifting this to a younger fan. Grown builders will get plenty of satisfaction from putting this one together - and reminiscing about the Lego sets of days gone by.

Lego Notre-Dame de Paris

ID code 21061
Number of pieces 4,383
Minifigures included 0

The Lego Notre-Dame de Paris may not look like it belongs on the list of biggest Lego sets. At 33cm tall, 22cm wide, and 41cm deep, it's nowhere near the tallest or longest of Lego sets. However, it's joined the ranks thanks to the sheer number of parts included in the set.

The large part count gives the finished build an extreme level of detail. The historic building's iconic bell towers and stained glass windows are recreated with surprising accuracy. Creative use of several tiny Lego pieces means that everything from the gargoyles to the buttresses has been made buildable.

The downside of all these tiny pieces is that the process of building Notre-Dame is repetitive and surprisingly complex. We think that the final product is more than worth pushing through for. Lovers of architecture shouldn't miss this set.

Lego Tower Bridge

ID code 10214
Number of pieces  4,287
Minifigures included 0

2010's Lego Tower Bridge has been usurped by much larger sets, but it was one of the biggest sets by far when it launched. At 102cm long, 45cm high, and 26cm wide, it still takes up an imposing amount of space. Lego has shrunk one of London's most memorable structures to fit inside your home as a wonderfully detailed display piece.

This value comes at a steep price, though - and we're not just talking about how much this set costs on the second-hand market. The building process is particularly fiddly thanks to literally hundreds of 1×1 slopes that need placing, and there are plenty of ways to make mistakes when setting up this symmetrical structure. If you've got plenty of patience and disposable income, this shouldn't put you off too much.

Lego Harry Potter Hogwarts Crests

A wall filling Harry Potter art set.

ID code 31201
Number of pieces 4,249
Minifigures included 0

The Harry Potter Hogwarts Crests set is part of the Lego Art line. This kit builds one ornamental crest for each of the Hogwarts houses, and thanks to the special frame tiles the finished result is sturdy enough to mount on your wall.

Lego wall art isn't a challenging or interesting build - almost all the thousands of bricks in here the set are flat topped single studs, that you lay out on a grid like you're painting by numbers. And unfortunately, the finished artwork is pretty dull and lifeless. Only the most dedicated Potterheads should go looking for it.

Batman: The Animated Series Gotham City

ID code 76271
Number of pieces  4,210
Minifigures included 4

A relatively new set from early 2024, the Batman: The Animated Series Gotham City Lego set is a true beast of a display piece - a huge, wall-mountable tableau packing a ridiculous number of tucked-away details from DC Comics' beloved 1990s TV cartoon.

Apart from the set's overall scale and splendor, a closer look finds 15 separate detachable panels that hide brick-built versions of scenes, locations, and characters from the show, including the iconic animated versions of the Batcave, Batmobile, Batwing, and Wayne Manor. You get minifigures for the animated series' iterations of Bats, the Joker, Harley Quinn, and Catwoman, too.

Flat, wall-art sets aren't usually as expensive as the $300 this will set you back, but you can't argue with the monumental piece count, and for B:TAS superfans, we daresay it's worth it. Just make sure you've got the space!

Jim Lee Batman Lego Collection

ID code 31205
Number of pieces 4,167
Minifigures included 0

Another epic-scale, wall-hangable Lego art project, the Jim Lee Batman Collection set gives you big flat display boards, detailed instructions, and a metric butt-ton of colored stud pieces (over 4,000) with which to put together one of three portraits based on the work of legendary comic artist (and current DC Comics Chief Creative Officer) Jim Lee.

You can make the Caped Crusader himself, the Joker, or Harley Quinn using the pieces from one set - but buy two or three, and you can size up to a taller, full-frontal portrait of Bats, or a landscape shot of an intimate moment with Catwoman.

Sadly, the set's out of production - but you can pick it up on Ebay for about $175-230 right now.

Tropical Aquarium

ID code 10366
Number of pieces 4154
Minifigures included 0

The Lego Icons Tropical Aquarium is, frankly, beautiful. Each of the fish is an intricate mosaic of 'scale' pieces, while the coral is as cleverly constructed as any of the Lego flower sets. There are even clever mechanisms that allow the crab to pop in and out of its hidey hole, the treasure chest to open and close, and the big red fish to wiggle.

And while for the same price you could get an actual tropical aquarium, it takes a lot of skill to keep saltwater fish alive, not to mention a very high electricity bill. And these Lego fish have been sourced from a factory in Denmark - you won't have to worry that an unscrupulous dealer has captured them from the wild.

River Steamboat

ID code 21356
Number of pieces 4,090
Minifigures included 0

The Lego River Steamboat is a perfect balance of building experience and display piece. The three levels of its interior are packed with details, from a Technic engine block to the narrow dining cabins, to the bar with a full set of instruments for a live band.

Sadly, no live band is included, nor any Minifigs, which is about the only issue we can raise for this kit. It may be a little more reserved than most toy sets, but it has all the hidden detail and interactive element that make a great toy kit. You'll have a great - though sometimes challenging - time putting it together, and it will easily fit into a nook or onto a shelf, where it will look stunning.

Natural History Museum

ID code 10326
Number of pieces 4,014
Minifigures included 7

The Lego Natural History Museum is one of those perfect sets: it starts with an entertaining build, and ends with a gorgeous display piece that also has incredible play value for (careful) youngsters. And it fits into the Lego Modular Street range - perfect!

The combination of Victorian architecture with modern exhibits make this is a much more varied build than many architectural kit. The mellow color palette serves as a backdrop against which the brightly colored Minifigs - and many, many fine details concealed in the rooms - really pop.

There's just something very right about Lego - the toy famous for inspiring a love of art and science - recreating a Victorian Natural History Museum, a real-world 'palace of learning'.

Gustav Klimt - The Kiss

ID code 31221
Number of pieces 4,000
Minifigures included 0

Gustav Klimt - The Kiss recreates a masterwork of early 20th century painting. Gustav Klimt was one of the leading lights of the Art Nouveau movement in Vienna and painted The Kiss around 1907. With beautiful bold colors, flowing natural lines, and a sensual theme, it's an iconic work of romantic artwork.

You could buy a print of this incredibly famous painting for a fraction of the price of this Lego set - but for those who can afford it, this is an incredibly rewarding build, that lets you spend long slow hours luxuriating in the colors, shapes, and details of this wonderful art piece.

Biggest Lego sets FAQs

OK, so those are the biggest Lego sets ever made by number of bricks - but surely we can get nerdier than that? Here's a pile more trivia about the largest Lego sets out there!

What is the tallest Lego set?

The tallest Lego set ever sold is the 10307 Eiffel Tower, at 58.7 inches (149cm) tall. No surprises there. The second tallest set ever is also an Eiffel Tower set: its retired predecessor (set number 10181), which came out in 2007.

More interestingly, the third tallest Lego set, at 39.4 inches, is the 92176 NASA Apollo Saturn V rocket set - despite it having a comparatively titchy piece total of just 1,969! Size comes in more than one form, after all.

What is the longest Lego set?

The longest Lego set ever sold is the 10294 Titanic, at 53.1 inches (135cm).

In second place is the awesome - and sadly long retired - 10221 Super Star Destroyer, at 49.2 inches (125cm). Not quite the ship's estimated in-universe size- quoted in lore books as anywhere between 5 and 12 miles (8 to 19 kilometers) long - but that'd need an extraordinarily long shelf.

What is the smallest Lego set?

There have been at least two official numbered Lego sets containing only one piece, making them tied for the smallest set ever sold.

Our favorite examples are set 1115 - a single 4.5 volt lighting brick - and set 630, the Lego brick separator tool - although these days you get one of those in every set priced more than about $50, so most collectors technically now own multiple copies of Lego set 630!

What is the biggest Lego build ever created?

We've been talking about official Lego sets that are huge - but what about the largest actual Lego model built? Well, it's almost impossible to be sure, because avid brick fans are constantly creating enormous new projects that beggar belief with their sheer scale, detail, and in built mechanisms.

The video above, by YouTuber Astro Bricks, profiles some of the previous record holding builds - from a 112 foot long Lego suspension bridge to a 250,000 brick Woolly Mammoth. But we can go bigger.

As shown in Popular Science's video interview above, in 2013 Lego Master Builder Eric Barsegi built a life size Lego X-Wing starfighter out of - wait for it - 5.3 million bricks, that weighed 46,000 pounds.

How long does it take to build the biggest Lego sets?

On average, building a Lego set takes most people around 20 seconds per piece. That calculation is based on our own experience and collating a few different estimates online. That works out to around 65 hours' build time for the Lego World Map (the biggest Lego set ever released).

With just one person, working at that average speed, it'd take 1,227 days (nearly three and a half years) to complete the 5.3 million brick X-Wing above - the biggest overall Lego build we know of. However, in reality the time taken to build each set can vary wildly depending on which set it is, how experienced the builder is, and which method of building you choose.
