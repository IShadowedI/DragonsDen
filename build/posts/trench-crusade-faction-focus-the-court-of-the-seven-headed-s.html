Trench Crusade Faction Focus: The Court of the Seven-Headed Serpent (Updated October 23, 2026)

October 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Today we’re back with our weekly Trench Crusade article, and this time, it seems that we’re not quite done with Hell just yet. In what can be described as Divine serendipity, since we recently covered the Sins of the Court, Factory Fortress Inc. released its latest October update, which focuses on Hell's denizens. 

Changelog

  • Update (Latest): 2026-10-23 Updated to reflect changes to the faction in the October update.
  • Published: 2026-1-30

The Lore

Behind any invading force lies a council that commands from the rear. In this instance, it is the Court of the Seven-Headed Serpent, a group of Archdevils representing the seven deadly sins. The Covenant of Heaven and Hell ensures no true demon may walk the Earth, but the Serpent's heads are nothing if not ingenious. By mixing demons with creatures that walk the earth, both humans and animals, they have found ways to invade the material plane without incurring His retribution.

The Seven Heads of the Serpent are masters of the Sin they represent, each notorious and ruthless, gaining their power through deceit, treachery, or outright brutality. First amongst all is Eligos, the Duke of Petals, who sits at the head of the Council as the High Cathedra of Pride. Next is Mammon, the Prince of Greed, whose seat is adorned with gold and marble. At the seat of Lust sits Asmodeus, the Monarch of Lechery. Envy's seat is occupied by Moloch, the President of the Sacrificial Sun, whose idols are grander than Pride's. Next is Gluttony, which has seen stability again since Beelzebub’s abdication as Bael, the Uncrowned King of Stolen Land. The seat of Wrath, practical for one only concerned with destruction, is filled by Apollyon, the Lady of Locusts. Lastly, the Silk Bed of Sloth holds Belphagor, Duchess of the Gap, in slumber.

If the Court unites under a common goal for too long, Creation's fall is all but guaranteed.

The Defining Traits of Court 

Goetic Spells & Powers: The Sorcerer has harmed me once more.

What gives the Court its unique flavour, aside from running demons of hell, are the Goetic Powers that these demonic abominations possess. Goetic Powers are usually passive buffs, while Spells are Actions that require the caster to spend Sin Markers to power them. These Powers and Spells cover a broad range of buffs, including offensive, defensive, movement shenanigans, and utility options. Goetic Powers and Spells will be an important pillar of your playstyle, and they become even more important depending on your chosen Sin.

Hey There, Demons, It’s Me, Ya Boi

All but one of your possible units will have the Demonic Keyword, marking them as denizens of Hell. Since Hell is what may be considered "toasty" in the most optimistic way, its inhabitants are well-suited to the whole "Fire and Brimstone" thing and have adapted, and even thrived, in Hell's scorching environment. As a result, those with the Demonic Keyword do not suffer the effects of the Fire keyword. Fire is a pretty popular way to deal extra Blood Markers, so being immune to it helps you stay alive and in the game. You'll still struggle with Flamethrowers' Ignore Armour properties, but negating secondary Blood Markers is a huge advantage.

The Beatings Will Continue Until Morale Improves

While there are a variety of unique ways to generate Sin Markers, whipping your Wretched into shape remains a consistent, reliable way to generate what you need to power your Goetic Magiks. You won’t need to keep them locked away in a corner, receiving nothing but a paddlin’, but it’s still important to keep a Torture Instrument wielder nearby to get a few extra Sin Markers.

I Might Be Out of Spells, But I'm Not Out of Shells

Whilst Goetic Spells and Powers play a significant role in your game plan, they aren’t the only tool in the box. Like every faction, The Court get their own unique Battlekit items, with arguably some of the best Ranged and Melee Weapons in the game.  The Serpent Assault Gun, Ophidian Rifle, and Malabranche are perfect examples of powerful yet unique weapons that make The Court formidable on the battlefield. The variety only increases when you decide on a Sin.

Elected to Lead, Not to Read

Despite being the literal embodiment of Hell, your Elites will, more so than any other faction, be leading from the front. That may be mostly out of necessity, as your Wretched will be busy getting whipped into shape to generate Sin Markers. Still, your Elites pack not only Goetic abilities and game-changing weapons, but also some seriously stacked Profiles. Your Praetor and Desecrated Saint have multiple +3s on their profiles, for example, including the Tough keyword. The natural price of this is that you’re low on effective models. You'll have a few Wretched and Yoke Fiends, mostly for Sin Marker generation or on a back objective if required. That leaves the rest of your models, mostly your Elites, to get into the thick of it. They can do the job, but each loss hurts, and each mistake is punished. Still, you can use your Wretched activations to push back the need to activate your important units, so you’re not at a huge disadvantage compared to a truly low-model warband.

Battlekit Highlights

Arquebus: 8 Ducats

A simple gun for simple minds. Those being your Wretched and your Yoke Fiends. Its ability to double as a melee weapon makes it a cheap way to bring your throwaway models into your game plan, and at 18” range it will at least be able to reach out in the earlier parts of the game.

Chthonic Vessel: 1 Glory

This piece of Equipment is a Consumable that a single Elite may take. During a game, the bearer may instantly gain 2 Sin Markers. It’s a nice way to get a quick surge of Sin Markers exactly when you need them. Would be interesting to see on a Hunter, who is hungry for Sin Markers more often than not.

Crown of the Hellfire: 15 Ducats

Able to be equipped by Elites and the Pit locust; if equipped, it causes a Blood Marker to all enemy models within 1” of the model with this item at the end of its activation. It’s a nice piece of equipment, but not vital. Less so, since models with Negate Fire don't suffer the Blood Marker.

Fiendbound Gatling Gun; 20 Ducats

This 2-Handed weapon may not look particularly unique given its Heavy and Shrapnel keywords, but its ability sets it apart. The Excruciate ability lets a model with Strong, or a model in contact with another, Excruciate the gun, giving it the Automatic 2 and Reload Keywords. Getting a couple of shots off for 20 Ducats, with Shrapnel and being at 24” is pretty nice. The intention here is to have a Yoke Fiend and a Wretched side by side, letting the former unleash pot shots from afar while your big threats move up the board.

Iron Maildel Flail: 4 Glory

This Hell Knight only 2-handed melee weapon comes with +1 Dice, Cumbersome, and Heavy Keywords. Its special ability requires you to sacrifice one of your Wretched to be put inside the Iron Maiden. If you do, the Flail gets the Ignore Armour Keyword, and each time the Hell Knight makes a successful melee attack, it also gets a Sin Marker.

Jaws of Iron: 5 Ducats

The Jaws of Iron are a grenade that only your Yoke Fiends can carry. With a 6” range and a -1 Injury Dice, they’re not going to be taking out many enemies. However, that is not their purpose. They’re meant as a set-up piece so your Yoke Fiends can then charge in and attack in melee with an extra +1 Melee Dice, thanks to the Snap Shut ability on this weapon.

Restraining Muzzle: 10 Ducats

Another piece of Equipment exclusive to your Yoke Fiends. Up to 3 can take this, and it grants them a +1 Dice to Ranged Attack Rolls. It’s simple, though a little pricey compared to other factions' offerings.

Torture Instrument

A defining weapon of the Court, the trusty Torture Instrument keeps your Wretched where they belong: under the hoof and claw of Demons. This 2-handed weapon is primarily used to hit your own Wretched, thanks to the Torture Action ability, which allows the bearer to make a torture action instead of a Fight Action. This Action is similar, where the bearer makes a melee attack, but on a successful hit, the target is automatically taken down, and the model making the Action receives a Sin Marker. The Agonising Injuries ability reduces the amount of Dice used in an Injury roll to 1d6, with + or + Injury Dice still only resulting in the highest or lowest single Dice.

Serpent Assault Gun: 45 Ducats

This large “hand-cannon” (that actually needs two hands to wield it) shoots bullets enveloped in a stinky, but deadly gas at a range of 36”. By almost all accounts, the Serpent Assault Gun is the perfect gun. The long range, coupled with the Gas, Automatic 2 and Assault keyword, gives it some versatility for the wielder. The biggest drawback is that it is 2-handed, meaning you can’t take a Trench Shield with it. The Heavy keyword isn't great, but since you’ll most likely put this on something like a Praetor, it's largely moot. It costs a whopping 45 Ducats, but you are certainly getting your money's worth. As said, this is great on a Praetor, whose +3 Range stat makes fantastic use of this gun, even when not shooting at short range and/or into cover.

Malabranche Sword: 35 Ducats

The Malabranche Sword is in Melee what the Serpent Assault Gun is at Range. Namely, pure carnage. It’s less flashy than the SAG, but its simplicity keeps the focus on its sole objective: killing. The Malabranche Sword's defining characteristic is the Deadly Keyword. This keyword lets the user roll 3d6 on an injury roll and add the results together, rather than taking the top two. So you’re essentially getting a Blood Bath with every injury roll! The downside is you can only take one, and it costs 35 Ducats.

Ophidian Rifle: 25 Ducats

Back to the available range options, we have the Ophidian Rifle. Another long-range weapon at 30” of range, as well as being 2-handed, this rifle’s main ability is Unnatural Inversion. Instead of a -1 Dice when shooting at long range or at an enemy in cover, you instead gain +1 Dice. The Ignore Cover and Ignore Long Range keywords ensure you’re actually gaining this bonus. This Rifle is particularly strong on a Hell Knight, who can provide some early Blood Markers in the early rounds through combining the Unnatural Inversion ability with their built-in Blood Magic spell, which grants a +1 Injury Dice.

Headtaker: 15 Ducats

The assassin’s best friend. A simple one-handed melee weapon with no special keywords, but it provides a +2 Injury modifier if you target a model that hasn't activated yet. Be aware that it’s a flat modifier, with its pros and cons. Regardless, this is a great weapon for a Hunter of the Left-Hand Path, who can quickly get into melee thanks to their Left-Hand Path ability, even after following up a shot with their Bow of Lethe.

Yoke Fiends Credit: Darius Minis

Goetic Powers

Black Heart

A useful and cheap spell at 5 Ducats that grants a +1 Dice to the Success Roll the next time you make one. For 1 Sin Marker, it can certainly help ensure you do what needs to be done.

Blessing of the Serpent Moon

Basically the same as the previous interaction that was on the Sorcerer’s profile. Before an Injury Roll is made for a model with this Spell, you can add a -1 Injury Modifier for every 2 Sin Markers spent (up to 6). Keep those important units alive and kicking!

Infernal Challenge

Exclusive to the Hell Knights, this Ability adds +1 Injury Dice for Melee Attacks if the attacker's target has the Tough Keyword. Nice and simple, and at 10 Ducats, it’s not too expensive.

Raining Blood

After making a successful Spell action, you can place 1 Blood Marker on a number of enemy models equal to the number Sin Markers spent for this Spell (max 3), as long as those enemy models are within 18” of the Spellcaster. Important to note that Line of Sight is not needed!

Slavemaster

An Elite model can cast this spell for one Blood Marker and force a Yoke Fiend in 18” to perform one of the following commands:

  • Sacrifice: The Yoke Fiend kills itself (A bit extreme, but okay). 
  • Fight: Force the Yoke Fiend to make a Melee Attack.
  • Move: The Yoke Fiend moves, but no Charge or Retreat.
  • Shoot: Force a Yoke Fiend to make a Ranged Attack. 

The Yoke Fiend can still be activated as normal if it hasn't already been. It’s a nice ability that can mess with your opponent if you use it well.

The Units

Praetor: 115 Ducats

A demonic commander bound to serve a demon lord or archdevil. They command warbands in their master’s service, fulfilling whatever task they are given, whether that means fighting on the front lines of the Great War or taking part in the many battles within the realms of Hell itself, for Hell's power dynamics are ever-shifting. Despite their prowess and status in the Great War, they are seen in damnation as barely above even the mongrel Yoke Fiends; their mortal blood, however small, taints the demonic purity. The disgust they inspire in their patron is only just outweighed by their usefulness, as their mortal aspect lets them walk on His land.

The denizens of Hell may deem the Praetor meager and weak; on the table, however, it is anything but. The Praetor simply slaps in pretty much every respect. Stat-wise, it comes with 8” Flying movement, a +3 in both Melee and Ranged, and 0 native armour. Right off the bat, this stat profile is amazing. Whilst most leaders gain a paltry +2 in these stats, the Praetor cements its place as the king of leaders. They can, of course, take any Battlekit from the Court Armoury, as you might expect. Keyword-wise, they come with Elite, Fear, Strong and Tough. They also have the Demonic keyword (which almost all your warband has, bar the Wretched), granting them immunity to the effects of the Fire keyword. Lastly, for Sin Marker generation, they gain one any time they activate.

Sorcerer: 85 Ducats

Sorcerers have no predetermined appearance; each is as unique as the next, but all share the same levitating ability: their feet never touch the ground. They make excellent warband commanders in their own right, thanks to their cunning and Goetic traits, but they mostly serve as advisors to their Praetor overlords.

Stat-wise, the Sorcerer comes with a 6” flying movement and a +1 in Ranged and Melee. They may take any Battlekit from the Court Armoury, except for Ranged Weapons and Grenades. This may seem like an odd restriction, but they can make up for the lack of weapon-wielding with up to 3 powerful Goetic Spells. In addition to the 3 spells they choose, the Sorcerer comes with a built-in spell at no cost. Burning Inferno is a powerful ranged spell that grows more destructive the more Sin Markers you spend to fuel it. For their final ability, they may purchase the Leader Keyword for 5 Ducats.

Hunter of the Left-Hand Path

Experts in hunting the vile creatures that lurk within Hell, these hunters follow the Left-Hand path and use the still-living organs of their prey for their Goetic Magiks.

If the Praetor is your front-liner, tearing into the enemy with demonic ferocity, and your Sorcerer is your backliner, hurling fiery carnage, then the Hunter of the Left-Hand Path is your assassin, waiting for the right time to take out key enemy targets. They come with the standard 6” movement, but pack an amazing +2 in Ranged and +1 in melee. Battlekit-wise, they carry the Bow of Lethe at all times (since it's, kinda, you know, part of their arm and all that), and they can only carry one-handed weapons (again, the whole bow-arm thing explains that), and no shields.

The Bow of Lethe is a 24” one-handed Ranged Weapon that comes with the Assault keyword. The Special Ammunition keyword allows you to choose one of two bonuses before making an injury with this weapon: Either a +2 Injury Dice or Ignore Armour. This offers great versatility, letting you pick the right bonus for the enemy in sight.

But that is not all; the Hunter comes with some truly amazing Goetic Spells. First off is Left-Hand Path, which costs 2 Blood Markers. You use this Spell when the Hunter touches a terrain piece as part of a normal movement or Dash. You can remove them from the battlefield and place them touching another terrain piece within 24” and more than 1” away from enemy models. This will be your key assassin ability, letting you reach any model on the battlefield and truly play mind games with your opponent. To take this Spell up a notch, the Hunter comes with the Infiltrator keyword, meaning they can start outside your Deployment zone. The second Spell, Oracle Beast Cloak, is your defensive Spell if the enemy catches you. After paying a hefty 3 Blood Markers and an incoming Injury Roll is made, you can change that Injury Roll's result to “No Effect”. So even though you’re about to be sent back down to the pit, you can shrug it off and survive. Watch out, though: this Blood Marker is hungry, and you can only use it once a Turn. To ensure you never get caught out, you have Shadow-Walker, which lets you retreat for 2 Blood Markers without the enemy being able to make a Melee Attack against you.

Hell Knight: 100 Ducats

Hell Knights make up the bannerlets of a high lord of the Court. They are warriors of silent servitude and are called upon to fight when the followers of the Tyrant God foolishly believe they can thwart the inevitable.

Hell Knights have the usual 6” movement, with a +2 Dice only in Melee and a built-in -2 Armour thanks to their Infernal Iron Armour. This armor functions like Reinforced Armor, but adds the Impervious keyword, meaning its Armor bonus works even against weapons with the Ignore Armor keyword. As Elites, they can take any Battlekit from the Armoury. To top it off, they do come with the Demonic and Strong keywords. They lack Tough, but the Infernal Iron Armour provides ample protection unless your opponent gets lucky.

For Spells, the Hell Knight may take two Spells, but one must be their own Blood Magic Spell. For a cost of 1 Blood Marker, before making an Injury Roll, you may add +1 Injury Dice. Very simple, but very good, since it isn't limited to Melee attacks. Despite lacking a positive Ranged Characteristic, the Ophidian Rifle pairs well with the Hell Knight because of this Blood Magic Spell. As we’ll see later with the Lust Sin, you can get some deadly Ranged Damage with a Hell Knight and Ophidian Rifle. Lastly, Corpse Candles allows the Hell Knight to gain the Fear Keyword for 5 Ducats.

Wretched: 20 Ducats

Wretched are the souls whose humanity is not blessed by the demonic blood. The lowest rung of the infernal ladder, they serve as fodder on the front lines or as batteries to power the Goetic magic of their demonic overlords. However, should a Wretched strike down a worthy adversary, they may gain their freedom in memory and honour of the First Rebellion.

Let’s not beat around the bush here: on the table, Wretched reflect their namesake. They get the usual 6” movement, but have a negative Ranged and Melee characteristic, at -1 specifically. They may take any Battlekit from the Court Armoury, as long as it costs no more than 10 Ducats.

Goaded Forth is a neat ability that gives them a +1 Dice to their Dash Action, as long as they are within 8” and in Line of Sight of a Yoke Fiend. Their other ability is Law of Hell, which removes a Wretched from your Warband Roster should they take down an Elite.

The Wretched will be little more than batteries for your spells, but you can use them as fodder on the front lines after you have kitted out the main pieces in your warband. Be warned, though: you cannot have more Wretched than you do units with the Demonic keyword. So you can’t just swarm the board with Wretched and laugh like a maniac.

Yoke Fiend: 35 Ducats

Yoke Fiends are Demonic and Bestial Hybrids, grafted by the Priests of Hell. Coated in muscle and a mish-mash of animal features and traits, depending on how they were constructed. Despite their origins, they are formidable on the field, standing well above the meagre humans the faithful sacrifice to the Great War.

Stat-wise, you’ve got a typical 6” movement and a single +1 Dice in Melee. They may take a single Ranged Weapon for less than 30 Ducats, but may take anything else they want.

Their only ability is Glory Lost that adds +1 Dice to Melee Attacks made by a Yoke Fiend if there are 3 or more Sin Markers next to any number of friendly Yoke Fiends on the battlefield.

Pit Locust: 90 Points

Ferocious, horse-sized beasts, Pit Locusts are armoured behemoths whose faces are a disfigured mockery of a human. Feared even within Hell, they attack with blade forelimbs and a stinger on their tail. Despite all that, maybe they are the goodest of boys. If not friend, then why friend-shaped, right?

The Pit Locust is a fast-moving flanker that, despite their quick movement and flying, is quite tanky. The 8” Flying movement helps them get up the board, and the +1 Melee Characteristic helps them take down some weaker opponents. Lastly, the -2 Armour helps them survive longer than most. Unfortunately, they can’t take any Battlekit apart from the Crown of Hellfire, so you will need to rely solely on their melee attacks. Luckily, their Insectile Stingers ability lets them make two Melee Attacks with the Shrapnel keyword. While they may not be able to take down tougher targets, they can stack Blood Markers on an enemy, ready for another model of yours to bank them and secure a kill. Throw in a Crown of Hellfire, which gives all models within 1” a Blood Marker at the end of the activation, and you have a decent way to generate Blood Markers quickly.

Desecrated Saint: 140 Ducats

When Hell’s minions or champions are captured, they are taken to be locked away to be interrogated and experimented upon, where few mortals will set eyes on them. When a future Saint, Prophet, or other holy figure of the faithful is taken, however, they are proudly put on display, albeit in a new, more grotesque form.

This behemoth boasts the usual 6” Movement and a +3 in Melee and a native -3 Armour! It comes with Fear, Tough, and Strong, which will prove useful, as it has three hands, letting you wield a single two-handed weapon and two one-handed weapons, or three one-handed weapons.

Ability-wise, Annihilator allows you make one Fight Action for each Melee Weapon that the model is equipped with. Aura of Sin causes enemy models to be unable to benefit from their own friendly abilities, Actions or Special Rules for their Warband or Battlekit, excluding Ranged Attacks. It’s important  Relentless ignores the movement penalty when the Saint stands up after having been Downed. Lastly, Totemic grants a Sin Marker to a friendly Demonic model, should it have activated within 1” of the Saint.  The Saint is a great unit, but it suffers the same problem as the Praetor: its size. Whilst the Praetor can get up the board quicker, despite its 50mm base, the Saint not only sits on a 60mm base, but it also is the largest model in the game and only has a 6” movement. This will be drawing all the firepower your enemy can throw at it. Some of the sins can mitigate this weakness; however, for the most part, your Saint will struggle to get into the thick of things.

The Sins

We’ll be covering each Sin specifically in future articles, but for a brief overview, here’s what the vibe is in each of them: 

  • Pride: Pride is your Strength. Defiance is your purpose. Wield the Morningstar’s Light and armaments to bring your enemy to their knees. Use Open Terrain and elevation to your advantage, while keeping your Demonic units safe as your Wretched throw themselves in front of incoming enemy attacks. 
  • Greed: War has a price. A price that Mammon is all too happy to pay, for far greater riches can be gained. You gain Sin Markers by targeting certain priced units that decrease in threshold each Turn, and your Spells and Battlekit only work when affecting targets with a certain (Ducat) worth. 
  • Lust: Turn the Sin of the Flesh to your advantage; your mortal Non-Demonic Units can extract more Sin Markers. Battlekit and Powers ask you and your opponent how much they are willing to gamble to achieve their goals or avoid pain. 
  • Envy: Hell Knights envy those above them, and as such, Sorcerers and Praetors may not be taken in this warband. Instead, you may take 2 to 5 Hell Knights, gaining a unique Glorious Deed to use each game, as well as free Sin Markers for one of your Knights. You also generate Sin Markers for nearby allies by taking enemies out of Action or Downing them. Take your enemy's weapons as your own; why should you not covet such power? 
  • Gluttony: Indulge on your enemies as you wear them down with Melee attacks, culminating in a feast for your allies. Your Elites are harder to hit, and your hungry Troops are nimbler, as they look for their next meal. Your Powers and Battlekit aim to keep you alive and hungry for more. 
  • Wrath: Use the very fires of Hell to lay your enemies low; attacks with the Fire Keyword grant Sin Markers. Powers and Battlekit play around with the Fire Keyword, letting models add it to all their Melee Attacks, or even turn the ground itself to fire. 
  • Sloth: Ensure inevitable victory by converting your Success rolls into Risky Success Rolls, and slowing your opponent's tempo. Your Spells and Battlekit punish enemy models that have already activated and pull them out of position.

Final Thoughts

So, there we have it! Court is one of the most rewarding factions to play; like New Antioch, you need to use every part of a unit's activation to juggle objective capture, board control, and taking out important targets, all with a very small effective model count. If you pull it off, you may just find yourself at the seat of the table.
