Heroes of the Black Library: Ghazghkull Thraka

June 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Across the grim darkness of Warhammer 40,000, the Mortal Realms of Age of Sigmar, and the battlefields of The Old World, a ceaseless conflict rages between steadfast champions of order, terrifying architects of ruin, and everyone caught in between. Heroes of the Black Library is a deep dive into the incredible characters that make these universes so rich, from the tabletop to the bookshelf and beyond. Join us as we explore their bloody, larger-than-life tales, proving that in an eternity of carnage where there is no peace, the line between an inspiring hero and a monstrous villain is almost always forged in the fires of war. Happy reading!

Image credit: Games Workshop

Prophet of the Waaagh!. The Beast of Armageddon. Warlord of Warlords. The Waaagh! That Walks. An Ork of many names, Ghazghkull Mag Uruk Thraka has earned them all standing as the 41st millennium's most recognizable greenskin. Last time we took a look at Commissar Sebastian Yarrick, Ghazghkull's storied arch-nemesis, as well as a short history of the first three Wars of Armageddon.

With that war-torn setting at the front and center of the game's 11th Edition kickoff, we've now arrived at the Fourth War of Armageddon with some familiar faces back in the frame. Thanks to an invention called the Mega-Tellyshocka, the Orks- led by Thraka- have managed to mass-teleport their warships directly into the planet's orbit, saving them the trouble of running a gauntlet of defenses. At the same time, Space Wolves bearing Old Bale Eye returned to the planet, inadvertently reuniting the two arch-foes for a rematch.

Whether Ghazghkull gets the upper hand or Yarrick manages to prevail remains to be seen, but it's clear the Imperium has a hell of a fight on its hands. Ghazghkull, after all, is something of an enigma, simultaneously the pinnacle of his species- and yet something quite a bit different.

There are three things it helps to understand when it comes to The Waaagh! That Walks.

He's Functionally Immortal

Don't get it wrong, Ghazghkull can be killed. It's staying dead that's the hard part. Before he was the boss of bosses, he was slain by a bolter round to the head, only to return to life after having his skull reconstructed with adamantium by Mad Dok Grotsnik.

Later, he'd be beheaded in a duel with Wolf Lord Ragnar Blackmane of the Space Wolves. Mad Dok Grotsnik came to his rescue again, recovering the severed head and reattaching it to a mechanical body.

There's even a moment in Ghazghkull Thraka, Warlord of Warlords by Denny Flowers where he appears to fall in battle once again (this time against the Black Templars), but as the Imperium keeps finding out to its dismay it's hard to keep a good Ork down.

He's A Spiritual Vessel

Religion isn't something that comes readily to mind when you think of Orks, though they do frequently invoke their twin gods of Gork (who is brutal but cunning) and Mork (cunning but brutal). Ghazghkull, though, is a full-blown prophet, and though him we've seen the Ork cosmology develop in some interesting ways.

The Great Green is the Orkish afterlife, a sort of greenskin Valhalla where Gork and Mork reside. Ever since he 'recovered' from being shot in the head, Ghazghkull has been driven by visions and revelations that compel him to labor to bring the Great Green into the real world by setting the universe on fire.

He's a Racial Revanchist

It's hard to imagine now, but the hooligan peoples of the Ork race weren't always the comic relief of the Warhammer universe. Long ago during the War of Heaven, the Old Ones engineered a race of warriors known as the Krorks. While they looked like the Orks of today (albeit significantly larger), they were highly intelligent and technologically advanced.

Over time they've devolved into the Orks of today, but Ghazghkull has a long memory of what his race once was- and a burning ambition to get back to that state again. This is the rationale behind why Ghazghkull hasn't just killed Yarrick out of hand when he's had the opportunity. Yarrick is a "good enemy," one that pushes Ghazghkull and the Orks to be the very best they can be- and in so doing he serves the greater purpose of the Orkish people.

While Ghazghkull hasn't featured in as many stories as Yarrick, there are a couple where he's taken a starring role.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Prophet of the Waaagh, by Nate Crowley (2022)

Serving as a sort of 'origin story' for the Warlord of Warlords, Nate Crowley's foundational work examines Ghazghkull Thraka through the eyes of his sidekick-cum-banner-carrier, Makari the Grot.

In a setup similar to Gav Thorpe's Luther: First of the Fallen, the book centers on an interrogation between Lord Inquisitor Tytonida Falx and the captured Makari. The Grot is plenty forthcoming, explaining Ghazghkull's meteoric rise from a simple Orkish soldier shot in the head through to his ascension to Prophethood.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Ghazghkull Thraka, Warlord of Warlords, by Denny Flowers (2026)

Just as with the previous book, Ghazghkull largely takes center-stage-by-proxy here. This leaves it susceptible to a common criticism of Black Library books titled after the game's largest characters (such as Jude Reid's Fulgrim: The Perfect Son or Ushoran: Mortarch of Delusion by Dale Lucas), that the titles of the books represent a kind of bait-and-switch. If you picked up Ghazghkull Thraka, Warlord of Warlords hoping for a full-on Ghazghkull story you may be disappointed (a point I addressed head-on in my review of the book), but all the same these kinds of stories tend to examine their subject through the lens of another.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Waaagh! Ghazghkull, a Codex: Orks Supplement (2014)

While the above books will give you a great look at The Waaagh! That Walks, old Codexes are treasure troves of information and insight. Back in the game's 7th Edition they released an entire supplement on the Third War of Armageddon- and the classic face-off between Ghazghkull and Yarrick.

There's a ton of lore in this one, and if you're a subscriber to Warhammer+ then you'll find it available for free in the Warhammer Vault.

The Waaagh! Ghazghkull Codex on Yarrick

From Paper to Plastic

Like his arch-nemesis, Ghazghkull has graced the tabletop in a number of iterations throughout the years beginning in 1994. That was when the game's 2nd Edition ushered in greenskin glory with the Codex: Orks, and Ghazghkull got to carry the flame as one of the faction's leading characters.

Image credit: Games Workshop

The original Ghazghkull miniature was cast in metal and came in a two-mini blister pack alongside his bannerman, Makari. The sculpt was made by Kev Adams, who had been responsible for the bulk of Games Workshop's early Ork and Goblin sculpts.

Image credit: tracksidecs

Just like Commissar Yarrick, the game's 3rd Edition ushered in updated miniatures to help showcase their role in the Third War of Armageddon in 2000. These were initially offered in metal, but then pivoted to finecast resin a decade later. You can see already the significant growth in stature Ghazghkull was granted in this sculpt by Brian Nelson, representing a complete overhaul and move towards the hyper-muscled brutes we know and love today.

Then in 2020, Maxime "Morbäck" Pastourel was handed the reins for a new sculpt, one that would now showcase Ghazghkull's head on the mechanical body supplied to him by Grotsnik.

Image credit: Games Workshop

And look who's back! After being unexpectedly absent from the year 2000 release, Makari returns to his banner-wavin' ways. The size difference between the two is now colossal, displaying the might and majesty of the Prophet of the Waaagh!

Shelf Candy

If you're looking to decorate your shelf with some goodies for Ghazghkull, you've definitely got a few options.

Image credit: Games Workshop

First up is the Limited Edition of Crowley's Prophet of the Waaagh!. This was announced during the first-ever Black Library Preview Online! back in December of 2020, and had bonkers reveals including Dan Abnett's Penitent and Alpharius: Head of the Hydra by Mike Brooks.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Sticking with the same title for a moment, while we don't have an ETA on it just yet the Black Library has teased an upcoming Illustrated and Annotated Edition release of Prophet of the Waaagh!

These make terrific library additions due to their combination of lower price (compared to deluxe editions) and bonus content, and Crowley joins Dan Abnett (the Eisenhorn trilogy), Chris Wraight (The Lords of Silence), Robert Rath (The Infinite and the Divine), and Sandy Mitchell (For the Emperor) in this exclusive-but-growing club.

Image credit: Games Workshop

I'll admit while I didn't love this cover, it's still a very nice version of Ghazghkull Thraka, Warlord of Warlords for your library, and Denny's Afterword is terrific too.

Image credit: Starforged

A lot of the collectibles Starforged produces- including this sterling silver men's necklace piece based on Ghazghkull's formidable klaw- are marketed as jewelry but can make excellent showpieces and decoratives. Sure at just shy of $300 it's not easy on the wallet, but when it comes to Warhammer what is?

Image credit: Starforged

At only two-thirds the price of the klaw, this Gork and Mork tandem ring is a (slightly) more budget-friendly offering that's perfectly on-theme with the trappings of their Prophet.

Image credit: WarhammerArt.com

WarhammerArt has this classic bit of Ghazghkull art by Dave Gallagher from the cover of 2000's Codex: Armageddon available in a fine print, which also features some other guy about to get krumped. But mostly Ghazghkull.

Image credit: Merchoid

Since Orks use teef as currency this collectible coin probably won't move their needle much, but it will look pretty fetching on the shelf. And at $30, your bank account will thank you. I own a couple of coins in this series for the Museum and they're nice pieces, suitably sized and heavy for the price and come in a sturdy plastic display cover.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Finally, with this coin of the month from December 2022 Games Workshop pays tribute to the Goffs, the tribe that Ghazghkull Thraka himself hails from. Obviously you'll have to trawl the secondary market to find them, but they're right in the usual and customary range of about twenty bucks.

I hope you've enjoyed the latest Heroes of the Black Library feature. If you have an Ork-themed Warhammer library, don't hesitate to comment below with what you've put together!
