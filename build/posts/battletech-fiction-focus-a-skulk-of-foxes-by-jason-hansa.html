BattleTech Fiction Focus: A Skulk of Foxes by Jason Hansa

July 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hey there, MechWarriors! I've been promising for a while now that I would eventually get around to talking about BattleTech fiction not written by Bryan Young, and that day has come at last! Today we're stepping back to one of the "Fortunes of War" novellas released as a tie-in with the BattleTech: Mercenaries Kickstarter campaign, A Skulk of Foxes by Jason Hansa.

We'd like to thank Jason Hansa for providing us with a copy of A Skulk of Foxes for our review!

Credit: Catalyst Game Labs

A Skulk of Foxes stands in contrast with the rest of the Fortunes of War series; the Screaming Eagles mercenary company plays an important supporting role here, but, as the title and cover graphics might suggest, this is very much Clan Sea Fox's story.

A Skulk of Foxes follows three main threads, all of which converge on the planet Son Hoa, an independent world sustained by the industrial output of its StarCorps BattleMech factory.

Watch Agent Aden is a MechWarrior by upbringing, newly tested-down into service with the Sea Fox Watch, its intelligence service. Volunteering to investigate an uptick in bandit activity in the vicinity of Son Hoa, he finds himself in very unfamiliar waters.

Colonel Zane Hokala is the commander of the Screaming Eagles mercenary company. Descendants of an SLDF unit (like the more-famous Eridani Light Horse and Crater Cobras), their once-prestigious reputation was tarnished by war crimes committed during the Fourth Succession War. More than a century later, Hokala's Eagles are a shadow of the unit they once were, still struggling to escape their forebears' infamy, scraping by in thankless garrison postings.

Merchant Commander Janelle's interest in Son Hoa, and in the Screaming Eagles, is initially purely financial: they're clients, and her duty is to cultivate a relationship with them in that role. Colonel Hokala wins her trust and respect, however, and events on Son Hoa become much more personal for her following an attempt on her life while she's on-world.

We've seen the Clan Sea Fox Watch spotlighted in another recent book, Bryan Young's VoidBreaker, but what Hansa puts Aden through in this book stands in sharp contrast to the Bond-movie-inspired action of Young's novel. He's under-trained and awkward at field work, but he has the great fortune to encounter a cell of agents from the Republic of the Sphere Intelligence Service (RSIS). They have all the skills and training Aden lacks, but have none of his financial backing, so they're able to form a mutually beneficial partnership. Their support allows Aden to really blossom into his role, bringing forward his strength in logistics while helping him address his blind spots.

The intelligence work at the spine of this novella is more Bourne than Bond in flavor. Sci-fi elements are definitely present, most notably in the form of cybernetically-enhanced infiltrator Mika, whose literally modular body allows feats up to and including impersonating a man closely enough to fool his boyfriend, but they share space with seedy safehouses, (offscreen) torture with a car battery, and the like. Our protagonists are certainly sympathetic, but Hansa isn't particularly concerned with framing them as heroic, and the narrative never shies from the brutal side of their work.

Despite the novella kicking off with Aden's viewpoint, he begins to fade into the background as the book picks up speed, dropping back to a supporting role as Janelle and Mika (who develops a romantic interest in the Merchant Commander) come to the fore. It's Janelle who's really at the heart of this story, and she cuts an interesting figure, a multifaceted blend of nobility, pragmatism, adventurousness, warmth, and cunning. The softer moments of the story, where humanity and emotion come to the fore, usually involve her, but she is also every inch the Merchant-Warrior, and profit never disappears as a motive and consideration for her.

Even though this isn't really a starring role for the Screaming Eagles, Colonel Hokala is fleshed out well in the page space he gets: He's a grieving widower, a proud father, and a man stubbornly holding to his dignity in a world which expects less of him. It's easy to see how he earns Janelle's respect, and hopefully this novella will help his unit gain prominence in a Doylist sense, not just in-universe; I'd be happy to see more from him and his daughters.

A Skulk of Foxes is a good example of how a book doesn't need to put a lot of energy towards a big overarching theme to have heart and humanity. It doesn't really try to say something in the way Young's BattleTech work I've reviewed for this site has, but the character beats it hits feel a bit more nuanced for that, a bit less like the means to a particular emotive end.

Clan Sea Fox Spina Khanate Tiburon. Credit: Lynn B.

Final Thoughts

Jason Hansa's A Skulk of Foxes is a brisk adventure building to a satisfying climax. It fleshes out aspects of BattleTech's setting which often fade into the background relative to the giant robot fights, like espionage, corporate intrigue, and mercantilism, while still finding room for a bit of giant robot fighting as a digestif. It is a novella, and thus fairly brief, but its characters are remarkably well-rounded for a story of its length, with authentic voices. It's an easy recommendation for a read if anything in the subject matter interests you!

As a final note, I'll mention that Hansa is not only a class act, he's also One Of Us to a greater degree than I'm used to seeing from tie-in fiction authors (our own Valk excepted!), an active participant in the same general tournament circuit Liberty, Peri, and I play in. Swing by one of the larger events like Southern Assault or Brawl in the Bluegrass and you might have a chance to throw dice with him!

I hope to review more of Hansa's work, and begin a broader look at the scope and legacy of BattleTech fiction (over 120 novels and counting!) soon. Until then, take care, MechWarriors!
