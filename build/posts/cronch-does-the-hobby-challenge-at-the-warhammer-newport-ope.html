Cronch Does the Hobby Challenge at the Warhammer Newport Open 2026

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer
At Tabletop Battles, we sometimes receive products or event invites from Warhammer for review. For this event, though, Rich purchased his own ticket, and attendance was not based on any existing relationship with, or an invite from, Games Workshop.

Although Britain is undeniably the home of Warhammer, and we’re lucky enough to have Warhammer World on our (figurative) doorsteps, for a while now we’ve lacked large Warhammer-run con-style events similar to things like the Tacoma Open in the USA. 2026 has seen that change, and the Newport Open is the second of the American-style Open events that Warhammer have run in the UK. Unlike the earlier Birmingham Open, though, which piggybacked off the UK Games Expo, Newport is an entirely Warhammer offering. It was my first time at an Open, and I was keen to see what it had to offer.

I’d originally planned to enter the three-day, eight-game Age of Sigmar GT, but I’m out of practice and have been riding a wave of burnout, and ultimately decided that it might be a bit much to jump into without planning and practice. I ended up refunding my ticket and pondering over what to do instead. Having been asking for this style of event in every possible survey opportunity for the last few years, I felt like it would have been a cop-out not to attend and support it, so I settled on the Hobby Challenge activity. Low pressure, and low commitment; something where I wouldn’t be inconveniencing anybody if I decided to hit the bricks and go home early.

“But Cronch, what’s the Hobby Challenge?” I hear you ask. Understandable, given that at least some of the event pages ahead of the event have listed “Hobby Challenge” with no further elaboration.

What Is the Hobby Challenge?

The Hobby Challenge has appeared at various Warhammer Opens in the past, and is a weekend-long kitbashing and painting challenge. The easiest visual representation is probably the episodes of Scrap Demon on Warhammer TV, and the Hobby Challenge takes that idea and extends it from a short video to a weekend-long activity. A ticket for the challenge (£50 at Newport, bookable in advance or pay on the day) gets you a boxed centrepiece model, an oval or rectangular base, unlimited access to the big pile of sprues, use of tools, brushes, paints, and the ubiquitous Warhammer Open swag box, with the end goal of creating a diorama or conversion to share with your peers.

The team running the challenge set a theme for each event - at Newport, this revolved around the idea of a space hulk crashing on Armageddon, with both Imperial and Ork forces dispatched into the wreck to retrieve something precious. The theme informs both the initial models available to choose from and at least some of the sprues available in the sprue pile, with Newport attendees able to choose from a selection of Ork and Marine characters.

Newport Open 2026's Hobby Challenge story. Credit: Rich Nutter

From there, it’s a simple enough prospect. Build your model, converting if you wish, and use the sprue pile to build a scene, or perhaps a big conversion like a vehicle, sticking to the suggested theme as much or as little as you like. When you’re ready, the event team will prime your mini in a colour of your choice (including zenithals if you like), and then you can use the paints and brushes supplied to paint up your creation. At the end of the weekend, there’s some judging for awards around theme, painting, converting, and an overall winner is chosen. Anything you’ve created is yours to keep.

Does that sound like fun? It is!

Friday: The Build

I had an unhurried start to my Open weekend, arriving at the International Convention Centre Wales at around 2pm. Checking in was easy, and I was surprised to receive the 2026 swag box with my entry. This contained a decent selection of merch items: a lanyard, a battle journal notebook with pen, some 40mm casino chip-style objective markers, a metal die, some 40k and AoS-themed sticker sheets, and a £10 voucher for the event store (off a minimum £25 purchase). Nothing mind-blowingly important or valuable, but a nice add-on when the ticket was only £50, compared to £125 for the GTs.

Swag box contents. Credit: Rich Nutter

More importantly, the box contained two items that would help with the hobby challenge. First up, an A4 cutting mat, replete with a Warlord Titan design. This is cool as hell, and will definitely get some use at home. Secondly, there was a foam insert with slots for a water pot, paints, and brushes, the idea being that you could use the swag box as a kind of project box for the weekend. This was a thoughtful addition that I got plenty of use out of during the event, even when just trying to keep my paints organised on the desk.

Swag box in hand, I headed through to the main hall, taking in the scale of the event before heading to the Hobby Challenge area. I was greeted by the team running the event, who explained this event’s lore: a Space Hulk crashes into Armageddon, and Imperial and Ork forces both dispatch forces inside to retrieve something. They then offered me a choice of my centrepiece model and a base to build around. The options were:

  • Beastboss
  • Warboss in Mega Armour
  • Boss Snikrot
  • Phobos Librarian
  • Lieutenant in Reiver Armour
  • Maybe one more Space Marine that I can’t remember!

I have a Marine army, so I opted to go against my usual instincts and pick an Ork. In the end, I opted for the Warboss in Mega Armour, a model I’ve admired from afar since it came out, but never bought. I also selected a large oval base, before immediately realising that that would be hard to make straight lines on, and swapping it for a large rectangle instead. Even now, a plan was forming.

I’d decided that, sprues willing, I’d like to make some kind of Space Hulk corridor, or hatchway, with the Warboss facing off against an as-yet undetermined opponent. The rectangular base would describe the rough size of my diorama, and I’d need to source parts to make a main corridor wall along the long edge, a floor, potentially an end wall and/or hatch, some ideally Imperial opponents, and some battlefield scatter. Having found a place to sit and deposit my stuff, it was time to brave The Sprue Pile.

The sprue pile was, frankly, magnificent.

The sprue pile. Credit: Rich Nutter

I’m not sure what I was expecting, but there was a lot of stuff here. Most, if not all, sprues were incomplete or broken down in some way, but there was a healthy mix of terrain, Ork, and Imperial parts, plus the occasional bit of something more esoteric, like Liberators from Age of Sigmar. To add some excitement, staff would occasionally announce the addition of something new to the pile, usually with a narrative reason. As an example, at one point we got a couple of lines of narrative about ancient warriors in relic marks of armour emerging from the warp to join the fray, which heralded the addition of some Horus Heresy kits to the pile.

First forays. Credit: Rich Nutter'

Initially I grabbed a few sprues that stood out to me - some barricades from the updated Aegis Defence Line, some stockade walls from the Battlezone: Fronteris range, a couple of random vents that I couldn’t identify, and a sprue of Death Korps of Krieg infantry that I thought might serve as my Imperial forces. With a bit of experimentation, I realised that I could cut the spikes off of the stockade walls, invert one, and pin/glue them together to make a wall that seemed about the right size for the interior of a Space Hulk corridor. Conveniently, it was also very close to the length of the base I’d chosen!

Taking shape. Credit: Rich Nutter

I’d identified that the aegis barricades might look good butted up against each other as either a vertically or horizontally opening door, but that meant I would need to build an end wall for them to mount to. Salvation came in the form of the top panel from a Rhino, trimmed flat to give a hatch hole that my blast doors would cover well. Some cutting and glueing of some wall corner pieces later, and I had the bones of my idea together. I also found a couple of broken-up floor tiles (possibly from a Necromunda kit?), which I glued down both to get the height of the doors right, and to square up the angle between the walls. In hindsight, this was an error, as I couldn’t find any more of these tiles later!

With the glue setting, I opted to head outside for some food, look around the game hall and store, say hi to Corrode playing in the 40k GT, and generally kill time for a little while. Refreshed, I came back to my table and decided to build my Warboss. I did consider a conversion, and hunted for a head in the sprue pile to give him a gas mask or similar (I was imagining the heads from the Heresy Ogryns), but I couldn’t find anything, and decided to build him as-is. Luckily for me, in the intervening time somebody had returned a load of flat panels to the sprue table, and I was able to grab a couple of octagonal plates from the Aegis Line to fill out my floor needs, as well as some corrugated pieces that I cut down to fill in the windows on the wall, and some assorted small parts to fill in gaps in the Rhino wall.

Further progress. Credit: Rich Nutter

This left me with some strange-shaped gaps in my floor, until a bolt of inspiration hit. I’d already planned to really rust this corridor up, and I’d also generated a sizable amount of plastic shrapnel during my construction attempts. I filled my gaps with glue, sprinkled on a bunch of plastic rubble, and then dripped more glue on. I also found some Astrogranite on the painting table, which I thinned and stippled around the scenery, ready to look like patches of rust texture after priming. Finally, I found bits to make three Imperials - an Arbitrator (with a stern-looking Sororitas head), a Cadian, and a Krieg trooper. I wasn’t sure if I’d use them all, but it was good to have them built at least.

Deciding that it was time to paint, but also that my work needed more time for glue and texture to dry, I opted to call it a day at around 7 PM and head to my hotel for check-in before finding a local pub for dinner. I was pretty pleased with what I’d managed to build in a day, and was looking forward to getting the paints out after a good night’s sleep.

End of day 1, ready to prime. Credit: Rich Nutter

I do think it’s worth mentioning here that alongside the clippers, mould line scraper, plastic glue, paints and brushes provided by the event, I brought some of my own equipment from home, mainly my building toolkit (reliable clippers, scalpel, files/sandpaper, hobby drill, plastic and super glue, small clamps), and around 20 reliable paints/washes alongside a wet palette, my brushes, and some corks/wire for subassemblies. I wasn’t really sure whether this was encouraged or not, and couldn’t find anything about it in the rules, so I ended up asking a friend who had done it before, and they informed me that I’d be fine. I was far from the only person with my own stuff, and it seemed like the staff were happy for people to use the tools that they were comfortable with. Obviously, Warhammer aren’t going to supply drills and knives at an event that kids can (and do) enter!

Saturday: Painting

Refreshed, showered, and breakfasted, I made my way back to the hall at around 10 AM on Saturday. First things first, my now dry piece would need priming. Staff were taking care of this, with a range of neutral colours available - they also seemed open to the idea of doing a layered zenithal for people hoping to take advantage of some built-in shading. I asked for my main built to be primed black, and gave them the Ork on one painting handle and the three Imperials pinned to another, all to be done in grey. They were sorted in record time, and I could get down to business.

Slop it up. Credit: Rich Nutter

I tackled the corridor first, as I knew it would take a while. Straight over the black, I slopped on big, thinned-down patches of Warhammer Colour Mournfang Brown, Skrag Brown and Balor Brown, then left these to dry for an hour or two. Once dry, I added some Troll Slayer Orange in more targeted spots, and then once this was dry I drybrushed the whole thing softly with Leadbelcher, trying to build up the metal tone gradually and not erase the brown tones. This is a great method taught to me by a friend who is a terrain painting whiz, and it came in clutch here for getting a lot of colour down quickly. With that done, the terrain is basically done for now, barring some details later.

Ork skin done. Credit: Rich Nutter

In the gaps between terrain steps, I tackled the Warboss. I used Pro Acryl Bright Green as a base, shaded by mixing in some Pro Acryl Dark Hot Pink and layered with Bright Yellow Green. At this point, I was very glad to have put the Rogue Hobbies Pro Acryl signature set in my case, as well as the Pro Acryl Bright Yellow Green (an all-time favourite). I really wanted to paint the Boss’ armour red, maybe with yellow details, but decided that compositionally it would not stand out from the rusty background well, so ultimately I chose blue instead. This was based with Warhammer Colour Kantor Blue, before being layered and highlighted with more of the Pro Acryl set. From there, I filled in metals and details with a bunch of colours, and then moved on to the Cadian.

Ork done, also featuring drybrushed metals on the diorama base. Credit: Rich Nutter

I’m not going to teach you how to suck eggs (paint Guardsmen), but I will highlight one notable point when I realised I absolutely did not have the right colour for the Cadian’s classic green armour. The event organisers had provided a lot of paint, but not covering every colour in the range. Fortunately, after some hunting (and briefly considering buying a paint from the store), I found a single pot of Contrast Creed Camo buried behind about 15 pots of Bad Moon Yellow, and the day was saved. Over the rest of the day I finished the Cadian, painted the crates I’d added to the corridor, and basecoated the Arbitrator's armour, alongside wandering around the event checking out the tables, admiring the pre-painted terrain (very nice), catching up with some of my Age of Sigmar club, and generally soaking up the atmosphere.

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

After round 6 of the GT was finished, I met up with Corrode for a late-night katsu curry delivered to a dark picnic bench outside the ICC before returning to the hotel for more sleep.

Rich's final Hobby Challenge entry with minis in place. Credit: Rich Nutter

Sunday: More Painting, and Results

I overslept a little on Sunday, and after breakfast and checking out of the hotel, it was nearly 11 AM before I made it back to the hobby area. Fortunately, I had a pretty defined set of goals for the day: finish the Arbitrator, and paint the Krieg soldier. I started with the Arbitrator, and the high proportion of black armour meant it was fairly swift going. I highlighted it up with blue-black colours, sticking to greys on the black leather areas to differentiate them. The hair was basecoated Warhammer Colour Corax White, then washed a couple of times with Russ Grey thinned with Lahmian Medium before being highlighted with a pure white.

The swag box did indeed come in useful. Credit: Rich Nutter

Whilst finishing the Arbitrator, I noticed a couple of places where I’d failed to add pouches/holsters, and there were obvious gaps. I opted to fill these in with pouches and grenades from a Cadian sprue, which I superglued on and then painted over bare plastic. At the same time, I managed to find a voxcaster backpack for the Krieg soldier, replacing the non-standard bag I’d added in place of a correct piece the day before.

With a couple of hours left, it was time to get the Krieg guardsman done. Fortunately, this wasn’t my first rodeo in this regard, and I got it done to a decent standard in the time available. With about 30 minutes left, my friend Chris came over and helpfully pointed at my diorama and said something along the lines of “it would look better with some hazard stripes” which felt a bit like being hit with a hammer. I decided to rush and try it, and managed to cover up my pretty messy stripes with rust and weathering to give a serviceable effect.

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

Last-minute hazard stripes, and the base is complete. Credit: Rich Nutter

With the clock ticking down (we had until 3 PM to finish), I blacked out the edges of the diorama, glued and pinned the minis in place, and then took it across to the display table to put it alongside everyone else’s creations. I’m so happy with what I produced! I had an idea and executed on it. I’m really pleased with the level of finish I achieved in a weekend.

Rich's final Hobby Challenge entry with minis in place. Credit: Rich Nutter

It was really amazing to see what everyone had come up with, spanning smaller dioramas like mine all the way up to huge, bulky Ork Gargants. I had a great time chatting to people about what we’d made, and if I have one regret for the event, it’s that I mostly kept to myself while working rather than making more of an effort to chat to my fellow Hobby Challengers across the weekend!

A selection of other entries. Credit: Rich Nutter

Amazing Ork Gargant which won Best Conversion. Credit: Rich Nutter

I liked this layout. Credit: Rich Nutter

Another take on the classic Space Hulk encounter. Credit: Rich Nutter

After we’d all had a chance to look around, we were ushered away so that the judges could review the entries and decide on the winners. After 45 minutes or so of waiting with bated breath, we were called back. Prizes were given out for best theme, best conversion, best painted, and a youngbloods prize for under 16s. Then it was time to announce the overall winner, and…

The Hobby Challenge Trophy. Credit: Rich Nutter

I won! Unbelievable! I really wasn’t expecting to, given the quality of the other offerings, but the judges said they liked the combination of painting and conversion execution whilst also sticking closely to the narrative theme of the event. My prize: the Hobby Challenge trophy, lovingly built and sprayed by the event staff while we were working on our entries. I was excitedly informed that it features 240 skulls and 15 grots - can you spot them all?

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

Alongside the trophy, I was also given a Horus Heresy boxed set and the promise of a Spearhead or Combat Patrol of my choice. I’m really impressed with this level of prize support, and grateful to win!

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

Armageddon Space Hulk diorama. Credit: Rich Nutter

Wrapping Up

So, all in all, I had a great time doing the Hobby Challenge at the Newport Open! Obviously I’m thrilled to have won, but even putting that aside, I had a great time just sitting and hobbying towards a defined goal, rummaging through sprues, and soaking up the atmosphere of the expo. Unlike tournaments, which often leave me feeling drained, I came home feeling refreshed and reinvigorated. The venue was also great, with helpful, friendly staff, great air conditioning, decent enough food options, and ample (if slightly expensive) parking. If you’d like to attend an Open but don’t want to play games, then I’d thoroughly recommend the Hobby Challenge as a fun and engaging option that’s good value for money. Do let us know if you attend one, or have done so in the past, and share your creations!
