[40k] 11th Edition Faction Pack Review: Grey Knights

June 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With a new edition of Warhammer 40K comes a whole new bunch of Detachments, and we’re handling them in groups to make it easier to separate the wheat from the chaff, at least for now. We’ll go through the relevant changes to the Datasheets and rules at play, then see how the Detachments themselves stand up.

Thank you again to Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of these rules.

Today we are looking at the most secretive of all the Marines, the Grey Knights. They have had a bumpy road throughout 10th edition, with some massive highs in the days of six Dreadknights and three Redeemers, to some pretty low lows when they are off meta, including right now in the land of Defilers. However, they will be going into the new edition with some shiny new Truesilver toys, and hopefully they will hit the ground running this edition. Let’s dive in.

Editors Note: Apparently Grey Knights ship with Space Marine faction packs now, rather than Imperium, if you were wondering where this was yesterday.

Datasheet Changes

  • Land Raider, Land Raider Crusader, Land Raider Redeemer, Razorback, Rhino, Stormhawk Interceptor, Stormraven Gunship, and Stormtalon Gunship: All of these units gain the FRAME keyword.
  • Brotherhood Chaplain: Add Leader to abilities
  • Stormtalon Gunship, Stormhawk Interceptor: Remove Hover, change M and OC characteristics to ‘-’.
  • Stormraven Gunship: Change M characteristic to 14” and remove the AIRCRAFT keyword
  • Brotherhood Techmarine, Guardians of the Machine Ability- changes to read: "Each time an enemy unit ends a charge move engaged with one or more friendly GREY KNIGHTS VEHICLE units and within 6” of this unit, you can target this unit with the Heroic Intervention stratagem, regardless of any other uses of that stratagem this phase. If you do, that use is -1 CP, and does not prevent any uses of that stratagem on other units this phase."

Rule Changes

Hallowed Conclave Detachment - Eye of the Augurium Enhancement: Change to;
  • GREY KNIGHTS model only. You can target this unit with the Heroic Intervention stratagem, regardless of any other uses of that stratagem this phase. If you do, that use is -1 CP, and does not prevent any uses of that stratagem on other units this phase.

Available Detachments

Grey Knights keep their Codex Detachments, and also get to hold on to Warpbane Task Force, which has probably been their most effective Detachment to date. Every single Codex Detachment is coming in at 2DP, and Warpbane, coming in as their most powerful, is sitting at 3DP.

They also get three new Detachments, all for 1 DP, focusing in on a single unit each - Argent Assault boosts Paladins, while Fires of Purgation helps (unsurprisingly) Purgation Squads, and Immaterial Interdiction boosts up Interceptors. We will take a look at each below.

In total, that gives them:

  • Warpbane Task Force - 3DP
  • Augurium Task Force - 2DP
  • Banishers - 2DP
  • Brotherhood Strike - 2DP
  • Hallowed Conclave - 2DP
  • Sanctic Spearhead - 2DP
  • Argent Assault - 1DP
  • Fires of Purgation - 1DP
  • Immaterial Interdiction - 1DP

Argent Assault

Grey Knight Paladins. Credit: Colin Ward

This is a solid Detachment choice that buffs the most special of the special boys that are Grey Knights, Paladins. This is a solid detachment that gives them some good boosts to damage and survivability. For 1DP, if you’re leaning in to Paladins, this is a no brainer.

Dauntless Champions

When a friendly PALADIN SQUAD unit is selected to fight, if the S of those attacks is lower than the T of the target, those attacks have +1 to the wound rolls.

This is straight up good. +1 to Wound rolls is exactly what S6 melee needs, as it means you’re hitting substantially harder into hard targets, a thing that Grey Knights have often struggled with outside of Dreadknights.

Enhancements

  • Psychic Celerity -  TERMINATOR model only. This unit has +1 to charge rolls. This is a very solid enhancement, and will help get your newly +1 to wound Paladins into the fight, including now looking at 8’” charges out of Deep Strike. You should take this if points are at all reasonable.
  • Vigilance of Titan - TERMINATOR model only. At the start of your Shooting phase, you can select one visible enemy unit within 12” of this unit. That enemy unit has +6” detection range. This is fine. Hidden will be an important mechanic, and chances are your Terminators of any flavor will be close enough to the enemy to actually make use of it. The real problem is that many Grey Knights units are wanting to be within 12” to shoot anyway, so often this won’t synergize well enough to make it worthwhile. But, points allowing, it isn’t a bad choice.

Stratagems

  • Truesilver Aegis - 1CP: In any phase, when a friendly PALADIN SQUAD unit suffers a mortal wound, that PALADIN SQUAD has a 4+ Feel No Pain against mortal wounds. Paladins don’t like taking mortals, and a 4+ is a great way to avoid that. This is a really good strat, and will keep a pricey unit alive longer.
  • A Threat Ended - 1CP: In the Fight phase, when a friendly PALADIN SQUAD unit is selected to fight, that PALADIN SQUAD unit’s melee attacks have [Precision]. Mass Precision is good now. It means that the enemy character is almost assuredly going to die first, leaving all the rest of the goobers to take worse saves after. This won’t be used every time, but it is a very nice tool to have in your pocket.
  • Aura of Vengeance - 1CP: In the Fight phase, when an enemy unit targets a friendly PALADIN SQUAD, that enemy unit’s melee attacks targeting that PALADIN SQUAD have [Hazardous]. This is less compelling, but occasionally will be strong. The changes to Hazardous means that this is going to fall pretty flat against multi-wound models or characters, but it will be fantastic to clear hordes of 1w models attacking you. They can expect to lose ⅓ of their unit just from trying to slap your 2+/4+ models, so who can be mad at that? Again, won’t come up all the time, but will be a benefit when it does. Can also be very nasty against enemies with  multiple Extra Attacks weapons.

Is It Good?

This is a solid Detachment. A good, general use rule, applied to a solid unit, with a few niche but interesting strats and a good enhancement. I think this fits the mold for what a solid Detachment will be. This may even justify a single big squad of Paladins led by Voldus, making a squad of -1 to Hit and Wound brawlers to go mow down your opponent. Really this Detachment’s viability will come down to if it has an interesting Disposition and whether you are taking Paladins, but if either of those is answered with a yes, I would heavily consider it.

Fires of Purgation

A Grey Knight Gunner armed with Psilencer - Credit: Pendulin

This Detachment, however, goes the other way. It attempts to layer solid rules onto a pretty bad unit, and so may not make the cut. Purgation Squads have taken pretty unremarkable shooting profiles in small numbers for not inconsequential amounts of points, and sort of always fell flat. Additionally, they were constantly competing with Dreadknight’s shooting, and have always come up short. Let’s take a look and see if the rules salvage this, though.

Searing Soulflame

When you select a unit to be pinned by a friendly PURGATION SQUAD unit’s Righteous Persecution ability, that enemy unit makes a battle-shock roll, with -1 to that battle-shock roll.

For those who may have forgotten, pinned here means that the target unit is at -2 to move and charge for the turn, but is not eligible to affect Monsters or Vehicles.

This is sneaky good. Everyone in their right mind (which therefore excludes fellow Tabletop Battles contributor Twohorse) has agreed all of 10th edition that Battle-shock is trash. That is maybe not the case anymore, though, as the changes to Battle-shock have actually made it an interesting tool. Being able to point and click to turn on the debuffs of pinned and force a debuffed Battle-shock will flip more primaries than you think, and will force units to continue to take shocks. However, the problem here is if you can shoot something you want Battle-shocked that is susceptible to pinning, why not just kill the thing? Grey Knights have better tools to do that than dropping a real unit close enough to both see and shoot the thing. However, because one guy in the unit must take a storm-bolter, tagging a unit with those shots just for the sake of debuffing them is a viable option.

Enhancements

  • Precognicient Volleys (Upgrade) - PURGATION SQUAD unit only. This unit’s snap shooting attacks hit on an unmodified hit roll of 5+. This is a 100% increase in hits on Overwatch, but without rerolls this is still mediocre. Probably a pass.
  • Boons of Deimos (Upgrade) - PURGATION SQUAD unit only. This unit’s ranged attacks have +2 S. This is much more interesting, as it makes flamers hit S8, Psilencer hit S7, and Psycannons hit S10. All of these are important breakpoints, and make this mediocre unit a whole lot scarier. The main problem, as always, with this is that it will add some amount of points, and will force the question “do I just bring a Dreadknight instead?”

Stratagems

  • Soul-Locked - 1CP:  In your Movement phase, when a friendly PURGATION SQUAD unit is selected to make a fall-back move, that move does not prevent your unit from being eligible to shoot. This is fine, but unlikely to be worth a CP for a 5 or less, depending on casualties, man unit.
  • Focused Immolation - 1CP: In your Shooting phase, when a friendly PURGATION SQUAD is selected to shoot, select one enemy unit, and that PURGATION SQUAD unit’s ranged attacks have [Devastating Wounds] and [Sustained Hits 1]. This is honestly quite good. The sustained will only benefit Psycannons, but honestly that is likely the best option most of the time, and Devastating Wounds flamers is also no joke. However, it is a counter synergy with both their datasheet and the detachment rule, as it goes more in the “just kill them” column and less in the “shoot them to debuff” one.
  • Spiritsear - 1CP:  In your Shooting phase, when a friendly PURGATION SQUAD unit has shot, select one battle-shocked enemy unit hit by those attacks. That enemy unit suffers D3+1 mortal wounds. Adding mortals isn’t bad but the terms and conditions are steep for a middling amount of mortals. Use it when it comes up and if it’s worth the CP.

Is It Good?

Sadly, no, I don’t believe this one makes the cut. The main time we saw Purgation Squads in the sun was at the height of Warpbane Taskforce, and with that sitting at 3DP there isn’t room to bring both. Without that, realistically these rules don’t do enough to buff a pretty mediocre unit, and the uses of it are still, sadly, overshadowed by the Dreadknight. I wish as much as the next person that Dreadknights would be less of a compelling unit, but here we are.

Immaterial Interdiction

Grey Knights Interceptors. Credit: Colin Ward

This is an interesting one, as it doesn’t really buff Interceptors so much as give them options. That being said, I do like this Detachment if you have the room, as Interceptors are a useful little unit, with decent output and a solid action monkey role too. There have been intermittent uses of these guys to hop in and out of transports, but unfortunately that's over for them in 11th Edition, so this needs to help them find a new role.

Echojump

In you Shooting phase, when a friendly INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit has shot, if that unit did not make an ingress move this turn, you can use this ability. If you do;

  • That unit cannot use its Personal Teleporters ability.
  • That unit can make a surge move of up to D6+1”.

This rule essentially gives Interceptors the option to forego their shoot and scoot in exchange for a shoot and surge. This gets them up into the fight, reducing charges and potentially ending the phase in combat without the need for a charge. I think, weirdly, in most situations you actually don’t want to surge into combat in your turn when you could charge and get Fights First, so it may lead to some weird play, so keep that in mind. Sadly this won’t do anything to shorten charges out of deep strike, but honestly that may be for the best for the game overall.

Enhancements

  • Predestined Coordinates (Upgrade) - INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit only. In your first Movement phase, this unit can make an ingress move. Turn 1 Deep Strike is always, always good. Because it is an upgrade, you also will be able to take multiples. Unless these boys are starting in a transport, if the points are reasonable you take this as many times as you take Interceptors.
  • Astral Overlap (Upgrade) - INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit only. This unit has Stealth. This, however, sucks. Stealth just isn’t great in 11e, as it doesn’t do anything that cover doesn’t already, and cover appears to be super easy to grab. Leave this one at home and take turn 1 Deep Strikes instead.

Stratagems

  • Blades from the Beyond - 1CP - In the Fight phase, when a friendly INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit that has made a charge move is selected to fight, that unit’s melee attacks have [Lance]. This one is great. As with Paladins above, S6 melee desperately wants +1 to wound. However, like I discussed above, this is yet another reason to actually not want to surge in your own turn, which is a shame. But this thing is great to have in your pocket.
  • By Thought Alone - 1CP - In your Shooting phase, when a friendly INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit starts an action, that action does not prevent your unit from being eligible to shoot. Situationally nice to have. If this unit is doing an action, is a bunch of bolter shots worth a CP? Sometimes, absolutely. Often, no. Keep it in mind.
  • Responsive Displacement - 1CP - In your opponent’s Movement phase, when an enemy unit that was engaged with the friendly INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit ends a fall-back move, if that INTERCEPTOR SQUAD unit is unengaged, that INTERCEPTOR SQUAD can make a normal move of up to D3+3”. Everyone loves a scurry away to safety. Great to have, but I do wish it was a more reliable flat 6”.

Is It Good?

This is solid, and will ultimately come down to 1- did you take Interceptors, and 2- do you have the free DP. If you took Warpbane or have already filled your floating 3rd DP, then you may not, but if you have these guys, giving them options and access to Lance is a great way to buff what is already a solid little unit.

Final Thoughts

Grey Knights have two good options and one dud, which I think is as good a place as you can ask for going into 11e. These detachments really focus on if you want to buff units that fit in to your game plan anyway, and if you do, they can really slot in with any detachment. You can double down on certain units, like Paladins using both Argent Assault and Hallowed Conclave, or you can just slot in one of these to boost your hammer unit or your skirmishers in Brotherhood Strike. Sadly, depending on your view of the ol’ baby carrier, none of these interact with the army defining Dreadknight, but that may force some new innovation and game play, which I can’t say I am mad at. Hopefully we see some fun new armies once the edition launches. See ya’ll on the next one.
