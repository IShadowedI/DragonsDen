Conquest First Blood: Might of the Orders Warband of Legend

October 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Exciting things are happening in First Blood! That has been true for some time now, since the launch of First Blood 2.0 has not only completely re-worked the game to be more of its own thing, it has also kicked off a slew of experimental rules writing that breaks the mold of Conquest as we know it.

First, Para Bellum included legendary Characters, something they had previously been very reluctant to do – and now we are seeing Warbands of Legends being added as an alternative way to play your favorite army. What are Warbands of Legends? Let’s find out together!

Hundred Kingdoms Iconic Artprint – Credit: Para Bellum Games

What Are Warbands of Legends?

For those of you coming from Warhammer, Warbands of Legends are best described in a nutshell as the Conquest equivalent to Armies of Renown.

For those who haven’t played Warhammer and therefore need an actual explanation, they are a mini army within your army, themed around a specific facet of the larger faction. In turn, they come with their own distinct Army Rules, special Upgrades for your Characters and fairly massive listbuilding restrictions.

The Might of the Orders

For the time being, there is only one Warband of Legend in First Blood, and it is The Might of the Orders Warband. It is available to the Hundred Kingdoms and, as you might expect, is themed around the Knightly Orders. As such, you are only allowed to play Order units with a very minimal number of Feudal units for variety and lore reasons. Before we go over them in detail, let’s quickly cover the Army Rules.

First up, the first Order Command Card you activate each round gets to choose between one of three bonuses which it gains for the remainder of the round. These affect all Warriors of the Command Card and are appropriately themed around the Edicts of Kleon – basically the knightly vows that the Orders adhere to.

  • Fight Without Fear grants Bravery (Warriors Rally as a Draw Event).
  • Persevere Through Injury and Fatigue gives you Regeneration (Warriors Heal 1 as a Draw Event).
  • Overcome Mortal Limits dishes out Flurry (Warriors Re-Roll Attack Rolls of “6” during Clash Actions).

These are all very nice to have, especially since you can adapt your choice to the current state of the game. If you win the roll-off for priority, you can push extra damage with Flurry while Regeneration or Rallying for free can help mitigate the blow back of losing priority.

All of your knights are very tough and fairly killy as-is, but the Orders tend to lean elite as we will see in the unit overview below. As such, any way to gain additional value out of your units is very welcome.

Next up, as an ode to the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rule, all your Orders Characters can perform the Duel Command Ability in addition to any other Command Ability they use. In turn, they can never Decline a Duel.

I really like this as a thematic touchpoint that ties the Orders Warband of Legend into the larger faction. In practice, it is almost exactly the normal Army Rule (double Commands) with a thematic limitation (double commands but only for Duels). However, all your Orders Characters (barring one we will get to below) are so killy that the limitation hardly feels like one in most cases.

Tying off the Army Rules is a true-blue copy and paste of the greatest Army Rule of the Hundred Kingdoms: Honored Heraldry! Like their parent army, any Standard Bearers in your army can be replaced by one of their mates within 1” if they are slain.

It is slightly worse here since you are more elite, lose access to a lot of the Infantry options and don’t want to bunch up your knights too much, but it is still very nice to have access to this.

Now that we have covered the Army Rules, let’s take a look at the listbuilding restrictions. The Might of the Orders Warband excises most of the Feudal elements and all of the Imperial and Theist options the Hundred Kingdoms usually have access to.

What you’re left with is a fairly short list. For Warlords, you have your pick between the Errant of the Order of the Shield, the Order of the Sword Priory Commander and the regular mounted Priory Commander. He still gets to choose between hailing from the Order of the Crimson Tower or the Order of the Sealed Temple.

Two of the Warlord options, the Errant and the Order of the Sword Priory Commander have the option to be included as a Hero, alongside the Servite. Beyond that, you have eight unit options, four Mainstay and four Restricted.

For Mainstay you to choose from among Militia and Militia Bowmen, the new Order of the Sword and the Penitents of St. Lazarus. These unlock the Restricted options, which are almost all Cavalry. The Order of the Ashen Dawn, Crimson Tower and Sealed Temple, who are joined by the Martyrs of St. Lazarus.

Since we didn’t go over them in our First Blood Faction Focus, let’s quickly discuss the new units, starting with the Character and then moving on to the three new Infantry options the regular Hundred Kingdoms also gain access to. Every other profile is the same as the regular First Blood army list we went over in our dedicated Hundred Kingdoms Faction Focus.

Order of the Sword Priory Commander

Conquest: Order of the Sword Priory Commander – Credit: Para Bellum Games

This new Infantry Priory Commander leads the footslogging knights of the Order of the Shield and is an absolute house in combat. For pure stats, his output is close to on par with a Dweghom Hold Raegh, with 3 Attacks at Clash 4 and Cleave 2.

Defensively he is also very tough to take down, owing to his Defense 3 and Resolve 4. 5 Wounds give him a sizable buffer, closer to most Brutes, and the fun doesn’t stop there. He has access to the Juggernaut special rule to help him get out of combats he doesn’t wanna be in, which he gets to use as a Draw Event.

And last but not least, before Rolling to Block, you get to Roll an additional dice and negate every Result with the same facing. This represents, as the rules are for some reason happy to point out, the Priory Commander catching a blow with his magic hand. Thanks Para Bellum!

It can miss sometimes, granted, but over the course of the game this will save you a lot of chip damage you would otherwise have taken and stacks the odds massively in your favor the longer the game goes.

Adding to his defensive prowess is the Counter-Attack Special Rule, which Drives Back Enemy Warriors that fail to cause him any Wounds during a Clash Action. It will be very hard to tie him down, indeed.

To finish, he has two Command Abilities:

  • Argent Tutelage provides Order Warriors in his 6” Command Range with Hardened.
  • Vehemence gives Order Warriors in Command Range Impact +1.

Short and to the point, you will almost always get to use these to decent effect, especially since the Priory Commander can use Juggernaut to Drive Back Enemy Warriors for more Impact Hits, even if it’s just him and a unit of his loyal knights on foot.

At 125 points he is not cheap, and the very tempting upgrades the Warbands of Legend have access to tempt you to deck him out further – especially so since he has the option to take two upgrades. I would therefore advise you to run him with at least one stocking stuffer of Militia for some bodies on the board.

Order of the Sword

Conquest: Order of the Sword – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Even more expensive are the Order of the Sword: 175 points for the buy-in with 55 added points per Warrior is not at all cheap and probably makes them a virtual Restricted slot in all but name, since you won’t have many points left over for mounted knights after buying a sizable unit of these guys.

What do you get for the price? Basically mini Priory Commanders: 2 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 2, Defense and Resolve 3, 2 Wounds(!), access to the Juggernaut Action, Counter-Attack and the same funny rule for blocking enemy hits with your magic hand.

So not much new here, but a lot to like. I would prefer these to be Restricted and the cheaper Martyrs to be Mainstay, just for congruency, but we’ll see in a bit why Para Bellum opted against that.

As is, these are basically much slower, much grindier Cavalry. They are not there to smash upon the anvil, they are there to be both hammer and anvil. Historically speaking, this has been a hard niche to write rules for, but from the looks of it, Para Bellum have pulled it off here. The staying power of 2 Wound Infantry Warriors should not be underestimated.

Penitents of St. Lazarus

Conquest: Penitents of St. Lazarus – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Speaking of anvils, we have the Penitents of St. Lazarus on the other side of the spectrum. Just one look at their Special Rules tells you what you’re in for: Dread to limit opponents’ damage potential, Insensate for +1 Defense until you activate them – a very fun rule –, as well as Pavise for an additional +1 Defense against shooting and Shield for Re-Rolls of “6” during Blocking.

The Penitents are, thematically appropriately enough, all about taking punishment. And they aren’t bad at it on stats alone. Defense 2 and Resolve 3 are above average for regular Infantry. And when they do activate, they are not terrible in combat with 1 Attack at Clash 2.

The only thing you can complain about with them at 110 points for four and 30 per additional model is their average mobility at March 5, but the additional Commands you will get out of your Army Rules will get them to where the fighting is thickest in no time.

Martyrs of St. Lazarus

Conquest: Martyrs of St. Lazarus – Credit: Para Bellum Games

And so we come to the Martyrs of St. Lazarus, which are basically Warhammer Fantasy Slayers: Angry men who want to die screaming in battle, just taller and less wide. Rules-wise this means that they are much killier and less durable.

They come in at 2 Attacks at Clash 2 and Cleave 1, with Bloodlust for extra Attacks while they are Outnumbered and Overwhelm to force Enemy Warriors to Re-Roll Hit Rolls of “1” during Clash Action. Lastly, Absolution has Enemy Warriors that Slay one of your Martyrs become Broken! for their trouble.

A nice little berserking package, but why aren’t these Mainstay again? At 125 points to start with 35 per additional model, these have all the makings of the medium-to-high-tier Infantry options we see in other armies.

My best guess is that Para Bellum wants Hundred Kingdoms players to make it harder to run both Penitents and Martyrs together, since both being Mainstay units would make it very easy to create lists that spam both.

That would be a lot of fairly resilient and or hurty fodder to chew through for most lists, and that is before we get to the Space Marine-tier knights chomping at the bits to ride and/or walk them down.

Hundred Kingdoms: Order of the Ashen Dawn – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Quick Review

Overall, there is a lot to like in the Might of the Orders Warband of Legend. It is a very cautious release, themed around the most straight-forward subfaction of the Hundred Kingdoms, with very little in the way of fancy rules – probably to not overwhelm the playerbase with the first of what is likely many upcoming Warbands of Legend.

It lays a lot of groundwork for people to become accustomed to the idea, but leaves a lot of potential by the wayside as a result – recycling the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rules almost whole-cloth.

That stings a bit, especially when you consider the potential that the concept brings with it, but you have got to consider how people might have reacted if Para Bellum came out with a super complex Warband of Legend that perhaps even ended up overtuned. Can you imagine?

So for the time being, a fun way for Orders enjoyers to play their models in a new way, if not particularly exciting in terms of listbuilding. The main point of interest for me – and many entrenched players, I would imagine – is to think what else Para Bellum might be cooking up.

Final Thoughts

And yeah, obviously the sky is the limit. Every army has access to at least three if not sometimes four distinct subfactions that could each get their own Warband of Legend. My money is on the first handful of releases to go to the older armies.

They have very fleshed out model ranges as-is, and Para Bellum seems to be aware of the danger of model bloat. Warband releases are a very smart way of shaking up older factions without just giving them new – and necessarily at least good, if not better – toys every time.

But then again, the first Warband release came alongside a very sizable expansion to the army with the largest model range, so what do I know?

As for what I would like to see next: Something a bit more daring and experimental, perhaps themed around a large centerpiece – kind of like how the Fallen Divinity or the Trebuchet warp the entire game around them.

An all-Jotnar Nords Warband of Legend or a Spires Warband themed around an especially powerful Siegebreaker Behemoth with a Biomancer to control him and an expanded repertoire of Command Abilities for that purpose. What would you like to see Para Bellum do next? Let us know in the comments!
