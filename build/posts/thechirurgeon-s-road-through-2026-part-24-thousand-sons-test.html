TheChirurgeon’s Road Through 2026, Part 24: Thousand Sons Test

June 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive progress through 2026. Last time around I played some games of 11th edition and talked about the Space Wolves I was painting. This week I'm running through another test game for 11th edition, with more rules to work with and reveal.

In particular, this week I can talk about Detachments and Force Dispositions in a way I couldn't last week... but I still can't talk about points.

I was originally planning to run Death Guard but I started thinking about the Thousand Sons Forgefiend and decided I wanted to try that instead. After looking at the new Detachments we reviewed in this week's Faction Packs review for the Thousand Sons, I decided to hastily cobble together a list for the faction using the new Ritual of Regeneration, which has two effects: The first is that it lets me revive Terminators and the second is that it gives me Purge the Foe, two things I really want to play with. I tragically can't combine it with Rubricae Phalanx, so I settle on Hexwarp Thrallband, which gives me rr1s to wound on psychic attacks and +1 to wound when I'm in my army's locus or whatever.

I have a lot of painted Terminators, so I cobbled together a list using those and some other stuff I have sitting around that looks something like this:

Hexwarp Thrallband

Ritual of Regeneration

Purge the Foe

Term Sorc + Empyric Onslaught

Term sorc + eruption of vitality (heal D3+3)

Scarabs x10

Scarabs x10

Rubrics x5

Rubrics x5

Rubrics x5

Forgefiend

Mutalith

Heldrake

Tzaangors

Inf Master

The idea here is that the two big Scarab bricks can do real damage and occupy the middle of the table, eating up space where they combine -1 BS from cover with -1 to be hit from suppression on the Forgefiend with -1 to wound from being Scarabs and the ability to heal models back to life every turn. The Rubrics are there for funsies and objective holding, and the Heldrake is to strip cover. The Mutalith I had painted. I think the Forgefiend is the real star here.

Test Game: vs. Andrew's Astra Militarum (Take and Hold)

Andrew is on Astra Militarum, running Combined Arms with three Dorns (one Commander), a Hellhound, two Chimeras, two Scout Sentinels, 40 Cadians, Gaunt's Ghosts, and some Kasrking. I'm a little worried when I see the list, because Dorns can be a real challenge for Scarabs, but the upside is that being AP-3 doesn't matter when you have a 4+ save, and that makes the cover bonus much better for me.

This is Purge the Foe vs. Take and Hold, or Unstoppable Force and Immovable Object. This is really good for Purge - The Take and Hold player has to hold the middle objective, and I score for killing him off it and then taking it, and I net more points each time I do. So that's pretty great.

Deployment is... interesting. Andrew has two sentinels which can scout 9" and them move 9" so they can see right into my Deployment zone, and his Chimeras scout as well. This means that Hidden, even with Go to Ground, won't be worth much on this mission. That said, he'd be sacrificing those units, so I stay back to force him to overcommit if he does and he holds back to avoid losing units.

This is one of the more interesting challenges with 11th - it's hard to be anywhere and not just be completely exposed. As a result, I find Infiltrators are really hard to use - they just cannot drop too far forward without being sacrificial units in a way I don't feel they were before. The only exception to this I've found tends to be on Search and Destroy maps, which are a bit more forgiving.

I'm Going First.

You know what? That's fine. I want to kill some shit. I move forward and get a bead on Andrew's Chimera, light it up with the Heldrake, Mutalith, and Forgefiend (while tagging one Dorn), and then use the Rubrics to kill everyone who gets out. That's a solid first turn, but it could have been better. Putting -1 on a Dorn means I can potentially get it to -2, which is the dream with this list.

Andrew then rolls out with his Dorns, kills my Rubrics, destroys my Mutalith, and kills my Forgefiend. I roll bad on the saves, but I wasn't likely to survive this one. I probably needed better positioning on the Mutalith to get it cover.

That doesn't matter much however, because on my turn, I drop the Scarabs.

I drop all 20 of them, and I even get to live the dream here of having one die to doubles on a Ritual, then reanimate immediately with Ritual of Regeneration, placing it 2" closer and getting a 6" charge. I drop AP-2 on one Dorn after I strip cover from it with the Heldrake, letting me get to AP-5 with my missile attacks and re-roll his on the other Dorn. I pick both up, plus another Chimera and a Scout Sentinel. I charge in with both units and get there, flooding the middle of the table.

That is more or less the game at this point. The left side Rubrics with the Infernal Master kill the entire 20man Cadian squad and the Scarabs in the middle pick up the final Dorn in my third turn. It's a hell of a bloodbath and swung really quickly - I wasn't sure how nasty the Terminators were going to be but holy shit they're good. Also PSYCHIC ignoring cover is just insanely powerful and something I want to build around more.

This was the deadliest game I've played yet and while it probably could have gone to round 4, it was absolutely over by end of 2. Andrew held pace OK on secondary through 2 with a double score on Outflank and Behind Enemy Lines, but his primary was shot after this. We talk out the rest and talk about what we could have done differently and how to play this. I don't know that there's a good version for him, but he needed to bait out the Terminators and find a way to drop one at a time. By having his Dorns out, I could just pick them both up. If I'd played the Heldrake a bit smarter I could have used it to finish the Chimera on my right and wiped everyone inside, but I wasted turns with it fighting Cadians for no reason.

Result: 100-35, Win

I really liked this Thousand Sons list, and I think it's worth refining and improving. After some thought, here's the next version I want to start testing, running a more serious list:

Thousand Sons

Hexwarp Thrallband

Ritual of Regeneration

Purge the Foe

Magnus

Term sorc + eruption of vitality

Inf Master

Tzaangor Shaman

10 Scarabs

Rubrics x5

Rubrics x5

Rubrics x5

Rhino

Forgefiend w/Hades

Forgefiend w/Hades

Forgefiend w/Hades

Tzaangors x10

After talking to TJ Lanigan a bit I think the second unit of Scarabs is overkill, and I'd get more value out of Magus, so I'm willing to try him and see how he feels. Taking him is about the same, though he saves me a few points. I also gain some points swapping out the Mutalith and Heldrake for two Forgefiends, and those things are cracked. That buys me a Rhino I can use for more infantry protection and move blocking.

This is a list I'm legitimately excited to try and I think it has real legs. Hexwarp isn't amazing but it isn't terrible, either and having regeneration on my units basically means channeling the warp is free most of the time. Expect to see me playing more of this one in the future.

Next Week: More 11th Edition

I don't have a ton of hobby progress this week, just the game and an assload of writing. So check back next week when I'm painting more Space Wolves and dusting off some unfinished Forgefiends I had planned to run in my Night Lords. They're already base coated blue and ready to rock, so I might as well put them to use.

See you next week.
