Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Deathtouch & Planeswalkers with Teyo, Diamondblade Mage

October 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

At time of writing, the least popular new Commander from Reality Fracture is sitting at a cool 2 decks. This is no surprise, as in every set released since around the time of Final Fantasy, there's Uncommon or even Common Legends which have abilities that rival core set chaff in their level of insipidity. No one would bat an eye to learn that Zog, Triceraton Castaway is the least-popular Commander from TMNT; things like this just come with the new normal of how Magic sets are designed. As of Reality Fracture, there have been more new Legendary Creatures printed in 2026 than non-Legendary ones, so a few stinkers are expected—why then, today, am I covering Teyo, Diamondblade Mage? It's not an especially glamorous title, but I'd mark him as the most interesting Commander to be the least-popular from a core set, following its release, at least in recent memory. Black Teyo does something remarkably unique, in providing any permanent with Deathtouch for a turn, so while it's understandable to balk at a mana value of 4, there's some really neat things to be done with this jaded geomancer! Let's get into the pitch.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Deathtouch-a-Touch-a-Touch-a-Touch Me

Teyo's a Flash 3/1 for 4 that upon entry provides a permanent you control with Deathtouch until the end of the turn, with two riders depending on the type that permanent is. If it's a Creature, it gets a +1/+1 counter, but more interesting is that Planeswalkers receive a Loyalty counter. The most common way you're adding Loyalty to Planeswalkers in Black is via Proliferate, so it's very cool to see another way of doing so in the Command Zone that isn't just Yawgmoth, Thran Physician (good as he is, and certainly a part of the 99). Instead of being a midrangey Creature strategy like Doc Yawg, Teyo's all about churning through tokens and wiping the board as often as possible. He's able to convert mass pings from any permanent source into kill spells, and while he of course dies in the process, playing a critical mass of Undying Evil-likes keeps him coming back again and again. Pestilence is probably the example you're thinking of, but that goes away if it sees an empty board, and we can't abide that—I much prefer Noxious Field, which inexplicably turns your Land into a pinger. You probably play both, but it's cool to see how many options exist! Withering Wisps is another banger, as it's quite literally just a Pestilence for 1 fewer mana, so long as you don't need to spend on it in a single turn beyond the number of your Snow-Covered Swamps. While this is of course a focus on Teyo, treat this piece as me begging more Black decks to play the Withering Wisps in its stead, especially if you intent to activate it over a turn cycle, and especially if you're a Monoblack deck. One last aside on this note, we can do something similar via Deathtouch countes and Nesting Grounds, which allows us to pass counters around to any permanent. Black already desires to play nonbasic Land tutors for Coffers+Urborg, so this as a third option to make our things that shouldn't have Deathtouch have it permanently is superb.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

So, Teyo turns these repeatable pingers into boardwipes, big whoop. You do of course hit face when these are activated, most of the time, but with Teyo being 4 mana he's not available at every stage in the game, doubly so if you lack mana for a way to recur him at Instant-speed. This leads us into our next category of card, being payoffs for pinging nonlethally. You may have heard You Are Already Dead called out as the cardname for a Demonic Consultation (my preferred current name is Leave), but here it's actually just a killspell that cantrips. It, alongside its sister card Mirrodin Avenged, are mechanisms by which you can turn the single ping from your Enchantments into proper kills without need of Teyo. There's more than that though, because the pool of 'dealt damage this turn'-matters cards is surprisingly deep, and underexplored in EDH. How about a one-sided boardwipe with Swift Demise? Flexible card draw with Faller's Faithful? Maybe you'd like to teach the table what Haunt does and run a rattlesnake effect like Orzhov Euthanist? Including these cards also makes chump blocking far more effective for both you and your opponents, as any measure of damage can prove fatal while you have mana open.

Liliana et al.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

You know what doesn't die to your Enchantment pingers, though? Planeswalkers. This permanent type is also conveniently mentioned on Teyo himself, as if to remind us, and it provides a proactive play if he's ever dying and re-entering without something worthwhile to give Deathtouch to. Alongside Way of the Necromancer—also in Reality Fracture—Teyo can push Planeswalkers to their ultimates ahead of schedule, while also as mentioned giving you access to consistent full-board wipes. He helps you take out threats, then speed up the clock on the things they'd threaten. Black does of course have Proliferate to help with this, but because a lot of it arrives via Creatures, we need to be careful about how deep the suite is given all these wipes. Of our options, Sorin Markov is likely the best candidate, with Liliana, Death's Majesty close behind: Sorin represents an endgame, especially with our capacity to use his -3 in consecutive turns, and Liliana of course just...gets back Teyo, recouping a Loyalty counter. We can even make a Jace token or two and The Elderspell them away to rush for certain abilities, or power up a Lili with Liliana's Scrounger over a turn cycle or two. Monoblack Superfriends hasn't really been an archetype yet, and Teyo solves a lot of the strategy's issues, albeit at an expensive mana cost.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The first time I heard about giving Deathtouch to a noncreature, it was maybe 2019, and someone was explaining their Sydri, Galvanic Genius deck, which quite enjoyed Caltrops. Sydri's no longer especially popular, especially with the bevy of new Esper options, but her legacy is retained in looking at Black Teyo. He broadens the options, at a similarly-inefficient cost, but the open-endedness has led to such a fun Scryfall deep dive that I'm unironically considering Desert. 'What are the weirdest ways we can deal damage repeatedly' makes for some fun deckbuilding, and I reckon you'd see cards in Teyo lists that appear nowhere else, were he to find a few dedicated pilots. There's essentially no information on this guy online, no hype, no real coverage; with a cool 2 decks on EDHREC at time of writing, the door is open for someone to make the Teyo list. I haven't committed to banging out a 99 just yet, hence his coverage being on The Puzzlebox and not a full Commander Focus, but dear reader if you're interested in that, let me know. I wager your pod or LGS could learn to fear an 'oops all boardwipes' deck that actually has a Planeswalker win condition. This isn't just another Child of Alara pile, it's potentially something groundbreaking. We'll see who has the Last Laugh.
