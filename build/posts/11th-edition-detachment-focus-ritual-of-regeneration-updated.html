11th Edition Detachment Focus: Ritual of Regeneration (Updated July 30, 2026)

July 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In our Detachment Focus series we take a deep dive into an army’s Detachments, covering what’s in them, how they play, and how they’ll fit into the broader meta and your games. In this Detachment Focus we’re looking at the Ritual of Regeneration Detachment for the Thousand Sons.

The Thousand Sons received three 1 DP Detachments with the 11th edition Faction Packs drop, each one focusing more or less on a different unit group between Tzaangors, Sekhetar Robots, and Rubrics. Of those, the Ritual of Regeneration is the one focusing on Rubrics, giving you a way to bring back dead models and extend the unnatural lifespans of your animated suits of power armor.

Changelog

  • 2026-07-30: Updated to reflect the new disposition and the July 2026 balance update
  • 2026-07-14: Initial Publication

Detachment Cost: 1 DP

Force Disposition: Take and Hold

The Ritual of Regeneration strongly benefits a Rubric- and Scarab-heavy strategy, giving you a way to recur valuable, durable units with strong ranged damage output. Being able to recur models can give you surprising longevity and durability, even on the incredibly lethal battlefields of 11th edition. The problem is that after the recent balance patch updates, there isn't a good Detachment for you to pair it with that also benefits those units, meaning that if you want to take a Rubric-heavy Detachment, you're better off just taking Grand Coven or Rubricae Phalanx.

Detachment Rule: Sorcerous Invigoration

When a friendly Thousand Sons Psyker unit (excluding a MONSTER unit) successfully manifests a Ritual, that unit heals D3 wounds. You can only do this once per turn for each of your units.

This is really good, particularly with the way healing works in 11th edition, letting you get back dead models. Rules like this are better on larger units of more expensive models, as those are both less likely to be destroyed in a single shooting or fight phase and also give you more value on healed wounds.

There are some really interesting quirks to this. The first is that you'll take damage from Channeling the Warp before you Regenerate the wounds, meaning that as long as you can soak the damage without dying completely, channeling the warp is free so long as you actually manifest. That's good insurance when you only have a five-model unit of Rubrics and losing a single warpflamer is a big deal.

Of course the second is that sometimes you want to lose a model - the timing here leads to a really tricky strategy, which is arriving via Deep Strike, losing a model to Channeling, and then getting one back with Regeneration closer to the opponent, creating things like 6" Deep Strike charges for your Scarab Occult Terminators. Doing this takes a bit of luck, but you can knowingly channel to push mortal wounds on your unit, and you get to make the channel decision after you've seen if you rolled doubles on the original 2D6.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Enhancements

  • Eruption of Vitality (35 points). Infantry/Mounted Psyker model only. The first time the model is destroyed, roll a D6 at the end of the phase. On a 2+ they stand back up as close to where they were as possible with 3 wounds left. This is a bit of a weird one. You don't often want solo characters in Thousand Sons these days but it has some solid value on a Terminator Sorcerer or Exalted Sorcerer; these are both models which can die, then get back up and use the Detachment rule to regenerate a dead model, getting a second PSYKER back in the process. 35 points is quite a bit to spend on this effect however, and you need to roll a 3 on your heal roll to make that work.
  • Curse of Life (20 points). Infantry/Mounted Psyker model only. When this model heals as a result of the Sorcerous Invigoration Detachment rule, add 3 to the number of wounds healed. As currently worded, this only heals the model with the Enhancement, making it kind of worthless and massively overcosted at 35 points. It honestly feels like a typo, as the characters who can take this Enhancement top out at 5 wounds and healing the model means you won't get spillover or get back dead models. It's a real dud and needed to be 10 points.

Stratagems

  • Relentless Rebirth (1 CP). Used in any phase, when an INFANTRY or MOUNTED PSYKER unit suffers a mortal wound. That unit gets a 5+ Feel No Pain against mortal wounds. Helpful for stopping mortal wound damage, and particularly good on Terminators, who otherwise will get completely melted by anything bypassing their saves and -1 to wound ability.
  • Mutagenic Magicks (1 CP). Used at the start of the Fight phase on an engaged PSYKER unit. You pick an enemy unit engaged with yours and roll 6D6. For each 4+, that enemy unit takes a mortal wound. This is a fun little Grenade effect you can throw out for some extra damage, which can be super helpful given how often your Terminators and Rubrics can feel anemic in combat. This one also works with Magnus, if you’re taking him.
  • Multitudinous Limbs (1 CP). Used in your Movement phase when an Infantry/Mounted Psyker unit makes an Advance or Fall Back move. That unit can still perform an action after the move. This can be situationally good, but I don’t actually believe Thousand Sons players will want to take an Action-heavy primary most of the time - half the value of Ritual is that it lets you take Purge and ignore actions altogether.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Playing This Detachment

As we mentioned earlier, this Detachment really wants big units of Scarabs and Rubrics. Scarabs really make the most of the regeneration rules here, letting you keep big, nasty units on the table for longer periods, holding territory. Unfortunately, you can't combine this Detachment with Rubricae Phalanx, and that means your options are incredibly limited, especially after the July 2026 update made Hexwarp Thrallband 3 DP.

This means that if you're taking this Detachment, it's likely either going to be with the other two 1 DP Detachments or with the Changehost of Deceit, which can be OK for large units of Rubrics, who will benefit from having a 4+ invulnerable save around daemons. That's not really how you want to run Changehost necessarily; those lists tend to run heavier on Flamers and Screamers with support from Scarabs and Lords of Change. 10-man models of Rubrics aren't really something you need when you're being incentivized to run daemons who can do a similar job but faster and cheaper. So the real value here remains in taking big units of Scarabs, who really like what Ritual can do for them and can benefit from the Sulphurous Veil Stratagem to pack an extra -1 to hit on top of cover to make for a unit that's -2 to be hit with -1 to be wounded and a 4+ invulnerable save, bringing back dead models every turn. Powerful stuff.

Strengths

  • Great support for big units of your more durable models.

Weaknesses

  • Doesn't do anything for Magnus or your monster/vehicle units.
  • Doesn't have a good combo Detachment

This should work just fine on Take and Hold, where the big units of Scarabs are the stars of your show. Kairos gives you access to the rare extra CP generation for Thousand Sons, and the Scarabs can sit on terrain and hold midtable while giving opponents -1 to hit using the Sulphurous Veil Stratagem. The Flamers give you some speed and extra firepower and you have a Lord of Change in the mix to help kill heavier infantry targets and focus down big threats. It's a bit of a monster mash list and in testing I'd experiment with the Lords of change and whether I wanted one, two, or zero in the list.

Final Thoughts

The Ritual of Regeneration is a fun Detachment to add to your army, giving you a lot of breathing room when it comes to smashing that "channel the warp" button on your units and ensuring you're pushing through ritual effects. It's also just a good add-on for terminator-heavy armies. It's a shame it doesn't really have a great combo detachment right now.
