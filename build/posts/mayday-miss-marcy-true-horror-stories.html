Mayday, Miss Marcy! True Horror Stories

October 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back dear readers once again to Mayday, Miss Marcy, our regular advice column in which your reader submitted questions are answered by me, with a dose of honesty and potential comedy (YMMV). This week our column falls exactly on the first of the month, and for October 1st, that means two things: It was my birthday yesterday, and we're now within the 31 day window in which Halloween and all things spooky and horrific are allowed before Christmas takes over the remaining 60 days of the year (At least in the United States).

Perhaps just in time to kick off October, I have 3 pretty horrific questions for this week. AS a note, I did reach out to all 3 of these askers to ensure they were okay with the questions and answers, and they respond positively! Why? Well, you might see, if you dare... (But as a non-joke, a few of these have some minor content warnings).

If you're curious to have your own (hopefully non-horrific) questions answered here, then please follow the instructions below:

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

And here was last week's pretty positive set of questions, perhaps as a good palette cleanser.

Parasocial Paralysis

Miss Marcy,

I'm writing to you on behalf of my gaming group, or, we are writing to you, but I'm the one who is sending you the message because I'm the patron and can use Gregbot. My gaming group has a... problem. I hesitate to call him a stalker, and we debated calling him a leech, but that feels dehumanizing. The problem is that when we go to our LGS to slam hams, there is a guy who will, almost like clockwork, show up and hang around our table. He doesn't play 40k, he doesn't know any of us beyond having figured out our names through osmosis, and we don't know or have ever interacted with him.

Now before anyone asks, yes the store is open to the public, so this guy is obviously welcome to be in the store and we don't have any way to control that ourselves. He also isn't... aggressive, or weird, or anything, but he really has begun bringing the mood of our group down because he has kind of attached himself to our group and acts like we are friends; I couldn't even say I know his name! We've talked to the owner of the LGS who has basically said that since he's not doing anything wrong, he has no reason to ban him, which felt kind of like a non-answer. What do we do?

Group Paranoia

Dear Group,

So, this question has some uncomfortable aspects that we need to probably address first. One, the owner of this LGS sucks, because it is their job to establish a safe and welcoming environment, and it sounds like he is brushing off your group's concerns. Frankly, even if the guy is not being "weird", he is being weird. Just because he's not doing something aggressive or concerning, his behavior as you describe it is obviously making you all uncomfortable; we don't need to invent hypotheticals, the main thing here is you asked the owner of a LGS to help with another patron (who apparently doesn't even play the game, so does he even spend money there?) and they said not my problem.

I would probably start looking for a new LGS, if I were you, but let's also address your, well, leech. Did you ever watch What We Do in the Shadows with Colin the "Energy Vampire"? Essentially, a vampire that feeds off of other people's "energy" by being exhausting and socially unbearable. Your group has attracted an energy vampire, for lack of any better way to refer to him. He shows up when you all are playing, hangs around, apparently tries to use and know your names, and just... exists. The fact that none of you have told him to kindly go the hell away shows either an immense level of patience or perhaps some form of social cowardice in that you feel bad for him, which is not helping either party.

You are a group of adults, I assume, and if this random other adult is making you uncomfortable, ask him to go away. If the LGS owner has abdicated helping you, you will need to probably just go "hey man, we don't really know you, can you go the hell away"? But, let's be potentially charitable. Have you all ever asked this guy if he wants to play? Because although I do think the behavior is a little weird and uncomfortable, my charitable side of reading this is that perhaps he is a shy and socially awkward person who wants to play but is afraid to ask, and just seems to hope that by being around, it will happen through osmosis. I think you could even ask if he wants to play, or learn to play, and if you find that isn't palatable or remains weird, you should tell him to buzz off, raise the problem with the LGS owner, and then I think start looking for somewhere to play.

A True Horror Story

CW: This question deals with tampered with food and drugging / spiking a drink.
Marcy,

I don't have an easy way to start this question. I was drugged at a gaming event held at my LGS, which only came to light because I was not the only person it happened to. My LGS had tried a 21+ gaming night, which allowed alcohol to be brought. I do not need the lecture about open containers, all I will say is anyone who thinks it can't happen quickly should know that what happened to me was, at my best guess, done in a few seconds in which my attention had been taken away. The brazen act is probably why they were caught, because they apparently didn't know which drink was actually mine, and also spiked another drink. "Thankfully" my friend was affected before I was, and we both cannot remember the night after a certain point, but others at the event called 911 when we apparently both started acting strange and one of us fell over. The person was caught, and that part is handled.

My question, really, is... what do I even do? How do I actually just go back to going to events? I wanted to play games because I am not really the type to go clubbing, I don't find bars and other places enticing or comfortable, and all the usual reasons one might have anxiety over this sort of thing happened... at my lgs. I can't even really stomach going back, despite a lot of promises for help, support, and even how the group/store acted, I just don't know how to go back. I realize this is a HEAVY question but... what do I do?

Struggling

Dear Struggling,

First, as a note to our readers, I did get in touch with Struggling before answering this question and provided some suggestions in those conversations, but I also asked if they wanted me to really publish this question, which they said they would like to in order to try and move on. 

My summary answer is:

Dear Struggling, I cannot even begin to imagine how horrific that is, and just the absolute violation of safety of yourself and others for what can only be assumed were far worse implications that did not come to be. That this person was so craven to recklessly attempt this and endangered multiple people is honestly vile, and I can't probably publish what I hope happens to them. 

I think I... do not actually know the answer to your question. 

On the one hand, I want to say that you should cut your loss and protect yourself, perhaps looking for a new place or a new shop and group. But the group is also who stepped in immediately to protect you and the other person, despite this other person's actions. I realize that the location can seem ruined, but the people at your store seem to be good people who did the right thing. I hesitate to try and prescribe an action because I think most people would struggle here, and I don't blame them! I do say that I would maybe talk to any friends you have who still go, or even the owner; it sounds like the store itself made a choice that was ruined and preyed on by a predator, but the store itself is not the problem. I urge you not to give up on your hobbies, though, even if it means moving on from this location, and I'm so sorry you had to go through any of this. 

Survivor's Guilt

CW: This question references suicide and death.
Dear Miss Marcy,

Back in February, my local gaming group was shocked by the sudden suicide of one of our regular members. This person was always friendly, polite, approachable, a pleasure to play against and with, all sorts of things. This probably sounds really stereotypical. As you might expect, no one could say anything but good things but then they were gone and our community has never really recovered. We've had a lot of members leave, lots of bouts of fighting and other things, and the only reason I've written you is that I actually ended up moving, and have just wanted to get a few thoughts out.

I don't really know what we, or me, were supposed to do. When it happened, the usual went around: people were sorry to hear, horrified, oh they were so nice, all of it. But none of us really knew this person, apparently. We didn't know what was going on, nor could we do anything about it. All sorts of pretty vile things were said to others in FB comments, people destroyed friendships, and it just all felt awful. Everything after the fact was handled so badly that most of us had no idea how to support anyone, if there was a funeral, anything.

I guess the thing I'm struggling with now is that, in my new home, I'm relucant to join a community again. I'm 34, I have a job and personal social circle, and I'm not exactly struggling for interaction. But when I think about joining a gaming group, I'm left thinking about what if it happens again? Do I just not make friends? Do I bother?

Surviving

Dear Surviving,

Your question is, tragically, relatable. The older I get, the more people I lose, and for many different reasons. Before this sounds like I am making it about myself, I want to simply say that unfortunately death becomes more familiar to people than others, and that as you get older, you start to get more acquainted with them.

It does not get easier.

Whatever the reasons for this person's choice, I hate that the scenario you described is also very... relatable. Death is huge, and it leaves a hole that a lot of people do not know how to manage. Some people prefer to simply fill it in, pretend the hole didn't exist, while others want to preserve the respect the hole; there's all sorts of healthy and unhealthy ways death is dealt with. For you, I suppose, it was managed by moving away, but it also sounds like that community was spiraling out due to what happened.

As to your actual question of what to do... I think you should do whatever makes you happy and comfortable. I think you are maybe asking me, a neutral, anonymous party, something you've already decided, which is "can I just give up this hobby". I think the answer is yes. You can give it up, because it is a hobby. But the problem is that your hobby isn't the actual issue, it is people's interactions and social networks. Any group you join after this can always have this problem arise; people die. It is a fact of life. Should that prevent you from making new friends? No. You shouldn't cut yourself off of things you like, of making new connections, solely to protect yourself from seeing the uglier side of what can happen when the inevitable occurs.

I urge you to find ways to interact with others; if giving up gaming and doing something else is how you can move on, do it, but don't give up on socializing solely for that reason. The games don't matter; the people do, and you're one of them. Isolating yourself and not engaging with things you like isn't going to help anyone, especially yourself. I hope you find some new people and places to do things you enjoy, and that you can move on.
