Tabletop Battles Goes to the London Grand Tournament! (Conquest: Last Argument of Kings)

September 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Each year the biggest Conquest event in the UK takes place at the London Grand Tournament, held at the end of September. It’s a fantastic event with people travelling all over the UK (and some even coming in from overseas!) to enjoy 5 games of Conquest. Players arrive with all ranges of experience and competitive aspirations, from brand new to very experienced.  If you can only make one Conquest event a year, we really recommend this one!

Unfortunately Para Bellum’s community team have been unable to join us this year, due to the event conflicting with the Conquest World Championships in Chios. Nevertheless, the UK Vanguard team (thanks Elliot!) is running the event with Para Bellum providing extra prize support.

As the LGT is often one of the biggest events in the Worlds season, it can often be decisive in determining who will represent our region next year, and many players will spend weeks or months preparing their list. Others will only just decide their faction in time for list submission! With a handful of us from Tabletop Battles attending, we’ll share some of our thoughts building up to the main event of the tournament year.

DanDaBossman, Deadly Trout and General Cross in theme for LGT 2025!

General Cross

With the small life change of parenthood this year, Conquest has been firmly on the backburner this year with my attendance only made possible a couple of weeks prior to the event. I’m happy to be able to attend once again, the people in the community are always the best reason to come along. I’ll once again be rocking my W’adrhun as neither of my other armies are assembled yet…

That being said, I can’t quite put down my inner competitive streak. Having two 4-1 and one 3-2 results, I’m hoping that I can still place well even with the lack of practice by faction experience and a little rust-removal with a few practice games. After taking 4th place last year with my W’adrhun “Warburton’s Wholemeal” list (so named for my four units of Warbred!), I don’t think I want to stray too far into more experimental ideas that I’ll be figuring out on the fly.

I’ve been torn between the classic Speakers list of Hunting Pack, Quatl and Thunder Riders, a well known competitive staple, or an updated version of the Warburtons Wholemeal list after the point changes last year. I’ve not had enough experience with Chieftain led Tribes to run it well, or had the time to try some of the newer infantry ideas out there using the Scions, despite both doing well in other events.

In the end I felt that the Speakers list would struggle into some of the more ranged-heavy lists like Sorcerer Kings, Weaver Courts and Dweghom so I’m going to run a revised version of my Scion of War list. Everything in the list hits hard and can stick around just a bit longer than expected thanks to the Tenacious and Hardened once-per-game supremacy. Now just need to see if I can get any more paint on this lot before the event…

Credit: GeneralCross

DanDaBossman

We’re men! Men in skirts. When we go to war we do so without any shirts. We’re men, manly meeeeeen! 

Yia su! It’s that time again that we City States leave our decadent metropolis to teach our peaceful ways by force. It’s your friendly neighbourhood Dandabossman, here to weave a tale of love and tragedy. With the biggest gaming event of the year comes the biggest decision to make, what to wear play. Last year was my first LGT with a 2-3 result, and also only a handful of games into the hobby. This year, with a string of 3-2 and 2-1’s under the belt, can we possibly come out with the mythic 4-1?

So, what’s the game plan? Why the list? Well…The most important and practical reason is that it's painted. I’ve been wargaming for about fifteen years. This is the first army that’s been fully painted. If I go down, I’m doing it in style! #SWAG. Last year’s list was what I had to hand from two starter boxes. My collection has grown since then, mostly on sprue (don’t judge me!), so we’re adding some new friends into the mix.

Thorakites were a staple last year due to being Light scoring regiments. That got removed and they dropped off my radar for a while in favor of less Lights, more Hoplites and maybe just a Selinoi unit. Recently I brought them back with a Polemarch each, Andromachos and an assortment of gubbins. In games recently, including Derby tournaments and the legendary Cows vs Dinosaurs, run by our own General Cross, they have performed quite well and have been scary. Five Cleave 3 Flurry attacks followed by the rest of the unit can put out a world of hurt. The Aristia and Alloys, coupled with Fluid formation, makes them very maneuverable.

Hephy is always a must for Cleave 3. He does a lot of the hephy lifting ;) [Editor: Ugh.]. Arguments can be made for Thyreans but I’ve found they are glass cannons and need to be used carefully. Hephy also gives a little bit of Interference which is always nice.

Hoplite blocks give that sweet, sweet Defence 4, and provided you’re not being flanked (Rule One of City States: don’t get flanked!) will be a solid road block to most enemy units. Unless they’re Varangians. Damn Varangians, they ruined Varangia!

New additions include the mighty Talos, for more Defence 4 goodness, Fearless and Resolve 4. Cleave 1 is ok in a pinch but his job is to plant his feet and brace for impact. Next is a single chariot for those infamous City States drive bys. Sadly I only have two and the other isn’t painted. Finally new for LGT is the Companion Cavalry. They were very useful in previous tournaments but usually needed an Ipparchos which is too expensive for what he offers, and they’re also more glass cannony than Thyreans which is saying something. 

Polemarch warlord means some flexibility in using his “I go first this round” supremacy and being able to choose between Polemarch Alpha and Polemarch Beta really helps with that flexibility. Aristarch is here to buff the Hoplites and being the slot slave to bring a chariot and Comp Cav.

Overall the list is a hammer and (D)anvil with strong defence blocks. Hopefully we can make our enemies take it in the rear flank, as is the Greek way.

Sam Isaacson (not Josh) 

The LGT is my personal highlight in the Conquest calendar. I've been to every one, and my performance is nothing if not consistent. 

I've entirely failed to make it to the podium so far, but have entirely succeeded in landing mid table. For someone who tends to lose virtually every game at our weekly get-togethers this is extreme overachievement for me! 

The reason it's always the highlight is the fact that those who turn up to participate, in my experience, are wonderful human beings who are determined to give everyone a brilliant weekend. I love it. 

My plan this year, therefore, is to take the opponent by surprise. Everyone there will have played Conquest a bunch and will have an answer for everything I can bring to the table. So I started thinking about what I could bring that they wouldn't have seen before…

Earlier in the year I experimented with an army list for The Spires that was only two regiments. My conclusion after running it was that two is probably too few, but it's far from being a gimmick. I've stretched myself to four regiments for the LGT.

Credit: Para Bellum - Sam is keeping his powder dry on this beauty of this monstrousity!

It's pretty much as it appears. Two blobs that are hard to remove, supported by a ranged unit that can see over the top of my drones and a monster that can crash in and take out something I don't like the look of. 

I don't think I'm going to win. But I do think I'm not going to come last! 26 stands of drones that heal back every turn is extremely annoying to face. 8 stands of Incarnate Sentinels with a Highborne in there will cause trouble for most units. 

Also, entirely coincidentally, I then have fewer cards to worry about, so that's always nice.

Deadlytrout - A freshwater fish with underwater weapons

I like bugs. 

Those of you may remember me from other bug related articles so this should come as no surprise. After a good year and a half of my beloved OD and ghosts I took to this new year with a pipe dream of being the bestest bug in Britain. Since then, I have taken only the Weavers to events and that’s not stopping now! Having braved the month of the first release and being the worst army in the game, to the lovely place we are in now has been a rollercoaster, but one I’ve enjoyed riding. I love the speedy and mobility of the faction and the variety of… who am I kidding, I just love the moths! The first purchase I made and a staple in my lists for the year they’ve been available is a nice big block of moths, ready to drop rocks (Pollen? A smaller moth?) onto unsuspecting heads! 

Hitting my second LGT I’m hoping my borderline obsession with the bugs gives me an edge this year. Coming almost perfectly midboard last year with OD and a 3-2 record I’m hoping to get the 4-1 this time around. I like my list, having a good mix of units rather than just spamming or two, but I feel this gives me options vs all opponents which I like. Also, I like variation and it looks pretty. The dragon is holding on to its place solely on how much effort went into painting and getting it to stand upright on its damn base!

I’m still a little concerned about facing other ranged heavy armies, or the magic dominance of SK as, despite the high defence and wound bugs, I have a lot of rather squishy units too, but only time will tell now. The addition of the Gallowglass has really helped vs armour, which was previously my biggest bug bear, but I do have to be very careful to ensure they deal the damage needed before they get focused down. 

For my season I went with spring, I find the extra speed very helpful the turns it pops, and I love the heal bloom effect that comes with it. Don’t be fooled by my winter snowy bases, that’s just the knock on effects from global warming. Yes, the tree-bug army player likes the environment, how shocking ;)

Credit: DeadlyTrout

The LGT

As you can see, we've got a whole range of armies coming in from the finely honed competitive lists, to whatever we happened to have painted, to whatever we dreamed darkly in the twilight hours! Not only does this show that the Conquest community isn't a cutthroat meta shark-tank, but that we're all very excited to play our favourite game in a whole bunch of different ways.

We're planning on doing a follow up article after the event talking about our experiences, which we hope also would be useful. If you're at the LGT this weekend, come by the Conquest event and say hello! You'll know who we are by the ridiculous costumes...
