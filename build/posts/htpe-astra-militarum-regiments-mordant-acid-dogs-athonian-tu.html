HTPE: Astra Militarum Regiments - Mordant Acid Dogs, Athonian Tunnel Rats, Savlar Chem Dogs, Penal Legion

August 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome, recruit, to a slightly different kind of painting article, full of the uncouth, the rough, and the criminal; loaded with the ill-mannered scrapings of Imperial Death Worlds and Prison Worlds alike, whether volunteers or pressed into service as punishment for crimes (real or not), it makes no difference -- the war machine demands bodies, and its wheels are greased with blood. This article will form the first part of a series detailing how to paint (and convert) the many regiments of the Astra Militarum, the army of the Imperium.

Note: This series is intended as a guide for beginner and intermediate painters and hobbyists to get models they can play with, and as such, the results achieved are commensurate with what a relatively inexperienced painter can achieve to a tabletop standard, rather than fancy display painting; you have been warned!

Mordant Acid Dogs

Mordant Acid Dog - Credit: Games Workshop

The Mordant Acid Dogs are one of my favourite regiments, hailing from a truly hellish mining world - Mordant Prime. The surface of the planet is a blasted, uninhabitable wasteland. Such a planet would usually be of no interest to the Imperium, were it not for the bioluminescent bacteria that colonise and excrete a very strong acid, which allows them to devour the veins of phosphorescent rock beneath the surface. The populace live in these bacteria-excavated subterranean tunnels, mining the bacteria to extract the prized acid, which is used in various industrial processes - incidentally, the word Mordant itself refers to a type of acid used in etching, so they’re kind of Acid Acid Dogs. Mordant is rife with violence; criminal gangs control most of the production (a bit like Necromunda), and as such, regiments raised from these rough conditions are characterised by their strength as tunnel fighters.

The conversion for this Mordant guardswoman was pretty easy; on a lot of their artwork, Mordant soldiers are depicted as being equipped mostly like other guard regiments, but with bare arms and a hard-bitten style. For that purpose, a pair of bare arms from the Necromunda Eschers box added to a Cadian Trooper were all that it took. These arms do look a bit small against the bulky flak jacket of the regular Cadian kit, but I think it works anyway. 

Loading...

I started with the fatigues, since they’re the largest and innermost part of the model, using Vallejo Game Color Charcoal as the base. I applied this with an airbrush, but you can paint it like an ordinary basecoat if you prefer. To begin the armour, I used Army Painter Warpaints Air Iron Wolf, which is perhaps an unusual choice as it’s an airbrush paint, but I really haven’t found a dark blue-grey (with a tiny hint of green) that I like quite as much as this for these blue-grey flake plates. 

The Mordant Acid Dogs don’t see much of the sun in their day-to-day, so along with a supply of Vitamin D tablets, I based the skin tone with Vallejo Game Color Skin Tone, this is already a really light skin from which to begin.

I basecoated the lasgun with some Pro Acryl Camo Green and filled in the leather boots and webbing with Pro Acryl Coal Black. The metallic blade and gun barrel were each painted with Pro Acryl Steel.

With all the basecoats taken care of, I applied Pro Acryl Black Wash to everything except the skin, which was carefully treated with Pro Acryl Flesh Wash instead.

Loading...

After the wash dried, I layered back over everything with the original basecoats, avoiding the places where the wash had shaded the recesses.  

I used edge highlights to boost the contrast, Army Painter Warpaints Air Wolf Grey for the armour plates, Vallejo Game Color Neutral Grey for the fatigues and leather, and careful highlights of Vallejo Game Elfic Flesh on the skin to really reinforce the pallid feeling of the skin. 

Because the Mordant Acid Dogs are such rough lads and lasses, I freehanded a couple of tattoos on the bare arms using various colours and a fine brush.

It’s quite a simple scheme, but I think it looks great. If you wanted to do helmetless heads, I think that Orlock or Escher heads would be a good fit for the rough-and-ready punky vibe they give.

Savlar Chem Dogs

Savlar Chem Dog - Credit: Games Workshop

An altogether different kind of dog to their Mordant counterparts, the Savlar Chem Dogs are the worst-of-the-worst. Imagine if the sweepings of the world’s most notorious maximum security prisons were formed into platoons and given access to guns. Savlar itself is one of the most horrendous penal worlds of the Imperium, being a prison that is also lawless and anarchic - prisoners are enslaved to work in the dirtiest kind of heavy industry, enduring terrible conditions as part of their sentence. There is one reprieve, however, from this lifetime of abject suffering: Inmates may join the Chem Dogs, the regiment drawn from the scum of Savlar, initially to fight as cannon fodder in the Armageddon Ork Wars.

The Chem Dogs stand apart from their fellow penal legionaries, both by the gravity of their crimes (including “being born on Savlar”) but also their methods of discipline; although they are overseen by harsh Commissars and off-world officers, the men and women of Savlar are motivated less by the exploding collars and commissariat bolt-pistols of the other penal legions and more by the fact that they’re allowed to loot just about anything they want, as long as it’s not alien, Chaos-tainted, or heretical. They’re also allowed to do drugs if they want, as long as they can still fight. Simple venal greed (and the threat of being returned to Savlar if they step out of line) keeps the Chem Dogs fighting.  

The base for converting any kind of Chem Dog is naturally using parts from the Genestealer Cultist Neophyte Hybrids kit, with any cult iconography carefully clipped or shaved away. For my Chem Dog, I copied a picture I saw in the 8th edition Astra Militarum Codex - the Torso and head are taken from the Genestealer Cult Neophyte Hybrids, while the legs and sword arm are drawn from the old Cadian Command Squad sprue; the Sergeant’s pistol arm comes from the Hippogriff AFV kit. This isn’t the most economical way to convert these fellows, though, since you only get one set of these legs in the whole Cadian Command box. If I were feeling particularly crafty, I would just use the Neophyte Hybrids legs and sculpt some ragged cloth skirts out of Green Stuff or Miliput. 

Loading...

I began painting my Chem Dog with Vallejo Game Color Parasite Brown. I sprayed it through an airbrush to save a bit of time, but you can of course simply paint it with a regular brush. 

For the armour and the genestealer chestpiece, I basecoated everything with Pro Acryl Dark Jade, while the skin was painted with Pro Acryl Olive Flesh, a really nice, very pallid skin-tone.

The boots and pouches were painted with Pro Acryl Brown Grey, while the trousers got some Pro Acryl Dark Umber.

Loading...

I painted the pistol, respirator mask, and various leather parts with Pro Acryl Coal Black. For the goggles, I used Ionic Blazing Orange, I wanted them to be really, really punchy, so the only option was the highest saturation orange I have. The barrel of the pistol, the respirator, and the sword had their metallic parts painted with Pro Acryl Steel, which is rapidly becoming one of my favourite metallic paints.

For the little spotlight on his shoulder, I went with Vallejo Game Color Scorpy Green. 

With the basecoats taken care of, I washed the leathers and coat with Pro Acryl Brown Wash, the skin with a little Pro Acryl Flesh Wash, and the armour with Pro Acryl Black Wash.  

Honestly, I could probably have left the leather coat like that - being grimy and horrible is very apposite for the Chem Dogs, after all; but I decided to layer back up with Parasite Brown, and highlight with a little Vallejo Game Color Bronze Brown. The boots were relayered with Brown Grey and given fine highlights of Pro Acryl Bright Brown Grey, while the trousers received highlights of Pro Acryl Light Umber. 

For the armour, simple fine highlights with Pro Acryl Jade and Pro Acryl Bright Jade completed the look I was going for. The skin was just layered back up with Pro Acryl Olive Flesh, and given some almost imperceptible highlights of Olive Skin mixed with Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White. 

When it came to highlighting the orange goggles, I mixed a bit of Vallejo Game Color Sunset Orange into the Ionic Blazing Orange and painted a specular highlight, topping it off with some Bold Titanium White.

This Chem Dog is ready to raise hell and steal everything he can carry!

Penal Legion

The Lex Imperialis is the code of laws governing how the Imperium operates, detailing all of the infractions and crimes one that may be accused of. Those convicted of crimes (real or invented) are sent to penal worlds - giant prisons spanning an entire planet (think Alien 3). From these colossal prisons, inmates are conscripted to fight in Penal Battalions in theatres the generals believe may benefit from a human wave approach to combat. Since assignment to a penal legion is a common sentence for crimes that don’t warrant immediate execution, they are a constant source of manpower for the Astra Militarum. Penal Legionaries are kept in line through the presence of a Battle Royale-style bomb collar placed around their necks, primed and ready to decapitate any legionary who gets the wrong idea about his ability to rebel against The Law.

There are innumerable Penal Legions throughout the worlds of the Imperium, so for our example I chose to paint one in a classic prison-jumpsuit style - there was no conversion work done here, he’s simply a Cadian trooper painted in a different scheme, though the modern Cadian troopers replete with pouches, backpacks, accessories etc. may be a little too well-equipped to really portray a Penal Legionary, in which case, the older Cadian kit is probably a better bet. If you want your Penal Legionaries to be really down on their luck, you could use the Traitor Guard kit with the Chaos iconography clipped/shaved off. 

Loading...
I began painting my penal legionary with Vallejo Game Color Hot Orange, sprayed all over the model with an airbrush. You could do this with a regular brush if you fancied, but just be aware that orange paint covers very badly, so either start from a white primer, or save yourself the hassle and buy a can of orange spraypaint. The folks at Colour Forge sell one with the very appropriate name of Convict Orange. Once the orange had an even and opaque coat, I shaded the jumpsuit with Army Painter Speedpaint Nuclear Sunrise. 

Loading...

With the orange jumpsuit nicely covered, I basecoated the shoulder armour and flak vest with some Vallejo Game Color Sombre Grey. For the leather boots and webbing, I used Vallejo Game Color Charred Brown, for the casing of the Lasgun, one coat of Pro Acryl Coal Black sufficed. 

I chose to have my Penal Legionary bare-headed and completely shaven - in lore and artwork they’re often depicted as having shaved heads with forehead tattoos. For his skin I decided to start from a base of Vallejo Game Color Barbarian Skin.

With basecoats finished, I used Pro Acryl Flesh Wash to shade the skin, and Pro Acryl Black Wash to shade the armour itself. The leather was shaded with a careful application of Army Painter Speedpaint Dark Wood. I layered back over the non-recessed parts with more Charred Brown before giving the leather a highlight of Vallejo Game Color Gorgon Brown. I edge highlighted the armour with Vallejo Game Color Steel Grey. After layering back over the wash with some Barbarian Skin, I highlighted it softly with Vallejo Game Color Elf Skin Tone and Skin Tone. 

Loading...

Athonian Tunnel Rats

Athonian Tunnel Rat- Credit: Games Workshop

Rounding out this look at some of the grubbiest of the Astra Militarum regiments, we come to the Athonian Tunnel Rats, a regiment that is almost certainly overshadowed by the green-brown shade paint that shares its name. The world of Athonos is, much like every other homeworld in the scrappy regiments, not a very nice place to live. It’s not quite a death world, but an urban hell where gangs constantly fight for control in sewers - a bit like Necromunda if the gangs were fighting one another in the presence of lots of poo and fatbergs.

This particular Tunnel Rat is a traitor guardsman with all the chaos iconography removed - but since the artwork has a distinctive cloth cape, I had a go at making one using a method I’ve seen Peachy use: Masking tape. That’s right, masking tape, made ragged-edged by tearing, then brushed with PVA glue like some bizarre papier-mache project. The cape has a bit more rigidity than normal masking tape once the PVA dries, and the texture is appropriate for rough cloth. I could probably have had a go at sculpting a cape from Green Stuff or Miliput (and I will do that for future regiments that have prominent capes, unless they’re already on the model), but for this guy I figured I’d give it a go; there’s nothing to lose if it doesn’t work, just peel it off. I also used some Miliput rolled thin to make some parchment hanging down from his waist.

Loading...

With the general modelling done, I primed him with Pro Acryl Black Brown.  I decided to try something a bit different this time, so I used Ionic Dry Paint Light Green to do a heavy drybrush, focusing on the upward-facing and raised areas, followed by a more selective drybrush of Ionic Dry Paint Pale Grey on the very sharpest edges and highest upward-facing surfaces. With the underpainting taken care of, I used Army Painter Speedpaint Slaughter Red to block out the deep red of the fatigues. With some Army Painter Speedpaint Desolate Brown and Satchel Brown, I marked out the furs, while using Pro Acryl Petroleum Brown to shade and define the colour of the parchment. The cloak got an appropriately named covering of Army Painter Speedpaint Mummified Grime. Black details like gloves, boots, and helmet were simply painted with Pro Acryl Coal Black. 

Loading...
When it came to metallics, I knew I wanted something quite dirty as a final result, but in order to get there, I began from a simple application of Pro Acryl Dark Silver for the silver metallic parts, and Scalecolor Decayed Metal for the flamethrower’s tank and zippo-lighter-shield-thing.

I shaded the metallics with Pro Acryl Brown Wash.

Loading...

Some of the Speedpaint shades dried too glossy, so I knocked back the shine with some ultra-matt varnish. With everything sufficiently shaded, I moved on to cleanup and highlighting, since I want this fellow to be quite grimy and mucky as befits his sewer-fighting origins. I didn’t go too hard on the cleanup, mostly just sorting out particularly egregious mishaps with Vallejo Game Color Gory Red and Dark Silver.  For fine highlights on the fatigues, I turned to Vallejo Game Color Bloody Red and Scarlet Blood.

Loading...

For the flamethrower and various armour panels, I highlighted using Pro Acryl Steel before thinning down some Pro Acryl Orange Oxide into a quasi-wash and letting it run into various recesses; this Tunnel Rat hasn’t taken particularly good care of his equipment.

For the bronze details on the flamethrower, I highlighted using Pro Acryl Copper before thinning down some Pro Acryl Green Oxide in the same manner as the rust, and applied it into the recesses, wiping the excess away with my finger. I cleaned up and defined the parchment and cloak with some Pro Acryl Ivory.

I highlighted the fur with an incredibly gentle drybrush of Pro Acryl Drab Brown. 

The helmet looked quite boring, so I changed the faceplate to Vallejo Game Color Sombre Grey with a coat of Pro Acryl 1-Step Dark Blue Black over the top, plus some Vallejo Game Color Dark Green - Angel Green - Scorpy Green lenses.
