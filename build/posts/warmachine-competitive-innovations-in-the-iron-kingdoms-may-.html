[Warmachine] Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms, May 2026

June 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our serialized feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

Welcome Warmachine fans, to our second Warmachine Monthly Meta Report here on Tabletop Battles!

In this monthly article series, we examine the state of the game each month by looking at what factions are being played, and the win rates between all of the factions. We also look at the results from a few of the biggest events from the past month. By looking over this data, we hope to see just what direction the metagame is going, see what factions or leaders are dominating the metagame, and analyze the current state of Warmachine’s balance.

All of the data and lists used for this article can be found on Longshanks.

May 2026 Statistics

Games Played

As we start, a quick review of the filters we use for the data pulled here. For this series, we are only looking at ranked tournament games and are excluding online only events. 

May was a pretty busy month for Warmachine, with 1575 games played at 79 events, dominated by two huge events: the Bokur Brawl and Lock and Load 2026. Let's break the player numbers down by faction. 

Note: All of the Legacy factions currently unavailable as a part of Warmachine 3D are grouped together in the “Legacy Armies” section, due to their lower number of games played per faction and lack of widespread availability.



A pretty even spread of factions here for the month, particularly between all of the Prime factions. Eight of these factions fall between a 6% to 10% meta spread, with only Old Umbrey (12%) and Necrofactorum (4%) landing outside of that range. Old Umbrey dominating the popularity numbers in May is little surprise, the faction boasts a strong combination of movement tricks and powerhouse melee punch that has propelled it comfortably to the top of the faction win rates (more on that later). Necrofactorum’s drop off in player numbers is a bit more surprising here. The faction has maintained a strong win rate despite being hit with some pretty heavy nerfs in January. This success is largely on the strength of Sepsira, as her trademark Mechanithall mayhem carries the faction with both the most games played a 57% win rate. Though some other casters are seeing success, like Nekane and Dekathus, there’s a steep drop off in both popularity and win rate in the remaining casters. Poor Mortenembra is in particularly dire straits for Cryx, with only 15 games played and an abysmal 27% win rate in May. 

Also, shout out to whichever sicko is out there playing Fane of Nyrro, putting the faction on the leaderboard with 3 games played. Go off, you vampire rock star. 

For non-Prime factions, Gyrmkin continues to be wildly more popular than any of the other Legacy factions, including the other Warmachine 3D options, with over double the games played compared to the next most popular Legacy faction, Convergence. I’d venture to say this is thanks to the strength of the Slaughterhouse, which has propelled the faction into a competitive terror with the double Slaughterhouse Housing Crisis list. It does look like this has been noticed by the SFG team at least, as the Slaughterhouse was one of the first units confirmed to be getting adjusted in the June Balance update. Depending on how that potential nerf goes, we could see a drop in Gyrmkin popularity over the next few months.

Faction Win Rates    

Note: Only Legacy factions with over 30 games played are included in the win rates chart here. This represents about 10 players at 3 round events, which feels like a decent breakpoint for data given the total population of the current Warmachine playerbase attending tournaments.



Pretty similar to what we saw here in April 2026, with Dark Operations, Old Umbrey, and Gyrmkin dominating the top spots at 55%+ win rates. While Dark Operations balance does need to be addressed, the huge player population of Old Umbrey while rocking a 58% win rate makes it the stand out problem child in the metagame. Considering that this is an average of all games played, even weaker players seem to be at least doing okay with Old Umbrey while strong players are wreaking havoc. Two Old Umbrey casters were mentioned as getting touched in the upcoming balance update, Lesnoi and Kovosky, and it does look like these two are problem children for the faction, rocking a 61% and 60% win rate respectively. Even so, as of this writing it looks like only those two models are getting touched in the update, which makes me wonder if the update will be enough to dethrone Old Umbrey from its position as top dog of Warmachine. Morozov has consistently seen above average performance, meaning his lists leaning hard into shape shifter models will likely stay very strong, and the Primeval heavy warbeast still hits so wildly hard that it's not unreasonable to think it can likely still pick up the slack for Lesnoi and Kovosky if they get hit in the jaw with nasty nerfs.


Credit: Steamforged Games

Now that more of the Kithguard range is widely available, it looks like players are finding the tools to make the faction sing, jumping from a 41% WR in April to 54% this month. Stormcraw stands head and shoulder above the other leaders in the faction here in both popularity and WR, rocking 72 games played and a staggering 67% WR. Stormcraw’s got an excellent suite of spells and abilities, with spells like Deceleration and Positive Charge letting his army tank gunlines and deliver a nasty punch in protracted slogs, while his feat opens up some wild assassination vectors to catch players off guard. As Kithguard’s range continues to grow and become more available, we will need to keep an eye on Stormcraw here as a potential meta menace.

While the chart otherwise looks pretty similar to April’s data, there are still a few surprises here. Both Infernals and AWR saw steep drop offs in their WR from month to month, which is a particular shock for Infernals, as they’ve dominated the charts for much of the year. On the other end of the spectrum, Gravediggers went from having a very poor showing in April, holding the title of worst Prime faction outside Kithguard, into a respectable 48% win rate for the month. Some of these wild swings may be due to the Bokur Brawl teams event, which gives players more flexibility in their matchup selection, and could potentially let Gravediggers dodge some of their worst matchups, but who knows? Maybe April was a fluke, and for Gravediggers fans I hope it was. It’s always better to see a faction do at least average, especially one of the Prime factions, rather than struggle to keep up.     

May 2026 Event Breakdown

We’ve got two events to look at today from May: The Bokur Brawl and the Lock and Load EU Iron Gauntlet 2026. While L&L and Mayhem will be similar to how we usually break down events here, Bokur Brawl is a teams event, so we’ll examine it differently than the rest.

Bokur Brawl 2026

A massive teams event hosted in New Jersey, with 31 five-man teams (155 players total) competing over five rounds between May 2nd-3rd.

Normally, for an event like this, we would highlight the lists of each player in the top cut and examine what makes them tick, and potentially even go over the final table showdown at the event. The teams nature of this event makes this a little different though, since we can’t really examine just one player but in fact need to look at a whole team. So, we’re going to do things a little different for the Bokur Brawl. First, we are going to take a look at the faction breakdown for the teams in the top 8 cut, and then we will highlight the team composition for the winning team, F*ck Mammoth. 

First, the team composition. Unlike 40k, where every player on a team must have a unique faction, players can share the same faction on a team, but there can be no overlap in casters. Even so, only two of the top 8 teams elected to double up on faction selection, with fourth place bringing two Kallyss players and eighth place rocking two Old Umbrey players.


Yeah, uh, Old Umbrey is pretty good, huh?

Out of the top 8 teams at Bokur Brawl, only one team elected to not include an Old Umbrey player on their roster, and even then we still ended up with double the number of players compared to the next most popular faction choice because one team picked Umbrey twice. With the Umbrey representation so skewed here, it's pretty clear that some of the best players in the world feel that in order to win a teams event, you need to take Old Umbrey on your team, and the faction is very much overperforming.

Outside of this, the faction selection is pretty evenly spread out, and every Prime faction saw at least one player in the top cut. Necrofactorum and Winter Korp both only saw one selection each though, probably meaning their inclusion on the team roster was because those players are faction experts or diehards, rather than included for optimal team composition. Convergence, Infernals, and Dark Operations also only saw one selection each, though their status as Legacy armies with smaller populations makes their low representation less alarming than Winter Korp or Necrofactorum.

Man, talk about a real murderers row of both players and casters in the current Warmachine competitive circuit. Park, Van Meter, and Owns are all three in the top five global leaderboard for this season on Longshanks, and Posner and Owens aren’t far behind.They’ve all brought some mean lists to boot, and a composition that gives them plenty of flexibility in matchup selection. 

Old Umbrey is no surprise here, as we’ve established, but rather than go with Lesnoi and Kovonsky for caster selection, Posner eschews Kovosky for a Lissya list heavy on Warbeasts, leaning into a cagier ranged-focused playstyle than what Old Umbrey typically does. Gravediggers is, unsurprisingly, the Caine gun show, one of the nastiest gunlines in the game, with a Jack heavy Hasker list as a fallback option in case of an attrition heavy slog.


Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Van Meter and Park bring scenario strength to the team line up, with Dark Operations having a wealth of tools to both manipulate enemy models and swarm scenario elements with hard to shift bodies, and Lylth just having an outstanding toolkit for dominating a player at range before dropping feat and pushing players out from scoring. Finally, Owens brings to bear Sea Raiders ability to get down in the dirt and duke it out in a battle of attrition, with The Butcher letting him melee damage output into the stratosphere and Oriax offering up a suite of great offensive spells, debuffs, and dice fixing thanks to Star Crossed. 

Overall, an incredible performance from F*ck Mammoth here, only dropping 3 of their 25 games over the weekend.

The top table at the L&L Iron Gauntlet was both a surprise and no surprise at all. On one end of the area, we have Refyougee piloting the infamous double Slaughterhouse build, and it’s nasty for good reason. Both Slaughterhouses can use their Porch Light to drag models all the way across the table from relative safety before ripping them apart with high quality attacks. If anyone starts getting too close, Gyrmkin can fall back on tools like Labyrinth to hinder enemy movement, and Refyougee has melee counterpunch capabilities with both the Cage Rager and even The Child herself in a pinch. Its difficult to find a way to be both active on scenario without putting your most important models in danger against this list.

Meanwhile, Sei’s Orgoth is a bit of a surprise here. Sea Raiders are by no means bad, with a solid win rate overall, but their preferred ground and pound style of gameplay hasn’t warped the meta nearly as much as the Housing Crisis. Sei went with The Butcher for this matchup, with a list heavy on Tyrants to maximise the damage buffs available to The Butcher, and notably in this matchup plenty of Shield Guards to take the hit on Porch Light shots. Even so, Sei’s the underdog here, as Refyougee’s double Slaughterhouse list can afford to wait Sei out if he tries to hang back and avoid getting shot.

Refyougee leans on this hard in the early part of their game, forcing Sei to sacrifice his Shield Guards to keep The Butcher alive and managing to bring down several of his Tyrants early. The tide starts to shift for Sei starting on turn 3 though, when he feats with The Butcher and piles on both Redline and Hand of Destruction onto his sole remaining Tyrant, turning it into a cruise missile that brings down one of the Slaughterhouses in one activation. From there, he’s able to play more aggressively and send out his forces to grab scenario elements on the bottom half of the board, eventually managing to come out on top on turn 5.

The Tyrant is one of the most competitively priced Warjacks in the game, and David leans on it for both of his lists here. With access to Relentless charge, a POW 18 flail, and a POW 15 open fist on a tough chassis at just 11 points, the Tyrant is a workhorse model for Sea Raiders. With The Butcher, he can use his feat and spells to turn them into wrecking balls that will fly across the table and blend whatever they touch. With Horruskh, he can buff the Tyrant to ARM 21 under feat and make it immune to knockdown as well as boosting the Tyrant’s accuracy with Carnage, making them a little less dangerous than they may be with The Butcher but still plenty tough. With the Horruskh list, Sei supplements the wall of metal with even more beef, taking along a Siege Tarask and Anathia, while The Butcher list adds can lean on Reavers and two units of Rhok Harriers to clear screens and act as fast moving skirmishers that can still punch above their weight on Butcher’s feat turn. There’s not a bunch of tricks with either of these lists, they’re fairly honest melee focused attrition lists, but boy are they fantastic at it. Great job on the win, David Sei.
