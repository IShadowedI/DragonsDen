How to Paint Everything: Armageddon Box Space Marines

June 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer
I chose to paint my Armageddon Marines as Crimson Fists; since they’re fighting Orks it seemed apposite to use the colours of a Chapter famous for their conflicts with the Greenskin threat. Plus, I thought they’d probably be quite quick compared to other chapters since their scheme is so simple (I wasn’t entirely right on that part, but more on that later).

Crimson Fists Vanguard Veterans - Credit: Keewa

I do most of the groundwork for my schemes with an airbrush, both because I like the easy way of creating smooth gradients and the huge boost to speed and efficiency it gives me (doing this kind of speedy batch painting isn’t really possible otherwise), but if you don’t have access to one (or a desire to use one) you can achieve a similar result using the same colours with a standard brush.



I started by basecoating the entire model (and all of the marines at once, big batches!) with Vallejo Game Color Night Blue, this is the perfect base tone for Crimson Fists, it’s both very dark and decently saturated. I thinned it down with ProAcryl Glaze & Wash Medium and water to allow it to flow smoothly through the airbrush without clogging.


Getting paint all over my hands is a recurring theme here.

Once the Night Blue basecoat had a solid coverage, I used ProAcryl Blue, thinned down the same way as with the Night Blue. As you can see from the photo, I mostly sprayed this from above and slightly to the side - the ProAcryl Blue is intensely saturated and very bold, which I love.



After the regular ProAcryl Blue, I very very sparingly sprayed the uppermost parts of the armour with ProAcryl Sky Blue - it’s quite a subtle highlight, but it really adds a lot of punch.




 

To blend the blue together, I glazed the transitions with ProAcryl Transparent Blue, this ink boosts the saturation massively while tying everything together and smoothing everything into a blended whole. It also has a slightly purplish cast which plays nicely with the Crimson Fist scheme. 



I basecoated the red of the gauntlets and the detail on the shield with ProAcryl Black Red, it’s very dark and a little satin-y, but this is just the foundation upon which to build the rest of the red.



I painted the silver details with Vallejo True Metalliic Metal Sterling Silver Base, the silver metallics are a really important part of the Crimson Fist scheme, so it was important to get that right.



The bronze/gold details I basecoated with ProAcryl Bronze, it was important to me that the gold metallic details should not be too bright or saturated, if I’d gone with a bright yellow gold I feel like it would have been too distracting and drawn too much focus away from the important colours.


The leather parts are basecoated with ProAcryl Dark Flesh, while the purity seal wax is ProAcryl Burgundy.


I painted the pteruges with ProAcryl Burnt Red and then layered up the gauntlets and shield detail with more Burnt Red.



Next I painted the parchment of the Purity Seals with Reaper Skeleton Bone, it needed at least three coats to cover, but when it does it’s nice and sturdy. I painted the non-silver metallic bits with ProAcryl Steel, it’s slightly darker and more blue, which helps to distinguish between the two kinds of metal. This includes the pieces at the end of the pteruges. The cloak was basecoated with Vallejo Game Color Twilight Purple.



Now was a good time to throw some decals on the model, and oh boy let me tell you those circular Crimson Fists decals do not want to sit flat on the complicated 3D shape of the Space Marine pauldron, even with Microsol and Microset, these things were a real pain in the Squiggoth. 



Following the decals, it’s time to get messy. When I’m speedpainting a batch of things in the run-up to a big release I tend to lean on oil washes for their quickness and general versatility. To start, I cover the whole model in a coat of gloss varnish, you don’t necessarily need to do this (and on the orks, I don’t) but when I’m painting something like Space Marines that tend to be quite clean, I prefer the surface to be as smooth and non-grabby as possible to reduce unnecessary staining. 



I make a wash of some Schmincke Norma Elfenbein Schwarz/Ivory Black thinned down with mineral spirits (turpentine ersatz). Getting the consistency right is a bit of a balancing act, you want enough thinner that the paint will flow, but not so much that it’ll simply run straight off the model - this is considerably trickier when working over a gloss varnished surface.

I like to wear a respirator mask and open the windows when using mineral spirits/white spirits/turpentine ersatz because the vapours make me dizzy, and it’s generally unpleasant.



Once the mineral spirits have dried away (they’re a solvent so it doesn’t take too long for them to evaporate into the air), I get some makeup sponges of various sizes, plus some cotton buds, and begin the task of removing the oil wash from the model. By gently rubbing the surfaces, the oil wash is spread around and removed from the non-recessed parts. Oil paints work in a different way to acrylic paint, their drying time is very long, giving you plenty of time to make adjustments and refine what you’re doing. They also function slightly differently - it’s a bit like spreading butter instead of covering a surface with water, you glob it on and then spread it out, whereas with acrylics, you always leave the most paint at the point where the brush ends a stroke. 



With the oil wash sufficiently removed from the details I want to keep light, I finished the process by drying it with a hairdryer on a high heat to burn away the last of the solvent, before sealing the work with a layer of Vallejo Matt Varnish, administered through the airbrush.


 

All that was left was the part I enjoy the most, adding details with edge highlights and further developing the contrast. I painted the braid clasping the cloak with some Vallejo Game Color Warlord Purple, and highlighted it with Vallejo Game Color Squid Pink. For the armour, I edge-highlighted, beginning with ProAcryl Blue, then developed the highlights further with ProAcryl Sky Blue and ProAcryl White Blue. 

For the fists, I highlighted first with ProAcryl Bold Pyrrole Red followed by Bold Pyrrole Red mixed with some ProAcryl Dark Warm Flesh, finishing with some corner highlights using only the Dark Warm Flesh. I glazed the fists with a little Army Painter Speedpaint Bloodmoon Red to tie everything together.



I highlighted the silver details with Vallejo True Metallic Metal Sterling Silver Light, and the steel details with Vallejo Model Air Steel. For the bronze details, I went back over with ProAcryl Bronze and edge-highlighted with some ProAcryl Light Bronze.

The power sword was another airbrush job, after carefully masking off the rest of the model to ensure no overspray, I sprayed the entire blade with Vallejo Game Color Abyssal Turquoise, followed by progressively smaller parts of the blade with Turquoise, Aquamarine, and a very small spot of Verdigris on the tip.  

For the wax of the purity seals, I just went back over the raised edges with ProAcryl Burgundy, for the paper I did the same with Reaper Skeleton Bone, very simple!

The leather is highlighted with Dark Flesh mixed with ProAcryl Tan Flesh. 

For the olive skin-tone of the bareheaded Crimson Fists (which I painted separately), I basecoated with ProAcryl Dark Flesh, with a zenithal highlight of ProAcryl Advanced Flesh Tone, further highlighted with a bit of Dark Warm Flesh on the upward-facing parts of the face, then finally with a very fine highlight of ProAcryl Olive Skin. In the shadows, I glazed a touch of Army Painter Speedpaint Brownish Decay. 



For the various gems around the models, the screens of the speeder, and the lenses of the space marine helmets, I settled on a nice green colour, starting with ProAcryl Black Green, before building up a highlight in the bottom corner with Vallejo Game Color Scorpy Green and Bile Green. I finished off with a bright reflection of ProAcryl Bold Titanium White.

For the wasteland bases for Armageddon, I painted everything with Vallejo Game Color Earth, before highlighting with drybrushes of Desert Yellow, and Khaki. As a final highlight I drybrushed the sharpest parts with ProAcryl Khaki.
