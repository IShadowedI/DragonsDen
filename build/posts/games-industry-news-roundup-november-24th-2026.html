Games Industry News Roundup- November 24th, 2026

November 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Steamforged Releases Long Overdue Financial Report for 2025

Credit: Steamforged Games

We’re starting things off this week with a particularly eyebrow raising story from Steamforged Games (SFG), as the company has posted their long overdue 2025 fiscal year financial report, revealing that the company posted an over £8.6 million loss for the period.

Steamforged got in some hot water already earlier this year when they missed the deadline for sharing these filings, causing the British crown to give the company a compulsory strike-off warning in early September. The warning stated that unless said financial records were posted soon, the company would be dissolved and its assets seized by the government. Steamforged reported that the lateness of the report was due to the layoffs and restructuring the company went through earlier in the year, and was able to provide the government with enough of an explanation for the Crown to call off the compulsory strike-off a few days after posting it.

On Monday, finally Steamforged published the report, and the information inside paints a grim picture for SFG. According to the report, the company posted an eye-watering  £8.6 million (around $11.2 million) loss for the year ending in March 31, 2025. Much of this came from a sharp decline in crowdfunding activity for SFG, which the company notes in the report that it plans to discontinue going forward in favor of focusing on Warmachine products.

Independent auditors stated in the report that SFG’s financial situation was unsustainable, and questioned the valuation of the IP presented by SFG. This led to the auditors to give SFG a “disclaimer of opinion” on its accounts, which means that the auditors could not find sufficient evidence to prove or disprove the presented IP and trading value of the company based on the information provided.

While Warmachine has been a strong sales line for the company, it’s unknown if those sales numbers are strong enough to drive the company out of this hole, though SFG does note in the report that it’s also working with potential outside investors for additional cash flow. SFG’s financial problems only compound its problems with outstanding crowdfunding campaigns that haven’t been fulfilled, totalling at around $19 million. One of the companies that partnered with Steamforged on these campaigns, Feuerland Spiele, spoke out against SFG on Monday, criticising the companies actions and questioning if the project they helped create, the Terra Mystica special edition, would ever see the light of day.

“Unfortunately, we share the concern of some backers that the Terra Mystica Special Edition will never see the light of day,” Feuerland wrote. “We put our reputation in other hands and while Steamforged alone collected the funding, this situation is already damaging our brand.”

At time of writing, SFG has made no official comment on the report.

Credit: Firaxis/Take Two

XCOM Developer Firaxis Cuts Third of Workforce

Heartbreaking news for the team at Firaxis, as it was announced suddenly last week that the company had cut around a third of its workforce, including the studio head Heather Hazen, as development on a new X-COM title stalled. 

“Firaxis is restructuring and recentering around the studio’s core strength — designing world class strategy games that have set the industry standard for the genre for the last 30 years,” a spokesperson for Firaxis’s parent company, Take Two Interactive, said in a statement. “As part of these changes, the size of the studio is being reduced to focus on design, technology and the player experience, which means saying goodbye to talented colleagues who helped build Firaxis. These decisions are never easy, but they’re essential to build the next chapter of Firaxis.”

According to Jason Schrier, who broke the story, this news comes after the company failed to hit development milestones set for an unannounced new X-COM project over the summer, and while the game is still in development as far as we know, it now faces a “significant shakeup” with the large scale departure of staff.

Hazen has been in charge of Firaxis since 2023, where she was promoted to the role by Take Two from her previous position as COO, replacing the previous studio head, Steve Martin, who had worked in the role for over two decades. Take Two has not announced publicly who will be taking over Hazen’s role following her departure.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

D&D Beyond and Secret Lair Targeted with Malware Attack

Dungeons and Dragons and Magic: The Gathering fans had a bit of a scare earlier this month as news broke that the official digital tabletop service for the game, D&D Beyond, as well as the digital storefront for Secret Lair, were some of the several hundred websites that was targeted by a massive malware attack last week.

The attack, which included other notable websites like Zoom, CNN, Salesforce, and BBC, was aimed at stealing user credit card data from affected websites, originating from the AI marketing platform Optimizely. The company released a statement acknowledging that the service had been targeted for a malware attack on November 16th, and was working diligently to determine the origin of the attack and how to improve security against future attacks.

D&D Beyond’s executive producer Brian Perry assured fans that their information was safe, despite the web service being one of the many targets of the attack, writing on Reddit that “Earlier this week, a third-party service provider experienced a security incident that affected a number of websites, including D&D Beyond. We acted quickly to remove the service from our site, and our team has been reviewing the situation carefully since.”

“Payments on D&D Beyond are handled through our secure payment provider and were not affected. If our review finds that any player needs to take action, we'll contact them directly.”

As for Secret Lair, Wargamer.com reached out to Wizards of the Coast regarding an update, and were informed by a representative that "our review of the Secret Lair site is complete and confirmed that no customer information was affected.”

Credit: Rockstar Games/Take Two Interactive

GTA 6 Exceeds $3.5 Billion in First Day Sales

After thirteen years of probably extremely expensive development, a massive marketing campaign, and an extremely volatile video game industry that often punishes even successful releases, it was easy to be nervous about the release of the new Grand Theft Auto game. With the November 19th release date looming after several rounds of delays, even with tons of hype and preorders, GTA VI would almost certainly need to make a simply stupid amount of money right out the gate for the game to not be considered a success.

Fortunately for the team at Rockstar Games, GTA VI did make an absolutely stupid amount of money right out of the gate.

According to reporting from Variety, GTA VI brought in $3.79 billion in first day sales, selling over 32.5 million units in pre-orders alone, making the game not just the highest growing video game of all time by a country mile, but the single largest entertainment media launch in history.

For reference, Avengers: Endgame, the highest grossing film of all time at time of writing, made around $2.9 billion during its entire theatrical run. Two of the largest movies of this year, Spider Man: Brand New Day and The Odyssey, both come up short of GTA VI’s first day sales figures by over a billion dollars, bringing in $2.52 billion and $1.8 billion respectively.

Previous sales estimates by third party service Sensor Tower placed sales estimates for GTA VI at around $2.36 billion, and analysts told Gamesindustry.biz that they expected GTA VI to move between 30-35 million units in its first year, not its first day.

“It is with immense gratitude that we enter a new chapter of Rockstar Games with the release of Grand Theft Auto VI,” Sam Houser, President and Founder of Rockstar Games, said in a press release. “We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world and we couldn’t be more excited to share this moment with you.”

Credit: Archon Studios/Blizzard Entertainment

New Two Player Starter Announced for Next Wave of StarCraft: TMG Releases

Exciting news last week in the Korpulu sector of the tabletop gaming world, as Archon Studios unveiled its next wave of StarCraft: The Miniatures Game releases, including an official first look at a new two player starter box releasing for the game.

While the Terran do get some love in this wave with the release of Firebats, the real stars of the show are the Zerg and the Protoss, who are featured in the new two player starter. The box set, titled “Purification”, pits two unique subfactions of the Zerg and Protoss against one another. The Zerg are led by Infested Zuhkov, whose forces focus on new Infested Terran units such as a new Infested Marine and Infested Missile Tower kit, alongside an Infested Goliath using an upgrade sprue for the existing Goliath model. Facing off against the swarm is Alarak and the ruthless Tal’Darim Protoss, bringing along with him the largest miniature yet for the Protoss line, the Reaver.

Archon also released a new look at what's coming up for StarCraft: TMG, giving players a first look at the planned wave nine releases in Q3 2027. Most notably, this wave includes a new hero for each faction, such as Samir Duran for the Terran, Abathur for the Zerg, and Fenix for the Protoss, promising to shake up listbuilding and the metagame once released.

Wave 4 of StarCraft: TMG is available now for pre-order on the Archon website, with the shipping scheduled to start for orders on December 7th.
