Tabletop Battles Reviews: The General's Handbook 2026-2027

June 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer
We’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of this Genera's Handbook. 

After a long year we're leaving Ghyran behind us and heading to the other half of the Dawnbringer Crusade: Aqshy, the Realm of Fire. Ghyran has been more than a bit divisive, the Obscuring rule was considered a misstep as it felt kind of gamey and just bad, especially for ranged armies. The missions were fine, but the lack of Battletomes going in made the general feel very dry.

We're leaving this behind for a brand new season which will hopefully be a bit more exciting to play. What does this new season bring us?

Season Rules

Fury Level

Games Workshop has basically revealed all the core season rules and it goes down like this: Each Round you generate a Fury Level which converts to Rage Dice which can be converted to use certain abilities. Your Fury Level sticks around from round to round, but the Rage Dice disappear at the end of the round. So there's no reason not to convert to Rage Dice every round, and try and use them all by the end.

But how do you generate Fury? Two main ways. First is the start of the game, the Attacker (Person who puts down the first detachment) starts at Fury Level 1 and the Defender starts with Fury Level 2.

Credit: Games Workshop

I don't love this metric of determining who gets what Fury Level. Attacker and Defender generally is decided in two ways: Being attacker is usually bad, something the winner of the roll off makes the other player do to try and force them to reveal information first. It only varies if the drops for both players are the same, in which case being attacker allows you to finish dropping first and decide who goes first.

Personally I would have given 2 Fury to the one who is top of Round 1 as that is usually a a worse situation to be in, and fits the theme of the army being so fired up they're charging into battle first. However 1 level difference isn't the end of the world and probably won't change the calculus for whether you decide to go first or second.

The main way to generate additional Fury is from Places of Power. While Obscuring remains as a terrain rule, you can't just make random bits of terrain and objectives Obscuring anymore, we're leaving that behind in Ghyran. Instead Places of Power now give these 3 choices:

  • Raise Fury Level By 2, Gain 2 Rage Dice (both to a maximum of 7).
  • Give Priests and Wizards +1 to Chanting/Casting roll
  • Give a Non-Wizard/Priest a single Unbind.

These are still Once Per Turn (Army) but the Hero who does it takes D3 mortals and has to roll a 2+. So there is still a cost to it and it may be risky to do if its not your turn and you may not get a chance to Rally before they get hit.

Rage Dice

So what do you do with Rage Dice? You got 2 options, one is exciting and one is not. We'll focus on the exciting one first: Eruption of Fury

Credit: Games Workshop

So that's a lot of words. The short version is at the end of turn if a unit is still in combat, you get up to 3 extra swings from a unit. They don't get Criticals, but on a 6 score D3 additional Mortal wounds, but on a 1 the unit take D3 Mortals. A niche situation but still worth mentioning but since the D3 mortals on 6s are not a critical ability they aren't stopped by units who shut off crits which is nice.

In general this is a huge boon for Monsters or other units with 3 or more damage attacks. Since you only get 3, you might as well go big. Do it on something like Shadow Queen's Tail for 6 damage or Skarbrand for 8 and get some big returns on this. It is End of Turn, after all combats have resolved, so it's only going to be useful if you failed to kill a unit or were locked in combat with 2 units. But it's essentially free so might as well do it unless the risk of killing the unit is just too high.

The other use of Rage dice is...a lot less exciting: Fight Through the Pain

Credit: Games Workshop

Each Rage Dice can reduce damage on a 3+. 1 Damage. On a 3+. When the alternative is "get a free attack" that is a terrible exchange. This is even before the fact it reduces your Fury Level for every die.

It's not completely useless. It's done before allocating damage, which means you get to make it after doing wards but before taking damage. If you make your Ward rolls and see that the damage will wipe your lynchpin hero, you can roll to try and stop it. It's not clear how it's written if you can roll one at a time or have to pick how many dice you want then roll (it's a passive so you're not limited to once per phase), but if you have some Fury Dice left over it might be worth it.

I wish there was a bit more push and pull here to make the mechanic more dynamic, but there's only one way Rage actually goes down and I don't think it'll be used a lot. That said I also have some concern about having yet another thing to track during the game, so its possibly a good thing it's not too complicated.

Cities of Sigmar Mallus Forgepriest. Credit: SRM

Battle Tactics

Battle tactics work identical to how they did in Ghyran: Six cards with three tactics each that must be completed in sequence, pick two for your list.

Tactics Card 1: Blazing Onslaught

At the end of Deployment but before deciding the first turn, your opponent will choose a piece of non-Faction Terrain to be a hideout.

  • Affray: Master of Arms - Deal at least 1 point of damage to the same unit for 3 phases in one turn.
  • Strike: Break Their Defences - Control the enemy Hideout
  • Domination: No Survivors - Control the enemy Hideout and kill 2 enemy units.

This feels like this season's Intercept and Recover, forcing your opponent to pick your target. Solid all around pick, but the first tactic might screen out some armies if they don't have a spell or other ability to deal damage in the Hero phase, or a ranged unit to affect the shooting phase. If you're missing one you can try and make it up with Power Through or Eruption of Fury in the End of Turn Phase but not both. After that it's just being able to push into your opponent's back line, as they're undoubtedly going to pick the one that's the biggest pain in the ass for you to get to.

Tactics Card 2: Siege of Ashes

  • Affray: Form a Wall - 2 friendly units are wholly outside friendly territory but still within 6" of friendly territory and 3" of each other.
  • Strike: Outpost Resupply - You control an objective in enemy territory that is contested by a unit that hasn't moved this turn and another that charged this turn.
  • Domination: Repel the Attackers - Have 3 units within 3" of the middle of the battlefield that are not in combat.

This one seems pretty difficult. Form a Wall is easy, send two chaff units out and have them stick together, It's basically Scouting Force, you were probably doing that anyway.

Outpost Resupply requires a lot of setup, since you can't move you have to either have captured it last turn, or used Deep Strike. However, since you also need to charge in it's going to be difficult to deep strike on top of an objective and have a valid charge target nearby. So the ideal situation is you have an anvil who held out in combat from last turn, and then another unit to charge in and rescue them.

Repel the Attackers is basically a victory lap at that point. If you have the units and there isn't an enemy there, just run for the center.

Tactics Card 3: Flanking Firestorm

  • Affray: Ambuscade - Control any combination of 2 objectives or terrain pieces outside of 6" from friendly territory with 2 different units that were not set up this turn.
  • Strike: Surround the Enemy - 2 or more friendly units not set up this turn are within 9" of a corner and outside friendly territory.
  • Domination: Claim What’s Yours - More Friendly units in enemy territory than enemy units and at least 1 Friendly hero

This one seems the most straightforward of the bunch. Ambuscade is basically just playing the game. Surround the Enemy's difficulty varies wildly depending on what the territories look like, and Claim What's Yours is basically going to happen if you're winning anyway unless your list is very elite like Sons of Behemat.

Tactics Card 4: Smokescreen

  • Affray: Keep the Enemy Close - Control an Objective that is contested by at least one enemy unit.
  • Strike: Feign Weakness - Lose more friendly units this turn than your opponent loses units.
  • Domination: Execute the Plan - Control all objectives in your opponent's deployment.

I love the vibe of this but it's not going to be for every army. Keep the Enemy Close wants you to avoid killing an enemy unit, either by not charging the unit, or a fight ending in a stalemate. This can be tricky to set up, especially if the opponent is bold enough to Counter-Charge to try and mess you up.

Feign Weakness, I think works as a tactic you can choose when you know a unit of yours is going to die this turn (like the old Soulblight Gravelord tactic Callous Overlord) but less great if you can't anticipate the board state. I don't love the idea of needing to not kill a lot of stuff for a whole turn, unless you have disposable stuff and are ahead enough that that won't set you back.

Final tactic is basically gravy if you got this far.

Tactics Card 5: Burning for Vengeance

During deployment, choose an enemy Hero (they can be in reserves) to be the Fugitive.

  • Affray: Search Every Inch - Have a friendly unit in each quarter but further than 9" from the center.
  • Strike: Quick Pursuit - There are 3 friendly units not in combat who are within 12" of the fugitive or the Fugitive isn't on the battlefield.
  • Domination: Bring Them In - There are 3 friendly units in combat with the Fugitive or there is no Fugitive on the battlefield and you have 3 friendly units on an objective Wholly Outside Friendly Territory.

I love the flavor of this one, and I think it's quite good. Most lists will have some sort of front line hero that you will want to try and remove anyway making them an easy choice for the Fugitive. The first tactic is pretty easy to do on turn 1, depending on the deployment. The other 2 reward you with making progress to apprehending the marked Hero, but still have backups in case you get lucky and kill them early.

I think a potentially understated benefit of this is how it can manipulate your opponent. The obvious benefit is it makes them a bit less eager to throw that hero into combat, which may force them to make less than optimum moves. The other is that Tactic 2 and 3 only check if the enemy is on the battlefield or not, not if they're destroyed. If you pick something in Reserves, and they haven't deployed it after finishing Tactic 1, that's free tactics, so they will need to deploy them soon or risk giving you free tactics.

Also, the mental image of going "It's Kragnos! Book him boys!" is very funny.

Tactics Card 6: Legend of the Parch

  • Affray: Daring Rescue - An enemy unit that was in combat with a friendly unit at the start of the turn was destroyed by a combat attack from a different unit that charged this turn.
  • Strike: Command from the Front - 2 or more friendly units on the battlefield, all Heroes are within 9" of a visible enemy unit and no Friendly Heroes were slain this turn.
  • Domination: Hero of Legend - Capture an objective wholly outside friendly territory you did not control this turn.

This is a nice bread and butter tactic that I think will be the one you take if you aren't sure what to take. All 3 are pretty normal game states to get into, and don't require much to set up. The first one might be a filter if you don't have something to survive that can tank the first round of a charge, but who among us hasn't had a time where we were charge and a unit miraculously survives with 1 model remaining? Just send  in the back up to rescue them the next round.

Clawlord on Gnaw-beast. Credit: Rockfish

The Missions

Annoyingly we couldn't get maps drafted up in time for this review, so I'm going to have to describe the missions in more broad design. I will say that general speaking the deployments on this are wild and feel like they're really pushing the limits of what they can get away with this season. It'll likely lead to a lot of confusion as more casual players forget what their Territory is, so definitely keep the cards on you.

There's also new objective markers, but the number hasn't changed. Two pairs, the Golden Lions and Sun Seekers, and two standalone. You can keep using your Ghyran objective markers if you wish, just remember which is which.

Mission 1: Into the Fire

This is a "Control 1, 2, more" mission with five objectives that encourages a defensive playstyle. Two objectives are within player's territory, with a Place of Power nearby. If you control the Place of Power in your territory, you get Anti-Charge, and Underdogs get +3 Control when wholly outside enemy territory. This does not mean just your objective, since there's one in the middle (in both territories) and two in no man's land.

A small point of order, the way the mission is written the Place of Power in player's territories needs to be "wholly within" so when setting up the map make sure that they are both wholly within the territory as that seems the intended play.

Mission 2: Bloodstained Coasts

Woo, this is interesting. The deployment is pretty typical with the territories going all the way to the center of the field, but the far left "column" is a no man's land. Also the objectives are in a strange < shape we haven't seen before.

This starts as a "1, 2, more" mission but quickly deviates. In your Hero Phase one unit controlling an objective can dig up an Emberstone Cache, which they then carry around until it is used. There is one objective that can be set up on for game start, so both players should be able to grab some Emberstone Turn 1.

How do you use it? Well after charging any unit holding a Cache use it to to gain +1 to Hit and Wound with Ember-stone Augmented Weapons. If you're the Underdog you use up the Emberstone Slivers you used, but if you're not you lose all the ones your units are holding, whether they used it or not. Scoring also shifts in the 2nd Round not to more, but for stealing an objective an opponent controls.

This encourages not trying to spread out too much and steal every objective, because it'll make it easier for your opponent to take one and score 4 points. The Underdog mechanic also encourages a bit of push and pull, getting too far ahead means you'll burn through Emberstone faster when you might want to conserve it.

Mission 3: Avalanche of Ash

This is an attempt to bring back Nexus Collapse. 6 objectives with a "1, 2, More" design but at the start of each battle round the Underdog has to engage with the Collapsing Pass mechanic where they roll a D6, adding 1 for each unit on an objective and on an 8+, it is pulled from the map.

The Underdog can pick one objective as the Focus, which lets them add or subtract 3 (after the roll) so they get to put their thumb on the scale to keep an objective around or remove it. This does give them disproportionate power to control the flow of things and probably means it's one of those missions you want to sandbag a little.

Unlike Nexus Collapse, the mission shifts toward controlling terrain once the numbers of objectives dwindles. You score 3 points if you control more terrain when there's only one objective, and 7 if all objectives are gone.

It's...fine. Nexus Collapse is a divisive mission but it saw a lot of play back in the day. This probably is playable as well.

Mission 4: Caverns of Slaughter

Mixing it up, we have Control 1, 2 and a Pair rather than more this time. This mission has a sideways deployment and 2 pairs of objectives, but the primary gimmick is the Shifting Passages ability where the underdog gets to pick 2 pieces of terrain and for that round, both players can teleport between the two of them.

It's honestly not that weird of a mission and the gimmick is pretty straightforward. Very playable.

Mission 5: What’s Yours Is Ours

Possibly the most insane deployment in the entire set. This mission feels like it grew out of Passing Seasons from the current season, using pairs instead of standalone objectives that turn "on" and "off" each round. This is a 5 objective mission with 2 pairs, each Odd round the Golden Lions objectives are worth 3 points for holding both, while each Even the Sun Seekers are worth 3. The Underdog can change the "coveted" objective it to the other one if they wish, at the start of the round.

Otherwise, scoring is Hold One and Hold more. Solid mission, having to control a specific pair is going to be a lot more difficult for elite armies, but otherwise very playable.

Mission 6: Hidden Under Ash-Clouds

This one is nutty. A simple five objective "One, Two, More" mission hides one of the biggest twists they've tried so far. There is a huge "Neutral Territory" in the middle 2x2 block of deployment that is frequently covered in ash clouds. During the start of the mission, it is covered in Ash Clouds, which makes it so units and manifestations can't enter (but can leave), be set up, or see more than 3" around them, or through it (which basically disables shooting entirely). The Underdog can choose to lift the ash (Or bring it back if its gone), and if there is no Underdog, the Ash must return.

This basically creates a thunderdome in the middle where units stuck inside are going to have to fight each other for the middle objective. They can leave, but nothing new can come in and nothing can shoot through it. Seize the moment when the Ash lifts to send some of your strongest stuff in, then fight to maintain the middle. Cool mission, great design.

Mission 7: Warped Ruins

Six objectives; One, Two, More scoring. This is a relatively straightforward mission with lots of terrain, on Round 2 the Underdog chooses a piece of terrain to be set on fire for the rest of the battle, anyone within 6" of a piece of Terrain on fire during the start of the round takes D3 mortals. Then each round the Underdog chooses a piece of terrain within 12" and also sets it on fire, repeating the process of dealing D3 Mortals.

Solid mission. Very nice "top down" design where they clearly had the idea of a forest fire mechanic and transplanted it to Age of Sigmar rules quite well. While it may force players to change the route they take, D3 mortals for being too close to terrain isn't too punishing on its own (unless you're going into Sylvaneth).

Mission 8: Curse of the Gnaw

Four objectives; the Golden Lion and Sun Seeker are each worth 3 points and then 4 points for holding more. The gimmick here is the Instability Level, which starts at 1 and ticks up every time someone uses a Place of Power, capping out at 6. Then at the start of the turn, the underdog can pick an objective and deal damage equal to the instability level to everything on it, and resets the level to 1.

Solid design, it encourages not shoving everything on the objectives, especially something like a foot hero that might get immediately blown up next round if it stays there, but very predictable. One cannot blame anyone but themselves for farming the places of power every round like clockwork and then it blows up in their face.

Mission 9:Seize the Embers

Five objectives; first round is a Hold One, Hold Two and Hold More scoring but after round 1, the Hold 2 scoring goes away. Instead, the intent is that you loot the Seeking Suns objectives on the border of each territory for an Emberstone Nugget. This is not a once per turn (Army) ability, so all your stuff can attempt to do it, and the Underdog gets an immediate 6" move to try to get to safety. Then to score, on subsequent turns you return to your Golden Lion objective deep in player territory to return it with the Back to Base ability.

You only get credit for one use of Back to Base, so no point in doing it with multiple units a turn. Honestly solid mission design here. It encourages trying to grab resources and run to safety but since the "Hold more" scoring remains, to keep some stuff behind to hold that territory for future looting.

Mission 10: Treacherous Ground

Six objectives with a Hold One, Two and More scoring. This is a clever twist on it. In each player's terrain, they have the two pairs (Attacker gets  Seeking Suns, Defender gets Golden Lions) and in the first Battle Round both must choose one to be "Unstable", which essentially just shuts it off.

Then in subsequent rounds the Underdog can choose one of the pairs and switch which one is Turned on and off. There are then also two other objectives in no man's land which are free from being affected by the mechanic to balance it out.

The two pairs are spread out from each other, so it does force players to ping pong between both sides of their board if they want to keep the lead. Or risk a push into enemy territory to steal their stable objective.

Mission 11: Escape from the Coast

Pretty tame by comparison. There are six objectives and our last "One, Two, More" but the Underdog can pull one of the objectives on the far right, eventually, possibly, leaving only 3 to fight over.

It's a more controlled Nexus Collapse, since the ones that can disappear are regulated to one side of the board. Not too bad, but not my favorite.

Mission 12: Power of the Realms

This mission feels like an extension of Tectonic Interference from the third edition 2021 General's handbook. There are five objectives in a cross shape, with the middle being the "Primary" objective and is worth 3 points, and controlling 2+ secondaries is worth another 3 points (And then controlling more in total is worth 4).

The Underdog can shift the Primary left or right by controlling the Place of Power in that direction, but that's all. Nothing too wild in this mission either, it's just a very meat and potatoes sit on circles mission with a mechanic to keep people moving to the left or the right.

Karlina von Carstein, Warhammer+ Vampire Lord. Credit: SRM

Wrapping Up

Overall I like the new GHB. I have some criticisms of the new mechanic, but its fine and still beats Obscuring objectives. The mission pack itself is a lot better and there doesn't feel like there's any I absolutely despise. There's some I'd be less thrilled to play looking at you (Avalance of Ash) but nothing I'd consider a non-starter.

There are also Rumblings of a "Scourge of Aqshy" rules coming along too, but we didn't get any information on that. Nor did we hear if any of the Scourge of Ghyran stuff might survive the transition. So stay posted in the next few weeks for details!
