Tabletop Battles Reviews Warhammer 40k Battlefields: Armageddon

October 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In this review we’ll be looking at the upcoming Battlefields: Armageddon boxed set for Warhammer 40,000, a box designed to give players a full table of painted terrain. We’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with preview copies of the box for review purposes.

One of the big reveals from Games Workshop at the Adepticon preview - before 11th edition even launched - was the release of a set of pre-painted terrain. Although not the first time GW have released pre-painted terrain - that honour goes to the Chapel of Sanctuary, released back in 2003 - the new set is arguably their most ambitious release in years, and certainly the first time they’ve offered a full “battlefield in a box” product that promised to be playable completely out of the box, no painting required.

Chapel Of Sanctuary

Attendees of GW’s 11th edition events this year will no doubt already be familiar with the assembled contents of these kits - Games Workshop have been eager to show them off at said events, allowing players to see firsthand how they look on full tables. What we haven’t seen to date however, is the assembly process for them, and how it differs, if at all, from the set of unpainted terrain GW released as part of their Starter Set product back in August.

What's in the Box

You get two full pre-painted sets of terrain. These are identical to the ones in the Starter Set, with identical packaging and sprues. The difference here is that the terrain has already been painted on the sprue. In addition to that terrain a set of the terrain footprints which are already available separately, and a set of "game mat boards" you can unfold to make a full 44"x60" table. Anyone familiar with existing GW boards or Kill Team will be familiar with these. They're thick and relatively heavy, and make up like half of the weight of this box, if not more.

Overall Impressions of the Terrain

TheChirurgeon: Generally speaking, this terrain set was a breeze to put together. It’s the exact same sprues as the unpainted kit from the Starter Set, but prepainted. This has one important knock-on effect, which is that the kit seems to have been designed with tolerances for the paint in mind. Everything here went together snugly and easily, no glue required - which was absolutely not true for the unpainted terrain. Compared to that kit, this was easier to assemble and stayed together much better.

The terrain has all been painted on sprue, and as a result you're going to have some gates and mold lines to deal with. For the most part, these are in decent places - only a couple were outright annoying or in spots I felt desperately needed a touch-up - but I also didn't feel like it was crucial to get these cleaned up, either - the coloring on the plastic and the paint makes it easy to miss them when you have it all assembled on the table. That said, we'll be looking to color match them later.

The paint jobs on the kit are, simply put, amazing. GW have done an impressive job capturing the ‘Eavy Metal paintjob on this terrain set, and the level of detail here is staggering. I’ve done a fair bit of terrain painting in my day, and suffice to say that there are details here I simply would not have bothered with if I’d been painting it myself. That said, the whole thing has a kind of "printed" effect to it - it's slightly grainy with a glossy/metallic sheen to it. I didn't have much issue with it but if it bothers you then I suspect giving the whole thing a coat of matte varnish will solve the problem.

This goes inside other pieces, and would have to be painted as a subassembly

This kit was absolutely designed to be pre-painted, and that was obvious back when we reviewed the unpainted version of the terrain box a couple of months ago. Pieces of this are absolutely set up to be painted before assembly, and I maintain that painting terrain with subassemblies is sicko shit. So much of the value here on this terrain is just how much work went into painting little details that I normally don't bother with when I paint terrain.

On that note, one of the nice touches here is the way the terrain is designed to go with the area bases included in the set. They match up aesthetically in a way that I find very pleasing.

All together, the whole thing looks great when you have it on a table. I'd be more than happy to play using this set, and I plan to, using it to replace most of my existing terrain. It's not that I don't love that terrain; it's just that the munitorum ruins end up being a bit too tall/wide for the 11th edition footprints. They make me think the board is less open and make it easier to hide - and I plan to expect worse.

Liam: Assembly-wise, this more or less goes together fine. I didn't have entirely the same experience as Rob; while it definitely fits more snugly than the unpainted version, glue was still required on some of the non-ruin pieces. I also found the plasma generator thing kind of a pain to put together, though I think I would have done myself a favour here by gluing each half together and then letting it set before carrying on. The paint is genuinely impressive. One of my favourite details is that the screens and plasma orbs have gloss finishes - it's seriously impressive stuff for a pre-paint and I would love more detail on the process. I can't imagine painting all of this myself - even with an airbrush it would be a serious time investment.

There are a couple of bum notes - as you probably guessed, the gates are pretty visible in places. There's some clever work done to hide them - the L shapes have a lot of them placed inside the join for example, so they're hidden away - but others are unavoidable. In some cases you just need to do a little bit of touching up, but a few of them are more difficult to fix, like the ones on the aforementioned plasma orbs. If you were desperate to scrape the mold lines off you'd create even more work for yourself.

That said, compared to painting the whole set, it's not the end of the world. The other issue is that in a few places I had parts that were simply not painted. The left edge of the taller barricades, for example, was pretty much bare on both sprues. I assume this is to do with how the manufacturing process works and you likely won't notice it in play, but it's worth mentioning that the process, while impressive, isn't flawless.

TheChirurgeon: For what it's worth, I don't think I had that issue in my sets.

Wings: I’m a dumb idiot who got very enthused about painting some of the ruins we got in the starter set, so I can provide a side-by-side comparison of what this looks like if you paint it yourself vs. get the pre-paints.

Credit: Wings

Credit: Wings

I guess the reassuring news given the time I poured into this is that I do think the one I painted looks slightly nicer. However. It does represent somewhere in the region of 6-8 hour’s work, whereas the pre-paint took me under five minutes to clip, tidy up, and assemble. I am not someone blessed with infinite spare time - if I was making a purchasing decision about which of these to get, I would obviously go for the pre-paints, they look good, they go together fast, and I’m hugely in favour of anything that lowers the barrier to entry for playing “real” 40K. I do, from examining my own paint job, think they missed a trick by not going for a brighter contrasting colour on the protruding metalwork on the ruin faces - doing that in the bright red really made mine pop, and something similar would look great on the pre-paints.

TheChirurgeon: Yeah I think the terrain easily compares to the stuff I hand painted. It has a lot more fine detail work than my paintjobs, where I'm letting washes do most of the heavy lifting. It's as good as the red Manufactorum terrain I painted, but probably not as nice as the paintjob on my white/gray terrain, and the Woodland Scenics greenery does a lot of heavy lifting there. The differences are easier to tell up close, and I think painted terrain has the edge in that department, but from above the table I don't think you notice much difference.

What's more important is that it looks great next to either of those, and great next to fully painted models. I'd be more than happy to have this all together on my table, and you're going to see it in a lot of battle reports moving forward.

Final Thoughts

Liam: For the price point, I think the product is great. It's high quality terrain with better paint on it than I would ever bother with, and you'll be playing games on a great-looking table faster than ever. I do kind of wish the card boards weren't included (the mats to make a playing surface, not the terrain areas). I've never liked these much as a product and I don't think I've ever known anyone to use them, though maybe they're more popular in groups where nobody wants to buy a separate mat. I understand the impulse towards having a battlefield in a box, though - this is a product they should have launched years ago. I'm interested to see if it remains a core stock item and is still available in say, 18 months' time. If it's successful I'd also love to see other spins on it with different aesthetics.

TheChirurgeon: Seconding the notion that I wish they'd left out the "mat" boards, as those are just fully extraneous at this point and the only part of this that's a legitimately bad product, especially compared to what else you can get on the market. I understand why it's in here but it adds a ton of weight and cost the kit didn't need. I agree that it's a solid value even at the MSRP of $395 in the United States, but if that price is giving you sticker shock, I'm not surprised. It's a big ask for a single purchase, even one you're going to use every game you play at home. That said, I think the terrain is just straight up worth having and I'd also love to see versions rocking different aesthetics. We've already seen slick MDF terrain mats at GW's events with snow and other themes for the terrain; I want to see those go up for sale with some appropriately themed terrain to follow.
