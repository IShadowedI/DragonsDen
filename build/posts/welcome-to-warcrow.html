Welcome to Warcrow

October 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to Warcrow, friends.

This is Corvus Belli's latest skirmish wargame. Do you like playing Fantasy games but hate the idea of assembling, painting and then transporting more than 50 models to and from your FLGS? Do you like being engaged with the game the entire time instead of turning your brain off during your opponent's turn? Finally, do you take great satisfaction in playing with gorgeous models that can be incredibly easy to paint? This is the game for you.

Thank you to Corvus Belli for providing some of these models and rules for review.

Narrative and Setting

Warcrow - The defenders of Feudom clash with the monstrous Scions (photo courtesy of Musterkrux)

Warcrow is set in the world of Lindwurm, which contains your usual Fantasy tropes. You've got an ancient seemingly extinct progenitor race that did weird, nasty things to the mystic flows of the world and has left it to the following civilisations to clean up. The Moon (one of two) is approaching a period of syzygy (you have no idea how long I have waited to use that word in an appropriate context; we're talking near on decades, people) and with it, the flow of magic within this world is reaching a dangerous peak. The nations and peoples of Lindwurm must contend with The Fog, the physical manifestation of magical corruption that creeps across the land, consuming and corrupting everything it touches.

Factions

Let's take a quick look at the different factions available in the game. You've got: Empire, Bretonnia, Orcs and Goblins, Wood Elves, Dwarves, and Chaos...Sorry, wrong setting. I joke but also, if you want a quick reference for understanding the setting, the Warhammer Fantasy (Pre-Age of Sigmar) parallel isn't wrong.

Hegemony of Embersig

Warcrow - Hegemony of Embersig

The Hegemony is a faction where your demographics is mostly Human but with a dash of Elf and a pinch of Dwarf mixed in (Note to self: 'A Pinch of Dwarf' would be an excellent band name), noting that Embersig is the in-game translation of 'All People' which could be taken as an ambition, promise or threat depending on the time of day. The Hegemony play style is advancing onto objective and holding them with a few well placed ranged weapons (gunpowder is a helluva drug when everyone else is using bows and throwing sharp sticks).

If you're looking for a centre-piece unit for the Hegemony you should consider the War Rig, which is a clockwork robot that stomps around and flattens people with a dragon-shaped hammer-flamethrower combo.

Warcrow - War Rig

Feudom

Warcrow - Feudom

You've got Knights, Peasants and Magical Damsels: This faction is Bretonnia AF and I am entirely here for it. The entire reason this initial Warcrow review has been delayed is because when CB announced the game and the relevant factions I knew, from Day One, what my faction choice would be. So, I didn't even jump into the game proper until they had released enough models for me to play Feudom and pay it the respect it deserves as a faction. So, while CB has indeed sent us some models to assist in reviewing this game, and we thank them for their generosity, I want to stress that I bought into this game before any review content was sent to us because I have a lot of faith in CB being able to deliver on a skirmish wargame with exquisite models. A faith that has, in my opinion, been rewarded.

Warcrow - Feudom (Photo courtesy of Musterkrux)

Feudom's game plan is relatively defensive; the faction is generally pretty good at arriving at a hill (or other notable landmark) and then not dying on it. Maybe this is the hill your opponent's models die on? I mean maybe, I don't care so much, all I know is that my models are here and they're not going anywhere.

A centrepiece or iconic model for this faction would be Roland Loussier and his Griffon, Whitetrail. The only thing cooler than a cavalry charge is a flying cavalry charge. Just look at him, what a heckin' chonker.

Roland Louissier and Whitetrail (photo courtesy of Corvus Belli)

The Northern Tribes

Warcrow - War Riders

Orcs and Varanks (who are not-Orcs, I guess, with blue skin and pronounced lower canines but are definitely not Orcs) live in the far north and are sweeping down from the freezing cold to conquer the other, weaker nations. As far as the character of the faction is concerned, there's a bit of the romanticism of living in touch with nature as a contrast to the industrialisation of nations like Embersig but only a little bit; the Northern Tribes read like they rampage and pillage as a choice more than a necessity.

The Northern Tribes play style is to use their superior offensive capability to offset their weaker objective holding capabilities, they need to push people off objectives because they can't contest them as well.

Sÿenann

Warcrow - Sÿenann

The Sÿenann are the elves that live in, and protect, the Great Forest (stop me if you've heard this one). The Great Forest is pretty rad, in as much as it's not so much a single, monolithic geographical location but it's actually scattered all over Lindwurm, connected via a network of portals. There's a cool side faction of the Sÿenann, the elves who live in the part of the Great Forest that suffered from some sort of mysterious blight, that part of the forest is diseased and dying and this is reflected in the inhabitants of that area: The Ashen. They look absolutely miserable and they look like they're going to make that everyone else's problem.

The Sÿenann play style is high mobility glass cannons playing at hit-and-run tactics with a fair whack of ranged gameplay.

If you're looking for a cool-as-heck unit for the Sÿenann, we recommend you take a gander at Eachann the Great Centaurelf. Does exactly what it says on the box: Half horse, half elf, all angry.

Mounthaven

Warcrow - Mounthaven Dwarves

I'm going to be real with you, you already know what a Dwarf faction in a war game is about. They're short, slow and tough as nails. Some of their magic-users focus on shaping Orichalcum, the precious metal that rests under the surface of Lindwurm. Mounthaven's playstyle is a bit of a Hammer and Anvil affair, your solid basic infantry advance on the enemy and aggressively Not Die while you set up your big hits from the flank.

When it comes to heckin' chonkers, check this guy out. The Waljan the Ancestor (the Last of the First) is a shaped being summoned by the Mounthaven Orichalcum Smiths. Not only does Waljan wreak absolute havoc in your opponent's lines but they also come back after they get put down. We've had one massacre, yes. But what about second massacre?

Warcrow - Waljan

Scions of Yalgometh

Warcrow - Scions of Yaldabaoth (Photo courtesy of Musterkrux)

Remember earlier in the review where we mentioned that the current problems that befall the people of Lindwurm are the result of poor decisions made by an ancient, extinct civilisation? Yeah, well, reports of the death of the Gae Assail are greatly exaggerated*.

Other factions suffer penalties when their magic-users drink too deep of the Fog, the Scions just go full throttle and chug the good stuff like a Fratboy at a Kegger. Corruption is just like Pringles, once you pop you can't stop.

The aesthetics for Scions is amazing. Lots of magic corruption, body horror (not quite Human Centipede levels of people sewing things to other people but that's the direction we're heading here...), and a lovely balance of the bestial and the demonic in both their human followers and their dark overlords. I love that the wannabe Humans who desperately want to ascend go full Buffalo Bill and wear the skins of other animals and even people to emulate their superiors who have undergone genuine transformations.

A great centre-piece for the Scions is actually the grand Master Uruk himself, a leader piece you get in the starter box. Dude is Diablo as hell and I celebrate his Big Red Energy (but, like, classic chunky Diablo-styles, less so stupid, sexy Diablo.).

Warcrow - Master Uruk (Photo courtesy of Musterkrux)

Gameplay Overview

Like Infinity, Warcrow is designed to keep both players engaged with the game as much as possible by providing reactions and decisions to be made by the non-active player during some contested rolls. Your ability to make reactive movements (or even attacks) in the opposing players turn adds a layer of delicious complexity to the game.

Dice Mechanics: Just like an earlier Corvus Belli game, Aristeia (now known as Hexadome Legends), Warcrow uses a proprietary dice system that uses Successes, Blocks, and Special symbols, though now on D8s instead of D6s. The ratio of these symbols is different across various dice. Your Red and Orange dice are more 'Success' focused, your Yellow dice are mostly 'Special' orientated and your Blue, Green and Black dice have the highest proportion of 'Block' symbols. It's also worth noting that there are 'hollow' symbols (where the Shield is just an outline or the Star has a missing 'dot' in the middle), these are only counted under specific circumstances.

Warcrow - So many D8s.

During a standard attack sequence, you and your opponent will roll certain dice (Attackers will usually be using abilities that use more Red, Yellow and Orange dice while Defensive skills are more Blue, Green and Black....but this is not necessarily always true for every character or ability available to them). On one level both players determine how many Hits they are sending at their opponent compared to how many Blocks the respective parties have. It's perfectly possible to have an Attacking model and the Defender both do damage to each other, it's not a Winner-Takes-All resolution system. Then, on top of the potential exchange of damage you have the Switch system, where you can spend your dice results (predominantly Special results but there's often a surcharge of Successes or Blocks you need to cash in, as well) to trigger movement/placement effects or inflict status effects on models (friendly or enemy).

Gameplay Example: Here we have Master Uruk, the big bad dude himself, attacking the noble warrior Morgane of Jauffret. His melee attack roll is one Red die, two Orange and a Yellow. Morgane rolls her Defence stat of two Blue and a Black. You'll notice in the photo that one her 'shield' die results is hollow instead of being entirely filled in/white (we'll cover that in a moment).

Master Uruk attacks Morgane (Warcrow)

Uruk is sending 4 damage (that's the number of hits he's rolled on the dice) but also note that he can use his two Special results (the ! symbols) to negate one of Morgane's Block results.

On the other side, Morgane has rolled three Blocks and two Special results. However, the hollow Block won't count here as they only count for triggering Switches or for Elite characters (Morgane of Jauffret, ex-Priestess of Selas turned Inquisitor? Not quite elite enough.). Sadly, she doesn't have any Switches to use those Special results so she's stopping 2 of Uruk's incoming damage before his Switch negates one of them. So, Morgane is taking 3 damage unless, furthermore, Uruk decides to spend some of his Hits to heal himself up from any damage taken earlier.

Models and Materials

War games are more than just their rules, part of the hobby experience is in the assembly, painting and enjoyment of the physical models. So, let's talk about Warcrow's models as well as the materials they are made out of.

Sculpts

Let's be clear, this is a Corvus Belli game. The sculpt quality is amazing. The proportions are nice, the way they communicate texture on models (Leather, metal armour, corrupted skin), and being a skirmish game CB can indulge more dramatic sculpts within units without worrying about duplicates ruining the look. The crisp details make these models really easy to paint, as well, you can go full Studio styles or toss some Contrasts onto them and let the model do the work.

Materials

Here is, unfortunately, a mild frustration. CB is using two different materials. One appears to be used in the rank and file models while characters or other important/unique models get a different material. Research indicates the first material might be Siocast while the other could be a thermoplastic (if someone can confirm otherwise, please sing out). Siocast has been previously criticised as an unfortunate compromise for managing the cost of materials. A common issue observed is the difficulty in removing mold lines from siocast models. This was certainly true for earlier iterations of siocast but the Warcrow models don't seem to have inherited this problem. However, they remain somewhat 'rubbery' to the touch, thin extremities (swords and bows in particular) flop around something fierce. You could argue that this degree of flexibility is better than rigid brittleness when it comes to surviving bumps and falls. Personally, I wouldn't but that flexibility might be someone else's jam, I guess.

Things We Like

The fact that the game focuses on scoring more than biffo, especially with models being vastly more resilient than you might expect for a war game, means that Warcrow avoids the common Negative Play Experience of a devastating alpha-strike; you're rarely going to be completely locked out of a game because your opponent got to roll Attack Dice first.

The use of Corruption and Fatigue as resources/risks that can be stretched to breaking point is an excellent way of representing incremental degradation of a fighting force without removing models from the table with a dustpan and brush. It helps keep the game tense and dramatic. It also means that your named special character, Marhael the Refused, hasn't been murked sixteen times over the course of your local campaign. He's not dead, he's just Le Tired.

The sculpts are, as expected from Corvus Belli, amazing. It's an absolute delight to see these models on the battlefield. The advantage to playing a skirmish game is that with such  a low model count, you're not likely to have too many duplicate sculpts on the table at any one time so your army is a gorgeous thing to behold.

The use of the dice system to track not just offence and defence but also trigger status effects and positional play makes the game incredibly enjoyable. You're not just taking turns hitting each other with pointy sticks effectively just trading hit points but each contested roll could see you jockeying for position, blinding your enemy, reducing their morale, loading their mages up with a lethal dose of magical Kool-Aid, all that jazz. It's not enough to count HP, you also have to consider whether you can push an opponent off an objective to stop them scoring or if they can do the same to you. It's a vastly more rewarding game experience than just exchanging punches to see who runs out of living models first.

Things We'd Like to See in the Future

Going to be honest here, the unique movement and distance measurement system of Strides (1 Stride = 2.5 centimetres, so not even an inch) was not a successful experiment. While it's great to build a series of unique value propositions for your game (you need to have a good reason for people to choose to play you game instead of another one, particularly where the industry is dominated by a few well-established properties, cough cough), you also need your game to be accessible and easily understood by your migrating players. Just let people use their measuring tapes. It's such a small quality of life change that I think would help people translate across to this lovely game.

As a minor gripe, while the material they are using for line troops holds detail perfectly well, it does seem to be a more rubbery resin/plastic (plesin?) than whatever they're using for the character units. However, if this material is what's keeping the game's cost of entry down I suppose that's a concession we'll have to make.

Final Thoughts

If you've played Infinity and really enjoyed the quality of sculpts, skirmish-scale and dynamic gameplay but thought to yourself: "Hey, I want this but in a Fantasy setting." then I'd describe Warcrow as about 90% of what you want. Warcrow is a great alternative game to the larger-scale systems in the fantasy space, it's dynamic and keeps both players involved at all times, the models are gorgeous and being a skirmish game you can buy-in relatively cheaply compared to the alternatives. It's not a perfect game and I'd be very interested to see what CB does with the rules in the inevitable 2nd Edition release to help reduce friction but overall it's definitely a winner for me.

Next time we'll talk about some factions, showcase some of the lovely models CB has kindly shared with us, and give you some tips on how to kick off your Warcrow experience.

*Note: The quote referenced here: Reports of my death are greatly exaggerated is, in fact, an exaggeration itself. Today we learned something (Well, I did at least.).
