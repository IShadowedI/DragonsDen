A Brush With Greatness 10: Beginning Cormac - Old Skin

September 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to the next edition of Brush With Greatness! I’m keewa, your picaro of pigments - if you’re new here, welcome! In this column, I detail my efforts to become a competitive display painter in detail, going through techniques and step-by-step instructions so you can follow along. Last time I finished the wonderful Kuri; this time I’m going to be tackling Cormac the Druid. This wonderful bust, also from Black Crow Miniatures, was sculpted by Lucas Pina Penichet from artwork by Guillermo Gonzalez. I love how characterful this fellow is, with plenty of textures to play with along the way – skin, fabric, feathers, hair, leaves, wood, snail shells; this guy has it all.

Loading...

I followed a tutorial by Arnau Lazaro to paint the skin, beginning by basecoating all the skin with Vallejo Game Color Lustful Purple. This might seem like a strange choice, but it gives some nice shadows with a different colour cast to the normal orangey brown that is the "default" shadow for skin tones. With everything based in Lustful Purple, I created a mix for the base skin tone by mixing the Lustful Purple with Vallejo Game Color Pale Skin, Tan, and Sunset Orange. The method is a little strange, but by keeping the original Lustful Purple in the mix, it preserves the overall coherency of the skin.

Loading...

With that done, I did something I’d not done before: following Mr Lazaro’s tutorial, I mixed together Pro Acryl Fluorescent Orange and Fluorescent Pink and painted it on the cheeks and nose to create a very strong blush. Once again, following the tutorial, I added some Vallejo Game Color Pale Flesh to the orange and pink fluorescent mix and began to carefully glaze the blush until it looked more natural, but still with a nice warm boost.

I shaded the furrows of his wrinkled brow and under the eye-bags with some pure Lustful Purple mixed with Tan. There was quite a lot of going back and forth with the shade mix and the base tone mix, but it’s really important to accentuate the detail on the craggy face. Since I started painting the skin with the airbrush, it’s essential to add this stuff back; otherwise, the skin in general will look far too smooth and unnatural for an old man with weathered skin. Basecoating the hair and beard is an important step in making sure that the skin looks correct, and it stopped me from wasting time applying skin tones to parts of the model that are not supposed to be skin. I bascoated the beard with Vallejo Game Color Charcoal and applied a first highlight by mixing the Charcoal with some Vallejo Game Color Dead White.  

Loading...

I progressively highlighted the beard, hair, and eyebrows by adding white to the Charcoal.

To get the arms looking as though they fit with the face, I glazed the knuckles and elbows with the same fluo mix I used to liven up the nose and cheeks. I also did my usual trick of painting the eyes with Army Painter Speedpaint Goddess Glow to prepare the sockets for painting. I put a bit of Goddess Glow in the hollows of the cheeks and the sides of the nose as well, to add a touch more contrast. To basecoat the eyeballs, I start with Pro Acryl Warm Grey, which I think is a good starting point for eyes, since real eyes are never actually “white”; I don’t want to start there. I also accentuated the under-eye bags with some of the flesh mix, with a bit more Pale Flesh added. The whole process of painting skin generally involves a lot of back and forth until you find the balance you’re looking for; you can see this clearly in the glazing to blend the skin tones with the fluorescents.

Loading...

Following on from the Warm Grey, I painted the first highlight on the eyes, treating them as globes, painting the spherical surface with Pro Acryl Bright Warm Grey, leaving Warm Grey in the corners. To finish the general shape of the eyeball, I added a little Bold Titanium White to the Bright Warm Grey; remember - eyes are never truly white! I also highlighted the tops of the furrows of his brow with Pale Flesh mixed with a really tiny amount of Lustful Purple. Now he looks sufficiently craggy. Now for the most important part: The windows of the soul. I painted the general shape of the Irises using Vallejo Game Colour Dark Green. This step is perhaps the trickiest part of the whole model; painting the eyes on busts is essentially a freehanding exercise. It’s very important to make sure that the gaze is fixed correctly and the eyes aren’t wonky (unless that’s what you’re going for). To fit the overall mood of the figure, I wanted him to be looking at the little bird perched on his hand, as though receiving some news. To achieve this, I went for a quizzical sideways glance.

Loading...

I painted progressively smaller crescents of lighter greens from the same triad - Vallejo Angel Green, Scorpy Green and Bile Green - in the opposite direction to the light source. Once the iris was nicely painted, I carefully made a pupil with Pro Acryl Coal Black.  In order to sell the illusion of the eye being slightly wet, it needs to have a reflection, which I created by painting a catchlight of Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White on the opposite side to the iris highlights from the previous step. 

Loading...

With a little more glazing with the original flesh mix, that concludes the skin and face of Cormac! I really like this method of painting skin; I feel like it has the right amount of warmth and colour contrast to make it really striking. Next time we’ll tackle his clothes and finishing touches!
