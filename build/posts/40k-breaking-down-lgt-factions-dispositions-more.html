[40k] Breaking Down LGT: Factions, Dispositions, & More

September 27th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Over the last few years, the London Grand Tournament (LGT) has slowly taken the crown for being the largest singles event in the world for Warhammer 40k with 1,028 registered players coming into this weekend. With that in mind, we decided to take a look at this weekend's lists and breaking down the current meta before everything is thrown into upheaval by the upcoming Codex: Space Marines update next week.

Faction Break Down

Cedit to date from Listforge.info

LGT has been able to scrounge together at least one player for every single faction, something that many events can’t manage. Usually at best a large event will have a single lone weirdo bucking the trend defiantly with Imperial Agents, demanding they be taken seriously (Editor: No). LGT has a whopping six, beating out its count of Deathwatch players - four (Editor: Wrap it up, Deathwailures).

At the top of the meta Orks and Custodes mark the top spots by volume, followed by Marines and Dark Angels. Fun Fact: There are almost as many Allarus Terminators in lists as attendees, with 870 of the dumb golden terminators (this is according to one of my friends, don't blame me if that math is wrong). These are the big meta threats; all of them either barely got nerfed in the last slate, or recently received a crazy powerful codex to shoot them to the top (Orks).

After the top four there are a few surprises: Necrons, Emperor's Children, Chaos Space Marines, and Tyranids make up the next four top factions by volume. Necrons generally are very popular in the UK, and have been consistently good since the start of 10th edition, so I am not shocked to see them here; same with Emperor's Children, who still are doing okay even after their big nerfs. Chaos Space Marines are a bit more unique; they have done fine in 11th edition so far haven't been dominant since Defilers and three quarters of the units supporting them received multiple points hikes. That said, they've always been a popular faction, and that'll be a factor here. Same kind of goes for Tyranids, who tend to be disappointing in 11th edition even with Norns, but gained a huge popularity boost from showing up in the 10th edition starter box.

T’au and Votann round out the top ten factions and are where we drop below the 5% threshold per faction. These are both okay factions; neither is considered too overpowered or bad, and both are likely driven a bit by a mix of solid faction performance and faction specialization. Then we have most of the other decent factions, with a few stand-outs like World Eaters and Grey Knights having much higher faction percentages then their win rate would suggest they deserve.

Rounding out the bottom five, Chaos Knights barely beat out Adepta Sorotiats for number six by just one player. We have Adepta Soroitas with eighteen players, Black Templars with fourteen, GSC with eight, Imperial Agents with six, and Deathwatch with four. Two of these lower player count armies are Space Marines which is often expected due to other Space Marine chapters that are more powerful dragging away all but the most dedicated players. The other three are all examples of factions that just have not quite hit the mark in 11th and are again most likely just drawing on the most dedicated faction specialists.

Now that we understand the armies of LGT, let's break down the next most important part of 11th: Army Dispositions.

Dispositions

Credit for data from Listforge.info

At first glance, this looks pretty bad for balance. On a deeper dive, it looks even worse - Take and Hold and Priority Assets take up a combined 62% of lists at the event, which in a world where there are three total options that would be perfect. Unfortunately, we live in the world with five options, and that's not a great outlook for the Disposition meta.

Take and Hold and Priority Assets are both the go-to disposition options for the field, they are both very similar to how 10th played: Hold objectives and maybe do an action. That's very easy for pretty much any army no matter how it is built, it's just a question of whether you need action goobers. In regard to Priority Assets, in a world where there are only two action secondaries - both of which give a free redraw when held together - it is really easy to plan on doing your mission action without having to worry too much about action economy.

Reconnaissance and Purge the Foe both can require a bit more to run, but for two different reasons. Most armies are totally fine running Purge the Foe, but it is very hard to pick as most of the best detachments lost easy access to Purge the Foe in the balance dataslate a few months ago, while the ones who do still have it tend to be on the lower power level. Reconnaissance is a bit odd in that a few of its missions are a bit closer to Take and Hold then to a recon-style mission, meaning your army has to both be able to spread across the board, while also being able to run a traditional hold primary style, which can be a challenge. This is offset in part by the Darkflight Pursuit Detachment, which just makes up for it by tabling opponents quickly.

The final option, Disruption with a measly forty five champions, is a travesty. You basically only run Disruption if you are forced to, or if you are the best player in the world having a laugh. This primary has to do so much more work for way fewer points in comparison to the rest of the field and often feels like playing with a handicap.

Final Thoughts

I think this event breakdown really puts into focus how polarized the current meta is. With almost a quarter of the event just playing the top three factions, it really puts into perspective how much the top armies, Custodes, Orks, and Marines are performing in comparison to the rest of the field. I hope to see some drastic changes in the next slate besides the best list going up ten points or we will be stuck with this for a while.
