Conquest Faction Focus: W'adrhŭn - Overview and Mechanics

May 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to the first article of the W'adrhŭn Faction Focus series for Conquest: Last Argument of Kings! This first part will give an overview of the background and overview of the faction as well as their unique War Chant mechanic for the tabletop. The second article will cover the Character options in depth, and the third will go over the Regiments available.

Background

Art by Para Bellum

The W'adrhŭn are a race of tribal warriors who have thrown off the chains of their oppressive makers and set out to create their own civilisation in the world of Eȁ. Originally created by the Directorate of the Spires, the W'adrhŭn were considered ‘true warriors’ who vastly outstripped the mindless clones that make up the majority of the Spire forces. The Spires created four beings known as the Primes, named Conquest, War, Famine and Death. These would form the biological blueprints for the W'adrhŭn themselves, huge muscular warriors with highly attuned senses that would be the vanguard of the Spires forces.

During the Dweghom marches and the time of the Fall, the W'adrhŭn were deployed against the enemies of the Spires and intended to be sacrificed as an acceptable loss. Despite huge casualties, including three of the four Primes, the fourth Prime Conquest would gather the survivors and lead to safety and freedom from their masters. They escaped though the Long Winter and into the Wastelands where they would thrive in their own cultures.

It is the fourth prime, known to the W'adrhŭn as the Uzunfazane, the living goddess, who is the deific figure for these survivors. She is worshiped through the Cults, who pay credence to her and each of her fallen siblings in turn with their Scions. Despite this religious zealotry though, the Tribes that make up the W'adrhŭn are fundamentally pragmatic rather than dogmatic. Each tribe is led by the Matriarchs and form a council made up of elders, Scions, Chieftains and other experienced leaders who run the day to day activities of the tribes.

The W'adrhŭn themselves are over seven feet tall, possessing highly-attuned hearing apparatus through their skulls and jaws to hear clearly in the heat of battle. This has let them develop a close relationship with the savage beasts of the Wastelands - huge reptilian creatures that join the W'adrhŭn as powerful beasts of war. This all comes with a cost however; W'adrhŭn have voracious appetites and the majority of their labour force are dedicated to cultivating food for the species. With such demands, only the very best become warriors chosen to take up military arms in battle.

A race made to be slaves, who escaped their oppressors and found sanctuary in the most desolate of places. The W'adrhŭn are survivors of great hardships but who will fight for their existence against the array of ancient and powerful empires that would deny them their place in the world.

Playstyle

The W'adrhŭn are an elite army that hits fast and hard but doesn’t generally want to stick around to see what happens next. Almost all of the abilities of the W'adrhŭn lean into an offensive mindset and generally boost the already respectable speed and damage output of their units. Their army rule War Chant supports this with timing based additional rules that can significantly boost the capabilities of any unit in your army.

That being said, they aren’t necessarily a true ‘glass cannon’ as their higher than average Wound count gives them a little more resilience than you might expect. With almost every unit sporting 5 or 6 Wounds per Stand and good access to Evasion, your regiments can have a nasty habit of sticking around longer than your opponent expects. W'adrhŭn is not a forgiving army, but it is rewarding to players who can set up significant activations and plan ahead through the game Round.

Strengths

  • A fast moving army with wide access to Move 7, Unstoppable and Juggernaut across the board.
  • Respectable offensive output with a high quantity of attacks, most commonly combining base Attacks with close range Volley attacks, impacts and Trample.
  • The War Chant system makes any unit in your list threatening when needed, allowing even basic infantry to hit above their weight when called upon.

Weaknesses

  • W'adrhŭn units are costed for their potential, not their base stats. Without support, your units can feel overcosted and underwhelming.
  • The War Chant system is flexible but takes time to set up and can be interrupted by losing a unit at the wrong time. This can be devastating if you don’t trigger an essential Chant.
  • W'adrhŭn suffers when getting bogged down in protracted combat with generally lower Defence and Resolve stats. This combined with many units having free Volley or Impact attacks means you tend to lose attrition grinds.
Overall, you should consider playing W'adrhŭn if you want a dynamic army that wants to take the initiative and hold it. This isn't your blood-crazed berserkers overwhelming with brute force, it's about anticipating the flow of battle and preparing your units to strike at the vulnerable points of the line. Timing is everything, and W'adrhŭn will reward players who can master it.

Plus y'know, orcs riding dinosaurs. This is objectively the coolest faction.

Art by Para Bellum

War Chant

When I talk about the W'adrhŭn army rule to other players, I’ll often describe it as like playing a rhythm game side quest alongside the main game. The War Chant system has been tweaked numerous times over the edition but the core mechanic is still very simple:
  1. When you activate a Regiment, add a corresponding Chant Marker to your pool of Markers (called your Sequence).
  2. If you have three Markers, you may immediately Chant the Battlecry.
  3. Apply the corresponding effects of the Chant to the unit that is being activated depending on which Cult has been Chanted and lasts until the end of the Round. The Cult bonuses are as follows:
  • Famine - The Regiment games Dread.
  • Death - The Regiment gains Deadly Blades.
  • War - The Regiment gains Cleave (+1).
  • Conquest - The Regiment gains +2 to it’s Evasion characteristic (capped at 3).
The type of Marker added by each unit is determined by the Warband’s Cult. This is selected by the character at list building and applies to all units in that Warband. Most units in the army also have a [Chant] Special Rule which is triggered when that Regiment Chants the Battlecry. This stacks with the Cult bonus, so you generally get a unit with two bonus effects when Chanting!

Scion of Conquest by General Cross

What this generally means is that every third activation, your unit will buffed by the effects of the Chant. There’s a lot of little nuances to the system that have changed over updates, so in no particular order here are some elements to be aware of:
  • A character or unit only generates a Marker if it starts its activation on the board. You can however still Chant the Battlecry with a unit arriving from Reinforcements if there are already three Markers.
  • Only Regiments may Chant the Battlecry, but Characters still add Markers when their Command Card is drawn.
  • If you already have three Markers in the Sequence, you may replace one of the Markers with the one generated by the Regiment. You don’t have to Chant the Battlecry and can hold onto Markers for future activations.
  • Markers are not reset between Rounds, so you can ‘bank’ them up at the end of a Round to Chant the Battlecry with the first activation.
  • You only need two Markers of the same Cult to apply the Chant effect, but you still need three Markers to Chant the Battlecry. Any Markers not used are ‘Unsung’ and simply removed from the Sequence.
  • Conquest Cult Markers are wildcards. They can be used to Chant Conquest or can be used as any of the other three Cults.
  • Whilst the Chant Marker generated is determined by the unit activating, you aren’t limited to only Chanting from that Cult if you have the other Markers available (ie a Regiment that is the Famine Cult can still Chant the Battlecry using Death or War effects if there are the correct Markers).
  • The War Chant rule occurs before the Draw Event step. This lets you Chant before certain effects like Aura of Death trigger as the Active Player (you!) chooses the order of simultaneous effects.
When playing W'adrhŭn you’ll quickly find that your chanting matters significantly to your play. When ordering your Command Stack make sure to count through and plan when your chants are going to trigger on which units to get the most out of your turn. If you’re learning the faction, it can be easier to have your whole army chanting from the same Cult to simplify the Sequence before mixing and matching.

Apex Predator by General Cross

That covers the background lore, the playstyle and the main mechanic behind the W'adrhŭn Faction. Look forward to the next article, where we go through each of the Characters available for the faction and how you might use them.
