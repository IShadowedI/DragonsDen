BattleTech: Liberty's Chop Shop, Marshal 6FR

October 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Howdy ‘Mechwarriors and welcome to the first installment of Liberty’s Chop Shop here on Fronc! This is something a little different from what we usually do, between the ‘Mech overviews, the tactics articles, the tournament reports, and the conventional articles we usually focus much more on gameplay than on hobby. While we certainly have the How To Paint Everything articles, that’s really where the story on the BT hobby front kind of ends. This is, unfortunately, neglecting a very fun part of the hobby that I’ve recently come to quite enjoy:

Kitbashing. Some people use it as a way to build franken-mechs, some to build their own specific models, and others to make better- more fitting -proxies for ‘Mechs not yet in plastic.

Well, we here in ‘Mech bay 9 have a rather unorthodox approach to what we do with junk salvage. Where others see a set of thunderbolt limbs to be stripped for spare actuators or weapons we see something to be used for experimentation. What kind of experimentation?

Well, I’m glad you asked:

Today we’re making a Marshal. 

The 1st Expeditionary Marshals Round, Credit: Ascii Squid as Commissioned by Liberty

The Equipment

Any good ‘Mech-bay worth its salt has to have the proper tools, how else will you work on your machines? Well, me and my ‘Techs have scrounged up plenty of tools, new and old, that any aspiring ‘Mechtech could find use in!

To start, we need to think about the material we’re working with, modern BattleTech minis are made of plastic, more specifically PVC. PVC is a very tough material, it is why- unlike many other minis you may own - BattleTech minis can fall off the table and skitter across the room and not really have any meaningful damage. But if you’ve ever left your minis in a hot car before then you’ll have noticed something: Barrel droop. PVC is, as all plastics are, quite susceptible to heat.

Hot knives cut through butter, but hot PVC gets… cut like butter? I don’t know. All I know is that if you bring a model up to somewhere in the 50 to 55°C( °F) ballpark it cuts clean, smooth, and beautifully. There’s more than a few ways to do this from a stove top or an electric kettle to a temperature controlled bath but there’s one that I have that I think stands above the rest:

A Vevor Ultrasonic Cleaner. I personally have this 2 Liter one that I picked up from Amazon like 2 years ago to clean random small shit I had around the house. Well, and to try and strip paint off models, but mostly to clean parts and jewelry… yea… parts and jewelry.

Anyhow a good heat source isn't the only thing we’ll need for our ‘Mech work today. We’ll also need tools. For those of you that play other games where you have to build your models you may well have these things but for my fellow ‘Mechs are all I need’ players it’s a thankfully short list.

A man with a tool, you know that's unheard of, Credit: Liberty
  • A Good set of snips.
  • A comfortable hobby knife, preferably with several different swappable blades. A flat blade, an angled blade, and some odd shaped ones can make a good use for extricating specific pieces off of donor ‘Mechs.
  • A set of, preferably, cross-lock, tweezers. I don’t have them here because I definitely took very good care of mine and have absolutely no reason to have ordered more of them because I absolutely obliterated the tips on my previous set. That would be ridiculous and making such aspersions on a Marshal’s character is very dangerous.
  • A pin vise with some good drill bits.
  • A few brass round rods that are the same thickness as, or slightly less thick than, your pin vise’s bit(s). I use 1mm rod but that’s just because they were a good price and I had a bit that would work with it. Feel free to get something thicker or thinner depending on your own availability.
  • A flat sanding stick.
  • Some super glue… ya know, for gluing things together. What, did you think you were gonna hold these together with your mind powers?

Some other things that can come in use are some green stuff and some rubber tools that you don’t mind fucking up with the green stuff. For this specific build I’ll be using liquid green stuff since I just need to fill in some holes but if you’re going to be doing some sculpting of parts you couldn’t find then the more solid stuff will obviously do you better.

Now that we’ve got our starter tool set filled in, what’s next? Well, parts!

The Donors

So, we’re building a Marshal today. First things first we need to take into account what it is we’re even trying to do. This raises the question: What does a Marshal look like?

Colonial Marshals Marshal MHL-X1, Credit: Liberty

Oh yeah! Like that!

It’s got a big squared up torso with an embedded cockpit rather than a head pod, a set of chunky shoulder pauldrons, big thick sturdy legs, and a set of hands on either side with the primary gun on the right. More particularly, with the body plan remaining the same, today we’re going to be doing a build of a 6FR Marshal, rather than an X1, 2L, or 6MC. This helps alleviate some complications which is good considering this is the opening article of the series.

So, where do we start with this? Well, unfortunately there are no 1-1 ‘Mechs out there that share the same parts and aesthetics but there are several things that get damned close and when we’re aiming to make something that does a good enough job of representing the unit rather than predicting Catalyst’s definitely-going-to-happen-any-day-now-I-swear redesign, then what we want more than anything else is the vibe of a unit then the exact same thing. That kind of detail is reserved for variant conversions!

With that in mind, where do we begin? Why with the torso of course! For my Marshal build I use two, and some change, ‘Mechs! Namely an Exterminator and a Thunderbolt!

I sure hope nobody comes cuts me... Credit, Liberty

The Exterminator donates its torsos, sans an AMS, and the T-Bolt donates its limbs! Sidenote: If you’re also a Draconis player you’ll enjoy the future conversion possibility I’m going to experiment with, using the leftover parts of this to see if we can’t make a 60-RLA!

This leaves a question: What do I mean by ‘two, and some change’? Well, the Exterminator doesn’t have good torso lasers and the Thunderbolts, while they could be used for the conversion, don’t really keep the right vibe. That said I’ve previously disassembled some Wolfhounds and Pixie IICs for various purposes and I usually end up using some of their barrels for the job instead. This time around we’re going to be using the muzzle disc off a Pixie IIC!

The Cutting

First we’ve gotta break our donors down into their constituent parts. To do this we begin by making our ‘Mech stew!

MMMMM, MECH STEW, Credit: Liberty

Tonight’s recipe includes an Exterminator for body, a Thunderbolt for spice, and a touch of Phoenix Hawk IIC to give it that Clan bite! Also there’s a Warhammer in there… I don’t know why he’s there.

Anyhow, before long the parts are all up to temp and ready to be served from the stew to the cutting table! So, let’s take some parts. We start by making clean, straight cuts to separate the bits we want from the bits we don’t.

Good clean cuts, Credit, Liberty

Of note: The glue that they use on BattleTech minis does not like the heat and will end up being malleable to just break the bits free of their mounts if you give them a little twist and some force.

Also of note: Please be careful with your hobby knife and don't stab or cut yourself. I'll feel real bad if I'm the reason you like... lose a finger or some shit.

Also also of note: THE WATER IS REALLY FUCKING HOT. DON’T STICK YOUR HANDS IN THE WATER. DON’T BURN YOURSELF ON THE WATER. I’VE NEVER DONE THAT. DEFINITELY NOT. I AM SMART AND USE TWEEZERS TO MAKE SURE I DON’T HURT MYSELF. ALWAYS.

Father, why? Credit: Liberty

With those warnings and notes out of the way, we now have a pile of parts! Look at those, all laid out and sorted into use and don't use.

So, what are our next steps? Well, now that we’ve got the parts all laid out we organize the ones we’re keeping and then set about preparing them for pinning. How do we prepare for pinning, you ask? Easy, we drill some holes!

The holes need to be lined up on the points you intend to connect them to, obviously. The easiest manner I’ve found of doing this with BT minis is, once again, heat. Drill out the first set of holes; I recommend doing it on the limbs first, personally, as you’ve got less real estate to work with and thus want to use the center of those as the dictate where the holes on the torsos will go. Then, once the holes are drilled we heat the torso back up and, with a bit of rod in the drilled hole, press fit them together with a bit of force, only enough to make an indent to use as a drill start for your pin hole.

Once you’ve got the holes drilled and ready, we can move on to the next step. Now, we set to gluing everything together!

Most of a man! Credit, Liberty

That wasn’t too hard, now was it? We’ve got most of a ‘Mech now! All that’s left is to block over that LRM bin on the left torso and put our laser barrel trio over it! For this we’ll use a bit of liquid green stuff. It’ll take a few dabs, smoothing them out while they’re still wet before applying a bit more once its settled in to continue filling in the hole. You’ll likely need to do a bit of sanding to knock it all the way flat but you can get it pretty close without that if you’re very careful.

GREEEEEN, Credit: Liberty

Lastly we stick on our laser barrels and let the glue dry.

A FINISHED MAN! OH FUCK, HE'S GOT A GUN! Credit: Liberty

Tada! A finished Marshal!

Final Thoughts

I'd say that's certainly something that gives the vibe of the Periphery's fattest 55 tonner. I love him. I cherish him. He is my own first begotten son. All is right in this world.

Kitbashing is fun. I enjoy it and, hopefully, you have as well! I intend to do this with a few other builds over the next few months. While, for the time being, most of them will be Fronc related I’ve got a few other big boys I’ll do again that come from all over the sphere! If you’ve got a ‘Mech you think would be a good candidate for this I’d love to hear it so let me know and I’ll see what I can’t figure out!

Also, go read the Marshal review; it’s not necessary for the build but it makes me happy and he’s my favorite little guy. Until next time! Fair weather, good seas and happy tormenting-your-’Mechtechs-with-terrible-work-requests-ing!
