The 11th Edition 40k Tabletop Battles Roundup: Everything We Covered During Launch Week

June 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

11th edition's finally here, and with the Munitorum Field Manual, players all around the world can finally play with proper points. The first tournaments of the edition are happening this weekend, and by the time you're reading this, I'll actually be at an RTT! Excitement—as you might imagine—is beyond the pale here at the Tabletop Battles offices, with this being the final recap pre-launch. You can expect coverage of 11th to to continue as our experience deepens with the game, and no matter what faction(s) you play, there'll be deep dives arriving soon! That said, for this week, our mind was squarely on initial evaluations of points, and army standings, so whether you're looking to build lists with your existing collection, or strike out on a brand-new army project, Tabletop Battles will be the premier destination for insight into 11th. Let's catch up with the content from our analysts here this week.

All of these sections have their associated articles linked below, where we go significantly further in-depth on each of these topics. Let's get into points.

Points Overview

  • Points, Point Structure, Trends

There was clear intent in how the points were changed across the board: Armies broadly looked to see key tools from 10th disincentivized to take at 3 copies, and the problem children of the edition paid dearly for their sins. That being said, points are up across the board, and many armies will feel smaller (especially hordes like Guard & Genestealer Cults). Still, few of the shakeups are so vast as to entirely remove options from lists; as an example, even the various Chaos legions likely still pay for their first crab. The armies in 11th look like they're going to either shoot for a fairly shooty, elite Purge list, or something that can combat it, and the opening meta looks to be an interesting one (barring the dozens of C'Tan we'll likely see).

Points - Astartes

  • Astartes Points Review
Cobalt Scions Space Marines Infernus Squad. Credit: Charlie Brassley

The Angels of Death broadly made out like bandits, and there looks to be a number of viable lists from the outset. It's easy to imagine a Fulguris Task Force + Ironstorm Spearhead list overwhelming you from turn 1 with Vehicles, or Forgefather's Seekers + Librarius Conclave to make use of undercosted flamer bricks. No matter how you slice it, the core Marine army list has some solid units below what you'd expect to pay, and that alone makes for a compelling choice early on in the edition. Beyond the core Astartes, it gets a bit less exciting, with small touches to a number of the less compliant chapters. It's not unlikely the main Detachments shared between Marines are the ones you're excited about, so work towards those fundamentals and you'll probably see success.

Points - Xenos

  • Xenos Points Review
Tau Piranha. Credit: Jack Hunter

Xenos are a bit of a mixed bag, because while on one hand you have some extremely scary-looking builds of Mont'ka & Cursed Legion, most other Xenos landed in either an equivalent or worse spot to their 10th edition iterations. It's rough being a horde army, it appears, and on top of the inability to string out Genestealer Cults got hit with a number of small point hikes. Both versions of Eldar, meanwhile, suffer from having unfortunate matchups into Purge, given how trade-reliant their core gameplans tend to be. Across all factions, Xenos has some of the biggest ups & downs in terms of points.

Points - Chaos

  • Chaos Points Review, Defiler
Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

It is deeply funny that this begins with a dissertation on the Defiler, but that's the most pivotal thing about 11th for Chaos: You're probably still playing one, might consider two, and that third you purchased for a single GT will sit on a shelf. Beyond that, Chaos was a big fan of the changes to Psychic, and functional buffs to Invulnerable Save-only units with the exchange of cover's benefit. Still, a lot of the core builds of Chaos factions have been punished hard for their transgressions in the final moments of 10th, with that recency bias hitting hard. Fewer players are going to be statchecked by crabs in the new edition, but even at a reasonable point value they'll be playing a vital role across every army they're legal in.

Points - Imperium

  • Imperium Points Review

The Imperium didn't escape unscathed from the point changes, and outside of many previously-unplayable builds being considered with the new Disposition system (Say hi, Explorator Maniple) not a single of the Imperium's factions emerge with a rating from us higher than 'neutral'. That's not a great sign, but unlike Marines, some of these factions' best tools for dealing with the issues of the new edition were costed appropriately, which is going to feel expensive. I do think there's a chance both flavors of Knights, spiky or otherwise, can roll through and win some Purge mirrors, but it remains to be seen if T'au can shoot them off of the board on new layouts. Ultimately, while you're probably set to have more fun at a casual-to-RTT level, armies like the Adepta Sororitas & Grey Knights got dunked on hard here if we're talking competitive play. For some factions, receiving fairly modest changes overall (e.g. Admech) is perhaps the best spot to be in, when the designers' panopticon swivels over to judge your unit costs.

Hammer of Math: Cover

  • Ideal AP/Save Characteristics, Cover

While the zeitgeist of 11th thus far has been to jam as many [IGNORES COVER] weapons in your list as possible, how important are they actually? This Hammer of Math answers that question, and shows precisely why packing AP -1 and competent Invulnerable saves are going to be worth your time in the new edition. The fact so many units received point hikes to Lascannon-equivalent options, while their Autocannons remained at or below 10th's cost, is rather funny in this new context. Expect your swath of 4++ units to be met with just as many S9/-1/3 shots for the foreseeable future. The wheel turns ever onward.

Ruleshammer: Save Groups

  • Precision, Feel No Pain

Fast rolling is the new normal for 11th, and while it solves a great number of problems (and certainly helps when you're on a chess clock) this has inadvertently led to a buff to Precision. Vrekais dives into exactly how grouping your saves work, and why forcing your opponent's rad Character to eat all the 1s & 2s feels amazing in the new edition. This is a fantastic resource to show someone incredulous that your massed [PRECISION] traded up a good 100+ points. You will still have to slow down for Feel No Pain rolls, but given you can jump right back into the sequence afterwards it should be far faster regardless.

For posterity, I wanted to link back to all of the recaps we've done here at Tabletop Battles for 11th Edition, because seeing the rollercoaster of rules is a trip. Remember when we thought this was going to be a melee edition? Fun times. Ultimately, while I'd say that more of our predictions shook out in unexpected ways, once things crystallized around mid-May, we had a good idea of how things were going to look at 11th's outset. Of course, points change everything, but if rules are good enough, you'll pay your taxes (Looking at you, Riptide). So, if you're looking to relive spoiler season, you can track all our thoughts above, and see how right (or more likely, wrong) we were.
