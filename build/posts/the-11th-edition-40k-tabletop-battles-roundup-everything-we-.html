The 11th Edition 40k Tabletop Battles Roundup: Everything We Covered Through June 5th

June 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The gates to 11th Edition are finally open, and with them, the Core Rules. An overwhelming amount of information regarding new tools, new rules, and all the associated changes to 40k released, and we're here to recap all the site's coverage of the new edition this week. We're only two weeks out from the official release of 11th, so while there's still plenty of unknowns to muse upon regarding specific factions & detachments, we do have a grasp on the new scaffolding of the system. To help navigate the flurry of coverage here on Tabletop Battles, let's break down everything touched upon by our analysts here this week.

All of these sections have their associated articles linked below, where we go significantly further in-depth on each of these topics.

Core Concepts

  • Presentation, Actions, Army Construction, Modifiers

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

This is our 'big picture' overview, the big changes arriving to the core systems of 40k with the advent of 11th Edition. No matter what army you play, or how you play the game, the impact to things like HAZARDOUS/Hazard Rolls, Actions, & army construction. On the smaller scale, though, there's also changes here to how the very concept of math works within the confines of the game; as an example, abilities which allowed you to shrug damage, previously hampered by attacks with Melta or +D, now fully negate incoming firepower. There's other small changes, to things like Battle-shocked units now having OC "-" rather than "0", and Invulnerable saves being non-optional, but the most important takeaway is that more is similar to 10th than is different. The differences are important, and will take some getting used to, but the skills you built throughout the course of 10th remain relevant.

The Command Phase

  • Start and End, Command Abilities, Battle-shock

One of the running themes of 11th is that of 'batching'—whether that's segmenting the different types of movements or consolidations, or dividing exactly the steps of phases, 11th puts clarity first. The Command Phase itself is broken out into five steps, giving designers the room to play around with when abilities trigger, and to reduce the conflict of ordering effects that might otherwise be...nebulously simultaneous. Furthermore, among these changes is a number of buffs to Battle-shock, making it both easier to be Battle-shocked, and harder to recover from it; not only do you test at Half-Strength now, but you do so before abilities which add back models, and can't even use Insane Bravery on a unit once it's already failed. The fact Battle-shock is persistent, and Bravery can't be burnt on a future turn to save a unit already stricken with fear, means the threat of failing your first check is all the more frightening.

The Movement Phase

  • Coherency, Movement Types, Fly, Ingress, Transports

Credit: Keewa

This is a big one, as the designers have done quite a bit to try and reduce both cheese and 'gotcha' moments here in the rules themselves. First and foremost, the age of the conga line is over. Models within a squad must be set up (or end a move) no greater than 9" from one another at their farthest point, and within 2" of at least one other model in the unit; this is a huge nerf to both screens & hordes, and rules as intended also prevents sub-groups from being created (but wait for an FAQ, to be safe). Engagement Range is also changing, with 'engaged' being a characteristic, and the range itself now being a 2" you can pass through while moving (but still not end your move within). What this means practically is that the overall footprint of move-blocking at the edges of available movement is bigger, but close-up jail lists lose a ton of ground. Movement types and parts of a movement sequence are also codified now, in line with 11th's priorities, but in an odd walkback the 2" cost to pivot is gone. That's been transplanted directly onto FLY, allowing airborne units to Take to the Skies (which you'll hear yelled in many tournament halls) to move through and over anything, counting solely horizontal distance at a cost of -2" to their move. Outside of the brand-new Combat Disembark, Transports are similar to their 10th iteration, but suffer from some changes to the slipperiest squads in 40k, with out-of-phase movement and re-embarking both disallowed during the turn a unit was set up.

The Shooting Phase

  • Attacks Sequence, Indirect, Close Quarters

This piece covers the new attack sequence, and is about as close to mandatory reading for 11th as something gets; in units with mixed defensive profiles, fast rolling is the law of the land, and grouping your profiles allows for both faster attack resolution and some cool tricks. In short, the defending player groups different profiles in order (with Characters last), and allocates save rolls of 1, 2, etc in ascending sequence to those groups. What this does in a practical sense is allow for a few straggling models to hang on more often, on top of cleaning up much of the slow rolling inherent to armies like Deathwatch & Black Templars. Close-Quarters shooting is now a mode of shooting attacks themselves, either made available by things like pistols, or in being a Monster/Vehicle. Indirect, meanwhile, was hit with a massive change intended to disincentivize all but true artillery from using it: Unless you Remained Stationary that turn, and something else from your army can see the target, you're only ever hitting on 6s. Even if you do meet those criteria, you're still back to 4s at best, and that's a hard ask unless these pieces end up aggressively pointed.

The Charge & Fight Phases

  • Charging, Heroic Intervention, Pile-In, Fighting, Consolidation

Credit: @badusernametag

For fans of Age of Sigmar, rejoice, because 11th's melee takes some fairly clear cues from its high fantasy counterpart. Charge targets are chosen after you roll, something we've known about since the earliest showcase of the edition, but coupled with a flat 2" engagement range, getting in and counting eligibility is far easier for most squads. Similarly to other phases, the Fight Phase is also broken up into several components, most notably with Pile-Ins and Consolidations all happening at the start and end step of the phase, respectively. Heroic Intervention has been split between a reactive mode for 1CP, with uncapped range, and a true out-of-phase charge of up to 6" for 2CP. With Fights First being found dead in a ditch, outside of multiple charge targets having access to it, this means midboard scrums of multiple squads fighting through walls is going to be common. One massive charge for melee sequencing comes in the form of the 'Overrun Fight', where a unit that began the Fight Phase engaged but is chosen to fight with no current targets can make an additional pile-in, giving you substantial reach if you blow up a screen.

Terrain & Objectives

  • Terrain Categories, Cover, Hidden, Objective Secured

11th arrives with one of the largest changes to how 40k boards have looked since 6th Edition, with a shift back towards terrain itself being the Objectives. This coverage dives into the differences between Terrain Area & Terrain Features, which canonize the footprints we've come to expect from 10th Edition play. The verticality of terrain is also highlighted, with 11th taking another stab at making Plunging Fire relevant. On the one hand, Hidden does allow for smaller blocks of things like Infantry to remain safe and stage, but on the other, things that aren't Infantry greatly appreciate the fact that toeing in to a Terrain Area allows you to see through it. There's an arms race to vision here, and it's something we're all excited to see play out at top tables. As for Objectives, 'sticky' is being shortened to Objective Secured, which we'll see clarified across datasheets, and the names often used for their position on the board (Home, Central, Expansion) are the official titles in Chapter Approved. These being delineated allow for clearer mention in missions, something greatly appreciated given the massive swath of Primaries in 11th.

Special Unit Types

  • Leaders, Supports, Towering, Aircraft

On top of the changes to fast rolling, attached Characters are looking great in 11th. Not only are they officially chosen during army construction, but this allows for the squad's abilities to be shared with the Character, and vice versa, even if one is destroyed. Similarly, because they operate as a unified squad, you can add back models if solely the Characters survive (and they often will, due to the aforementioned fast rolling). Mobility is increased for Monsters/Vehicles by way of them being able to move through any model that isn't a M/V during movement, not just friendly ones; for one particular class of Monster/Vehicle, it's especially so. Aircraft are being once again re-examined by Games Workshop after their dearth of playability in 10th, being changed to support pieces which dive in solely via Ingress, and are forced to dip out at the end of your opponent's turn, being largely uninteractable. There's maybe a world where these see play (looking at you, Night Scythe) at low enough cost, using their engagement range to drop difficult-to-charge bodies along the sides of the table, but this looks to be another edition where flyers are finicky.

Core Stratagems & Abilities

  • New and Returning Stratagems & Keywords



The bedrock of reactive play has remained fairly recognizable in 11th, with the vast majority of the strats you know and love returning in some form, with the exception of Go to Ground (which will hardly be missed). One sizeable change is to that of Overwatch, which is now solely in the Movement Phase, and at the end once everything's shifted around and been Ingressed; this is a sidegrade, leaning nerf, that both removes bait potential but also prevents the threat of its usage from freezing a near-death unit. Larger changes arrive to core abilities, with Psychic now having the upside of ignoring negative modifiers to hit and Heavy allowing for 3" of movement, among other things. The more impactful changes are to how both Stratagems and abilities interact with other core rules (e.g. Fights First), which is worth brushing up on given the interconnectedness of 11th's systems.

Force Dispositions and Missions

Asymmetric Primary Missions is a hallmark of 11th Edition, and will be a defining factor on how lists are built, when planning for scoring paths. Given each has five potential matchup combinations, you'll have to plan for each of these accordingly, as Dispositions are locked in at listbuilding for events. We've rated each of the associated missions on a 1-5 scale, and gone in-depth on how each player scores; there's a shocking degree of complexity to these, so it looks like one of the major learning curves for the new edition. As it stands, certain factions are going to consider 1DP Detachments to access Purge the Foe at the outset of 11th, but these of course are ripe for rebalancing if things slant too far in one direction. Once all of the Dispositions are revealed for the existing Detachments, this is your bible for maxing Primary.

The Terrain Area Set

  • Footprints in the New Edition

Credit: Wings

Something we'll be seeing purchased by many TOs is the new official footprint set, something that's welcome in the world where they're defined across all levels of play. They work with the existing small L-shaped terrain features available in countless Kill Team boxes, but moreover, the smaller pieces allow for flavorful scatter (something previously unused). One specific thing of note is that the bumps along certain edges of these areas do matter for toeing in at present, which does mean they'll need to be considered when purchasing your set, whether from Games Workshop or a third party. On the whole, so long as these are priced well, they should be a great get.

The Armageddon Boxed Set

Credit: Games Workshop.

Armageddon drops soon, and with it the first models of 11th Edition. We were lucky enough to get our hands on the new kits, and things are looking fantastic on the modelling front! The starter set is going to be one of the bestsellers of the entire edition (as it always is, barring AoS Dominion), and we've got ample tutorials on how folks at the Tabletop Battles offices have built & painted their boxes. As far as the rules for these units go, while the Marine half is a solid continuation of what the faction's normally about, the Ork side has a host of new options that look to be shaking up listbuilding. As the points are released, we'll touch on these again soon.

Dominatus

  • The New Way to Play Narrative

As part of the efforts to unify the narrative & matched play, many of the changes made to the core systems of 11th lean into the thematics of 40k, meaning all games played should feel more akin to what you'd expect in the grim darkness of the far future. That said, Dominatus is releasing as a narrative supplement to this paradigm shift, as a deck of cards to guide you through a mini-campaign that could probably be finished in a week or less. Our feelings on Dominatus, especially given it looks to be replacing Crusade, are mixed to say the least.
