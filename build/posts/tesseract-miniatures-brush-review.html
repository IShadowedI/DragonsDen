Tesseract Miniatures Brush Review

May 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With our thanks to Tesseract Miniatures for providing their brushes for review.

Tesseract Miniatures is a commission painting studio in Leicestershire run by artist Stu with a sideline of selling busts, and has recently branched out into producing their own quality sable brushes. As one of Tabletop Battles' resident brethren of brush botherers, one beloved of the bristle, I love to try out different brushes - from the cheap and cheerful art-store own-brand, to the absurdly expensive "boutique" offerings. There's just something about the way a brush feels in your hand, the springiness of the bristles, how the tip is shaped and held, that tells you how good it is - and it's not necessarily the most expensive brushes that give you that feeling.



Tesseract Miniatures' brushes come in three different sizes of sable round, and six different sizes of soft drybrush (charmingly referred to as Ticklesticks - I have to wonder whether Tesseract were thinking of Ken Dodd when they came up with that name). The Rounds are available as a set of three for £36, or individually at £12 for the smallest and £14.50 for the largest), while the Ticklesticks are available as a set of 6 for £30, alternatively, there's an all-in bundle that sells the whole whack for £56, for a saving of about a tenner compared to buying separately. The brushes also come in a very snazzy Magenta-Teal colour, and anyone who knows how I paint knows that I love me some magenta and teal.

I have to admit to not being sentimental (or, sadly, gentle) when it comes to brushes. Brushes are, ultimately, a consumable resource that are subject to wear, and they do degrade with use as paint gets into the ferrule or bristles fall or are pulled out. If you're particularly precious about your brushes, you can make them last a long time, but that's just not how I paint. As a result of my somewhat negligent brush treatment, value for money is as important a consideration as quality itself. I’ll never buy another Raphael 8404 or Winsor and Newton Series 7. “But Keewa!” I hear you cry, “You paint miniatures for a living, shouldn’t you be using the very best brushes you can, doesn’t it reflect in the final product?” Well… not quite.

So then, how are the Tesseract Miniatures brushes? How do they fare against the increasingly crowded market for mid-to-high tier brushes? In terms of brush sizes, the Tesseract Miniatures offering is very restricted compared to the other brands, with three sable rounds compared to the fifteen sable rounds in the Artis Opus catalogue. This isn’t necessarily a bad thing, though; it’s a generally accepted truism that for most miniature painting, you only really need a good size 2, 1, and 0, and while Tesseract doesn’t include a numerical size descriptor for their brushes, the three sizes they sell are pretty much equivalent to those. Do you really need a size 000 or a size 6? Unless you’re painting 6mm models or 1/6th scale large figures, the answer is almost certainly “no”. 

The bristles are high-quality sable with the right amount of snap for miniature painting, letting you paint both crisp freehand lines and solid blacklining without the bristles separating on you and making a mess. The bristles of the Ticklesticks are much softer (although if you fail to keep them clean, they’ll go hard really quickly, so get yourself some brush soap) with a beautifully swishy profile that makes them a very good choice for drybrushing compared to the synthetic polymer bristles on most of the cheap makeup brushes that people often tout as a replacement for “actual” drybrushes. Unsurprisingly, they’re also really good for dusting your miniatures (which you should do before you start painting).

Finally, the value proposition, the Tesseract brushes are not cheap, but neither are they super expensive - a Size 2 Raphael 8404 would run you about £22, the equivalent Tesseract brush costs £14.50, and I’m fairly confident that in a blind test, painters would have a lot of difficulty trying to discern them from one another. 

I’ve used the Tesseract brushes very heavily since they arrived a few months ago, painting pretty much everything I’ve done since with those magenta wonders, and they have yet to let me down. If you want a premium brush from a friendly small company, and at a reasonable price, give Tesseract a look. I don’t think you’ll be disappointed.
