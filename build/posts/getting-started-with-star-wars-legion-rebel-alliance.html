Getting Started with Star Wars Legion: Rebel Alliance

July 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone, to another episode in our Star Wars Legion series, where we cover the fundamentals of the game! This time, we're taking a look at one of the core factions of the game, the Rebel Alliance.

Thank you to Asmodee for sponsoring a series of articles on Star Wars: Legion.

What Is the Rebel Alliance?

The Rebel Alliance is a ragtag group of dissenters and defectors who have coalesced in order to overthrow the tyrannical government known as the Galactic Empire as well as attempting to instill the previous government system the Galactic Republic had prior to Emperor Palpatine's rule. They utilize guerilla tactics, espionage, and all manner of splinter groups loosely connected to a larger main force in order to cause havoc for the Empire on multiple fronts. The main force created by Mon Mothma and Bail Organa have multiple generals and heroes amongst their ranks that keep their hope alive, including but not limited to General Leia Organa, Luke Skywalker, Han Solo, and many others. 

Along with their main fleet come splinter groups and covert agents causing havoc for the Empire on their own way such as Jyn Erso and the group who fought in the battle of Scarif, Luthen Rael and his network of spies and moles in the Empire, Saw Gerrera and his partisans whose tactics go beyond what the main Rebel fleet approves of, as well as many other unnamed groups. These splinter groups sometimes don't even know of the main force and just want to escape Imperial Rule. The Rebellion as a force is built on hope and collectively fight for a brighter tomorrow.

Essential Media

Original Star Wars Trilogy: These films are not only the birthplace of Star Wars but show off some of the largest conflicts the Rebellion endures as well as the path many of their heroes follow to become the legends they are now. The original films also establish the reason for their existence and why you should care.

Rogue One: A prequel that timeline wise happens right before A New Hope (and leads directly into A New Hope with one of the best scenes in the entire franchise) that is a bit grittier than the rest of the media in this list. The story is more focused on a smaller group and how they impact the Rebels fight against the Empire, and serves as an introduction to Cassian, K2, and Jyn Erso who are characters available in Star Wars Legion.

Star Wars Rebels: An animated series that goes over the creation of a Rebel cell and their adventures culminating in their fight against Admiral Thrawn himself. This series not only provides backstory for characters such as Sabine Wren, but also contains multiple characters available in Star Wars Legion from across multiple of the factions including but not limited to Thrawn, Kallus, and Maul.

Andor: The final essential media I am recommending is Andor, a prequel to Rogue One that shows the more independent side of the Rebellion that is separate from the main fleet. It also touches on the creation of the Rebellion before Mon Mothma openly becomes their leader. It’s an even grittier show than the Rogue One movie that gives a perspective we don’t see in other media. Units that were inspired from this show would be the Rebel Sleeper Cell as well as the titular character Cassian Andor.

How Do They Play?

The Rebellion is an army that plays very much like they do in Star Wars media. They rely on units that are fast, hit hard, but have a habit of dying quickly on the tabletop. Cheap units like your common Rebel Soldiers will normally be backed up by units that deal massive amounts of damage in decisive attacks, hoping to wipe out their targets before they can strike back.

Boasting range 2-3 guns across the army with Black dice almost universally with a surge to hit the army has good damage. The units in the army are usually speed 2 with some exceptions so they are not having too many difficulties getting to where they need to. The rebels are almost universally a white defense dice army, but to make up for their poor defense dice their units usually come with some sort of defensive ability such as Agile which 

Another core part of the army is the swath of named characters they have access to. These characters will drastically change the armies playstyle, whether you lean into your defensive options with Leia and her ability to let her troopers keep Dodge tokens between rounds or Han Solo’s command cards to interrupt your opponents plans and even force them to choose a new command card on their turn. The Rebellion is flooded with options for their Commanders and Operatives, with each one feeling completely unique from the others.

Strengths

  • High damage attacks: Multiple units with 1-2 black dice per attack and 4-6 minis per unit as well as surge to attack on most of your units means that your attacks will be doing damage, if not eliminating units entirely from the field. While there isn’t a lot of Pierce in the army to try and cancel your opponents defense dice, the amount of dice available in your attacks more than makes up for it.
  • Fast units: The only speed 1 units in the army are the Rebel Veterans, the MKII Medium Blaster, and the A-A5 Speeder Truck (which is on such a big base it might as well be speed 2) while everything else is speed 2 or speed 3. The army is not lacking keywords that generate tokens while you are moving such as Tactical which generates an Aim or Agile which generates a Dodge, so move and shoot to your hearts content or even double move since you will be rewarded for it.
  • Heroes with major game impact: Rebels characters all drastically impact the way the army plays. Whether it is heroes like Commander Luke on Tauntaun and Leia provide the army with more token generation and command cards that act as force multipliers or Ashoka who can deal enough damage in melee to completely wipe units off the field you are not wanting for options. You also have multiple characters who are upgrades for units so they can lead their own squads into battle without taking up your Commander and Operative slots, freeing you up to bring your favorites.

Weaknesses

  • Fragile: The entire army aside from very few exceptions have a white defense dice. There is only one success and one surge out of all 6 sides on a white defense dice so not getting shot at is the best plan for rebels, or stacking defensive abilities like Nimble providing you dodge tokens when you use a dodge token. Even with the defensive abilities, any defense rolls you are rolling are more likely to roll a blank than anything else which severely hinders the army's defensive potential.
  • Poor order control: many of the army's command cards only provide 1-3 orders to your units. The army also lacks multiple sources of order providing abilities such as Coordinate which lets a unit issue an order to another unit if they are within range 1 or other abilities that allow you to give out orders to units or Direct which would allow a unit to issue an order to another unit.
  • Poor Courage: Most units in the army are courage 1 and most of your commanders are also courage 2. This means that units don’t hold up well against getting focused by your enemy attacks or suppressive weapons. There are a couple sources of Inspire which lets you remove suppression on that unit's turn, but outside of that there aren't a lot of ways to mitigate suppression.

How Do I Get Into Them?

As with all the factions, there is a starter box that contains everything you need to play Star Wars Legion: Enough models for a 600 point list, dice, tokens, and measuring sticks, all at a reduced price compared to buying them individually. With that in mind, the Rebel Alliance starter box contains the following model-wise:

  • 1 Commander Luke Skywalker 
  • 22 Rebel Troopers
  • 7 Rebel Commandos
  • 6 Wookie Warriors
Credit: Atomic Mass Games

We covered a Starter set list for the Rebel Alliance, as well as the other factions, in a previous article here, along with a quick overview of how the list looks to perform. 

Starter Box List

Commander: Luke Skywalker with Force Reflexes, Improvised Orders, Up Closer and Personal, and Prepared Positions

Corps: Rebel Troopers with DLT-20A Trooper, Rebel Trooper Squad, and Targeting Scopes

Special Forces: Rebel Commandos with Proton Charge Saboteur, Up Close and Personal, and Recon Intel

Special Forces: Wookie Warriors with Bowcaster Wookie, Up Close and Personal, Tenacity, Recon Intel, and Concussion Grenades

Support: Rebel Commandos Strike Team with DH-447 Sniper and Dug In

What Else Do They Have?

Ewoks are a major fighting force from the battle of Endor, and to represent that on the tabletop they have their own battle force as well. Characters like Logray and Wicket are joined by their Golden God C3PO and your favorite Wookie commandeering an ATST. Ewoks are a funny little unit with poor dice but large numbers that can flood the opponents board faster than they can deal with them.

Since the Rebellion is such a major force, many of the Star Wars shows have had their characters introduced to Legion. Characters such as Cassian Andor from the Andor TV show,  Ashoka and Sabine from the Rebels cartoon and the Ashoka series. There is also the crew of the Ghost from Star Wars Rebels coming out later this year as of the writing of this article.

Rebels also have the most access to mercenaries in Star Wars Legion. These mercenaries usually operate as lone wolves or guns for hire and can add to the army to serve more specialized purposes. These are characters such as Din Djarin and Grogu and IG-11 from The Mandalorian, the version of Boba Fett that appears in his eponymous television show, the Pyke syndicate from Mauls Shadow Collective, and of course the infamous pirate Hondo Onaka and his band of Weequay pirates.

Battle Forces

Before we move on to expanding on the starter set, we should go over the Battle Forces available to the Rebellion. Battle Forces are a different way of building a list for your army, they come with restrictions on what you can take but can provide unique army rules or even entire command cards for your army to take that are different from the faction specific command cards. They are similar to sub-factions in a way, coming with a list of pros and cons. The Rebellion currently has 2 available Battle Forces, the Bright Tree Village and the Echo Base Defenders.

Bright Tree Village: Remember when I discussed Ewoks earlier in this article, well this is where you can play more than just the amount available during regular army building. This Battle Force also includes C3PO the Golden God, Logray, as well as Chewbacca stealing an ATST for combat against the Empire. The only Rebel units allowed for you to take are Commander Han Solo, Commander Leia, operative Chewbacca, Rebel Troopers, and finally Rebel Commandos. The special rule for deciding to use a Bright Tree Village Battle Force is that you must bring at least 2 rebel units and your rebel commanders get Allies of Convenience so they can give orders to mercenary units. A downside is all your Ewok affiliated units remove 1 fewer suppression during the End Phase. This Battle Force is the only way to amass an army of teddy bears to drown your opponent in furry bodies.

Echo Base Defenders: This Battle Force represents those brave rebels who fought to delay the Empire in the battle of Hoth whilst the Rebellion escaped the planet. This battle force doesn’t have any special rules, but it does have access to 3 new command cards limited to just this Battle Force. The available units are Commander Luke and Han Solo as well as their versions on Tauntaun, Leia, Chewbacca (sadly only on foot), the Rebel Officer, R2D2, the Rebel Agent, Rebel Veterans and the Mk II Medium Blaster, Tauntauns, the 1.4 FD Lasercannon, last but certainly not least the T-47 Airspeeder. This Battle Force has a much longer list of allowed units than some of the other Battle Forces as well as having some powerful synergies with Support units using their powerful command cards. This battleforce is also the only way to take a unit version of all your favorite characters from the original trilogy.

Expanding the Starter Set

From the starter box you can expand your force in multiple ways. The Heroes of the Rebellion pack contains Leia Organa, Han Solo on foot, Chewbacca, Luke Skywalker as both his commander and operative variations. Some additional Rebel Troopers are a great thing to get as well since you will need at minimum 3 for a 1000 point standard list. From here you can look into getting your other Special Forces options such as more Rebel Commandos and Wookies, or Rebel Sleeper Cells. If you're into vehicles, Rebels do have the AT-RT, the T47 Airspeeder, and the X-34 Landspeeder which provide fast and hard hitting vehicles to the army.

Final Thoughts

The Rebellion is the army I play the most, and have brought to competition multiple times. Over the time I have played them I have had to learn how to position my units well, and play to their strengths. Since the army is frail defensively, they heavily rely on proper planning of your turn and how to utilize your units for maximum effectiveness. With so many options and combinations of characters and units, they are fun to build lists with and their playstyle is rewarding when all the pieces finally start working in tandem with each other and you start making 10 men feel like 100.
