Trench Crusade Warband Focus: The Red Brigade

September 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone to another Trench Crusade Warband focus! This week we round out the New Antioch with everyone’s favourite crimson warriors: The Red Brigade. Prepare for a story of grief, revenge, and the goodest of doggos. 

Credit: Westfalia Miniatures

The Lore

The Red Brigade is comprised of volunteers who share a common grief: The grief of loss. All members, from the very founding by St. Ernest, have experienced a profound loss as a result of the Great War. All members wear armour tarnished with the blood of a fallen comrade as proof of a “Blood Pledge.” Under the direct command of the Duke, before they set out on the most dangerous missions that regular units cannot do, they dine with the Duke and the elite in the “Feast of the Dead Men.” To be a member of the Red Brigade is to know your life is at its end. Reality simply needs to catch up with the inevitable truth.

The Variant

Before we get into things, let’s quickly break down the Variant’s rules: 

  • Displeasure of the Church: You may only bring up to one Trench Cleric and one Sniper Priest, or up to two Sniper Priests and no Trench Clerics. 
  • Fury of the Saint Ernest: Your Lieutenant may purchase the “Gunslinger” Skill (found in the post-game skill section) for 5 Ducats. Additionally, this warband may purchase Molotov Cocktails for 5 Ducats, but may not have a Troop Flag.
  • Glory Hounds: You may purchase Trench Dogs of the Red Brigade, and any Trench Dog bought in a campaign via Glory becomes a Trench Dog of the Red Brigade. You cannot have more Trench Dogs than other models, and the first two Glorious Deeds performed by a Trench Dog of the Red Brigade give 2 Glory Points, instead of 1. 
  • No Retreat: No model may perform Retreat, unless a special rule forces them to do so. 
  • Remember the Fallen: You may skip the Reinforcement and Exploration post-game steps in a Campaign to recover all possessions of models that died as a result of the Trauma step. 
  • Wear and Tear: You start the game with 1 Blood Marker per 200 Ducats of the total cost of the Warband. The opposing player distributes the Blood Markers however they like, with the stipulation that each model must have 1 Blood Marker on them before a 2nd can be applied. 

Before we get into what this all means, there are a couple of important rules to this variant that aren’t directly in the rule changes: A “Crimson Communicant,” which is a new (albeit a variant of) Communicant style Tough model. 

All in all, what we have is a strong, campaign-focused variant that can quickly snowball Glory acquisition through the clever use of Trench Dogs. Sure, the Wear and Tear rule is a downside, but it can be negated with medi-kits (*cough* Med Dogs *cough*). There aren’t many non-campaign-focused rule changes, but a big highlight is the Gunslinger ability for your Lieutenants, pushing them to an aggressive dual-pistol-wielding maniac on the board.  

Unique Battlekit

Blood Cloak - 5 Ducats - Limit: 4

A simple piece of Equipment that gives the wearer the Skirmisher Keyword. Skirmisher has some niche uses, and can help get you out of a potential charge, but is often unreliable. Still, nice and cheap piece of kit, if you really want to give an extra bit of survivability to something important, even if it won’t always help. 

Red Banner - 2 Glory - Limit: 1

A replacement for the lost Troop Flag, this 1-handed Flag gives the Block and Held Keywords, but also grants a +2 to Morale checks as long as the bearer is not Down or Out of Action. 

Key Units

Lieutenant 

Credit: Westfalia Miniatures

As always in any New Antioch warband, your Lieutenant is your key model, with fantastic combat stats, and great activation shenanigans. In Red Brigade, your Lieutenant is able to grab the Gunslinger skill for just 5 Ducats, which allows them to make two shooting actions if they have are weapons with the Pistol Keyword. If you immediately thought, “the Automatic Pistol has the Pistol Keyword,” then you’re right on the money, as a Lieutenant with double Automatic Pistols can shoot both of them in ranged Combat. 

Ideal Starting Loadout:
  • Automatic Pistol, Automatic Pistol
  • Standard Armour

Trench Dogs of the Red Brigade

Credit: Westfalia Miniatures

As the bestest of friends, as well as demanders of pets, Trench Dogs are usually useful units to pad out your model count, as well as cheap screeners to disrupt infiltrating units getting a useful position. However, in Red Brigade, you can pick from a bunch of useful abilities to make each of your four-legged friends more unique and useful to your playstyle. The Trench Dogs of the Red Brigade may have Grenades or Molotov Cocktails for 5 Ducats (more on that later). 

For basic abilities, we start with Dogs of War, which stipulates that your Dogs can’t be promoted, but do count towards Morale Checks. Next up is Four Paws, which grants a +1 Dice to Climbing, Jumping, Falling, or making a Dash, which is very useful on an 8” movement model. Next up is Grenade Harness, which allows other Non-Trench Dog models to make a Shoot Action as long as they are within 1” of a Trench Dog model with this ability. Loyal Hound allows up to two Trench Dogs of the Red Brigade, along with up to two non-Trench Dog models, to form a fireteam. Each Trench Dog must be with a Non-Trench Dog model. These two Fireteams are in addition to the regular Fireteams, and you can still use the Concentrated Attack ability. For the last of the regular abilities, Teeth and Claws allows a Trench Dog of the Red Brigade to make a melee attack without needing a Melee Weapon.

For the Special Training ability, you have to give each Trench Dog one of the following abilities for a 5 Ducat cost:

  • Attack Dog: +1 Dice to the Melee Characteristic
  • Guard Dog (Limit: 1): Enemy infiltrators cannot be deployed within 16” of an enemy Guard Dog unit. Additionally, it may charge an enemy model it cannot see. 
  • Mercy Dog (Limit: 2): Mercy Dogs may use medi-kits. Additionally, as long as the Mercy Dogs start their movement in contact with a friendly model, they may move that model along with them when the Trench Dog makes a normal move, reducing the movement by half when doing so, as long as the other friendly model is outside of 1” of an enemy unit.

Ok, so that is a lot to take in, but putting it all together, it makes your doggos incredibly useful, allowing them to fill in as speedy utility pieces that can block off parts of the board to your opponent, or act as a potential setup unit as part of a Fireteam. These are a great unit to have in your pocket, and at just 35 Ducats for the base, it’s a nice unit to have.

Crimson Communicant

Credit: Westfalia Miniatures

If getting a pair of small companions wasn’t enough of a reason to join the Red Brigade, what about a large (but equally cuddly?) companion? Crimson Communicants join the grim Red Brigade to help forget the loss and despair they feel, able to channel the sorrow of the Second Meta-Christ through the power of the Atonement Bell they always carry. 

The Crimson Communicant comes in at 75 Ducats, and has the regular 6” move, a -1 Ranged Characteristic, and a +1 Melee Characteristic. They are Elite and have the Strong and Tough keywords. Their special ability is Strength through Pain, where they gain a +1 to their Melee Characteristic for each Blood Marker they have. Secondly, an enemy cannot spend Blood Markers to add - Dice to a success roll for a Melee Attack Action. 

As mentioned, they come with the Atonement Bell, which is a 1-handed weapon they always have. A Crimson Communicant may use the Bell to make an off-hand attack that moves an enemy model D3”, instead of making an injury roll. The movement must be in a straight line, and can be used to make them fall, retreat, climb, jump, etc. You can’t use the Bell on a retreating model.

Whilst the Bell is a nice-to-have, it does take a 1-handed slot, so that means no shield-and-weapon combo. However, getting another model with Tough in this warband is fantastic. 

Ideal Starting Loadout: 
  • Two-handed Hammer
  • Standard / Reinforced Armour 
  • Medi-kit

The List 

Red Dawn

Faction: The Red Brigade

Rating: 699 Ducats | 0 Glory

Patron: None

## Elites ##

Lieutenant - 130D

Upgrades & Choices: Gunslinger

Battlekit: Automatic Pistol, Automatic Pistol, Standard Armour

Crimson Communicant - 105D

Battlekit: Atonement Bell, Great Hammer/Maul, Medi-Kit, Standard Armour

Sniper Priest - 69D

Battlekit: Pistol, Pistol, Frag Grenades

## Troops ##

Combat Engineer - 125D

Battlekit: Submachine Gun, Shovel, Engineer Body Armour, Satchel Charge

Mechanized Heavy Infantry - 120D

Battlekit: Heavy Shotgun, Reinforced Armour, Satchel Charge

Yeoman - 60D

Battlekit: Flamethrower

Yeoman - 50D

Battlekit: Musical Instrument, Molotov Cocktail

Guard Dog - 40D

So this list has a lot up front and doesn’t utilise a lot of cheap Dogs, but for me personally, I would rather bring my expensive units up front and acquire my cheaper units further down the line. We do have our three Elites, as well as the icon Combat Engineer and MHI combo. Lastly, with a single yeoman with a Flamethrower, we can at least tickle our opponents. Lastly, our cheap chaff is a Yeoman that can still contribute if needed with the cheap Molotov Cocktail, and the Guard dog is to stop early Infiltrator shenanigans.

As the campaign goes on, you can get cheap Attack Dogs to complement your other units, as well as get another Yeoman with a Flamethrower to make another power pair. 

Final Thoughts

The Reg Brigade is an incredibly unique faction that really excels in Campaign play, thanks to the use of Trench Dogs gaining you more glory. The Wear and Tear rule, whilst quite a hindrance, simply balances out the sheer amount of snowballing you can achieve whilst in a campaign. The use of Trench Dogs, as well as a larger Communicant model, adds a lot of versatility to your list, allowing you to approach games in some refreshing ways that can’t be found in other variants. All in all, the Red Brigade is great for Campaign players who love the core New Antioch formula but are looking to diversify how they approach their games.
