Lore to Table: Building Imperial Fists with the 11th Edition Codex

September 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
The new release of the 11th edition Codex: Space Marines gave us a ton of new Detachments to play with, including a mix of generic and chapter-specific options to choose from. In this article series, we're going to be looking at different chapters and how to represent them on the table, talking about different combinations and lore-accurate forces.

I personally never loved the decision to jettison subfactions from the game. I understand why it happened; the idea of your army's rules being tied to a paint scheme definitely chafes some players and isn't ideal for competitive events. But on the other hand, I think it's common for players to associate themselves with a chapter or legion. You aren't a "Space Marines" player, you're an "Imperial Fists" player or a "Space Wolves" player. You don't want to run an army of yellow assault marines; you want cool rules for representing your Imperial Fists on the table the way the lore does.

Hence, this article series. In this series we're going to look at different ways to represent chapters and their forces on the table, using different Detachments and units. Today we're starting with the Imperial Fists. In addition to looking at current armies, we'll also play around with the occasional Legends unit where it makes sense.

What Kinds of Armies Do We Want to Build?

The Imperial Fists are traditionally the defenders of the Imperium, masters of siege warfare who build and maintain fortifications such as the Imperial Palace. They're also known for their discipline, and have had rules around that in the past. They were also among the first chapters to use Centurion warsuits in their ranks and, depending on what you believe, Centurions may have been originally intended as an exclusive unit for the chapter.

I think that there are fundamentally three or four different forces we want to look at for the Imperial Fists:

  • Trench Fighters - An army that's heavy on bulky gravis or terminator units and represents the army fighting to the last man over key defensive positions, entrenching themselves as they get dug in.
  • Bolter Discipline - An Intercessor-heavy force which focuses on the chapter's renowned use of synchronized, high-volume bolt weapon fire and tactical coordination.
  • Exploitation Force - Based on a piece of the Sternhammer Strike Force concept in the 7th edition Angels of Death book, this is a force dedicated to take advantage of enemy weaknesses and force open holes in their lines. This one's more about shooting.

This gives us three concepts to build around; a take-and-hold force heavy on Terminators and first company units, a force filled with Intercessors firing at short and medium range, and a devastator-heavy force led by Tor Garadon pounding an opponent with ranged weapons.

Imperial Fists Captain Lysander. Credit: Jack Hunter

Trench Fighters

The Core of a Sternhammer Strike Force is a force of heavily armored marines, geared up for brutal close quarters combat and grinding advances. This is where we'll want to really make use of Lysander and his Detachment, running Ceramite Sentinels as our base. On top of that we'll add Terminator Storm Force to create an army that runs 30 terminators, give or take.

This is the kind of army I love building for Take and Hold: Just running a ton of terminators to clog up the middle of the table. The concept here is pretty easy: Build around Lysander and a group of ten Terminators + Ancient, using his -1 to be hit with Cover for a unit which is -2 to be hit, has 4W each, -1 to be wounded with Stand to the End, and the option for -1 damage with Tactical Dreadnought Fortitude.

The core here are two big Terminator bricks, giving you 20 to work with total. That's a huge amount of beef to clog up the middle of the table - that Lysander unit is a ridiculous brick of meat to put on one or both objectives. It's difficult to shift, and having 4W models lets you sail past even 4-damage threats. And if you do come across one of those, you can give them -1 damage for 2 CP, making even those only half as useful against your unit. You can reliably tank the Lion for a couple of turns with these guys, and if you're lucky you might even get a few shots back into him.

Double Terminator bricks are a popular army trope right now in competitive play and that doesn't figure to change with the new book - 30+ wounds of T6, 2+/4++ save models are hard to chew through, and that combo pairs well with the primary mission on Take and Hold. We're just expanding on that concept here and getting more out of it with Darnath Lysander.

Here Lysander and his unit are fine to walk to midtable most games - they always count as entrenched, so you don't need to worry about when or where they come in, but the other unit won't be as fortunate. The Terminator Librarian goes in the Land Raider with his unit, and standard Terminators a better getting benefit out of both of his psychic abilities than Assault Terminators. Heavy Bolter Eradicators are just good value, and while scouts don't always have a place in the trenches, we still need fast, versatile units to help us score secondary objectives from time to time.

The Legends Version

We don't have points or Legends Datasheets yet for some of the things passing into Legends with the new release but I don't have a ton of changes I'd make here. I'd strongly consider swapping out the Eradicators, the Terminator Librarian + Terminators, and Land Raider Redeemer for Centurions and a Vindicator just to give it more 7th edition/pre-Primaris flavor if I wanted to go that route, and you're always welcome to swap Intercessors for Tactical marines in that same vein. Running 25 Terminators and a Land Raider already has enough old school feel, but I'm a sucker for Vindicators.

Imperial Fists Primaris Intercessors. Credit: Jack Hunter

Bolter Discipline

The Imperial Fists are equally known for their unparalleled skill with bolters and their ability to drown enemies in a hail of overlapping fields of coordinated fire. This is also a trait they passed down to Crimson Fists, and so I'd also consider this our "Crimson Fists" Detachment option, given there's no Pedro Kantor - though we can certainly look at him again in the Legends section.

There are no formal rules for Bolter Discipline these days, but with Gravis Linebreaker Force, Devastator Brethren, and Tacticus Firestorm Force, we can approximate a pretty good setup for making bolter marines do real damage. This army can stay mobile while putting out an absolutely punishing hail of midrange, high-AP firepower, and Tor Garadon can help them get there by pointing at targets and stripping them of cover. I initially wanted to include Boltstorm Aggressors here but they're pretty bad in the new book, so I've given Tor Garadon a unit of ten Heavy Intercessors instead.

The big downside to this list is taking on heavy armor and vehicle targets - bolters aren't great for that without some additional LETHAL HITS boosts but the good news is that you have the Annihilating Force and Storm of Fire Stratagems to help with those, giving you lethal hits and AP boosts in Devastator Doctrine, respectively. Tor Garadon is also good for that in a pinch, and anything that isn't a knight should be within reach just using sheer volume.

The Legends Version

As above, we don't have the new Legends handover yet, but my immediate thoughts here were: 1) Pedro Kantor once he finally gets his Legends Datasheet, as a way to make this Crimson Fists, and 2) Devastator Centurions. That said, I think this list gets the point across pretty well as-is, and doesn't feel like it's really missing anything from Legends at the moment. If you wanted Tactical Squads over Intercessors, I guess go for it. But they're better off as Legendary Proxies, in my opinion.

Imperial Fists Primaris Eradicators. Credit: Jack Hunter

Exploitation Force

In this final list, we're building a Devastator force. We want something that hits hard, and really makes an opponent feel our firepower in their bones. This is supposed to be the fists exploiting their ability to find weak points in enemy armor and widen those gaps to massive wounds. Here we want to take good shooting units with strong firepower, going for quality over volume, in contrast to the Bolter Discipline force.

We're going to use Tor Garadon again here, because he fits into our plan of "shooting the crap out of key targets." We'll opt for the Ceramite Sentinels Detachment for the ability to re-roll wound rolls of 1, and because we're taking Garadon. We also want the Gravis Linebreaker Force Detachment again because we want to be able to move and still get +1 to hit. You've got two Impulsors here which are perfect for holding 3-man Gravis units like the Eradicators, while Tor Garadon's unit just kind of walks. It's a heavy-hitting unit with lots of OC which is capable of shrugging off attacks with Indefatigable Fortitude. Meanwhile, the Repulsor Executioners give you an anti-vehicle punch you didn't have in our Bolter Discipline list.

The Legends Version

I probably replace the RepExes with Vindicators in the Legends version. They're cheaper and do lots of damage without wasting points on the other piddly guns, and while they're not as good without oaths, they can still do some real damage. You can't really replace the Gravis marines here with Centurions without changing the whole detachment, and Devastator marines might be a good fit here but you'd need to move things around - I think if I were going that route I'd swap out Gravis Linebreaker Force for either Devastator Brethren or Ironstorm. Devastator gives you more movement and works better on things like Centurions and Devastators, while Ironstorm will benefit your vehicles more.

Imperial Fists Aggressors. Credit: Jack Hunter

Final Thoughts

There are certainly more ways to run the Imperial Fists, and as a chapter they almost certainly make use of units like Assault Intercessors, Outriders, and Bladeguard Veterans. But at the same time, when you think about the Imperial Fists, those units aren't necessarily what comes to mind and it's fun to think about how we might build armies which match that mental conception while still being reasonable to run on the tabletop.

But this is just the tip of the iceberg - we'll have more of these in the coming weeks, looking at other Chapters and how to build their armies as we become more familiar with the Codex.
