Mayday, Miss Marcy! Ruler Ruling, Cleaning Out, Consistency Chaos

July 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello beautiful readers, and welcome back once again to Mayday, Miss Marcy! in which I take your lovingly submitted questions and then produce my lovingly crafted answers, as long as the internet service I pay for decides that it will work long enough for me to write the article that week (Shout out to Spectrum! You suck! Keep it up!). As always, this column seeks to find helpful answers to reader submitted questions, and as we are nearing the approach of Autumn and the end of Summer, that just means we can finally hope to put 100+ degree temperatures behind us and continue wondering when, exactly, is it actually okay to prime a model (Now. Do it now.)! If you'd like to have YOUR questions answered in THIS very column, please read below:

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

Last week, we talked about post event burnout, solo gaming, and more! This week, I've got 3 new questions for all of your reading pleasure, so let's dive in.

Changing the Ruler

Miss Marcy,

Recently, an issue came up at my LGS that has not really been something I've personally encountered before. While playing a game, my opponent and I began having a dispute over measuring the distance of something. I know that occasionally players are a little pushy with distances and sometimes even obstinate about how far or short something is in their favor, but the problem I ran into was that my ruler and my opponent's ruler were a literal inch different! I called the shop's judge, but he seemed pretty unsure of how to rule (ha) when putting the two tape measures side by side. My opponent insists his distance was correct, while I pointed out that it seemed like his tape measure was obviously defective, but I'm not sure what anyone could have done in this situation.

Meters Apart

Dear Meters,

I appreciate the wordplay of your name even if you're mixing measuring formats. Anyway, for people who are unaware, this is something that can actually happen pretty often! Tape measures being off by various amounts (an inch is pretty egregious but not unheard of) does indeed happen more often than you'd think, and it can make things pretty strange when using them for measurements around your house or such, let alone playing a game in which measurements are kind of important. I think that the actual answer that should have been made here is that your judge should probably have some form of measuring tool he could have used to apply a standard to (IE, which of your two tapes matched his the closest) or provided something to be used once the discrepancy had been found. There is also the problem that without some other form of item to judge against, it is actually possible YOUR tape measure is the faulty one, even if that sounds ridiculous on the surface.

There could be some merit into looking in to other measuring devices, or simply having a back up tape measure or even just a good old fashioned straight ruler to use; if you think your opponent is someone you're going to play against often, this is not going to be the first and last time this argument comes up, and having alternative measuring tools can ensure that some form of consistency is found; or, you two could agree to share the measuring tool; even if your opponent is cagey about it, you should point out that you otherwise have a literally different understanding of 1 inch, and that handing each other the tape measure is better than having to keep getting a judge involved.

Once You Pop, You Can't Stop

Miss Marcy,

I recently started thinning out my collection of gaming stuff: board games, supplies, models, etc that I don't really feel are being used and also I could use some extra money. The issue I am running in to, though, is that I've now started enjoying the sensation of putting things up for sale and getting them out of my apartment, to the point that I realized I was nearly listing everything I own. Does this mean I just don't care about gaming or should I not be worrying so much about reducing stuff? It just feels like I've cleared out a lot so far, to the point that I almost have nothing now.

Marie Kondo'd Myself

Dear MKM,

I think what you're experiencing is the clarity that comes from reducing clutter and unused items, which is that it feels good to get rid of things you aren't actually using and kind of condensing the space around you to be more open, even if that's removing a few boxes from your line of sight. You mention Marie Kondo in your name, but this is actually pretty in line with the philosophy she was originally working on: you are feeling better because you are no longer seeing those things that aren't  being moved or touched and feeling guilt, anxiety, or some other form of unpleasant feeling for a thing you haven't touched in some amount of time. There's really nothing wrong with that, and the fact that you are getting rid of so much is actually pretty admirable! I think the issue you might be running in to is that (since I don't know how much you're talking about, really) you haven't hit anything yet that you are conflicted about getting rid of, which means that so far you've gotten rid of things you knew you weren't going to use or need. You aren't making the choice of selling your favorite army or game for some sense of profit, but instead you've gotten rid of things you didn't want or need anymore, and you feel good. When you do start hitting those things, you'll know, and if you don't, well, that means that maybe your priorities have shifted and you're just not in to gaming anymore, which happens!

Routine Maintenance

Dear Miss Marcy,

I think I've fallen into a rut, and I'm mostly emailing you because I want the reality check from another person about it. Lately I have a very standard routine to my week: I work most days, I go to play Commander on Wednesdays, and I play 40k on Friday. This is great, in theory, but it has started to feel like I am just doing these things as part of some form of maintenance or regular routine. I will say I have fun playing them, but it just feels like there's nothing new or interesting that occurs, and I'm worried I've sort of stuck myself into this.

Round in Circles

Dear Round,

Well, yes, you are in a rut, or at least a routine, which is only as good or bad as you make it. I don't want to sound unhelpful, but your question doesn't really engage with the core problem of this: a ROUTINE is something you do regularly, usually out of enjoyment or necessity, and a RUT is when you are stuck doing something over and over that you don't enjoy and find monotonous. Your situation is only a problem if you feel it is the latter and not the former, because that means that you aren't finding these events enjoyable even if you are attending them and perhaps even having fun at them while in the moment. If you are viewing it as a rut, that problem extends beyond your hobby and more to your other daily life duties and issues, which means you may want to start there and work up to your hobby routine.

If you think it is just the hobbies themselves that have grown stale, then perhaps it is time to shake up what you're doing. I'd say pinpoint which of the two brings you the most consistent joy and enjoyment, and then see if there are other things you could do with the day you are now freeing up; are there other events you could attend, other activities you could do instead, social events you could go to? Because the reality is that giving up your social outlets because you feel they are becoming boring without checking to see if it is your work/duties balance that is off is a possibility of increasing your overall stress, but there is also nothing wrong with admitting that playing essentially the same games over and over with no variety can eventually become boring. Perhaps you could look for new games to play on those days, or new places to play them, or something to try and spice up what you're doing: new army, new deck, etc.

In the end, you probably just need to take a bigger picture look at things, as it seems like you're noticing a problem but you're starting with what you feel is "extra", i.e. your hobbies, and not "the rest", which likely has a bigger impact on how you feel either settled into a routine or a rut. Go from there and you'll likely figure out where the real issue lies.
