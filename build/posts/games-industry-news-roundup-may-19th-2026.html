Games Industry News Roundup- May 19th, 2026

May 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Mechanicus 2 Gets Surprise Release Date for Warhammer Skulls

Credit: Games Workshop/Kasedo Games/Bulwark Studios

After years of silence regarding the full release date for Mechanicus 2, Games Workshop announced last week that the much anticipated strategy game is releasing this week as part of the annual Warhammer Skulls celebration. 

Warhammer Skulls is an annual celebration of Warhammer video games hosted by Games Workshop. Since the first year of Skulls in 2016, GW has used Skulls as a way to promote upcoming Warhammer licensed games, as well as reveal new future titles in the Warhammer universe. Additionally, Warhammer Skulls heralds a week of deeply discounted Warhammer games on digital storefronts, particularly Steam.

To kick off Skulls, Games Workshop is hosting a reveals show video event on May 21st to show off trailers for all of the upcoming games and content that’s set to be announced this year. Alanah Pierce, who voices the new Sister of Battle character in Boltgun 2, is hosting this year's show.

Pearce writes on Warhammer Community, “When the Warhammer team sent me the list of games that are going to be shown in this year’s Skulls showcase I was honestly so excited! I think fans are going to be super happy with the 10-year anniversary special, and I’m honoured to be even the tiniest part of it.” 

GW confirmed that we can expect announcements for Space Marine 2, Darktide, Rogue Trader, Dark Heresy, Boltgun 2, Warhammer Survivors, and Dawn of War IV. The release date of Mechanicus 2, set to coincide with the start of Warhammer Skulls on May 21st, was revealed prior to the event to build excitement for Warhammer fans.

The Warhammer Skulls reveal show is scheduled to stream on Warhammer’s official Youtube and Twitch channels on May 21st at 12pm EST/5pm BST.

PlayStation Plus Price Increase Incoming

Credit: Sony Entertainment

When it comes to price hikes, as Xbox goes, PlayStation seems doomed to follow. On Monday, Sony Interactive Entertainment (SIE) announced that the price of their PlayStation Plus online service subscription is increasing for multiple international regions.

SIE informed the public of this change with a post on PlayStation social media pages, writing “Starting May 20, PlayStation Plus prices for new customers will increase in select regions. Due to ongoing market conditions, prices will start at $10.99 USD / €9.99 EUR / £7.99 GBP for 1-month subscriptions and $27.99 USD / €27.99 EUR / £21.99 GBP for 3-month subscriptions.”

The company does additionally note that current PlayStation Plus users won’t be subject to this change provided that users keep their account active, writing that, “This price change does not apply to current subscribers (except in Turkey and India) unless the existing subscription changes or lapses.”

While this price increase is minor, only about one dollar per month, it follows a larger trend of Microsoft and Sony steadily increasing the prices for both their software and hardware products in an increasingly unstable console market. Sony raised the price of the PS5 in the United States in August of last year, and earlier this year Microsoft increased the price of GamesPass subscriptions, though it dropped the prices in April after severe backlash. 

eBay Rejects GameStop Buyout Proposal

Credit: Gamestop

The saying goes is that you miss every shot you don’t take, a motto that was almost certainly rattling around in the brains of GameStop executives when they sent an unsolicited proposal to buy eBay earlier this month. eBay officially rejected the offer last week, and made it clear that in this case, GameStop probably shouldn’t have shot at all. 

After consulting their legal team and discussing it internally, eBay has decided that GameStop’s offer “is neither credible nor attractive.” The board cites multiple factors in this rejection, such as eBay’s own strong independent prospects and the uncertain means GameStop would use to finance the purchase. 

“eBay is a strong, resilient business that has delivered meaningful results over the past several years,” the board wrote in a press statement. “We have sharpened our strategic focus, strengthened execution, enhanced our marketplace and seller experience, and consistently returned capital to shareholders. With its differentiated global marketplace and a clear strategy, eBay's Board is confident that the company, under its current management team, is well-positioned to continue to drive sustainable growth, execute with discipline, and deliver long-term value for our shareholders.”

GameStop’s offer to purchase eBay at $125 a share (for a total of around $55 billion) left many scratching their heads. GameStop wrote in their offer to eBay that by merging the two companies, they could create a retailer rival to Amazon, leveraging eBay’s online presence with GameStop’s brick and mortar storefronts. GameStop made this offer despite being a much smaller company than eBay, and was avoidant on specifics for how the capital would be raised to make the purchase. 

GameStop has not yet responded to eBay’s rejection, though its CEO Ryan Cohen has said that the company is willing to take the offer directly to shareholders should the eBay board reject it. 

Osprey Games Forced to Sell Down Board and Card Game Departments

Credit: Osprey Games

UK tabletop games publisher Osprey Games announced last week due to a decision from Osprey’s corporate parent company, Osprey is selling both their board game and card game divisions.

“After 11 fantastic years, Bloomsbury Publishing, the owner of Osprey Games, has made the difficult decision to sell the board and card game line. This was not a decision taken lightly and reflects a strategic refocusing on book publishing,” Osprey writes. “Going forwards, wargames and roleplaying games will form the core part of what we publish.”

Osprey Games was founded in 2015 as a spinoff of Osprey Publishing, known for producing rulebooks for historical wargames. Both Osprey Games and Publishing are under the umbrella of Bloomsbury Publishing, whose main focus is publishing books all over the globe. Since their founding, Osprey Games has developed and launched multiple successful board game and card game titles, including the popular Undaunted series and The King is Dead.

According to an interview with Boardgamewire, the selloff comes as a shock to the team at Osprey Games, whose recent releases had been financial successes. 

“I don’t think the decision has anything to do with how the games have sold,” said Peer Sylvester, one of the key figures for designing games at Osprey. “From what I see, there are some strong titles, like The King is Dead (which is still selling well), Undaunted, and apparently also Lost Expedition. So it’s a decision made from the parent-parent-company at Bloomsbury.”

Bloomsbury hasn’t made any announcement regarding a potential buyer for Osprey Games’s Board and Card games division at this time. While the company won’t be designing any new board or card games, Osprey Games will still honor the production of said games until a buyer is found. 

Hasbro Hires Anti-Union Firm Following Unionization of MTG Arena Team

Credit: United Wizards of the Coast

Last month, the team behind Magic: The Gathering Arena announced that the entire development team had agreed to unionize under the Communications Workers of America (CWA), creating the United Wizards of the Coast (UWOTC). Though initially hopeful that their parent companies, Wizards of the Coast and Hasbro, would voluntarily recognize the union and be cooperative with its workforce. Now, it looks like the exact opposite is happening, as according to the UWOTC Hasbro hired an anti-union firm to attempt to break up the UWOTC.

“Since the announcement of our union, Wizards of the Coast and Hasbro have hired Fisher Phillips (a union avoidance firm), and chosen to engage in a daily union-avoidance campaign by sending emails to the Arena team that is spreading misinformation and sowing fear among our colleagues,” the UWOTC wrote on their social media page last week. “Instead of voluntarily recognizing our union, they've forced us into a redundant and drawn-out election process, rather than listening to the overwhelming majority of employees who signed our initial letter to management.”

Fisher & Phillips, a law firm with offices all across the United States with a focus on workplace law, is a well known advocate for “union avoidance”. This practice focuses on avoiding unions in the workplace by promising to improve communications between employers and their employees, and leveraging the feeling of a “workplace family” that unionizing would destroy.

By not voluntarily recognizing the union, Hasbro is forcing the Arena team to engage with a union election process with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB), a lengthy process that in the eyes of the UWOTC buys Hasbro time to try and bully members of the team away from unionizing. 

The UWOTC has not shared any further updates regarding the election process with the NLRB, though it encourages supporters to sign a petition to Hasbro leadership to voluntarily recognize their union.
