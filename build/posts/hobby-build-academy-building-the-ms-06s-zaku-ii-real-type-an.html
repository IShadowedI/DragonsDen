Hobby Build Academy: Building the MS-06S Zaku II Real Type and Gundam GQuuuuuuX

August 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back dear readers to a new edition of Hobby Build Academy! We hope you've been enjoying working alongside of us as we work our way deeper and deeper into the world of hobby model building; once again I am Professor Marcy, and I'm joined by my amazing assistant Elly! This week we have 2 model builds to review and discuss: The MS-06S Zaku II Real Type, as well as the Gundam GQuuuuuuX. Last time, we talked about the various types of grades in Gundam and also how to perhaps grab a Lego set that was right for you, and during that, we ended up building these two HG kits that surprised us with the pretty big gulf in what an HG kit can ask from you in terms of complexity.

Elly's Build: The MS-06S Zaku II

Elly: When we went to the Gundam Base Pop-Up, I saw this kit and really wanted to give it a shot. I know I've said I've actually not built a model in a long time and that the last time I did was very likely not an official model, but I don't think I expected this model to be quite as challenging as it ended up being. When I really get into something, I really get into it all the way, so I'd already been researching waterslide decals, and we picked up some Micro Sol and Micro Set at our local hobby shop in anticipation of having to use them.

Marcy: I've never actually used them either, so I really didn't know much about it.

Marcy: We picked up some new nippers, and I found these clay pushers very useful for some of my metallic decals; the white part separator we picked up was VERY useful in a few spots on GQX.

Elly: Well let me tell you, they were a lot harder to use than I expected! Maybe because I was a first-timer, or maybe because this model is smaller than a RG or MG, but it took me two hobby sessions to actually get this thing built and properly stickered. I hadn't really expected to spend so much time on doing so, either, so it really threw off my weekend plans. The ironic part of all of this is that the model itself was really simple to build! I had really no problems at all getting the parts together, and the new nippers we bought did a great job of cleanly separating them all so I never really needed to file or smooth anything down.

Marcy: Yeah, I almost felt bad that you had to spend so much time on the stickers, frankly; I never expected it to take you so long to get them on. But it looks amazing all complete!

Elly: My little work station, before disaster struck.

Elly: Yeah, that's basically my main takeaway from this: if you have a model that has a lot of waterslide decals like this, you should really plan for it to take twice as long as you expect if you are putting them on. And, honestly, I think the model looks pretty bad without the stickers on. When I was looking at the model without them, it looked fine, but very "toy" like. It really doesn't have a lot of detail that makes it look very spectacular otherwise. But once I had the stickers mostly on, it really started to look impressive.

Elly: Sadly, I had my first hobbying accident while building this kit; I spilled a good amount of the Micro Sol that we had bought, and I was worried that it would ruin the build, as it ended up getting on the sticker sheet. I did lose one decal, but they give you a lot of extra ones, so honestly it didn't end up mattering that much, but boy did it make this a lot more stressful than I would have liked!

Elly: This is where I ended up stopping on day 1.

Marcy: Well, you're officially a hobbyist now; at least you spilled something clear and easy to clean, unlike the many bottles of Nuln Oil Warhammer painters have ruined models, clothes, carpets, and probably marriages with.

Elly: You're saying that too seriously for me to read that as a joke, which is concerning. Anyway, I think overall I'm very happy with how this model came out, and I really learned a lot in the process. Although putting on the waterslide decals was time consuming, I think the next time I have to do it, I'd be able to do it much faster and with more confidence.

Elly: These 3 shoulder cones were probably the hardest, and I think they came out great.

Marcy: You seemed to really have the hang of it, especially on the second day, and I've still never done them, so I may have to ask you for help when I end up with them myself! What would you give the model overall?

Elly: This panel shield gave me so much anxiety. And I have enough of that already!

Elly: I'd say that the model wasn't that difficult to build, just time consuming. I think it looks super cool built and really evokes that dangerous, slick feeling of Char's Zaku that I was hoping from the box. I also think it is interesting that the stickers made it so it felt like painting wouldn't really bring me much extra from the kit, except maybe weathering, but I don't really know much about that. For an HG kit, the stickers really seemed to greatly up the difficulty, but for a first timer like me to get it to come out so good, I think just about anyone could build this kit with some patience.

Elly: I will say the bazooka is fiddly; it just kind of rests on the model and falls off easily.

Elly: If I were to give this kit a number, I'd say I'd give it an 8/10; if you don't plan to put the waterslide decals on it, I would probably skip it and look for a different Char's Zaku kit to build, as without the decals it looks VERY simplistic and toylike. I even now can see in the pictures where some panel lining paint could go a long way, so I might touch it up with a paint pen later, but I like the posability and look, and its a great testament to my first time using these types of decals!

Marcy: Here's looking at you, Amuro.

Marcy's Build: HG Gundam GQuuuuuuX

Marcy: for me, I wanted to build the GQuuuuuuX (or GQX). I'd heard that the GQX line of models had amazing posability and articulation, as well as some very modern aesthetics that help them look great even without extra painting, so I opted for the cover model itself. Although I know GQX has a pretty mixed opinion amongst Gundam fans, I really liked what the series was trying to do, and I love Nyaan and Machu a lot.

Marcy: GQuuuuuuX mostly done. You might notice a lack of red stripes. That's because the decals all sucked.

Elly: Yeah, you have a lot of GQX Machu merch, honestly. And you're even playing her in the TCG.

Marcy: Machu rules! Shame the rest of the show happens, I guess. Anyway, I am going to start this review off by making one thing clear: My build was partially ruined by decals, although mine weren't waterglide. You see, GQX is supposed to have these red line decals that give it a lot of detail, and you can see where they're supposed to be on the box. The problem, however, is that for me, these decals just refused to adhere to the model at all, and after a few attempts, even using glue, I gave up on them completely.

Elly: I felt bad, you really seemed to be having a bad time with the model because of it.

Marcy: It was very frustrating in the moment; had I known about that ahead of time, I think I would have planned to simply pick up a red paint pen, but I'll just do that next time I'm at the hobby store. When I looked up what people had to say about the model online, I found mostly people saying the same thing happened to them, which... I guess made me feel better, sort of, in that it wasn't just me messing it up.

Marcy: The arms, torso, and head of this build were some of the most fiddly, but it all went together okay.

Marcy: Unlike Elly, I will say that I had a few sections that required filing on this build. The piece that connects the torso to the legs in particular had a piece that required quite a bit of filing to get it to fit together snugly, and once it did, it was fine, but I was a little shocked at that particular piece; if you lacked a file, it could have been very frustrating to make the build work. After the model was together, though, I have to admit I was impressed.

Elly: You really couldn't stop talking about how posable it is.

Marcy: Yeah! I had heard and read that people said the GQX models have some of the best posability so far, and it really lived up to that. I was able to basically bend the model into a ball, turn the legs and arms in various ways and positions, and it never felt like the model wouldn't, or shouldn't, move in various ways. I was also pretty surprised at the fact that the weird half-feet GQX has worked so well; the model never once toppled over or felt like it would lose balance while I was posing it.

Marcy: A very cool looking kit that captures how slick GQX really is, but marred by weird decals.

Marcy: You get 2 green plastic swords, an axe, shield, and gun to play with, as well as a the backpack that can also be posed separately. I think the amount of accessories was nice for a HG kit, and the green energy swords do look pretty slick for basically requiring no real assembly or accessorizing.

Elly: Energy sword sundayyyyy!

GQuuuuuuX overall looks and poses great, but the decals really sucked the fun out of the model for me.

Marcy: If I had to give this model a rating, I'd say that for the build quality alone, an 8/10; the decals really sucked, but they don't matter to the overall build, and I can handle that part with some red paint pens; your own mileage may vary if you luck out, but just know going in that this particular kit does have some notoriously weird decals for these red lines. The other decals were great, I had no problems with them, and they were easy to get on and looked real nice easily; it was just the red line detail ones that sucked a lot of the fun out of the model build.

For HGs, this is a great kit for people who like to pose and play with models. I hear the entire GQX line is like this, so I am curious to check them out.

Wrap-Up and What We Learned

Both models presented some interesting challenges; the Zaku certainly provided the most challenge as it was our first time using waterslide decals, micro sol and micro set, but overall there weren't many problems actually building the model and getting it completed. The GQX was a similarly smooth build, but it had a few sections that required heavy filing and the decals really made for a bit of a problem in the overall build vibes. For being a modern kit, though, I think the GQX is a great kit to show how far HG models have come and how easily you can make a great looking kit at a smaller scale.

If you're a Gundam fan that wants a good piece that comes with the price of decal usage, the Zaku kit is great; as for GQX, while I like it and enjoy it now that it is built, I think the attraction factor of the kit mostly lies in whether or not you enjoy GQuuuuuX; since I'm a fan, it was an easy pick up, but if you haven't seen it or didn't like the show, this one's probably not going to be high on most people's build priorities; if you do want to give it a shot, though, I'm going to be real curious to know how you fare with the decals in your box!
