Malediction: Monofaction Reinforcement Packs

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

New releases for the miniature card game Malediction are on the horizon, and no matter which faction or seeker you play, there is something for everyone. The new faction packs bring new units, spells, and items for you to utilise for your expedition into the Malediction. Let’s peek behind the curtain and take a look!

The Four Reinforcement Packs for Malediction - Loot Studios

What Are These Packs?

These new sets are Reinforcement Packs that focus on a specific monofaction with the game. They have fixed contents and are not boosters, so nothing is randomised within them. Each focuses on a specific aspect and playstyle of a faction, expanding your tools for the tabletop. There are 3x Units, 3x Spells, and an Item in each set, alongside the STLs for you to print the models. If you’re not a fan of printing the models, then you can pick up the standee pack that will be coming out with the Reinforcement Packs, allowing you to play with the cards straightaway. 

Hands of the Church Miniatures - Loot Studios

Hands of the Church - Order of the Shattered Throne

Sacrifice for the greater good is the core concept of this set. We are finally diving into what makes The Church of the Order’s lands tick; powerful effects that have a dire cost.

Units

Siro, Hand of Law - Gaining echo is likely one of the strongest effects in the game considering how premium of a resource it is. The Pray ability from the Valcarist Priests have been used by the Order to help generate more echo for the player to spend, and here you can do so through a similar ability as a trade for damage, which can be so easily healed since you are playing the faction that heals but also prevents damage. 

But you can couple this with doing more damage using the echo you just gained. Turning Siro into a little bit of a beast. But the true power lies in using him with Sigrith. Her Legacy allows you to discard cards to prevent damage, as well as allowing you to make a free attack if you fully prevent the damage. Siro can force free attacks with his abilities, whilst also generating more echo. 

Sigrith and Siro will be an incredible duo, imposing an incredibly tough control on the board. You just have to get Siro out of your deck and onto the field as soon as possible. 

Gilded Sentries - The sentries are beasts! They can charge an enemy model if it has made an attack on a friendly, and with their base 5 damage on a hit, they will mess you up. It isn’t often you see such a high damage on a non-Seeker model, so it makes these standout. They’re also pretty tough to take down with 14 defence and health. Will be in my decks going forward for sure!

I also like the idea that this can work well with a “fights back” deck. You could have the Armoured Revenant be the front line with the Gilded Sentries being nearby. If the Armoured Revenant is attacked, it will fight back, followed by the charge from the sentry. Then, the enemy needs to get through the sentry which is no easy feet. 

Discordant Voice - What a crazy cheap unit to add to the game, but its playstyle around control is brilliant. The best way to understand these units is by treating them as traps. They do damage when an engaged enemy activates, and you can set them up when your own units activate. Having these work alongside the Revalis Penitent would mean when an enemy model activates it would take 3 damage. 

I love the idea of throwing these annoyances into opponents who will either need to kill the model, or spend their activation getting away from them. You can delay the most important models on the table with such cheap troops. Love these as an addition to the Order’s roster. 

Spells

Triumvirate Liturgy - This spell is a really useful card since it is technically free as long as you have five echo to spend to play it, since you can get it back from the effects. Drawing some more cards to help get through your deck or refreshing an ally is incredible value. But, the trade off for it being for free, would be giving an effect to your opponent. 

You can get around it by playing it in a round in which you would go first, and then drawing two cards whilst refunding your spent echo. Your opponent can then refresh an ally, but no one would have been exhausted to refresh. But, refreshing yourself in the direst of moments, could be a powerful play. The versatility is what excites me about this card. 

Demand Confession - This spell is not that impactful, but it could be a very good card for players in the competitive scene. This is because those at the top of their game would need to have knowledge of most of the cards your opponent could have, whilst also needing to know which ones in a specific moment could do the most harm, thus gaining the most value from this card. 

However, if you have a high cost card in your hand, you could use this to thin the hand of the opponent. It would be very unlikely for them to have an 8 or 9 cost card for example. What you would have to weigh up is whether spending two echo to remove one of your opponents cards is worth it over using the echo to block damage or get a unit out. Unsure on whether we will see this card that often. 

Twisted Clarity - This spell could end up being one of the strongest in the whole pack, perhaps even the game. Being able to fully restore the health of a model can turn the entire tides of a combat by undoing the hard work of the opponent. It is a classical Order style of play, who thrive on controlling the battlefield.

A perfect place to use this card would be in a Londriel Spellwarden deck. Londriel decks that bring the damage-preventing spells of the Order, with the immovable constructs of the Conclave, can be one of the hardest nuts to crack. Now imagine you poured all your resources into weakening the wall you face, and she just plays Twisted Clarity, undoing all you have done. Of course this would mean the model would lose its turn but often it is there to be shield.

It is also a great way to pull your vanguard models out of a bad situation, since they are likely to already be exhausted, whilst taking the main bulk of the damage. I can see this being used on Rogarth or Ephr since you only have one in your deck and they are tanky models for Order. A very good card that will find its way into all of my Order decks. 

Item

Expatrine Scourge - This is utterly incredible. I have not been overly impressed by items in the game; they haven’t provided enough excitement for me to care about them being used. But this scourge changes that. Taking damage to do damage doesn’t seem that incredible on the surface, but with Sigrith you can get extra attacks if you prevent the damage.

Epher, from the Griza Seeker Deck, can do two attacks for one action, each one could take damage through the scourge, allowing you to block the damage on each. You could end up doing eight attacks in one activation with Epher. With half of those attacks doing seven damage on a hit, and all with a range of 3”.

Overall, this set was made with Sigirth players in mind. All the cards have possible places with other Seekers, but their value rockets when brought alongside Sigrith. I expect to be very concerned playing across from Sigirth players going forward, not knowing how many counter attacks could be launched. If you love the paladin of order, then pick this set up as soon as possible. 

Coutly Spectacle Miniatures - Loot Studios

Courtly Spectacle - Legion of the Fallen

Dirty tactics, and cruel debuffs. The Legion rolls out the red carpet for the despicable machinations they have conjured. 

Units

Champion of Drav - This unit is menacing! It reminds me of Strong Belwas from A Song of Ice and Fire with how it encourages an opponent to strike it before striking back, thus allowing them a shot before it demolishes them. Drav wants to get struck in and do some damage, which seems to be low for nine echo, but the Feign Opening rule has a way to increase the damage. If you can find other ways to increase this, then Drav will be a big problem. If I see Drav get played, all my attention will suddenly turn to dealing with it. Very strong and cool model.  

Marsarin Deathsinger - Legion are all about debuffs, and have a few ways to make decisions costly for the opponent. The Deathsinger has a cruel ability which can force the opponent to lose echo if they miss their attacks. Literally rubbing salt in the wound! Again this is another model that I will be gunning for as soon as it hits the table because you can not allow such a leech to remain. You can combine it with a Disciple who can siphon echo from the opponent as well, resulting in a drought of resources. 

Mashr - Most of the lower quality units in the game are not impressive unless they are brought in large numbers. Furthermore, they all fill similar roles of being bodies for the fight more so than anything substantial. The Mashr is actually useful, and I can see them being in every deck going forward. They are there to die so that you can bring on other low quality models such as a Men-at-Arms, but for less echo. They are an investment, and will be a nuisance until they die. 

Spells

Choice of Malady - A cursed decision. Does the opponent exhaust a unit that could be crucial for the turn ahead, all in the aim of preventing more cards drawn? It can be a difficult choice for sure and I am not too sure it is as clean cut of an answer. The power of this card lies in choosing the right moment to play the card. Nothing too crazy with this one. 

Soul Mimicry - Those who have played against Morida know how strong her abilities are when she starts stitching together the abilities of units into an abomination. This spell is like a mini version of that, and can have a massive impact in the game. Grabbing Onslaught and Regeneration from Vorendal as an example, or Morida’s Necromancy action. Normally with these spells they would state “a non-Seeker ally”, but this doesn’t and it can cause a world of pain. Very good card indeed. 

Horrific Archery - A brilliant card that works perfectly with Vorendal’s Legacy, so this will certainly be used with his forces. However, the ranged buff part of this card is a really good effect as there are not many things available to the Legion that has ranged. You could pair it with a model that has the Ambush rule, and wants to attack a target but can’t due to terrain or models. This card is a great utility piece for the Legion. 

Item

Oskarite Blade - This item is incredible, and I can see all factions looking to play it. A passive buff to damage, whilst also offering more scoring points for the game. This is worthwhile in all decks and for use on all capable units. There are some like Ephr from the Order roster, that can do multiple attacks against targets, that would be the best place for this attachment as an example. One of the best attachments in the game. 

This is a brilliant set for all Legion players, and I can see quite a few cards falling into the builds of many decks. Drav is the coolest model created to date, and their rules are full of flavour and awe. I love some of the spells, and I am shocked with how useful the attachments are. If you’re a Legion player, do not miss a chance to grab this set. 

Liastrum Research Miniatures - Loot Studios

Liastrum Research - Conclave of the Spheres

Spells and ranged attacks are the life blood of the Conclave faction, this pack tursn it up to 11.

Units

Athan, Prime Decimator - Athan’s special action can turn his ranged attack into the longest ranged attack in the game that does a whopping ten damage on a hit, and seven damage on a miss. He also suffers damage each time he attacks, whilst also being flung back by the power of his weapon. I can see a few moments where you would take two attacks to blast him 8” away from danger, which is hilarious. Athan is very deadly, and it will be hard to pin him down whilst he snipes your units. 

Thaumite Mercenary - This unit is nothing special in the early game, but will be one of the best fighters towards the middle to end game. Polinore and Londriel burn through spells quite often, so the Arcan Precision ability will be insanely good the longer the game goes on for. But you should equip this model with some damage bonuses from attachments such as the Oskarite Blade from the Legion set we just talked about. You will see these cheap troops become deadly assassins if you’re not careful. 

Liastrum Accumulator - If you couldn’t get your spells out due to shortages of echo, then look no further than the Accumulators. This will be a very strong model to get out on the field as soon as possible. Conclaves thrive on playing spells to buff their forces, so finding ways to make them cheaper is key to keeping the pressure on the enemy. Whilst they aren’t going to be doing much more than counting the pennies of Liastrum, they will become a common occurrence on the battlefield. 

Spells

Liastrum Decimation - What a well designed card. You choose how much echo you would like to spend, based on how much damage you need. There is a discount on some more damage if you buy in bulk. Jokes aside, this is an incredible card. The versatility is incredible, and I can imagine some troll who spends no echo and kills Thundersteps turn 1. A very powerful card that feels like a perfect swiss army knife for almost any situation you may find yourself in. Every deck will surely have this spell included. 

Crackling Trap - Name a more iconic duo than the Conclave and damage dealing spells. This spell adds to the long list of spells in the arsenal of the Conclave that does damage to the opponent, albeit with a little bit more of a restriction. However, everyone should be charging towards Husks as they win the game, so there should be ample opportunities to trigger this card. More damage through spells is music to Conclave ears. 

Salvage - What an interesting card, and one I can see coming into play with a few different units. Reckless Researchers to exhaust the enemy, Virtahn Battlecasters to assail the opponent, and Akheenzan Shieldmages to defend your forces. These elite units are tough to get out, but are even tough to keep around, so pulling them back is incredibly useful. I don’t think this is a card that will have a lot of impact, but in the odd times where it really counts, it can be a life saver. 

Item

Myrkosian Magnifier - This attachment is decent, and has a lot of play no matter which faction brings it. Conclaves are going to see the most value as they should be the ones that go through a lot of spells, but I can also see Sigrith players, or Thundersteps players bringing this card. There is potential in this card in making a unit consistent in combat and there are quite a few models in the game you wouldn’t want to take many attacks from. 

This set comes with added cards that continue the theme for the Conclave, with more ranged attacks and spells that do damage. The highlight will be the Accumulator, which will help you manage your resources far better with how many spells you will be likely using. Athan is a super addition, and I look forward to hearing stories about this sniper on the table. If you love what the Conclave are about, then this is right up your alley. 

Bestial Advantage Miniatures - Loot Studios

Bestial Advantage - Primal Blood

Bestial Advantage focuses on expanding tools around movement to control the battlefield, whilst unleashing plenty of pressure from swarming beasts. 

Units

Irynia and Aratan - This unit allows for fast play with it granting extra movement actions to itself using Dash, and other beasts using Command Beast. Primal Blood are a very fast faction, so adding more of this could make them impossible to catch, or run from. But these abilities work nicely with Swarm, which needs nearby friendlies to gain bonuses to damage. 

The only problem with Irynia and Aratan, is that there are few beasts in the game. I would be using it on the Cursehost Boar for a better position for when it dies and explodes. Or on the Bloodfeast Raptor since it does damage when an enemy activates whilst engaged. More beats are needed, so let’s take a look at them. 

Behemoth Khuyakine - There is no stopping a Behemoth from reaching its prey. I look at the rules for this model and imagine it more like an assassin, getting to far spots to pick off models that believe they are safe behind the frontline. Being able to redeploy can cause a lot of problems for someone trying to control the board. It is also a damaged dealer with five damage on a hit, whilst also being tough to kill with the Resistance ability. A solid model to add to the Primal Blood roster.  

Murder of Shriekers - Another unit that meddles with movement restrictions by swarming through targets doing some chip damage. Chip damage is an interesting concept I have not thought of diving into, but you can stack them nicely with the beasts available for Primal Blood. There isn’t too much to talk about with this unit, and the Primal Blood units have not been that thrilling. The Behemoth has been the highlight however, and I will for sure be bringing them in my decks. 

Spells

Ymirian Rage - This is an incredible spell. There have been many instances where relics alone can decide an encounter or game. But now you can strip such a strong attachment and force another one onto the model, and do some damage to them on top of that. I can see this being in every single deck going forward as it is an incredible card, especially with how overpowered relics can be. 

Landshift - An interesting card for deciding where a fight will be taking place for a round, but not one that is so powerful that it will be a must include. With the speed the Primal Blood has, it could be very easy to contest any Husk. However, you could move the Husk that is being contested towards friendly models, but the path to the husk is blocked by your own. Forcing the opponent to break through the lines to try and stop you claiming it. I can see this card being great against castling opponents. 

Raptor’s Lift - Did I mention Primal Blood are fast? A cheaper card, that gives a nice boost to movement, whilst also providing Fly to get through obstacles. This is a nice situational card that reinforces the faction theme of speed. Nothing too crazy here so not much to talk about. 

Item

Avakran Flute - Battle Cry is an ability we have seen on a model from the Order faction and on the shamans in the Primal Blood, and it is surprisingly very good. Action manipulation can turn an entire game, so being able to bring this as an attachment which a faction can use is quite nice. With Primal Blood being more of a glass cannon, this can help reinforce some damage before a counter assault. For one echo, the versatility it grants is very good. 

Overall the Primal Blood seem lackluster, but perhaps I don’t see the combinations and synergies the cards have to offer? That being said, there are some cool aspects with the Behemoth relocating, a relic recycle, and plenty more movement. Not too bad of a set to pick up. 

What’s the Verdict?

The concept of focusing on one aspect of the background to these factions in small packs is a genius idea. We get to peek into what makes the Church of the Order of the Shattered Throne tick, or how the Primal Blood tame beasts of the land. The rules advance the game in interesting directions, and the miniatures are gorgeous. 

There are competitive pieces, insanely fun aspects, and something for each and every player within these packs. It is a superb release for Malediction that will help freshen the game for those who have been playing for almost a year or longer. I for one cannot wait to paint up some of the models and get them out on the table, with the only question remaining being; which faction should I pick first?
