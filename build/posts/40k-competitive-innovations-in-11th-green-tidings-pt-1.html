[40k] Competitive Innovations in 11th: Green Tidings pt.1

July 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

11th Edition has accelerated to attack speed, and so have the Orks - this week's big news is that the Boyz are very much back in town, showing incredible dominance of both win rates and trophies. There's still plenty of other strong options available and a pleasingly diverse metagame, but it's both our single biggest week of 11th Edition yet, and also by far the biggest outlier performance from a heavily-played faction (it's one of those weeks where a single army is top of every metric we track). The power couple of Taktikal Brigade and Green Tide dominates, but it's not the only list putting up top finishes, with dakka-heavy setups also thriving. Will the rest of the metagame be able to stem the tide? I'm sure we'll find out as people start gunning for them.

Archetype

Freebooter Dakka Goodstuff

Thoughts

Our first undefeated Ork build of the week is one of the shootier ones, combining Freebooter Krew and More Dakka into something that, for the first time ever in this faction, I feel pretty comfortable calling a goodstuff build. Shout out to all my haters on that one. The big Ghaz brick is pretty much a lock for any Ork army, and this list backs it up with a nice mix of shooty threats and brawling mechanised forces. These two detachments have really good synergy for ranged units, turning the Flash Gitz in particular into all-purpose damage dealers. You have extra AP if you need it, can hunt tanks wth Rolling Loot-Heap, get into position with Boarding Rush, really just all sorts of things. Meanwhile the Tankbustas and Ghaz block benefit from access to extra Waaagh usage, and although the support for melee threats isn't as good as for shooting, the list gets re-roll Wounds when it needs it, and that's basically all you need. There are several variations on this setup out there, and clearly a tonne of depth in Ork options to work with, well done to Tyler for setting this one up and unleashing it!

Archetype

Recon Stealers

Thoughts

A new challenger in the second undefeated slot here, as Genestealer Cults haven't had a great run of things so far in 11th. This build plays to their strengths, which is having an infinite pool of small units to be places if that's what scoring requires (which in recon it often does), plus a few things you can use to spike foes in emergencies. Here the spiking is relatively literal via large units of Purestrains with the upgrade, and the combination of that, 9" Hidden and reactive moves definitely makes those squads spicy. Hopefully the Tacoma FAQ fixing the inability of units with Leaders to respawn gives GSC a bit more room to manouvre list wise, and we start seeing more cool experiments. Between this and a few other results, Recon definitely seems to be the Disposition that provides the most upside for armies that are a little underpowered. Congratulations to Erik for going undefeated anyway!

Thoughts

Board control faces off against sinister brutality in today's showdown, Recon Guard trying to evade the swift forces of the Emperor's Children. The former shows off the Reconnaissance factor I mentioned above - if your faction can't quite compete with the top end factions on rate or unit quality, this Disposition is the best bet for turning the tables on others. It puts more weight on positioning for your points, and gives you more agency on Primary denial than most Dispositions can manage in some key matchups. This one is a good example - the Emperor's Children have picked Priority Assets instead of Purge, which in many matchups lets them be cagey about how they engage, and leverages the Daemonettes, but is probably not what they want here. The Guard can and will have a solid go at screening them out from ever completing their action, and if they can start on that and keep things rolling, they can be tough to catch. The melee-heavy Emperor's Children would definitely be happier with Purge here, as "charge foes on objectives and paint them in their entrails" is an easier lift (though even there, the Guard can take things slow early and try to delay the ramp up of points for their foes, especially shooty ones).

I don't think that means this is a complete wash for the Emperor's Children, as they do have the classic Priority Assets fallback plan of trying to table the guard at speed. My guess here is that the Militarum will go for a heavy move-blocking plan with the Recon squads to ensure they get to claim the central position, and once they have they can cycle through the Scions to eliminate the highest power target that's available each turn. the Emperor's Children tools aren't bad for working round that mind - they have a unit of Tormentors that can carve out some space during Infiltration, and the cheap Daemonettes give them a higher volume of units that will flay a Guard squad than is normally available. While I'm pretty sure both sides want to go first here to try and assert control over the centre, the EC do also get a bit more of a fallback from their end-of-game scoring condition if forced to go second, as they have a wealth of things that can potentially flip an objective a the last if they're still on the table.

I feel like a decent amount of this is going to come down to what happens when the lascannons swing at the Defiler - if they bounce, maybe the EC have enough momentum to meaningfully sweep up the guard army and pull things back at the end. If the Defiler goes down to easy then I onestly don't even think they EC stay on the table - there's quite a bit of power in the Guard Infantry units, and decent mortal output besides. There are also a lot of lascannons, and if the Defiler kills the Krieg units wrong on the clap back, they can potentially take it down with them. Definitely Guard favoured with these Dispositions, and they took they day, though I'd be fascinated to see this replayed with the EC on Purge.

Thoughts

The original Reconnaissance army - accept no substitutes. Other than maybe the other, already (in)famous Guard build we're going to see later in the week, but Recon element is great also, as we see here. Very different from what Guard have been in the past, and it would be nice to see more conventional builds get a lift, but it's also encouraging that a faction specialist can still deploy them to great effect, congratulations Nemo.
