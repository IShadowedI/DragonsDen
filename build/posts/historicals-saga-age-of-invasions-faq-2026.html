Historicals: SAGA Age of Invasions FAQ 2026

September 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

There’s clearly something in the air over at Studio Tomahawk - hot on the heels of a new FAQ and rework of Age of Vikings comes one for Age of Invasions. That’s particularly exciting as Age of Invasions has legendarily gone un-FAQ’d since release, possibly because it was blessed by Constantine and possibly because Studio Tomahawk just didn’t get around to it. There’s nothing in here on the level of the near complete rework the Irish board got in Age of Vikings, but some big changes for popular factions are sure to put the barbarians among the limitanei, so let’s dive in for a look.

Clarifications and FAQs

There’s a couple of clarifications to core rules and equipment that bring Age of Invasions in line with other, faq’d, SAGA ages. Elephants in units of two (you mad bastard) now do 4, (or 8 vs units that have closed ranks or are mounted on horses) automatic hits on the charge. This was clear in other books that were heavier on the elephants, so your elephants are not getting worse with age and hit just as hard now as they did in the Age of Caesars. Elephant fans will also rejoice that the manubalista hits them with a paltry aggression of 1, proving that the Romans literally couldn’t hit an elephant at this distance. Combined with a couple of the battle board changes, the few elephants we see in Age of Invasions are confirmed to be deadly and hard to remove.

ELEPHANTS. Age of Invasions is a great excuse to slap some powerful pachyderms on the tabletop, as this Sassanid war elephant shows. (Credit - author's collection)

There are some possibly needed clarifications to how Heroic Units work with regards to the Britons board. I’ve played a couple of games against people using this in all sorts of ways, so it’s good to have it nailed down:

  • Warlords in Heroic Units can have a Banner
  • Heroic Units with the Warlord will not count as galvanised even though they’re within range (there’s at least one game I can think of where this would have made a big difference!)
  • You cannot use Bodyguards until all the Heroes are dead (no sacrificing a hearthguard to save a companion)
  • We Obey is measured from any point in the Heroic Unit
I think this comes out fairly even as - I think! - most people were playing with more or less these rules. If you were one of the few who had permanently galvanised Companions in a unit with the Warlord you were playing it wrong anyway!

The biggest clarification is also one of the most obvious - you cannot use the fatigues of a Goth unit activated with Resolve to cancel their activation. The entire and obvious point of the ability was to let you charge with two fatigues, so having this plainly said is much appreciated by all tired Goth lovers everywhere.  

Mercenaries

A few minor clarifications to Mercenaries make for very little change as, beyond the Personal Champion accompanying a Chariot-mounted Warlord having a horse and javelins (not a chariot), these were all clear.

Guides have been clarified to work with Tournament scenarios, guaranteeing their extra terrain placement, and the rules have been clarified. 

Every box should have a dog in it. Barons' War Command, Wargames Atlantic

The Molossians can no longer close ranks, which makes sense, because they’re just eight dogs.

Faction Changes

Three changes that will turn up frequently, and one that no one will notice.

First off, the Romans get to take TWO manuballistae, which immediately struck fear into the heart of everyone who takes large units of cavalry. As a reminder, the manuballista is a 2xL special shooting attack that rolls half as many dice as models in the target unit, and is therefore terrifying. It’s still a unit of levies, so you can’t use Combined Formation on them, but Barritus and In This Sign, Conquer, are still up for grabs.

Wait, Fuck, that's not the right kind of Ballistarii
Credit: Pendulin Briton Companions are clearer, with their own unit profile (identical to the Hearthguard), and can’t be galvanised, but other than that there are no real changes. It all reads better though, which is good. 

Frankish Franciscas can now be equipped by Loyal Mercenaries, which will make for some fun axe throwing Bishops. Franciscas power up some of the main offensive Frankish abilities, so you could plausibly end up with a Bishop who is incredibly good on the charge, but more likely you’re using this to beef up a Personal Champion, who goes from an interesting pick to a very good one with the Frankish board.

The one no one will notice is Theoderic, King of the Visigoths, has got a little bit choppier, as instead of getting bonus dice he just receives additional aggression for each fatigue. Unfortunately, you almost never see legendary units on the table!   

Battle Board Changes

Saxons

A unit of Late Anglo-Saxon warriors. Credit: Eric Lofgren, painting by Nevermore Painting Studios.

Two changes for the Saxons that amount to a small buff. Ferocious has had it’s downside removed, as previously you’d exchange 3 attack dice for the loss of a defence dice. This was pretty poor, unless you were beating up levy, so the new version is much improved. Now, you get the three dice (in every melee in the turn), and the first time you win you power up Furor, letting you situationally clear off fatigue. Furor is still risky, but improving dice economy is always a good thing. 

A substantial change to Jeers as well, where previously you’d hoped for a ranged unit to kill 6 of your levy, leading to a situation where you were hoping to fail defence rolls. Instead, the new version boosts your defence against shooting and fatigues the enemy if they fail to remove a model. Matches the idea of jeering your enemy’s paltry shooting much better than the old version!

Britons

Britons have changes to three abilities and one outright replacement.

Hold the Line! Is entirely gone, so you can’t increase the armour of most of your army anymore at the cost of an uncommon and a rare. Good riddance, because that was the leading cause of the Briton Clump, the second most annoying thing about SAGA. The replacement, Forced March, might actually let Briton players move around the board in more than one big pile, as it allows a Hero and up to 2 units to galvanise and move S - not much of a move, and takes an activation, but allows you to galvanise whoever you like for only one common dice. 

Pendragon became more flexible, giving you the choice of both prior effects (or both for the cost of a rare). It’s very good in the rare form, removing fatigue in melee and allowing you to reroll attack dice, but also then works as a situational fatigue removal or attack boost if you need it. Working on your heroes only, this is clearly designed to be an every-turn ability for a Heroic Warlord unit. 

Tenacious can now cost a common, so you’ll put dice on it every time - gaining two defence dice and cancelling a casualty is incredibly good, particularly when you can galvanise much easier now.

The new Shieldwall functions as a bubble of light cover based on a hero, providing light cover to all galvanised units.

Franks

Franks were clearly doing okay, as changes here are fairly small. Momentum now no longer works on Mercenaries, and to be honest, you’re lucky it did in the first place, Clovis. Determination and +1 attack dice is still fantastic. Meanwhile, the Frankish People is clarified to be targeting a single hero, rather than just 3 units around some heroes somewhere.

Goths

Pleasantly for me, as I’m playing Goths in an event in a few weeks, there’s little change here. What change there is is a minor nerf, as Gratia now only provides +1 to defence dice if your unit is on foot. It was too good on Cataphracts, but if they’d waited a few weeks to update it I’d have had mathematical proof it was. Ah well.

Victrix Islamic Horse Archers because we don't have any pictures of Huns - credit Bair

Huns

Bair continually suggests he’s going to build and play Huns, but he’s yet to do it, so I feel good about these changes happening before he has a chance to beat me with them.

Two small nerfs to Hun abilities. Panic has been changed to remove the dice recycling ability, which is good news for Hun enemies as it’s a dice hungry board. The Endless Horde also receives a hit, with the same no fatigue, three unit movement activation remaining, but ruling out any further shooting from those units. It’s now something you’re using to get away from your enemies rather than to spool up the composite bow machine gun.

Picts

Some significant changes here aimed at getting you moving and attacking rather than sitting in solid cover waiting for a reluctant enemy charge.

On that theme, Scouts receives some clearer wording but remains unchanged, letting you zip about the board with ease, while Feint now only works as the attacker. Stalking also gets a bit of a hit, as the ability to sit next to dangerous terrain has been removed - uneven terrain only.

Masters of the Field changes substantially. You’re still picking a terrain piece and getting a benefit for being within it, but now you’re getting additional defence dice instead of solid cover. Ignoring the movement penalty has been shifted to a rare dice effect. Not sure if this is a sidegrade or a significant change, but units in uneven, solid cover, terrain will only benefit.

Romans

Wargames Atlantic Goth Warriors and Victrix Unarmoured Late Romans in comparison. Credit: Lupe

You no longer need an advanced course in logical reasoning to use Combined Formation, as it’s now very, very clear. 

Sassanids

A swathe of changes for the Sassanids that pushes them away from taking all composite bow warriors (take Huns!) or all bow Levy, putting some serious weight behind Elephant and Heathguard units instead. 

We ALSO only have one picture of Sassanids and I've already used it in this article. Saracen horse archers can be deadly. Credit - Ne Cede Malis

Ahriman’s Curse has been nerfed to only give the previously rare dice effect, cancelling cover for a unit being shot at. Still useful, and goes to a common or uncommon dice. 

That’s handy because you’ll want to use a rare dice on Second Wind, which now removes two fatigues from an Elephant or unit without ranged weapons on a rare dice, while retaining common and uncommon single fatigue removal.

Inexorable Advance sees a huge change, with the previous casualty causing effect gone altogether. Now you’re using any dice to inflict fatigue on a unit within S of a single activated Cataphract or Elephant unit, with a rare dice making these activations fatigue free. Less complicated than previous, but potentially much less impactful. Inflicting fatigue is useful, doing so with a fatigue free move is useful, but with good positioning you could chip away at enemy units for a common dice in the previous incarnation.

Armoured is now an activation ability, giving you another (albeit expensive) way to activate for a charge. The effects remain the same but are (handily!) taken out of the melee SAGA ability chain.

Volley fire looks like it’s changed a lot, but as helpfully pointed out to me it ends up doing what it was supposed to be doing in the first place. Instead of gaining bows and determination, a composite bow unit increases its range by M. The results are still shooting L, but now without determination you’re going to have to use a dice to activate them.

Overall

I know you’re thinking this all looks pretty good, but what does it mean for me, Justinian, Emperor of Rome?

All told the changes here aren’t as enormous as in some of the other SAGA age FAQs, but there’s still a shakeup coming. Most of that shakeup is that if you haven’t painted a Personal Champion yet, you really should. A lot of boards just got better for it, and if you’re without one in the Franks and Britons particularly, you should get on it now. Footsore is a good choice, go have fun.

Britons will see a much more interactive playstyle than castling up and slowly walking across the field with armour 5, and might even see some serious use out of Personal Champions and Companions as heroes able to galvanise outflung units. This is a fantastic change, even if the battle board is much the same, as it will let Briton players have fun with things like “movement” that they haven’t utilised before.

It’s likely that you’re celebrating as a Roman player just on the strength of gaining another manuballista and mourning your old list as a Sassanid, so if your plan was to shore up a shaky claim to the purple with a jaunt to the Euphrates, you’re in luck. If you did go all in on composite bows for the Sassanids, the new board will force a different playstyle, where elephants and cataphracts are your main damage dealers - they’re a little easier to clear fatigue off and hit just as hard as ever. It might all end up as a slightly weaker Sassanid board, but to see if that’s true we’ll have to get out there and play!
