James Bond Should Be a Wolf

November 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

I don’t actually know what the Robert Pattinson James Bond era is going to look like; no one does yet. The Pattinson era is about 24 hours old. What we know for sure is that he has the job, and at least for the first film, Denis Villeneuve (Sicario, Dune, you know who he is) will be directing him. There is word floating around that Pattinson has a three-film deal; that’s about as officially confirmed as the murky stories around the casting itself, where all that’s really known is everything came together very quickly at the last moment. The inference you can take from that is, perhaps, that the slightly-younger men known to have auditioned for the role -- Jack Lowden, recently of an ever-growing number of seasons of Slow Horses; Josh O’Connor, recently of a number of award-winning pictures and, additionally, the third Knives Out; Callum Turner, recently married to Dua Lipa -- didn’t exactly impress the new Bond management team over at Amazon MGM Studios. It’s also possible that when Pattinson became available and sent in his tape, it simply became undeniable to put the A-lister currently on an incredible heater into the role. Pattinson’s lucky they weren’t doing this last year, which was mostly defined for him by extremely mixed reactions to Mickey 17 and Die My Love.

Alright before we go any further, though, this is going to bother me -- here’s the promotional picture for Pattinson as Bond, the one that got released alongside the news.

Robert Pattinson as James Bond. Credit: Jaap Buitendijk/Amazon MGM Studios

I am not what you would call a “gun guy.” I don’t know the things, and outside of fiction, I don’t particularly like the things. But I’ve learned enough about them to play characters in tabletop RPGs that seem like they know what they’re talking about in genre work like Hunter: The Reckoning or Delta Green, which means I know enough to say that the thing on the top of this gun is a reflex sight, and the thing below it is some kind of tactical flashlight, and aesthetically speaking: What in the world is James Bond doing with that shit on his gun? Get that outta here. That’s Call of Duty multiplayer behavior. Hopefully they just had it hanging around for the shoot and the new team’s concept of James Bond isn’t a guy who needs to upgrade his pistol to add a bar of accuracy at the cost of a bar of handling.

GoldenEye 007 for the Nintendo 64 aside, the character isn’t iconic for his gunplay; the Bond game out earlier in the year understood that. IOI’s vision of Bond didn’t quite do it for me either, though; a bit too modern, and a bit too nice. In the cutscenes, that is -- in the gameplay he was pretty much right there. My ideal Bond doesn’t do much aiming down sights; honestly, if he’s shooting people, I’d prefer him looking them in the eyes while he does it from close range, maybe after hitting them a couple times with the gun. And while you’re at it, hopefully he’s doing it somewhere between the years 1956 and 1988. That’s a range for period pieces that covers a couple different actual periods, but all of them are years where the character’s image (both his self-image and the one he has as a brand) as the ultimate man of the empire for post-war Great Britain makes the most sense. I enjoyed the post-Cold War malaise of the first 90s Bond offering, speaking of GoldenEye, but the Brosnan films managed to land that once before becoming completely unmoored in their search for suitable new villains in the post-Soviet era. And, let’s be honest, Great Britain: You’re not what you once were, either. You don’t want to see James Bond up there on the screen representing the empire and the enterprise of Keir Starmer and Nigel Farage anymore than we do. At least for us, that’d be conceptually funny. But Bond is a retreat into the past; the IOI game attempted to bring the franchise into the present with a story about evil tech CEOs and AI that felt credible as a video game plot but was merely acceptable and unremarkable as far as a Bond story goes. It didn’t help that they handed Lenny Kravitz the only actual role that jumped off the screen the way bad guys in Bond movies used to, because Kravitz did not have the juice for it. Unless Villeneuve has an idea to completely remake the franchise -- which is presumably not what he was hired to do and not what Amazon MGM is paying for, but is at least theoretically on the table -- the next James Bond film has the choice of either continuing to pretend the end of the 20th century didn’t actually happen, and Great Britain is still as it once was, or it can just actually go back there to when the suit still fit.

And in that spirit, I want the proper James Bond for the era: James Bond as the wolf. This is not quite the same as Daniel Craig’s Bond, who was the sociopathic thug humanized, over and over again, by the love of a good woman -- about three-fourths of Craig’s Casino Royale and bits and pieces of Quantum of Solace had the sort of energy I was looking for, but especially in the later entries (as Craig himself checked out of the franchise mentally) there were only echoes of that character remaining as the aristo Spy Batman lore of Skyfall and all the Blofeld nonsense of Spectre took the character over. Thanks to a quirk of when I saw the movies for the first time growing up and what I was reading when I did, the character in literature I identify the most with what I want out of Bond predates him -- Philip Lombard, the second-to-last to die in Agatha Christie’s And Then There Were None. He is, in fact, described consistently in terms of animalistic predation; Christie uses the panther as her first touchpoint, because at the time the novel takes place the imagination of British Empire was captured in no small part by Africa, but on at least two occasions his “wolflike smile” is mentioned, and that’s the detail that stuck with me -- it’s the detail Vera mentions in the last moments the two share together. Lombard is distinguished by being a genuinely brave, accomplished, skilled, and charismatic man; this sits alongside his self-interest, propensity for violence, and misogyny, the last of which is his downfall as he doesn’t believe Vera, a woman, has the mettle to kill him. These are all distinguishing qualities of Bond to me, even -- especially! -- those three negative traits; I see some of them in Connery, in Dalton, definitely in Craig, and I am very confident that Pattinson can bring that same energy to the character, especially with Denis Villeneuve directing. Obviously there can be quite a light touch on the misogyny unless the Bond girl is going to do him like Vera did Lombard (it's a brave new world, anything is technically possible under new management), but the first scenes between Craig's Bond and Vesper Lynd don't work like they do without the chauvinism of empire informing him.

But let’s be real: Robert Pattinson can bring any energy he wants to the character. They could tell him the next film’s going to be a love letter to the Roger Moore days, and what’s more an intentional comedy to boot, and he’d deliver a fantastic farce of a Bond performance. The film would bomb, but Pattinson would be impeccable in it. So he’ll do fine in whatever Villeneuve and the new Bond executive management team are cooking up for him -- I am ambivalent on whether or not this new group will step in it, because we haven’t seen what they are or are not capable of yet.

https://bsky.app/profile/danielduncan.bsky.social/post/3n2gua5zgds2g

That’s not a confidence I had about the other named public choices. O’Connor is one thing -- he hasn’t proven he can play this exact kind of role yet, maybe, but come on; he’d probably kill it. I can see both the studio wanting a surer thing and him maybe not wanting to sign a four movie deal just as he’s coming into his own as a critical darling. (In fact, as I'm putting the finishing touches on this, there's reporting coming out that O'Connor removed himself from consideration for precisely those reasons.) Callum Turner is coming off a hard L with that weird movie about the Sex Purge; not only was it a bomb but very notably, Turner’s character doesn’t actually get laid during the Sex Purge. That’s evangelical behavior, not Bond behavior. And Jack Lowden…well, earlier in the day before Pattinson was announced as Bond, it came out that Slow Horses had been renewed for an additional two seasons, so if he stays there everyone will be happy. He is very good at doing River Cartwright things. Not a single one of those things ever made me think he’d be any good at James Bond. If there’s a movie spy role he fits into for me, it’s not even in the Bond franchise; he reminds me of nobody so much as William Brandt, Jeremy Renner’s secret agent character from the Mission: Impossible series of films. This isn’t me being mean! If I was being mean I’d say he reminds me of Josh Holloway’s character from the Mission: Impossible series, who dies in the Ghost Protocol cold open. If Lowden had to be a Bond, he'd be the James Bond from the IOI video game, not the one you'd see on the silver screen. (There was a report that Nicholas Hoult was under consideration? Not a Bond for me, but a fantastic choice for when they do the 006 MI6-agent-turned-supervillain thing again.)

So I am cautiously optimistic. About the future of this franchise, at least. Pattinson’s other big tentpole character, Batman? How is he gonna have time to work both characters, you ask? Well, if you’ve paid any attention to goings-on over at Warner Brothers and DC Comics over the past couple months, that is a question that seems to have swiftly answered itself. Who knows what shape that second Batman movie will be in, when we eventually see it; if we eventually see it, given that studio's previous behavior. What seems certain, however, is that there will not be a third. The very fact this all came together so quickly in such a hurry over the last two weeks should tell you what you need to know: Robert Pattinson’s big IP blockbuster schedule has suddenly opened up.
