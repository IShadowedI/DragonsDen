Tabletop Battles Reviews: Broken Token Kill Team Tools, Part Two - Markers, Rings, and Snap Bracelet Ruler

June 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Something that isn’t really told to players when they first pick up Kill Team is that it’s a very token heavy game, more so than any other game that Games Workshop produces. There’s going to be a whole lot of them on any given board at any given time. While GW does provide cardboard tokens for the game, there’s plenty to be desired for a more premium product, as well as a demand for game aids that GW doesn’t provide, like objective control markers or game trackers. 

Enter Broken Token Gear, whose online store is dedicated to an impressive library of tokens, tools, and game aids for Kill Team. Alexis, who operates Broken Token Gear, was kind enough to provide us here at Tabletop Battles the entire library of products from his store for review, which is expansive enough that we’ve decided it best to split this review up into three parts to spare my delicate fingers from carpal tunnel. 

You can find the first part in the series here. In this second part, we’ll be reviewing the products focused on measuring markers and operatives on the Killzone, such as the objective markers and smoke sets, the control range circles, and the Snap Bracelet Ruler.

Objective Markers and Smoke Markers

While these are two separate products, they fill a very similar role, so to avoid repeating myself I’m going to discuss both of them here. 

Objective markers are key to any game of Kill Team, and Broken Token has an offering of very high quality objective markers for use. Each set of three numbered markers comes in one of four colors, each themed around a different Killzone. They’re a high quality plastic, they’ll bend without breaking while being nice and thin for easy placement under objectives. As far as objective markers go, I really love these. They not only look great on the table, but they’re boundaries are nice and clear, with bright colors that pop. The numbering is also a nice touch, especially in the odd Joint Ops scenario where those are important. 

Credit: Broken Token

I love the smoke markers as well for similar reasons. Both of the color options are nice and bright, and they look aces on the table compared to the usual smoke markers on the market. The smoke decal looks great too, and I’ve already gotten a few compliments on them when I’ve used these markers in my own games. 

Both of these products are easy to recommend if you’re in the market for smoke or objectives. Broken Token Gear’s emphasis on a premium look really shines here, we love a clear and accurate gaming aid that looks great doing it.

Engagement Range Control Rings

Out of all of the Broken Token products I’ve tried out so far, the Control Range Rings are least favorite of the bunch. 

The positive stuff first; like all of the other items from Broken token, the quality of the material is still top notch. This is a high quality plastic, and despite it being very thin it feels durable. You can travel with these very easily without worry they’ll get damaged. Plus, setting up the bendable ghost handles is easy, and there’s even a handy guide on how to do it. Additionally, these rings come in every circular base size currently available in Kill Team, so every team will get some utility from it.

Issues arise with both the look of these rings, and their actual practicality on the tabletop. Despite being a very nice plastic, the fact that they’re almost totally clear with the minimal text makes them look cheap compared to the other products from Broken Token Gear. I understand the logic here was to make it clear as to when models were in control range, but even more colorful text or a circle around the edge of each marker would really help make this product pop. 

The second concern is with how practical these rings are during the game. Because of the inch diameter around each marker, you can’t just place them where you intend to end a movement without potentially knocking around everything nearby. In order to use the ring to check control range, you need to either hover over where you intend to put the model, which is a pretty inaccurate method, or move everything around to place the marker, which raises questions of putting something back incorrectly or tampering with the table. If measuring control was more litigious, I’d be more keen on the control rings, but measuring an inch from a model's base tends to be pretty easy to accurately confirm.

I get the idea behind these rings, and even applaud it, but they’re one of the few items from Broken Token I can’t recommend for most players, as using them during games feels very clunky.   

Snap Bracelet Ruler

Okay, this thing is just a ton of fun. I love the Snap Bracelet Ruler. 

Like all of the other products made by Broken Token, the quality of the ruler is great. It’s a nice, soft plastic, and the snap bracelet part of it is well made and keeps working well, even after being slapped onto your wrist dozens of times. The lettering for both versions sent to me, the orange version and the black version, are crisp and legible, and won’t rub off after repeated use. All major pluses here for using the ruler, as the most important thing a ruler needs to be is eminently readable. 

Speaking of the ruler, the snap bracelet itself is nine inches long, with each inch marked off with a dash. In a fun throwback to the previous edition of KT, the 1”, 2”, 3”, and 6” markers on the bracelet are marked with the old measurement shapes from the last edition, while the eight inch mark simply has an 8. I think this is cute, but if I could make one change to the snap bracelet ruler, it would be to just do numbers the whole way. 

On the reverse side of the ruler, Broken Token has done a great job with printing a reference sheet for all of the weapon special rules currently in the game. This is a godsend for new players, they don’t even need to look in a book to remember what the keywords on their weapons do; all they need to do is check their ruler! It’s a thoughtful addition, and I applaud the Broken Token for it.

Credit: Broken Token

I’d be remiss, of course, if I didn’t mention that a huge selling point of the snap bracelet ruler is straight up the novelty of it. It’s just plain fun to snap the bracelet on your wrist at the end of a game, or see the “ooohs” and “aaahs” from folks when you pull the bracelet off your wrist for a measurement. And it doesn’t look terrible on your wrist either, though that’s in the context of being at a game store or event. I don’t think this bracelet is as iconic in, like, the shopping mall. But you’re welcome to try! 

Bonus: Broken Token Ops Counter

Credit: Broken Token

This one is an odd duck, but handy if you prefer a more portable analogue way of keeping track of score, the Ops Counter is for you. Basically, this is a handheld clicker with parts to track: the Crit Op, and the Tac Op (it doesn't track the Kill op). Every time you score a point, you press the button, and click, the number goes up. These go all the way from 0-9, though the only way to reset them is to just keep clicking through.

It's not the most efficient way to keep track of points, but it is nice and portable, fitting right in the palm of your hand, and that can come in handy at crowded events where there's fairly limited table space. Plus, it looks nice, and I like the contrasting orange and black colors. I wouldn't say this one is as handy of a game aid as the snap bracelet, but I'm sure there's folks out there who swear by this thing.

Final Thoughts

While the Control Rings just don’t do it for me, I think all of the other products here are great for any Kill Team fan. Broken Token has a keen eye for making these items look great on the tabletop or on your wrist, and the Objective Markers/Smoke Rings and the Snap Bracelet rules are items that are both useful and sturdy. If you’re in the market to upgrade you table at home, or want a fun gift for the Kill Team fan in your life, I highly recommend these products.
