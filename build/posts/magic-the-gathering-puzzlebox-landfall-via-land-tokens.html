Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Landfall via Land Tokens

July 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Landfall is, without exaggeration, the most common archetype you'll see in Commander. Discounting typal strategies, nothing approaches the ubiquity of 'play Land, get advantage', and to some extent that makes sense; Lands in Magic are a burden most decks are saddled with, a 'necessary evil' to allow your cool cards to do cool things. Even in design discussions surrounding the original Zendikar, Mark Rosewater quickly realized how appealing it was to allow players to enjoy their vegetables, and since the early ~2020s Landfall as an ability word has been functionally deciduous. Something far newer, however, is that of Land tokens—in the formal sense, not laundering them via animation & Creature clones—by way of Overlord of the Hauntwoods in Duskmourn, followed shortly by Mutable Explorer in Lorwyn Eclipsed. Landfall strategies are often rate-limited by, well, the physical Land cards available to them, so what if that weren't your limiting factor? Join me as we take a quick dip into the nascent world of Land tokens, and why Ghired, Mirror of the Wilds might just be the coolest way to play Landfall.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

A Horror; An Explorer

First, let's meet our trio of key cards, starting with the main event. Ghired is of course a hasty 3 mana Commander that affords all our nontoken Creatures the ability to copy a token that was created that turn, which does generally mean you need to plan in advance how best to make key tokens while retaining untapped, un-summoning-sick bodies to extract additional value. The good news is that Ghired himself has haste, and is cheap enough to be the cherry on top of an otherwise reasonable turn. Moreover, a lot of the best Landfall payoffs (Tireless Tracker, Tireless Provisioner, etc) lack inherent tap abilities, and you wouldn't generally attack with them anyway, so Ghired gives them something to do. Stone-Seeder Hierophant is the best of these Land-matter enablers, because as we'll see, that particular trigger effect to untap can serve as a combo enabler for our strategy.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Onto the token producers, and we'll start with Mutable Explorer. This mischievous little scamp is eminently findable, as not only can we use Green's suite of Creature tutors for it or Overlord, but in being a Changeling we can pull upon some additional consistency. It's an Ally for Tale of Momo, a Squirrel for Thornvault Forager, and even a Spider Hero for Radioactive Spider. Now, I don't think it's wise to just use Mutable Explorer as the sole target for these cards, but luckily there's competent secondary targets which are, at worst, bodies we can use alongside Ghired to copy a relevant token. For those three examples, I'd call out Smellerbee, Rebel Fighter/The Duke, Rebel Sentry, Curious Forager/Bloodroot Apothecary, & Spectacular Spider-Man/Spider-Man, Brooklyn Visionary. Not to say you should include all of those, but instead should select your favorite alternate target alongside the Explorer to add redundancy for their searches. Overlord is more straightforward, usually being cast for 3 mana, and able to also create Land tokens on the attack, which is especially relevant for Ghired's 'entered this turn' restriction. As an Impended card, it's able to be turned back into a Creature by way of cards like the OG Flicker and Felidar Guardian, each of which can also result in an additional Landfall trigger. Note also that there's a couple of cards which make Land Creature Tokens in our colors—specifically Dryad Arbors—, being Staff of Titania & Awaken the Woods. These are just a hair worse for us, given as Creatures the Dryads are a bit more fragile, but definitely worth including. Equipment like the Staff are reasonably tutorable, but Sorceries less so. We know how to find these Creatures, we know our Commander, and we have a rough idea of the payoff. How's it all come together?

Landfalling Upwards

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

I think one of the best skills to develop when putting together an off-the-wall deck is the ability to re-contextualize, and analyze things from their 'standard' scenario. In our case, once all is stripped of its luster, we're playing a deck with the following ability at its core: "Nontoken Creatures you control have {T}: Get a Landfall trigger, and ~1 mana". There's setup required, certainly, but between the ability to readily find, trigger (e.g. blink), and swarm with Land tokens I think that's a compelling statement to be made. Even once we've had our Commander removed the first time, something you can expect to happen often, paying 5 mana for a Commander that can come in and immediately create another Land has immense appeal. So long as you're getting either Treasure or Land with your taps, you can afford Ghired's climbing Commander Tax, for that beautiful ability in context. Even so, there's a bit of redundancy in the form of Second Harvest, Esika's Chariot, & For the Common Good, but Ghired is the main source for this. You will in the vast majority of your games have a pop-off turn, where you create ~4 Land tokens and get the table spooked, but from there you have a ton of benefits compared to other decks that do a single big turn; Lands are implicitly difficult to remove, and per the Bracket system verboten to remove en masse, meaning your resources stack forever. Being a Landfall deck, you kind of aren't allowed to lose your core form of advantage, because of how Commander's social contract works. Not saying you should exploit that, but indeed that decks like this one hypothetically do.

Even if you aren't actively copying a Land token over and over, doing so for something as innocuous as a Vibranium or Meteor (via various cards from Marvel or Roxanne, Starfall Savant) still gets out of hand rather quickly. Heck, the new Heartstone producers in Reality Fracture like Woodwork Prodigy seem like shoo-ins for a more normal Ghired. The difference here, that's gone relatively understated, is that Landfall synergies aren't just difficult to remove...they're some of the best value-over-time engines in the entire format. Most Landfall decks will get 1-3 triggers each turn, with more off of something like a Scapeshift, but here we can essentially treat our capacity to tap like a mana-positive storm trigger, able to go far longer by way of 'token every turn' cards like Three Blind Mice & City of Death. In a midrange mirror, with no worries of combo, you're bound to just get ahead of everyone unless thoroughly focused, barring a lack of access to your initial Land token producer. Even so, as I alluded to, this deck can run the fantastic (and in-theme) combo of Ghired, Stone-Seeder Hierophant, and a Land token that entered this turn, producing infinite mana & infinite Landfall triggers. All those cards which can find Green Creatures, like Overlord & Explorer? Surprise, they're also part of your endgame.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Landfall is fun, it feels good to play, and that's been known since the very first feedback Wizards received following its debut as an ability word. There's a host of reasons it's a quintessential archetype in Commander, but this is the first concept I've had that breaks parity on what Landfall is normally limited by: Land, physically. Most Commander decks run 28-38 Lands, with Landfall decks maybe getting into the mid-40s, but here you're limited not by the 'vegetables', but by already-good nontoken Creatures that functionally trigger themselves. You ditch the downside, the opportunity cost, and end up with Landfall cards unshackled from their purpose, as a means to make Magic's least exciting card type more engaging. I do encourage you, prospective Landfall player, to try building something like this list. I do encourage you to turn your Lands into Creatures, by way of Kamahl's Will and the like, and tap them to create additional Everywheres. Show your playgroup just why Landfall ought to be rate-limited, and have fun.
