11th Edition Detachment Focus: Guardians of the Throne

October 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Following hot on the heels of the Emperor’s Angels come his Praetorian Guard. The Adeptus Custodes get an early 11th edition Codex, and with it a slew of new rules and Detachments. In this article, we’re taking a look at the Guardians of the Throne Detachment.

Every 11th Edition Codex comes with an all-rounder 3DP Detachment aimed at providing a solid introduction to the way the faction plays. These have been hit and miss so far, in that there's been one hit for Orks and one miss for Marines. Happily for fans of glorious golden hosts, Guardians of the Throne lands firmly in the hit column, providing a very solid way to start your journey into fielding Custodes.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of this Codex and a provisional version of the Munitorum Field Manual. Any rules or points costs we reference are subject to change until they are published on the Warhammer 40K App.

Guardians of the Throne

Detachment Cost: 3 DP

Force Disposition: Priority Assets and Purge the Foe

Guardians is the 3 DP beginner-friendly Detachment in this Codex designed to let you do a little bit of everything with your golden army without having to overthink about pesky things like good unit choices or synergies. Just trundle up and play. On the plus side, this Detachment has access to some great strats and enhancements meaning if you want to keep things simple you don’t end up losing out on too much of the power trying to mix and match the others will get you.

Pendulin's Custodes

Detachment Rule: Martial Mastery

In the Fight Phase when an Adeptus Custodes is selected to Fight, that unit’s melee attacks have either Sustained Hits 1 or Lethal Hits. Current Custodes players will recognize these as the 10th Edition nerfed Ka’tah forms that were the books’ original main rule. Given just how hard and fast every unit in the codex can hit, having access to these is a neat little boon to apply army-wide.

Favored Ka’tah

None. You aren’t running a Shield Host you selfish dingus, you don’t get this cool rule. All Ka’tahs are equal to you.

Enhancements

  • Bane of Abominations (20pts): Adeptus Custodes model only. This model’s attacks that target an enemy Character/Monster/Vehicle have +1 to wound rolls. This is just an excellent enhancement to have with the boosts to the output of the entire gamut of Custodes’ characters.
  • Castellan’s Mark (25pts): Adeptus Custodes model only. Once both players have finished deploying their armies you may select up to 3 units to place into strategic reserves regardless of how many units are already there. Castellan’s Mark has seen a few iterations over the years, all of them with powerful knock-on effects and this one is no exception. Being able to potentially null-deploy your army can be amazing.
  • Eagle’s Eye (30pts): Adeptus Custodes model only. This model has +1 wound and once per battle when attacks are allocated to this model you can improve its invulnerable save to 3+. This is fine but you probably won’t use it.
  • Emperor’s Light (15pts): This weapon can be allocated to an Adeptus Custodes model in your army. It grants them Extra Attacks 3 at WS 2+, Str 5, AP 2 and 2 DMG. When combined with the Sustained Hits from Martial Mastery you can make a pretty brutal one man MEQ wrecking crew if you so desire.

Stratagems

  • Superhuman Focus (1CP): At the end of any phase, select an Adeptus Custodes unit that is not readied and make them readied. There are times when you need to do more than one type of karate in a turn. This lets you do that without stacking expensive Shield Captains throughout your list.
  • Shield of Honour (1CP): At the start of the fight phase when an enemy model engaged with an Adeptus Custodes Infantry/Mounted makes its attacks, if it did not choose to make all of those attacks against that unit then it suffers -1 to hit AND -1 to wound for all of its attacks. Punishing your opponent’s big punchers for not focusing down a single unit is a helluva drug.
  • In Auramite Clad (1CP): When a friendly Adeptus Custodes Unit that is not a Wardens unit is targeted by a shooting attack or in the fight phase, reduce the AP of those attacks by 1. Armour of Contempt on 2+ armour saves is just swell. 
  • Unlimited Endurance (1CP): When a friendly Adeptus Custodes unit ends an advance move they are still eligible to declare a charge and all of their ranged weapons gain the Assault keyword if they did not already have it. Advance + Shoot/Charge on a stick is something you’ll always desire given the low activation count of some of your lists.
  • Prime Target (1CP): In your shooting phase when an Adeptus Custodes unit is selected to shoot, that unit may reroll 1 hit, wound, and damage roll. 3 CP rerolls for the price of 1? That’s a pretty good rate!
  • Swift as the Eagle (1CP): During your opponent’s movement phase when an enemy unit ends its movement within 8" of a friendly unengaged Trusted Sentinel unit. That Trusted Sentinel Unit can make a normal move of d3+3". As always, reactive moves are incredible and the narrow band of it being only on Trusted Sentinels is not that big of a dip. This strat is also found in the Honoured Companions Detachment.

Detachment Combos

None, you can’t do that. It would be cheating.

Is It Good?

Custodian Sentinels. Credit: Pendulin

It’s 3 DP, so while fine, it won't beat out the best combos you can land with other Detachments. But also, it still has some sick stratagems, a decent Detachment rule and a couple of really solid enhancements so it’s ‘good enough’.

Final Thoughts

As far as 3DP Detachments go, Guardians of the Throne is pretty well-designed. If you’re a beginner player that doesn’t want to overcomplicate things and get used to the army you honestly can’t go wrong with this set up. The stratagems are all very useful to amazing and 2 of the enhancements are great to have in your back pocket. The lack of a Favoured Ka’tah and the sheer power of most of the other 1 DP Detachments is the main reason you’ll rarely want to call on this one.
