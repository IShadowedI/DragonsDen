Detachment Focus: Frenzied Host

August 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In our Detachment Focus series we take a deep dive into an army’s Detachments, covering what’s in them, how they play, and how they’ll fit into the broader meta and your games. In this Detachment Focus we’re looking at the Frenzied Host Detachment for the Emperor's Children.

The Emperor's Children were in a weird place coming into 11th edition, heavily dependent on Defilers for competitive play and then suddenly saddled with the reality that the army's best Detachment - Coterie of the Conceited - was labeled a 3 DP Detachment and given Priority Assets as a disposition - not ideal for an elite army with few action units. Fortunately the Frenzied Host Detachment has been a major boon for the army, helping other Detachments punch up by turning the army into a kind of "World Eaters lite" with combat bonuses whenever you Advance or Fall Back.

Detachment Cost: 1 DP

Force Disposition: Disruption

The Emperor's Children tend to rely on a melee-heavy approach to combat, owing partly to a lack of good ranged options. Unfortunately, their melee options, particularly those on Infractors - the army's workhorse - tend to be a bit lackluster, with just a large number of S4, AP-1 attacks and a reliance on Lords Exultant and [LETHAL HITS] to punch up into heavier targets. Frenzied Host helps with that substantially, giving your battleline units +1S when a unit makes an Advance or Fall Back move. Your Battleline units here are Tormentors and Infractors, both of which commonly show up in Emperor's Children lists.

This Detachment is all about your Battleline units and has the unique HOST tag, meaning it can’t be taken with other HOST Detachments. Specifically, that means you can't double up with this and Mercurial Host.

Detachment Rule: Frantic Focus

When a friendly Emperor’s Children Battleline unit is selected to make an Advance or Fall Back move, that unit’s attacks have +1 S until the end of the turn.

This is a very solid ability. One of the key challenges that you’ll face with your Infractors is that they’re only throwing out S4 AP-1 attacks, and while you have a good volume of those, you’re going to lose a lot of them to failed wound rolls against T4 and T5 targets. You normally get around this by adding a Lord Exultant, but that only goes so far, and is better for punching up into high-Toughness targets. This lets you more effectively convert attacks into kills and punch up into T8 and T9 targets. It’s particularly good on the Lord Exultant, where going to S8 with [LANCE] helps you hit key breakpoints and wound units with T5-7 on a 2+.

This rule notably also doesn’t specify melee attacks, so your Tormentors can benefit as well, buffing their plasma gun and meltagun - having a S10 meltagun is a pretty solid deal. Just be sure to keep tabs on when and where you're falling back and charging so you don't run afoul of the thrill seekers restrictions on which units you can charge or shoot.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Enhancements

  • Euphoric Crown. Lord Exultant only. The model’s melee attacks have +1 S. Now we’re getting indulgent, but in a good way. The idea here is that your Lord Exultant can go to either S9 on his spear (with LANCE) or S10 on his power fist whenever you advance or fall back and charge. S9 on the Spear is likely the better deal of the two, as you’ll be charging often with him while he’s alive. Being S9 instead of S8 gets you past some interesting break points like Rhinos and Bloat-Drones, but there aren’t that many T9/10 threats.
  • Howling Plate. Lord Exultant only. The unit’s ranged attacks have +1 AP. This is potentially pretty solid, giving you AP-1 on things like bolter shots and AP-4 on overcharged plasma. Getting good value out of this mostly means putting a Lord Exultant on a 10-model unit of Tormentors, though - and that’s just not really what you want to be doing with an Emperor’s Children army.

Stratagems

  • Possessive Mania (1 CP). Used in your opponent’s Shooting phase or the Fight phase when an opponent targets one of your BATTLELINE units in range of an objective. Attacks targeting your unit have -1 AP until the end of that attack sequence. This is a very situational Armour of Contempt, and while that’s a very solid ability (especially in melee), it’s also pretty limited. It definitely has more play in 11th, where you no longer get a bonus to your save from cover.
  • Agonised Cacophony (1 CP). Used at the start of your Shooting phase to pick an enemy unit that is within 12” of one of your BATTLELINE units and visible to it. That unit gets +6” Detection Range. Shooting in EC is pretty limited, and the units you have who can shoot tend to be pretty fast and they can all Advance and Shoot. Which is all to say that most of the time you won’t need this.
  • Absolute Sensory Overload (1 CP). Used in your Shooting phase, when a BATTLELINE unit shoots. That unit’s ranged attacks won’t prevent it from being hidden. This is cute, and can be helpful if you need to shoot your way out of some trouble near your home objective, but it’s pretty situational.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Playing This Detachment

The Enhancements here are solid buffs for your Lord Exultants and the Stratagems are only OK. What this amounts to is that you're here for the Detachment ability, which is very good on its own. It gives you much more consistent output into marine-shaped targets and helps you punch up more effectively into bigger threats with units which otherwise struggle to contribute. Every Emperor's Children army takes Infractors, and having a Detachment to make them shine helps the army across the board. It's worth noting that the Detachment rule here won't work on Daemonettes despite those being BATTLELINE; they lack the EMPEROR'S CHILDREN keyword.

Because of the simple nature of this buff and the passive benefit, you can pair this Detachment with any of the other 2 DP options, giving you immediate support, even if the Disposition you get - Disruption - isn't worth taking. The key is to always be falling back or advancing with key units, but you were already doing that anyways.

Strengths

  • Really strong Detachment ability that makes common units much more effective
  • Good Lord Exultant buffs

Weaknesses

  • Mediocre Stratagems

A Sample List

Here's a great example of a Frenzied Host list, combining the detachment with Carnival of Excess in a Recon build. It's a common top list for Emperor's Children right now, using Daemonettes to score recon and act as cheap, dangerous wide threats while Exultant-led Infractors can handle anything in the middle of the table. Conan Jennings took this list to a 5-0, 1st place finish at the Gem City GT in early August.

It's a good mix of battleline infantry units and big threats - the Defilers pair with the Keeper of Secrets to give the list three big threats to worry about, and Carnival of Excess makes Defilers insanely strong shooting threats with Critical Hits registering on a 5+.

Final Thoughts

If you're running Emperor's Children, Frenzied Host are going to be a common competitive pick. The Detachment ability is strong, easy to remember and use, and goes on units you were already taking anyways. In many ways it's the ideal for a 1 DP detachment, and makes for a common pairing with Carnival of Excesss.
