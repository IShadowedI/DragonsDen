TheChirurgeon's Road Through 2026, Part 41: Testing Death Guard Point Drops

October 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I talked about painting models using the new Tone Pro Paints - they're pretty good - but if you want a deeper dive or more involved review of them, you can find our full first impressions review here.

The big news this week is the release of the September 2026 Balance update, which gives us a ton of Detachment and Point changes but also updates Chaos Marines and Grey Knights to match the statline changes for space marines. We wrote a full review of it this week, and in it I talked about how much I liked the changes to Death Guard. Today I'm going to explore that, and the impact on the game I played.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Friendship Ended with Thousand Sons

This week's updates were a mixed bag for the chaos factions but were particularly rough on the Thousand Sons - Scarabs and Rubrics took serious point hikes, along with the characters you'd attach to those units. This is a nasty set of point hikes to my army specifically, which also was, notably, not very good - I wasn't impressed with how Rubricae Phalanx played (though I definitely wasn't playing the best version of it), and it took a brutal hit. There are probably Thousand Sons lists that can get there waiting to be built, but they likely rely much more on Tzaangor Enlightened and Tzaangors - units I just don't have painted at the moment.

Pair that with the oncoming rush of sudden feel no pains against Psychic attacks and it felt like a good time to rotate back off Thousand Sons. I've had a good run with them at Tacoma, the Lone Star Open, and Challengers Cup, but I think it's time to look at other armies in my stable.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Death Guard Are My New Best Friend

On the other hand, the new point drops on Death Guard were intriguing! Death Guard are an army that have struggled in 11th edition so far: They lost -1 to hit as a contagion ability and only recently regained a worse version of it, they've had restrictive detachment rules, and they've just been a little too expensive, with their durability not amounting to much. That said, the only real buffs they got in the new update were to bolter and melee weapon strength, and as a result, most of their point costs dropped. I started tooling around with lists, looking to understand how to take advantage of cheaper terminators and terminator characters as well as PBCs - the Plagueburst Crawler is still a very underrated unit in the Death Guard arsenal, in my estimation.

The general idea here is that all the Drones and Haulers have [ASSAULT] from their Detachment rule. The Defiler can strip cover and is a priority target, the PBCs grind forward and shoot, the Deathshroud teleport in and use the Hammer Detachment rule to open up gaps that the Poxwalkers can't, and the army can just be faster and deadlier than it looks. There are a lot of vehicles here, and it's a bit of a stat check in that regard. That said, these vehicles have a lot of hitting power as well, and can do surprising damage in melee in a pinch.

I'm Going First

That's good here - Purge generally wants that, so the reserves drop in sooner. It's better into Priority Assets than Recon, but T chose to test Recon in this mission. The good news is I'm familiar with the mission after Challengers Cup - you need to consecrate three objectives early and hold off your opponent from Triangulating before round 4.

I push up early with the goal of saturating the board with threats and locking down T's action units - he doesn't have a lot of sisters, just a lot of tanks, and if I kill the sisters that will leave him unable to Triangulate effectively with chaff units. I lose the Defiler on turn 1 but that ends up being OK - it takes two units and Vahl to bring it down (I did not pass many saves with it), and that pulls fire off the PBCs and Blight-Haulers. I use my Poxwalkers to harass T's units and get a turn 1 charge off on one of his Immolators to mess up T's movement plans.

Despite my excellent turn 2, Sisters remain an army which can do insane amounts of damage off a very small number of units. T picks up about half my army with the remianing third of his, and suddenly the game looks much more like a stalemate than it did a few moments prior.

The play of the game happens when I get the 7" charge off my Deathshrouds into Vahl's unit, picking them up with melee damage. I'd been slowly closing in around the rest of the table with the rest of my army, pushing T back into the lower right corner. Vahl's unit going down was a backbreaker, and while T still had the Castigator and an Exorcist to do damage, the net result is that it's too late. Early splash damage form the mortars helped turn decent damage from the MBHs and Bloat-Drones into Lethal Damage when those hit.

What won me this game is pure, stupid volume. That second and third wave of Terminators made a huge difference, dropping in when I needed them and taking out ke targets while forcing T to hang back and screen out his deployment zone to prevent a 6" deep strike. Meanwhile having [ASSAULT] on all the vehicles was huge - I had a ton of movement on early turns to run around the table, and being able to move through walls on top of that enabled some crazy options.

Result: Win, 98-45

Tweaking the List

After the game I took a look at the list and worked through what I wanted to change. I had room to work with for enhancements, but the only ones I'd consider are Shriekworm Familiar and Tendrilous Emissions - and I'm already attaching the Lord of Virulence.

What it boiled down to was the question: Is the Defiler better than two units that aggregate to more/similar output? Dropping the Defiler plus the 40 points I had left over was enough to buy a third Plagueburst Crawler (one with double plague spitters), and a third unit of 3 Deathshroud, or upgrade to 6. I really like adding the PBC and a unit of 3 Deathshroud at the moment - the sheer volume of units that gives me with 2+ saves and high toughness is legit, creating a nasty stat check on the opponent while still being very mobile.

So that's my next iteration - the same two characters, each attached to a unit of Deathshroud, but I'm running 3 each of Deathshroud x3 / PBCs / MBHs / HBLs and two units of Poxwalkers. It's more or less the list I ran last year when I won my first GT with Death Guard, give or take a few tweaks, and I really like the sheer volume of units it can bring with the increased mobility. Except it has one more entire unit of Deathshroud, which is a hell of an add. I think that's just a shitload of value to be able to bring to the table, having 14 unit drops, all of which have to be dealt with - and the ones that don't move 10" either have Infiltrators or Deep Strike. It's good stuff.

I'm going to test again next week but if I'm not seeing the results I want I'll likely look instead at swapping out the third PBC for something more efficient - the next choice is a third unit of Poxwalkers and a Predator, where I'd get similar firepower output at a lower cost while adding a third objective holder unit. That said, I really like the idea of a third PBC with spitters to rumble up the middle of the table and pile on a third activation of indirect mortar splash.

Final Thoughts

That's it for this week. This weekend I'm heading off to the 2026 Las Vegas Open but I'm not actually planning to play Warhammer this weekend; I'm there for a variety of business development tasks, saying hi to people and schmoozing on behalf of Tabletop Battles. And also kind of just on a weekend vacation with the missus, so while I considered playing in the GT, I decided against it - at best I'd play Friday and drop after three rounds, which seemed like a waste. Next year, maybe.

See you Thursday.
