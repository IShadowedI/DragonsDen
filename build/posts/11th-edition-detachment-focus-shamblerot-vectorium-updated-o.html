11th Edition Detachment Focus: Shamblerot Vectorium (Updated October 21, 2026)

October 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

In our Detachment Focus series we take a deep dive into an army’s Detachments, covering what’s in them, how they play, and how they’ll fit into the broader meta and your games. In this Detachment Focus we’re looking at the Shamblerot Vectorium from the Death Guard.

Night of the Living Dead, 28 Days Later, High School Musical 3: Senior Year. All of these films feature glassy eyed, brainless zombies shambling towards a unified goal. If you’ve ever seen any of these and thought the concept was pretty cool, Shamblerot Vectorum may be for you. It's currently the strongest way to run Death Guard, for some very funny (and frustrating) reasons.

Changelog

  • 10-21-2026: Updated for 11th edition
  • 4-26-2026 (Initial Publication)

Detachment Cost: 2 DP

Disposition: Disruption

The Detachment goes all in on Poxwalkers. On their own, Poxwalkers are a pretty solid unit. They’re cheap, can start at midboard, and often take more investment to remove than their cost would imply. This Detachment gives you a suite of tools to overwhelm your opponent with bodies and eventually win through attrition. The detachment rule is incredibly unique, giving you free additional units to work with, while the stratagems on offer are versatile and powerful - Shambling Wall is one of the game's most frustrating effects, and can create some devastating results on the table when opponents aren't prepared for it.

Detachment Rule: Numberless Horde

In your Command phase, in each of the specified battle rounds, you add a 10-model unit of Poxwalkers to your army, placed into Strategic Reserves. The rounds you do this on depends on the game size - 2 and 3 for Incursion, 2, 3, and 4 for Strike Force, and 2 through 5 for Onslaught.

Additionally, Poxwalkers units in your army gain the BATTLELINE keyword.

This is unique as a Detachment rule, and it lets you run nine units of Poxwalkers. That's insane, and while you may not actually run that many, chances are good you're going to run at least 30 to 40 Poxwalkers in your base army, then add three more units of ten during a game.

Poxwalkers already boast a very solid statline, with T4, a 5+ feel no pain, OC1 and LETHAL HITS on their weapons. They're effective speed bumps - particularly against infantry units, where they're liable to get back some dead models when they swing back - and act as potent plague spreaders for your army. The true value of Poxwalkers is being vectors for your contagion auras, delivering that incredibly important -1 to saves for Rattlejoint Ague. They aren't powerhouses even with 20 model units, but what they do bring to the table is the ability to take up space, project auras, protect your units, and cling to life with 1-2 models remaining in ways which are guaranteed to frustrate your opponent.

One key change with the 10th edition Codex was allowing the Noxious Blightbringer and the Malignant Plaguecaster to attach to units of Poxwalkers. The Blightbringer does a lot to help them zoom up the table and the Plaguecaster helps push them toward being a more consistent threat (although still not a particularly potent one). Typhus can also join a unit of Poxwalkers, and his Eater Plague ability can return models to the unit, making him a very strong include in this Detachment. However, if you attach any of these models to the unit, the unit will lose its Infiltrate ability, which means you’re either coming from reserves or footslogging through the table or riding in a transport. That's not nearly as big a deal in 11th edition, where Infiltrating is worse than it used to be.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Enhancements

All of these enhancements only work while the models bearing them are leading Poxwalker units, so they’ll be going on Plaguecasters and Blightbringers. It's worth noting you can combine some of these effects by attaching two characters to the same unit.

  • Witherbone Pipes (25 points) – This one can only go on a Noxious Blightbringer. Poxwalkers in his unit get +1 OC and +1 to leadership/battle shock tests. 2 OC Poxwalkers can be incredibly annoying and make for a great brick to sit on an objective, and the +1 Leadership is helpful for avoiding Ld 8+ failures. That said, you're going to use your 20man Poxwalker unit as ablative wounds for a Defiler, so this isn't really what you want.
  • Lord of the Walking Pox (15 points) – Lets the bearer's unit come in from strategic reserves at any time as if it's the third battle round. This lets you apply some serious pressure early and forces your opponent to screen their deployment zone well or sets you up for a gnarly turn one charge. That said, you aren't trying to bring in units of Poxwalkers from Strategic Reserves eary game anyqs.
  • Sorrowsyphon (10 points) – This one gives a Plaguecaster +1 damage to his Plague Wind weapon at the cost of d3 Poxwalkers every time he shoots. This isn't optional, but it's pretty good, as doing D3+1 damage is really good. That said, this won't apply to the weapon being shot from a Transport, so just keep that in mind.
  • Talisman of Burgeoning (25 points) – Gives the Poxwalkers in the bearer's unit +1 toughness. Really simple, but T5 Poxwalkers are super annoying to remove. More important now that bolters and marine base strength 5, and something you want on a unit of 20 models if you're taking it, just to maximize value. But again, the problem is that your 20man brick isn't meant to be tough, it's meant to be a bunch of ablative wounds for other units. 
Poxwalkers by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Stratagems

As you might expect, your Stratagems primarily benefit Poxwalkers. They're all priced to move at 1 CP.

  • Grip of the Walking Pox (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in the Fight phase, when an enemy unit targets one of your Poxalkers units. After that unit fights, roll a D6 for every poxwalker that died from an enemy attack; on a 6 you do a mortal to the enemy unit and if those mortals kill anything you can return models to the unit. This is a neat threat and could theoretically make attaching your unit a chore, but the reality is that you aren't likely to get a ton of models back this way - if you lose 18 models, on average you'll do three mortal wounds, and that may mean getting one model back. It certainly be swingy, but the juice isn't worth the squeeze here most of the time. 
  • Smeared With Filth (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in the Fight phase. Pick a Poxwalkers unit that died this phase, and an enemy unit that made attacks which targeted it this phase. That enemy unit is afflicted until the end of the game. This is pretty handy for punishing opponents who charge into your Infiltrator screens - your Poxwalkers' primary objective being that close is mostly just to put the opponent in contagion range and block their movement early, so if you can force them to charge you and then still keep them in contagion after, that's pretty much mission accomplished.
  • Gnawing Hunger (Battle Tactic, 1 CP) – Used in your Command phase. You select a unit of Poxwalkers and they get +1 move, strength, and attacks until the end of the turn. This makes them movement 7” with a blightbringer and can really help them close distances and clog up the table. The extra Strength and Attack put them at S4 with 3 attacks each which isn't amazing, but with Lethal Hits and the -1 to saves from Rattlejoint Ague means they can punch up more effectively than people realize. Good in a pinch, but the movement bonus here is the real winner.
  • Hidden Amongst the Dead (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – This can only be used on a Poxwalker unit without an attached character. Used in the movement phase to give a unit of Poxwalkers held in reserve the DEEP STRIKE ability. Unfortunately this can’t be comboed with Lord of the Walking Pox but this is still really solid for getting your spare Poxwalkers where they need to be for actions or to capture objectives which are otherwise 
  • Shock and Horror (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – This one works on any Death Guard unit. Used in the charge phase just after you finish a Charge move on a Death Guard unit. Every unit within Engagement range of your unit must take a Battleshock test at -1. This can be really nasty in a pinch, especially if you can combine it with the extra -1 from a sigil on a unit of Plague Marines or Deathshroud Terminators. It's not necessarily reliable, but if you can get off charges against multiple units it can turn of Stratagems and stop your opponent from scoring key objectives. It's worth it to throw this out when you can directly cost your opponent VP with it.
  • Shambling Wall (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Pick a Death Guard unit that was just targeted by a shooting attack and one friendly Poxwalkers unit within 3" that is visible to the attacking unit. Until the end of the phase, every time your unit is targeted by an attack, if the poxwalkers unit you picked is visible to the attacking model and an eligible target to attack, then instead of allocating that attack to your unit, you don't make a saving throw and instead you kill a number of Poxwalkers in your unit equal to the damage characteristic of that attack. This is amazing for something like Mortarion or a Defiler to deter enemy attacks and keeping him in the game, and great if you have a lot of damage 1 shots you want to shrug off. Note that your poxwalkers have to be visible for this, so make sure they aren't HIDDEN to the attacking unit.

This last one needs an extra addendum because it has some proper bullshit associated with it. As of this writing, according to how the rules for wound allocation work in the attack sequence in 11th edition, this is busted. That's because save rolls happen before damage allocation now, so when you shoot a unit like a Defiler or Mortarion with this, the sequence will be:

  1. Opponent rolls hit rolls
  2. Opponent rolls wound rolls
  3. You roll saves
  4. You allocate damage to models based on the allocation group, starting with the lowest rolls and going to the highest

WHAT THIS MEANS: If you take say, 18 wounds on your Defiler from AP-1, 1-damage shots while it's standing within 3" of a visible unit of ten Poxwalkers. You'd then roll 18 saves. Then you immediately pick up ten Poxwalkers, "using up" the ten worst saves in the batch. If all of the remaining 8 rolls were a 4+, then you'd count 8 successful saves and walk away with no damage taken.

This is, frankly, bonkers. It's technically correct, but definitely taking advantage of a loophole in the 11th edition rules where the new way saves and damage are allocated don't mix with the way the Stratagem was written.

NOTE: YOU DON'T HAVE TO LIVE THIS WAY. You are, of course, welcome to play it more sensibly, or if you're running an event, rule it in such a way that the original intent - no saves are rolled - is preserved. But if you're playing against this and it comes up, note that the "correct" interpretation is the far stronger one.

Poxwalkers. Credit: Rockfish

Playing This Detachment

One key value here is that this is a 2 DP Detachment, freeing you up to pair it with Paragons of Putrescence or Flyblown Host. Which is good, because you'll need to get a better Disposition. The usual pick here is Paragons, because that will get you Priority Assets, a disposition that works very well conceptually with having tons of cheap Poxwalker bodies to play with. One thing Paragons gets you is the Rejuvenating Swarm Enhancement, which can go on a Plaguecaster or Blightbringer and be used to give a 20-model blob of Poxwalkers -1 to be wounded by attacks with a Strength higher than their Toughness. That's a much stronger buff than +1 Toughness, and you can combine the Blightbringer with Typhus on a big unit of Poxwalkers to get the most value out of their buffs and Typhus' ability to bring back dead models with his psychic attack. That unit also gets +3" to its contagion range from having a character.

Outside of the strategy of “bring a lot of Poxwalkers” you’re looking to control the table and continually apply pressure to your opponent throughout the game and win by attrition. Even with buffs, Poxwalkers aren’t exactly offensive powerhouses, so don’t expect to remove units with even a 20 block of them. Instead your primary focus here is using Poxwalkers to perform actions for your primary, block enemy movement, and spread contagion for your big threats. You want to put a 10-model block near your big threats (Mortarion, Defiler) to soak up damage and keep them around.

In that regard, Shamblerot armies love Defilers and Mortarion, as being able to eat a bunch of wounds intended for your big targets makes removing them all that much harder. Opponents already have to gamble on whether it's worth it to throw mass resources at these units; knowing they'll be wasting their first ten damage even trying - only to watch that unit get replaced, free of charge - can be downright demoralizing.

If you're taking Paragons, the character-focused Stratagems give you a lot of value out of taking attached units. Territorial Infection is insanely good on a unit of 20 Poxwalkers, giving them +1 OC for your turn at the start of the Command phase, while the extra 3" of Contagion range makes for Poxwalker units which can cause more problems without getting too close. And Aggravus Spasms can be used to "out" enemy units near your character-led poxwalkers, upping their Detection range by 6" for units further away to shoot.

Strengths

  • The Most Unique Detachment Rule in the Game. Nothing else in the game works like this detachment does. Getting a total of 9 of the same unit is something no other army in the game can do and it's a very strong ability even if it's just Poxwalkers.
  • Incredibly Frustrating Board Control. With Poxwalkers gaining Infiltrate, you can feasibly have 60 bodies in midboard at the start of the game while only dedicating about 400 points to the effort. If you’re going first, your opponent is gonna end up in Pox jail pretty quick.
  • Durability. Protecting your biggest threats with Poxwalkers can make them maddeningly difficult to kill, especially when your opponent has to wound against T11 and T12 to even push through.

Weaknesses

  • The Hobby Commitment. While not a mechanical weakness, you are gonna have to paint 100 Poxwalkers (and that's on the low end) to make this Detachment really sing. The models aren’t as simple to paint up as Termagants so this is gonna take a significant amount of time to get battle ready.
  • Blast and Cleave. Blast and Cleave weapons are gonna rip right through your hordes and they’ll generally be on platforms your hordes aren’t equipped to deal with.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

A Sample List

There's no getting around the Poxwalker commitment you're going to need to play this Detachment. That said, the big threats are also key here. This teams-focused list from Innes Wilson gives a good view of how this kind of list plays in a competitive environment. Innes took this list to a 5-0 finish at the Scottish Open Team Championship and it's very similar to other successful lists we've seen. The big difference is using a battle cannon Knight instead of a second Defiler.

Your three big threats here are Mortarion, the Defiler, and the double Battle Cannon Despoiler. You can protect the prior two with Poxwalkers, while the Knight is just here to take advantage of Rattlejoint Ague and the extra AP push. The double-attached 20-model unit of Poxwalkers here features Typhus and a Noxious Blightbrlinger with Rejuvenating Swarm, which gives the list an annoyingly difficult to kill swarm of T4 goobers to sit at midtable. The other unit features a plaguecaster and that unit and the solo Plaguecaster go in the Rhino to create the "party bus" with two plaguecasters shooting out of the top. The third Poxwalkers unit is there to protect Mortarion or the Defiler, and so will new units added to the army.

Final Thoughts

And that's a wrap for our beloved brain-dead bozos. This Detachment is really neat, giving you extra units throughout the game gives you a great lever for applying continuous pressure. Beyond that, it's also very strong. There’s a lot of great support here and a lot to like. Poxwalkers are already one of the best units in the book and this gives you another way to play them with a very unique playstyle.
