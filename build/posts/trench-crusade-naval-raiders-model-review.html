Trench Crusade Naval Raiders Model Review

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With the release of the Carcass Front box comes two new plastic kits for Naval Raiders and Sacred Affliction. Considering these are the second and third plastic releases for TC, this will set the tempo for what we can expect going forward. We'll obviously be starting with the cooler of the two factions (unless you read our Sacred Affliction article first), the Naval Raiders.

Thank you to Factory Fortress Inc for providing us with advance copies for review.

The Build

Most of the models in this kit go together relatively well. If you’re not familiar with Archon’s sprues, they number pieces like you’d expect but also have little arrows pointing to which piece the number refers to, and that's always welcome. All the models stay on their own sprue sheet, but you’ll have to jump around the sheet a bit to find the pieces you’re looking for sometimes.

Each model has an A or B build which can vary from being a completely different looking model, a gun change, or just moving their foot on or off a pipe. Most are the former in that list which is good, but in the instructions we got, it was unclear what pieces were tied to what. For example, Raider 3 can either be built reloading his gun or as a shrouded dual weapon melee guy. The melee weapons on the melee guy can’t be put on the normal body without some careful cutting and greenstuff because the shroud gets in the way. On the flip side, the captain's arms are interchangeable as far as I saw, meaning you can do Shield + Wrecker’s Torch. This can all be figured out with a bit of dry fitting but it’d be nice if in the final instructions it was clearer where the point of no return was.

Now in the initial paragraph you may have noticed I said “most” of the models go together well. The biggest nightmare here is the Chorister. The chain bits all look very similar as bare plastic and it can be really hard to tell what's supposed to connect to what where. I eventually just sorta pressed them all together and they fell into place, but it was a headache for a bit there. In addition, you can’t give him the shark and a shield at the same time, which feels like an attack on me personally considering I’ve never run a chorister without a shield in my life.

There are a few minor gaps on some of these models, with the worst being on the Anointed Heretic Raider, but it’s nothing a bit of pressure, glue and patience can’t solve. All that said, on the whole these models go together without much hassle, I built the whole set in an hour or two after getting the box. You also get a wealth of bits which’ll be going directly into my bits box for future conversions.

MawQueenMinis:  I only had time to build one of the Heavy Anointed, but this model was A LOT OF PIECES. One dude should not be this many pieces, but hey, he looks amazing when he’s together! He was simple compared to the horrors I’ve seen with the Chorister, and I’m not looking forward to building him next. 

Painting

Naval Raider Warband Credit: Mildnorman

My favorite thing about Trench Crusade models is they have just the right amount of bullshit on them. You can sink a ton of time into individual sculpts or get them looking pretty nice and ready in just a few painting sessions (I painted these quickly for the article and got them all done in 2-3 nights of painting). These models are no exception, although some are more annoying than others (it's the Chorister again). 

Not Choristers

For all my normal bozos I followed the same recipe for their armor, going from Games Workshop’s Thunderhawk Blue, washing it with Vallejo Black Wash, and then mixing the original blue with a white to get all my highlights in there. The most annoying part of the model to get done well were by far the lenses. They’re deeply recessed and you want a bright color in there if you want them to be the focal point. I ended up just stabbing Vallejo Troll Orange in there and then white with a smaller brush to try and get in there for some lighting highlights. From there it was just cleaning up with the same armor blue I used everywhere else.

For the cloth, I used Vallejo Bloody Red, washed it down with the same black wash as the armor, and then highlighted with the same color, mixed with Vallejo Grey-White as needed. The cloth provides a nice opportunity to add some uniform color pop to your warband while not being present on every model. This is especially true if you’re doing a raw metal color for the armor like the box art. Same goes with the undersuits, I went with Games Workshop’s Caliban Green highlighted with Vallejo Goblin Green but it's a good place to put some nice contrasting colors.

The Captain was not painted red like the box art because frankly it’d bother me. Instead I put down some Games Workshop Dryad Bark near all the gaps in the armor and went over that with Vallejo Thick Blood to give the suggestion that blood was oozing out of him. Honestly its a hard effect to sell and the model doesn’t really have it modeled on him so you can forgo that little lore blurb entirely if you want.

All in all, these models all have nice dynamic poses that are pretty easy to get a brush anywhere you’d need to with the exception of the captain’s cloak but if you prime black you won't notice the spots you can’t reach. These were fun to paint up and quick which is the most important thing.

Heavy Anointed Naval Raider - @MawQueenMinis

MawQueenMinis: Painting the Anointed may be one of the most satisfying and simple models I’ve painted in a long time. There are just the right amount of unique textures to play with depth, while the flat surfaces are fun to layer up. I went with a layering sponge method for this guy, starting with a black airbrush basecoat. Using Warpaint Fanatic Angel Green, Greenskin, and Ferocious Green I gave him a sponge bath building up each color less and less, taking into account where my light source was. I then edge highlighted a lot of the panels using Ferocious Green. The metals are a simple Vallejo True Metallic Metal Green Gold Base, highlighted with the Light variant, and pinwashed with a watered-down Army Painter Dark Wood. Finally, the tubes are a combination of Pro-Acryl Bright Yellow Green, highlighted with Vallejo Game Color Bile Green. 

I have so much fun basing Trench Crusade models, and this guy was no exception. Wet blending various oranges and throwing a layer of UV resin on top was a really solid way to integrate the model’s base! 

Chorister

Drowned Chorister Credit: MildNorman

This guy… less so. Okay, he’s not that bad, but he is way more annoying than the other models in this set. The chains overlapping the skin in the way they do means you’ll be doing a lot of back and forth between the two materials as you fix mistakes and make highlights. The best method I found was to paint the skin first, then the chains, then the fish, then go back and do it all over again to fix your mistakes. The helmet is mercifully one solid texture outside the porthole so that part is easy enough. The Chorister is also a bit of an odd duck; he doesn’t have the armor or cloth the rest of the warband does so visually there’s very little connecting him to the rest of the force. I elected to paint the big weights on the model’s chest the same color as the armor to add at least a little visual similarity to the other models.

STLs

Leviathal Chorus Credit: Mildnorman

But that's not all! FFI sent us the STLs for the Leviathal Chorus, the Leader for the Chorister warband variant. The model is separated in three pieces and the supports come off relatively easily. If you’re not stupid like me, they’re in pieces that are pretty natural sub assemblies for painting. If you are stupid like me and just put the model together because you got excited, you have a bit of a challenge ahead of you. The base flows into the model and the model’s middle section is a texture nightmare with the choral, intestines, and fish all overlapping each other. I did the same thing I did with the normal chorister, starting with the skin, then intestines, then choral and then went back and did it all over again.

Once it’s all said and done though this is a fantastic sculpt. It's super striking and this guy is Large. In terms of playability, he might be too large. It's gonna be really hard to hide this guy behind most pieces of terrain on your board. That said, it's certainly an imposing silhouette on the battlefield.
