Infinity N5 Tournament Report: Playing Corregidor at the UK Games Expo (Part Two)

June 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Continuing the story of trying out updated Corregidor rules at a 5-round tournament (Part One is here), we pick up having won 2, lost 1 game on the first day.

Round 4 - Corporate Appropriation

At dawn on the second day, I looked to the east (about 30 minutes to the east), and, free of any need to transport or set up my table, had a leisurely breakfast with the family before strolling into the event hall shortly before 10 AM. Blissful! 

Pre-Game and Deployment

My opponent was playing USARF, which despite some nice profiles after the October 2025 update, are still a very limited Sectorial. Looking at the table, I saw some opportunities for the Intruder sniper, and felt OK about using my Deep Space list. My opponent won the roll and chose to go first. This gave me the right to decide which Prototype was which. My usual play when going first in Corporate Appropriation is to ensure I carry the enemy Prototype right back into my deployment zone, where I can defend it, and conversely, that’s the thing I most want to prevent when playing second. So I was able to designate the one on my left, with lots of wide open space around it as my opponent’s Prototype to carry off. 

My opponent set up a fair few Mimetism-3 Camouflage markers, and a couple of Grunts with flamethrowers, across the midfield. The main threat in his DZ was a Minuteman with APHMG. This is now a BS Attack(+1SD) unit, so in a Fireteam, it offers a brutal 6 dice in the Active turn. He also deployed a Desperado I thought would be his first pick to shoot forward to the objectives. 

My opponent's Desperado, poised to leap forward and supported by an AP HMG on a rooftop.

I deployed with Morans covering both Prototypes, and everything else more or less tucked into cover. I made very sure to cover all the board edges with a combination of flash pulses and lethal AROs - Van Zant is a real threat. Critically, I deployed my Gecko/EVAder team on my centre-left, with the Gecko peeking out along what I thought was just an oblique lane to my Prototype on the right. I also had a Jaguar panzerfaust watching a long line to the enemy panoply.

The view from my DZ. Note the position of the Gecko, which I thought wasn't visible straight across the table!

My opponent deployed the Unknown Ranger, with a Minuteman FO buddy, as his reserve, placed on his left to push forward. I deployed my Intruder sniper right near the back centre of my DZ, watching diagonally across the Prototype on my left.

Round 1

My opponent immediately kicked off with his Desperado, zooming past his Panoply and avoiding my Jaguar’s panzerfaust. He then picked up on a sloppy mistake I’d made. His Minuteman APHMG could see my Gecko over/above the large building in the centre, and promptly unleashed his full Burst. I might have been alright anyway, as the range was well over 32”, but my opponent crit and hit, with the Gecko taking 2 wounds. Dangerous stuff, but fortunately I was able to Guts Roll back into Total Cover. My opponent was able to draw D-Charges from the Panoply with a Foxtrot, which then re-camoed and walked into the centre, into sight of my Jaguar panzerfaust. As the range was long and he was clearly going somewhere, I declared Discover, and my opponent elected to shoot on 5s. In a slight stroke of luck, he killed the Jaguar stone dead, and was able to climb up onto the central building. He spent 2 Orders firing down at my right hand Moran on a lower roof, but the range was over 16”, so with Mimetism-3, he was aiming to hit on 2s, and missed entirely. In fact, reasoning that if he did hit the Moran, he’d as like as not crit, on the second Order I simply laid a koala, threatening the Unknown Ranger, who’d come forward earlier to shoot his deployment-placed koala.

My Jaguar, pictured right before messing up his one job.

In his other combat group, my opponent made some play with a Beasthunter, closing in on my other Moran. I declined chances to ARO with my Intruder, mostly because I didn’t want any chance of being attacked head-on by that Minuteman APHMG. Eventually, the Beashunter got prone on the rooftop with the Moran and scragged him, while the Moran laid out another koala. The Beasthunter tried a long range Panzerfaust shot at McMurrough - probably a mistake given his Immunity(ARM) - and died to the koala.

Overall I got away from that turn with light casualties, and saw some opportunities to hit back. I managed to move my Intruder and grab a bead on the Minuteman FO accompanying the Unknown Ranger - it crit Dodged into safety. The Intruder moved again and could draw a bead on the Grunt paramedic accompanying the Minuteman APHMG, where it was hiding Prone on a roof - it passed its saves and Guts Rolled into Total Cover. I also had a shot at the Desperado - it passed two saves and Guts Rolled into Total Cover. Hmmm. I had to spend several Orders moving, my NCO Order Discoballing, and one Order using White Noise, but I worked the Gecko and EVAder into the centre, while risking an engineer roll to bring the Gecko back up to 2STR. This let me shoot at the Unknown Ranger through stacked Albedo/White Noise, forcing him to Dodge. He crit Dodged. This wasn’t going very well!

Right, let's see if you can dodge this!

At this point I was running down a lot of my Orders and had to commit to the assault. I moved my team again, this time with the Gecko leading, and very satisfactorily blasted the Unknown Ranged to smithereens with that vulcan shotgun. Balm to my soul. I then sacrificed the Gecko in by moving right across the open in the face of the Minuteman APHMG, also provoking from the Minuteman FO and Desperado. Facing lots of +SD shots from Minutemen with an AP heavy pistol and a T2 rifle, I simply laid my PS5 chain rifles twice. The Gecko went down Unconscious and the APHMG died. The EVAder was then able to kill the Desperado, at the cost of a wound, and the Gecko dying, from the biker’s chain rifle ARO. These losses were acceptable. It let me grab the Prototype and tuck into a building, which also housed McMurrough, who I trusted to keep it safe at close quarters.

Round  2

Having taken a fair number of casualties, but full of fight, my opponent made some bold plays to re-establish control. He risked his Foxtrot on the central building against my Intruder, which within 16”, with Surprise Shot, wasn’t the worst idea (3x 8s against 2x 7s). However, the Intruder had dialled in his aim and blew the Foxtrot’s head off. Thank the lord for +1SD. A Grunt with flamethrower moved down from a building (either the angle was wrong or I simply forgot my Intruder’s ARO) and went in to speak with my EVAder and McMurrough. I Dodged with the EVAder, who failed and burned to death, and templated back with McMurrough, but both the Grunt and the dog warrior passed their saving rolls.  

EVAder gone, McMurrough survives . . . but who's that behind him!?

The next move was to bring on Van Zant, but rather than try and find a hole in my backline (I had moved several pieces out, and I think it would have been possible) my opponent went for the objective with him. This was probably sensible - Van Zant has Martial Arts Level 3 and a T2 CCW - but he’d have to get through McMurrough first. Because he was hiding in a building, the only way to shotgun him was from very short range, and McMurrough, luckily making a Dodge in FtF, got into melee combat with Van Zant. Now the American is very slightly better, rolling with +1SD on 22s to McMurrough’s 1 dice on 23. But it’s risky, given the dog’s impressive toughness and deadly PS3 AP+DA CCW. In the event, the combat went for several rounds with McMurrough tanking a couple of saves - he might have taken a single wound - before he eventually connected, and Van Zant exploded. 

There was a bit more running about behind cover, but my opponent couldn’t get at either Prototype past the Intruder or McMurrough. In my Turn 2, McMurrough splattered the Grunt, and was able to make off with the enemy Prototype, ending up tucked away in my DZ. I sent the Stempler Zond, with D-Charges from the Panoply, towards my Prototype to destroy it. There was a troublesome mine covering the Prototype, so I thought I’d be clever and Sensor + Deactivate it. I failed two successive Sensor rolls on a 20 (it needs a 19). Womp womp. This left me a bit short of Orders, so I Sensored it on the third attempt and used the NCO Order on my Lobo to shoot the mine away. This left me one Order with which to blow up the Prototype - it passed one save, so was left just clinging on at 1STR.

My Intruder sniper was left dominant after surviving the Foxtrot's attempt to take him out from a neighbouring rooftop.

Round 3

My opponent was surprisingly far from Retreat, but was in Loss of Lieutenant - the Foxtrot he risked against my Intruder in his previous Turn had been the main man. This left him without great options, but he did manage to use a Foxtrot minelayer to shotgun my Stempler Zond and a 112 Doctor to carry that Prototype back slightly.

All I needed to do in my turn was send the Lobo & Diablo after that Prototype. They ran up the table, Speedballing the Stempler Zond on the way, forcing the Foxtrot back into cover and killing the 112 Doctor, but the Diablo’s Trench Hammers bounced off the Prototype’s armour, leaving them incapable of fulfilling the mission! This was why I’d Speedballed the Stempler, a decision which now appeared fully justified. The remote was able to nip forward, D-Charge the Prototype again, destroying it, and use its last D-Charge to perform the Sabotage Classified Objective on a nearby building. Finally it moved laterally across the table, surviving a gunfight with the Foxtrot minelayer to Secure the HVT. What a little mechanical hero. 

At the end of the day, this Remote was the vital piece. I forgave it for failing to Sensor (twice).

Results & Thoughts

The score finished 9-1 to me, since I’d made all my classifieds (I was lucky to draw Telemetry, which Jazz had got for me early on), and my opponent’s only score was for drawing more items from the Panoplies. While that was a decisive victory, and I felt I had control for most of the game, I have to acknowledge a couple of points where I took big risks. If the Gecko’s assault had failed due to dice, I could have really been on the back foot, and I think my opponent might have given me a lot of problems if he’d used Van Zant differently. On the other hand, I think I had much better access to and sight over the Prototypes throughout, and that’s ultimately what the game comes down to. There were also 3 or so casualties I thought the Intruder was likely to cause immediately in Turn 1, and the dice let me down each time, so I can’t say I simply coasted on luck. 

I enjoyed the Deep Space list, particularly the Intruder (available in either Division). The signature EVAder & Gecko team seems OK rather than super strong. A TAG with a shotgun is funny, but there are a lot of things that can threaten or stop it.

Round 5 - Annihilation

I was 3-1 going into the final game, and one of my wins was minor, so while I certainly wouldn’t win the event, I thought I might make 3rd place if I could secure this last game. Annihilation, I have to say, is my least favourite Mission - I hesitate to call it a Mission at all. There is no interplay of risking your models to achieve the Objectives, no varied or interesting goals to work towards. Maybe that’s not fair, though, because there are special tactical considerations in Annihilation. You need to kill enemy models without risking too many of your own points to either AROs or counter-attacks. I don’t particularly love that it encourages such a turtling, cautious style of play, but it does have its own logic. I chose to play my Surface Division list here, because of the defensive Fireteam, the raw power of the Iguana, the potential of the Bearcat for Lt assassination, and the usefulness of guided missile play for safe attrition. 

Pre-Game and Deployment

My opponent was another very nice visitor from Northern Ireland, playing vanilla Yu Jing, which is an army I rate very highly for this kind of mission - they can deploy a lot of points camouflaged or off the board, and have some very good cheap defensive elements. He was also playing Sun Tzu, and so won the Lt roll handily, sensibly choosing to go first. Given his ability to hold 2 models back, he didn’t place that many points on the board. Sun Tzu himself is a fair chunk, but it was mostly Monks, Flash pulse bots, a missile bot, and some camouflage markers, including the ubiquitous Gudans (but only one Long Ya). 

The view from my DZ. Some flash pulses have limited views, but the Coyote, in the perch on the left, is watching far out.

I deployed with my Fireteam set up in my far left corner, with flash pulses strung across the DZ, the Vertigo Zond prone in the centre watching my own table edge, and the Morans left and right in the midfield. Between these cheap defences, the Coyote E/Mitter, and Shifta’s EM mine and E/Marat, I thought I looked fairly secure. My opponent reserve dropped Bixie on my right - a very mobile piece, but one which would have to cross the table and then fight through a Repeater net, and a Yan Huo with HRMC on my left. This was more of a problem, it’s an apex firepower piece which is only temporarily stopped by EM ammunition, and can brute force almost anything. I placed my Iguana safely on my centre right, where it could hold back Bixie or any light threats, but wasn’t easily accessible to the Yan Huo. 

Round 1

My opponent didn’t mess about, but immediately moved that Yan Huo to blast 5 dice into my Coyote - my 3 dice in ARO were no match for it and the Coyote dropped Unconscious at the first exchange. This bullet-spewing terror then moved again to knock out a flash pulse bot, but to advance any further towards my DZ would have brought him into range of the Shifta’s E/Marat. Instead, my opponent put Orders into what turned out to be a Zhencha hacker, trying a boarding shotgun Surprise Attack against McMurrough. Unfortunately for him, this was under a Moran’s Repeater area, and between the chain rifle ARO and Jazz’s Trinity, the Yu Jing HI died, wounding McMurrough in exchange. The only other major plays this turn were some repositioning of Shaolin Monks, Bixie, and the Sacha, who tried a very difficult speculative fired EM grenade at a Moran, but missed. He placed the Yan Huo in Suppression Fire and passed the turn over.

Oh lord, he's a coming! The Yan Huo moves against my Coyote.

Starting my first turn, I rather felt that I’d weathered the storm and could hit back. McMurrough advanced via Impetuous Order, and with his Irregular, I tried to throw a smoke grenade - that would allow my Iguana to vault terrain and try a shot at Bixie on my right, without provoking AROs from the Long Ya or the missile bot. I failed, and tried twice more with Regular Orders, failing again. Frustrating. I moved slightly differently and tried for Bixie anyway, risking an >40” ARO from the missile launcher. This was a moment of supreme risk for Bixie - out of cover, she was Dodging on a 16 (thanks to BS Attack(-3)) against my 4x 17s, and needed 6s to save any hits. Unfortunately for me she rolled a great Dodge and only took one hit, getting wounded and Guts Rolling Prone into Total Cover. I took a wound on my Iguana in return. 

My frustrations were not over! I readied to Speedball my Coyote, moved the Bambadroid and landed an AP mine covering the Yan Huo. That let me move my Iguana again and engage the HI from well outside 24”, forcing it to shoot back at B1 and eat the mine. All of this was to no avail. The Yan Huo stood my Iguana off for 3 Orders, taking no damage whatsoever and wounding the Iguana again. I was running out of wiggle room, and felt I had to remove the threat, so I brought my Bearcat on behind it.

Ha, I've got you now!

This was clearly the best way to stack the odds, but I’d wanted to save it for a later attack run, perhaps an attempt at Sun Tzu, as tough as he is, later on. Well, the Bearcat also failed to take the Yan Huo out, over 3 Orders, including 2 where it was shooting it in the back. On the final Order of my turn, I did my first wound to the Yan Huo, getting my Bearcat Immobilised by a nearby Gudan’s Zapper in the process. The Gudan also passed its saving roll. Now Yan Huo are extremely tough, even out of cover, due to ARM5 and Immunity(ARM). But this was rather a fluke of dice. 

As it turned out, I did not have anything.

Round 2

My opponent again took the direct approach and started blasting at my Iguana with his Yan Huo. I assumed I was simply dead meat at this point, but in fact it took 4 Orders, partly because the Yan Huo was out of cover for the first ARO, and partly just due to good rolls on my part. Throughout, I was hoping the Bearcat would Reset - it needed a 10, and then its shotgun ARO would significantly complicate the Yan Huo’s life. But no, I failed all 4 attempts. At that point, the Iguana Operator was once again on his NWI in Eclipse Smoke - and bloody thankful for it - and the Yan Huo executed the Bearcat with its AP Heavy Pistol. I rolled the Normal Reset roll a 5th time, just to check, and yes, it failed again. The other play my opponent made was to send a Monk against McMurrough, which is a decent solution. McMurrough was wounded already and the Monk dropped him in one Order.

I was feeling rather aggrieved at my dice at this point, but hey, my Iguana Operator was alive, and while there’s an AP HMG, there’s hope, right? I gunned down the Monk to avenge McMurrough, and another Monk using my Bambadroid firing laterally across my DZ. Back to the Iguana Operator, and after a couple more Orders, I had finished off the Yan Huo. With my second-to-last Order in that group, I went forward to snap up one more kill, the enemy missile bot. Or I would have, if it had not crit and blown the Iguana Operator to pieces. Oh dear. That seemed to slip the attritional battle decisively away from me, and what was worse, the missile bot was still preventing me from using my Speedball on my Coyote. Due to the impact template, it could ARO and kill not just anyone who tried to help, but the patient as well. So I had to try my Bambadroid thunderbolt against the missilebot, which is a fair chance, but no, the missile bot won again and killed the Bambadroid. Well. All I could do was shoot the Unconscious Yan Huo off the table to prevent Speedball, lay a koala down and pass the turn back.

The Iguana Operator goes to his doom against a Yu Jing missile. (the Yan Huo, still visible, is Unconscious)

Round 3

Scenting victory, my opponent dropped a Liu Xing hacker and jumped past my right hand Moran. This provoked a koala and a Trinity ARO as well, but the Liu Xing survived on NWI, while dropping my Moran. At this point, I was checking my remaining VP against the Mission Objectives, and that last casualty put me at the point where reviving my Coyote would still leave me below the next scoring threshold. My opponent didn’t have any other options to dig more of my surviving troops from my DZ, but then he didn’t need to kill any more to be in the lead.

An extremely stubborn Liu Xing holding out.

My turn saw multiple Orders washed through Jazz to Trinity that wounded Liu Xing, all to no avail. This was not a very decisive FtF - both models had Firewall(-6), so the Yu Jing drop troop chose to Reset - but even so, I was again quite unlucky. My last move was for the Shifta to engage and gun down a Gudan, but that didn’t affect the outcome.

Results & Thoughts

The game ended 6-2 to my opponent - I was below the 150pt ‘cut-off’ with ~120VP and he was above with ~180. It is generally a sign of limited thinking to reflect on a game of Infinity and say simply “I would have won but for the dice”. In this case, given the mission parameters, I am fairly confident in saying I would have won the game if that missile had not crit my Iguana Operator in ARO. But, in line with what I've previously written about risk, there's an argument that I should simply not have risked the Operator at all once it was into an NWI state. At 68pts, it was clearly a big swing piece under Annihilation's objectives, and I should have looked for FtF rolls where the negative outcome wouldn't result in losing the wider game. Perhaps I should have used Jazz’s pitchers to try and immobilise the Yan Huo before attacking it directly. Certainly I should not have wasted two Orders trying McMurrough’s smoke, or even sent him forward at all, without a definite objective for him. Annihilation, as discussed, needs some extra thought to preserving one's models and staying hunkered down tight. But I really don’t think I would have played my Iguana that differently - that’s just the luck of the dice sometimes. No disrespect intended to my opponent, who clearly had a workable strategy for the mission - despite losing most of the pieces he actually sent forward, he still had a healthy VP total by the end of the game.

To cap it all, when writing this report, I realised I’d forgotten to reckon in BS Attack(-3) for the opposing missile bot’s ARO against my Iguana Operator, meaning that the critical roll should have been a miss. You’ve got to play it as it lies, but wow, that realisation hurt.

Tournament Results & Conclusion

For the actual results, I came 6th of 16 players (I think we had 18, but a couple had to drop out after the first day). A completely unexceptional finish, but I was really glad to stretch out to 5 games, and with new Corregidor, for the first time in a long time. All of my opponents were lovely people, and with some beautiful armies, whom I’d be happy to face again.

The event was won by the player I would have picked as favourite anyway - he is certainly one of the strongest players in the current UK tournament scene - using Haqqislam. Of course this means Saladin, and I understood him to say that his list had only one flyer, because he would feel bad about using both. But he did win another flyer model as a tournament prize, so perhaps we have that to look forward to next time! The winner shared some thoughts about the overall state of the game, and was unambiguous that he thinks flyers in general need a nerf, as do some other top-meta units. He faced, and beat, the JSA player who did for me in Round 3, in his words 'because I won the roll to go first'. Certainly the offensive potential of top-meta lists, particularly with Aerial Vehicles, is a hot button topic at the moment.

Bostria and others from the Corvus Belli team were in attendance at the UKGE and dropped by the tournament regularly. At the final ceremonies, we were all told that CB hope to see the event grow bigger next year, and to conduct a seminar/announce or preview new releases, in the same way they do at GenCon, AdeptiCon and Spiel Essen, adding a fourth major event to their release calendar. Something to hope for!

What do I think of new Corregidor? They certainly have some strong profiles. The Coyote FTO, Jazz and some of the Fireteam support staff are very efficient. Moran Masai of course remain faction-defining. The Bearcat, Intruder sniper, McMurrough, and the Iguana with BS Attack(-3) are genuinely fearsome. Does the whole thing hang together in a competitive list? Yes, but I would characterise it as a Sectorial that plays a strong but fair game of Infinity. I need some time to play with lists and see if there are any hidden gems or synergies in the less obvious profiles. Gecko & EVAder teams, and Deep Space Division in general, I fear, will remain an off-meta pick, capable of good games here and there, but not something which will attract good players seeking to win events. 

I will try to master the detail to write a Faction Focus article for Corregidor next. Until then, my only advice for Corregidor players is to do as I shall do, and throw all their new stuff at the wall to see what sticks! Good luck.
