Infinity N5 Tournament Report: Playing Corregidor at the UK Games Expo (Part One)

June 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The UK Games Expo, the country’s largest exhibition for boardgames, wargames, and all manner of nerdy stuff, took place as usual on 30-31 May, and since it is held 30 minutes’ drive from my house, and there was a 5-round, 2-day Infinity tournament, I was in attendance. Following the release of Operation Mazebreaker, which had begun pre-orders and was due to be sold in person at the Expo (it sold out by the end of Saturday), my Infinity enthusiasm was sky high, and I was determined to try out the new Corregidor rules. 

This tournament was qualified as a Satellite event, but ended up being quite small, with 18 players attending. I think that the Infinity scene in the UK, as in many places, skews a bit older than 40k, and in some ways a two-day event, while very welcome to keen players like myself, is harder to get to than a one-dayer. In fact it was the first two-day event I have attended since becoming a father, four years ago. The very popularity of the Games Expo also might have worked against tournament attendance. I know a couple of Infinity players, and ran into a few more at the Expo, who were coming specifically to wander around the halls and see everything on offer. They didn’t feel able to get that full experience while spending the bulk of their time playing five games of Infinity. Apart from conflicting commitments, the costs were slightly higher than the very low baseline for Infinity events - beyond the ticket price, I had to buy entry tickets to the Expo and pay for parking on both days. Some attendees could have taken the train, but then again most needed to pay for accommodation as well. 

For all those hurdles, we had a good crew on the day, with some very strong players in attendance, and some from overseas who took the opportunity to visit the UK. 

Mission Analysis & Lists

So I knew I would be bringing Corregidor to this event; old loyalties and new rules demanded it. I had a strong urge to bring one Surface Division and one Deep Space Division list. I don’t really approve of, or see the point of, the split system, but it is what we have and I want to work with it. The mission set was also quite forgiving in terms of list design. Rounds were Crossing Lines, Area of Interest, Cutthroat, Corporate Appropriation and Annihilation. This meant very few Objectives for Specialists to interact with. In Corregidor, which naturally abounds in Specialists, the only real constraints were:

  • Cutthroat meant I had to consider my Key Ops candidates. They need to be troopers, not markers, on the table, and be both tough and aggressive. A TAG is mostly ideal, but the Key Ops gets +3 Dodge and TacAware, if they don’t have those skills already. So I had a slight hankering for some other piece to take on the role. This mission also has open information Lieutenants, no Loss of Lt, and points for nabbing them. So that meant a Brigada Lt list for this mission - no great hardship!
  • Corporate Appropriation requires mobility, which is good in any context, and some D-Charges, which are more reliable ways to destroy the Prototype (due to the Automatic Defense System rule it has) than any melee fighter Corregidor can take. 

Apart from those small wrinkles, I was free to shape my two lists however I liked. Since I was already committed to the Division bit, I attempted to clearly differentiate them in several other ways, to give me options for varying tables and match-ups in most of the Missions.

List 1 - Surface Division

This would be my first real stress test of the new units (I had played a few games already with their previewed rules). Accordingly, since one of my favourites was the Coyote KHD with E/Mitter, I made sure I had one in there, sheltered under a Brigada Lt’s Firewall-6, and accompanied by Jazz for hacking tuition. That basically ensured this would be my Cutthroat list. That Fireteam went through a couple of permutations, and I tried to have enough valid models to field two small teams, but eventually, under other points pressure, I simply had to throw a Bambadroid (primarily for the mine dispenser) and a Shifta (for EM defense and Sensor) on top to create a 5-model Core team. That’s a solid utility team for defence and hacking control, although it is expensive. It also naturally led me to take two Moran Masai and a Vertigo guided missile Zond - daring, I know - as well as a Salyut baggage bot to reload mines and pitchers as needed. 

That was a lot of points spent, however, and I hadn’t added in much active combat power, so I reached for another new profile that I think is the pre-eminent firepower in Corregidor, the Iguana with BS Attack(-3). For more close-quarters work, I went with McMurrough, an old favourite who has only gotten better in N5, and eventually with a Bearcat shotgun KHD. I really love that last profile, I think it’s one of the main wins from the Mazebreaker update for Corregidor, but it was a real pain to fit into the list, and I’d considered other options like a Lobo/Diablo Duo, which would put less pressure on my Order pool (and provide Discoballers). In the end I went with the Bearcat, because it’s awesome, it gives me another avenue to achieve hacking dominance, and ultimately most of the Missions at this event rewarded aggressive play. This did mean I had to take a Warcor to hit 15 models, hardly a bad thing.

I thought this was a fairly hardball list, and it certainly has a lot of the elements I think are strong in Surface Division. In retrospect, it has some flaws - the large Fireteam essentially has to deploy as one team, which makes it inflexible, and there are plenty of points in utility, setup of mines/pitchers/missiles, and a secure Lt and hacking base. All of which are great things, but it did occur to me that the only simple solution for long range threats was the Iguana. It seems like a list where if I lose 2-3 important pieces, I could be rendered toothless, even though most of my points and Orders were on the board. Something to play with as the update settles in.

List 2 - Deep Space Division

While it is a bit silly, with significant weaknesses, I knew I had to try the new option for a pure Gecko/EVAder Duo, specifically with the Gecko’s Vulcan Shotgun profile. Having a non-cc-hardened TAG with an 8” active gun is pure insanity, but it is PS4 with AP/Continuous damage. You’d need a heart of stone to be unmoved by that. I paired it with the Engineer EVAder with AP Spitfire, partly to provide a high Burst ranged weapon and partly because it’s the only engineer option that also includes a Firewall. To cover and complement the long-range weaknesses of that element, I also knew this was a list for the Intruder sniper with its gleaming new Special Dice. I’m quite fond of Gecko/Intruder lists, and this is a new twist on that old classic. 

To pay for all these toys, and to differentiate from the first list, I took a plain Alguacil Lt and decoy, and non-FTO Jazz with Billie. I did take 2 Morans and McMurrough - I barely know how to function without them - but I avoided the missile bot, partly as another differentiation, and partly because with just solo Jazz, it’s a fragile system. You don’t have a very reliable Pitcher, and the Vertigo and Jazz are both single, relatively vulnerable elements which it relies on. Filling out the list, I took a Jaguar Duo, for defence and to provide some reliable smoke for the Intruder which wasn’t tied to McMurrough, who often needs to shoot off on a one-way ticket to the opponent’s Deployment Zone (DZ). I went with a Lobo/Diablo Duo, primarily for the Discoballer, which I might need to cover the Gecko team’s advance, but also for a chance to try out the Diablo’s new strengths as an active turn melee threat. Finally I took a Tactical Awareness Stempler Zond, planning to join it to the Gecko or Lobo Duo as needed. 

I wasn’t sure this list was that mean, but it has a lot of separate elements I really like. The only proper ‘Deep Space’ element is the Gecko/EVAder, and as cool as they are, I made a note to try a similar format while replacing them (they’re a hefty 84 points) with a couple of the best new Surface Division profiles. If there was one mission I thought I’d use this for, it was Corporate Appropriation, not so much for the EVAder’s D-Charges or the Diablo (McMurrough was present in both lists and the Bearcat is probably the best solo piece in the Sectorial for reaching and destroying a Prototype) but for the Discoballers, and the D-Charges I’d paid extra for on the two Moran Masai. But I was prepared to pick this list for any mission where I thought that Mimetism-6 would be a key skill for my opponent. 

Round 1 - Crossing Lines

Pre-game and Deployment

After arriving at the convention centre and pushing through enormous crowds of people queuing for the various halls, I got the table set up and had a great time talking to some old friends and familiar opponents. There was a bit of a scare because someone (who was bringing a table) was late, and I thought I might be presented with a bye, but it was sorted out and I was rematched against Cams, a very experienced player whom I have won and lost against in the past. I was keen to use my Surface Division list for this mission, as I might need the defensive Fireteam, with its Coyote and Shifta, if I had to play second. He was playing Onyx Contact Force, and promptly won the roll and picked to go first. OCF have access to Nourkias, so I clenched my various body parts accordingly. 

The view of the table from my Deployment Zone.Cams deployed a big, Level 5 Unidron Fireteam with a missile, a plasma sniper, and a character KHD with bonus White Noise, plus a couple of scrubs. They went on his left flank, supported by a Base Operator shotgun engineer. He had a few flash pulse bots scattered about, and an Ikadron in the forward zone, near the console on my left, but it was very light. His only expensive model was a Suryat HMG (I suspected it was his Lt as it has +1 Order and Nourkias is an NCO) on his right flank. He was holding 2 models back, and I practically knew there would be a couple off the table. I set my team up on my far left, with the Coyote looking out and the Shifta screening their front with his E/Marat and EM mine. I spread my own flash pulse bots and Warcor as cautious AROs, my missile prone on a roof in the centre, and of course my Morans in the forward zone covering the consoles. In an unsurprising reserve drop, my opponent placed Nourkias (an NCO) prone on his far right in the forward zone, ready to leap into my main defensive position, and he successfully rolled WIP to place an Imp-2 Marker (ie a Greif) next to Jazz. I counter-deployed the Iguana in my centre, where it could see Nourkias and was hidden from the Unidron team’s long range weaponry.

Round 1

As an amuse bouche, my opponent risked his Greif against Jazz, and rather than contest the FtF, I simply used her boarding pistol template. I was lucky here, the Greif went down and Jazz passed two survival rolls - ironically, the Greif’s update-given breaker combi rifle is significantly worse against Jazz than the normal combi it used to have. Setting up with a 40”+ pitcher shot from his Core Fireteam (still on good odds thanks to an X-Visor and +1SD/BS), Cams then risked Nourkias against the Iguana’s AP HMG shot for Total Control. This was a one-dice, disadvantaged FtF roll thanks to the TAG’s ECM Hacker(-3) and BS Attack(-3), but he won the roll on the second attempt and the Iguana failed its saves. Uh oh. Now here, I think my opponent should have looked for spots to place B2, PS5 heavy flamethrower templates on multiple of my models, and eventually used the Iguana to trigger my Shifta’s EM mine. It would have forced me to break up my Fireteam and I’d have been virtually certain to lose some important stuff. Instead, he moved the Iguana about to dump HMG rounds into Jazz, but between my hacking AROs (the Iguana was under my own repeaters) and the unsuitable range, he didn’t get anywhere. 

Trying to clear the way for Nourkias, my opponent dropped a Fractaa boarding shotgun, and traded it for my Shifta, springing the EM mine in the process. Nourkias sprang forward, but couldn’t deal with my Firewalled hackers and simultaneous flash pulse AROs, ending up wounded and Stunned by flash pulses in the open. Hindsight is golden, but I think my opponent really needed some vision control in his list for this sort of assault - a weakness of OCF. My opponent moved his Suryat into his scoring zone.

Nourkias (on the far left) ends up Stunned. At this point the Iguana is still Possessed.

Going back to my (his) Iguana, but sticking with its HMG, my opponent killed my Vertigo Zond, and I re-Possessed the Iguana, but it was then in LoF of the Unidrons. It took 4 wounds from plasma sniper fire and the Operator popped out into Eclipse Smoke, on his No Wound Incapacitation. It was touch and go, but it looked like I had survived the first turn.

My Iguana Operator, barely alive and sweating heavily, but safe inside Eclipse Smoke.

I was able to sweep up Nourkias in short Order, using my Brigada Lt, but the Unidron plasma sniper gamely resisted my Iguana Operator, passing a fair few survival rolls. I sent the Bearcat in to weaken that Fireteam, killing one Unidron with a shotgun and Kerr-Nau with Trinity, and left it in the enemy scoring zone. In a mental lapse, I thought at the time this meant I’d secured the zone, completely forgetting about the Suryat HMG. But the Bearcat, by forcing Unidron sniper to Dodge into a new position, did let me move McMurrough into my own scoring zone, making the score at the end of the Round 1-1.

Round 2

My opponent brought his Base Operator (who carries a Heavy Shotgun!) out to fight my Bearcat, and we fought a shotgun duel in the open, with a lot of rolling for 19s on the dice, but luck took a hand and we were both tanking multiple shots. My opponent brought on a Cube Jaeger, and my Bearcat got lucky and knocked it out. The Base Operator finally dropped the Bearcat, and managed to take out my right hand Moran, tanking a koala. This incredible run of luck saw it end up in close combat with McMurrough when he made his 4” Dodge.

Bearcat (proxied by a Mobile Brigada) heroically drops the Cube Jaeger and holds up the Base Operator.

I felt I had the whip hand in terms of casualties and board control, but things weren’t quite clicking for the mission. I put a lot of Orders through my Iguana Operator, taking out the remaining Unidron heavy weapons, advancing up the table, and getting a bead on the Suryat HMG out of cover, knocking it out. That was nice, but I had made a vital error…my opponent would be in Retreat. I had, somehow, got the idea that Crossing Lines was a No Quarter mission (it’s so aggressive!) but in fact it does end in Retreat. 

I was proud of killing this Suryat, but it proved a mistake.

Realising the danger, I went to secure the far zone, and might have also been able to flip the Onyx-controlled console for a major win. But that Base Operator refused to die. He survived a round of combat with McMurrough(!) and promptly made his Dodge while my Iguana Operator moved up past the console into the enemy scoring zone. So I had to kill him with heavy pistol shots, rather than try to flip the console - the NWI Operator would likely have been killed by the Heavy Shotgun ARO. I scored both zones, sending the score to 3-1, but I knew I’d messed the result up properly.

Round 3

My opponent was in Loss of Lieutenant, but had 2 Regular Orders from his Warhorse Imetrons, and Command Tokens left. He promptly landed a Vitapack Speedball, ran and climbed his Imetron to the Suryat, tanking an AP mine along the way, and revived it. This was something of a hail mary play which relied on a couple of rolls passing, but I didn’t have AROs in place to contest it. Together with the Imetron’s Baggage bonus, this let my opponents out-point the Iguana Operator in his scoring zone, and of course he scored 1OP for the console I hadn’t been able to flip, making the final score 4-3 to me.

Together with an Ikadron, this plucky Exrah really helped my opponent keep me to a narrow victory.

Result and Lessons Learned

I was rather deflated, feeling I had thrown away what could have been a solid win there. Crossing Lines is always a chaotic first turn and I’d come through it alright (as said, if my opponent had played the Possessed Iguana differently, I think I might not have). I should have played my Bearcat differently, and gone after more cheap Orders, rather than miscalculating its effect on scoring zones. Overall I just didn’t think accurately about the mission. Fair play to my opponent, who despite the failure of his initial attack, which is the kiss of death in Crossing Lines, played his outs for a creditable final score.

Round 2 - Area of Interest

Pre-game and Deployment

My opponent, James, was a great guy who had travelled over from Northern Ireland for the expo, and was playing Kestrel Colonial Force. The table was dominated by a tall structure in the centre which cut off a lot of long firelanes. I thought this was excellent terrain for the big team in my Surface list, and I didn’t see anywhere I could leverage an Intruder sniper’s long range, so I chose my Surface Division list again. I won the Lt roll, and chose to go first - it’s advantageous in the mission, because you decide placement of the Special Area, as well as the normal advantages and not having to survive a Redeye attack run in the first turn. 

I deployed my big team on the large central structure, ready to move up and dominate the ground with Pitchers and/or Mines. All my other stuff was strung out for defence, with McMurrough and the Iguana held back. I placed the Special and my other Area of Interest on my left flank, and made a minor mistake by not placing anything particularly ready to dominate the Special Area in Round 1. 

My DZ, dominated by access (with Strategic Deployment) to the central building. Scoring Zones are the templates on the left.

My opponent placed a lot of Jackals, Camo markers which I knew to be Yamabushi, a Fennec Thunderbolt, and even a Hetkari with Multi Red Fury, quite aggressively looking out on ARO. His Magistrate Lt and some supporting chaff were hidden well back. I placed McMurrough and the Iguana behind the central structure, and he placed a Redeye partially behind a building in the centre of his DZ, blocking McMurrough’s Impetuous movement. His other reserve (he had Strategos from the Magistrate) was a Hidden unit, which I was convinced would be a Blink Heavy Rocket Launcher.

My opponent's quite bold AROs - you can see the Hetkari and Redeye both peeking out slightly.

I launched my first turn, provoking a few fights with the Iguana, and luckily winning them with its BS Attack(-3), taking out a Fennec, Jackal and Yamabushi. White Noise allowed me to engage the Hetkari separately, and force it Prone after taking a wound. My aim was to free my Coyote E/Mitter to move across the central rooftop and have a shot at his Redeye, but my plan was rudely interrupted as my opponent revealed his Hidden ARO - a surprising Crux Knight with AP SMG (+1SD)! It was right near my Fireteam on a separate tall rooftop, and revealed to take a shot at Jazz while my Bambadroid was the team Leader. Rather than contest the ARO (2 dice on 17s) I simply placed AP mines where they would cover the Crux Knight in future Orders - Jazz went down. I then pressured it with a Moran’s boarding shotgun and the Coyote’s Carbonite program. But this stubborn knight, although wounded, wouldn’t stay immobilised. I was able, by scaring him into Total Cover, able to take out a Yamabushi with the Bambadroid, and then brick the Redeye with my Coyote E/Mitter.

At this point, I sent McMurrough in, despite only having 3-4 Orders left. My reasoning was that the Redeye, of all the remaining enemies, had the mobility to hurt me despite the haemorrhage of Orders on my opponent’s side. He had a Tech-Bee behind it with a Gizmokit, so would certainly be able to un-brick it in a couple Orders at most. McMurrough leapt across the table, exchanging ineffectual chain rifle and SMG fire with the Crux Knight, finally killing the Redeye, and a Pathfinder Dronbot, with his chain rifles, and the Tech-Bee in melee. He’s a lethal weapon. At some point towards the end, I broke my Brigada Lt from my team, to Dodge a koala the Crux Knight had laid (as an ARO to McMurrough) and stand up watching my backfield.

McMurrough, exhausted after doing his thing.

My opponent had lost 8 models in Turn 1 - not a good position. His Magistrate Lt did take McMurrough out neatly in one Order. Then he tried a Black Air Parachutist (DZ) in my rear, taking a big risk against my Brigada’s multi rifle ARO. He survived his landing, but died performing the Sabotage Classified Objective. Personally I think my opponent might have risked him to score the Special Area instead, but that was well covered by flash pulses and Repeater area, so had its own issues.

The Black AIR Parachutist sacrifices himself for a Classified Objective.

Finally he dove for the central antenna with his Crux Knight, activating it and dying in a hail of mine shrapnel and bullets. That did put the score at 2-0 in his favour, but it didn’t look likely to last.

The brave Crux Knight, moments before sacrificing himself for the Objective Point.

Round 2

Pretty firmly in control of the game, I went to place models in the Areas of Interest, and flip the central antenna with a Moran Masai. I might have killed a couple of things with the Iguana.

My opponent sent his Hetkari against the Coyote, who luckily bricked it in ARO, and a Yamabushi against the Iguana, which did reduce it to Operator status with a panzerfaust shot. He next tried a game play to Discoball a Fennec around my left flank to shoot away the Transductor Zond which was holding the Special Area for me, but luckily my Remote won the FtF and Stunned him. The Round ended at 2-2 since I now held the Antenna and the Special Area.

My opponent's last real offensive options, unfortunately let down by the dice.

Round 3

With putting the enemy into Retreat no longer a risk, I swept up some remaining models with the Iguana Operator and finished with it holding the Area of Interest my opponent had placed, on his far left flank. My Fireteam finished moving about and locked down the other Area and the Special Area, removing the Fennec on that flank and gunning down the bricked Hetkari. I also achieved my Classified Objective, HVT Designation, by pitching and using Jazz (I Speedballed her earlier) to Spotlight his HVT. Just for a laugh, I combat dropped my Bearcat, stuck the landing, and shotgunned the Magistrate Lt. Take that, you cheap Strategos hack. 

My opponent had only a few backfield models left, so his turn didn’t really happen.

Results and Thoughts

The game ended 8-2 to me - the last Round scores very heavily in Area of Interest. Overall this game was massively shaped by me playing first, and my opponent’s very bold placement of his AROs. Not to criticise him unfairly in hindsight - I have made similar strategic mistakes myself many times - but Fennecs and Jackals are going to get killed in ARO against a competent player and a tournament list, which is bound to have some apex gunfighter. Gambling on the slim chance of a lucky panzerfaust shot is silly, and there’s simply no reason, in this mission, to offer AROs to do much except keep the first player out of your DZ. Similarly, I think he was too quick to reveal his Yamabushi, rather than make me Discover/Shoot them, or save them for his Active turns. That said, I didn’t have any real strokes of bad luck in this game, and in fact it would have been plausible for his Redeye to tank more chain rifle hits and survive to be repaired or Speedballed. 

This game really showcased the value of my big Fireteam, with the Coyote, Bambadroid, Jazz and Brigada Lt all playing their parts. The Iguana was also a star, doing what I needed by blasting through AROs and showcasing White Noise.

Round 3 - Cutthroat

Pre-Game and Deployment

Well, now I had to face a properly strong opponent, Corrie, who has beaten me before and was using vanilla JSA. This is a very aggressive army and I think entirely suited to Cutthroat as a mission. They are far more resilient against McMurrough than most factions and just generally brawl well. I was tempted to use my Deep Space list, because Ryukens are excellent and an Intruder can snap them up, but I thought that the Gecko team would just be too vulnerable at close quarters against all those horrible samurai and ninjas. Surface Division once again! I luckily won the roll and of course chose to play first.

My deployment, note the Fireteam sheltering my Lt and Jazz atop the building on the left.

I chose to hold back the Iguana, and, oddly, my Bearcat - I had an idea to make it my Key Ops for the mission, to try and get some kills in Round 1, which would lock in an exclusive 3OP if I could also kill the enemy Key Ops. Taking advantage of the 16” DZ and the Shifta’s Strategic Deployment, I stuck my Fireteam in my left-centre, McMurrough in the centre, and bots scattered elsewhere, with my Morans in the traditional left and right of the midfield. 

My opponent placed a Musashi & Shizoku APHMG Duo on his right (the Shizoku would be his Key Ops) with some scattered supporting models, a Ryuken covering them and a Mim-3 marker, probably a Kyojin Killer, in front. On his far right he had a Hatomoto spitfire, supported by a Tanko Discoballer and a couple of bots, with his Domaru Lt right behind them in the corner - far from my Pitcher capability. He also had a Hidden Deployment, which he rolled to over-infiltrate. The target number, 15, revealed it to be an Oniwaban, as Corrie sportingly explained to me. 

I set my Iguana in the centre, and my Bearcat on my right, ready to pressure his Lt. Corrie deployed a second Ryuken unit on his right, ready to hold back my Bearcat.

Round 1

Corrie very astutely stripped both Orders from my Iguana’s group, which restricted my options for ranged firepower. I set up a trap with a Moran’s koala, and then moved my Bearcat aggressively to force both Ryuken to reveal in ARO during successive Orders. Luckily my Bearcat escaped with a single wound, but less luckily, the Ryuken on my left tanked the koala. I managed to take it out with my Iguana, leaving the TAG a little forward of my DZ in the centre. The Bearcat was free to pick out a Baggage bot, but then lost a FtF and die against the Tanko’s light shotgun - again a bit unlucky. 

My other main play was to pitch, Spotlight and kill the Shizoku AP HMG. As well as being a terrifying active turn gun (5 dice, BS13 on an ARM5, Mim-3 HI), since it was his Key Ops, this locked me into the 3OP for my Key Ops having killed more models than his, even if that was one unimpressive baggage bot. However, missile plays take a fair few Orders, and this denuded my Iguana’s group, leaving it a bit vulnerable. With the Bearcat dead and little else to do, I scattered some mines around the TAG and hoped for the best.

This turn ended up dealing just 3 casualties to the enemy, and despite one of them being the Key Ops, it wasn’t really enough. While my Bearcat was a bit unlucky, I think in retrospect I should have sent him down the left flank, where he had softer targets, and, if the missile play had worked out similarly, might have ended in a safe position. I also left too many models exposed to potential counterattack, which was definitely coming.

Most unfortunately, my opponent’s Hidden Oniwaban was within 4” of my Iguana. I knew this was a potential risk, but as a matter of principle, I don’t worry too much about Hidden AROs, as it just lets opponents get inside your head. In hindsight I should have tried to find a fighting position where I ended up inside my DZ, but I’m not sure it would have been possible. As it transpired, the Oniwaban crit and neatly bisected the Iguana, while provoking 2 koalas and 2 mines, with predictable results. An excellent trade for my opponent. He then followed up by trying his remaining Ryuken against my Coyote. This was a coinflip roll (2x 12s against my 3x 10s) but Corrie accurately judged that failure would barely matter - the Ryuken would just be Isolated - and success would be crucial. The Ryuken won the roll and my Coyote burned to death.

Rook to Queen - the super-ninja takes my Iguana cleanly. The mines' and koalas' positions are just to highlight the mutually assured destruction.

This drastically collapsed my structure, and suddenly a Kyojin Killer was able to launch across the centre and stab one of my Moran Masai. Now my defences were laid bare, and his Hatomoto spun up. Moving very carefully and skillfully to find angles, it killed my other Moran, McMurrough, and my Shifta. I was very definitely on the back foot.

Round 2

Although some casualties had been inflicted on my opponent, I was really running out of active models to accomplish things with. I thought that my only real option was another pitcher play with Jazz, and resolved to move my Fireteam rightwards across the table to find that line. Unfortunately, my Bambadroid struggled to finish a Kyojin Killer out of cover, and the rest of the team was unable to move efficiently until that was done, for fear of the flammenspeer ARO. This meant I ran short of Orders, and had to throw a defensive pitcher, rather than chaining into a possible Oblivion, or even missile strike, into his Hatomoto. Instead I had to use 2 off-group Orders repositioning the missile bot and a flash pulse bot in a desperate effort to fend off the incoming attack.

My Fireteam unfortunately struggles to fight through a Kyojin Killer, and falls short of getting me back into the game.

The Hatomoto now spun up again. It might actually have had the full 10 Orders plus 2 Lt Orders to spend here. Corrie moved, again, very neatly around the angles, and despite being held back outside its ideal range, eventually dealt with my Bambadroid and Vertigo Zond, found an angle outside ZoC on my flash bot, and worked his way around my pitched Repeater, killing my WarCor, to approach my Mobile Brigada Lt from along my own board edge! This was an incredibly display of banzai mobility, but after failing a Dodge to get round a corner into melee range (it might actually have been 2 successive Dodge attempts), the Hatomoto fell short of actually finishing my Lt off.

Round 3

With my forces in rags (I would have been close to Retreat, were that a thing in Cutthroat), all I wanted to do was save my Lt. Scoring is odd in this mission - I had 3OP for more models killed via Key Ops, my opponent would score 3OP for more models surviving, and we would both score 1OP for killing the Key Ops. I could force a 4-4 Draw if my Mobile Brigada could just survive. I Carbonited and then gunned down the Hatomoto, then I moved the Brigada right to my board edge, with Jazz and the Shifta trying to screen him, and set him into Suppression Fire. This would prove to be inadequate and a mistaken strategy - I’ll come back to that.

My opponent was coolly confident, because, as he put it, he had Miyamoto Musashi. This legendary samurai proceeded to spend ~10 Orders, and move literally 60-70” in a giant ‘L’ shape around most of the board, from the opponent’s DZ on my left, across and forward to enter my DZ on my right, close in on my Brigada Lt, and cut him down in melee. The only ARO he provoked was an AP mine, which he didn’t even trouble to Dodge (passed his survival roll), until he was actually fighting the Brigada - suppression fire doesn’t do much against Martial Arts Level 5, and I didn’t crit. Corrie even had one Order left in the bag. 

This was extremely well played by my opponent, and I have never really seen JSA’s assault capability stretched out to that extent. In hindsight, I might have had a chance if I had motored my Fireteam to the far right corner, instead of staying in the centre. This was less because of distance, but because Musashi might not have been able to move around AROs from Jazz or the Shifta’s E/Marat. I put those assets between my Brigada and the threat, not realising how far he’d be able to move around them. If I were in a corner, or if I’d remembered the Shifta’s EM Mines and placed them directly covering the Brigada, I’d have had more of a chance. 

Results and Thoughts

This game was mostly won by my opponent’s very cool play, not necessarily lost by my mistakes. That said, I could have sent my Bearcat in differently, or even kept him off the board - with a passed Combat Jump, he might have had a decent shot at the enemy Lt. My play of going for the enemy Key Ops was sound in terms of mission scoring; my key mistakes were in leaving myself open to attacks following that. I could have deployed my Coyote where he tried to cover my DZ instead of looking across the table, but I’m not sure I would have done any differently, given his uses against Kyojin killers, and/or to keep other models back at long range. Overall I was just a bit sloppier than Corrie was. If I learned one thing about the list, it was its over-reliance on the Iguana as the most direct solution to problems.

Part 1 Conclusion

Losing that third game would put me firmly out of the running for winning the event, but there was still time to go 4-1, which I guess is still what I personally consider ‘fine’ for a two day tournament. More importantly, I hadn’t had the chance to use my Gecko shotgun yet! We will resume this article next week to run through the final two games.
