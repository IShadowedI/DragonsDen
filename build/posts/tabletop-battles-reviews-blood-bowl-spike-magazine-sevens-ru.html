Tabletop Battles Reviews: Blood Bowl Spike! Magazine (Sevens Rules) and Pitch 2026

August 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Well hi-de-hi sports ball fans! Welcome to the Tabletop Battles review of Spike 22 aka the Blood Bowl Sevens edition! Spike! Magazine is BACK with issue 22 and this one is ALL about the popular ‘Sevens’ format of Blood Bowl. There is also a new pitch to go with and we have had a good nose of that too! As always our thanks go out to Games Workshop for providing us with an early copy for us to sink-gnaw our abnormally large in both size and number of teeth into!

Spike! Issue 22.
Games Workshop

Games Workshop are also releasing THREE new plastic big guys today, go HERE for all our thoughts on these BIG new additions to your Blood Bowl roster.

Why Play Sevens Blood Bowl?

Spike! Issue 22. Credit: Games Workshop

Badusernametag: Blood Bowl Sevens is a smaller, faster-paced game than regular Elevens, which presents coaches with differing challenges and play styles while maintaining all the carnage and mayhem that you'll have come to expect from a full match. The fluff is that Sevens games are the amateur and semi-pro leagues underneath the majors, with players coming up and trying to make it BIG in the beautiful, bloody game.

Dakari-Mane: It's a great way to get your Blood Bowl fix in if you need to play at lunch or if you want to get more games in than you'd otherwise be able to in a day. It’s shorter in turns and is played on a smaller pitch, so you get a truncated experience, but with the full rules. A big core difference here is that the two teams' lines of scrimmage are further apart, so you don’t get to throw blocks at each other right from the off. This means it can be a cagier game, with more ball play, ducking, diving and dodging!

The Fluff

Dakari-Mane: As always, there's some amazing background included in this edition of Spike! covering where Sevens originated, how it spread, and the ongoing popularity of the game in the Old World; there's also "Dirt from the Dugout", "Chat with the Rat", "Ask Lord Borak" and "Coffin Corner," which add some lighthearted fun.

Badusernametag: I’d not paid Spike(!) much attention before and it’s a really nicely formatted piece of work. Engaging from the beginning for a fan and a player. The layout, production and art are spot on, rules presentation aside. The in-world adverts are pretty hilarious:

Amazing art on these in game ads!! Spike! Issue 22. Credit: Games Workshop

The Rules

Badusernametag: There's a lot going on here and this is a definite expansion of the Sevens rules presented in Deathzone last season. We will mainly focus on changes here, as most of this has been covered before. We assume you have a basic idea of the OG Sevens rules.

Team Building

Previously you could take any number of your large volume linos (the 0-12 or 0-16 options) and up to four other players. Now you can only have any number of Lineman Keyword players and then four other players. This has a big impact for teams with multiple types of Lineman on the roster. This is a very big buff to Old World Alliance, who have a variety of useful linemen. The changes are also very useful for Humans & Orcs, both of which have cheap stunty lineman access. You might not have had space on the squad for a goblin, but if they’re just replacing a Lino and not a Blitzter they might just make the squad!!

Chaos Renegades and Underworld Denizens LOVE this. Chaos Renegades open up all their fun and flavourful types of Lineman and can still take a thrower and big guys. Whilst Skaven can now take as many Snots and Grots as they like! Both teams are also functionally unaffected by these rules as each team only has 4 non-lineman players available on their roster!? (Well kinda Dakari-Mane, they can still only take four non-linemen, they just could have one of each!!!). Chaos Dwarves suffer somewhat from not having the Lineman keyword.

Whereas Snotlings seem to get scuppered, as the Insignificant trait doesn't allow them to include more players with insignificant than those without & they're restricted to four non-insignificant players. So I guess you're running two Trolls, two Pump Wagons & four Snotlings!

Re-rolls flat 100,000 GP from double. Definitely a change I'm in favour of as for many teams forking out over 100k made a RR unaffordable. Cheerleaders are 0-6 now, No big thing. Dedicated as fans restricted to +1 at time of drafting. This makes sense for ‘Rookie’ teams. Veteran - 1 player per team -1ma, but gains "semi-pro" (1 per game RR for pick up, pass, catch or self inflicted pow in a block) a useful little addition to the team,  particularly when re-rolls are 100k.

Team Creation

CrabStuffedMushrooms: The change in Lineman definition to the Keyword instead of the 0-12/0-16 restrictions is clean and easier to understand, but it has some interesting effects. Namely, I think it makes OWA and UW incredibly flexible - and they are already amazing teams. It lets OWA in particular put a lot of skills on the pitch. I made a few 600gp teams, and they were certainly great. But I think with cash, the teams could grow into real monsters if they can get an extra Dwarf or Linerat.

I think overall, the flexibility this affords to allow things like Halflings on the field is worth it. It is clean and easy to understand. They didn’t seem to accommodate the Insignificant trait, which is an easy fix, but creates a problem in the moment.

Loner 5+ change for Journeymen is really fun, and while I don’t think it will have a big impact with how rarely rerolls are used, I bet it happens in clutch moments from time to time in a way that hilariously ruins a drive.

I like the flat 100k reroll. The former method of doubling the cost was extra punishing for some of the more expensive team rerolls. I think this is just cheap enough that some people may opt for the Team reroll at creation, and hope to fill in their positionals with more cash as a league progresses. Veteran player rule is amazing fluff, is unevenly impactful from team to team, and I love it.

Inducements

Like 11s, the big thing here is that Prayers to Nuffle are too impactful. Especially since you can use your own Treasury on them, it seems hard not to take them. Rerolling duplicates means you are almost guaranteed at least one really good result.

  • The Desperate Measures retool is great and keeps the energy of the original. 
  • Sports Espionage was already insane, and now TWO rerolls is pretty crazy.
  • Set Piece still adds some dice rolls instead of being automatic. It prevents an auto-score situation.
  • Discarded Banana Skin is going to be easy to forget, but it creates a tension similar to Magic Scroll where you have to think about it the whole game!
  • Unfortunately, Prayers to Nuffle dilute the desperate measures a little. I think it will front-load too many random rolls at the start of the match. A TV difference of 5k to 10k does not need balancing.
  • I wish Team Mascot was available for 50k.

In Game

Dakari-Mane: The Kick off table has had a thorough going over like a fallen Dragon Prince surrounded by goblins. Many entries have been tightened up with minor tweaks, amendments or changes.

2 - Get the Ref: No change.

3 - Time Out: Turn 5+ now instead of turn 4+.

4 - Alert Defense: May move into no man's land.

5 - High Kick: May move into no man's land.

6 - Cheering Fans: Block assist.

7 - Brilliant Coaching: No change.

8 - Changing Weather: No change.

9 - Quick Snap: No change.

10 - CHARGE!: D3+1 open teammates can move. Alternatively: one can blitz, one can throw a teammate, and one can kick a teammate instead.

11 - Dodgy Snack: Roll off, loser selects a random player 2+ -1ma, av for drive, on a 1 off for the drive.

12 - Pitch Invasion: Only 1 player now.

Amateur referee: Reflecting the less prestigious nature of Sevens, the quality of the referees cannot be guaranteed - argue the call succeeds on a 5+.

Throw-in distance is now d6+2 and kick of scatter is now "roll 2d6, pick the lowest." This is a nice change that reduces scatter to reflect the smaller size of the Sevens pitch. Fewer wild throws and kicks that always go off the pitch!

Badusernametag: One of my (few) gripes with the first version of Sevens was the ambiguity over how you resolved kick offs. The Deathzone rules told you to kick off as normal, but not having a single central Line of Scrimmage didn’t allow for that to be resolved clearly. You place the ball in “your opponents half”, but does that include no mans land? When is a Touchback triggered? I was hoping this would be resolved in the new rules and it’s not really.

So it doesn’t define where you place the ball. So we assume your opponents “half” is half way through no mans land again, although it does tell you its not a touchback if the ball goes into no mans land. So an improvement at least!?

League Play

Dakari-Mane: A whole section dedicated to the best way to play the beautiful game!
  • Journeymen: Due to this being the rookie leagues, a Journeyman that joins your team to get you up to 7 players has the loner (5+) trait. Probably best off avoiding doing anything with them then!
  • Desperate Measures: The table has been updated from last edition & you can take up to five of them.
  • Prayers to Nuffle: A modified table is included & teams can have up to 2 rolls.
  • Player Advancement: no player may gain leader or pro.

Nobility comes in clutch here with a potential four pros in the starting line-up! I'm unclear as to how this rule interacts with the team captain rule; do you only gain the 6+ re-roll retention? Probably needs clarification.

Skill Progression 

After each game you can either:

  • Randomly generate a primary skill from a stack of three (you choose two, your opponent chooses one) by rolling a d3 for a player of your choice
  • Randomly give a secondary skill to a random player.

Players leaving the team after gaining skills remains the same as last edition, however they have added an option to pay a player to stay, at the reasonable price of what they cost (base player cost plus skills), however if you pay a player to stay,  their cost increases, meaning they'll cost more to keep on the team the next time someone tries to poach them!

I can really see this driving people towards not skilling up particularly expensive players. Would you risk your wardancer quitting the team after ever game?

CrabStuffedMushrooms: I think the new advancement randomization is a great balance between control and chaos. Almost all trees have several great skills to choose from. It will be fun to see the vibe between player groups for your opponent to also pick a good skill or to mess with you. I wish there was still an Elite skill tax of some kind. If it was totally random, I would understand. But since you get to pick your stabs at Dodge, Block, etc., some sort of premium would still be warranted. They are just too good and get even better with lack of rerolls.

In the previous version, any player on the team could advance. Now, players have to not be taken out as a casualty. This will add more excitement to losing a player to a casualty, as Lasting Injuries don’t happen, and Death is almost as rare as in 11s. I like that you can pay to maintain your drafted player, but I don’t like that your advanced player may get taken before you even get to play a single game with them. 

Because the Drafting Players step happens before the Hiring, Firing, and Temporarily Retiring step, you will get to buy a replacement player before the next game. So this is a bit of a salve, but still requires you to have cash. Love the ban of Leader and Pro for chosen skills, but like that it could still randomly pop up if anyone was crazy enough to do a Secondary Skill.

The new hiring restrictions continue to be a thing I think is fluffy and fun, but may be even more punishing for leagues in sevens. There are no lasting injuries, but we may go into key games down a couple of positionals for Serious Injuries, which is a big deal if you only have four!

New Rules

Dakari-Mane: There are modified rules for redrafting Sevens teams for new seasons, and a matched play section describing how to run tournaments of Sevens, covering drafting a team, inducements, star player points, team tiers & skills. Most interesting to me is that there will be a revised tier list being released to reflect how different teams perform under the Sevens rules.

Then we have five example Sevens teams -- Skaven, Norse, Goblin, Khorne & Chaos Chosen. Each team listing comes with a "how to play this team" guide and a "what to do when you start skilling up" guide.

Badusernametag: We really like this as a concept. Fun, thematic, mixes hobby with rules and makes a nice package of it all as a team building display. Would like it if they did more of them. Maybe in a future release? This is the kind of copy that really makes this release interesting as well as just being rules content. 

New Sevens Pitch 2026

Badusernametag: The new pitch comes in the exact same size as the original and comes with the same two small format dugouts as well. The design here is generic, simply bringing the Sevens pitch up to date in design with the third season pitch and dugouts. It’s neat and looks good.

I like that the endzones are distinct and clear colours in this new one.

Bloodbowl Sevens Pitch 2026 Credit: crabbedstuffmushrooms

Bloodbowl Sevens Pitch 2026. Credit: crabbedstuffmushrooms

Final Thoughts

Dakari-Mane: Overall we’re in favour of the tweaks and changes that have been made, it's tightened things up nicely and reined in some of the potentially apocalyptic kick-off table results. The matched play rules look like a great way to play one off pick-up games where you can each have some upgrades. I'm genuinely looking forward to playing some sevens again. And once the official Sevens team tier list drops, I might even give running a one -ay event a go.

Badusernametag: Sevens continues apace as the fast and furious way to crack out a game of Blood Bowl. Really pleased to see it grow a new lease of life in these comprehensive and dedicated rules for the third season. It will continue to be my favourite way for a friendly or a teaching game, but with these new and developed rules for leagues and tourneys it may gain a greater life yet. Seven is my new lucky number.

Until next time, happy gnawing on the bones of your enemies!
