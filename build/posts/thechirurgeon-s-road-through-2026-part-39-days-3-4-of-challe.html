TheChirurgeon's Road Through 2026, Part 39: Days 3-4 of Challenger's Cup

September 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Yesterday I wrote about the first two days of my time at Challengers Cup - arriving at the event and the first day of games. Today I'm diving right into days 2 and 3 of the event, talking about the rest of my games and our final finish. Please avoid checking ahead and wait until I'm done to find out how we did, thanks.

Saturday: Trying (and Failing) to go 0-0-6

Saturday starts with a solid breakfast. We bought a dozen eggs the night before, so I scramble those up and heat up the leftover taco meat and refried beans to make tacos. Now, a good breakfast taco really needs potatoes, but all of our potatoes were more or less reserved for dinner Saturday night, so we go with what we have. Which includes some store-bought pico de gallo.

That said, the tacos are a great starting point for the day, and we also have some leftover donuts delivered by Kelling's wife. Then it's a pair of trips back over to the venue for rounds three and four.

Max's list is a Carnival of Excess list running Fulgrim, which is interesting. My goal is to generally stay the hell away from it and let him come to me a few models at a time, taking down bigger targets as they make their way onto the table. Shalaxi is a problem, but not a massive one in the way Fulgrim is.

This game had one of my favorite moments of the weekend: In an effort to get an early Assassination kill and lock me in my deployment zone, Max sends a unit of Infractors and a Lord Exultant across the table to try and kill my Tzaangor Shaman and one of my Rubric units. I throw out some limited Overwatch at them, killing 2 models. Then he closes the gap on a big charge, and I drop the combat grenade, killing two more models and leaving just one Infractor and the Lord. The Lord activates his once-per-game "best day ever" mode and drops six 3-damage precision wounds on my Infernal Master, who rolls four 5+ saves. Then I flip one of the two fails to a 6 to survive on 1 wound. Meanwhile the Infractor doesn't kill the Shaman. They turn around and kill both models, giving me a solid start on the game.

This one goes really well early for me - Max feeds me all of his Infractors, Flawless Blades, and Noise Marines without getting much in return - and then turns disastrously late when I set up wrong for my Rituals on Fulgrim and fail the double move and re-roll casts, leaving me unable to dump shots into him until he's dead. That ends up being a brutal turn as I'm staring down Shalaxi and Fulgrim at the same time at the top of the board. I failed half my casts again the following turn, and while I was able to kill Fulgrim, the damage was done. Max draws a stellar final turn with Forward Position and Assassination and scores 15 on Secondaries and while I have second turn advantage and pull 15 of my own, I needed to stop him from being able to just trivially pick up units for Overwhelming Force and Assassination.

The game ends up being an 82-84 loss for me and a 10-10 draw but it definitely could have been a 12 if I'd been able to close out Fulgrim earlier with a double move. Our team would end up winning the round, 85-75, giving us our first round win of the event and killing our dreams of 0-0-6. Max was a treat to play and so was the rest of Tabletop Hawaii.

Lunch was more manageable on day 2, and I ended up getting a burrito from the place I'd had to cancel with the day before. It was aggressively mid, rolled in that way where all the meant is on one side of the thing and I started with the side that was apparently all cilantro and sour cream. Gross.

This game was a little awkward at first but picked up quickly. Jerred was a fun opponent and we had a solid game. The problem with Votann is that they just generally cost too much for what you get, and so most games go the same way: I kill things reliably until turn 3, then ask my opponent where the rest of his army is, at which point he'll say "this is it" and I get to be like "damn bitch, you live like this?"

That said, going second into Priority Assets with Purge is never easy. My goal here is to control both center objectives all games with Terminators and avoid taking too much damage from the 10man Hearthguard unit. Jerred screams forward at the start of the game with his Kapricus Carrier, bikes, and Saggitaurs, and I promptly take them all off the board and go to work killing his Yaegirs and Warriors - they're the key here. Votann don't have a lot of units, so what you want to do is force him to have to do actions with the Thunderkyn or the Steeljacks, neither of which want to be doing them and give up a ton of shooting value.

I play aggressively with the Mutalith early and focus on controlling board with my shooting threats for a turn. Jerred gets a single marker on the top center objective but I'm able to hold it for much of the game - he leaves me room to drop Terminators on the middle and I never look back.

Jerred's Hearthguard would ultimately end up dropping on the left side of the table outside of my deployment zone, where they'd clear my home point but be unable to do much else. My Terminators take the middle and spend a few turns getting whittled down by Thunderkyn and Steeljacks while firing back as the other unit wheels around and captures Jerred's expansion. I kept Jerred off primary scoring in rounds 3 and 4 almost completely but he still managed to outscore me on secondary objectives, in part because he drew Secure/Assassination on turn 1 and Cleanse round 2 before finishing round 5 with Bring it Down and No Prisoners.

I'd end up winning this one 12-8 and securing a W for the team. It wasn't super close, but the mission makes it hard to pull away. What also helped me was Jerred not just aggressively castling on one objective; he didn't realize you can keep dropping priority markers on the same objective, which helped me.

We tied the round again, 84-76, and finished the day 1-0-3, still undefeated. I'm honestly OK with these results - we haven't been on the losing end of a draw yet and we've only been a couple points away from wins. We keep getting paired down and that helps us.

Anyways none of this matters because IT IS STEAK NIGHT.

Steak Night

One of the things I try to do at every one of these gatherings is do a night with some steaks. The first day at the grocery store I stopped by their butcher's counter and picked out eight of their best ribeyes and had them wrapped up. I'd prefer filets but they didn't have enough of those. The plan here is almost always "steak and potatoes, plus vegetables." This year I added some mac and cheese, broccoli, and asparagus, and Boon had picked up some naan bread so we heated that up too.

Grilling isn't the ideal way to make steaks, but it's not bad. I do a little salt and Montreal steak seasoning on them, and I've prepared a rosemary garlic butter to finish them off - mince garlic and rosemary, mix into softened butter. I put rosemary on the steaks and cook them about 7 minutes each side while the asparagus also sits on the grill, brushed with salt, pepper, and olive oil. The Broccoli was tossed in olive oil and Montreal steak seasoning, then roasted at 425 for about 20 minutes, and the potatoes were boiled with a clove of crushed garlic, then drained, mashed, and combined with butter and heavy whipping cream.

The steaks were left to rest for about 5 minutes, during which time they got a dollop of garlic rosemary butter. In all it was an impressive spread, and we ended up with like eight pounds of mashed potatoes - I could have definitely used on for breakfast tacos, in retrospect. Overall another successful meal - the steaks turned out beautifully and there was plenty to eat even for Chase. My take is that it's very important to have vegetables at these things - you need to stay healthy and not just eat pizza all weekend.

Sunday: The Struggle to Not Lose

We wake up Sunday with two games to go. Saturday night was probably our best post-dinner hangout, with most everyone engaged and talking about games and pairings before we hit the back deck and spent the rest of the evening debating politics and talking about how trains are rad while looking out over the valley and watching planes land. I think I crashed around midnight again.

Sunday morning was another two-trip morning and this time our first game was upstairs in the bleachers. This meant having a lot of space but also music in the background, which wasn't as bad as it sounds - it was basically top radio hits from the late 90s, which was catnip to my opponent and me. We spent half the game softly singing Sugar Ray and RHCP songs.

I beat AJ en route to the top cut, then lost to Rich playing Necrons on Purge the Foe. This was more or less an exact replay of that, even using the same layout. The big difference? Both of our armies were worse. Rich had one extra C'tan and a unit of fast-moving Lychguard; the Lychguard could catch me by surprise and taking down two C'tan is a tall order. This time around I had an easier go of it.

I'm going first in this mission and that sucks - you want to go second here, even with the changes to the "kill more" part of the Purge mirror primary. That first turn going first is often dead for kills, and that would prove to be true here as I tried to play cagey on the first turn.

I tend to play too aggressively most of the time, so a lot of what I'm trying to do in these games is slow down, score where I can, and keep games close. My primary goal was to take the lokhust destroyers out of the equation and avoid the Scarabs. I did the former and failed at the latter. I made a big mistake turn 1 on my end, losing a key model to a failed Hazardous check which cost me the ability to score Centre Ground.

Despite being dumb enough to engage the Scarabs, I did manage to tie them up in the lower corner for the entire game with a unit of Rubrics, also taking out a unit of Lokhusts, though I suspect this was a bad trade on my part - that Wraiths unit costs about 200 points less than my Terminators. I eventually managed to dig out a bit more with the second unit of Terminators, but that was throwing good points after bad - I needed to use them more at midtable.

I was eventually able to take out the Void Dragon with a mix of shooting from the Mutalith and Rubrics, and some clever use of the Chaos Spawn I made from my Terminator Sorcerer and the Rubrics let me drop in and score Plunder and Behind Enemy Lines on the game's final turn to pull ahead, but Jeremy was going second and had a trivial time catching up on the final turn. Jeremy was a good opponent, and played a solid game.

I'd end up losing 81-88, a 9-11 loss. Not too bad, and keeping it close was my goal here. Our team would end up winning 94-66, thanks to big wins from Campbell, Wallace, and Goatboy. Campbell had to play Custodes three straight rounds and won two of those games big. He was a huge value add this year. That put us at 2-0-3 and in a pretty good spot - we'd absolutely improved on last year's 2-2-2 result.

Lunch was even easier today and I grabbed a gyro bowl from a food truck which was largely fine. It was filling, and that was what I needed at the time. Then we met up for our final round, which was absolutely fated to happen.

Round 6: vs. Sanctum (Astra Militarum)

LET'S FUCKING GOOOOOOOOO

I know a lot of people who rankle at the prospect of traveling to an event to play their friends. These people are wrong and dumb; I love playing against my friends, and I do it all the time.

We initially offered Sanctum a draw - let's just call it, play some games for funsies, and go home. A podium finish was possible for both teams but extremely unlikely. We were happy to merely go undefeated. But I could see the gears turning behind the more competitive Sanctum faces: They wanted to win, and thought we were beatable. So they declined our offer and we paired up.

Round 6 vs. Andrew Corban

And in a prodigious development, I got paired into Andrew Corban! Andrew and I played a practice game on the prior Monday (Labor Day), and so I was familiar with his list. He's not a blowout win guy on the team, but neither am I. This is the most chill game of Warhammer I've ever played at an event. Just hanging out, casual takebacks, nice trash talk, jokes, beers. Great times. Andrew is a 10/10 person.

Andrew's list has lots of medium sized tanks but he's on Recon so my goal is to Consecrate early, slow him by a turn, and kill his action units. That's hard, because killing Gaunt's Ghosts is really difficult and they can just jump around on the table. The plan here is to just score early and often and try to squeeze out 11 or 12 points in a win. I'm going second, which is a big help for scoring.

Andrew has a few scout moves, but spends his first turn playing cagey. As a result, I don't get to kill anything - my shooting up top goes disastrously - but I whittle a few things down during the turn I can capitalize on and I get needlessly aggressive with a turn 1 advance and charge from the Mutalith, which does nothing except end up giving me a dead Mutalith the following turn. Not my strongest strategic play, to be honest.

I picked up a couple of units on the following turn for a triple consecrate. That was a big deal, and this was all powered by a successful 9" charge out of deep strike with one of my Scarab units, which more or less sealed this game as a win for me.

Those Scarabs spent the next three turns getting killed by Leman Russes but managed to hold on long enough to be annoying, while the other Scarab brick dropped midtable and got to operate more or less unmolested for the rest of the game. Andrew and I had some fun back-and-forth while his final Kasrkin unit stayed locked in combat with my Forgefiend, refusing to die. This was bad for Andrew, however - he wanted to use the Reinforcements Stratagem to get the unit back, and so eventually had to do so by overcharging a plasma pistol and letting his model die from a failed Hazardous roll.

I'd end up pulling the Tzaangors across the table late to score Plunder and Outflank, but this left my home open for Gaunt's Ghosts to triangulate a third time. They were Andrew's beacon unit, and I tried taking them out with a Tzaangor Shaman + my Forgefiend, but wasn't able to make it happen - Gaunt re-rolled his final invulnerable save and stayed alive on 2 wounds, helping Andrew close the gap. I'd catch up on my final turn, but that essentially kept it from being a 14-6. I'd win 13-7, more than I expected after Monday's practice game and very solid.

Either way, it was a fun game. Andrew fuckin' rules. One of my favorite people.

And after all that, we tied with Sanctum anyways - Kelling, Wallace, and Shane scored big wins, Goatboy kept his game to a tie, and Campbell and Craig managed to score a few points to avoid a 20-0 loss. Chase took a big 20-0 loss here from Conor but that was honestly fine; Conor was playing the busted Dark Angels speeder build and Chase had our weakest Disruption list. Putting Conor into Chase was honestly a pairings mistake for Sanctum; we were already set to eat a big loss for Chase. This was our only points loss of the event - 79-18 - but still a tie by event standards.

UNDEFEATED, BABY!!!

Finishing 2-0-4 is very respectable and we ended in 14th place. That's very, very good for a team with no practice time or ability to coordinate in person and a cobbled-together set of lists. We're all stoked about this result and heading home as champions. Mrs. Kelling is on hand to help us do a single ride back, which is a massive upgrade.

Our final night is pasta night. I take a pound of ground pork and a pound of ground beef (season with salt, pepper, and Italian seasoning), and brown them in a pain before draining. We'd accidentally used all the garlic the night before on the mashed potatoes, but I still had a healthy ball of garlic rosemary butter and so I cooked up some small diced celery and carrots in that before adding the browned meat back in with a can of tomato paste. Let that cook a bit, then add water and once it cooks a bit, some heavy cream. It's not amazing as a bolognese - it needs another two hours to simmer with some wine - but the taste is absolutely there and it goes great on both the cheese tortellini I've bought and Campbell's lentil pasta (gluten free). We combine it with a Caesar salad and croutons and parmesan cheese for an incredibly solid meal. Chase got regular marinara sauce.

Chase's hair was going crazy

We drank and talked and recapped another successful teams weekend, satisfied with our results. We largely brought a bunch of off-meta or suboptimal lists and through sheer force of will managed an undefeated result. Chase had the worst go of it, losing every single game over the weekend with his Disruption Emperor's Children. I don't think it was a good list but when you chase your off-meta dreams, sometimes you fly too close to the sun and get burned.

The final night is always bittersweet, especially when people leave early. Kelling and his wife skipped dinner to go do vacation stuff. Craig, Campbell, Wallace, and Shane bounced early to catch late flights back home. Which left me, Chase, and Goatboy to clean up and lock down the house before leaving on Monday morning. The good news is I slept better that night and the next morning we were ready to go by check-out at 11am. We made the trip to the airport together, got some breakfast, and said our goodbyes.

Final Thoughts

Phew! What a great time - that was a lot to write about. Next week's update is bound to be pretty light - I don't think I'll have time to get a game in - but I'm going to try and get some hobby progress in the mix there. I had a blast at Challengers Cup this year - almost enough fun to want to do it again next year. I'm on the fence, mostly because the logistics behind 8-man teams events makes them a serious pain in the ass, even when the event is loads of fun. I know I'm definitely doing LVO Teams in February next year. After that, I'm less certain.

See you Thursday.
