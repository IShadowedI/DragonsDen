StarCraft Tabletop Miniatures Game Terran Faction Focus

June 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Terran are your human representation in the StarCraft universe. Unlike other sci-fi properties with Marines in power armor, these are not paragons of humanity. They’re dirtbag losers shoved in power armor twice their size and given a C-14 rifle. Today we’ll be going over how these idiots play on the table and how best to maximize these guys on the table.

How They Play

Coming from other tabletop games, you may expect the human faction to be all rounders who can do a little of everything. In StarCraft though, Terran are ranged specialists. They have exactly 0 melee units but they also have the only core units with a proper long range shooting attack that can handle both ground and air (although at the time of writing this, air isn't really in the game yet). A lot of your games are gonna be focused around playing keep away from your opponent and pelting them with punishing ranged attacks the entire way.

Cards

Faction Cards

You have two faction cards to work with right now. Terran Armed Forces is your vanilla pick, giving you three Core slots, an Elite slot and a Support Slot. You have two abilities you can exhaust: Tactical Retreat, which lets you ignore the Disengage Penalty for a round with a unit, and Terran Tenacity, which lets you immediately claim the first turn marker for the next phase. Tactical Retreat is a nice bread and butter option since Terran does NOT want to be stuck in melee but the other ability will be the one that makes or breaks games. You’ll want to set up a go turn where you’ll commit a bunch of units, likely in the movement phase, and claim the token to make sure you can set the tempo of shooting.

Your other option is Raynor's Raiders, Jimmy’s personal subfaction. You get a Hero slot (for Jimmy), three Core slots and a support slot. The two abilities here are Rapid Ingress, which summons a Point Defense Drone outside an inch of enemy models. The Point Defense drone is technically a Unit but we’re gonna talk about it here since it's really just an ability. It’s got a 6+ armor and 6+ evade with just 3 HP and no weapons but you’re taking this thing for its Point Defense Laser. If a friendly unit within 4” gets attacked by a ranged attack without INSTANT, you can remove 2 dice from the pool. Since you can windmill slam this thing every turn, there’s no reason not to throw this on the unit you expect to be catching the most fire this turn.

The real juice on this card though is Ready For Pickup? which lets you once per game resolve the PLACE (12) effect with the only stipulation being you can’t set up in combat. This is an amazing ability. While it precludes you from performing a standard action, this can let you shoot a unit where they need to be and when combined with Jimmy’s Orders ability, you can get pretty much anything where it needs to be.

Of the two, Raynor's Raiders offers a bit more flexibility. While you miss out on the once per game token stealing, Jim can have models Disengage without penalty so you can find that effect elsewhere. Also taking Raynor’s Raiders gives you access to the titular Raynor's Raiders Marine unit, which may be the all star of the roster (more on them below).

Other Cards

Terran Medics by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Academy (35 Gas) - Gives you two support slots and +1CP on exhaust and has the Advanced Training ability which, once per round, lets you reduce the cost of a support unit’s ability by 1 without exhausting this card. This effectively lets one of your Medics heal for free. While the algebra on whether Medics are worth taking when you could just take more dudes is a bit closer than we’d like to justify them, this definitely helps.

Armory (30 Gas) - Gives you only 1 Elite slot and a CP when exhausted but you get two abilities for your Mechanical units. In any phase you can use Vehicle Plating which gives a unit TOUGH (1) for a single roll, this can be nice with heavy armor piercing shots going into your Goliath. Alternatively in the Assault phase, you can use Vehicle Weapons which gives the first Ranged Weapon a Mechanical unit fires CRITICAL HIT (1). This is a nice to have but currently it only goes on the Goliath who only really has 1 damage weapons which makes this only kind of ok.

Barracks (25 Gas) - This just gives you the one core slot and a single ability but its a good one. Go! Go! Go! gives you the ability to move a biological unit 2”. This may not sound like much, but this ability wins games allowing you to move 2”, then set up from that leading model. In addition this can happen after you move, so you can stim, move, Go! Go! Go!, remove the token with Jimmy, and do it again. There's a lot to gain out of this one.

Barracks (Proxy) (40 Gas) - This is like the Barracks but more. You get an extra CP from exhausting it, get another Core slot, and an extra ability. Armed and Ready gives you the ability to deploy from any edge as an entry edge 10” away from enemy models. This can’t be used before deploying anything else in the round, but this is so good. Every faction has access to similar abilities to this, but with a primarily shooting army you can take advantage of it more than most. We love this card. Don’t leave home without it.

Barracks (Tech Lab) (45 Gas) - This is yet another alternate Barracks, giving you an extra Elite slot and also exhausting for an extra CP. You also get a new ability Let’s Have a Blast! which gives a unit’s first ranged weapon Anti-Evade (1); this can be neat but you also have access to slugthrowers for Marines and Marauders generally don’t want to be in combat so its usefulness is a bit limited with the current selection of bio units.

Dropship (40 Gas) - The dropship is a Unique card that gives you a Core and support slot. The real draw is its two abilities though. Strap In! simply gives you the ability to take an unengaged ground unit (which is all units right now) and put it in reserves. Combo this with something like Raynor’s Raiders for some cool interactions. You also get Ready for Dust-off which lets you put an indicator down 10” away from enemy units and at the end of the round you can deploy a unit in base to base contact with it. The only way your opponent can really stop this is by standing on the indicator so there’s a lot of cool stuff you can do by putting a Goliath down with this ability.

Engineering Bay (25 Gas) - This gives you a single Core slot and two options for single use unit upgrades. You can either use Infantry Armor which gives a biological unit TOUGH (1) which combos nicely with your Point-Defense Drone. Meanwhile, Infantry Weapons gives you CRITICAL HIT (1), nice if you’re going into a Hero or just need some extra oomph pushed through with Marauders.

Factory (35 Gas) - This gives you 2 Elite slots and the ability Field Repair, which simply lets you Heal (2) a mechanical unit in the movement phase. This is basically your easy button if you wanna slot in a Goliath but the heal is plenty nice.

Orbital Command (25 Gas) - This only gives you a single core slot but gives you two powerful control abilities. Comsat Station lets you select an edge that’s not an entry edge and prevents units from coming from that edge. This is nice anti Barracks (Proxy) tech, and considering every faction has access to it, it's a nice ability to have in your back pocket. The challenge is knowing the right time to use it since it means you have to activate before your opponent and since those abilities are generally the first deployment you have to do in a turn, it can be hard to find a good time to use this. Scanner Sweep is the main reason you take this, and the reason it hasn’t left any of my lists. It lets you throw down a Faction Indicator and anything near that indicator loses hidden. Considering Protoss (stalkers) and Zerg (everything) have access to hidden, this is a necessary counter to have in your back pocket.

Supply Depot (40 Gas) - This gives you a Hero, Elite, and Core Slot which is a nice splash off everything and a single ability. Additional Supply Depot lets you treat a unit’s Supply Value as improved by 1 for contesting objectives. This means a single Raynor’s Raider can be treated as Supply 2 when bullying an objective, which can be extremely annoying for your opponent.

Units

Heroes

This is Jimmy - Jim Raynor by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Jim Raynor (250 Minerals) - This is Jimmy! Hero of Mar Sara and wanted criminal of the Terran Dominion. In game he acts as a support piece for the rest of your biological units. He gets the general Commander ability all the Heroes in the game currently have, allowing him to count as +1 supply for most situations. For your nearby friends though, we have Freedom Fighters, which makes it so nearby units’ supply can’t be reduced below 0 for contesting markers and doing objectives. The real meat comes from his ability Orders which is repeatable and lets you select a unit within 8” and choose one of three modes. You can give the unit either Critical Hit(2) for 1CP, let a unit ignore the Disengage Penalty for 1CP, or for 2CP you can remove an activation marker from that Unit. All of these abilities are great, but that last one is the one that’ll let you bust the game wide open so long as you have the CP to do it. Need to get a unit off the table before you lose supply? Disengage, activate Jimmy, order the marker off, and dropship them out of there. Need to get places fast? Stimpack, move, Go! Go! Go! with a Barracks and then order them to get the marker off and do it again. So long as you have the CP you can do a lot of stuff. 

Combat wise, he has a C-14 rifle like the Marines do, but it shoots almost as much as an entire Marine squad if you can get close enough to proc Burst Fire. It also has Surge D3+1 for light which is nice. He can swap that out for the Commando Rifle which is a 3 shot PIERCE Armoured 3 gun if you think tanks are a bigger issue. While his C-14 is good, having a nice long range weapon is really solid to have in the roster. In addition to either of these, we have the “Justice” Revolver, a 2 shot gun with Anti-Evade (2), Sidearm, and Pinpoint that’s 2 damage, allowing you to get guys out of nasty situations if you need to eek out some extra damage. Overall Jimmy is really expensive, but well worth his weight in minerals. Making a list without him is a bit of a tall ask but can absolutely be done.

Supports

Medics (110 Minerals) - Medics are your one and only support choice and focus mainly around keeping your Biological units in the game. Their key ability is Life Support which lets you, for a CP, reduce the total damage of an attack by the number of Medics in the squad. You can only ever have three Medics in a squad but you can buff the ability with the Stabilizer Medpacks upgrade for 30 Minerals to have the ability treat it as if there’s one more model in the unit. This is neat, especially since it lets you use Stimpacks without having to worry about the damage, but at 1CP and a pretty hefty mineral cost you need a good reason not to just take more bodies over these.

They have another ability, Optical Flare, which reduces the range of ranged weapons by 4” for a CP. This is neat, but generally the threats you’re up against are going to be melee threats so this isn’t at the top of the list of things to use unless you’re playing other Terrans. This can be further upgraded with A-13 Flash Grenade Launcher which increases the range of the ability from 12” to 16” for 20 Minerals, not really worth it to double down on this ability. 

The last upgrade is the most interesting. Advanced Medical Facilities costs a whopping 60 points, more than half of the cost of the base unit, and makes it so its supply value counts as 0 when calculating your supply pool. This is neat, but StarCraft is ultimately an activation focused game, where you have to carefully think through when you want to commit a bunch of units or keep things in your pocket. Adding an extra deployment, even if it's not eating your supply limit, is somewhat a tall ask. If you’re going to make this unit cost an extra 60 points, a unit that only has bad combat attacks to use, you should really consider just another Marine unit.

Core

StarCraft Terran Marines by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Marines (160 Minerals) - Marines are the bread and butter unit for the faction that’ll make up the backbone of your lists (either the vanilla kind or their variants). Each Marine is a standard 4” move, 5+ save and evade, 2 wound body that comes with a C-14 Rifle that can hit all Targets at 12” range with 2 shots that hit on 3s. Each of these shots are only damage 1 but that kind of flexibility can’t be found in any other core unit in the game. They also come with the ability Stimpack which gives the unit 2 Non-Lethal Damage, basically giving them some damage that won’t kick in until they get hit. This gives you a whopping 3 extra inches of movement and PRECISION (3) to help extra shots go through. This costs a CP and isn’t a windmill slam, but you should seriously consider Stimpacking when you activate your units to get some extra oomph out of them.

For upgrades, there’s the Combat Shield which gives you an evade roll for close combat attacks and enemy special abilities. This is great but it also costs a CP, and it's an ability you need to use in the movement phase making it a bit harder to time. The Slugthrower meanwhile gives your C-14 rifles ANIT-EVADE (1) within 8”, making shooting into combat slightly less painful. Also within 8” is Grenades - Frag which changes the surge dice for your C-14 rifles to a D6. These two are your baseline buys for the Marine squad to make them proper killing machines. Lastly there’s Bayonets which gives each model an extra melee attack. These melee attacks hit on 5s though and start at just one attack, so I wouldn’t bank on this. That said, it does combo well with the Stimpack ability if you wanna go that route.

There are also two weapons you can buy. The AGG-12 replaces one of your C-14s and acts as a 2 shot anti armor weapon, which at damage 1 isn’t super impactful. More interesting though is the Rocket Launcher. This costs 40 Minerals but gives you 4 shots of Indirect Fire, 18” Long Range, shots that can’t surge but are Sidearm, so you get it in addition to your C-14.

For our money, the general pick for a Marine squad is all C-14s with Slugthrowers and Grenades and maybe a Rocket Launcher. The other upgrades are all good, but a bit more situational and it may be better to just have your dudes just kill light models well.

Raynor’s Raiders (230 Minerals) - This is the special unit you get if you’re playing the Raynor’s Raiders subfaction. At base they’re just a unit of Marines that can’t have their Supply dropped below 1 until they die and have Slugthrowers, Grenades, and Bayonets baked in. Where they really shine tough are their Movement abilities. When you deploy you can use Raiders Roll! which lets you use a Stimpack while deploying for CP which is great for setting up alpha strikes or getting where they need to be fast. You can alternatively deploy using Rapid Reinforcements which lets you PLACE (10) from another unit so long as the unit stays outside 8” of enemy units. This immediately ends your activation so you can’t stack too many shenanigans with this unless you use Jim’s Orders ability, but this can wreak absolute havoc with your opponent. There’s an old adage that Movement is what wins wargames, and with these guys being able to teleport around the map they can win entire games on their own. It's worth noting as well that while other specialist units are usually unique, these guys are not, meaning you can take as many units of these as you’d like.

Marauder (150 Minerals) - Marauders accomplish two roles, anti armor shooting and pretty tough objective sitters. They have standard movement, with a 4+ save and 5 HP so they stick around for quite a while even with just 2 models. You can also reinforce the unit to double the size for 280 minerals which can be nice especially with the upgrades they can take. Offensively they have Quad K12s which are 3 attacks each that hit on 3’s that surge on Armoured units and have PIERCE ARMORED (2). This can let them tear down armored targets, especially since they generally don’t have too many wounds.

Ability wise you have Stimpacks which do what they do for Marines, which is always a nice ability to have and the 2 nonlethal damage is much less debilitating. They also have Concussive Shells which you can use in the Assault Phase to reduce a charge by 2”. This costs a CP but can make the difference between key units being tied up and you controlling the table.

For upgrades you have Laser Targeting Systems which gives your weapons 16” Long Range, although with the number of shots you’re throwing out the -1 to hit is kind of harmful. The other two upgrades are focused around making this unit one of the best objective sitters in the game. Veteran of Tarsonis which increases their armor to 3+ if they’re on an objective and Kinetic Foam which increases their wounds by 1 makes them very difficult to shift and well worth considering if you want something to sit and not die.

Elite

Goliath Online Credit: Mildnorman

Goliath (190 Minerals) - The Goliath is an interesting little unit. As a big walker mech it kind of does what you’d expect. It's a single model with a 7” move, no evade but a 4+ armor and 10 HP. It's also Size 3 which lets it see over a lot of terrain. It comes with three guns. The Autocannon has 9 shots that hit on 4s and doesn’t surge but has a long range of 18” (over its base 12”). This can be boosted though with the Target Lock ability which designates a unit and all Goliaths in your army get Surge of both Light and Armored at D3+1 which turns that gun into a generalist good pick. You also have the Underbelly Machine Gun, which is only 8” range but has 6 attacks that hit on 3’s and surges on Light for D3 with Pinpoint and Sidearm. This means this can be a great ability for clearing hordes out of the way for engaged Marines that are screening for your big guy. These two guns can also be upgraded with the Ares-Class Targeting System which gives them both Precision 1, which is a pretty nice upgrade if you have extra points. Lastly there’s the Hellfire Missiles which have the same profile as the machine gun but are a bit of an odd duck right now since they only Target Flying units and none of those are in the game right now. This means you’ll need to purchase one of the upgrades that trade out the missiles for the Haywire Missiles which have just three shots but surge armoured for D3 and pierce for 3, making for a brutal anti armored package, or the Scatter Missiles which are 6 attacks at 18” that hit on 5’s but have Indirect Fire, gain an extra 6 shots if you stand still, and can shoot at 24” at -1 to hit. These last ones are also anti Light, and generally when I build my Goliath I take these to make it an anti-light monster. All

It’s interesting in melee as well. It doesn’t get many attacks, with just 4 that hit on 5s with no surge, but it does 4 3+ impact hits on the charge and can shoot (and be shot) while in combat thanks to Indomitable. This doesn’t mean you want to go around shoving your robots into melee threats, but it makes it not terrible for tying something up with a Supply of 2, and it doesn’t mind being charged nearly as much as the rest of your army.

Combos

Move Move Move

This is less of a combo and more general advice for Terran. Your dudes don’t want to be in combat almost ever. While you can shoot, your opponent gets evade rolls and you won’t get nearly as much tactical flexibility. Thankfully you have a lot of movement tricks available to you to help you stay out of combat and play keep away. That said, your guys also want to get close-ish for their attacks to be fully optimal. Frag Grenades and Slugthrowers on your marines in particular mean they want to skate around 7” away at any given time to keep your damage high.

You can also use your movement tricks offensively. When you need a unit to be on a specific objective, Raynor’s Raiders can usually get there one way or another. Their built in abilities do a lot to help with Rapid Reinforcements specifically letting you get to most places on the table. If your opponent has you screened out though there’s a lot you can do. For one, you can use your once per game Ready for Pickup? to put anything pretty much anywhere. For a lighter version of that rule though, you can use Rapid Reinforcements along with Jim Raynor’s Orders ability to then move them again closer to where they need to be. Don’t need to be there anymore? Use the Dropship next turn to put the Raiders back in reserve and teleport them somewhere else next turn.

Big Guys on Big Objectives

This one’s pretty straight forward, but Marauders with Veterans of Tarsonis and Kinetic Foam are some of the best objective sitters in the game. A reinforced unit with these upgrades will cost a pretty penny, but 24 wounds on a 3+ that needs anti armor to shift means these guys can sit and plant for quite a while.

Example List

Boom, baby! - Starcraft Terran Marauder by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

2000 Minerals 200 Gas

Faction Card

Raynor’s Raiders

Vespene Gas Cards
  • Barracks (Proxy)
  • Engineering Bay
  • Supply Depot
  • Dropship
  • Armory
  • Orbital Command
Units
  • Jim Raynor w/ Commando Rifle
  • 2x Marines w/ Rocket Launcher, Slugthrowers, and Grenades
  • 3x Raynor’s Raiders
  • 2x Marauders w/ Veteran of Tarsonis and Kinetic Foam
  • 1x Goliath w/ Ares-Class Targeting System and Scatter Missiles

How It Plays

This list mostly leverages your movement tricks to play keep away with your opponent, shooting them the whole time through. Where this list is somewhat weak, is the fact that your only anti armor is Jim and the Marauders, but the Goliath can flex to it with Target Lock and there’s not a ton of it in the game right now. That said, once you master the movement systems available to you, this list can be anywhere at any time and throw an absurd amount of dice around to cleanup enemy models.

Jacked Up and Good to Go

That about covers it for Terran. They’re a hyper mobile shooting force that plays keep away with their opponent to get results. Overall, they’re a pretty high skill ceiling faction that’ll reward understanding ranges and activation priority. There’s a lot of room for error here, and you’ll want to develop a good understanding of what your opponent can do to get to you, but once you get that down you can play the objective game with the best of them.
