Tabletop Battles Reviews: Magnagothica Maleghast Games for Freaks Volumes 1-4

August 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer
A few years ago, I covered a very cool indie skirmish game called Magnagothica: Maleghast for the site. It’s the brainchild of Tom Bloom, of Lancer and Kill Six Billion Demons fame, and was written in a frenzy over a few short months back in 2003. Bloom’s knack for drawing, in my own words from the first article, “the coolest shit I’ve seen in my life” with impressive levels of detail is what initially caught my eye about Maleghast, especially since the game’s style is a love letter to extreme metal, a genre of music whose riffs and visual language is very near and dear to my heart.

In my initial review, I was very positive about Maleghast. It's a very affordable game, with the rules only costing $6.66 on itch.io (hell yeah), it's absolutely beautiful to look at, and the game is fun to boot. Each of the teams included in the rulebook felt very distinct from one another in how they wanted to play, and presented a surprising amount of depth in terms of how you would ideally build your crew and combo potentials you can lean on. Its initial offering of scenarios was pretty limited, but aside from that I was very happy with Maleghast, and would happily recommend it to anyone interested.

I’d honestly expected Maleghast to be a self contained little game that Bloom would design, wrap up in a bow, and walk away from with a smile. As it turns out, I was wrong, and Bloom has written not one, not two, but four expansions on Maleghast since its release (alongside balance updates) with his Games for Freaks zine. With the recent Kickstarter from Taiga Miniatures bringing extra attention onto the tactical Necromancer warfare of Maleghast, it seems like the perfect time to check in on the most heavy metal skirmish game on the internet, and review what each of the Games for Freaks issues bring to the table.

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

Games for Freaks, Vol.1  

The first update for Maleghast brings us a brand new House with which to terrorize the crypts of Anzenmezzeron, the Steeplewracks. Worshipers of mystical flesh-eating owls, the Steeplewracks are feral in appearance and in gameplay. The team’s main gameplan is to overwhelm their opponents quickly and tear them apart with strength of numbers. While individual Steeplewrack monsters and necromancers may be weak, when they descend upon their prey together they can be one devastating. 

Their main mechanics are Dive Bomb and Rip Apart. Dive Bomb allows for one Steeplewrack unit to take a free move at the end of each turn, which allows for you to quickly reposition a model on the board or to come screaming in from downtown unexpectedly. Rip Apart makes Steeplewrack units more effective the higher number of allied units or walls are adjacent to the target, meaning if you have an enemy surrounded they’re going to get, well, ripped apart.

The unit roster is filled with a pretty unsettling cast of characters who are particularly good at either getting in your face really fast, like the Harpy, or overwhelming you with sheer numbers, like the rapidly reproducing Flock. The Carniphagous Owl, which is the big Tyrant for the team, can even make a dynamic deep strike-esque entry onto the battlefield at the start of round 3, which lets you place a gigantic problem right in your opponent's lap. Finally, the Necromancer leading the Steeplewracks, the Haruspex, can push the pressure up even higher with abilities that can spawn more flock thralls, teleport around the battlefield, or grant nearby models cover against ranged attacks with immunity to grazes. Plus, the Haruspex also counts extra for Rip Apart for adjacent allies, which is brutal. 

Out of all the Houses added so far to Maleghast, Steeplewracks are the most unsettling to look at if you ask me, which is only appropriate given how they fight: they are predators from on high, and you are prey. 

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

Games for Freaks, Vol. 2

Games for Freaks, Vol.2 is even bigger and better than the first, which is only fitting for a sequel. Inside it’s pages is a little bit for everyone, with each team receiving a new unit, a new addition to games of Maleghast in the form of Merchants, and a whole new house focused on parasitic corpse worms who love to evolve, the Hellminth.

Lets start off with the new units. Wisely, these new units don’t slot in to the missing role spot on each house roster (like thralls, scions, hunters, etc.) but instead duplicate an existing role, which further accentuates each team’s identity while also adding interesting options for building your force. A few of the standout options include the Gearhead for Goregrinders, who can hand out buffs to nearby units as well as set a friendly units HP to 1, which is handy for a unit with Blood Rage to bring them back from 0 HP, the Deadsouls Lithogheist, which teleports between walls can can pull units in or weaken them, and the Abhorrers Abbot, which can use an unholy bible to grant allies Vitality by hitting enemies with their book. 

There's also the Steeplewracks Greatstork, which continues the trend of that house having really upsetting units to look at. I don’t like looking at it. I really don’t. Why does it have teeth, man. 

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

The next addition, Merchants, introduces a new step to the setup phase of Maleghast, and gives your crew some extra customization options before the fight starts. If playing with merchants, prior to the start of the game, you can randomly select one of ten different merchants for both players to spend up to three pieces of black gold at. Each shopkeep has a distinct flavor of wares, and some even have special abilities that are persistent throughout the game. The Shady Man, for example, is basically the merchant from Resident Evil 4 and lets your team outfit themselves with some extra firepower like shotguns, pistols, and even a rocket launcher. The Last Bodega, meanwhile, ensures you have the opportunity for your Black Mass to stock up on snacks before the battle, which can be used on adjacent allies for healing and special effects. Prigenbog, the Duke of Hell, has a vast collection of metal records to pick from that affect every model on the board when played. Plus, he asks a very important question in his dialogue flavor text: “YOU INTO DOOM OR WHAT?” 

Finally, the Hellminth are gigantic parasitic worms that burrow into their victims spine and begin a symbiotic evolution into terrifying and alien creatures of hell. Their playstyle focuses on constant evolution to adapt and grow in power depending on what the situation calls for through the Evolve rule. The Hellminth Knight, for example, can evolve to either gain extra armor and defense with Carapace Shield and Royal Guard, or become a murder tornado with Thirsting Blade and Executioner. Hellminth also can consume nearby corpse tokens to gain vitality tokens for even more toughness with the Necrophage mechanic.

The Hellminth Necromancer, the Nephilim, is unique among all the other Necromancer options from the other houses as its the only Necromancer that takes up a 2x2 space on the board, like a Tyrant model, and it is a standout model in terms of deadliness. Its evolve ability, Blood of the Worm Gods, lets the Nephilim gain +1 current and max HP and +1 soul every time it’s used, which makes the Nephilim extraordinarily hard to take down and extra scary with its increased access to powerful SOUL abilities. The Nephilim upgrade path is also befittingly mean, including a trait to hand out even more HP and SOul when the Nephilim evolves, the ability to grant one of your units wings for the rest of the game, or an attack that obliterates an enemy unit and replaces them with a friendly Page thrall should it manage to kill.  

Games for Freaks, Vol. 3

Volume 3 of Games for Freaks is the only volume at time of writing that doesn't add a new Great House to the foray, but it does address one of the original hang-ups I had with Maleghast way back when it released: scenarios. Tom Bloom must’ve heard my prayers, because boy oh boy, Vol. 3 is jam packed with scenarios. 

Thirty six out of the thirty nine new scenarios all take place on different sorts of wild and wacky battlefields of various shapes, sizes, and hazard density, providing all new ways to make each game particularly memorable. Petrified Necrosphinx, for example, adds a gigantic stone statue smack dab in the middle of the battlefield, and units can pray to it for the possibility of boons, at the risk of being totally obliterated. Blood Gulch adds destructible floors, which can be attacked to create adverse terrain and inflict vulnerable tokens on anyone standing on the destroyed tile. Underhive adds multiple Minecarts to the battlefield, which can be used to reposition friendly units quickly or can be pushed into enemy units who are standing on the tracks. These three, and the thirty three others included here, add some really great twists on the game, and are a much needed breath of air into the game.

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

For those who want a match with more focus on objectives rather than simply ripping out your opponents throat, the Skulls! Skulls! Skulls! Game mode is just for you. In these three scenarios, all units respawn when killed, even your Necromancer (at half health and no SOUL, though), and victory is instead achieved through scoring objective points. The first scenario, Skull Soccer, is just what it sounds like. There's one skull, and you score points by gaining control of it and shooting it into the other player's goal. Capture the Skull is also just what it sounds like: grab one of the skulls in the middle and get it back to your side, and stop your opponent from doing the same. Skull Harvest is most similar to the sort of objective style gameplay we see in Warhammer, where there are three areas of the map marked as objectives that units must stand on to control, and whoever has more units on the zone scores points for it. Adding these objective focused game modes to Maleghast is outstanding, and I really hope Bloom does more like these.

Outside these new scenarios, Vol. 3 adds a new unit for Hellminth, the Peon, a Thrall unit that excels at creating corpses for other units on the team to feed from, as well as introducing mercenaries to Maleghast. Any faction can take a single unit slot of any type to be a mercenary, which does give teams access to slots they may not have had access to before. The two introduced here are the Tarsal Ghoul, a Scion who focuses on consuming and creating tokens to further their rampage, and the Famine Demon, a Horror that damages itself while draining positive tokens away from enemy models. While these mercenary units are powerful, they come with the tradeoff of lacking the same synergy that other units from within your Great House already have    

Games for Freaks, Vol. 4

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

The most recent volume of Games for Freaks returns to adding a new Great Houses to play with, the mecha-cops of the Volvadani, as well as introducing a few more merchants and mercenaries to the fray.

The Volvadani are a really cool team, and its not just because it gives me an excuse to blast Strong Arm of the Law by Saxon during a game of Maleghast. Volvadani are a team focused on well timed, devastating offense, where you wait out for the perfect moment to strike and then hit like a runaway train when the moment comes. The main way this is done is through the Volvadani Tithe Law ability. Each round, you select one of six laws to put into effect until the end of the round, at which point that law is discarded and you must pick a different one. These laws trigger off of actions your opponent might take during that turn, such as ending adjacent to a Volvadani unit or adding a positive token. If you meet one of these conditions, you gain a guilt token, and become a criminal. Volvadani units power up when targeting criminals, proving the old adage that Crime doesn’t pay, and forcing your opponent to either do what they can to avoid gaining guilt, or disobey and face the full wrath of the law.

Volvadani units also have the Burst rule, which allows you to spend burst tokens to repeat a burst ACT ability multiple times in one turn if you have the tokens to do so. The Bobby, for example, can use Bloodhound to charge up their burst tokens based on the guilt of a target enemy in range, before using burst tokens to either buff its own speed or add extra attacks of Ferocious Walloping into some demonic ne’er-do-well.

The cybernetic Necromancers of Volvadani crews, Praetors, are brutally effective distributors of undead dirtnaps when they’re attacking a unit marked as a criminal, with their Hammer Shotgun gaining Pierce against criminals with 3+ guilt tokens and handing out a guilt token to a unit that damages you or an adjacent ally once per round. Its upgrades include dangerous abilities like gaining an extra burst charge if you follow your own laws, causing a unit to gain extra guilt during a turn if they break laws, or Imprison someone inside a cage of walls. You could even just Throw the Book at someone for 4 SOUL and make them subject to every single law for one turn, and they pick up a vulnerable token on top of guilt when they break a law. 

As for the mercs and merchants, there's some really fun stuff here for any team to take advantage of. The Heliosphinx Tyrant, for example, can hand out debuffs and curse damage with the power of its gaze, and turns into a 2x2 wall section when it’s killed. The Eglen Ghoul thralls, on the other hand, are little guys who just like to run around and eat corpses to gain power and are pretty weak, but if they gain enough HP through consuming corpses they gain multiple stacks of buff tokens and become a major problem real quick. As for merchants, the Moon-Shrouded Man can provide ways to obscure parts of the battlefield in darkness, and Graft, the Penumbral Baroness can give players access to her hounds to wreak havoc on the battlefield. You like dogs? You won't in these dark halls, that's for sure.

Final Thoughts

Credit: CHASM/Tom Bloom

It's a delight to see Tom Bloom adding so much into Maleghast post-release, giving this game the opportunity to grow even more and just get so wonderfully weird. So much of this game is bursting with creativity, and in particular the new mechanics of the Hellminth and Volvadani are really creative and a blast to play. Plus, I love the big expansion of scenarios here, it adds so much extra playability to the game and resolves one of the few hangups I found with Maleghast at release. Alongside all of these updates, Bloom has also been releasing balance updates too, which shows that Bloom isn’t just dedicated to making the game fun by just adding more stuff, but also by being involved in the game’s community and addressing imbalance issues where it arises for a more even, fun experience. 

Maleghast was very exciting when it was released, and the four expansions for the game found in the pages of Games for Freaks only make it better. Bloom design feels very confident, and I cannot wait to see where all this momentum takes the game. I’ll probably get my answers in the pages of a future Games for Freaks volume, I’m eagerly looking forward to it. It turns out, I’m the freak this game is for, and hopefully you are too!
