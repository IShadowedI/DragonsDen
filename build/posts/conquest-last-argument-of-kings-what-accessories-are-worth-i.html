Conquest Last Argument of Kings: What Accessories Are Worth It?

July 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

It’s difficult to know where to start and end when it comes to the concept of ‘stuff’ in a tabletop wargame. The official Para Bellum One Player Starter Sets include cards, terrain, a measuring tool, dice and a map of Eä, and that’s barely the beginning when it comes to accessories.

Some accessories simply make the game possible to play. If you want to play Conquest, you absolutely must get these. Others genuinely make it a more pleasurable experience, even though arguably they’re not necessary. If you ask me, you really should get these. Some can make it nice but it’s probably down to personal preference - you could get these if you like, but you shouldn’t feel like you’re missing out if you don’t get them. Finally, there are some accessories that I personally wouldn’t get, because they’re just more trouble than they’re worth.

Helpfully, I’ve structured this article under those headings. You’re welcome!

Must Get

Let’s start off with dice, a topic that could catalyse an entire category of articles by itself. You could possibly get away with a dice-rolling app if you’re looking to save $3, but please. It’s not the same without them, even the down sides (loud noises, risk of hitting minis, high likelihood of dropping one on the floor) are an important part of the experience, don’t try going digital.

Conquest uses 6-sided dice, so those are easy to pick up from anywhere, but it’s worth giving a special shout-out to Para Bellum’s own faction dice. They’re a nice size, feel aligned with the minis on the table, but come with two issues. Firstly, there are ‘only’ 24 in a box. If you’re running a big block of something - a genuine possibility in The Last Argument of Kings (TLAOK) - you might end up needing more than that. Secondly, they’re all one colour, and in TLAOK you’re likely to end up in a situation where as part of one action you’re rolling for more than one objective (e.g. a character clashing alongside a regiment), where the numbers you’re trying to hit are different. Different coloured dice make that quick and easy - not adjectives we often hear to describe TLAOK!

As an aside, I seem to have ended up with an amusing printing error on one of my Wadhrun dice that gives me two 5s. Having seen a bunch of these sets I don’t think that’s at all common and I’m sure Para Bellum would replace it if I asked, but if you’re ever playing against me and I offer to share my dice don’t take the bait!

What's wrong with this picture?

Next up would be a measuring tape. By all means go with funny-shaped sticks if that’s going to float your boat, but anything with reliable numbers and lines on it ought to work.

Alongside dice, the other thing you can’t miss on the Para Bellum eshop is their objective zones, and these are genuinely helpful. Before they came out we used everything from cereal box cutouts through cake stands to 3D-printed discs, and they’re the best.

Perhaps it goes without saying - they come in the mini boxes anyway - but the last must-get is command cards. Without these it will be almost impossible to properly play. There are potential workarounds but these all fall into the categories of ‘unacceptably low quality of life experience’ or ‘unacceptably high level of effort with not enough payback’.

Should Get

Oh, maybe this is must-get, but I’m going to put terrain as the opener here. I look back with a nostalgic sense of fondness on the days where a sheet draped over books or a couple of T-shirts becomes a hill, where a cardboard box becomes anything you want it to. And the truth is that this all requires imagination in any case. There’s some terrain that’s truly beautiful, and I personally push very hard for it, but a lot of it isn’t that much of a step up from a discarded envelope extracted from the recycling with the word FIELD scrawled across it.

This is possibly particularly true for TLAOK, where 2D terrain is disappointingly practical. The one player starter sets include 2 double-sided pieces of 2D terrain, which work nicely but simply isn’t enough so I’d suggest get some more. There are decent packs of 2D terrain out there, but because they’re never going to feel as immersive as good-quality 3D terrain I wouldn’t try throwing much money at this problem.

3D terrain, on the other hand, can make a massive difference. The First Blood terrain kits feel robust, look very nice, and pack flat, which is extraordinary for a hobby with massive typical storage requirements. They’re not plastic, so are less 3D than you otherwise might hope for, and would benefit from a black Sharpie around the edges to take away the obvious feel of cardboard, but they tick the 80/20 rule so I’m a fan of them. One note from me: Use a craft knife to extract them, or risk the beautiful coloured veneer ripping off. I did it so you don’t have to.

Conquest First Blood terrain and objective marker set

The slightly strangely connected second entry under this heading would be tokens, because there’s quite a lot to track in Conquest, particularly in TLAOK. And the strange connection is that the First Blood terrain box comes with some objective markers that on any other day I’d describe as tokens, and the tokens box contains a bunch of command cards anyway (so if you play any of the TLAOK officers (such as Ward Preceptor, Seasoned Veteran or Aberrant) as characters in First Blood you might want to pick it up anyway. The current Para Bellum tokens kit is different from the last one in that the plastic tokens come on a sprue, all ready to be added to my grey pile of shame. Part of me feels like I don’t want to take on that extra work, but never mind.

To be fair, the tokens themselves are really nice. I’ve never seen a game where I’ve wanted to comment on the quality of the piece of plastic that tells me that a unit has suffered a wound before, and these do that. Well done, Para Bellum. They’re detailed enough to be interesting but will work fine with a single colour drybrush, and sound lovely as they clack together if you’re in the mood to fiddle as if they’re poker chips (and who isn’t - but look out for chipping that paint job). But those upsides are unfortunately marred by the fact that out of the 52 tokens (some double-sided so they serve more than one purpose, very clever), only 14 are wound tokens. I feel like Para Bellum is seriously underestimating my ability to do badly at this game.

Conquest tokens and command cards set

My final entry under this heading would be magnets. There’s a lot of minis getting pushed around the table in a game of TLAOK, and they love to fall over. Para Bellum have done the clever thing and created stands and bases with holes designed for magnets, so please make use of them. The magnets that will fit are 5mm diameter x 1mm thick - the stands actually fit a 6mm magnet so that’s something to get marginally annoyed about, but 5mm for both is probably good enough if you don’t want to think too hard (but you’ll be grateful if you get both). If you can get away with it, please don’t glue the bases into the stands. I know there are different opinions on that, and that’s ok. But (a) it’s already hard enough to get the bases touching when in combat without needing to give someone a panic attack about breaking the immovable lance that’s reaching into their airspace, and (b) First Blood is a genuinely good game that deserves the ability to have a round base.

Could Get

Conquest is a game that uses cards, and card sleeves are a good idea to protect those cards. And they serve more purposes than that! Firstly, it’s a lovely outlet for creative expression - get some unique card sleeves and allow them to communicate something to your opponent (which might be as simple as “please don’t get my cards mixed up with yours”). Secondly, they provide a built-in reminders system. Want to have instant access to the unit’s stat line without getting the app out every turn? Print them off small and slip them inside. Want a reminder of which spells do what? Scribble a tiny cheat sheet and pop it in with the right character. Make a tactical mistake that costs you a game? Make a note of what not to do and keep it inside the sleeve. But, of course, go without them and the world will keep on spinning.

Another thing that might make sense to get would be the Conquest lore companion book. If you’re interested in the world of Eä, it’s genuinely good. It won’t make any difference to the game in front of you, but will answer the questions everyone who comes to Conquest asks themselves (why do the orcs that ride dinosaurs hate those steampunk Greeks so much?). It will also bring you a new sense of appreciation when the next faction is announced, because it will feel somehow familiar.

Finally, never underestimate the value of a good old notepad and pen. In a game where keeping score is necessary and the alternatives seem to be mobile apps that are separate from the official Conquest one and dice, which can too easily get confused for one that needs to be rolled, this shouldn’t be overlooked. As above, when it comes to the old 80/20 rule this is something that really punches above its weight.

Something else that punches above its weight - at least, it does at 7am before you leave the house - is clothing. The Conquest T-shirts are good quality, for the most part don’t draw undue attention and communicate in a relatively subtle way your interest in the game. Why not express your unique passion for your hobby by fitting right in with the rest of the niche?

Wouldn’t Get

Let’s start of with that map of Eä. The map is a nice thing, but it’s not what I’d call useful. I’ve ended up with a delicious ironable cloth map, and for the most part it just sits smugly on the shelf next to the aforementioned grey pile of shame. Possibly one day it will begin to play an integral role in the Conquest experience, but for the moment it doesn’t have as much going for it as I’d like it to. Probably leave that one on the shelf.

Secondly, and rounding out our list, is that black hole known as the laser pointer. In a game where drawing lines between units can literally change the outcome of the game it’s tempting to feel like it’s a necessity, but I think we can all admit that one nerd’s shaky hand drawing a line that seems like it moves when casting my eye from one point to the next doesn’t provide the level of clarity and zen-like calm that was promised on the box. Do yourself a favour and invest instead in a decent-quality digital camera, which will allow you in the moment to take a quick top-down snap of the situation, then whip out the SD card, shove it into your laptop, open up MS Paint and draw a straight line, send it to print, and then argue over that (or good luck seeing if it’s possible to do that on your phone).

What Do You Think?

I’m already arguing with myself in my head about the placement of some of these. Some are definitely borderline. But where have I got it completely wrong? And what have I missed?
