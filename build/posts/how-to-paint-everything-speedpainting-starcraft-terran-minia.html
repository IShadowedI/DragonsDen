How to Paint Everything: Speedpainting StarCraft Terran Miniatures

May 26th, 2026 via Goonhammer
When I first got my box of StarCraft minis at Adepticon, I was intimidated because there were a lot in the box!  And while I do have some good memories of StarCraft, they were from playing it at brunches on my Uncle’s computer back in the 1990s.  There wasn't a lot of visual memories attached to the actual game for me.  But then when I opened the box and built my first Terran mini, I knew exactly what I wanted to do.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

The Terran minis are cast in a metallic blue plastic with orange (and for the medics, green) visors and blast effects.  They looked fantastic unpainted, but I said to myself, how do we get these painted but keep the vibe of metallic blue and orange.  At the same time, I also wanted to play with OSL and under lighting for these models.  StarCraft is fought on a variety of alien worlds and since all of these models are in heavy space suit armor, I wanted the basing to reflect an alien world.  Because the figures would be blue and orange, I wanted another contrasting color to the blue.  I picked pink.  The story here is these models are on an alien world brimming with pink energy crystals.  This not-entirely geologically stable world would have veins of the energy bleeding through, lighting the models with alien light from above.  

I also wanted to paint them quickly.  So for the Marines, Marauders, Raynor, and the Combat Drone I gave myself two days to do them from black prime to site-ready.  This may be relevant for those who get a big order and don't have a lot of time before they need to have them ready to put on the tables of the Final Goonhammer Open and challenge MasterSlowpoke!

All models started from a black prime, and on temporary bases unless otherwise noted.  

Terran Marines, Marauder, Goliath

I began by spraying the model with a coat of Vallejo True Metallic Amethyst Purple.  This would establish both the shadow color and a brighter base for the eventual base coat.  I then sprayed all but the bottoms of the model with Vallejo True Metallic Metal Sapphire Blue.  This color is pretty close to the original metallic blue plastic, and when sprayed over the purple is a very bright blue.  For highlights, I used the TMM Sapphire Blue highlight color.  This was subtle but noticeable to highlight, and then gave the models a soft drybrush with ProAcryl Silver.   

Credit: Kevin Stillman

Of course, these models are not all uniformly blue.  My next step after that was to use Citadel Black Templar Contrast Paint to block out the pasts that would be another color.  

For the metallics, I used a drybrush of ProAcryl Magnesium followed by a drybrush of ProAcryl Silver.  This indicates the weapons and metallics are newer than the weapons and superstructure of a GW Venerable Dreadnought.   

For the guns, I left them metallic.  The rifles don't generally have a lot of decoration in the pictures or promo shots I've seen, though there's enough space on them to color the weapon casing or add artwork if you're so inclined.  The exception was the muzzle for the Goliath’s main guns: Those I painted black more to break them up than anything else.  

For the holsters, I kept the blue banding but colored them with Balthazar Gold.  I also used Balthazar Gold for the grills on the Marauder’s big guns and on some random knobs on the Goliath.  

For the big cables on the Marauder that connect the gun arms to the main body, I added hazard stripes to them.  Likewise, I did the same to the area around the canopy of the Goliath to warn people the canopy opens and could hit them.

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

I then added the energy effects via Army Painter Fluorescent Pink.  This was a struggle because the paint kept clogging up my airbrush, but was still quite thin.  Eventually Keewa pointed out I needed to thin it.  I painted this on all the downward facing areas of the models, and on some other areas where the metallic armor would bounce the light onto upper areas.  This bounce light was less subtle.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

Lastly, I painted the visors orange.  I mixed Black Templar with ProAcryl Nova Orange, highlighting up to white.  For the Marines, I used this to give the faceless helmets a bit of direction: the highlight would go to where the Marine was aiming their gun.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

For the canopy on the Goliath, I took the approach that the canopy was one pane of whatever transparent space materials the Terran Confederacy makes their war machines out of.  I highlighted from black to orange with the brightest being in the central segment, where the pilot is looking forward and aiming his guns at the enemy.  

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

On a lark, I also painted the ends of the Goliath and Marauder Guns with energy like they were melta weapons.  

At the same time I was working on these models, Keewa and I started working on our 1-Step tests.  So Raynor and his Combat Drone were the perfect choices to give them a whirl.  

Jim Raynor

Credit: Kevin Stillman

Raynor, unlike the other Terran Marines, is usually depicted in black armor.  I was eager to try out Dark Black Blue 1Step as a potential Ravenwing juice, so I prepared him like I do my Ravenwing minis: I used Army Painter Air Crow’s Hue and Wolf Grey for a zenithal, then sprayed him with straight DBB.  This part of the experiment wasn't entirely successful, as it came out a smidge *too* black.  I needed to have thinned it out with Secret Sauce beforehand to keep it blue-er.  I gave Raynor a drybrush highlight of grey then silver.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

For Raynor’s base, I was a fool - a foolish fool at that, and used the sculpted base.  This meant that instead of relying on crackle paste like I would for the other minis, I would need to replicate it in the aggregate on the alien environment of that base.  I base coated the base with Corvus Black, before dry brushing to Dawnstone.  I then watered down PA Bold Titanium White and painted it into the recesses of the rocks, before spraying the base with AP Fluorescent Pink.  I then went back in and added thin lines of white to brighten the energy up.

Credit: Kevin Stillman

The area on the back of the base posed a bit of a challenge because I had no idea what the material was supposed to be.  Was it supposed to be a plant or mineral?  I took the position it was a mineral melted by the energy flow and made it into a less vibrant pink.  

I painted his face like I would a standard Caucasian face, and then freehanded a five pointed yellow star onto his shoulder, a la the 2000s era US Army.  

Combat Drone

The Combat Drone was the only one of these I painted in subassemblies.  For the majority of the Blue parts. I painted them like I wound up painting the rest of the infantry.  The exception was that these parts did not get hit with a pink highlight: the rocket blast energy instead overpowered that light.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

For the rocket blast base/stand, I primed these in Wraithbone.  I then shot PA 1 Step Warm Yellow, Orange-Red, and Crimson through the airbrush in descent, and painted the pointy tips black.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

Medics

The Medics were painted separately from the rest of the Marines because I was missing a sprue.  Once I got the sprue, they were swiftly assembled.  They were not, however, swiftly painted.  I knew they were going to be mostly white models, but there was going to be a secondary color on the shoulders and certain armor segments to break it up.  

In order to keep the metallics consistent across the army, I was going to need a metallic white.  Unfortunately, there is not currently a Vallejo TMM White.  So instead I decided to use one of the two Pearlescent metallics on the market: TurboDork Pearly Gates.  This would need to go over a white base coat, so I primed these black then attempted a zenithal spray of Grey Seer to White Scar.  However, the grey clearly left a bit of the splatter showing through so it did not look right at this stage.  I was able to mostly blend things together via drybrush, but it wasn't the easiest thing in the world.  I then sprayed the model with Pearly Gates.  This worked best of the areas that had been hit with White Scar.  After a few coats, we had metallic sheen built up.  I then used my Tamiya panel liner in the recesses, before dry brushing again with Bold Titanium White.

For the secondary color, I chose a greenish-blue.  This was inspired by my Reverse 1999 home screen character at the time, Marsha Rosenheart.  Marsha is an utterly broken sustain in R1999 as of the time of this writing, where her continuously applied shields keep her allies (Beryl Bouanich, Isolde, and Lorentz Butterfly) alive.  When you click on her in the home screen, her voice line is “The color of this medical uniform is excellent, being highly visible on the battlefield”.  Who am I to disagree with such a wise sustain?  The problem is that the best metallic color to match Marsha’s Insight 2 outfit is TurboDork Cuarco

Also unfortunately, TurboDork Paints are not brush-friendly paints.  They want to go through and airbrush and have major separation issues.  So getting a consistent color out of them is a pain in the tuchus.  

For the Medigel tanks, I painted them with a metallic.  I cut the green effects off because I found them 1) Silly and 2) potentially breakable if they were in a case.  I did add a green energy effect at the pack’s nozzles.  

For the red crosses, I used a combination of TMM Gold with Contrast Blood Angels Red layered of them.  I then used my Goblin Hobbies stamp art kit to put the medical caducei in the center of the cross.

For the visors, I painted from black to green.  I left one face bare, and tried to paint strands of red hair to suggest that she is indeed Marsha reborn After The Rain.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

I also added the pink glow like above.  

Bases

The bases, besides Raynor and the Combat Drone, were all rattle can primed with White Scar.  I then sprayed them with Monument Hobbies Rogue pink to produce a bright pink.  I then used my airbrush to spray sections of the base white, before gloss varnishing the bases and leaving them to cure overnight.  

Credit: Kevin Stillman

I then attached the models to their bases, and spread Citadel Mordant Earth on the base.  It's important to apply this as thickly as you can, despite the fact that GW texture pots are possibly the single worst application of the pot in the entire line.  For the Marines I didn't go thick enough and left the paint to crackle over night, so the cracks where the energy shows through aren't quite as pronounced as the ones on the Goliath base.  The Goliath had thicker paste and I hit it with my hair dryer to cause bigger cracks to appear and faster.  

I finished up by painting the rim of the base black, and boom: We have the entire Terran starter range painted and ready to do battle at the Goonhammer Open!

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman
