BattleTech Tournament Report - Southern Assault '26

November 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome, MechWarriors! Lynn here is back from the Classic BattleTech tournament at Southern Assault 2026, and I'm eager to share with y'all how things went!

Let's Get Down to Business...

Southern Assault is an annual event hosted by the well-known BattleTech-heavy webstore, Fortress Miniatures and Games, from their home base of Clemmons, North Carolina; one of, if not the, biggest BattleTech-centric events in the country. It has historically been Alpha-Strike-focused, but the last two outings have added on a Classic BattleTech tournament run by the hosts of the Mechbay podcast, and this year's Classic tournament drew a colossal field of 84 players despite running on a non-holiday Monday (11/9)!

Southern Assault '26 was also set to be the first event accessible to me running with the new Core Rules, so I was particularly eager to take part. With Core being all fresh and shiny-new, floating through-armor criticals were the only optional rule in play. Aside from that, we were looking at...

  • 8k BV, mechs only, 3-6 units, no duplicate chassis, Core Rules equipment only.
  • Unit choice was faction-locked, but not era-locked.
  • Skills were limited to the 0-5 range and required to be within one of each other (e.g. 3/4 would be fine, but 3/5 wouldn't work).
  • Max of two tons of ammunition per unit swapped for specialty ammo.
  • Max of two units capable of generating a +4 TMM.
  • Max of 40 pulse damage.
  • No Arrow IV launchers.

In addition, we could spend 50 BSP on Battlefield Support, with all* published Assets available to all factions. Assets would count against the 6-unit body-count limit and the 4+ TMM limit, ASF Assets weren't allowed, Strikes were limited to one per type (one Light Strike, one Heavy Strike, etc), and minefield, artillery, and immobile-asset support were prohibited.

* All Assets with physical cards, anyway; Peri was apparently denied the use of one of the Eurus BSP Assets from Hot Spots: Hinterlands because... it was published in a book rather than as a card? Dunno how that decision was made.

Missions were revealed at the event, rather than in advance.

...To Shoot Lots of Guns

I knew I wanted to test units improved by (or allowed due to) the new Core Rules, and I fairly quickly decided to focus on one of their biggest beneficiaries: Autocannons. Between the increased usability of Armor-Piercing ammo, their improved resilience to critical hits, Ultra ACs not jamming any more, ammo explosions having a gentler impact... everything's coming up roses for Big Bullet Enthusiasts.

Clearly, then, I reached for the faction most known for its love of autocannons...

...the Capellan Confederation!

What? Were you expecting something else?

In all seriousness, the Thunder 1L is one of the game's premier caddies for a plain AC/20, and the Capellans have built, bartered, or salvaged (frequently salvaged) plenty more solid shooters. I ultimately ran the list I nicknamed "Capellan Jagerbomb," with the following roster, all at base 4/5 skills...

JagerMech JM7-C3BS (1,354 BV)

Warrior House Imarra JagerMech. Credit: Lynn B.

I already explained my rationale for embracing this mech in my JagerMech Overview, but briefly, I looked to pair the great reach and good damage of its Snub PPCs with spin-to-win crit-seeking by loading AP ammo in its four Light Autocannon 2s.

Thunder THR-1L (1,471 BV)

This is actually the Thunder's cousin, the Lightning. It has a nicer miniature! Credit: Lynn B.

A phenomenally efficient mech, the THR-1L is a full-armored 70 tonner with 5/8 speed carrying an AC/20 with three tons of ammo, three medium pulse lasers, an emotional support LRM 5 with one ton of ammo, and enough heat sinks to only build one heat at a run firing everything, even if you somehow have a target for both the MPLs and the LRM. With CASE on its ammo, it can even survive an ammo explosion despite its Inner Sphere XL engine now, and its rear armor is decent. Its only real glaring weakness is a total inability to direct any offense against a target in its rear arc, save perhaps a punch; the arm-mounted AC/20 spills two critical slots into the right torso unnecessarily, and all its other weapons are torso-mounted. It's simply fantastic, and I have bones to pick with CCAF procurement over the 1L model dropping off Capellan availability in the ilClan era; its upgrade models may make sense in-universe, but with BV balancing, Thunders never get better than this.

Malice MAL-XV (1,996 BV)

Warrior House Imarra Malice. Credit: Lynn B.

Another BV-efficiency monster, the Malice XV may be packed with explosive critical slots, possessed of an Inner Sphere XL engine, and have an optimal range of 3 hexes while moving at 4/6 speed, but its full armor coverage, ability to load specialty ammo in its quartet of LAC/5s, phenomenal point-blank damage output, and CASE II make it a fearsome mech for its price tag. I like their flavor plenty well enough, but the Capellans yoinking this mech from the crumbling Republic was honestly the biggest factor pushing me towards playing this faction.

Blood Asp C (2,270 BV)

Warrior House Imarra Blood Asp. Credit: Lynn B.

The Blood Asp, like the Executioner, has weirdly soft side torso armor for its size, but it's still a solid Clan Assault Mech moving 4/6, and the C configuration slaps a BV-efficient, autocannon-heavy weapons load into its chassis. The Blood Asp, along with the JagerMech, helped keep me from skewing too short-ranged in this list, although it asks for good Ultra AC cluster rolls to maximize its ranged damage.

Fun fact: I actually miscounted shots when deciding to run half-ammo in all the UAC/10 slots in the Blood Asp: Its default ammunition load is too much for an eight-round game, and it does benefit from being able to bleed bins dry faster to minimize crit risk, but it doesn't really have fully twice the ammo it needs. In both my Round 2 and Round 3 games, the last shots I fired from the Ultras were the last shots I could have fired on Ultra mode, I was very, very near running dry.

Mongoose MON-76 (894 BV)

Warrior House Imarra Mongoose. Credit: Lynn B.

Okay, it doesn't have an autocannon, but it was cheaper and faster than a Blade! This Mongoose gave me a nice budget mech with the speed to chase objectives, enough firepower to hunt light mechs or backstab larger mechs, good armor for its size, hands, and an ECM suite. It doesn't have a probe, and I'm going to try to emphasize those more in my future light mech picks (this is called foreshadowing), but I do think it's a really solid machine, and in the best tier of speed-boost-users available (since 8/12[16], like 7/11[14], doesn't actually get to a new TMM threshold, so they dodge that BV surcharge).

For support I ran a Veteran-skill Patton Tank (Taurian), a Light Bombing, and Heavy Air Cover. The Taurian variant of the Patton has a really good blend of traits for a budget BSP cost, in my opinion: Fast enough, hard to hit enough, tough enough, shooty enough, without being so exceptional that it draws focused hate. On offense, I tend to rate bombings higher than strikes... they do more damage clusters, when AE damage makes a difference it makes a big difference, and their scatter can turn a near-miss into a hit on another enemy if your opponent clusters their force. I also err on the side of Heavy Air Cover if I can only afford one air cover, since Heavy Strikes/Bombings are more impactful than light.

Since the Capellan Warrior Houses have no vehicles under their direct command, only mechs and infantry, I chose to paint my Patton using my Imarra ivory as a base color and my Imarra green for camouflage splotches, but in place of Imarra's gold I instead brought in brown camo splotches; the goal was to coordinate with the rest of my force without matching them. The end result came out a little toyetic, but I liked it.

All the strength of a raging fire. Credit: Lynn B.

A Meal Worth Fighting For

The travelogue portion of this tournament report is bound to be somewhat uneventful, since about 90% of the drive for me, both ways, was just "Get on I-85 and drive straight for several hours," but the most important part is this: The Carolinas have Biscuitville.

Credit: Lynn B.

Among horses, Red Hare; among regional breakfast chains, Biscuitville. Get yourself to one of these if you're ever in the right vicinity, I swear you won't regret it.

Alas, we had not yet realized that Clemmons now has a Biscuitville in close proximity to Southern Assault's venue as Monday dawned, so we instead indulged in our less-geographically-constrained ritual of pre-tournament Waffle House that morning. I know some of our non-United-Statesan readers have inquired about Waffle house before, so let me show you my regular order at Waffle House.

Mysterious as the dark side of the moon. Credit: Lynn B.

That's Double Hashbrowns All The Way, a concoction which takes a pile of fried potato shreds and adds every conceivable topping. Both chili and gravy are on that thing at the same time. It is heartburn on a plate, and I love it.

I just don't love it quite as much as Biscuitville. (This post is neither sponsored nor endorsed by Biscuitville.)

For a Long Time, We've Been Marching Off To Battle...

Liberty had gotten the impression the night before that we'd be able to get the mission packet as soon as we showed up in the morning, so we got to the tournament well before the actual scheduled check-in time... and, lo and behold, no, the mission packets were not ready that far in advance, so we had a while to kill. I'd like to say that I spent that time socializing and networking, but I actually spent it checking back over the Core Rules to try to make sure I remembered everything relevant.

I was naive and ruffled a few feathers in my last Southern Assault tournament report by critiquing the promotional nature of the items in the event swag bag; that's something I regret in retrospect. Yes, promotional consideration is necessary to make the economics of a very generous swag bag make sense, I just had no prior exposure to such events and overreacted to the prominent inclusion of Steel Rift items.

For better or worse, this year's swag bag, while just as generous, focused more heavily on Fortress and Southern Assault branding specifically. It still had a bit of an Alpha Strike skew, completely understandable given both the event's roots and the fact that AS attendance for Southern Assault is still substantially higher than the Classic crowd (the AS tournament drew 120 players!), but there was a pretty strong through-line of stuff that's useful for painting (a dot painter, a collapsible paint cup, a travel case for brushes), which is great to have no matter what game you play. I'd like to say an unalloyed thank you to the hosts and sponsors of the event!

The actual check-in process was a bit awkward. The sheer scale of how many players needed to be corralled was the biggest hurdle, but the TOs' efforts to mitigate the impact of the numbers were... imperfect. A pre-judging queue for the paint rankings turned into one of the longest lines I've seen outside an airport or amusement park, and the decision to pre-pair the first round and give table numbers at check-in was better on paper than in practice. I personally forgot the table number I'd been given by the time I made it through the paint-judging line (I really should've written it down!) and then since attendance wasn't perfect, several players, including me, had to be re-paired and moved to different tables anyway.

They honestly gave it a very valiant try; I don't think I could've done better with the resources they had available, but I think it probably just isn't practical to funnel a group that large through any single choke point, much less two in a row. Perhaps they could muster a few more volunteers for next year?

In any case, we did get the full mission packet with a decent amount of lead time before round one's start, so we had time to review, and each mission fit on a single page with a pretty slick unified layout. While I do prefer missions to be released in advance, I didn't have any real complaints with their presentation here. I do quibble with a few of the specifics of said missions, but I think their formatting was a clear win for the Mechbay crew.

Help Me To Not Make a Fool of Me (Round One)

As I start talking about missions, my Mulan-theming for this article runs into the TOs' own theming; all the missions for this tournament had Lord of the Rings references for titles. This one, "The Two Towers," was quite simple: Enter from the short edges, go stand next to two objectives on the centerline, control of said objectives was determined by the total sum of the weight classes of each player's standing/conscious/not-shut-down mechs on or adjacent to the point, with crippled mechs counting for half (so, for instance, an Assault Mech, normally worth 4 control points, would count for 2 control points like a medium mech if it was crippled).

Less-simple was the fact that the environmental rules for high wind were in play for this mission. That meant +1 difficulty to all PSRs, a forced PSR at +1 difficulty for any mech that jumped, further penalties to DFA attempts, and hover, jump, and VTOL assets effectively had their MP cut in half. I kind of get the idea of this condition + mission combo, upping the fall risk for big beefy mechs in the control zone, but it was really annoying, and I say that as someone who didn't bring any jumpers.

My opponent, Ben, brought a really scary list to this mission, running a lot of my own favorite mechs: a Kingfisher C, Gargoyle T (with newly-buffed AP ammo in its PAC/8s, of course), Hammerhead, Phantom H, and Spider 8X, with the Spider's Piloting improved to 4.

Same as last year, all of the maps in play were 3D terrain placed on HexTech mats. They tended to be fairly symmetrical, but a bit sparse. Nearly(?) all of them had a big LoS-blocking hill in the middle, however, and our map's example of the type fully blocked mechs on one of the objectives from seeing mechs on the other, naturally dividing our forces in two. I sent my Blood Asp, Thunder, and Patton towards the objective to my right, and was answered by his Gargoyle and Spider; my Mongoose, Malice, and JagerMech went after the other objective (the Mongoose just managing to snag it first turn to jump-start my scoring by using MASC) and were met by his Hammerhead, Phantom, and BSP Skulker (which I destroyed with my light bombing before it accomplished much of anything); his Kingfisher hung back in an attempt to provide overwatch for both objectives.

The general flow of things. Credit: Lynn B.

This game went oddly slowly, and I definitely shoulder blame in that; I missed my last tournament due to illness, and I've not been making it out to local games with any regularity, so it had been a while since I'd last thrown dice in person. MegaMek is fine for revising tactics, but it's no substitute for the actual mechanics of playing the game on tabletop. In particular, I recall wasting time resolving multiple falls for my JagerMech before recalling that, due to engine hits it had taken from a Phantom backstab, it was actually running too hot to have made that many attempts to get up in the first place!

We only finished four rounds, and by the end of the shooting I had only managed to formally cripple the Gargoyle (the Hammerhead, which took the brunt of my Malice's... malice, managed to cling to one of its side torsos by a single point of Internal Structure!), while I'd lost my Mongoose to his own fast assets and saw the Thunder die to a lucky armor-piercing crit from one of the Gargoyle's PAC/8s which found an ammo bin, then confirmed another crit off the internal damage from that ammo explosion and hit a second ammo bin, doing enough damage in aggregate to kill it through its CASE.

My own AP ammo had an understated impact in this game; it definitely increased the amount of damage the Malice did to the Hammerhead, but my dreams of spicy crits didn't really come to fruition... the JagerMech did get one TAC into one of the Kingfisher's legs, but that was through an old-fashioned snake-eyes roll, not the AP ammo, and it only popped a heat sink to little effect.

Also everyone fell over a lot. Thanks, wind. Credit: Lynn B.

My personal goal with this tournament was to do more killing, rather than winning low-scoring Pyrrhic victories off objective points while being out-shot, but this game saw me... win a low-scoring Pyrrhic victory off objective points while getting out-shot. In fairness, the weighting of the points on offer favored objective play (70 objective points vs 30 kill points available), and I did dominate the objectives in a way my opponent was unwilling to match (the Kingfisher really should've come forward onto a point), but I was a little disappointed in myself, even in victory.

This one went into the record books as a 23-14 win for me; I realized while writing this that it should've been 23-17, since the JagerMech should've counted as crippled from the two engine hits it sustained; my opponent and I both missed this. Apologies, Ben.

Had we the time to play this out further, I'm confident I could've put the Hammerhead and Gargoyle down, but the loss of all my pulse lasers and my Precision AC/20 would've been a big problem against the Phantom, and the Kingfisher had barely been troubled by my efforts; things easily could've snowballed against me.

I had to put my self-doubt about round one behind me fairly quickly, though; lunch was a shorter-than-tournament-typical 45 minutes, and even without running around trying to find food (I was prepared with fistfuls of Uncrustables shoved in my backpack pockets, a great event meal hack) it passed quickly.

Hope He Doesn't See Right Through Me (Round Two)

This round I had the unusual honor of playing against BattleTech's Assistant Line Developer, Aaron Cahall, he who edited the very Core Rules we were playing out of! He was at Southern Assault on a fact-finding mission, looking to see firsthand how Core handles in a competitive environment. Unfortunately for him, the first lesson he was learning was the one he couldn't do anything about in the middle of a tournament: Invest in your metal before your meat. He'd brought an up-skilled Draconis Combine lance: a Jenner JR7-N and Wolverine 9R at 3/4, a BHKU-O at 4/3, and a Hatamoto-Chi 28T at 4/4. All of his Battlefield Support points had gone towards air strikes and air cover, no Assets on the ground to spread his footprint, and by the start of our game he was already really feeling his lack of numbers.

That said, I was a lot more scared of his force on this mission than I would have been on any other. "Keep It Secret" was a scanning mission with a fair number of house rules piled on top of it. Regardless of probes, scan range was restricted to units on or adjacent to the objectives, with probes only improving the TN; mechs... specifically only mechs, not assets... could choose to refrain from firing in order to guarantee a successful scan should they still be standing and active at the end of the turn. While I had a clear advantage in both numbers and in firepower, between his Probe and his upgraded Piloting skill, he had much better odds at completing scans than I did.

This mission started with two objectives in the same location as round one's objectives, but on every round after the first they scattered 1-6 hexes in a random direction, and on Round 3 they were joined by a third objective marker.

Slightly rougher terrain on this map prevented me from getting my Mongoose onto an objective round one, and on turn two he scored a TAC on the right leg of the  Mongoose with a strafing run, popping both its foot actuator and its hip, pretty immediately ruining my best asset for chasing bouncing objectives. My heart dropped as the next couple rounds went exactly as I feared they would, with me failing scans and his forces succeeding at them. My Thunder was able to pop his Jenner pretty quickly with its Precision AC/20 + pulse combo, however, and things turned pretty quickly from there.

I missed getting a photo of turn 2, but I believe this was end-of-3, after both our light mechs evaporated. Credit: Lynn B.

I got luckier than a lot of folks, with our objectives never really scattering outside the midfield; one, in particular, got stuck on the edge of the map for multiple turns (since these objectives simply stopped if the rolled movement would move them off the map, not wrapping around to the other side or anything) while my Blood Asp and Aaron's Black Hawk scanned it and traded pot shots. After the Jenner's death I also re-tasked the Thunder to pure scanning duty, forgoing its short-ranged shooting and leaning into its relative speed to guarantee me points.

Things snowballed from there... my Malice + JagerMech + Patton methodically dismantled the Hatamoto-Chi and intimidated the Wolverine off the westernmost objective while securing scans through sheer weight of numbers, since each of them could attempt their 8+ roll each turn, while the Thunder did its objective duty and the Blood Asp kept the Black Hawk from getting too comfortable. Aaron kept up a steady trickle of scoring with the Black Hawk, however, and we came out tied 35-35 on objectives after six rounds... this time it was kill points which put me over the edge, securing a 48-39 victory off having killed the Jenner and Hatamoto while only losing my Mongoose (to consecutive head hits from a bombing run and the Wolverine's UAC/10, incredibly!).

Endgame. Credit: Lynn B.

Aaron was a very gracious opponent (going so far as to actively cheer on my scanning rolls in the early goings, when my dice were being mean to me!), and I had a good time in this game, but this mission was my least favorite by a good margin. I got relatively lucky in several ways (the opposition I pulled, the way the objectives scattered, etc.), but the stacked random factors of random objective movement and random ability to score felt bad, and the option to remove the randomness of scoring by not shooting perversely discouraged interaction between the players... with another 70/30 objective/kill split, the best way to guarantee a good score in this mission, under most circumstances, was to simply ignore the enemy and focus on your scans.

I get the purpose of missions like this in trying to encourage listbuilding considerations beyond sheer killing power - probes, mobility, improved Piloting, etc - but, of course, the fate of Aaron's Jenner shows that you can bring a seemingly-perfect mech to a mission like this and still get next-to-nothing out of it if you don't also have an edge in killing power, and my instinct is there ought to be a middle ground... it should be possible to incentivize speed/probes/Piloting without introducing this many random elements. I heard too many anecdotes from other players of folks just getting screwed by the objectives beelining for their opponent's side of the field and never coming back.

In any case, I did feel good about having won a game explicitly off kill points, and being undefeated headed into the final round, and that put me in high spirits headed into round three!

All The Force of a Great Typhoon (Round 3)

"Skirmish at Amon Hen" was a straightforward kill mission to end the event (a mercy for folks whose brains were fried after having also played days of Alpha Strike in the lead-up to the Classic tournament) with a couple of added flourishes. Each player had to nominate one of their mechs as their Commander and one of their opponent's mechs which wasn't their Commander as their Target. If your Commander survived, you scored 10 points, while if you killed your Target it was worth double kill points. Base kill point scoring was also boosted relative to the other missions, with Kills and Crippling being worth BV/100 and BV/200, respectively, vs BV/250 and BV/500 in other two missions.

I did the math, and as long as your Target was worth 2000 BV or more, you could max out your scoring without keeping your Commander alive by tabling the opposing force.

My strategy was to nominate my most expensive unit, my Blood Asp, as my Commander, so my opponent's logical Target would be the Malice, both the most heavily-armored mech in my list and one which would give up (slightly) fewer points if it went down.

We were on long edge deployment for this mission, rather than the short-edge of the other two, throwing us into the action immediately. The other complication for this mission was that it was contested under 2x Terran gravity, so jumping triggered a PSR at a -1 TN and, more critically, fall damage was doubled.

I drew into a repeat opponent, Michael B. (always a class act), for this round, one of the aforementioned Alpha Strike / Classic double-header players whose brain was slightly leaking out of his ears by that point. He had a unique Bounty-Hunter-themed force anchored by a Mad Cat in the Bounty Hunter 2 configuration at 4/4 skills (his Commander), backed by a Falconer 9R, a Bushwacker X4 (carrying regular SRMs and Acid SRMs for its MML 5s, with the acids dead weight in this matchup), and a Locust IIC. He'd also brought a veteran point of BSP Asset Elementals, whose potential damage output is pretty damn spooky, a Heavy Air Strike, and, uniquely among my opponents, an aerospace Bluff card.

I believe I lost initiative to start off this match. Considering that our only objective was to kill each other, entering the map first actually gave me the ability to determine, or at least to strongly suggest, which end of the map we'd be fighting over. I chose an unusually-heavily-wooded stretch near my end of the table, my thinking being that it would allow me to screen the Malice more effectively while moving it into range. There were more woods on his side of the board than on mine, however, allowing him an even safer deployment while my JagerMech, Thunder, and Patton were relatively exposed.

Tranquil as a forest... Credit: Lynn B.

I'm now doubting my own recollection of who had the Initiative, because I know I was able to get my Mongoose into the Locust IIC's rear arc, with +4 TMM to its +3, on the first turn through another MASC run onto the board. MASC activation never bit me in the butt this tournament, and I'm sure I will in no way grow overconfident, nor learn any wrong lessons, from this experience. In any case, I was able to get internal on the Locust IIC's CT, and while I didn't hit any of the really spicy crits, I did take out its MPL, which would become... more relevant than it should have been later. I also scored a Precision AC/20 hit to the leg and an MPL to the head from the Thunder to the Falconer, my target, right out of the gate, forcing it to play cagey for the rest of the game. In turn, my JagerMech took +5 heat from the Bushwacker's plasma rifle, which I didn't expect and which limited its movement for the next two rounds before I finally remembered to let the damn thing cool down, and my Light Bombing (aimed at the Elementals) whiffed entirely.

Round 2, I held my Thunder back from the aggressive play I really wanted to make for fear of the Elemental BSP, while the Falconer successfully jumped to a vantage point which gave it partial cover against my whole force, the Bushwacker and Mad Cat drew back into deeper tree cover, and the Locust made a valiant attempt to backstab the 12/20/12 back armor of my Malice... from a distance of three hexes, just outside of ER Small Laser short range, so it really didn't accomplish much.

...but on fire within. Credit: Lynn B.

I did honestly panic a little in this turn. The Falconer laughed as everything my JagerMech shot at it went into the hill in front of it, rolling leg locations against its partial cover, the Thunder was held back by my fear of the Elementals (who deal a whopping four clusters of six damage when swarming), and I realized that I'd deployed my Blood Asp and Malice far enough away from his forces that his faster, longer-ranged mechs could simply continue backing away from me and kite the hell out of at least the Malice, my most reliable damage-dealer. I was kicking myself over my deployment choices, and I could feel the game slipping out of my control.

Then the next turn his Bushwacker and Mad Cat stood still in their woods, and the Falconer jumped (succeeding at the PSR, once again) deep enough into that stand of trees to neuter its own shooting entirely. The pendulum swung back in my direction.

I dropped the Thunder back to address his Locust IIC (my Mongoose having been Heavy Air Struck to death in R2 after he successfully Bluffed out my Heavy Air Cover in R1) and held the JagerMech stationary to try to guarantee the little guy's death. My Malice, meanwhile, was able to charge into optimal three-hex firing range of his Bushwacker while staying one hex outside BA swarming range, and the Blood Asp... was also present; its positioning relative to all the trees ensured its shot into anything would be suboptimal, but I directed all its fire at the Elementals in hopes of wiping them off the board.

Counterattack. Credit: Lynn B.

This was a turn of mixed successes. The Malice only inconvenienced the Bushwacker while eating a Bounty Hunter alpha strike in return, my Blood Asp only landed a single shot on the Elementals and then fell on its face, and I missed with all my big guns against the Locust IIC and "only" knocked off its arms... it was alive, but completely weaponless, and removed itself from the fight to keep from giving up kill points. Also, after flagging down a TO to confirm that I could (since they were in the adjacent hex rather than my own hex), I successfully punted the Elementals with the Malice, and that did kill them.

(It's worth noting we played this incorrectly; I rolled a Destroy check after the Blood Asp's attack, then realized I could make a melee attack and rolled a second Destroy check after it... as it happened, the result of that second Destroy Check would've killed the Elementals either way... I rolled an 8, which would either pop them if their check had been degraded by the Blasp failing to kill them or would pop them if all the damage to the Elementals that turn was properly added together and my roll got the +1 for dealing 21+ damage to them, but I don't recall what the first roll was, and in any case the order of operations was all wrong. Michael realized something had gone awry midway through, but let things stand since he'd been the one who encouraged me to roll the first check at the end of the shooting phase, not thinking about the possibility of melee. Long story short, we've all got a learning curve to deal with when it comes to Battlefield Support.)

Round four was dramatic, and was where the momentum irreversibly swung in my direction. I moved my Malice up into the smaller stand of woods in front of Michael's forest, and he responded by jumping the Falconer (which still didn't fall over) behind it. My Blood Asp and Patton both moved up to get a good firing angle on the Bushwacker, while my Thunder threatened the Mad Cat and my JagerMech... mostly tried to get to a better firing angle now that finally wasn't overheating.

In the violence that ensued, my Malice managed to land a Precision AC/5 round on the Falconer's head, which had been clipped by one of the Thunder's MPLs back on turn one... this left it alive on a single point of structure, but did go internal, the crit check confirmed one crit... and it scooped the cockpit. The Blood Asp and Patton, meanwhile, biased their fire into the left torso of the Bushwacker and simply ate its XL engine, guaranteeing its death. On the other hand, my Malice's right torso got ripped open, then it fell over. One of the four(!) resulting five-point damage clusters hit the right torso, and I rolled boxcars on the crit-confirmation check against my own mech. The Malice XV has CASE II, but six out of the 11 crit slots in that torso are explosive, and the single pips of internal structure damage from the resulting explosions managed to trigger another two critical hits. By the time I was done resolving everything, the Improved Heavy Medium Laser and 3/4ths of the tons of ammunition in that torso had exploded, and on top of a prior head hit and a failed seatbelt check, I managed to kill the Malice through pilot hits even with Core's leniency in that regard.

I didn't get a picture before units were removed... the hole in the forest near the Blood Asp is where the Bushwacker used to be; the hole in the smaller stand of trees south of it is where the Malice used to be! Credit: Lynn B.

Still, I had traded very well. I hounded the Mad Cat, his only viable remaining unit, across three(?) quick subsequent rounds, critting out its Large Pulse Laser with AC/2 AP ammo and then legging it with a boxcars crit, but I failed to cripple it, and soon enough it was time to finalize scoring.

I've yammered on at too-great length about this game, but it was a really high note to end the day on, a hell of a game with some wild turns of luck in both directions. I did discover while writing this report that it unfortunately ended on another mistake on my part... while scoring, I confidently quoted the old crippling rules and said that the Locust IIC was crippled because it had no ranged weapons left; this is no longer part of the crippling criteria in Core, nor in the summary of said rules in the tournament packet, so I did score two more points than I should have off the Locust, submitting a score of 73-59 which should've been 71-59... still a comfortable win for me either way, at least.

When We Come Home In Victory (Results)

I'm going to confess to y'all that I have no earthly idea who won the top prizes at Southern Assault.

Due to awkward timing around a planned ~2 hour closing of Fortress's on-site store, I tried to do some shopping while scoring was being done (with a particular hunger to pick up an old Ral Partha Unseen Scorpion in blister which was on offer for cheap), but due to PoS issues (point of sale, but, you know, also the other acronym) it was a total bust, and when I gave up and ran back to the hall the tournament was in, the top prizes had already been handed out.

I understand that Liberty got second overall? I have utterly no clue what any of the other results were; AFAICT the TOs didn't post any results in the MRC Discord. I don't have photos of most people's forces, either, since they were never all laid out at the same time for judging, so even if I knew who won Best Painted I couldn't share their force with you.

Sorry, y'all.

What I can say is that below the podium prizes seem to have most-probably been awarded in Best Overall ranking order with points scored as the sole(?) determiner of quality generalship rather than factoring in wins. At the very least I know that 2/3rds of the opponents I defeated in this tournament were ranked higher than me, and I, while undefeated, landed in the middle of the pack; the same was true for Peri. The prize pool for Southern Assault was once again extremely generous, however, and even with a mediocre placing I came away with a good haul of winnings, ironically including the very thing I kept scouring the store trying to find, Fortress's new(ish) paintbrush set!

The spoils of... the middle rankings? Credit: Lynn B.

Reflection

My list did much of what it was supposed to do. AP ammo isn't as oppressive as I hope-feared it might be, but autocannons are quality weapons nonetheless, and the Precision + AP combo is a good toolbox to have on hand; I'd definitely overall rather have a regular AC/10, 20, or LAC/5 over their Ultra equivalents, though I do still like a good LB (and AC/2s of all stripes remain... pretty bad, actually, though I still think the JM7-C3BS is a good mech [it's really hard to ruin a cheap pair of Snubbies]). I did succeed at focusing damage well enough to notch kills this tournament, though I certainly still have room for improvement in that regard; I really just got lucky against that Falconer in particular.

Random-rolled scanning certainly seems to be here to stay with its enshrinement in the Core Rules, and I think every tournament list going forward needs to account for that. I'm currently narrowing down a list of what I consider to be the best Probe caddies, with the Ice Ferret I near the top of that list... statistically speaking, bringing a Bloodhound Probe improves your scanning odds significantly more than a bump to 4 or even 3 piloting without a probe, even if the event limits the Bloodhound's range, like this one did. Still, it's worth remembering that tournaments can require that you stay standing to complete a scan, like this one did, and Piloting helps with that, while Probes don't; on a cheap enough unit (Nyx 110, Ostscout 8J, something like that) a combination of a Bloodhound and going to 4 Piloting is probably ideal.

How many probes do you need in a list? I'm not sure. My current instinct is that combining one high-quality probe on a fast asset and one of some variety on a heavier asset (something like a Gargoyle K which won't evaporate quickly under fire) would probably be helpful; if you go past two I think you're probably limiting your unit pool too much for too little gain. This merits further investigation, and I might write a BattleTechnical article with my findings once I have some more scanning experience.

The topic of how to rank players in BattleTech tournaments has come up several times recently in communities I'm active in, and my opinion continues to crystallize in the direction of feeling that individual game wins must be factored in. I understand a lot of the arguments against... with 84 players and three rounds, Southern Assault would've expected to see a minimum of ten undefeated players, for instance... but that's where tiebreakers come in, and total points scored makes a lot more sense as a tiebreak than as the sole determiner of placement, in my opinion.

I understand, too, that points scored are a measure of skill. The big issue with placing purely off points, however, is that a player who eked out a very close win over seriously difficult opposition ends up looking worse than a player who scored a blowout against a weak opponent, or, in a worst-case scenario, than a pair of friends who agree to run up their score together. Prioritizing points also punishes players whose games ran slowly, since fewer rounds played is fewer opportunities to score... I do realize that particular complaint is self-serving, and discouraging slow play is obviously a desirable outcome for TOs,  but it is a factor.

I've run events on the TO's side of things myself; I know it's hard to settle on just how to score things, and I know there's no perfect answer. Unless you somehow have time for a full Round-Robin, every ranking of players is going to be a rough guesstimate in some regards. I just firmly believe that win-loss record is the best starting point.

Still, if fast games and high scores are what the people want... I can try that. Peri has been a big advocate of high-speed, high-damage buzzsaw lists lately, and while my last flirtation with that archetype didn't go very well, it's worth another go. If you race forward like a screaming berserker and focus fire, your games will end quickly, one way or the other...

Southern Assault was almost certainly our last tournament of the year, but I've heard tell that the packets for the 2027 outings of Carolina Classic and Brawl in the Bluegrass will be released soon, and I'm certainly looking forward to seeing what list restrictions I have to build towards. Who knows, maybe I'll see you at one those events! I look forward to it. Until then, don't worry; once you find your center, you are sure to win!
