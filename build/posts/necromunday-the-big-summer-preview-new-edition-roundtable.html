Necromunday: The Big Summer Preview "New Edition" Roundtable

June 29th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Games Workshop's Big Summer Preview showed off an incoming box for Necromunda, bringing with it a brand new edition. We brought out the extended Necromunday family to chat about the minis, the terrain, and what we want from the future of the game.

Credit: Games Workshop

GW has announced a new edition of Necromunda, along with a full rules reset. What is your first impression?

Fowler: I just finished collecting all of the books a month ago. Alexander wept.

Ezekyle Catbaddon: I am so excited, I cannot wait to see what new nonsense will be added, what old stuff will be kept. Here’s hoping for a new edition that’s just as silly, and with twice as many charts!

Hive Market Minis: Were my new Ogryn Venator dreams just born and buried within the same preview? But hey, at least the dice didn’t change.

Primaris Kevin: I’m cautiously optimistic. There's a lot to love about Necromunda, but it can be overwhelming. My initial response was negative, but after thinking about it I had a mental picture in a newspaper clipping titled “Old Man Yells At Toxic Gas Cloud” and decided to be a bit more open minded. Also why is it called Necromunda: Skirmish? Are we going to get Necromunda: Apocalypse later? If GW eventually gives us Titan rules I’m not going to complain. 

SRM: I don’t usually clamor for a new edition of a game, but after nearly 9 years of material for this game, I’m happy to see it go back to basics and hopefully clean some stuff up. Necromunda 2017 has a great ruleset at its core, but nearly a decade of stuff is hard to keep track of, and likely daunting for a new player. Hell, it’s daunting for me and I’ve been playing Necromunda on and off since like 2012. Over that time, I could argue that it’s lost some of its core identity (idiot schmucks with duct taped together weapons contracting hyper-tetanus in the sump where death is around every corner) and given way to trained specialists and Mad Max vehicle combat. That’s all fun, mind you, but not what I initially came to Necromunda for.

Michael O “Mugginns”: I love Necromunda, so I’m happy for more. I’m just worried that the two ton stack of books I have for Necromunda is all worthless now. I really hope not! Of course, being a historical gamer and overall rulebook collector, there are always things you can use no matter what. The new Goliaths look amazing!

Genghis: This is not at all what I expected to see for Necromunda in the preview, but I'm cautiously optimistic. Ultimately, changes to the core game - which I love dearly, but has a host of issues I try to house rule or minimise as an Arbitrator - are going to make much more impact on my gaming experience than any new splatbook.

DYLON: I love games that never change - because my Swiss-cheese brain can just play them, like Necro and BattleTech. A part of me is upset to have to learn new rules and the obsolescence of ALL the books and cards. But...the new minis are amazing and jive nicely with the old, and let’s be real, there is room for improvement, so off we go! 

Manofwaaagh: It feels perhaps a little bit too soon. We’ve been bombarded with rules over the past few years, and I think many veteran players will have wanted more time to try the things that are already out there. That being said, there are good holistic arguments for a reset to try and simplify entrance for new players, cut down on rules creep and overhaul some core mechanics.

head58: I’ve said before that Necromunda is a game that you have to love in spite of itself, and I’m excited to see a more simplified and coherent package. The last few years have just felt like a mess without a strong editorial hand on the tiller. Credit: 40kHamslam

Looking out at the end of the edition, where are we at? 

(The timeline of the previous edition: 2017 launch, almost immediate 2018 re-stack, major errata, 2023 rulebook.)

Hive Market Minis: I have been really happy with the state of play since the 2023 rulebook landed, making this a bittersweet reveal for me. We have a nice variety of campaigns, a staggering amount of gangs, and a thick stack of Apocrypha supplements. No part of me was hoping for a new edition, especially having built so many models and gangs on the current fringes of the rules, and I would have been thrilled with another N23-style refresh. That said, the game has something like 1,800 pages of material kicking around (per Fowler), which is not exactly accessible to new players without a handful of the excellent resources floating around the web. Distilling that down to a few books would certainly benefit the Necro-curious.

Michael O “Mugginns”: Necromunda is an amazing ‘your dudes’ game that I’ve been playing since I was 12, when it first came out. It’s still a great game. The hard part is it’s difficult for newbies to get into - there are so many books, so much source material, so many different options. We usually start a campaign and half or more of the people don’t even have a rulebook. It’d be great for it to be easier to get into. 

Genghis: It is indeed a massive, sprawling game at the moment, which has pros and cons. I think that level of detail, and the investment it represents for committed players/collectors, is making the initial public reaction one of wariness. People don't want to have their existing books invalidated. I personally think change is worth that loss - my Necromunda books are worth more to me for inspiration, lore, and campaign ideas than as rules references - but the new Necromunda skirmish will need some time to reach anything like the same amount of supported options. 

Primaris Kevin: Near the end of 9th Edition of 40K I remember playing an army that included Space Marines, Imperial Knights, and Assassins. This army was not good, but it was the only way that I could cobble together enough points to actually play a full size game. There were so many rules, datasheets, and stratagems that I put together a binder that looked more like an engineering manual than a game reference. That’s where Necromunda feels like right now. While it’s fun for someone who has been playing this stupid little game since 1998, a reboot and refresh is definitely needed.

DYLON: Necromunda is my favorite game. I’ve managed to play 18 games of it this year so far and I’m finally playing a campaign with a friend. I even got to introduce a new player to it this year and they’ve fallen in love with it.

Manofwaaagh: The breadth of the rules is frankly incredible at this point, and Necromunda truly feels like it lets you represent just about anything at a gang skirmish level. Obviously this is potentially overwhelming, but I think the extent to which this shuts out new players can be overstated: the system is crunchy but one or two books is still usually all that is needed to get started. It’s a great game, but in some ways it’s become like the classic idea of a corporate IT stack resting on some ancient Fortran code — no matter how many new things get added, the melee combat system is still oversimplistic and vehicles were, in my view, badly integrated. So there’s room for foundational improvement.

head58: I’ve seen seasoned veteran gamers get turned off by the complexity and sprawl of the game. I’m still not convinced that the Ash Wastes vehicle rules were ever playtested. The Spyrers felt like they were pushing the game too far beyond the scrappy gangers in the underhive aesthetic (yes I know they were there in the Old Days). And I don’t even want to talk about Corpse Grinders. The 2023 core book was a great step toward cleanup but the game really needs a full strip down and rebuild, especially if it’s going to bring in new gamers.

Fowler: I’ve run six campaigns at my game club, along with a handful of weekenders and events. Since 2017, I have probably played 200 games of Necro. If this is going to be a fundamental re-rack, I sure as hell got a lot out of the old edition. Hopefully, porting over old content will be pretty simple as well, which will leave us a ton of missions and modifiers to play with.

Credit: Hive Market Minis

What do you think about the new box?

Primaris Kevin: My first impression was that it looked a little thin on terrain, but taking a second look I’ve changed my mind. As always the models look fantastic and display a high level of quality. This is definitely a direct replacement of the original Underhive box, but the scatter terrain means it’s useful for even veteran players. You can never have enough scatter terrain. NEVER.

Fowler: The new ganger models are great, but look at how many open hands there are! Typically, Necro kits are stingy with non-weapon options. Some gangs are especially cursed with little access to hands without a pistol or knife. Your rookie might only have a stubgun and a dream.

Ezekyle Catbaddon: Oh my GODDDD I love the look of some of the new models. An Escher with belt-fed autopistols? A Goliath with a Heavy Stubber? I already wanted to collect more gangs/models for each gang, now I have 8 more reasons.

Hive Market Minis: I’m with Fowler on team Open Hands, and the new models are gorgeous. They certainly didn’t demilitarize either of these gangs with both sporting flamers and beefy guns, dashing hope for a new edition getting back to an “in the gutters” feel. The terrain is as nice as always, though it’s sparse for anyone used to battling it out on Zone Mortalis or Sector Mechanicus boards. The tokens rock, and it looks like a solid introductory box if the price is right.

SRM: As a longtime player of the House of Himbos, I’m glad my Goliaths are gonna get some new guys to further flesh out my gang, and these new minis look fantastic, no surprise there. I’m just happy to see both gangs get more Ganger level models here. Past sets have focused on new Aspirant and Champion archetypes, and its felt like the regular ol’ Gangers got left in the dust. It’s definitely opening up more design space for each gang to expand further, as we’ll undoubtedly see similar expansions for everyone else. The terrain looks good too, and will definitely end up in my 40k layouts as well, but it did look a bit slight while arrayed on that fold-out mat.

Michael O “Mugginns”: I love the new minis - especially the Goliaths, as a Goliath since day one - and I’m excited to grab some. The terrain and mat are a big eh to me - it doesn’t look like nearly enough to play a game. I guess I’ll paint it and have more for us to play on our regular boards?

Genghis: I'm also a Goliath more than an Escher player, and what intrigues me is that these sculpts are more dynamic and individualised than the existing sprue, which is a fairly restrained set of poses. They're also more shredded but perhaps less blocky than the originals, although we'd need more pictures. That original sprue doesn't seem to be going anywhere though, so new players can always mix them up. They have the same design language of the furnace plates and general kit, so should look good together.

Addressing Kev's fears on the apparent lack of terrain, I would remind readers that the 2017 box just had a set of barricades, the bulk of the terrain effect in the game came from walls printed onto the cardboard tiles it came with. There might easily be similar aspects to the Necromunda mat in this new set.

Primaris Kevin: I’m just spoiled by Dark Uprising, Hive War, and Ash Wastes. Don’t mind me.

Manofwaaagh: The new minis look awesome. I’m operating on the assumption that these new gangers are (in a model range sense) the Goliath / Escher equivalent of the Van Saar Tek-hunters who came with the Hive Secundus box, and that we’ll get similar options for the other House gangs down the line. Working on that basis, I really hope they’ll play nicely for kitbashing with the old kits. The only downside of this is that it might mean we’re stuck with neckless Orlocks forever. Reading through to the rules, it indicates a new model in which every fighter is more unique — obviously Necromunda was close to this point anyway, but regular gangers could end up feeling quite vanilla. Hopefully it’s all still sandbox-y though, and we’re not just heading for a kind of Kill Team: Underhive in which all the gangs have a kind of preset roster.

head58: The new box is…fine? I agree that the new minis look good but I’m not thrilled by them doing Goliath/Escher again. I don’t see anything that makes me need to preorder this box when it goes up - more likely to just get the rulebook separately. I’m glad they haven’t done away with templates, but I do wish we could get away from needing special proprietary dice.  Credit: Games Workshop

How about that new terrain?

Genghis: Don't make me do this GW, I have huge piles of unpainted terrain already. But I have a mighty need for that scatter terrain. It's incredibly detailed, which I guess won't be a problem if I just never get round to painting it. 

Fowler: It looks great. My only complaint is that I wish there was more of it. I hope the box has some way to add more LOS blocking.

Primaris Kevin: It looks great, and I’m excited to inevitably buy too much of it to fit inside yet another 27 gallon tote full of future potential. Scatter terrain is great because you can put it anywhere, especially this stuff. Need something to block the top of a Sector Mechanicus walkway? Scatter terrain. Need something to fit between your Munitorium Armored Containers? Need something to sit next to your pile of scatter terrain? Scatter terrain. 

DYLON: Lovely little scatter bits. You can never have too much scatter. The stairs over the pipes are cute. 

Grinding to a halt as the final Hull Point is removed, the Cawdor win. Credit: 40khamslam.

What do you want from a new edition of Necromunda?

Primaris Kevin: What I want the most is for GW to stop repeating the same content over and over again in every book. I really want to see a campaign system that doesn't require an extensive amount of modification to be fair and accessible. There are a lot of different ways to play a campaign, but unless the person running it keeps an eye on things, snowballing can easily make it so that some players just don't bother keeping up. It would also be nice if every gang had an equal amount of opportunity to access the Trading Post. Gangs like Slave Ogryns really suffer from their arbitrary access rules.

I’m clearly biased, but after several years of trial and effort I would say the fundamentals that we’re using in the 2026 Goonhammer Open Necromunda Weekender campaign are one of the best ways to play Necromunda. It also reflects how flexible the campaign system is. Hopefully N26 keeps the same format with a strong and robust set of combat rules that can be applied to a variety of narrative structures. 

Ezekyle Catbaddon: Somewhat Piggybacking off of Kevin, I think not only a new campaign that doesn’t need as many extra bits glued onto it, but maybe a whole new flavor to campaigns? Like a multi-level system with different tiers of complexity. If you have new players or want a really simple campaign, some simple rules and upkeep needs that lower the chance of new players becoming overwhelmed and bowing out. Or if you have some very experienced players and/or weirdos (like me) who like lots of charts and bullshit that requires trigonometry to work out, choose the complex ruleset. Having a variable amount of complexity to one campaign system would both allow for increased replayability and a better chance of getting your friends into the game!

Hive Market Minis: Everyone I was frantically chatting with during the reveal was spooked by one word: "Streamlined." I love the RPG crunch of the current iteration, and hope that stays intact. Beyond that, I do think there’s a lot of room to turn down the power creep we’ve gotten over the years, with a number of models able to reliably chew up and spit out enemy fighters on a 2+. Melee combat could certainly use some tweaking on that front to create a little more back and forth, and I would be open to cranking up the overall lethality if general reliability was turned down on close combat or at long range shots. I’ve always enjoyed the spirit of the vehicle rules, but in my experience, folks would rather not play with them in casual games or at events. I would love to see that revisited and possibly even…streamlined?

Going back to the basics across the board could be nice, and revisiting elements of the game that never felt fully balanced or baked, like the heap of underwhelming skills or custom vehicle rules, would be an easy win. I’ve always been a fan of the Warcry and Kill Team wound systems to further differentiate, say, a Bonepicker from a Bruiser, but I’m not sure if that would actually simplify play.

SRM: I also hope Necromunda stays crunchy, I just want it to be better organized. Necromunda is where I want gangers to get shot, catch on fire, run in a random direction off a ledge, land on another ganger, set them on fire, and have a lasting spine injury after the fact. If I want a streamlined, clean and competitive sci-fi skirmish game, Kill Team is right there. I do hope balance does get touched on though. In the same way Ganger models were left behind after the initial launch, after a certain point they just became cannon fodder for someone’s tooled up 250 credit Champion. 

Michael O "Mugginns": I’d really like for them to clean up some of the issues with the game, like Fire being crazy, gangs that seem forgotten (like GSC). I would also love it if the game went more basic - like dudes feel like pieces of shit rather than super heavy amazing warriors. 

Genghis: There are almost too many issues for me to list here, and that's the major reason I'm keen for these new rules, even if it means losing some of the options that have built up since 2018. A lot of that has to do with the balancing of credit costs and effects on fighters, weapons, armour and equipment. Players that are bemoaning this change, do you really want to play with Infiltrating Web Guns for a few more years? Wouldn't it be nice to shake up those confirmed opinions on what's good and bad?

Fowler: I would love to see a better way to implement captured gangers, rescue missions, and sentries in general.

Genghis: I can't speak for whether they will make Necromunda more basic/low-powered. Personally I doubt it, I suspect the model designers have a lot more fun putting funkier things than broken down stub guns into kits, and then the rules demand those things be widely usable. But I would love to see a system where fighters skills had more of an impact than stats, and where weapons were more granular, so that the best weapons in the game were good personal weapons, rather than military anti tank stuff. Like HMM, I don't think the word ‘streamlined’ sparks joy, and I hope it's just corporate buzz speak. One of the most wished-for changes to N18, for example, is a combat system similar to old Necromunda, where the defender's weapon skill mattered, rather than the rocket tag we have now, where a strong champion will paste most targets on the charge. 

Fowler: Mordheim has been my game of choice lately, and I really enjoy the interaction of Weapon Skill and to-hit. For Necro I may want a bit more granularity than (most) everything hitting on a 3 or 4 in melee, but it’s nice that being good at fighting can benefit defensively.

Genghis: But my biggest desired changes are probably to the mission and campaign structure. Necro gameplay on the table is a very robust system, fun despite balance issues. Where things often fall apart is the mission design - almost no missions go past both gangs slamming into each other until one Bottles out. The existing system for picking a mission and staking Territory over the game is also very basic, which isn’t a huge problem for heavy arbitrated or narrative campaigns, but will limit the experience for many groups. Aside from mechanical changes, all that mission and campaign layer stuff could be far better explained and laid out. 

Manofwaaagh: Some great points there. I don’t have a strong view on the mooks vs militaries debate (to me arbitrators have always had the power to clamp down on plasma spam etc. on that if they want). But I think there’s handful of rule areas that are distinctly ripe for improvement though, of which three jump out (although there are plenty more).

Firstly, as mentioned above, melee: the current Necromunda ruleset has way too many fighters who are borderline guaranteed to kill on the charge, regardless of their target. There must be a better way of doing this. Secondly, vehicles. The “physics” of being hit by a vehicle, or jumping on/off one, are weird (because the rules do not reflect momentum / speed, only max speed), some vehicles are way too hard to kill, and vehicle damage tables are overcomplicated (there’s no other part of the rules I find myself having to look up mid-game so often). Thirdly, the cost balance of arms/armour vs equipment has basically always been skewed too much towards the former, and it would be great to incentivise players more to carry interesting equipment without it feeling so much like self-sabotage. Again, the VS Tek-hunters’ specialised gear suggest some good thinking in this kind of direction.

The other big thing is this: The current edition of Necromunda has consistently been riddled with inconsistencies, editing errors and rules holes. Is it too much to ask for GW to do a bit better this time around?

Finally, I don’t want to lose the versatile new-style Venators so soon, so I hope they return quickly in the new edition.

head58: I really want better consistency and balance. Have someone looking at the whole line holistically to make sure that some gangs just don’t stomp others, to minimize weird rule contradictions. And I don’t want to have to buy a ton of books to just play one gang (but I know I will). 

Also I want a darn digital version of the rulebook again! Being able to search through the rulebook without having to rely on a shady third party document was the best thing from the 2023 book. With Warhammer Digital going away, I’m afraid that isn’t going to happen.

Necromunda. The only game where people will hate a model so much they will offer real money to see it dead. And yes, that's a horrifying cyber-monstrosity attempting to ram the Land Train to claim the bounty. Credit: Kevin Genson

Any parting thoughts?

Ezekyle Catbaddon: Maybe a question about new campaign options? I feel like maybe a campaign creator type deal might be cool? Not a thought, just an idea for another question!

SRM: I'm guessing we're going to see the same cycle of the "Gangs of _" books giving way to the "House of _" books as the edition goes on. I’m curious if vehicle combat is going to be in the main rules too, but it’s likely going to be saved for a future Ash Wastes-esque expansion. Lastly, the Underhive Crew boxes just seem like a handy way to get a gang in a box. Anything that makes this game easier for people to get into is a win in my book, and I’m glad it's still getting some love and support.

Genghis Cohen: Yeah, I'm interested to see if they keep the individual gang boxes (and specialist/champion boxes) in circulation alongside. Those are strictly speaking a lower cost of entry into the game. But one of the most common questions I see from new players is some variant of ‘what boxes do I need for this gang?’ so it seems like a good change. 

Hive Market Minis: Pour out a shot of Second Best for the good times so far, and cheers to whatever stories this next edition might help tell. I still don’t fully understand calculating bottle checks.

Manofwaaagh: I’m hoping we enter a (literally) more lightweight system in which we get new rules via Blood Bowl-style journals. I’m assuming that a lot of models won’t get updated rules for a while, and expect Dramatis Personae will be the biggest victims of this. But I think GW know what they’re doing, and understand what Necromunda players are after, so I’m confident everything will come good in the end. And, if not, the old rules aren’t going anywhere.

Fowler: My campaigns will still play Murder Cyborg, and Fuel Hunt, and Claim the Spire, regardless of their inclusion in the new books. If the minute-to-minute play is good, I’m here for whatever is next.

head58: At the end of the day we really don’t know anything concrete, do we? WarCom says “new edition” and “refreshed rules” but we have no idea how deep that goes. Despite really enjoying some top shelf performative salt this weekend I think people need to chill until we get more details. Anyone being surprised that GW is launching a new edition of a game and that their shelf of books will soon be useless hasn’t been paying attention to how games (not just GW, not just minis) work.  Have any questions or feedback?
