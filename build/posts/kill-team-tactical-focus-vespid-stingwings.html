[Kill Team] Tactical Focus: Vespid Stingwings

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In this series of Kill Team articles we take a deep dive into one of the available teams, analyzing each team's strengths and weaknesses, how to set up important plays, and ultimately what to consider to pilot the team to victory. Note that this article assumes you already have knowledge of the basic rules for each team, and will reference abilities and ploys from that team throughout. If you want to learn more about the rules for each team you can find the most up to date version of the rules on the Warhammer Community website here. 

Live to fly, fly to live, do or die. Not only are these the lyrics to the chorus of Iron Maiden’s Aces High, but the motto of Kill Team’s insectoid shooting menace, the Vespid Stingwings. Carried aloft on buzzing wings and dealing death with advanced neutron weaponry, the Vespid Stingwing Auxillia of the T’au Empire are some of the most dangerous hit-and-run shock troops that the Greater Good has at its disposal. With the help of advanced stimulants and communication tools from their handler, the Vespid can override their natural predatory instincts to become organized, efficient, and dangerous killers in any killzone.

In Kill Team, Vespid are a team of severe limitations and incredible strength, focusing on a fast and aggressive playstyle that can send even the toughest teams reeling when played correctly. We will break down just how the Vespid Stingwings do this so well here in this tactical deep dive on Vespids, introducing how they play, what they’re good and bad at, what plays and ploys to use, what matchups and mission play to consider, and how to bring it all together to claim victory in the name of the Greater Good.    

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Basic Overview

Like almost anything hailing from the T’au Empire, the Vespid are primarily a shooting team first and foremost, with ten of the eleven operatives the team can bring rocking some serious dangerous firepower with neutron guns, all of which have Devastating 2 on their crit damage profile. Coupled with the Neutron Charge ability to give any neutron weapon piercing if they move prior to shooting, the Vespid are one of the most dangerous shooting teams in the current metagame, with even your basic warriors acting as serious threats to elite operatives. Neutron Charge pairs extremely well with Fly, which basically lets you remove an Vespid and place them back within their movement range when they reposition (or within 3” for a Dash), ignoring all intervening terrain and operatives when they do so. This unrestricted movement makes Vespid one of the fastest teams in the game, especially on open maps and when paired with the Darting Flight ploy or Accelerant Stimulant equipment, able to stage in complete safety before suddenly appearing to deal an eye watering amount of damage to a target.

To get the most out of your team though, you need to override their instinctive behaviors through Communion. Every turn, you generate d3 communion points, with an extra point if the Oversight Drone is alive. These points must be spent to perform certain actions that a Vespid operative normally would be unable to, such as shooting a target outside of 8” or isn’t the closest target, performing mission actions (excluding open hatchway), or charging operatives that aren’t the closest enemy operative. Vespid can also spend a Communion point for one free reroll when shooting, which is often handy considering the team’s lackluster hit stats across the board.

Vespid Strain Leader. Credit: Jack Hunter

As far as operatives go, your Strain Leader is one of two main support operatives on the team, able to perform mission actions without spending a communion point or hand out an out of activation mission action to another vespid within 6” for a communion point, plus giving you access to a free strategy ploy of your choice for the whole game as long as he’s alive. The Oversight Drone is the other support piece, who does almost no damage on their own but can project a 6” aura of Lethal 5+ and Saturate to friendly Vespid operatives within 6”, provided the target they’re shooting at is visible. 

While every Vespid is a damage dealer with neutron weapons, some are even more threatening than others. The Longsting has a sniper rifle that can absolutely flatten whatever it shoots at with it’s heavy profile, and leaves a lingering damage effect to any target that somehow survives. The Skyblast has an AoE neutron weapon that can catch multiple operatives in it’s blast 2” radius as well as dropping a neutron fallout marker in control range of the target, dealing damage to any enemy inside the area during their activation. The Shadestrain is the team tank, and though it’s guns are the shortest range of all, it’s ability to be completely untargetable outside of 6” while on a conceal order, even out in the open, combined with the Shadestrains ability to ignore all piercing and take a cover save as a critical save makes them just a pain to remove. 

The humble Warrior meanwhile is a dangerous and reliable damage dealer for the team, giving accurate 1 if you don’t use any Communion points when they activate, which does wonders for flattening the damage curve, even more dangerous since you can take multiple warriors. Finally, the Swarmguard is the only operative without a neutron weapon, opting for a flamer that makes it more situational than it’s peers, but its Skytorch Assault ability can be used to damage operatives on the move that would otherwise be impossible to target, or even bail out any of your operatives trapped in melee.

Vespid. Credit: Rockfish

Team Strengths and Weaknesses

The number one strength of Vespid Stingwings is just how wildly dangerous every single operative is on this team. The most basic neutron weapon, the Neutron Blaster on Warriors, is 4 shots at 3/3 dev 2, made even deadlier by the fact it’s probably going to also have piercing 1, and with some clever positioning also rocks lethal 5+ and saturate. Remember, that's your baseline on this team, the specialist guns are often even scarier than that. Combined with the high model count, vespids are one of the premiere trading teams in Kill Team, often able to punch way above their weight when they do commit to the fight. If you spike even a little with Vespid shooting, dropping a full health Space Marine in one shooting action is very doable. Plus, if you don’t quite get the kill, you can the plentiful rerolls the team has access to from Communion points and the Airborne Predators ploy to fish for better damage, or push through extra damage on a crit with Neutron Overload in a pinch at point blank range.

While not nearly as adept in combat as they are in shooting, Vespid are surprisingly competent at combat when they need to be, thanks to the Sting strategy ploy giving operatives improved hit stats in combat, along with lethal 5+ and shock. Vicious Venom, like Neutron Overload, helps push through extra damage on a combat crit and if you’re really desperate the Aggression Stimulant equipment hands you ceaseless when fighting. Its still only 3 attacks, so proper melee teams will just blend you, but in a pinch Vespid can still get work done in combat as well as shooting.    

Fly only makes their offense even more dangerous, since it allows for you to move around the killzone with near total freedom (unless its through close quarters hatchways). This makes hiding from Vespid or slowing them down with equipment very difficult, and it means that Vespid players can lean hard on equipment choices like barricades, razor wire, and mines to jam up key lanes on the killzone while you just move right through them. 

Credit: Paulie Wallis

As if all this wasn’t enough strengths, Vespid are even surprisingly durable for a horde team. Most models on the team have nine wounds base, with the leader rocking an extra wound. The only exception to this is the Oversight Drone, who only has 5 wounds but ignores all piercing and has super conceal against targets outside of 2”. Vespid armor save is pretty lackluster at 5+, but between Hardened Exoskeleton to reduce normal damage in combat and Aerial Agility to give you a way to possibly ignore damage against enemies that move before they shoot or are counteract/guard shooting, Vespid can take a surprising amount of firepower to take down, making their main game plan of aggressive piece trading even stronger.

So what’s the catch? Well, with the lofty highs of Vespid also come some pretty crushing lows. The first drawback is the one most players will feel the most keenly, which is Vespid’s generally poor shooting accuracy. Your guns may be some of the most dangerous in the game, but almost every operative on your team hits on a 4+ base. Anyone who’s played a faction with a 4+ base hit stat knows just how wildly inconsistent this is. You will live by the 4+, and oftentime you will die by the 4+ when you whiff hard and fall on your face. Vespid have quite a few ways to mitigate this, like easy access to rerolls and even accurate 1 from warriors or the Occeli ploy, but in the end the best way to defend against the wildly swingy shooting is by making sure you overlap your threats carefully, so that when you miss badly with one operative (and it’s not an if, mind you, it is a when), you have another operative (or several) in the wings with their wings ready to try again.

The next standout problem Vespids have to deal with is that they are one of the very few true 2 APL teams. Unlike most other 2 APL teams, who have a comms-style operative to hand out APL boosts to key operatives or even natural access to a 3 APL operative, Vespid have absolutely no way to boost their APL stat at all. This means that despite having more operatives than most other teams, Vespid action economy is at a premium. Any crit op or tac op that requires heavy AP investment to score is a big commitment for this team, and you’ll need to be mindful of that when playing the game or you’ll find yourself shutting yourself out of points. 

Finally, Communion restrictions are a problem the team does have to work around, but oftentimes as long as you’re playing mindfully you’ll have plenty of Communion points. Still, not having Communion points when you need them is back breaking, and the best spot any Vespid player wants to be in is having an excess of points that can be spent on Communion rerolls for shooting. Some best practices for managing Communion is to use as little as possible turning points 1 and 2 before going wild on turning point 3 and beyond, keeping your leader on your home objective to perform a mission action each turn without spending a communion point, and to take either the Neurostimulant equipment or Convergence Stimulant. Taking both is almost certainly overkill, so rule of thumb take Convergence Stimulant for any Crit Op that requires high APL investment, like Loot, and Neurostimulant for the ones that don’t, like Secure.  

Credit: Can You Roll a Crit?

Standout Tactics

Some teams like to win games of Kill Team by playing cagey, minimizing interaction with their opponent, and using their toolkit to take advantage of the Tac Op and Crit Op to win while keeping the violence to a minimum. 

Vespid are not that team. Vespid win by kicking the enemy teams teeth in. You trade up hard, trade up often, and by the end of the game your opponent just doesn’t have enough steam left to stop you from winning. 

The first step in controlling the trade game is controlling the battlefield, and for that equipment is your friend. Barricades, razor wire, and even mines can and should be used to build a maze and limit where your opponent can go freely on the killzone, and while also making it harder for them to charge hug your Vespid to stay safe. The Skyblast’s Neutron Bombardment ability gives Vespids even more battlefield control tools on top equipment blocking, since a well placed fallout marker will be a headache for your opponent all game long. Since you don’t need to do damage to drop a marker either, its worth taking shots with the Skyblast you may not normally take, like shooting into an obscured target or shooting without having moved first. Dropping a fallout marker on a choke point or an objective will have your opponent pulling their hair out for the rest of the game. 

Credit: Can You Roll a Crit?

In most cases, Vespid will out activate the enemy team, which means you can wait to activate your most dangerous operatives until your opponent can’t do anything to stop you. Generally, this means you’re getting the party started at the end of the second turning point once your opponent has done all their activating. It also means turning point 2 is the best time to play Aerial Agility, since you always get the 5+ ignore normal damage ability on both counteract shots and guard shots. The Shadestrain in particular is really good at playing forward early on in the game since it can’t be selected as a valid target outside of 6” on conceal, even if they’re in the open, and their anti-piercing ability and 3+ armor save makes them very hard to shift even if they do get a shot.

Once you get the pressure up, the Warriors and the Longsting are the best damage dealers, especially when the Warriors are near the Oversight Drone. Since the Drone’s Aerial Guidance ability is a support ability, it greatly benefits from a well-placed Comms Device to boost that buff bubble to 9”, though you’ll want to remember to place the drone both far out of harms way as well as somewhere where it can see the enemy, since Aerial Guidance’s buffs do require visibility. This will mean you’ll lose operatives in the trading game, but the great news here is Vespid benefit greatly from the very weird Kill Grade breakpoints for 11 operative teams. Your opponent has to put down 7 Vespid before they hit Kill Grade 3, which gives you plenty of breathing room to stay ahead of your opponent on the Kill Op. 

Generally, try to avoid using CP on rerolls, since you have plenty of reroll access with Communion and Airborne Predators and several very strong firefight ploys you’ll want to use often, particularly Occeli. Occeli is a very confusingly worded ploy, but once you make it through the word salad and understand what it does it’s probably the best tools in the Vespid kit. Basically, you get to be on pretend vantage that's 3” higher than its current spot for the shooting activation, which has two very strong uses. First, and most importantly, you can shoot operatives hiding in light cover, which is outstanding on close quarters maps since heavy cover is at a premium and there’s no natural vantage. Its other use is to gain accurate 1 against targets with an engage order, which is great if you really need to make sure you get damage through on an important shot. 

Lastly for this section, getting your expended operatives bully charged is very bad for Vespid, since it protects enemy operatives from shooting and sets them up for kills the next turning point, but you do have a very funny tool to bust you out of jail here: the Swarmguard. If you fly over your own operatives during a Skytorch Assault, you do shoot them as part of the move. Normally, damaging your own operatives is very bad. But, if you manage to kill the Vespid stuck in combat, suddenly that enemy operative who charged them is a sitting duck for the rest of your team to light up with neutron fire, making this an especially attractive option into elite teams. 

Credit: Happyracoon

Tac Ops and Crit Ops

As mentioned a few times now, Vespid are a team all about killing that don’t like doing mission actions, which makes the Crit Op their weakest of the three scoring metrics in Approved Ops 2025. While some Crit Ops are easier than others for Vespid, generally the best way for Vespid to do well on the Crit Op is by making sure their opponent does badly on the Crit Op by, you guessed it, killing them. Don’t try and stretch so thin to control all three objectives, rather just focus on your home objective with your Strain Leader and the middle objective, and then use those lanes to push into your opponent and apply pressure. The best time to try and score big on Crit Op is at the end of the game, where if you’ve played it right your opponent will be out of gas, giving you free rein to catch up on Crit Op points.

For Tac Ops, your archetypes are Seek and Destroy and Recon, which has a few great options for scoring that don’t require heavy APL investment. The two standout ploys are Dominate and Flank, with Dominate in particular being just brutal into elite teams right now thanks to Vespid’s activation advantage and propensity for violence. Flank leans on the other Vespid secret sauce, high mobility, and is great on open maps in particular for two reasons. First, it's real easy to score if you out activate your opponent by just flying on conceal into a vantage point in their territory. Second, your opponent has to come to you if they want to stop you from scoring, which makes picking them off very easy. Just be careful with Flank that you aren’t accidentally feeding your opponent operatives as well.

Rout and Retrieval are honorable mentions here. Rout is another good tac op focused on killing, but it's easier for your opponent to deny than Dominate or Flank. Retrieval is very safe, and is great for scoring at the end of the game. but also requires more APL investment than the other options which means you’ll need to have both operatives and Communion points to spare in order to get it done.

Scout Enemy Movement and Sweep and Clear are both way too reliant on APL to be useful for the team, just close your mind to both of these options and move on.

Matchup Considerations

Credit: Robert “TheChirurgeon” Jones

Elite matchups are your bread and butter, you are so good into these matchups. High damage coupled with high model count and mobility makes you great into almost any team you out activate, sure, but your damage being high enough to fry most big operatives real quick and just painfully out activating them means that some elite teams will just spend most of the game feeling straight up bullied. Ravenors and Stealth Suits can throw a wrench into this somewhat, since both teams can simply choose to not engage, but otherwise Vespid are one of Kill Team’s premiere elite killers in the metagame right now, space marines hate to see these bugs coming. 

As for problem matchups, any team that out activates Vespid can be a real pain for them, since it’s such a strong part of their toolkit. Brood Brothers, Kroot Farstalkers, and Inquisitorial Agents in particular are real tricky, since both can turn the same wait you out shooting tactics Vespid like to use most of the time back on you and getting double kills with Vespid is tricky. In these matchups, Sting and blast weapons are your friend, as charging an expended operative before killing them in the following fight phase before shooting another is the best way to get double kills, alongside blast and torrent. Goremongers are also a real problem since Runes of Khorne means you can’t kill them in one shooting activation, but for this matchup its important to remember your damage over time effects from the Longsting and Skyblast, as well as making sure you keep overlapping threats.

Finally, while Vespid are a menace on open maps with fly, close quarters maps like Tomb World can hamper their mobility options and can be difficult to stage safely. For these matches, setting up your equipment in key chokepoints is key, make sure you remember to open doors early so you don’t waste precious APL on your go turn, and make clever use of Ocelli to get shots into light cover. It’s important to remember when setting this equipment up, however, that you can’t fly through doors, so be careful not to block yourself out there. A Breaching Charge on Tomb World is also near-mandatory for this team, especially with the update to allow your leader to spend Communion points to have another operative within 3” do a 1 APL mission action. 

Vespid Stingwings Kill Team. Credit: Jack Hunter

Piloting the Team to Victory

Vespid aren’t a particularly subtle team, they want to fly over to you, shoot you in the face with high tech weapons, and then scoop up all the points once you’re dead. High five, praise the Greater Good, time to go to the space bar for space beers. 

That being said, there is a delicate art to neutron blasting the enemy, and its all about making sure that you’re evaluating the game state and staging in a way to make sure you’re trading up consistently with this team. Don’t push too hard too early and overextend yourself, while you may be tough for a horde team you’re still no Astartes, and losing activation advantage for Vespid is brutal to come back from. Stage early just out of reach, wait until your opponent is expended at the end of TP 2, and then strike out hard. Keep your team together to overlap their threats and overwhelm your opponent, which both mitigates the team’s accuracy problems and makes it harder to stop your momentum. Focus on bloodying the enemy team’s nose badly enough that they just don’t have the resources to stop you. Remember, they can’t stop you from standing on circles if all of their operatives are dead. Keep your opponent on the backfoot with Vespid mobility and firepower and don’t let up once you start, keep track of your Communion points, and you’ll find that Vespid can do some nasty work.

And, remember, you are at the mercy of a 4+. Do everything you can to mitigate it, but at the end of the day, the dice giveth, and the dice taketh away. Make sure you’ve got overlapping threats so that when the dice do taketh away, and they will taketh away, you’ve got a backup plan.

Conclusion

At the start of this edition, Vespid were probably one of the worst teams in the entire game. After multiple dataslate passes though, the team crawled up from the pits of despair, and now the Vespid Stingwings are a premiere team in the Kill Team metagame for their killing power and outstanding mobility.

If you love the idea of playing a fast and aggressive team where almost every single operative is exceptionally dangerous, then it’s hard to go wrong with Vespid. Fittingly for their insect nature, Vespid work best when they’re like a swarm of angry bees: fast, all over the place, and stinging you constantly from every direction. Except in this case, the stings are fancy neutron blasters. The Vespid move and shoot plan is simple, sure, but it is dangerously effective, and makes these alien auxilia some of the most dangerous warriors the Greater Good has to offer.
