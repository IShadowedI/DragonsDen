Getting Started with Star Wars Legion: Separatist Alliance

July 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone, to another episode in our Star Wars Legion series, where we cover the fundamentals of the game! This time, we're taking a look at one of the core factions of the game, the Separatist Alliance.

Thank you to Asmodee for sponsoring a series of articles on Star Wars: Legion.

What Is the Separatist Alliance?

The Separatist Alliance (AKA the Confederation of Independent Systems, or CIS) is a loose group of star systems that created a huge droid army and seceded from the Galactic Republic, causing the Clone Wars.They would fight a back-and-forth battle with the Republic for a few years, until the entire Separatist movement was revealed as nothing more than a plot from then-Senator Palpatine to allow him to consolidate political power and become Emperor.The CIS would meet a rather ignoble end as Anakin Skywalker would slaughter their leadership on Mustafar and the newly-reformed Galactic Empire would clean up the pieces.

Copyright LFL, AMG

Essential Media

At this point I think everyone knows they should go watch the Prequel Trilogy and the Clone Wars cartoon to see the Separatists in action, so I’d like to recommend a few other things.

Star Wars: Darth Maul - Black, White & Red (2024): A four-issue miniseries released by Marvel Comics. Each issue is a standalone story with a different creative team, all following the rule that the only color used beyond black and white is red. Basically four horror stories, as nothing goes well for anybody once they encounter Maul.

Star Wars: Darth Maul (2017): Another Marvel miniseries, this time by Cullen Bunn, Luke Ross and Nolan Woodard. It tells the story of Maul’s first time fighting a Jedi, and also features appearances from some fan favorites like Cad Bane and Aurra Sing. It’s pretty entertaining, and fleshes out Maul’s internal motivations a bit beyond just being angry all the time.

Star Wars: Age of Republic - General Grievous (2019): Jody Houser and Luke Ross bring us a Marvel Comics single-issue story about Grievous encountering an ancient Jedi temple. I like this one because it’s a reminder that Grievous is supposed to be really scary

How Do They Play?

The Separatist Alliance tends towards lists that have a lot of cheap, cannon-fodder corps units combined with a few heavy hitters that get most of the work done. They rely on out-activating their opponents by bringing more units on average than any other faction. Leaders in the CIS army are almost universally fantastic, so lists built around them tend to do well, whether that list is lead by Count Dooku and backed up by Droid Commandos, or by General Grievous on his Wheel Bike running around with a bunch of STAP Riders.

Star Wars Legion Separatist Crab Droids - Credit Bair

Strengths

Order Control: If you take care during list building and play it’s normal for CIS armies to have perfect order control, giving the player freedom to activate units in any order. Between B1 droids chaining orders to other units with Coordinate: Droid Trooper and multiple leaders with the ability to Direct other units, as long as you keep units relatively close to each other you’ll have little trouble issuing orders where you need them.

Versatility: If there’s a niche you need filled in your list, you can find it. If you need fast movers, you can take STAP Riders or Droideka. Scouting units? Droid Commandos. Long range firepower? AAT Battle Tanks. Lightsabers? Count Dooku, Darth Maul, Asajj Ventress. The list goes on and on, and with your corps units being so cheap you can afford to put a lot of fun stuff in your lists.

Activation Count: The Separatist Alliance has a dirt-cheap corps units in the B1 Battle Droids. It’s not uncommon to see lists that have taken 4-5 units of B1s to let them hit 12-13 activations in a round. This gives you a big advantage since they can hold their important units to act later in the turn, potentially taking advantage of any misplays by your opponent. There’s also something to be said for sheer weight of fire. A unit of B1s might not do much damage by itself, but get three of them going after the same target and you’ll see some results.

Weaknesses

AI: Most of the units in the CIS army have the AI keyword. This keyword means that if the unit doesn’t receive a face-up order and meets one of a few other conditions, you no longer get to choose what that unit does with its first action after activating. It must do one of the actions listed in the AI keyword on the card (For example, B1s have AI: Attack, meaning they must attack) or, if they can’t legally perform that action, they just lose the first action of the turn. This is what makes order control so important to CIS, you really don’t want to lose actions if you can help it.

Individually Weak Units: Most of your units are pretty weak on their own. As a CIS player you really have to learn how to leverage your numbers advantage over your opponent and coordinate actions between units to get tasks done. This can be a tough adjustment if you’re used to playing games where you have a couple of really good units and then a bunch of chaff that supports them. You have to learn when a “throwaway” corps unit of B1s is really ok to throw away and when you need to work to keep them alive to maintain your activation advantage.

How Do I Get Into Them?

Image courtesy Asmodee

We covered a Starter set list for the Separatist Alliance, as well as the other factions, in a previous article here, along with a quick overview of how the list looks to perform.

Starter Box CIS Recon

Commander: General Grievous with Improvised Orders, Inspiring Presence, Up Close and Personal, and Prepared Supplies

Corps: 2x B1 Battle Droids with E-5C B1 Battle Droid, B1 Battle Droid Squad, and Hacked Comms Unit

Corps: B2 Super Battle Droids with B2-ACM Battle Droid, B2 Super Battle Droid Squad, and HQ Upling

Support: Droidekas with HQ Uplink

Legion Separatist AAT - Credit Bair

What Else Do They Have?

The Separatist Alliance has so, so much else to offer. They’ve got a pair of big tanks in the AAT Battle Tank and the Persuader-class Tank Droid. There are plenty of lighter vehicles between the STAP Riders, Spider Droids, Crab Droids, and Wheel Bikes.

If you like specialized infantry units, you’ve got a lot of options there as well. You can take Commando Droids to give you both a scouting melee threat and a sniper team. Magnaguard are a good bodyguard unit to give your melee Commanders some help with their Guardian skill and they can pack a punch if you give them an Electro-Whip. If you want an infiltrating brick wall that will frustrate your opponent unless they bring enough anti-tank to deal with them, you can take Aqua Droids, and have them lock down an area of the board with their mere presence.

The Alliance also has a great roster of leaders that enable a bunch of different playstyles, from the various Super Tactical Droids to Count Dooku. There’s also what amounts to an entirely separate army you can field if you buy Sun Fac, Poggle the Lesser and all their Geonosians, although that’s more of a specialized theme army when you’re just starting out.

Battle Forces

We cover the Battle Forces in more detail in this article, but to briefly summarize them:

  • Separatist Invasion Force: Represents a droid army invading a planet, like the one that lands on Naboo in The Phantom Menace. It’s a pretty vanilla Battle Force, with the major change being that you can only bring B1s for corp units, but you can bring eight of them instead of the normal limit of six. It’s for people who really love horde armies.
  • Rapid Interdiction Force: It’s Mad Max time! Grab all your wheel bikes, because that’s what this Battle Force is all about. Led by Grievous on his wheel bike, this list wants you to pack as many vehicles into it as you can, backed up by Commando Droids which now serve as your Corp units.
  • Experimental Droids: This Battle Force is a big strange compared to the rest. It’s built around unique types of Magnaguard and Persuader Tanks that are only available to this Battle Force that completely changes how they play. The Magnaguard become a mainline melee unit instead of playing a bodyguard role, and the Tank gets the ability to direct Trooper units and become a kind of secondary commander. A good force to play with after you’ve gotten tired of using the normal versions of these units.
B1 Battle Droids. Credit: Rockfish

Expanding the Starter Set

I put together this list with two thoughts in mind: The most cost efficient way to get more B1 units is to buy another starter box, and there are four CIS Commanders and Operatives getting repackaged together in (as of this writing) September 2026, so they shouldn’t be purchased before that comes out.

To that end, I’m recommending three purchases to get you up to 1000 points: A second Starter Set, Darth Maul and Sith Probe Droids (currently unavailable from Asmodee but easy to find elsewhere), and an AAT Battle Tank. This will let you start learning how to play a force using character, which has a surprisingly high skill ceiling, and give you a solid foundation for building any kind of list you like in the future as you figure out exactly what you like about the CIS army.

Expanded Starter Box CIS

995/1000

10 Activations

General Grievous (Improvised Orders, Tenacity, Prepared Supplies)

Maul (Saber Throw, Force Push, Into the Fray, Tenacity)

2x B1 Battle Droids (E-5C B1 Battle Droid, HQ Uplink)

B2 Super Battle Droids (B2-ACM Battle Droid)

2x B1 Battle Droids (E-5C B1 Battle Droid, HQ Uplink, Electrobinoculars)

2x Droidekas

AAT Battle Tank (OOM-Series Droid Pilot, Linked Targeting Array, High-Energy Shells, Armor-Piercing Shells)

Final Thoughts

The Separatist Alliance plays very differently from the other three factions in the game, but I find their playstyle to be very rewarding and powerful once you wrap your head around it. The biggest hurdle to getting into them is assembling and painting all the B1 Battle Droids you’re going to need, but once that’s done you have a fun army that’s very flexible but always has its own feel. I find that these are the fun bad guys to play, while the Empire and its Stormtroopers are very serious business, shoving a bunch of units of B1s around and going “Roger Roger” makes it easy to have fun no matter how the game is going.
