Trench Crusade Warband Focus: Expeditionary Forces of Abyssinia

August 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone to another to another Trench Crusade Warband focus! This week we’re exploring the horn of Africa, as the Expeditionary Forces of Abyssinia try to hold back Mammon’s forces from rolling over the land. We’ll take a look at the Variant rules, unique Battlekit and key units. Let’s get started! 

Editor's Note on the art: We've included artwork we have in our model library for the base New Antioch forces in question, but they're very much not specific models painted for the Expeditionary Forces of Abyssinia. When we get some of those painted, we'll update this piece and republish.

Credit: Raf Cordero

The Lore

Battling the forces of Mammon, the Expeditionary Forces of Abyssinia still pay their tithe, sending their warriors to New Antioch, where they receive cheers and shouts upon arrival.  Hailing mainly from Oromo and Amhara, where the rough terrain has led to many warriors forgoing shoes to battle barefoot. Abyssinia favours a variety of battlefield tactics, but particularly emphasises short-range shooting in formation, allowing the strengths of their detachments to shine. From the mighty Chewa who favour close combat and Shotels, to the Holy Warriors, who are even capable of staving off the spread of the Black Grail, each warrior is honed to achieve their role and defend the Solomonic Throne of Ethiopia.

The Variant

Before we get into things, let’s quickly break down the Variant’s rules: 

  • Abyssinian Healers: You can include up to two Combat Medics, and you can have up to two Misericordias.
  • Chieftain Panoply: No Machine Armour for your Mechanized Heavy Infantry.
  • Faith of Ethiopia: You can’t include any Sniper Priests.
  • Holy Warriors: You can have a single Trench Cleric in your Warband, but also up to two Holy Warriors. Use the regular Trench Cleric rules, but also include the following abilities:

    • Blessed Psalm: As an action that requires no roll, you can play a Blessing Marker from this model and give it to another. 

    • Arise and be Healed! After succeeding a Risky Success Roll, pick a model within 3” of the Holy Warrior, and that model may stand back up and can remove up to D3 Blood Markers and / Infection markers. 
  • Short-Range Marksmanship: Any non-Grenade or non-Heavy ranged weapon fired by a Lieutenant or Yeoman gains +1 if the attack is made at Short Range. 
  • Vanguard Forces: You can take no Trench Moles, but up to 4 Yeomen can have the Flanking ability for 5 Ducats each.
  • Flanking: After regular deployment, but before Infiltrators, you can place a model with this ability touching a battlefield edge and 8” away from any enemy model. However, if a scenario disallows Infiltrators, then this ability cannot be used. 
  • Warrior Nobles: Shock Troopers and Elites may purchase the Chewa ability for 5 Ducats. 
  • Chewa: Add +1 Dice to Melee attack Success Rolls for each other friendly model within 1” of the target. This caps at +2 Dice. 
  • Weapons of Mobile Warfare: You may only take up to three Ranged Weapons with the Heavy Keyword. This does not include Satchel Charges.
Cardinale de Albe. Image: Beezer

Wrapping this all together, we can see that Abyssinia offers a good amount of flexibility in how you approach constructing your warband. Do you want to go for more of a Ranged focus Yeoman build, that is cheap, but can add some fairly reliable firepower, or a Melee build, that focuses on ganging up on targets with your Elites and Shocktroopers through the Chewa ability. Whilst the flexibility is there, I think you’re going to want to lean on the Melee power, not just because of the wider net your Chewa ability can affect (Short-range Marksmanship is just Yeomen and Lieutenant, after all), but because of their unique Battlekit. With that in mind, let’s take a look!

Unique Battlekit

Shotel - 5 Ducats

A cheap 1-Handed weapon that has not only the Critical Keyword but also bypasses the Armour benefits of Shields (but other keywords or special rules still apply). At 5 Ducats, you can’t go wrong with taking this in the early stages against those that like to equip Trench Shields as their only source of Armour, as they are cheaper than Standard Armour.

Holy Water of Lalibela - 3 Ducats, Consumable, Limit: 5 

When a model makes a Success Roll for an Action that can remove Blood or Infection Markers, you can add +1 Dice to that Roll. A really nice consumable to have on your Holy Warriors, and not too expensive at all to have both with this.

Anfarro (Warriors Crown) - 10 Ducats, Chewa & Elite only, Headgear, Limit: 6

This Headgear grants the Negate Fear Keyword, as well as +1 Dice to the Melee Characteristic. Whilst this headgear comes with a few specific restrictions, it is good for your melee bruisers. The Chewa restriction does mean that you’ll be forking out 15 Ducats to get a Shocktrooper with this Headgear, but it can help in the mid-campaign where Ducats are a little more plentiful. For your Elites, it can go nicely if you’re expecting a lot of Fear to crop up.

Tabot - 4 Glory, Holy Warrior only, Limit: 1

Playing into the Blessed Psalm ability of the Holy Warrior, the Tabot is what helps your Holy Warriors generate more Blessing Markers. If you remove Blood or Infection Markers from friendly models with another friendly model, this unit gets a Blessing Marker. Nice and simple, and pairs well with not only Blessed Psalms, but also the cap increase of your Combat Medics.

Key Units

Lieutenant 

New Antioch Lieutenant. Credit: SRM

As with any New Antioch force, your Lieutenant is a key lynchpin in ensuring victory. The lieutenant makes great use of both the Automatic Pistol and Shotel combo, as you will want to get in close with the Pistol to eventually charge, meaning the +1 Dice and Short Range can come in handy, possibly netting you a nice Crit. The Shotel plays nicely into this as well, as with a +2 Dice in melee (and possibly more!), the chance of critting and proccing the Critical keyword is only more likely. 

Suggested starting loadout: 
  • Automatic Pistol
  • Shotel
  • Standard Armour

Holy Warrior 

New Antioch Trench Cleric. Credit: SRM The Holy Warrior is an amazing unit, with two more abilities than their Trench Cleric counterpart whilst still costing the same. With a staggering four abilities, they're able to dish out Blessing Markers around the battlefield as needed, whilst also providing some much-needed healing to a faction that is often very vulnerable to being taken Out of Action from even the smallest of changes to their survival odds. Their damage can catch opponents off guard too, as abilities like Chewa help them punch above their weight, and as we know, more dice mean more Crit chances, so they make use of the Shotel’s Critical Keyword almost as well as the Lieutenant. 

As you progress, you’re going to grab a Tabot for one of your Holy Warriors, so you can start dishing out those Blessing Markers like they’re Tic Tacs. 

Suggested starting loadout: 
  • Automatic Pistol
  • Shotel
  • Holy Water of Lalibela

Combat Medic

In any other New Antioch list, it would be quite rare to see a Combat Medic take an early spot in a list, but with Abyssinia’s unique set of abilities and battlekit, the Combat Medic fits into the playstyle this variants wants to play perfectly. Their natural skills in using Med-Kits pair nicely not only with the Holy Water of Lalibela, but also ensures more consistent proccing of your Holy Warrior’s Tabot ability. Their included Misercordia means they can have some punch in melee, ensuring Downed models stay down, but you need to invest into that to make it more accurate. If your Medic gets promoted, you can grab a Anfarro for them to ensure those strikes hit. 

New Antioch Combat Medic. Credit: SRM

Suggested starting loadout: 

For the most part, the Cmobat Medic comes with almost everything you need, but you may want to add in a Holy Water of Lalibela to get even more reliability in your healing.

For this list, it’s nice and simple and combines a couple of core aspects of New Antioch, with the added uniqueness of Abyssinia. At the NA core, we have an Abyssinian Mechanized Infantry, with the staple Heavy Shotgun and Satchel Charge, as well as two Yeoman with flamethrowers. Whilst they can’t infiltrate in the usual sense, they can still use flanking, which is better than nothing. For the Abyssinian flavour, we have all three Elites equipped with Automatic Pistols and Shotels, ensuring we make the most out of each activation. A double Shocktrooper is in there to get into the thick of things, and a double Shotel is no laughing matter for shielded opponents. Lastly, we have our Combat Medic, who supports the Warriors, and has the potential for an early promotion.

Final Thoughts

So there we have it! The Expeditionary Forces of Abyssinia take some core aspects of New Antioch frame and play around with this core nicely, relying on positioning and racking up positive modifiers to punch up, along with the Blessing Marker and healing meta game. If you’re looking for a flexible variant that requires you to move many levers at the same time, then certainly take a look at Abyssinia.
