Tabletop Battles Reviews: Horus Heresy Journal Tactica - The Battle for the Signus Cluster - Assault on Kol

October 31st, 2026 via Goonhammer

The new Journal Tactica is out and I’ve picked up a copy. We didn't get an advance copy this time so this review is a bit later than usual, but hopefully it's still of use.

It’s a long-established part of the Heresy lore that the Blood Angels were sent to the Signus Cluster by Horus before he kicked things off at Istvaan, where they had a big fight with Khorne daemons. You might reasonably expect a Journal about the Signus Cluster to feature Blood Angels and Khorne Daemons but you’d be mistaken. This book is about Custodes, who’ve recently had a lot of model releases, and Slaanesh daemons. There's also a narrative mission, a bit of content for the Traitor Legions and a Slaanesh-themed Cohort Doctrine.

Background

This section has an unusual amount of work to do, explaining why this book is about the “wrong” people altogether.

Custodian Sentinels. Credit: Pendulin

Fortunately for the background writers, the Custodes are a secretive bunch and it’s relatively straightforward to come up with a story for the Templi Prudentia, the most tight-lipped of the lot, doing something we didn’t know about. We learn how a ship full of them quietly showed up in a hitherto unmentioned world in the Signus Cluster, fought a whole lot of daemons that nobody else knew about and then left without telling anyone they were ever there.

This is all set out pretty well. The Custodes show up on a planet, then things kick off and go very badly for them. A cavalry officer does stupid things. Various people do heroic things to daemons and their worshippers, with swords, spears and so on. I won’t spoil the ending but it’s worth a read if you like this sort of thing.

Legendary Mission: Path of the Unforgiven

“The Unforgiven” is a Custodes Shield Captain called Medraut, who goes into a daemon-infested underhive and beats their apparent leader. This is represented by a 1500 point Zone Mortalis game played between Custodes and Daemons of Slaanesh.

Custodes Shield Captain. Credit: NotThatHenryC

The attacker has to take 1500 points of Infantry with no Tribune and one Shield Captain to represent Medraut. The defender has to have an Ascendant Lord of Slaanesh and may have an allied Solar Auxilia Detachment with the Sublime Cohort Doctrine.

The battle is fought on a 48x36” board which is divided into four 12x36” sectors, separated by walls with large doors in. The Custodes deploy in the sector on one side and have to fight their way through to the other side and kill or rout everything in the third and fourth sectors.

The Ascendant Lord has to stay in the fourth sector, waiting for Custodes to show up. The rest of the daemons get the Attack Waves special rule to

Additional Legio Custodes Units

This is really two new units and a new gun for an existing unit. It’s useful to get two new Command options as previously we’ve only had Shield Captains. A bit of variety is nice to have and both have their distinct uses.

Epheroi Venatari-Proconsul

Kind of like an Epheroi Venatari-Proconsul. Credit: NotThatHenryC

This is essentially a Venatari character, made from Venatari and Shield Captain bits. They come with two Kinetic Destroyers for 160 and can swap one of them for a Vaultsword, the same as the one carried by a member of the Adjutarum Sodality, for an extra 15. You can also buy Neutronium Cascade Mines for 10 points.

The Venatari-Proconsul has an impressive stat line, as you’d expect. 14” move, WS6 and BS7 with I5 and A3 - which goes up to 5 if you take a Vaultsword. They have W3 and only get 3+ armour thanks to their lighter armour, though they have a 4+ invulnerable save too.

You get a stack of special rules like Precision 5+, Firing protocols (2) and Firestorm. The Epheroi Excelsis rule lets you choose to automatically pass a reserves roll for a Venatari Sodality with one of these attached. They have Vanguard (3).

Either load-out would be fun. The sword obviously gives you a very scary melee fighter with a 14” Antigrav move, though you might get pretty much the same effect by just taking more Venatari with spears. Personally I think two pistols wins on rule of cool. It would be great to have a Moritat-style Venatari, maybe with Paragon of Battle for BS8, shooting people’s characters between the eyes.

Hykanatoi Proconsul-Delegate

This is a basic Custodian Guard with one better WP and IN and the Command rule. They come in at 80 armed with just a Sentinel Blade. You can give them a shield to go with it for 5 points, which might be a good idea as it comes with a 5++. Alternatively for 10 points they can have a spear.

The Hykanatoi Excelsis rule means that any Magisterium Vexillae in the unit get 2” more range. That 20” bubble ought to be enough to cover a couple of objectives. That’ll be quite a problem for your opponent, especially now Vanguard actually works. A Sentinel squad with a Hykanatoi Proconsul-Delegate will make it tough to score anything from objectives and punish the enemy in your turn for daring to stand on them.

If we’re honest, the main reason you’d take one of these is because they’re the cheapest way to unlock a Detachment. They’ll be useful to have around though. Custodes are so powerful that you might even have your Proconsul-Delegate split off to fight an enemy unit alone.

Gyrfalcon Jetbike Sodality

Gyrfalcon Jetbike with Arachnus Volley Cannon. Credit: NotThatHenryC

The only change here is the new option of taking an Arachnus volley cannon for 20 points, in addition to all the previous options, which remain. Like other Arachnus weapons it has two modes: Concentrated fire with FP1 S8 AP2 D2 and Armourbane or Burst fire wiht FP3 RS5 AP3 D1 version for gunning down marines.

This is a pretty good gun, though S8 and D2 isn’t great against tanks. The Burst fire mode might be best, letting your jetbikes clear their route to whatever they intend to charge. That’s useful as Gyrfalcons have enormous bases, making it fairly easy to screen them out. If you just want a unit to kill Spartans and Super-heavies, stick with the Las-pulser.

The Daemons of Slaanesh Army List

This is a new supplement for the Ruinstorm Daemons list, providing a bunch of new units, a Gambit, Advanced Reaction and so on.

Traitor Legion additions

There are two additions to what Traitor Astartes armies can take, put in the Slaanesh Daemon list, somewhat confusingly. Let’s get this out of the way first and then look at the actual Slaanesh Daemon list.

First there’s a Detachment that a Traitor Esoterist can unlock, containing a Command, two Troop, one Heavy Assault and one Fast Attack slot, all of which have to be Slaanesh Daemons. This is fine and lets you take anything from this army. You could just take an Allied Detachment of Daemons with a Herald instead of the Esoterist but this would allow you to bring something like a unit each of Seekers and Fiends without needing a Herald to unlock them.

Word Bearers Esoterist / Diabolist - Credit: RichyP

The second change is to allow Traitor Praetors to be affected by Insidious Corruption, giving them the Malefic Sub-Type for 20 points. It’s available for all the Praetors in the Liber Hereticus but not the Legacies mounted ones, which is especially odd given that this entire list is a supplement to an online PDF. This is basically an errata because they realised Praetors couldn’t usually be made Malefic, as they don’t have a Prime slot, and therefore can’t join Malefic units.

Thousand Sons Praetor in Saturnine Terminator Armor - credit: Colin Ward

Malefic Praetors are going to be pretty useful because they never run away. It’ll help Traitors to have the option of a big scary unit of True Believers with their Praetor leading it. And it was just a bit odd from the narrative perspective that the leader of an army of the lost and the damned always remained pure themselves. This fixes that.

Slaanesh Daemon army rules

This is an expansion to the Ruinstorm Daemon list, meaning you can take all the other units from that list as well as the ones listed below. Those units don’t get any of these rules, however, and can’t take the Slaanesh Aetheric Dominion.

The units in this book have Aetheric Dominion: Slaanesh, which is actually just listed as the “Slaanesh” trait on their profiles. This gives them the Hedonists and Soporiphic Musk rules. Slaanesh Psykers have the Enraptura Psychic Discipline and can’t have others unless noted.

You can freely include units with the Infernal Tempest, Rapturous Sensation, Encroaching Ruin and Malevolent Artifice Dominions with your Slaanesh Daemons. If you include any others in the army, however, all your Daemons will get a -2 modifier to their LD, CL, WP and IN Characteristics.

It’s notable that the Tzeench and Slaanesh Daemon lists don’t include each other in the “friends” lists. You can have both sets of units in an army but their mental stats will be much worse, which is especially bad for an army that’s so reliant on Psychic abilities.

Gambit

The Blissful Pain gambit lets your daemon power itself up by getting hit. You choose to surrender Challenge Advantage, so the enemy gets to hit you first. Your model gets +2A for every successful wound inflicted on it, whether or not you save it.

I think this is probably something you should only choose if you expect to lose Challenge Advantage anyway. It gives you a chance to have a really big counter-punch against a superior opponent, potentially killing them before they get to hit your model a second time. If you’re likely to attack first then you may as well do a different Gambit and just try to kill them before they strike at all or, worst case, after they’ve had one set of attacks against you.

Advanced Reaction

The Battlelust Advanced Reaction can be used once per Turn in the enemy shooting phase. At Step 11, after removing casualties, you can move your unit D6” in any direction you want. They also gain +1 to their Initiative till the end of your next turn.

The intention is probably that you have your unit move straight at the enemy but you don’t have to. You might be able to move out of LoS, avoid a charge or even get onto an objective with this. Movement abilities are always great so the option to do this once per turn will really help.

Psychic Discipline: Enraptura

Slaanesh Psykers get the Whisperer trait, which doesn't do anything, and two Psychic powers.

Beguile is a Curse that stops an enemy unit from doing Reactions. You cast it in any phase at a unit within 18” and LoS. The target takes a Resistance Check and, if they fail, they can’t take any Reactions against Models with the Slaanesh trait.

That final condition is important. A Slaanesh Herald taken as an ally won’t achieve much here by only protecting your allied Daemons from Reactions. However it’s a big help for an Ascendant Lord or Keeper of Secrets. They can Beguile the unit they intend to charge so it can’t react away, Return Fire or fire Overwatch at them.

Malignity is a Psychic weapon with 12” range, using one of the enemy unit’s weapons. You make a Willpower Check, taking Perils on a Double. If you pass the check then you can choose a ranged weapon in the target unit and have it shoot the unit, using your Psyker’s BS (of 3 for a Herald, 5 for Ascendant Lord and 6 for the Keeper of Secrets). Blasts and Templates do D6 attacks instead of using their templates. Your Psyker is “firing” the weapon, so rules like Heavy will activate depending on whether it has moved, for example.

The effectiveness of this attack will obviously depend a lot on what the target unit is armed with. If you can use it on a Rapier Laser Destroyer unit then they could wipe themselves out. Beware things like Heavy Disintegrators though, as you might overheat and hurt your daemon.

I’m not sure if it’s supposed to work on vehicles but I can’t see any reason it wouldn’t, which is honestly a horrible mess. The shot comes from the Daemon so you’d use its position to determine the facing you hit. For now, you can have a Falchion somehow shoot itself with its centreline-mounted laser. This is particularly nasty for things with Demolisher cannons or similar, which will shoot themselves D6 times.

This needs a FAQ. It seems like yet another example of the Heresy writers spending a lot of text without quite managing to write a rule as they intended. Couldn’t the rule have just been that one non-vehicle model in the enemy unit made a shooting attack as normal, targeting its own unit if the power went off?

Special Rules and Equipment

Slaanesh Daemons are Hedonists. This has a useful effect of giving one of your units per turn Line at various values, removing Vanguard if the unit has it. You pick an enemy unit within 18” of and visible to your unit and get Line 1 for every Tactical Status on it. This has the potential to score you a fair number of VPs across a game, especially if you prioritise applying statuses at range, which will be the best way to have multiple affected units to score off.

Credit: Liebot - https://instagram.com/liebot_pics

The Soporiphic Musk coming off Slaanesh Daemons means enemy units in melee with them need to pass a Willpower check to do an Aftermath option. Fail, and they can only Hold. Routed enemies don’t run away if they fail their Willpower test but they remain Routed.

The Keeper of Secrets is a Primal Terror. Enemies have to make a Leadership Check to charge it or react to it. Fail and they become Stunned, with the additional penalty that they can’t charge or react to anything else.

Some units can carry a Totem of Dominion. They only take 1 wound instead of D3 when they would gain a Tactical Status. This is pretty useful, though it’s fairly expensive so you might prefer to just have more models in the first place.

Daemons of Slaanesh Units

Ascendant Lord of Slaanesh

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

The High Command choice is a sort of Daemon Prince, almost as powerful as the Ruinstorm Daemon Sovereign, though it loses one point of Strength, a Wound and an Attack. You can get one on foot for 300 points with a 7” move or add wings for +30, giving it 12” and Antigrav. The one on foot is Bulky (7) and wings make it Bulky (8), which probably doesn’t matter much. Both versions have Fear (1) and Eternal Warrior (1). It’s a Malefic Infantry Champion with 2+/4++ saves.

The Ascendant Lord has no guns and hits in melee with a Tormentor blade, with IM+1, AP2, D2, Duellist’s Edge (1), Critical Hit (6+) and Phage (WP). For 15 points that can be replaced with a Tormentor Greatblade, with IM-2, SM+2, AP2, D2, Critical Hit (5+) and Phage (WP). That’s quite a big loss to your initiative in challenges, though that might be fine if you use the Blissful Pain Gambit.

Speaking of Challenges, the Ascendant Lord is a Champion of the Dark Gods, meaning it has to be declared as the challenger or to accept challenges if eligible. If it gets disgraced it gets put into Reserves, which is a bit weird and potentially a nice escape mechanism for it.

It doesn’t have the Psyker trait written in the book but I’m pretty sure this is a mistake. You can take Biomancy for 20 points, which is good, or Telepathy for 10, which is rubbish. Biomancy gives your Daemon Impact (S) and the option to do Biomantic Rage for even more Strength and Toughness. Telepathy gives it Fear (1), which it’s already got, Mind Burst, which makes it stand still instead of move off to kill something and Cursed Whispers, which is sort of ok but nothing special.

As a High Command slot, the Ascendant Lord unlocks an Apex Detachment. However, the Slaanesh Daemons list doesn’t have any Retinue or Elite choices, and neither does the wider Ruinstorm Daemons list, so this isn’t especially valuable. You can just take an Auxiliary Detachment of course. This may make it more attractive to take an Ascendant Lord in an Allied Detachment, using the Prime Command slot - potentially without any other Daemons unless you want to bring them.

Herald of Slaanesh

In the Command slot we have our Infantry Command option, rather like a much better Daemonette. It has the Fast (1) and Impact (I) rules like them and its Ripclaws deliver a total of 6 attacks at S5, AP3, D1 with Rending (6+), Breaching (6+), Force (D) and Phage (WP).

It’s got WS5 and I5 so it’s a bit average in terms of melee skill. T3, W3 and 4+/5++ saves make it relatively soft though it does have Eternal Warrior (1). If a Herald finds itself in a challenge it’ll probably want to try and kill its opponent quickly rather than using Blissful Pain. It’ll be happiest slicing up marines with its claws and avoiding anyone who might fight back.

The main point of a Herald, other than unlocking Auxiliary Detachments as a Command slot, is using Herald of Unreality to bring units into the battle. You put a unit of Daemons in Reserve at the start of the game, inside a warp rift, which gives it the Deep Strike rule. Then, so long as your Herald isn’t locked in combat, it can automatically bring that unit in from Reserves. The unit Deep Strikes in, with its first model in Base-to-Base contact with the Herald, and is then allowed to declare a charge later in the turn, in an exception to the usual Deep Strike rules.

This isn’t easy to make work. You need to have your Herald start its turn pretty close to the enemy, so that the unit it brings in is close enough to Charge. But you have to somehow keep it alive and out of melee while in this close up position. If you can pull it off to chuck a unit of Daemonettes straight into melee, great, but you won’t always manage that. Having Fast (1) on Daemonettes will help with this, slightly.

Daemonettes

Daemonettes of Slaanesh. Credit: Richyp

These are in a fairly bad place, unfortunately. 140 points buys you 10 of them and you can get another 10 for 12 points each if you want. That’s just too many points for T3 bodies with a 5+ save (albeit invulnerable) and W1. Bolters exist and even in melee you can expect to take casualties.

In the relatively unlikely event that Daemonettes make it to melee they’re very dangerous. The basic profile of WS4, S4, I4 and A2 isn’t amazing but their Razorclaws have AM+1, AP3, D1, Rending (6+) and Breaching (6+). The Daemonettes also have Impact (I), so they’ll tend to strike before marines and do them a lot of harm. They also have Phage (WP), which is fun but probably doesn’t matter.

Daemonettes also have Fear (1), Fast (1) and Exquisite Suffering, which does… nothing really. If their target has Feel no Pain (which hardly anything does) then for every mitigation roll of a 1 the enemy makes in combat, the Daemonettes get +1 to Combat resolution. So this is something that will happen incredibly rarely and probably won’t matter when it does, as the enemy will be dead.

You’ll take a unit of these for your Herald to join but I don’t know if it makes sense to bring many more. They probably will succeed sometimes, especially in settings like Zone Mortalis. All those Rending and Breaching attacks make them a real threat into anything except vehicles but they will often be swept away by gunfire. Have your Herald’s uni, hide somewhere and be ready to charge into enemies who come close.

Slaaneshi Seekers

You can have five Daemonettes riding on Steeds of Slaanesh as a Fast Attack choice for 90 points, with up to five more for 18 each. They’re Cavalry with a 12” move, 4s for most stats except for W2 and A2. 5+ armour and invulnerable. You can get a Totem of Dominion for 20 points.

Special rules for these are a bit different to Daemonettes, though they also have Exquisite Suffering and it still doesn’t do anything. They’re armed with the same Razorclaws, so are basically the same in melee as the infantry version. They have Outflank and Impact (S) instead of (I). They also get Vanguard (2), which is interesting as it works now and this is a quick (though not Fast) unit that can be sent to chase people off objectives.

In theory Seekers could kill or Rout an enemy unit, score Vanguard, then use Hedonists to get a bunch of VPs from the objective. That could be game-winning, though Soporific Musk might prevent a routed enemy from running away, which would ruin everything.

Slaaneshi Fiends

The Heavy Assault choice is actually kind of badass and one of the stand-out units in the list. Fiends are basically a better version of Ruinstorm Daemon Brutes, costing 150 for 3. You can’t enlarge the unit at all but you can make one of them a Fiendlord for 20 points, with an extra attack and LD7 instead of the pretty rubbish 6 that they start with.

Fiends have a great stat line. M8, WS4, BS2 (and no guns), S5, T5, W5, I4 and A3 (though really 4 because their claws give them +1). They have 4+/5++ saves, Bulky (4), Fear (1) and Impact (I), meaning they’ll tend to strike before things they charge.

Their Giant Razorclaws get AM+1, AP3, D2, Rending (6+), Breaching (6+) and Phage (WP). That’s a profile that you can send into pretty tough opponents, like Dreadnoughts and Terminators, expecting good results. They may struggle a bit due to WS4 into more elite enemies but things like Veteran Assault Squads, with W2 and 3+ saves, will still fear these, especially if they charge with I5.

Keeper of Secrets

Credit: Liebot - https://instagram.com/liebot_pics

The Keeper of Secrets occupies a Warlord slot, Like the Lord of Change. That means it’s only available if you take Slaanesh daemons as your Primary Detachment.

This is a great big scary monster that will rip stuff up. There’s no subtlety here at all, just a blur of big sharp claws cutting through the enemy army unless it meets something scarier or loads of guns. I liked how the Lord of Change has a really serious Shooting phase as well as melee but this thing has no guns at all, unfortunately. None of its Psychic powers offer great shooting options either really, though Malignity will sometimes do good things.

The stats are very good of course. M10 and otherwise mostly 6s, though S5 and an excellent I7. Mental stats are scarily good and it has a 2+/4++ save, like most Warlord-level stuff. It’s Bulky (7), Fear (1), Eternal Warrior (2) and Primal Terror, so anyone who wants to Charge or React to it has to take a Leadership test or get Stunned and do nothing.

As standard the Keeper of Secrets has a Shimmerblade, which gives it AP2 and D2 with Critical Hit (5+), Force (S) and Phage (WP). You can swap that for a Shimmerspear and Shield, gaining IM+1 but AM-1, still with AP2 and D2. It has Impact (I), Critical Hit (6}+ keeping Force (S) and Phage (WP). The shield gives it the amazing Deflect rule, letting it turn a 6 that the opponent rolls to hit it in a challenge into a 1. Losing an attack and reducing your chance to get crits definitely hurts but having I9 on the charge is pretty crazy

You can give a Keeper of Secrets Biomancy for 20 points or Telepathy for 10. Just like the Ascendant Lord, Biomancy looks interesting and Telepathy doesn’t.

New Solar Auxilia Rules

For Solar Auxilia, we get a new Traitor unit and a Slaanesh-themed Cohort Doctrine.

Exalted Section

These are Auxilia who have become tainted by Slaanesh. 90 points gets you an Elites choice of 10 Debased, including a Sergeant called a Depravor. They’re Malefic, which isn’t otherwise easily available to Auxilia. Naturally, this means they’re locked to Traitors. They have Vanguard (3), which looks pretty good following the recent rule changes.

The Exalted Section has fairly normal stats. WS and BS 4 but S and T3, W1, I3 and A2. The Depravor has LD7 and they all get the standard Auxilia 4+ saves. They come with Lasrifles, Frag and Krak grenades. These can be exchanged for Laspistols and Charnabral Sabres for free, which seems like a good plan as it brings them up to I4.

Any model can swap its pistol or sabre for a Razorclaw, like the ones Daemonettes have, for 5 points. This gives them AP3 D1 attacks with Rending (6+), Breaching (6+) and Phage (WP), like most of the Daemons. The Depravor can have a pistol or melee weapon from the usual Auxilia lists or a Hooklash for 10 points. This gives it IM+2, AM+2 and Phage (WP), though at only S3, AP- and D1 it isn’t especially scary compared to something like a Power Fist, which also costs 10 points.

You can also get them an Augury Scanner that they don’t need or a Totem of Dominion that could be pretty useful, for 10 points each. One Debased can also have Melta bombs for +10.

This is a pretty weird unit, but potentially quite dangerous. You could have a large unit with sabres and a few claws. As a Vanguard unit, you could potentially take the Solar Pattern Cohort to have them charge from a Dracosan.

Cohort Doctrine: Sublime Cohort

Solar Auxilia Credit: realSnice

This is a Cohort dedicated to Slaanesh. The Cohort Tactica grants all Auxilia models the Disciples of Pain rule. This gives them the Slaanesh Trait and Fast (1). It also makes everything with the Command Sub-type the Psyker Trait and Enraptura discipline.

Your usual Lasgun troops won’t benefit enormously from being Fast but things like Veletarii and Ogryns will get quite a lot out of it. Your commanders also don’t tend to have especially good WP, though it’s usually possible to draft in something like a Navigator.

For every Infantry Tercio in the army, you can also bring an Antemasque Detachment of Slaanesh Daemons. Like the Legion version, this has a Command slot, two Troops and one each of Fast Attack and Heavy Assault. Infantry Tercios aren’t bad and they unlock a couple of slots for the Dracosans that your Exalted Sections’ are likely to want. In theory you could quite easily unlock two or three of these Detachments, sending Seekers and Fiends all over the place.

On the whole this is quite an interesting Doctrine. Units like Charonite Ogryns will gain a fair bit from being Fast but the switch towards melee may be a bit of a challenge for most people’s existing collections.

Final Thoughts

There’s a lot of content in this journal, which is good to see. There's a whole new story rather than a re-telling of something we already knew like the Prospero book, and meaningful new content for several factions. The mission looks quite fun, too.

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought credit: Crab-stuffed Mushrooms

For the Custodes I think it’s a bit frustrating to have had their units split across two books. These journals aren’t expensive but just having to carry lots of books about is slightly annoying. On the plus side, it does mean we get to explore the faction a bit more with different episodes of Lore, for a faction that didn’t otherwise have a huge amount of background. It's definitely a good thing to fill out the faction with more HQ units. I'm interested in trying out some kitbashes to build the new HQs.

The updates for the Legion lists are ok but I'm not sure if they work for this format. Letting Praetors buy the Malefic trait for 20 points could have been in a FAQ/Errata. I personally won't expect Traitor opponents to show up with this book if they want to do that.

I quite like the Slaanesh Daemon list. Most units look playable, though I still think Daemonettes are overpriced. It’s an army that should work ok on its own but will probably more often show up as allies for Legion or Auxilia forces, in which role it will work very well.

The Auxilia stuff is kind of fine. It feels a bit like it’s in there because they felt it had to be, rather than because they had a keen idea of what Slaaneshi Auxilia would be like. Personally I think I’d have based a Slaanesh cult army on the Militia list.

Overall I think this is one of the better journals in terms of content. I expect we'll see the units out of Assault on Kol on tables quite often. I look forward to seeing the Khorne and Nurgle daemon rules at some point in future.
