Blood Bowl Season 3: Underworld Denizens Team Guide

September 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer

If you like your Blood Bowl ultra-violent, full of fouls, touchdowns, Star Players, and general insanity, then we have the team for you!  Underworld were one of the game’s boogeymen in the early days of Second Season, as their access to Swarming Snotlings, great Star Players, and strong individual positionals made them the perfect cheap chassis for tournament cheese.  After a succession of nerfs and changes, they’ve slipped under the radar somewhat, but they still have a tremendously varied roster and a unique flavour (or should that be smell?) that makes them one of the most interesting, flexible, and effective teams in the game.  In this team guide we’re going to run through all of their many options and sort the sewer rats from the biological waste.

Pros

  • Extremely flexible and varied roster that can be built for any play environment.
  • The Gutter Runner and Skaven Blitzer are two of the best positionals in the game.
  • Powerful fouling and Star Player support.

Cons

  • Many squishy players.
  • No redundancy in positionals.
  • Lots of different rules and pieces that need to be coached in harmony.

The Roster

Goblin Linemen

The hunched backbone of the Underworld team, the Goblin is a familiar and not very impressive sight.  Underworld Goblins have one major advantage over their conventional peers: Access to Mutation skills on Primary.  That means very fun possibilities like Two-Heads letting Goblins go anywhere on an unmodified 2+.

In practice, Dodge and Stunty will often prove to be extremely useful tools in general play, with the former being a very powerful defensive tool against unskilled opposition players, and the latter allowing for unique re-positioning (and occasionally offensive) opportunities.  And if nothing else, Goblins also make great foulers, so Devious skills like Dirty Player and Lone Fouler are great choices for them.

Snotling Linemen

Snotlings painted by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms (Joey K)

Underworld Snotlings lost one of their most important tools in Third Season, with Swarming being taken away from the team.  So why continue to take the weakest and most easily injured player in the game?  Well Snotlings do have some saving graces; Dodge and Sidestep mean they can actually be extremely annoying to pin down, and the Stunty and Titchy combo means they can dodge anywhere on a 2+.  They are also astonishingly cheap, at 15k a pop, which means they are your best tool for fouling, risk-wise.

Snots also gained access to the new Devious skills category in Third Season, which means a plethora of fouling and annoyance skills are within easy reach.  While they also get access to Mutation skills on Primary, there’s not much appealing for them.  Not skilling Snotlings is probably the best plan, as it more than doubles their cost and doesn’t give you much in return, but Dirty Player and Lone Fouler are definitely options, while Diving Tackle has strong synergy with Dodge and Sidestep, and can lock down weak and unlucky opponents.

Skaven Clanrat

Skaven Lineman by @7s_and_2s on Instagram

The third of the Linemen on the Underworld team is the Skaven.  For the most part these are exactly the same as those on the regular Skaven roster, which means they are good all-rounders, with very high movement, but a little lightly armoured.  Despite that, they will end up doing a lot of marking and line of scrimmage work, simply because you have no one else who can reliably do it.

Clanrats can be built in many different ways, and Mutations are also an option.  Wrestle and Block are the most solid options (with Wrestle cheaper and probably preferred in Third Season).

Skaven Thrower

Skaven Thrower by @7s_and_2s on Instagram

The Skaven Thrower is one of two main options as ball carrier on the roster.  With Sure Hands, PA2+, and Pass, he easily pick the ball up and move it on to other players as required.  With that role in mind, the Thrower generally needs to be kept out of trouble and might want to sit out defensive drives.

Block is a good development skill for Throwers that keeps them alive and can let them contribute more on both sides of the ball.  Dodge is a Secondary but another great survival and mobility option.  Leader is a very cheap reroll, but thanks to the nature of Underworld team building, you arguably won’t need more of those outside of tournament settings.  Extra Arms is another good option that synergises with Sure Hands for pickups in difficult circumstances.  +MA and +AG are good stat ups.

Skaven Blitzer

This boy got quite the glowup in Third Season, getting an extra point of MA and the Strip Ball skill, making what was already one of your best two players into a bonafide out-of-the-box star.  Underworld lean heavily on the Blitzer as their only truly reliably blitz option, so using it effectively will be one of the main skills to master as an Underworld coach.  Ideally, it should be kept free of contact so that it can use its huge movement range to blitz, or support an advancing Gutter Runner each turn.

Tackle or Mighty Blow are probably the strongest first skills for Blitzers, helping them be even better at their primary role.  Guard is a good general support option, while Mutation access allows Claws, which is also a good first skill, but even better in combination with Mighty Blow.  Break Tackle is another strong option that enhances their mobility even further.  All of the stat ups apart from +PA are also excellent options for Blitzers.

Skaven Gutter Runner

Skaven Gutter Runner by @7s_and_2s on Instagram

Amongst a team of assorted and sometimes dubious merits, the Gutter Runner is your touchdown-getter par excellence.  MA9 will often leave even experienced coaches saying “he can move how far?!” as it races out of trouble and toward the endzone.  With Dodge and AG2+ it is perfectly equipped for its role and is unquestionably the most important and precious player in your entire team.

Accordingly, keeping it safe is your number one challenge.  Unlike the Gutters on the Skaven roster, trying to use your lone Gutter as a ball-hawk is a dangerous proposition.  You may even chose not to field it on defensive drives, especially if you plan to lean into bashing and fouling.  Using the gutter to score on your own drive, and then using it  to try a two-turn or one-turn touchdown after your opponent’s drive is a perfectly valid strategic approach.

So, how do you improve the best scoring threat in the whole game?  Well perhaps I could offer you Two Heads?  Extra Arms?  Yes, Gutters also get access to Mutations, and as if they weren’t spoiled for skill choice before they are now.  Sidestep, Block, Dodge, Hit and Run, and Sprint are all great options.

Trolls

Blood Bowl Trolls (sculpted by BruteFun) painted by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms (Joey K)

The first of our Big Guy options is the Troll, as seen on numerous other greenskin teams.  And the Troll has all the benefits and drawbacks we have come to love(?).  It is strong, tough, and cheap; but they are slow, and Really Stupid is one of the worst negatraits, meaning they require constant babysitting to accomplish anything.  Unlike other teams’ Trolls. Underworld retained Loner on theirs in Third Season, making them just as risky and unreliable as ever.  

Did someone say Tentacles?!  Yes, just like everyone else on the team, Underworld Trolls can take Mutations as Primary skills.  Claw is another that can really enhance their killing power and make opponents hesitant to base them.  Otherwise, as always, Trolls benefit hugely from Block, though it is a Secondary, as it helps them stay on their feet and prevents turnovers.  Guard is a good old reliable option, while Stand Firm can also add to the roadblock role.  If you want to lean into throwing team-mates, Bullseye is a fun new option in Third Season.

Rat Ogre

If the Troll is the roadblock option, then the Rat Ogre is the bulldozer.  Blitz, blitz, blitz is the tactic of choice for Roger, and in a team that lacks muscle and blocking power, the Rat Ogre certainly delivers it.  The downside as always is higher fragility, and the possibility to injure your own players with Animal Savagery.

Rat Ogres benefit the most from either Juggernaut or Block to provide a safety net for their blitzes.  Juggernaut also has some additional utility.  After that, Claws and Break Tackle can up their killing power and mobility respectively.  Guard is also an option, but Frenzy and Animal Savagery can make positioning Guard problematic.

Star Players

Hakflem Skuttlespike, Blood Bowl Star Player painted by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms (Joey K)

The Underworld Challenge Star Player pool got a little shakeup in Third Season, losing a former UW favourite in Varag Ghoulchewer.  However, it still has a superb selection of some of the most impactful stars at various price points.  That includes the likes of Bomber Dribblesnot, Fungus the Loon, Glart Smashrip, Ripper Bolgrot, and the two super-gutters, Hakflem Skuttlespike and Skitter Stab-Stab.  This selection allows you to tailor your team to any opponent or fill any hole or need you have.  Gutter Runner MNG’ed?  Then take Hakflem or Skitter.  Facing AV10+?  Glart is your guy.  Cut-price mayhem?  The Goblins have you covered.

This is one of the strongest areas for Underworld, and be it in tournament or league play, you are likely to find yourself with star support, so make sure to familiarise yourself with their various strengths and their particular special rules.

Tactics and Playstyle

Blood Bowl Goblin Troll by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms

Underworld can be played and built in many different ways, but primarily they play somewhere between a heavyweight Stunty team and lightweight Hybrid team.  This means you want to exploit your movement and mobility, and use your blitzes and fouls as surgical tools, rather than heavily basing-up your opponent.  With multiple Dodge and Stunty pieces, and extremely fast Skaven players, Underworld are able to escape bashy opponents and reposition the whole team easily.  You can outmaneuver most opponents while trying to pick off their more influential and important players.

Fouling is one of their strongest tactics, so in a typical turn, a Blitz on a valuable opponent with either a Rat Ogre or Skaven Blitzer, followed by a Snotling or Goblin foul, is a good approach.  Remember also that Underworld have the Bribery and Corruption team rule, which means that once per game you can reroll a 1 on Argue The Call, and Bribes become a very good inducement option at 50k each.

On defense you will have to make a call on whether nor not to field your strong ball-players, The Gutter and Thrower, which will give you a much bigger chance of making a defensive score, or let them sit out and just lean into punishing your opponent.  There's no right or wrong answer here, but if your opponent has nasty Gutter-hunting pieces (anything with block/mighty blow/tackle) or likes fouling, then just consider the risk to your own offensive drive (and season!).

With a Gutter Runner (and optional Frenzy Big Guy) and/or Throw-Team options available, one-turn touchdowns are a huge potential play for Underworld.  Moreover, if you have both threats on the table, your opponent will not as easily be able to set up to defend against a particular one.  These can be complicated to set up (and certainly dice-dependent to accomplish) so you will again need to do your research and practice to make sure you are ready for the opportunity.

Starting Team Builds

Blood Bowl Star Player, Glart Smashrip painted by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms (Joey K)

Underworld have a multitude of options for team building in every environment.  You can build them at very low TV and lean into the inducement game, or aim higher and have a deeper roster.  For league play we’ll do the latter, and then look at some cheesier tournament options.

League Play

  • 1 x Rat Ogre
  • 1 x Skaven Thrower
  • 1 x Skaven Blitzer
  • 1 x Gutter Runner
  • 3 x Skaven Linemen
  • 4 x Goblin Linemen
  • 5 x Snotling Linemen
  • 3 x Team Rerolls

This comes to a round 1m GC, and gives you 16 bodies to feed into the meat grinder.  You could also drop 1 Snotling to buy two additional Dedicated Fans which will generate more cash for inevitable replacements during the season.

League Development

For the above build only the Apothecary needs to be added to the mix (and reserved probably exclusively for the Gutter Runner).  Any other spare cash can be used for replacements.  Keeping the team lean will give you more inducement cash down the road, allowing you to add powerful stars and bribes as desired.

Tournament Play

  • 1 x Rat Ogre
  • 1 x Skaven Thrower
  • 1 x Skaven Blitzer
  • 1 x Gutter Runner
  • 3 x Skaven Linemen
  • 4 x Goblin Linemen
  • 3 x Snotling Linemen
  • 2 x Team Rerolls

This is a cheap 900TV shell that lets you play the inducement game.  At 14 players it will let you take up to 2 stars (assuming your tournament pack allows it).  You can use a skill to get Leader to bump up the rerolls.  You can make it even cheaper by swapping the Troll in for the Rat Ogre, but if you do this I would look to ensure you take a good blitzing star like Glart.  You can tweak this roster very precisely for your tourney pack so that you can get hold of particular inducements.  Some strong options at various price points include:

  • 130k Bomber or Fungus + 1 bribe
  • 175k Glart
  • 180k Bomber or Fungus + 2 Bribes
  • 250k Ripper
  • 255k Glart + Bomber or Fungus

Tournament Skills

Priorities for skilling are the Gutter, Blitzer, Rat Ogre, and Thrower.  If your pack gives Secondary skills then Block on the Rat Ogre (or Troll) is a good use of it.  Otherwise take Juggernaut (or Guard on a Troll).  If skill stacking is allowed, The Gutter and Blitzer can make great use of it.  Combos like Block/Sidestep on the Gutter or Mighty Blow/Tackle and Mighty Blow/Claw on the Blitzer can be extremely powerful.  Wrestle clanrats are an excellent tool both defensively and offensively, and a Goblin with Dirty Player or Lone Fouler, and another with Two Heads, will be very useful.

Tier

GW have kept Underworld in Tier 1 in Third Season, and this largely reflects their potential for shenanigans when given more cash and star options in lower tiers in tourney play.  Nevertheless, they remain a very strong team whose flexibility means that coaches can carefully curate the team to meet their playstyle and the threats of their meta.
