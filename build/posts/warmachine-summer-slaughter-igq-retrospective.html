[Warmachine] Summer Slaughter IGQ Retrospective

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Credit: Wolfe_The_Cat

Hello and welcome to another debrief of my time attempting to be a semi-competent competitive player of Warmachine! This week I’m talking about my recent excursion to the most awful place in the world for a pretty good two-day event. That's right, we’re talking about the Summer Slaughter held at The Crowne Plaza Princeton Conference center in… New Jersey. We’re gonna go over the venue, the preparation, the lists, the games, and a quick retrospective after the fact, so let's get right into it!

The Venue

Summer Slaughter is a multi game system event held by the fine folks at “Wicked Dicey.” I don't know a ton about the history and the internet has been less than helpful in that regard unfortunately. It did look like there was a decent turn out though between various GW games, Star wars Legion, Infinity, Halo: Flashpoint, Flesh and Blood, and of course Warmachine. The convention center (and future home to the Bokur Brawl) was attached to the hotel by means of a fairly long sky bridge, which made it very easy to get around and made the after tournament activities even easier, knowing you could have a drink and not worry about driving. We also had plenty of room between tables, in the rooms and within the venue itself, so you didn't have to be on top of anyone. The amenities were pretty nice, with lunch being available nearby and, while not outstanding, the food was good enough at relatively reasonable prices. The only real downside is the two conference rooms we were in did not appear to have working AC. This became a problem very quickly when the temps outside hit 90+ and the rooms all around us were full of people, effectively turning it into a swamp almost immediately. Not going to lie; it felt a little less than ideal. We were however told that we would be in different rooms come the Bokur Brawl next year and the AC did work in those rooms (we went up there to cool off a few times), so I have high hopes for how that is going to go and am looking forward to it!

Prep and Lists

Now onto the juicy stuff. Prep for this event was a little different than usual, what with Fane of Nyrro just coming into its own and looking at the caliber of player attending. There were only two people below a 450 rating on Longshanks out of 30 and while Longshanks player ratings don't mean much (seriously they don’t), I find they do give a decent idea of the general amount of games played and a vague notion of skill level. All that is to say the event was full of what I would consider mostly competent players; an excellent training ground for sure.

What this really meant for practice, though, was that the fundamentals of positioning and clock management would be key as no one was likely to make a big silly error, and so we could expect the games to go long and be solid. Going into any event, I have found it is important to set goals that are achievable and measurable. If you walk into every event planning to win, you’re going to be disappointed almost always. For this one I decided I wanted to learn my match-ups better (a thing you should be doing anyway) but was aiming to finish 4-1. I felt like that was achievable, given the competition, but not a foregone conclusion -- it was something to work towards that was infinitely doable.

I have been running Old Umbrey and loving them, but have found making a caster paradigm I'm happy with kinda hard. The army has a bit of an issue in that guns exist and largely aren't very fast outside of being able to yeet a warbeast or two very far, which means your options for dealing with that feels limited either to Morozov, or Galina and then whatever else you want to play. Out of the two of them, Morozov is far more consistent as an answer, which is why he’s in almost every pairing; he kinda has to be. Hopefully this gets addressed in January by a tweak to Azlanov or Yana but for now, it is what it is. So following the crowd I decided to bring a 4 heavy Galina list, and a Morozv list I mostly borrowed from Dan Riker with one minor change; mostly I swapped out Stryx for the Starved. My thought process was I could drop Galina into most things and be fine, while Morozov would be a bit more targeted to gun lines and maybe one or two other things depending on scenario.

The Tournament

Saturday comes around, and we get over to the conference rooms, the veritable Seth Cohen and Zosia VanMeter are running things. The judges give their normal pre-event spiel, the tables are set and labeled, and the pairings are up!

Round 1

Scenario: Trench Warfare

I get to go into the wonderful Erica Bevenour playing Grymkin. Erica and I have been friends for a while now and never gotten a chance to play in person, though there have been a few matches on Wartable, so I’m excited to get into it with her and it felt very much like she had the same thought. We were joking around and the shit talking began almost immediately. Genuinely I couldn’t have asked for a better start to the day and round 1 opponent. Scenario is Trench Warfare and Erica is running two identical lists with different casters, Old Witch and the Heretic. I opt to follow my strategy and play Galina; Wild Aggression Primeval should put the fear of Morrow in the house, and the extreme threat ranges coupled with the RFP and work heavies should give me a good chance. Erica to no surprise drops the Old Witch. We roll off and I choose to go first, knowing that picking up board space to hopefully bully out an Early Ill Omens is going to be essential when I still have the option to not have to commit super heavy to it.

Turn 1: Everything runs forward, Refuge goes onto my Spear Primeval, Wild Aggression goes to Stryx, and we get the Sword Primeval as many corpses as we can, then we get as far up the table as I can safely. Erica responds by putting the Slaughterhouse within 15 of Stryx and the Sword Primeval, and spreads out around a forest leading to her 50 objective, and popping shroud to give most of her army stealth.

Turn 2: I look at it and decide taking the House before it can do anything is a worthy goal especially without Ill Omens being involved. The totem pops forward to get -1 Arm on the house, Galina goes, feats, rushes the Primeval and shoots the House to get Bait the Line, I reposition back and realize that I’m not supposed to be camping 2. I was supposed to pop Blessing of the Gods, get an extra fury and swap Wild Aggression to the Sword Primeval to crit fish. Well…shoot. I messed that up, but I decide it's still probably worth going for even if my odds just went down because of my own stupidity.

The Primeval gets loaded up to five corpses and charges the House, boosting to hit the charge on the Sword, and surprisingly gets the crit...but that’s the only one it gets, and leaves the House alive on 18. Stryx follows up and manages to get it into the dirt, and has enough fury leftover to use its animus and sprint back to relative safety. The rest of my army positions and we try and push scenario as much as we can. Erica responds by having her Cage Raged kill my Sword Primeval and pushing left hard. The game grinds down from there and finishes after top of 6 with only 17 seconds left on my clock and 1:20 on Erica’s, with her taking scenario 13-10. A great game and a loss that I am honestly not upset about; we both made some mistakes but played as best as we could and had a hell of a time doing it.

Round 2

Scenario: Wolves at Our Heels

I get paired into a gentleman by the name of Elliot I had never met; very friendly guy who immediately struck up a rapport and reminded me why I love this community. Elliot was playing Kithguard running Craighorn and Grimtusk. I honestly did not know what to expect in this match; my experience with Kithguard is incredibly limited but I felt like I could mostly figure it out, and if not, then it would be a great learning game.

Elliot decides to drop Grimtusk, and I drop Galina. I win the roll off and decide going second is the best bet here. Wolves is a very live scenario and from what I know, Kithguard have a very live scenario game. I feel like if I can control the scoring tempo I will be OK.

Turn 1, the trolls run forward with Grimtusk getting Carnivore and taking position behind a big central forest. Treewalker feels like a little more nonsense when someone else is using it against you. Wild Aggression goes onto Stryx, Refuge to the Spear Primeval and I get corpses onto Jezhana. The hounds walk forward and Skrobala rolls max shots and picks up a Guard Tower, before repositioning back out of melee range, planning for a big push and feat next turn to try and get as far ahead on scenario as I can.

On turn 2, Elliot ambushes a Jungle Troll off the board edge and picks up one Liegeman Warden. Grimtusk uses Blessing of the Gods for an extra fury, positions on the left flank in a forest with Chameleon Animus up, and uses Soothing Song to make sure her beasts stay calm. She's camping five Fury and within 15 of my Sword Primeval and Stryx, and importantly, she has a Mistborn base to base behind her. Bottom of 2, I stare at Grimtusk 15” away from two heavies and think there's no way she is safe there, but she is def 19 and camping 5. I measure some things out and realize Jezhana is in range of the Mistborn behind her. I need a boosted 9, but if I get that, she's blinded and drops to def 15, making her much more killable. If it misses, Jezhana is still in a good spot for a feat turn, and I think I can score 4-5 this turn, have my entire army under feat, be safe from a counter assassination and follow through on the original plan. Really, there's no reason to not try it.

Jezhana goes and hits the boosted 9. Game on. Galina goes, and boosts two shots into Grimtusk to get Bait the Line, puts 7 damage on her and forces one transfer out. Stryx charges with Wild Aggression needing boosted 8s...and misses the charge and second initial. Not great. Thankfully he doesn’t miss another attack and leaves Grimtusk camping 1 fury on 4 boxes. The Primeval gets rush from the feral and charges with just the Sword in range. He hits the boosted 9 on the charge, forcing out her last transfer, then buys and boosts to hit with his second attack, hitting and Grimtusk toughs, before I buy my last attack and auto hit to kill her. I realize afterwards I never moved my totem forward for the -1 Arm aura, which would have helped tremendously (running the numbers after its a 90% to kill her if the blind hits). I then get a nice long break to go cool down.

Credit: Wolfe_The_Cat

Round 3

Scenario: High Stakes

After the nice break afforded by an early assassination, round 3 pairings go up and I get paired into another Jersey local, Cori, who is another wonderfully friendly gentlemen playing Necrofactorium, specifically Nekkane and Sepsira. I am not sure what he will drop but the assassination angles in both lists has me concerned, so I decide to drop Morozov. Cori drops Sepsira, wins the roll off, and decides to go second.

Forced Evolution goes on the Starved, and Fog of War goes up, while we take up table space and try to hold the middle and we position around each other. Top of 2 I move the Starved up to contest the middle 50. I figure with Forced Evolution and Star Crossed from Jezhana, he is going to be a bear for Mechanithralls to hit and there are a lot of them, mostly just positioned to score my side of the board. Cori does the same; he puts Last Word on a Sludge Thrall before moving them up the left to contest my 40, in a cheeky attempt to kill a bunch of my Liegemen with the explosion when I have to feat it; he goes up 2-1 bottom of 2.

Top of 3, Morozov feats and combo smites the Sludge Thrall away. I send the Starved and the Sword Primeval into the middle to clear it out with the Primeval contesting his 40, and clear my flag, scoring 3 and going up 4-2. Cori responds by flooding the middle, but Star Crossed is too much for the thralls and he leaves the Starved alive on 5 and doesn’t contest my flag, bringing the score 5-4.

On top of 4 there are a bunch of Thralls; clearing them is hard and dice are not helping, but since the Starved gets a bunch of corpses to heal and goes back up to 18 boxes or so, I contest and just focus on killing models, bringing the score 6-4. Bottom of 4, Cori is running out of models and just keeps jamming, using the feat to push me from his flag and some cheeky slams to get the hounds out of the picture. Score 6-5. Top of 5 I finally manage to clear out most of the fighting units, and with both heavies still alive in the middle manage to go up 8-5. Cori, out of front line pieces and mostly down to supports, does what he can but ultimately cannot clear and contest, and ends his turn with 1 second left scoring me 9-5 and getting the scenario win.

Credit: Wolfe_The_Cat

Round 4

Scenario: Two Fronts

After three rounds, a bunch of us go to dinner to celebrate Dan Riker's birthday and chat games for the day. I’m feeling good going into day 2 and when Sunday comes and pairings go up, I see I am playing into House Kallus piloted by Mr. Jimmy Livingston. Following the trend of everyone else I had the pleasure to play, Jimmy immediately starts cracking jokes. We talk about the day before while setting up and he wins the roll off opting to go second. He drops Hellyth and again I drop Galina, knowing dusk doesn’t love anything that makes things harder to hit and outside Hellyth feat turn its likely to be an issue.

Turn 1 is positioning and the usual shenanigans that come with it. Top of 2 I realize I can get a Sword Primeval onto the Void Engine and with Galina feat reposition it behind the House, so Imperitus will either have to swing over the obstruction making it Def 17 (15 with Hellyth feat) or be under Star Crossed from Jezhana, both of which just sound miserable for him and gets me a boostable gun and some very annoying solos out of the list. I decide to go for it; I flood the middle and feat, picking up the Void Engine and a few Soulless Guardians. The Chosen of the Witch sit on the left flank under feat and get the Set Defense command card to really discourage the Last Watch from even trying to come in. Jimmy cracks back with Imperitus and leaves the Primeval grievously wounded but alive on 4 boxes, but importantly without a way to clear the center flag. He scores 2 to my 1.

Top of 3, the Sword Primeval frenzies and misses his attack (sad trombone) but is still engaging Imperitus with Vorony keeping him securely stuck in place. Jezhana charges Imperitus, getting Starve on him, and I burn Careful Recon to back her into the Trench. My game plan at this point is to not commit into Imperitus unless I absolutely have to and just keep pushing scenario instead. My ambushing hunters swarm his 40 and I clear and score mine, my 50, and the flag, going up 4-2.

Imperitus kills the Primeval and Prisoner 102822 gets to live the dream, eating through four dudes before stopping in my backfield and the last of Lanyssa's Beasts back up to score the 50 on that side. He can't clear and score his 40, though, so the score goes 5-3. Galina starts backing out of the middle, knowing assassination is going to start looking more temping and making a point to get out of dodge. The Spear Primeval manages to hit the crit on Lanyssa's Beast and pushes it off scoring/contesting, letting me score the 50 side; the last Chosen of the Witch charges the Prisoner and doesn’t kill her, but does 4 boxes and blinds her. Bringing the score 9-3. Jimmy makes a valiant last stand but, low on clock, is only able to clear his 40 and contest before calling it a turn and giving me the win 9-4 on scenario.

Round 5

Scenario: Payload

Going into the final round at 3-1, I’m feeling good. Pairings drop and I see I get to play into the one and only JVM. Jake and I have played a few times over the years between various cons but with his dislike of the mirror he's always tried to dodge me when possible. This time, though, he can't dodge and we're both excited for what is sure to be a great game. To no surprise he drops Lylyth and again I drop Galina. I wish I could remember more of this game, but it was round 5 and intense. We both made some very silly errors early on and he capitalized on it better then I did, on top of both of us having a turn where boosted 8s were apparently a thing that was unheard of for our dice. We played it out to turn 7 where he took me on scenario 21-17, with him having two seconds left on clock and me having a little over a minute, and the only models left on the table being Lylyth, a Shadowmancer Scion, two Hydrix (one which was two-thirds dead), Galina, a Feral, the Totem and one of the Hounds. It was an absolute drag out slugfest that I learned a lot from and was glad to play.

Credit: Wolfe_The_Cat

The Aftermath

So after all is said and done, I finished the weekend 3-2 and 12th place overall. Goal not exactly accomplished, but both losses were close. Importantly, what did we learn? First, the clock training is working; aside from game one I was doing better then every person I played into on time. It made those later turns much easier and gave me a little more space to make sure I wasn’t making a mistake. Definitely a thing I think I’m going to continue doing for the foreseeable future. Second, knowing when to play an off list is important. I feel like my match into Dusk might have been a Morozov drop, and while things worked out this time it is absolutely a thing I need to learn more and make sure I’m dropping the right list into the right matches. Third, a highly mobile battle group is only as good as your positioning and fury management. Multiple games with the Galina list I had beasts taking frenzy checks turn over turn. You need to be able to manage the fury or plan for things to frenzy and be pleasantly surprised if they don’t, but you cant walk into every turn 2-4 FURY over, and think nothing bad is going to happen. Additionally I was positioning Stryx like he is any old heavy, and not taking advantage of flight and admonition as much as I can/should have.

Honestly great lessons to learn, a great experience and one I’m happy to have done, will likely do again. If you've made it this far, thanks for reading and see ya next time.
