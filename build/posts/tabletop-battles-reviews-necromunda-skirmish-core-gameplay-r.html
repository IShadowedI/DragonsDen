Tabletop Battles Reviews: Necromunda Skirmish - Core Gameplay Rules

August 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer
What a glorious day! The new Necromunda rulebook, together with the other contents of the ‘Necromunda Skirmish’ box set, are in our sweaty hands, and we can now share our thoughts on the changes to the best skirmish scale game Games Workshop makes. We would like to thank them for providing us with an advanced copy of Necromunda: Skirmish for review.

The announcement of this new edition of the rules caused quite a stir online amongst the denizens of the underhive. Doom-mongering ran rampant, as players speculated on the use of words like ‘streamlined’ or phrases like ‘every dice roll matters’ in the promotional article. At the worst, some people were despairing because they thought that the game would be redesigned around competitive, one-off games, similar to Kill Team. We can now say, with great confidence and pleasure, that is not the case. In fact, the rulebook calls out early on in a sidebar that Necromunda is ‘Not intended to be competitive’ and instead encapsulate a spirit of down and dirty underhive chaos (small c). 

We are overall bullish about the changes. The core gameplay loop is basically the same. Some important details about moving, shooting, close combat and incurring (and recovering from) injuries have been changed; we think most of these are positive, although some are simply different, and some we can’t yet predict the full consequences of. As you might imagine, there is a lot of content to pick through here, and we have released our authors from all constraints of brevity, like Daedalus & Icarus from the labyrinth – let’s hope this goes better. So, in this main article, we are going to cover the changes to the core rules, explaining changes to how models and concepts are categorised, and how they work during a game of Necromunda. 

This article is meant to be read together with the articles on Scenario/Campaign rules, XP, Advances & Skills, Gangs, and the Trading Post. There are a lot of moving parts in Necromunda!

Changes to Core Rules/Terms/Basics

The first thing that jumps out in the new rules is that they begin by explaining some core concepts and terms. This isn’t actually new, the previous, 2023 rulebook featured a similar section, but it does feel like the design structure has been tightened up in this iteration.

Format & Definitions

Models are defined as either Fighters or Vehicles. As we’ll explore in detail later on, Vehicles have very important differences, but they are now much more consistent with how Fighters operate, in their stats, movement and Skills. It’s easier now because whenever things treat Fighters & Vehicles differently - certain Skills, for example, are unavailable to Vehicles - this language is consistently built in.

Models’ stats are displayed slightly differently. Initiative is now a simple number, e.g. a 4 rather than a 4+. This means that a higher stat is better. Initiative Tests are taken on a D6, rolling equal or under to pass, just as Strength or Toughness tests are. The ‘mental stats’, Cool, Leadership, Intelligence & Willpower are also presented as simple numbers, so again, a higher stat is better - their tests are still taken on 2D6. 

All models now have a Save stat on their profile, e.g. all of the Goliath & Escher models in the starter set have Save 6+. This is reflected in changes to Advances (models can improve their Save sometimes) and equipment (armour works either by improving the model’s Save and/or granting other benefits). See the relevant sections for more details.

This will be covered in detail in the sections on resolving damage, and in the Trading Post  article, but weapon statlines have changes. To-hit bonuses for differing ranges are gone. The Damage stat has been replaced with Lethality, which is slightly different.

Arbitrator’s Toolkit rules are little boxes scattered throughout the core rules which offer an optional extra or alternative rule that some Arbitrators or groups may prefer. Resolving Stray Shots, for example, is now one of these, rather than baked into the core rules by default. We are big fans of this approach, since it allows players to either play the game in its new, cleaned up state, or add on their preferred approaches. It’s also an endorsement of the community-led, customisable ethos of Necromunda as a game.

We will mention one such toolkit now - the rulebook assumption is that pre-measuring is allowed. Forcing players to guess at range is now listed as an Arbitrator’s Toolkit option. This is a great example of that method being used to present the best of both worlds, by endorsing groups’ freedom to adopt the method they prefer.

Status

The lexicon of the game has developed from the 2023 rules – we can see the same concepts followed through. Now, Models (Fighters and Vehicles) exist at all times in one of 4 Statuses:

  • Active. This is the default Status, without the restrictions of any of the others.
  • Engaged covers models in melee combat - this depends on base to base contact, and as before, models can’t move within 1” of an enemy (barring those already Seriously Injured/Damaged) except via Actions which explicitly allow them to. The big thing about Engaged is that it restricts the models to Fight or Retreat Actions. It has also become far riskier to shoot at allies Engaged with enemies - see the Shooting section.
    • This Status differentiates between Fighters, who are always mutually Engaged when in base contact with each other, and Vehicles. When Fighters Activate, within 1” of an enemy Vehicle, or a Vehicle Activates within 1” of an enemy Model, they can choose to count as Engaged. The rest of the time, Vehicles aren’t considered Engaged with other Models. What this means, essentially, is that Fighters can Charge/Engage Vehicles, and vice versa, to make close combat attacks, and can continue attacking them if they’re still adjacent in later activations. But they won’t stay Engaged with each other - both remain fair targets for opposing shooters, and either Model can choose to move away, shoot point-blank, or do whatever else when it next comes to Activate. 
  • Suppressed. This is the new version of Pinned, usually incurred in the same ways (generally being hit by shooting, or falling off something). There is a slight mechanical difference in that it no longer simulates models going Prone by making them crawl, or restricting the type of Actions they can do. A Suppressed model simply has one Action when it Activates, rather than two (so it can’t take a Double Action either). That’s often the same in effect, and allows for shortening the list of Actions in the game. But, in a major change, models automatically recover from this state at the end of their Activation. 
  • Seriously Injured (Fighters) or Seriously Damaged (Vehicles) are extremely vulnerable. As before, this Status is usually entered by Models rolling an Injury dice and getting the associated symbol. Vehicles are now dealt with in almost exactly the same way as Fighters (see their Section). Seriously Injured/Damaged models have very restricted Actions, which we’ll go through below, they can only really move slightly, leave the battlefield voluntarily, or concentrate on trying to Recover. 
    • A major and interesting change is that just as recovering from Suppressed, models must roll to see if they Recover from Serious Injury/Damage at the end of their Activation (and there are some ways to make that roll during an allied model’s Activation) - it doesn’t happen in the End Phase. This is one of the biggest changes to the gameplay loop, and we need to play more games to really understand all the implications. Beyond being a simple change in the order of operations, if a model rolls its Recovery Test when an enemy model is within 1” (this includes Engaged or Suppressed enemies, but not those which are Seriously Injured/Damaged themselves) they automatically go Out of Action (OOA). This change puts the onus on the downed model to crawl away however they can.

There is a one change we were surprised by – when a model Recovers from being Seriously Injured, it goes straight back to being Active, it doesn’t change to Suppressed. This seems a bit odd – hard to imagine even the toughest underhive ganger tying off a tourniquet with their teeth and then springing back to their feet – but it works fine from a mechanical point of view.

These states are all exclusive, which helps to remove confusion, and they are simpler and neater than the previous attempt to define them. One interesting practical change is that they are recommended to be denoted with Status Tokens (see our review of the starter box contents) rather than the old-cromunda method of lying them face-down or face-up. That method is noted as an optional house rule for Arbitrators.

Subtypes

To handle the complexity of Necromunda’s cast of characters, models can have a variety of Subtypes. Think of these as baked-in special rules. Some models inherently gain Skills as part of their Subtype - that may seem like unnecessary layering, but there is a reason for it. Skills cease working when models are Injured (reduced to 0 Wounds). See the section on resolving damage.

Lots of Subtypes simply define types of Fighters, which is a much neater presentation than the previous rulebook and puts all those rules under one section:

  • Prospects are defined as models that don’t cause Nerve Tests in nearby allies when taken OOA, and gain the Ganger and Specialist Subtypes on reaching 13XP. Specialists, promoted or recruited, get to pick a starting Skill from a list of archetypes. Gangers (we think all Specialists will also have the Ganger Subtype) promote to Champions at 37XP. Champions can lend their Cool to nearby models for Nerve checks and have the Inspiring Skill (makes Group Activation easier), as do Leaders. All of this is really part of the structure that governs XP and Advances, so go read that article. It all seems more elegant than in previous rulebooks.

    • There are still a lot of unknowns about Gang composition, but there is no Juve Subtype - we think they may be rolled in with Prospects, in terms of a Subtype, but almost certainly the unique N18 Prospects’ equipment, and the option to have more plainly equipped rookie Fighters, will remain available - example text calls out the Van Saar Subtek. 
  • Brutes get an inherent Skill, Juggernaut (resistance to Suppression, similar to N18 Spyrer’s resistance to Pinning - see the Skills article), and should they be deleted from your gang roster, all equipment is lost, not added to the stash. That same downside applies to Hangers-On, who can be excluded from Random Crew generation as the player wishes. 

    • These relatively brief caveats make us think that recruitment and progression for these Subtypes may be more similar to that of ‘ordinary’ gang members, which would be nice. Even if their equipment is still heavily restricted, it will be nice to have Hangers-On, especially, gain XP, and allow them to be used more regularly on the tabletop.
  • Beasts cannot use the Interact action, assist allies with Recovery tests or take the Treat Ally action, nor can they offer their Leadership to help allies avoid Bottling. This is a separate Subtype from Pets (although we imagine many of what N18 called Exotic Beasts will have both Subtypes). Pets have a much cleaner and pithier set of rules which essentially makes them operate as intended previously. They have a Leash range to their owner and can’t voluntarily move outside it; if they are outside it when their owner Activates, they are considered Panicked and must take the allowed Actions for their current Status to get back to them, or as close as possible. 
  • Support Fighters can always be included in a starting Crew, don’t count against the Crew limit on numbers, and can never be targeted by ranged attacks unless they are the closest model. We have no idea what models this is going to be applied to. Perhaps Servo Skulls or something? As far as we can see, such models would still get their own Activation, so having them, over the normal Crew numbers, could potentially be very powerful. We need to see some examples to know. 

Some other Subtypes are more special properties - remember that models can have multiple Subtypes:

  • Flying is pretty self explanatory, they can move over things, and never take damage or end up Suppressed from falling.
  • Wyrd just lets the model take Wyrd Powers, which they can gain during Advances as if they were a Secondary Skill set - see the article for more details.
  • Loners can’t lend their Leadership out for Bottle checks, unless every single ally left is a Beast or Loner. This will probably apply to certain types of weirdo Champions.
  • Mounted is definitely the most complex. As before, you can create alternate fighter cards for Mounted models, and field them with or without their mounts (Fighters can’t dismount during a battle). Their Mounted versions can’t use Heavy or Paired weapons, or use a secondary weapon in close combat. The convoluted Ash Wastes rules for movement speed, drive by attacks and such are all gone, thankfully. Instead, Mounted Fighters automatically get the Nerves of Steel and Hit & Run Skills - essentially, they might not be Suppressed by incoming fire and can Consolidate out of ongoing close combats. Finally, Mounted Fighters are restricted to one 90-degree turn during any Charge or Dash Actions (so in any Activation when they’re trying to move at full speed), but that restriction is dropped if they have the Agile Subtype.
    • Genghis: Look, that’s sort of a sub-Subtype, and this structure isn’t exactly simple, but to me it seems easier and neater than it has been hitherto.

There is a sub-section of Vehicle Subtypes, but we will deal with those in the Vehicle section below, since many readers may not use Vehicles often and prefer to continue on through the core rules. 

Conditions

These are, well, conditions, that models can gain or lose throughout the game. This means they can overlap with a model’s Status, Subtype etc. 

Most universally, models can be Injured/Damaged, when reduced to 0 Wounds - this turns off all their Skills. A big change which we will have to play with to see how impactful it really is. Intoxicated is a relatively minor condition which will probably crop up in certain characterful scenarios or models’ special rules. There are several psychological conditions: Frenzy forces models to attempt a Charge, if in range of an enemy when they Activate, but also grants +1 Attack. Hatred (X) specifies a certain type of enemy or an individual model; the user can re-roll all close combat hits against that hated enemy in the first clash of combat. Fearsome forces a Willpower check for enemies trying to attack the user in close combat, with failure setting their WS to 6+. Terrifying forces the check before enemies can Charge or Engage the user, ending their Activation should they fail. In a bit of complexity reminiscent of Warhammer Fantasy Battle / The Old World, all Terrifying models are also Fearsome, and are immune to both rules. Fearsome models ignore enemies’ Fearsome, unless that enemy also has Terrifying. Phew.

Webbed is now a condition, stopping all movement and imposing -1 to Hit rolls. The affected model gets a chance, at the end of every Activation, to shake it off via a Strength check. That’s obviously still a punishing condition, but compared to N18’s webs, which were basically automatically Seriously Injuring their targets, this is much less devastating. We’ll go into it in the Trading Post article. Insanity now appears far more complex, with a D6 roll made when the affected model Activates. This gives a table of what they do, depending on whether they are Unengaged, Engaged or Seriously Injured/Damaged (it essentially makes the model ignore the Suppressed Status). This gives 18 possible results, but they are mostly iterations of moving towards or away from this or that. 4 of the results lead to shooting or fighting indiscriminately. This is one entire page, and seems emblematic of the N26 ethos. It’s an insane piece of cruft, but sensibly and coherently arranged. Rather inappropriate for a representation of going stark raving mad! Wibble. 

The Turn Sequence, End Phase, and Bottling Changes

Slightly out of sequence, we should just note that removal of several negative Statuses or Conditions is resolved (automatically or via a roll) at the end of the affected model’s Activation, where it might have been at the end of the Round in N18. This will take some getting used to. Examples are removing the Suppressed Status, attempting Recovery from the Seriously Injured/Damaged Status, or removal of the Webbed Condition.

The other major changes to Necromunda’s order of operations are in the End Phase of each Round. The sequence starts, as before, with Bottle Checks. Now, these are only taken only if a gang had any models taken OOA during the Round. A gang tests on the highest Leadership their crew has on the table. If passed, fine. If failed, the player must choose 1 model and remove them (they can’t choose one Engaged, unless all models are Engaged). If boxcars are rolled, the player must remove two models! Despite that little bit of random spice, failing a Bottle Check is now far less punishing, and failing one shouldn’t automatically cost you the game, unless it’s a very desperate endgame stage. Bad luck on multiple Bottle Checks, of course, will still have a cumulative effect, and Leadership may now be a more important stat. The check is untethered from your overall numbers, so you could start flaking away immediately. But equally, you’ll never dissolve completely from a position of strength. We think this is great. Losing a model can be crucial, especially in the endgame of a close fought battle. But it won’t be the terminus of 90% of games any more.

We’ll discuss this more in the article on Scenarios, but Voluntarily Bottling is now only an option once a third of the number of the starting crew have gone OOA. It’s done at the start of checking for victory step. Everyone leaves, and the gang automatically loses the scenario. Voluntarily bottling remains a good option to avoid getting thrashed without mercy. But this change has fixed the two major issues - first, players have to at least partly engage with the game, they can’t leave after suffering 1 casualty when they had 10 models at the start. Second, this scotches any Scenario wonkiness where you could achieve objectives, then just leave and win the game. 

  • Genghis: Of course, that interaction could be argued as appropriate for some hit and run narrative missions in the underhive. But, leaving that option open wasn’t worth the price - it was too unsatisfying for the opponent. It’s better this way.

Actions

There has been a cull of some Actions, either because they were rarely used and/or because they aren’t needed with the new structure of models’ States and Sub-Types.

Remember that the Suppressed Status, in replacing Pinned/Prone, has simplified the list somewhat. Actions are no longer Basic/Simple. Instead they are simply Free, Single or Double. In some cases that means common combinations in N18, like Move+Move, or Aim+Shoot, have been effectively replaced with Double Actions. We will cover the changed options here:

  • Dash (Double) is the new way to move as far as possible in a model’s Activation, rather than being twice its Move stat, it is Move+Initiative. This will in almost all cases reduce models’ straightline speed from N18. It also makes mobility more difficult to pump up by repeatedly picking Move when choosing Advances. A perfectly fine change, one of several which boosts the importance of Initiative. 
  • Charge (Double) is now Move+D6”, and Engage (Single) now exists, letting a fighter move D6” to try and get into melee, even if they were Suppressed. These are welcome changes which will make it less reliable for shooting gangs to keep melee-seeking opponents back, and make those melee models less dependent on Skills that resist Suppressed Status. Both ways of moving into combat allow the Active model to make a free Fight or Coup de Grace action, as before. 
  • Resolving close combat changed significantly (we’ll get to that later), but in terms of Actions, we just have to note that Coup de Grace is no longer an automatic OOA result - the Active Fighter must roll equal or higher than their target on a D6+Strength roll to take them out. That’s a hefty knock to the traditional underhive tactic of sending in a cheap Fighter to cut the throats of downed enemies. But note, with the new timing of Recovery attempts, even a failed attempt to Coup de Grace means the surviving, Seriously Injured model will then need to be rescued, or spend its own Activations crawling desperately away, to avoid automatic OOA later. The same logic applies to Seriously Damaged Vehicles, although there, a new Action, Sabotage (Single), has been introduced - the Active fighter must pass an Intelligence check to send the Vehicle OOA. Cool. 
  • Fight is (Double), which removes the occasional edge case where a Fighter could start their Activation in melee, put their opponent down, and then do something else. 
  • Retreat is also (Double), but is now risk free, it doesn’t require a roll or provoke attacks from the model’s opponent in melee. The moved distance is now equal to the model’s Initiative (in inches). This change means being caught in melee by a stronger opponent isn’t an inevitable death, if you can survive once you might get away. But, a model can’t Retreat and blast away at its erstwhile opponent in the same activation - they’ll need some help from a friend, or a lucky Priority roll later.
  • Shoot (Single) and Aimed Shot (Double) are effectively the same as N18’s Actions, but both stipulate the weapon used cannot be Heavy. Those guns need the user to take the Braced Shot (Double) Action. Note that Vehicles use the same Actions, or can take the Fire All (Double) Action 
  • Group Activation is now a Single Action and requires a Ld test. But it’s open to anyone, and you can try it (with cumulative -1 penalties to the test) on more than one friendly within 3”. Because all Leaders/Champions can take this as a Free Action via their Inspiring Skill, the main change is the Leadership requirement, and its nice to have an actual use for that very thematic stat. But we also really like the idea that in certain situations, a common Ganger, or even a Prospect, can step up and try to carry their mates along with them in some badly thought out plan. It may lead to some really cinematic moments, rewarding players who keep their eyes open for the opportunity.
  • We’ve mentioned that Recovery from Serious Injury/Damage happens automatically at the end of the model’s Activation. But, there’s also Treat Ally or Repair - only available to Fighters, these let a Fighter/Vehicle  within 1” make a Recovery test immediately. respectively. The same rule as in N18 applies - having a friend there to help, at the point of making the Recovery test, lets you roll an additional Injury dice and pick the desired result. These are excellent tactical options to have and will make the action economy around Seriously Injured/Damaged models more interesting.

Speaking of those poor saps, there are some Actions which are only available to the Seriously Injured/Damaged:

  • Fighters can Tend Wounds (Double) and Vehicles Conduct Repairs (Double). Both allow the model to roll an additional Injury Dice at their end-of-Activation Recovery test. This will of course be the default choice for Seriously Injured/Damaged models which don’t have an enemy standing over them.
  • Fighters can Crawl (Double) their Strength in inches, while Vehicles can Freewheel (Double) up to D6”. These options will be forced upon players sometimes, because they can’t end their Activation within 1” of an enemy, it will automatically take them OOA. 
  • Fighters or Vehicles can attempt a Desperate Escape (Double), where they voluntarily go OOA. Why would you ever do this? Well, beyond denying your opponent the satisfaction and XP, it gives you a good chance (3+ roll) to escape with no ill effects. On a 1-2 you roll for Lasting Injury/Damage as normal. This option is quite cool, it mitigates the potentially unfun experience of players just have to crawl round while their opponents bully them.

Changes to Core Gameplay

  • Allies within 1” give +1 Injury dice as now. (limit of 1)
  • If an enemy (who’s not also Seriously Inj/Dam) is within 1”, the fighter automatically goes OOA. This to some extent replaces the CdG action, which is is no longer Automatic.
  • Suppressed state replaces Pinned. Only 1 action in your activation, but recover automatically at the end of that activation.

Terrain & Movement

The basic rules for terrain and how models interact with it are similar to the previous edition: difficult terrain slowing models’ movement by half, measuring distance around solid terrain, all that is the same. The biggest change is that models can no longer move through friendly models. This makes life substantially more difficult for grouped fighters in complex terrain.

The other biggest change involves cover. Line of Sight is broadly done the same way. But, now a model either has cover - it (not its base) is obscured from the firer by terrain or other models - or it doesn’t. There’s no differentiation between ‘partial’ and ‘full’ cover as in N18. 

  • Genghis: experienced players may miss the granularity of levels of cover. After all, standing behind a solid barricade that covers you feet to neck seems safer than standing with your knees behind a pipe. But Infinity moved to this system around 6 years ago, and I can say from long experience that I find it preferable. It reduces the number of disagreements and fiddly investigations into whether a model is >50% obscured. It won’t eliminate them completely, but most players find it easier to agree whether a model is completely in the open or not. 
Doors now work differently. Opening or closing them is not an Action; instead, it costs a model 2” from its Movement to open or close a door, and they have to be within 1” to do so (they can move through that proximity, they don’t have to be there at the start of their Activation). This is a change to the action economy around doors. Effectively, models will still have to declare Move, Dash, Charge or Engage (risky!) to get through a closed door, but if they can do so, it gives them more options. Together with other tweaks, this should remove the rather absurd situations that sometimes occurred, where two melee Fighters would stand either side of a door, neither wanting to open it, for fear of receiving a Charge from their opposite number. The Interact (Single) Action can still be used near terminals to lock/unlock doors, or doors can be battered down - they are T5, and if Wounded 4 times (no need to roll Injury dice, or any saves) they are destroyed and removed. That’s cool, but honestly so niche we can’t remember what the previous rules for destroying doors were! Agility Tests are the new method for what used to be jumping over gaps, and, importantly, falling over the edge when Suppressed on a ledge. These tests are by default a simple 4+. Skills etc can modify this, e.g. all Escher get +1! 

Shooting

First off, there are some slight tweaks to how ranged weapons actually hit:

    • Just as before, a natural 1 will always miss, and now a natural 6 automatically hits. No more process for unlikely shots on a 7+. Because cover no longer applies a to-hit penalty, most shooting rolls probably won’t go so high anyway.
    • On the other hand, weapons no longer grant built-in bonuses (or penalties) to hit at favoured ranges. A major blow to certain builds, and we’ll look at this more in the Trading Post section.
    • Shooting at Seriously Injured models applies -1 to hit. There is no penalty, or any restriction, to shooting at Suppressed models, even when they’re in cover. This is a massive change, and makes life far easier for gangs whose primary plan is to shoot the opposition off the table.
  • When shooting at Engaged models, all hits are randomised between models in the combat. An excellent change which makes it impossible for high-BS models to snipe enemies locked in desperate hand to hand combat with their allies. It also extends to Blast/Template weapons - if they clip an ongoing combat, all models involved cop a hit.

Some rules which have been well established in N18 are now Arbitrator’s Toolkit options. Stray Shots, testing the shooter’s Cool to pick a target other than the closest (this is now recommended to only apply when that closest model is within 12” - sensible), and splitting Rapid Fire hits amongst grouped enemies are all in this category. Now we have fond memories generated by each of those rules, and we are all the kind of Necromunda sickos that relish the complexity. But, all of them were commonly forgotten about by newer or more casual players, so we can see the logic here.  

Those changes are all quite significant, but there’s another category which seems a very striking change, and that’s ammunition rules. Now, only weapons with the Rapid Fire(X) or Ammo(X+) rules can run out of ammunition. All other weapons simply keep functioning. You don’t even have to roll the Firepower dice when rolling to hit for weapons that don’t have those rules. For those weapons that do run dry, Reload (Single) is an automatic success, but some weapons have Scarce(X+). This means that to Reload, the model must pass an X+ roll. The complexity comes in with the interaction of those rules. Weapons with Rapid Fire(2) or higher, and/or weapons with both Rapid Fire(X) and Ammo(X+) can permanently jam and be unfixable for the remainder of the battle. We’ll explore those interactions in depth, and look at the weapons with such combinations, in our Trading Post article. The bottom line is that many weapons don’t have to worry about ammunition at all. Some weapons will run out of ammo, somewhat more frequently than before, but will be reliably Reloaded. A few usually very punchy, weapons are even more likely to run out of ammo, and to be permanently jammed for the duration of the battle. 

Close Combat

The changes here might be even wider than those to shooting. We already mentioned the expanded range of Charge and the introduction of Engage. Instead of taking the Active model’s attacks, and then Reaction Attacks from their target(s), models now resolve their attacks in Initiative order. A charging model gains +2 Initiative and Unwieldy weapons set the user’s Initiative to 1, before modifiers (shades of The Old World again). But, only the Active model makes their full complement of Attacks (they can also claim +1 from Charging and +1 from Frenzy if they have it, and can use a Secondary weapon (see below). Any and all other models in the combat make just 1 Attack each, none of the usual bonuses can be applied. This is a tremendous shift in the advantage towards the Active model. Initiative has become far more valuable for melee types, but the Charging model will often strike first, and is now risking less of a clap back. These changes are really quite dramatic and need some experimentation to sink in. We are excited to see how the meta changes in terms of Initiative, Unwieldy weapons, and all the other close combat-relevant kit.

The long-established advantages of fighting with two melee weapons have also changed. The Active fighter can pick a Primary weapon to fight with, and as long as it’s not Heavy, can pick a Secondary weapon as well. They make their full Attacks, including other bonuses, with the Primary weapon, and take 1 extra attack with the Secondary weapon. Other models in the combat just pick a Primary weapon and take their 1 attack with that. Light ranged weapons (i.e. pistols) can be picked as either weapon type but can never roll more than 1 attack each, nor do they use Rapid Fire in close combat. 

Hit rolls are done via the attacker’s WS as before, but note that no weapons have any inbuilt hit bonuses. Traits and weapon profiles overall have changed around lots, so check out that article. Headline news, the Versatile trait is gone completely. We can see why, it didn’t play well with the new method of Engaged as a Status, exclusive from the others, and it was always a bit janky taking close combat attacks against enemies who couldn’t fight back.

Along with all the other changes around Seriously Injured fighters, when an Active model puts its opponent down in melee, it no longer procs a free Coup De Grace Action. That’s quite a blow to the immediate lethality of strong melee fighters, but of course standing within 1” of a Seriously Injured enemy is exactly where you want to be now.

Consolidation moves are now the model’s Initiative in inches, so generally further, but models still can’t end them in the Engaged state.

We're not sure of the full consequences of close combat changes for tactics. Perhaps this will shift canny players towards highly resilient fighters, using super high Lethality and/or Damage(X) weapons (see the Trading Post article for details), even if they are Unwieldy. Or perhaps Initiative will be prioritised. Both have advantages. After all, the bonus to Initiative applies on Charging, not Engaging, so in new Necromunda, perhaps it will be more likely for combats to involve simultaneous attacks, albeit with more flowing from the Active model. 

Resolving Damage

For both shooting and combat, there are big changes to how damage is applied. First, all Saves automatically fail on a natural 1 or 2. This essentially hardlocks any 2+ saves out of the game. It’s especially important because of the cover bonuses to shots at long range - even expensively armoured models in cover can’t count on being invulnerable. 

Lethality replaces Damage as a weapon stat. Lethality dictates the number of Injury Dice rolled, but only if the target is reduced to 0 Wounds. Any hits before that point effectively deal 1 Wound, Lethality not withstanding. There is a weapon trait, Damage(X) which makes the weapon take that number of Wounds from the target. So essentially, a weapon with Lethality 3 (L3) and Damage(3) is even better than an N18 weapon with D3. But the number of weapons with the Damage(X) trait seems much reduced in this edition (as far as the knowledge we have now). 

The actual results of the Injury dice are also adjusted. What was the Flesh Wound symbol is now Injured (Damaged for Vehicles): a model reduced to 0 Wounds gains no benefit from any Skills. Because it’s automatic for any model on 0 Wounds remaining, a Seriously Injured model is normally also subject to this condition. Now N18’s Flesh Wounds could be very punishing, especially if multiples built up, but Injured will, in some cases, be more of an immediate restraint on a model’s offensive capacity.

The 2023-introduced cumulative nature of multiple Flesh Wound/Serious Injury results, however, has been walked back. If a model is reduced to 0 Wounds and then wounded again umpteen more times, they can remain Injured/Damaged or Seriously Injured/Damaged indefinitely, should they roll the right Injury dice results each time. The only ways to go OOA are to roll the skull on the Injury dice, to take a Recovery test within 1” of an enemy, or to have an enemy succeed in a Coup de Grace or Sabotage action against you. We’re not sure this is necessarily a positive change, but so many things have changed simultaneously that we can’t be sure.

Nerve Checks still apply for allies within 3”, but now only when a model goes OOA, not on Serious Injury. Models which fail become Suppressed, the Broken condition has apparently been removed from the game. This is understandable, while thematic, it was a bit of a pain to administer. It also allows for more opportunities for nearby friends to offer assistance with Recovery (Suppressed models are allowed to do so), which should lead to cool experiences.

The Lasting Injuries table is broadly similar to before. Results 11-16 are positive effects, notably the ability to gain Hatred against the House/Gang/Fighter, for differing results, that caused the OOA. 21-26 are Out Cold, with 31-46 Recovery (it’s back, Convalescence is gone). 51-56 are Recovery and a stat decrease, 61-62 is Captured, 63-65 is a doc’s visit, which is rather more forgiving than before - we’ll look at those below. 66, as ever, is a Memorable Death. The rules now side with what was a fairly common house rule - if multiple OOAs were caused at once, simply roll once on the table. 

A new Lasting Damage table is here for Vehicles, and sticks more closely to the shape of the Lasting Injuries table. The main differences are the stats which can be dinged, but also, amusingly, on several results, most devastatingly on 66, the vehicle can blow up and inflict hits on nearby models.

The changes to the Captured result are presumably in the name of simplification. Now it provokes a Post-Battle roll: on a 1, the model dies trying to escape. 2-4, Ransomed, the owner must pay the captors D6x10 credits or the model dies, but the owner keeps its equipment. If paid the model goes into Recovery. 5-6, the model escapes and rejoins its gang for free, but still goes into Recovery. This process is actually the least convoluted that Capturing has ever been in Necromunda! Some grognards may miss the interaction of Rescue missions, but they were always a nightmare to administer in group campaigns, with the vagaries of both players becoming available to play each other again, quite apart from the balance issues with the old Rescue mission. This is better. Our advice to groups that miss it, is there’s nothing stopping you writing your own house rules, based on the old system or otherwise.

For Critical Injury, doc visits are still a Post-Cycle action, needing a Leader/Champion, which does have an opportunity cost, but the credit cost is now just 30 credits. The D6 table is still death on a 1, but now only 2-3 are permanent stat decreases, 4-6 are just Recovery. Additionally, gangs can pay 50 credits for +1 on the table, and that’s not capped. So a total of 80 credits will ensure the fighter lives, and 180 will ensure they don’t cop any permanent debuffs. This is a forgiving change which may again attract some ire from old salts who liked the frequency of permadeath.

  • Genghis: I personally am a fan, I usually ran the ‘Necromundan Health Service’ rule in my campaigns, where doc fees were only D6x10 credits. In N18 rules, it was all too common for it to simply not be worth paying to try and save a critically injured fighter, compared to hiring their identical twin afterwards. This change makes it easier to actually engage with the narrative.

Vehicles Summary

Vehicles are now vastly simpler. We have already gone over the distinctions between Vehicles and Fighters as they arose in the core rules, but here are some general truths and an overview:

  • The distinction between Vehicles and Crews is gone. A Vehicle is now its own, discrete model, and it includes any crew members modelled with it. Its statline, equipment etc represent the whole parcel. Note that this means the end of passenger-served weapons and such things. Transports (see the Vehicle Subtypes) are better defined and simpler.
  • Vehicles, except Walkers, are still treated as Terrain for Fighters to move over, and those Fighters will still risk falling off (now on an Agility, rather than Initiative check) if the Vehicle moves.
  • One interesting and easy to overlook disadvantage against Fighters - if a Vehicle is deleted from the Gang Roster, its weapons and equipment are lost as well, they are not added to the Stash. That makes sense in an immersive sense, but clearly it presents a risk to your investment in campaign play.
  • All Vehicle movement is far simpler than before. They Move, Dash and even Charge or Engage like other Fighters, simply with a restriction to one 90-degree turn in a Charge or Dash. All of the N18 stuff about jack-knifing, rolling and other involuntary movement is gone.
  • Resolving Damage against Vehicles is done as outlined above, just the same as with Fighters. The equivalent of Coup de Grace against Vehicles, Sabotage, is slightly different, and Actions available to Seriously Damaged Vehicles are named separately from the very similar Actions available to Seriously Injured Fighters, which seems mostly for clarity. There is a different (although similar) Lasting Damage chart, and a different Post-Cycle Action for repairing Lasting Damage.

Do we like these changes? Yes, absolutely. The N18/Ash Wastes system of special damage dice, randomised locations, Hull Points and separate Crews was interesting, but grew to be painful if playing games with multiple Vehicles. Even more than the book-keeping in creating gangs, games took too long to resolve all the consequences when firing at vehicles, and/or restarting stalled engines. We think these changes will see more groups willing to give vehicles a shot.

We haven’t seen any stats or costs for Vehicles yet, so eagerly await any changes to their relative balance (i.e. the Ridgehauler problem), which we’re fairly hopeful will be sorted out. We do hope there is some extra Vehicle equipment in the forthcoming books, as it adds a layer of interest. The rulebook’s Trading Post is mostly armour and ‘personal equipment’ and beyond common sense, it’s not yet clear what can’t be given to Vehicles.

Vehicle Subtypes

Wheeled, Tracked, and Skimmer Subtypes are essentially the same as in the previous Ash Wastes rules. Skimmers resolve ending their move on Difficult/Dangerous terrain slightly differently, now they take an Agility test. If it’s failed they take a L1 hit, Wounding automatically with no saves - quite a convoluted way to say they lose a Wound, and roll 1 Injury dice if appropriate. Hybrid Vehicles, as before, have two Subtypes and can choose to use either when they Activate.  Manoeuvrable Vehicles, the hardest type to spell, can make an extra 90-degree turn during a Charge or Dash. Walkers have no restrictions on turning while moving, and can be Engaged in close combat exactly as Fighters are.  Transport (X) is a major change; the old rules for transport beds or cages are now gone. Up to X models can now deploy in a Vehicle with this trait (no Brutes or Mounted Fighters). They get Ready markers, but essentially aren’t on the battlefield, similar to how 40k does things. Fighters, when they Activate, can disembark by taking the Interact Action, which places them within 1” of an Access Point - presumably these will be specified on vehicle profiles when we see them. Fighters can embark on a Vehicle mid-game, if there’s space, by taking the Interact Action within 1” of an Access Point. Should the Vehicle be taken OOA, transported Fighters must be placed within 1” of the Vehicle before resolving any explosive consequences, and if there’s nowhere to place them, they too go OOA. This is all simple enough, and removes the terrible jank of resolving templates against vehicles and the Fighters riding on them.

Changes to Weapons/Equipment & Trading

We have already mentioned the introduction of Lethality instead of Damage, and the removal of to-hit modifiers. Other than that, weapon Traits follow the same system as before, but almost all have been changed. Some mimic the effects of N18 rules with a different structure, but there are fairly sweeping balance changes. Without yet having the full Gang rules for comparison, it’s impossible to make qualitative assessments, but there has clearly been some thought put into the relative capabilities and price of weapons. The old categories of Pistols, Basic, Special and Heavy weapons appear to be gone. 

Equipment has similarly seen some changes, with certain things tweaked, and certain things reworded to play better with the newly structured rules. Again, there have been cost changes, but our initial impression is that other equipment, especially situational/environmental kit which doesn’t directly protect the wearer from damage, is still overpriced compared to weaponry. 

Visiting the Trading Post is tweaked from before. Instead of rolling for Rarity, and modifying that using the number of models making the Trade action (plus relevant Skills), the models taking the action generate Trade Points (TP). Items have a TP cost, e.g. common things like Stub Guns are all TP0, and a Needle Rifle is TP2. You spend your TP during the Post-Cycle phase (any excess is discarded) and that allows you to spend the credits and buy items. So, if you have 3TP, you can buy 1 item that costs 3TP or less, or 3 items that each cost 1TP, etc. You can’t just buy any number of <3TP items. Together with more restrained campaign income, this is a good step towards fixing the common complaint that Necromunda gangs found it too easy to accumulate gear that would make a Space Marine tactical squad proud. 

For all weapons and equipment, please look through our Trading Post article, where we break down all the new Traits, stats, and offer initial opinions on the goods. Here are some headline, stand out changes:

  • Blaze is massively cut down as a trait. No longer an asymmetric terror that can neutralise any model without a Hazard Suit, it’s now just a chance to proc an extra hit. But flamer weapons in general are relatively far cheaper and look fun.
  • Web, similarly, is a very useful Condition to apply to enemies, but because web guns no longer cause Serious Injury, they aren’t nearly as filthy as before. 
  • Ammo changes were mentioned above, and widen the gap in reliability between ordinary weapons and things like bolt and plasma weapons, which while still very good, present risks.
  • Heavy weapons in general are cheaper, but Suspensors have apparently been removed from the game. Currently, the only way to move at all in the same Activation a model fires such a weapon is the Bulging Biceps Skill. 
  • Armour now modifies the model’s basic Save characteristic, and may provide other effects. So it’s not necessary (or even particularly sensible) to buy everyone armour anymore. 

What We Still Don’t Know

There are huge gaps in our knowledge of the new rules, since we are awaiting the already-announced gang compendiums, Gangs of the Underhive & Gangs of the Outlands. Those gaps make it impossible to give accurate predictions about balance or the overall state of the game just yet. We could extrapolate some things about the design ethos from the starter box’s Goliath and Escher models, but those are very clearly custom groups of what are essentially Specialists. They don’t represent the shape of even a ‘normal’ gang from their own Houses.

  • Most obviously, we have no details on the relative stats and costs of fighters, weapons or equipment for any Gangs, or their special rules. This limits our perspective on the contents of the Trading Post because we don’t know what more exclusive options compare to them. Will gangs still have their own unique Skill trees? It seems likely but we don’t know.
  • We lack full information on Gang composition. We know there is still 1 leader, and similarly to N18, your total number of Champions, Brutes and Hangers-On must not exceed the number of other sub-typed fighters. This implies that Brute and Hanger-On recruitment is consolidated with other fighters, and our working assumption is that those types can gain Advances (maybe not equipment) as other fighters. But any other restrictions are unclear. It might be that Juves are now lumped in as Prospects, although it seems unlikely that ‘rookie’ level fighters will all need to be the specially-equipped Prospects of N18. Similarly, Specialists have an enhanced role with their starting Skill added, but we can’t assume they will be ‘designate one Ganger at creation’ as in N18. We know that Prospects become Specialists at 13XP and Gangers (probably including Specialists) become Champions at 37XP. Presumably Prospects start at 1XP and Gangers at 13XP. But the Escher & Goliath in the starter box, which seem like customised Specialists, all begin with 19XP - we have no idea if that is extraordinary or average. 
  • We know how the Trading Post works, and this includes some of the restrictions on adding Exclusive equipment to fighters, and that fighters can still pick equipment from their own Fighter lists. It seems possible there will be one ‘House’ list for each gang type, with restrictions on what status of fighter can access each item, rather than N18’s page of options for each fighter type. But not only do we lack all those details, we lack the format of restrictions. N18’s categorisation of Pistols, Basic, Special and Heavy weapons seems to be gone from the new Trading Post. So presumably the weapon restrictions will be more tailored going forward. It seems possible that lower-ranking fighters will be able to pick weapons from the Trading Post now, but we just don’t know – certainly uncontrolled access, with no limit on higher-TP items, sounds unlikely.
