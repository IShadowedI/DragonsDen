RagnarokAngel's Road to LVO 2026 - Part 1

May 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This is part of our Road to LVO series, sponsored by Frontline Gaming, where Tabletop Battles contributors and shareholders discuss their journey to the Las Vegas Open later this year, from October 1st to 4th. Get your tickets today!

Hello! I'm Alice "RagnarokAngel" Lirette, head of the Age of Sigmar writing team. I've gone to the Las Vegas Open every year since 2023 (including both times in 2025) to attend the Age of Sigmar GT, and this year will be no different.

Toward the end of each month I will be posting my progress as I prepare for the Las Vegas Open including painting progress and battle reports.

Why Go to LVO?

LVO is my favorite event of the year to attend. It's where everyone comes together, meaning the event has a size and spectacle that is unmatched by any other event. If you want to meet the most players to get a wide diversity of games from people coming in from all over the world, it's the best option out there.

Plus, I totally own that I am a mark. I love the glitz and glam of Vegas and there's no better place to go out and grab some drinks with friends after. I've gone pretty much every year with my partner in crime and fellow Tabletop Battles author "Contemptor" Kevin Stillman, who will be coming along this year as well.

Grabbing a Chocolate Martini at Hershey World on the Vegas Strip with Kevin, October 2025

What Am I Running?

The plan for now is to run my beloved Ossiarch Bonereapers. I've not really loved the army when the faction pack hit. It was just terribly dull and didn't leave much opening for flexibility. They eschewed the Relentless Discipline points mechanic, a system which gave tokens to be spent on various buffs, for an aura system. This required either bringing a very expensive Hero that ate up a quarter of your list to get an aura big enough to cover everything, or lots of heroes to remain close to your troops. It was finnicky and not fun to play, and for the most part only one of the two auras was any good for your turn or your opponents.

With the new Battletome it does bring some challenges. Relentless Discipline points are back, but it's in shorter supply, and scales to cost more for bigger units. The armies is a lot less of an anvil than it used to be and has more movement tricks that give it more of a control vibe. This is quite different from what I'm used to playing, especially when Ossiarchs tended to be a pretty straightforward army. This means I'm going to need to practice much more often to get some of the fine details down.

I do have Daughters of Khaine in my periphery as well. I haven't played them much since the Third Edition OBR tome dropped, since their tome dropped in 4th they are busted and so they open a potential avenue. It is another book to learn, and my Ossiarchs remain my first love of Sigmar so for now I'd like to stick to that.

RTT - Age of Springmar

My showing at Adepticon was pretty bad, I went 1-4, but the list was straight up bad. I had to go back to the drawing board and try something else. It meant cutting Katakros, my favorite son who I've always run, and tried something else. Because I like running big models, I still went with Arkhan, and then tried to pack the list with a bunch of hammers.

I went to an RTT in April at a local store Alpha Omega Hobbies in Quincy, MA. Since I was still trying to get a feel for the book, I experimented with this:

I figured with Zandtos+Reinforced Deathriders, Morghast Archai and Triaxes, I had 3 really solid damage dealers. Arkhan is an excellent caster, but in his new incarnation is actually a pretty strong fighter too, since his mount swings with Rend 3 Damage 3 attacks and wounds on 2s (Shame about the 4+ To Hit but we can work with that).

I'm not as comfortable with playing "Shenanigans" lists, so playing something with a clear gameplan might be exactly what I needed to shake off the rust.

Game 1 - Michael Welch (Soulblight Gravelords) - The Liferoots

I hate the Liferoots. It's a bad mission for me, full stop. There are 2 objectives, and the scoring really comes from trying to hold Terrain, which is difficult without lots of chaff units. since Terrain isn't "sticky" like objectives.

Michael was a nice guy, but pretty new to the game. So it worked out pretty well for me that he just pushed his stuff up across the board and I charged right into it. Zandtos inflicting Strikes Last followed up by some hammer swings from my Deathriders and Archai removed the Vengorian Lord and Blood Knights, which were the biggest threats. He didn't even bother bringing the Draconith out of Deep Strike and surrendered at the bottom of round 2.

Final Score: 80 - 17 (Victory)

Game 2 - Christian Lefebvre (Helsmiths of Hashut) - Grasp of Thorns

Hoo boy I knew this was gonna be a rough one. My army really struggles against Ranged armies and the Archai, my best hammer, can't hide in the obscuring Terrain. So I'd have to take some careful approaches.

I put my Deathriders and Zandtos on the left side with the intent to activate the Place of Power and obscure them, buying me some time while they got to remain hidden. The Liege-Mortek and Triaxes on the right side of the board followed suit on the second turn, so all I had to do was play the waiting game.

I eventually did move my Kavalos up, and Christian moved his Spearmen to guard that side of the board. He cast basically every buff on them and so I decided to juke it to the right and try and charge into his artillery. It was a long bomb charge, but I made it and was able to remove one and beat up the other, but I then had to watch as they were cannibalized by the rest of the army.

This, however, was enough for him to panic and move his spearmen on the other side of the board out of formation, putting the Blunderbusses out in the open. I shot my Archai up the right side of the board and charged in, absolutely clearing them out and removing probably the biggest ranged threat in the list. After that it was just a matter of piece meal dismantling each threat with Triaxe fire and Morghast, and I narrowly pulled out a win.

Final Score: 54 - 52 (Victory)

This was a tense game. Connor knew that he was doing, and we spent so much of the early half of the game trying to fake each other out with our own cavalry, with me using my Teratic Cohort as a barrier. At the bottom of Round 2, he did finally try to charge my Deathriders with his Slickblade Seekers, but absolutely whiffed and picked up one guy as my Deathriders picked up 2. From being charged. Embarrassing display as he powered through to fall back. On my next turn I responded in kind by having Zandtos and the Deathriders charge his Seekers and made quite a bit of progress with them.

The problem was the rest of the board really struggled, I just couldn't quite seem to kill enough guys in the Blissbarb, Twinsouls and Slaangor units, and they have recursion where as I do not which ultimately lead to me losing by attrition. We went at it for the full 5 rounds, and I got Glutos though, that was a big win.

Final Score: 54 - 61 (Loss)

Evaluating the List and What's Next

Generally speaking I do like the list. It felt good that I was winning pretty consistently and still walked away with a 2-1 at the end of the day.

A few pain points found in the list:
  • Neutered Damage Output - Ossiarchs lost a lot of their high damage units, due to Crit (2 Hits) being tied to a spell with a limited range, and Kavalos Deathriders being possibly the only infantry unit in the game that doesn't have Charge. They have options. Morghast are still as strong as they were (as one of the few models that didn't lose the built in Crit), and Deathriders can get Charge through Zandtos, but all that gets expensive fast. It means that relying on the old hammer method just isn't as reliable, since it leads to the next problem...
  • High Value Targets - Investing a lot of points into a single unit makes it a high value target, especially for ranged fire. Morghast are great, I love them as a unit that you can just set and forget, but since they're Flyers they can't really hide from ranged fire. With only 4 models total, each lost model is a brutal hit to their overall damage output since reviving them is virtually impossible, and it's going to be the most obvious target to an opponent with half decent ranged attacks. Similarly, while Zandtos and the reinforced block of Deathriders can hide, they're still an obvious target and such a big footprint is hard to hide forever. If they don't get to charge, pack it up, it's all over.
  • Lack of Tricks - Probably the biggest issue is not seizing on the Ossiarchs breadth of new movement tricks and control powers. The list is simply a blunt object, and it struggles against armies with lots of screens or other ways to keep away from my biggest units charging straight at their face. I'd like to be able to have more ways to play around big hordes and not feel stuck.
Looking at what other OBR players are doing, I'm going to look more toward running more Deathriders, like 3-5 5 mans with a Liege-Kavalos, so I can do the fun Deathrider Wedge trick to get a bunch of Mortal wounds. I have an RTT at the start of next month, so I will be trying that out there!
