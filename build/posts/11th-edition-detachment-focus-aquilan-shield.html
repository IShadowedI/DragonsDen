11th Edition Detachment Focus: Aquilan Shield

October 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Following hot on the heels of the Emperor’s Angels come his Praetorian Guard. The Adeptus Custodes get an early 11th edition Codex, and with it a slew of new rules and Detachments. In this article, we’re taking a look at the Aquilan Shield Detachment.

The Aquilan Shield are staunch defenders of the Imperium's most important servants, holding the line until they have fulfilled whatever key duty is their destiny. Their Shield Host Detachment provides durability and withering firepower to achieve this end.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of this codex and a provisional version of the Munitorum Field Manual. Any rules or points costs we reference are subject to change until they are published on the Warhammer 40K App.

Aquilan Shield

Detachment Cost: 1 DP

Force Disposition: Take and Hold

Unique: SHIELD HOST

If you dream of golden hosts mowing down ranks of the unworthy with ranged superiority, Aquilan Host indulges that fantasy more than any other Shield Host. This is the ‘shooty’ Detachment for your core Custodes, rewarding you for sitting on objectives and pouring bullets from the windows, as to deny any intruders entry. Moreover, this is also the Detachment that best-utilizes Aquilon Terminators, so if you’re excited to deploy a number of those gorgeous new plastic sculpts, this is the premier option to get them on the table.

Shield Captain - Credit: keewa

Detachment Rule: Gilded Guardians

While on an objective, enemy ranged attacks have -1 to wound your non-MONSTER/VEHICLE ADEPTUS CUSTODES whose toughness is less than the enemy’s strength. I feel like this could have been keyworded by now, given the frequency with which it appears. Regardless of how common this is, it’s still a welcome ability to have, given how resilient Custodes are in the new book.

Your standard Custodian statline is T7, with Terminators being T8, and given S14 isn’t exactly common, oftentimes Gilded Guardians reads as ‘your opponent wounds Custodes on objectives on 4+ at best’. For those around for the old Transhuman Physiology, that should strike fear in your heart. This Detachment ability really makes the +1T that your infantry received in this book soar, as while Lascannons and other S12 is common, were they T6, very little wounds modern Custodes on 2s.

Favored Ka’tah: Salvus

When selecting Salvus as your Ka’tah, shooting attacks gain 6” of range. This isn’t a massive deal for most Custodes units that have access to Ka’tahs, as your average guardian spears and axes have ranges of ~18”-24”. Where this comes up is with the units that benefit most from Aquilan Shield as a Detachment, which we’ll soon talk more about: Aquilon Terminators. Both the Adrathic Combi-destructor & Infernus Firepike have a range of merely 12”, so an extra 6” matters immensely in terms of getting that damage where it needs to be. This can also make attaining the extra shots from [RAPID FIRE] weapons a bit easier, and go figure, Aquilons also have that option via Lastrum Storm Bolters.

Enhancements

  • Pareldor’s Cruciatrix (30pts): ADEPTUS CUSTODES model only. Once per battle, at the start of any phase, the bearer heals D3+3 wounds. The premium paid for wounds of Custodes means that even if this is 4 more, it’s still likely worth it...so long as you guarantee the model survives to heal. That’s not difficult, but if the bearer gets cleared in a single phase, you’re down both the model and the speculative 4-6 wounds from the Cruciatrix. Whether the model is leading a unit or not is another consideration, as this heal doesn’t spill over (being just a heal on the enhanced model), so if your opponent undershoots and only deals ~2 damage to the bearer while clearing their squad, is it worth popping? This is a powerful tool, but requires some pregame math into key matchups to not waste. As a final note, annoyingly this doesn’t work with the Gilded Champion stratagem from Auric Champions, as it’s (Once per battle, per army) rather than (Once per battle, per unit), which would otherwise be a great plug-and-play synergy.
  • Not a Shell Wasted (10pts): ADEPTUS CUSTODES INFANTRY model only. The bearer’s unit’s ranged attacks have +1A. In any of your 3-model squads with a leader, this is usually +4A, so you’d prefer to use this on a squad where the relative volume increase has more impact than just extra shots on a profile that’s already [RAPID FIRE]-ing. Surprise surprise, it’s Aquilon Terminators once more, with a 50% increase in shots for their Combi-destructors.

Stratagems

  • Manoeuvre and Fire (1CP): During your Movement phase, when your ADEPTUS CUSTODES unit makes a fall back move, target it to also be able to shoot and charge that turn. This is a great tool to have, given your units are more likely to survive a combat than basically anything else in the game. On units which actively want to both shoot and fight in melee, this does double duty, but frankly most Custodes profiles do both admirably as a baseline, so it’s rare not to get max value from this stratagem.
  • Tip of the Talon (1CP): During your Shooting phase, when your ADEPTUS CUSTODES unit is selected to shoot, target it to gain +1S for that attack against enemy units within 9”. The only time you’ll use this stratagem is when it results in hitting a relevant breakpoint, and therefore this is effectively just +1 to wound in shooting...with a sizable asterisk. On weird profiles like Combi-destructors, which are -2/3D in spite of a measly S5, suddenly getting to wound the new Terminator statline on 4s helps put the hurt on TEQs. Similarly, this pushes many of your Vehicles’ best shooting to wound hulls on 3s, so cracking Land Raiders/Land Fortresses is far easier. This won’t come up in every matchup, but given that Aquilan Shield is the dedicated shooting Detachment for Custodes, it’s great to turn 5s to 4s, and 4s to 3s on relevant targets.
  • Rapid Reactions (1CP): During your opponent’s Movement phase, when an enemy unit falls back from your ADEPTUS CUSTODES INFANTRY unit, you can target it to make normal shooting attacks against only that enemy unit that fell back. This is one of the most punishing stratagems we’ve seen in recent memory, and is likely one you’ll retain a spare CP for, even just as a deterrent. While there’s a volume problem with a lot of Custodes shooting, especially with the overall reduction in unit sizes throughout the book, the fact you still get to hit on 2s for the vast majority of profiles means those paltry shots *will* convert, and *will* clear MEQs. The units you’re taking in this Detachment ought to excel at both shooting & melee, and the guns on most of your infantry are nothing to scoff at.

Detachment Combos

In spite of explicitly calling out that its Detachment rule doesn’t work with monsters or vehicles, this is likely the best Shield Host to pair with Grav-assault Force, for the fact that your tank profiles dearly appreciate fall back & shoot and the extra +1S from Aquilan’s stratagem toolbelt. With your tanks losing access to Ka’tahs (no more tank karate, sorry folks), it’s a bit of an uphill battle to make them worth building around. If you’re looking for trade pieces though, the Pallas being able to Deep Strike and push its guns to S11 isn’t anything to shrug at, especially given you can then scoot it backwards from the required <9”, and push in with Custodes (who in turn threaten Rapid Reactions). Furthermore, given the cost of everything, you tend to be more keen to burn a CP rather than points to access the ability to pull something like a Caladius from melee and fire into a key threat.

Given the benefits to Aquilon Terminators as well, it makes sense to consider Lions of the Emperor in order to better reposition your squads and have access to Fury of the Emperor. After all, Rapid Reactions isn’t worth much if you can’t actually get in, and tempt your opponent to fall back.

Is It Good?

Credit: Corrode

Unlike the other books of 11th edition so far, the design of Codex: Adeptus Custodes has severe locks placed on Shield Hosts, so where you might say Aquilan Shield is good in another book, it has to be *better* than other options here to see significant play. That’s not as clear, unfortunately. A lot of the tools here are designed explicitly for use with a unit that’s solid, but not extraordinary (in reference to Aquilon Terminators), and elsewhere the benefits of super-Salvus can fall flat. Custodes can Deep Strike or move quick enough that an extra 6” of range isn’t amazing, and playing the faction as a ‘stand and shoot’ army without access to Hidden retention leaves you vulnerable to get traded-up against by a shootier foe. If you find yourself playing against melee armies or those with a ton of 3W 2+/4++ profiles, Aquilan Shield seems amazing, but its cracks show compared to other Shield Hosts when facing superior ranged firepower.

Final Thoughts

For as much as this Detachment seeks to buff Custodes shooting, the steps taken to allow most available profiles to wound things tougher than a Terminator on better than a 5+ are slim. Certainly this can be offset by, as stated, taking a few Vehicles and using your on-foot Custodes to deal with things of Toughness 6 or less, but against skew lists like Knights I expect frustration from pilots of Aquilan Shield.

If you get out-shot, or are paired into an army that’s better at standing on objectives than you (again, Knights) a lot of the luster of this Detachment fades. You absolutely need to supplement your core of Terminators with proper S10/12 firepower, and recognize matchups where protecting something like a Pallas or two is the difference between victory and defeat. This Detachment can excel, but it requires a meta with minimal vehicle-heavy skew, and a player willing to understand difficult matchups; it’s not as easy to pilot as it first appears, especially in the face of other Custodes options. If tanks still knew karate, we’d be singing a different tune here at Tabletop Battles.
