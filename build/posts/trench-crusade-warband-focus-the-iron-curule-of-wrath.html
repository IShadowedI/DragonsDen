Trench Crusade Warband Focus: The Iron Curule of Wrath

November 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome, everyone, to another Trench Crusade Warband focus! This week we enter the domain of Wrath. We know Hell as all fire and brimstone, but Wrath asks: What if the fire and sulfur below were unleashed upon the earth? Well, in this article, we'll find out how you can burn it all down as a Captain of the Lady of Locusts. 

The Lore

Credit: darius_minis

Wrath is hatred made manifest. It is the burning vengeance of the fallen, who desire to overthrow the heavenly Tyrant, but not before first rolling over that which He created. How ironic it is for the whelps of the Earth to condemn the hatred and resentment within each mortal soul, but welcome the Tyrant’s Wrath with open arms, under the thin disguise of “Divine Righteousness” and “Heavenly Justice”. This sickening hypocrisy fuels the fire burning within each Demon in Apollyon’s domain. Unlike her underlings, whose frenzy is uncontrolled wildfire, the Lady of Locusts is the icy wind, whose cold pragmatism fuels and guides the flames of war to where their destructive power can cause the most devastation.

The Variant 

Beginning with the Variant’s main special rule, we have Exterminas Omnium. This replaces the Mortal Suffering rule, and instead, when a friendly Demon unit makes an Injury Roll with an Attack with the Fire Keyword, place 1 Sin Marker next to the attacking model. So right off the bat, you know what this Variant is all about (if you didn’t get that from the lore already), and like me, you automatically started thinking of all the weapons and abilities with the Fire Keyword and putting them into the “auto take” category in your list building.

For Recruitment changes, almost everything is the same as the base faction, with a couple of exceptions. First, Pit Locusts have an increased recruitment cap of 5. Additionally, Pit Locusts replace the Shrapnel Keyword on their Insectile Stingers ability with Fire. Pit Locusts can also be given the Hellfire Spit Action for 10 ducats. This ability means that should a Pit Locust have 2 or more Sin Markers, they may make a Ranged Attack at 4”, with the Fire and Assault Keywords. More ways to make Fire = More Sin Markers. Whilst the Pit Locust may not be an auto-take in regular warbands, taking a couple of generated Sin Markers can be a great way to boost the effectiveness of other units. Hellfire Spit is a nice little touch, but it’s a little too short on range. Yoke fiends also see a change, where those without a Restraining Muzzle ignore Difficult Terrain and gain Wrathful. This ability means that if the Fiend is outside of 1” of an enemy, within 12” of an enemy and in Line of Sight, its first action during its Activation must be a Charge action. This is a pretty punishing downgrade if you were looking to keep your Fiends permanently paired with Wretched and using them to take potshots at Ranged. You can mitigate this with some movement and hiding, or alternatively, play into it with a rush build and yeet those Yoke Fiends up the board!

For your Desecrated Saint, they replace their Aura of Sin ability with Seething Heat, which adds +1 Dice for Melee Attacks and Dash Action rolls for friendly models that are within 8” of the Saint. This is absolutely fantastic and makes the Desecrated Saint incredibly useful in the early game, ensuring your slower units do what they do best: run up and bonk things in melee with fire and fury.

For Goetic Powers, we start off with Blind Rage, which gives up to 3 Elites a +1 Dice to Dash Action rolls. It’s simple, but it’s a must-take for your Elites. Combined with Seething Heat, you have a surefire way of getting up the board without hindrance. Next is Charge of Hatred, where you do not gain the Charge Bonus dice when charging, but instead your movement for the charge is 12”. However, if you target an enemy model that is not the closest, you must first make a Risky Success Roll. If you succeed, the Charge continues as normal. Coincidentally—or should I say, intentionally—this can also be given to up to 3 Elite Units, further cementing the approach that your Elites need to beeline it straight into your enemy and engage in glorious melee. Lastly, we have Undo Creation, which is a Spell a single Elite can take. It’s got a whopping 30 Ducat cost, but the effect is truly devastating. After a successful Spell action, you pick a terrain piece within 12”, within Line of Sight, and up to 8”x8” in size. For the rest of the game, that Terrain Piece has the Dangerous Terrain (Fire) Keyword.

Unique Battlekit

We’ve got some spicy (and by spicy, I mean hot) Battlekit pieces to explore here. Starting off with Apollyon’s Ember, an Equipment piece that grants the Fire Keyword to all Melee Attacks. Your favourite weapon doesn’t have the Fire Keyword? Well, now it does. Great for multi-Melee attack weapons, as it applies to all Melee Attacks, not just to a particular weapon. It does have a Limit of 2 and can only be taken by your Elites, however. As we have seen, though, your Elites are going to be the beatsticks, so it’s not much of a restriction. Lesser Mark of Cain grants a permanent -1 Injury Dice to all incoming attacks. It’s only for 1 Elite model, and it costs 25 Ducats, but it is 100% worth it. Always take it. If you want your Hell Knights more nimble, consider taking Locust Gait. This gives your Hell Knights the Fly Keyword. It’s simple, but it can help your Hell Knights overcome more vertical terrain set-ups. The first of the two Weapons on offer is Ruinous Pernach, a 2-handed Melee Weapon with the Heavy keyword and a +1 Injury Modifier. The Crushed Plate ability adds the Armour Piercing keyword to all subsequent Melee Attacks against a target hit with this weapon. It’s a nice little upgrade to a standard Hammer. It’s a nice starter weapon if you’re building a more budget-friendly Desecrated Saint, for example, who can follow up with a couple more attacks. Our second weapon is the Spear of Apollyon, the Lady of Locusts' weapon of choice. I preface this part by noting that it comes in at 6 Glory, befitting a Serpent’s weapon. It has the +2 Injury Dice, Block, Cumbersome and Fire keywords, as well as the Shield Combo rule. Its ability is Rend the World. Instead of a Fight Action, you can make a Rend the World Action. Measure a straight line 1mm wide up to 12”. Then remove as many Sin Markers as you wish. Roll a number of D6s equal to the number of Sin Markers. Then take a single D6 and place a number of Blood Markers next to a model the line touches, equal to the number on the D6. Do this for each D6 rolled. You must place at least one D6 on the attacking model. A great potential army clearer, if the stars align. At 12”, it’s hard to get many models in a straight line, but if you can get 3+ models on the line and you can roll hot, this could do a lot.

Key Units

Desecrated Saint

Credit: Darius_minis

Of all the units in the Court roster, the Desecrated Saint is the most hotly contested in terms of usefulness, often seen as a giant model that walks around with a big crosshair(s) on its forehead(s). Within Wrath, that is still the case, but arguments can be made for scaling it back in offensive output and focusing more on its supportive potential. An 8” aura for +1 to Dash Action rolls is just too good to not even consider taking a saint. Coupled with potential 1st-turn Sin Marker generation thanks to Totemic, which Wrath sorely needs to get the ball rolling, since their Sin Marker generation relies on attacking, something you can seldom do on turn 1. The problem arises with how you equip your Saint. You see a 140 Ducat model decked out with those stats and feel compelled to give it the best equipment you can, but that just increases the likelihood it’ll be priority numero uno for your opponent. -3 Armour and tough, whilst great in its own right, is not a defensive profile that is hard for a good opponent to crack. The problem lies in its size. Among official models, it’s one of the biggest, if not the biggest. Some good Ignore Armour at good range, and the -3 armour counts for nought. Spread out your Ducats and disincentivise the focus fire on the Saint, and you can still use it as a potentially great support piece. With just 3 Trench Knives, you’re still hitting three times with +2 to your Melee Characteristics. There is still great value in that.

Possible Loadouts

Big Budget Saint: 

  • Trench Knife x 3

Semi Budget Saint: 

  • Ruinous Pernach
  • Trench Knife x 2 / Trench Club x 2 / Sword x2

Bullet Magnet Saint: 

  • Malebranche / Hellblade
  • Headtaker x 2 / Trench Club x 2 / Sword x2 

Hell Knight

Credit: Josh Tedd (@thepaintingtedd)

If survivability is a must-have, Hell Knights may be your preferred anvil. The Impervious armour they have allows them to soak up both blade and bullet easily. How you kit out your Knight offensively depends entirely on what you intend to do with him. Do you prefer to get into melee and duke it out with your opponent? Then Hellblade and Trench Shield can create an annoying threat for your opponent to deal with. However, because Wrath is all about fire, the Heavy Flamethrower is an amazing fit for dealing consistent Blood Markers, Sin Markers, and dealing with Armoured enemies. Two attacks net you 4 Blood Markers and, crucially, 2 Sin Markers. All of that from 1 Ranged Attack action. If you’re using it to burn through armour, then the Hell Knight’s Blood Magic Power pairs very nicely, offsetting the -1 Injury Dice from the Heavy Flamethrower. Whilst a 10” range isn’t the best, and there’s no Long-Range penalty, you may still find it frustrating to get your Hell Knight into position, but that is where Locust Gait comes in handy, allowing you to fly up to favourable positions to make the most of the Flamer’s range.

Possible Loadouts

Melee:

  • Hellblade
  • Trench Shield / Trench Club
  • Corpse Candles
  • Blind Rage

Ranged:

  • Heavy Flamethrower
  • Locust Gait
  • Corpse Candles 
  • Blind Rage 

Sorcerer

Credit: Josh Tedd (@thepaintingtedd)

Everyone’s goat-faced wizard has some great synergy within Wrath. The most obvious is Burning Inferno, where you can easily make back all the Sin Markers you spend to cast it. Spend a single Sin marker? If you hit 1 target, you get your Sin back. Spend 2 and hit 2? You get them back, etc. If you’re particularly lucky, you can even make back more.  It’s a really neat way to ensure consistent ranged output from a variant that guides you into moving forward into Melee. A backboard threat that keeps your opponent honest can’t be underestimated. For other potential Goetic Powers, there is, of course, Slavemaster, which, if you equip your Yoke Fiends with Flamethrowers, can potentially rack up more Sin Markers quickly. If you tend to play on terrain piece-heavy boards, Undo Creation will go a long way, since you can cast it multiple times a game. It is expensive and a little board dependent, but if you can get this off a couple of times on key terrain pieces, your opponent will be forced out into open terrain, ready to be picked off. Blessing of the Serpent Moon and Raining Blood round out other potential Power picks, ensuring survivability or mass AoE damage to opponents, respectively.

Possible Loadouts

  • Slavemaster
  • Blessing of the Serpent Moon
  • Raining Blood 

Pit Locust

Credit: Darius_minis

This pesky pest, even bare-bones and without the Hellfire Spit, can be a pain for your opponents to deal with, while also being a quick means of Sin Marker generation thanks to the innate Cleave 2 and Fire of your Insectile Stingers. Successfully hitting with both attacks gives you 2 Sin Markers, which you can then quickly spend on other more important units.

  • They’re perfect just the way they are! Just look at that little face!

Final Thoughts

At first glance, one may think that Wrath seems like a very simple army, that lacks the depth of some other Variants. However, on closer inspection, there’s not only depth, but breadth. The list I showed above could easily be changed around to include a Desecrated Saint, or a Praetor. Heck, shift things around enough, and you can get both. Whilst it offers a straightforward style of play, if you want that, there are many ways to approach it. There is method to the madness. What’s particularly nice with the Variant, however, is that every unit is playable. The Saint offers such tantalising value it would make Greed blush, and depending on the rest of the list, you can take it without the enemy concentrating their attacks on it. Likewise, the goodest of flying, horse-sized locusts can quickly generate Sin Markers without being overly frustrating if they perish in a game. With Fire being such a fundamentally important keyword to the game, having a Sin revolve around it opens up thematic, strong ways to play with it. Give Wrath a try, and bathe all of Creation in flame.
