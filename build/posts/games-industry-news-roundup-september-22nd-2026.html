Games Industry News Roundup- September 22nd, 2026

September 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Mammoth-Sized Updates for Kings of War

Credit: Mantic Games

We’re joined this week by Cytoplasm to start us off with some big updates to Kings of War, and yes, the pun was intended.

Cytoplasm: The biggest Kings of War event in the world, Clash of Kings UK, has just wrapped up and there have been some big reveals about what’s coming up over the next year. 

The biggest news is that the biannual releases of new books (of which we have already had two, Invasion and Fracture) will be turning to a yearly release schedule. Mantic says this will give the playtesters more time to provide feedback, but also allows them to expand the scope of each book to give rules updates to all factions rather than a select few. This will be in the form of new Command Orders, updated Unit Profiles and even a change to army construction to enable greater flexibility.

This is definitely a good move. The overall sentiment among Kings of War players that two books a year was a bit too quick. Jokes about mental aptitude aside, the real issue is that Kings of War is a long-term hobby. Every army is a long journey from start to finish, and to find out by the time that it’s finally complete that half of it needs to change does not resonate well. While the general contents of the books has been well-received, slowing it down can only help both the players enjoy what they have, and Mantic make the best update they can.

The Sheer Arrogance book, like the books before, will have a few armies that will receive particularly close attention in the form of revised army lists and new models! Twilight Kin, Northern Alliance, Abyssal Dwarfs and Xirkaali will be the focus. 

The Twilight Kin will be getting new units including some War Machines and Impaler variants. The Abyssal Dwarfs are getting new sculpts of older models, including the Overmaster on Ancient Winged Halfbreed. Even the Xirkaali, which are the newest faction to join Kings of War, are being expanded with new units like the gorilla warriors known as the Shaoquan and Yan Warrior Monks on horseback.

The most exciting update however is the new Mammoth for the Northern Alliance. Whether you think the Mammoth is a good unit or not, all can agree that this new model sets the standard for Mammoths. The best thing is, multiple factions in Kings of War have access to the Mammoth, including Ogres and Kingdoms of Men, so it’s almost like a an update for three armies rather than just the one!

Wizards Cancels Product for Upcoming Star Trek Set

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Universes Beyond sets released over the past few years have become the cornerstone to Magic: The Gathering’s financial success, breaking sales records and driving a wider engagement with the venerable card game. Wizards has pushed UB hard to maximize profits, with each release bringing a plethora of new releases, limited edition collectible sets, as well as big pushes for community events. In the past, Wizards has been more than happy to oversaturate the market and the brand with each new UB release, but in a rare move, it looks like with the Star trek release, we’re dialing it back rather than speeding the train up, as Wizards of the Coast announced on Monday that it would be cancelling the Star Trek Beginner Box set.

“Due to delays in production, logistics, and shipping, we will no longer be producing the Magic: The Gathering® | Star Trek™ Beginner Box,” Wizards of the Coast announced on the Magic blog page. “This product has been canceled in all regions. If you preordered a Beginner Box, contact your local game store or retailer for more information. For Magic events that planned to include the Beginner Box (like MagicCon: Atlanta), we are currently updating our plans and will share details in the future.

“No other Magic: The Gathering | Star Trek products are affected by this. We appreciate your patience and understanding.”

The set, which was set to retail at $25, contained two twenty card starter decks, with one themed for Starfleet and the other for Klingons, designed to be played against one another for learning games. Once players felt comfortable, the set also included eight half decks of twenty cards each that could be mixed and matched with one another for players to kickstart their Magic collections with and dive into full games. Alongside the two hundred cards included in the set, the beginner box would also include playmats and learn to play guides for each player, as well as two spindown tokens for tracking life. 

Wizards gave no further elaboration as to what exactly the logistic, production, and shipping issues with the Star Trek set are that would necessitate cancelling the Beginner Box, though the company has been recently facing a plethora of supply chain issues with its releases. This includes the recent delays to the upcoming Reality Fractures set, which was set to release in early October, though now several products have been delayed and will have very limited availability at launch, particularly in North America. 

Focus Entertainment Fires Back at Toxic Community Members with Legal Action

Credit: Focus Entertainment

It’s no secret that online video game communities can produce some of the most wretched, despicable posters ever to troll through the alleys of cyberspace, with focused harassment campaigns against video game developers becoming a grave concern in the modern industry. Focus Entertainment has had enough though, and in a recent statement stated their intention to pursue legal action against members of their fan communities that are engaging in online harassment towards their team. 

Focus released a statement on their social media page addressing the toxic actions taken by unnamed members of their online fan community, condemning their actions and expressing their right to take legal action in their team's defense. The statement in full reads that, “Today we’d like to remind everyone about the rules of conduct of this community.”

“We expect you to contribute to an environment where everyone feels welcome, respected, and included, be it fellow community members, moderators, dev teams, or Focus staff. Focus staff have sadly seen their share of toxic messages over the past years, but a recent escalation has led Focus Entertainment to file formal legal complaints against players directly threatening team members.”

“Our networks and hubs are about passion, not hatred. We are proud of our team and will not tolerate violent or sexist comments. We reserve the right to take all necessary legal action to protect our staff. Thanks for your attention.”

Focus, wisely, did not share any further details as to the nature of the harassment against their team, or who was targeted by the attacks.

While most reasonable folks would read this, nod and say, “Hey, good on them for standing up for their team”, there were others who attacked the statement as “a violation of the free speech of community members.” Focus responded to this by writing that, “Racism, sexism, death threats, harassment of any kind: are NOT free speech, we will never tolerate any of this.”

One particularly tenacious jerk even tried to reference badly interpreted United States law regarding freedom of speech to defend the harassment, to which Focus Entertainment simply responded, “We’re French.”

Focus Entertainment has not put out any further official statement regarding the legal action at time of writing.

Hyperlight Drifter Studio Faces Possible Collapse After Publishing Deal Falls Through

Credit: Heart Machine

Hyperlight Drifter was one of the most visually striking indie games of its time, mixing retro graphics with a bright, surrealist artstyle that quickly made the game a classic. At the time, the future looked incredibly bright for the studio behind the game, Heart Machine. The future is here now, and rather than being filled with hope and possibility, it is instead deeply grim. Heart Machine studios announced that due to a recent publishing deal for one of its titles falling through the studio faces imminent closure, having already been forced to lay off the majority of its staff to continue to operate in any capacity.

Alx Preston, the studio head at Heart Machine, shared the news on his LinkedIn. 

“We had been working on an unannounced title, funded by a publisher. This week, they decided to no longer move forward with it. Without this key income for the studio, any other immediate prospects or other funds, nearly everyone at the studio had to be laid off. It's devastating.”

Preston elaborates that the studio will do everything it can for those employees that were let go, as well as offering to help with networking and finding new jobs for those affected. As for the future of Heart Machine, Preston writes that he does not know what it will take for the studio to survive, but that the remaining staff will do everything they can to stay afloat. 

Despite the success of Hyperlight Drifter thrusting Heart Machine to the international gaming stage, the studio has struggled to make lightning strike twice, having gone through multiple rounds of layoffs since October 2025.

Heart Machine voluntarily recognized a workers union formed by 13 of its employees earlier this year, and at time of writing it is unknown if any of those workers who were let go were a part of this union.

Bungie Announces Plans to Return Vaulted Content Back to Destiny 2

Credit: Bungie

It’s been a real bad year for Bungie, having launched Marathon to far less success than initially hoped and laying off nearly its entire Destiny team as they told its dedicated player base that it would be closing the doors on Destiny for good following one last update earlier this year. In a welcome bit of good news though, it looks like Destiny might not be so dead after all, as the new leadership team for Destiny announced last week that all of the previously locked away raids and story content are being released from the vault, alongside teasing work on future destiny titles. 

The blog post and associated video statement from Bungie come from two of the newest members of studio leadership, studio head Poria Torken and Marathon’s general manager Josh Deane. In the statement, Torken and Deane recognize the frustration and disappointment that fans have felt with bungie regarding many of the company’s recent decisions, highlighting a need for the company to hold itself accountable and do better in the future. 

“Bungie has been creating games for over 30 years and we have welcomed the communities that have formed around them,” the pair write on the blog post. “Over the last few years, we lost sight of that, and there have been real consequences. We lost trust, we lost incredible colleagues, and we lost sight of what we should be prioritizing most, our players. As a new leadership team, we must own that.”

Alongside general updates to Marathon to expand its PvE content and build a more widely engaging game, the widespread outcry and sunsetting Destiny spurned the team at Bungie to open the doors back on the franchise. Alongside planning for new content in the future for Destiny, the team is focusing on one of the most prominent criticisms of the game: the expansive amount of old content that has been cut from the game. 

Bungie plans to release these old raids and storylines out from the content vault for the Destiny 2 player base to enjoy, letting old and new players alike experience the history and story of the game. The development team does note though that this process will take time, since the old content will need to be modernized and balanced to work with the current version of Destiny 2.

“When we look to the future of Destiny all up this restoration is the first step to lay the foundation for how we reforge into new stories and games that you all deserve,” the team writes. “We’ll have more details on our unvaulting efforts in the coming months, and we’re very excited to share more about our vision to celebrate Destiny’s legacy and build its future with you all as soon as we can.”
