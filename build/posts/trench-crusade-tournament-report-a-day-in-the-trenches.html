Trench Crusade Tournament Report: A Day in the Trenches

May 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Hosted by my FLGS, Dark Sphere London, A Day in the Trenches was a one-day tournament showcasing the fast pace of Trench Crusade to newer players. It was based around playing four games with warbands of increasing point values, from 400 to 700 ducats, and had no painting requirement!

Originally, I signed up with the intent to get some practice in with my work-in-progress New Antioch or Trench Pilgrims, but ultimately decided to fall back on my fully painted Iron Sultanate to help show off the variety of factions. Besides, it couldn't hurt to get more games in with my go-to faction, as so far I've only taken part in a single campaign (against the event organizers actually, so it was only right to come along and support their efforts to grow the local player base).

My Iron Sultanate Warband. (Credit - hydriatus)

Game 1: King of the Hill

vs. talosviii's Trench Pilgrims

The first game was a classic "control a piece of scenery in the middle of the board" scenario. Rather than one player scoring for having the most models in the scenery piece at the end of each turn, both players scored points equal to the amount of models they controlled in the scenery piece at the end of each turn.

It was also the only mission to allow Infiltrators to use their special deployment rules in the entire event. Having practiced this mission before the event, I brought along an Assassin to try to grab an early advantage - and thanks to some lucky dice rolls, it paid off!

My list was:

ELITES
  • Yuzbasi - Greatsword, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Combat Helmet, Wind Amulet
  • Assassin - Assassin's Dagger, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Combat Helmet, Wind Amulet
TROOPS
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
My opponent brought along standard Trench Pilgrims - a War Prophet leading a pair of Pilgrims and an Anchorite Shrine! Recognizing I would be unable to do much once the giant robot parked itself on the objective, I abandoned my plan of having the Assassin control the objective early, and instead deployed him far more aggressively to act as a speed bump.

Game 01 Deployment. (Credit - hydriatus)

A surprisingly dangerous speed bump actually, as he managed to knock the Anchorite Shrine down on his first turn charge, and proceeded to run back to my lines ahead of the vengeful pilgrims. The War Prophet's chants turned out to be more of a danger, rapidly allowing the whole enemy warband to swiftly threaten the objective, and bring their bolt action and shotgun to bear effectively before my Azebs could get their jezzails into short range.

My Yuzbasi was able to capitalize on my Assassin's early luck by charging the Anchorite Shrine and stripping away it's tough with his first attack, before triggering Bloodbath on his second to kill it outright.

Despite this early edge, the superior shooting of the pilgrims took out my Azebs before the Assassin could kill them, and my Yuzbasi took an anti-tank hammer from the War Prophet to the face. As the game wrapped up, we were left with just a bloodied War Prophet and an Assassin locked in melee.

The dust settles... (Credit - hydriatus)

Counting up the points, it turned out my first game had ended in a draw!

Game 2: Claim No Man's Land

vs. bitslådan's Trench Ghosts

Buoyed by my relative success last game, I went into another new match-up - this time against Trench Ghosts!

And I was not prepared for it at all.

Something spooky this way comes... (Credit- hydriatus)

The scenario called for both players to try to control as many of five objectives spread across the board as they could. My plan going in had been to use my Azebs to hold the objectives on my side of the board, and use the hard hitting Yuzbasi and Assassin to claim the center.

Going in, my list was:

ELITES
  • Yuzbasi - Greatsword, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
  • Assassin - Assassin's Dagger, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
TROOPS
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition, Shovel
Early hubris threw my plan out the window when an early grenade launcher shot from the Heretic Priest in the tank palanquin scattered my left-most flank. My shorter range meant the ghosts were able to inflict blood markers far more reliably than my Azebs, and I was slowly whittled down.

The totally foreseeable consequences of my choices... (Credit - hydriatus)

The second turn saw my opponent land a second grenade shot on my left flank and kill both Azebs there, before using Puppetmaster to force my Assassin away from my home objective.

At this point I desperately had my Assassin trigger his Wind Amulet and charge across the board, slaying a Wretch and engaging the Death Commando - and was promptly betrayed by my dice, and my poor Assassin was butchered in turn.

The game ended in a solid loss, as the numerical advantage of the ghosts allowed them to contest all of the objectives I was claiming.

Never forget - grenade launchers are like discount Artillery Witches, treat them as such!

Best Painted

As lunchtime came, participants in the event were invited to display their warbands, and to vote for which one was the best painted. Ultimately, the single Great Hunger player won with their small but very detailed force!

Great Hunger Warband by guy_mcman (Credit - hydriatus)

Game 3: Relic Hunt

vs. David Raven's Trench Pilgrims

Relic Hunt is one of those scenarios that forces confrontation - players have to claim reliquaries to score points, but the number of reliquaries diminishes as the game progresses.

Once more, I would face off against Trench Pilgrims - no Anchorite Shrine this time, but a Stigmatic Nun and a Communicant would still prove more than enough to foil my attempts at a victory!

My list for this scenario was:

ELITES
  • Yuzbasi - Greatsword, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Frag Grenades, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
  • Assassin - Assassin's Dagger, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Frag Grenades, Cloak of Alamut, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
  • Alchemist - Halberd Gun, Alchemist Armour, Alchemical Ammunition, Combat Helmet
TROOPS
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
This was the game in which I brought a Jabirean Alchemist to provide fire support - and due to poor placement, she wound up doing absolutely nothing at all! With a halberd gun I probably should have tried to be more aggressive and used her more offensively, but she has been my go-to sniper for many campaign games, and old habits are hard to break...

Time to hunt some relics amidst the ruins! (Credit - hydriatus)

This scenario proved frustrating - not because of my opponent, who was an excellent and sympathetic sport, but my dice choosing to once again betray me. Claiming the reliquaries required a Success Roll, and I just could not manage to pass one, despite numerous attempts.

Turn one saw both sides slowly advancing and attempting to claim the reliquaries closest to their deployment zones. I boldly had my Assassin run into the center of the board to start grabbing the objectives there, as a Pilgrim's lucky shot through a broken window killed one of my Azebs, leaving my Yuzbasi to hold the left flank on his own - which, in fairness, he did!

Turn two saw a particular upset when my Assassin attempted to charge the incoming Stigmatic Nun, only to fail by rolling a two on his charge distance! Needless to say, my opponent capitalized on this misfortune and the Nun butchered my Assassin. Meanwhile, the Communicant had lumbered into position to rip apart an Azeb on my right flank trying to support the now expired Assassin.

Desperate to try to claim some points, I had my Yuzbasi begin running towards the center of the board to claim the reliquaries there, but it was too little too late - my last Azeb attempted a daring play to claim the reliquaries out from under the Nun and Communicant, but poor rolls killed that plan as surely as the Nun slew that daring Azeb.

With my Alchemist camped on top of a tower unable to actually see anything due to intervening rooftops, and my Yuzbasi failing his own Dash rolls, the game came to end with another loss for me.

Game 4: Sacred Object

vs. bear_and-a_brush's Iron Sultanate

The final game of the day saw my paired off with another Sultanate player - who brought along a Brazen Bull to what on paper seemed destined to become a game of Blood Bowl.

Each warband was split into two equal groups - one half would deploy normally, and the other would have each model arrive on the table on a random board edge on the first turn. The objective? Grab a relic from the center of the board and either be carrying it by the end of the game or walk off the board with it.

For this final game, my list was:

ELITES
  • Yuzbasi - Greatsword, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
  • Assassin - Assassin's Dagger, Sword, Reinforced Armour, Cloak of Alamut, Combat Helmet, Gas Mask, Wind Amulet
  • Alchemist - Sniper Rifle, Sword, Alchemist Armour, Combat Helmet, Rocket-Propelled Grenade
TROOPS
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Jezzail, Alchemical Ammunition
  • Azeb - Flamethrower
This fog of war deployment threw a lot of plans out the window, resulting in situations like the enemy Alchemist appearing on a flank to take a shot at my now exposed Alchemist, only to be charged in the back by my suddenly appearing Yuzbasi.

Skirmishing around the relic. (Credit - hydriatus)

Poor dice rolls on my part resulted in my Assassin being trapped in a melee against a Lion of Jabir for the majority of the game, whilst my opponent's Assassin manged to trigger my Yuzbasi's Tough before disengaging and intercepting my Azebs threatening the objective.

The relic wasn't actually claimed until the last turn of the game, when my opponent's musician Azeb moved in, grabbed it, then passed it off to the Brazen Bull. Before the giant beast could get away however, my Yuzbasi managed to charge and knock it down, making it fumble the relic closer to the enemy deployment zone - and out of reach of any of my models.

So the game ended with the enemy Yuzbasi walking over and picking it up, securing another loss for me!

Final Thoughts

And with that, the day came to a close, and I walked away with three losses and a draw to my name - but honestly, that is what I was expecting, since the event brought in new players and factions that I had not had a chance to play against!

Prior to this, my experience of playing Trench Crusade had been limited to campaign and one-off games against mostly New Antioch, Heretic Legion, and Black Grail. So to face off against Trench Pilgrims (twice!), Trench Ghosts, and Iron Sultanate was a welcome change of pace - even if most of the time I was taken aback by some special rule or other.

As for the logistics of the event itself, I honestly cannot think of anything that could have been done to improve it - the player pack released a month before the event included all the missions and their scoring mechanics, giving folks plenty of time to practice. The location was spacious and there were 12 distinct tables laid out with wonderful scenery - though some may have benefited from a little sheet defining if something was difficult or dangerous terrain.

Now the format itself: campaign games already start at under the recommended ducat value for a one-off game, and the new player nature of this event had us start far below that. This did lead to some very quick games with small model counts (perfect for teaching the game or getting back up to speed with its rules), though it also felt somewhat limiting - my experience of campaign play taught me that most Trench Crusade scenarios favor claiming objectives, in which case trying to field the maximum amount of models to make sure you can do that is good sense. I couldn't see the value in bringing more expensive models like a Lion or Bull (who in my experience need some investment in equipment to perform well).

That is why I went in with as many Azebs as I could take after equipping my ELITES. Whilst they did not achieve much in the games themselves, they did take shots and threaten objectives quite well. Maybe I just over-equipped my Yuzbasi, and should have been content with Standard Armour to save points, and dropped the pieces of kit that grant NEGATE to either GAS / FIRE / SHRAPNEL as it was not as widespread as I feared.

But in the end, I walked away eager for more. I am not much of a tournament player (my luck with dice curtails any ambitions in that field!), but events such as this do remain a great way to connect with the greater community around a game, and experience match-ups you do not get to face in your regular games!

Maybe I'll invest in some Janissaries next time - I hear the siege jezzail is a nifty piece of kit, and I've been on the receiving end of a machine gun enough times to know it's a sound investment...
