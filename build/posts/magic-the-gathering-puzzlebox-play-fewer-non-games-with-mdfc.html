Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Play Fewer Non-Games with MDFCs

May 26th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Let's start strong with a statement: MDFCs, or 'Modal Double-Faced Cards' are one of those tools in your Commander toolbelt that should always be top of mind. First introduced in Zendikar Rising, these are a class of cards with a fairly simple concept, being non-Lands on the front, and Lands on the back. The utility there is obvious, that when you need a Land they fill that role, but if you're looking to spend mana each is a (usually overcosted) version of a Spell you'd consider playing. The fact they're overcosted is less of a downside than you might think, though, because if we're counting them as Lands during deckbuilding, the only time we're excited to cast them as Spells is when we have mana to burn, or few options in hand. There's a ton of quirks to how you can include these cards in your lists, intended ratios, and specific use-cases, and I want to pitch every single way to play more MDFCs in your lists today. They're essentially the 'fiber' of Commander, helping things flow a bit more smoothly, if you'll excuse the metaphor. With that being said, let's talk about eating your veggies, and enjoying it.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Delicious Veggies

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Let's start with a rules quirk: You can play the Land side of MDFCs from zones other than your hand, if you have something like a Crucible of Worlds or Courser of Kruphix, as a counterpoint to those who could claim they reduce actual Land density in your list for those synergies. Unfortunately, by that same token you cannot put them directly into play with Growth Spiral, because the key word is 'play'; any time you could use your "Land drop" during a given turn, you can play the Land side, but they're still whatever type the front face is outside of those instances if not on the battlefield. This means you could, in a color like Black, add back "Lands" from your yard to hand with Creature recursion if you included a Blackbloom Rogue or Boggart Trawler. The fact different colors can make use of the card types inherent to the front sides of MDFCs is vital to remember, because having the ability to never miss a Land drop is pivotal to winning games of Commander; sometimes you'll have to Archaeomancer for a "Land", and get back your Sea Gate Restoration.

This is all a component of the ethos that, in a game of non-cEDH Commander, the player who does the most is usually in a winning position. Plays the most Lands, draws the most cards, performs the greatest number of game actions—if you're exchanging resources & keeping the engine hot, you're on track to victory. Commander has trended towards parallel play, in opposition to 60-card Magic's orthogonal play. You can think of it like sandcastles, where parallel play is comparing the grandeur of yours against the opponents, while orthogonal play is about knocking down your opponents' castles while having at least something left standing on your side. MDFCs help you always put something on the castle, because they're both mana and outlet, even if either side isn't especially efficient. That being said, MDFCs are also tools of orthogonal play...because so many of them are interaction on their front side. Fell the Profane, Sink into Stupor, Witch Enchanter—all of these are ways to include more answers if an opponent has gotten too far ahead of the pack, but at worst, if you don't need to remove something at any given time, they help you not miss your Land drop (and even come in untapped, to boot).

The 2026 Modalympics

So, now you're ready to include MDFCs in your list, what are the best options in each color? For starters, following the release of Modern Horizons 3, each color has 3 options that come in untapped for the cost of 3 life, as well as a tapped MDFC for each 2-color pair. That said, some of the tapped MDFCs from ZNR are still worth considering, largely because of the quality of their front side. The second thing to consider, before we have our little MDFC awards show, is whether you're replacing Lands with MDFCs, or replacing non-Lands. If you're swapping non-Lands with MDFCs (exchanging say, a Reclamation Sage for a Witch Enchanter), you're increasing the relative density of Lands in your list, while slightly raising the curve of your non-Lands. This all works out in the end, as the statistical increase in Lands helps you miss fewer drops, which in turn means you can more consistently afford the slighly-higher cost on said MDFC when you need the front side. The more dangerous, greedy option though, is to replace things like Basic Lands with MDFCs. If you've seen any of my articles, this tends to be where I sit, as especially in the case of something like a Monocolor deck, the best way to extract utility from your reduced number of options (due to having a single color) is to pack as much utility as possible into your manabase. This can be via utility Lands, sure, but you should think of MDFCs as utility Lands of a sort. It's another reason to jam Arid Archway & Guildless Promenade as well, in any case, as these cards allow you to exchange MDFCs from their Land half, to the non-Land half, if you've hit a point where they become necessary. If your goal with MDFCs is to miss fewer Land drops, replace non-Lands, up to a 37-40 count of total Lands+MDFCs in your list. If your goal with MDFCs is to have more to do, and not flood out, replace Lands, usually to a total count of 33-37 Lands+MDFCs. Statistically, 37 Lands is the sweet spot for Commander, so something like 34 Lands and 3 MDFCs works in basically every deck, though you can option for more in 1 or 2-color lists.

Okay okay, so what are the best MDFCs? I've rated what I believe to be my top 3 choices for each color in a vacuum, that you could slot into nearly any deck to see how the card type feels to play around with. Of course, there's lists where you lack removal and would rather run something like Hagra Mauling & Fell the Profane as Black options, but this is meant to be a broad categorization. Many of these are obvious top contenders, being effects decks already eagerly play, but there's plenty of odd choices, the most glaring of which is Tangled Florahedron. This is either a tapped Land that produces {G}, or a 2-cost vanilla mana dork—what's so special about that? The secret is in the consistency it provides to your deck's opening lines, especially with a 4+ mana Commander. It either allows you to take a turn off and play a tapland, or serves the role a signet might, ramping on 2 to keep competitive with your opponents. Flexibility, not flashiness, is the name of the game for MDFCs. Not listed here are all of the 2-color options, though as a brief rundown, the three best (if you're in the colors) are Revitalizing Repast, Waterlogged Teachings, & Legion Leadership, in that order. As for ratios, I use a 3/5/7+ schema to include these cards, depending on the relative necessity for untapped Lands in my lists. For 3+ color decks, usually only 3 MDFCs make it in, often among the top of their class above, and almost always untapped options. You can consider MDFCs to be akin to an Otawara, Soaring City, where it's usually an untapped Monocolor Lands, but sometimes utility. For 2-color decks, I try to fit in 5 MDFCs, usually 4 untapped and 1 tapped, with the latter being a 2-color MDFC if appropriate. For 1-color lists, though? I'm jamming essentially every playable option I can, with 7-9 MDFCs depending on how badly I need the untapped Lands. Squeezing every drop of utility from a Monocolor deck's manabase is essential to its function, and MDFCs are one of the best ways to do so, while producing colored mana.

I hope this has pushed you to consider slotting in a few MDFCs to your lists! While they're rarely the snazziest cards one can include, in being boring and highly flexible they just help you play fewer non-games of Magic. Nobody likes sitting at a Commander table with one player way behind on Lands—least of all that player—and including 37-40 by way of MDFCs keeps you consistent in terms of available mana. What if we ran...~30 or so MDFCs, though? What if we wanted to push the mechanic to its limit, and see what happens? There's a Legacy deck that does just that, and a Pauper iteration as well; on Thursday, we'll see the triumphant return of 60 to 99, as I take Pauper Oops! All Spells into EDH. That's a weird deck that's shockingly budget for how complex it tends to play, so you won't want to miss it! It came as a direct result of being reached out to via Reddit, for coverage on the conversion, so that goes to show asking for articles may bear fruit!
