BattleTechnical Archetype Overview: Double Juggernaut

May 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Howdy ristars and welcome back to BattleTechnical, your source for high level strategy and low level mechanics so you can get the most out of your mechs. The Netlist article we did recently covered a very basic list archetype, the beatdown/aggro list. It was focused on aggression and overall list mobility, focusing on diving under the enemy's guns and doing as much damage to them as possible as fast as possible at point blank range. That is one of the easier and stronger ways to run a list, but it is far from the only way.

Today we will be talking about a list archetype that both Liberty and I have used to great success at multiple tournaments now, Double Juggernaut. Double Juggernaut is a list archetype centered around a pair of high end (typically Clan) assault mechs, hoping to use overwhelming firepower and thick armor belts to overpower and crush lists in the mid-fight, while not surrendering the maneuver game completely through the use of high speed and high quality movement assets. Where Beatdown tries to get under your guns as fast as possible to deliver point blank damage, Double Juggernaut lists typically don't particularly care what range they engage at and are difficult to play outranging/skirmishing archetypes into due to the sheer amount of guns and armor that they are bringing to table.

Archetype Basics

Double Juggernaut tends to only work at 7000 BV or more. Below that you functionally only have room for your assault mechs with no support elements, which is risky but possible. For that reason, the sample list will be Scale 4 (8000 BV) and this article will be written assuming that much BV.

The core is, of course, your Juggernauts. For these, you want a pair of 4/6 assault mechs with thick armor belts and high damage, something in excess of 50 points of consistent per-turn damage each. Minimum ranges should be avoided, and weapons with a 3/6/9 range profile should be taken with caution/in limited numbers. Your juggernauts are unlikely to be able to control ranges but they are exactly fast enough to not be helpless, so anything longer than that is probably fine. When you are using the Juggernauts, keep them next to each other. You may be tempted to split them up for map coverage or to do multiple objectives, this is the devil talking, do not do this. Every loss I have had with this archetype can, in some way, be blamed on splitting up the Juggernauts.

The second component of your list is your mobility assets. You run a serious risk of losing on objectives in a well-balanced game with only a pair of assault mechs, and this is the primary reason that this archetype struggles below 7000 BV. It is important to grab at least 2, preferably 3, units with a walk MP of at least 8. You want to be fast and nimble, and aiming for high ground MP instead of jump MP saves BV that you can spend on your assault mechs. These are unlikely to be the decisive arm of the list in combat, but they serve an important light cavalry role, seizing objectives, scoring points, and harrying damaged enemy units that try to escape your Juggernauts.

Generally speaking, you want to keep your Juggernauts together and run them down the most direct path to the enemy. Once you reach the enemy's frontline, do your best to force the enemy to change targets between the two of them as much as possible. If one of your Juggernauts takes a nasty hit, have the other one stand still next turn while your damaged mech backs down/runs for TMM. This should make the less damaged Juggernaut a more appealing target. You want your enemy to split their fire between the two of them as much as possible.

Meanwhile, you need to be firing both Juggernauts at the same target every single turn. Never split their fire. Assuming you have hit the 50+ damage breakpoint, that is at minimum 100 damage pouring in to their target every turn. Most competitive frontline mechs have 200-250 armor and the hit location table tends to focus damage towards the torso. Odds are good that you go internal in one turn of fire at a given mech, and odds are exceptionally high after two turns. The list of mechs that can withstand 3+ turns of being beaten on by a pair of good Juggernauts is populated almost entirely with all energy zombie assault mechs like the Kingfisher C or some of the Archangel configs, anything less durable than that is going to die very quickly.

Your maneuver elements meanwhile exist to do objectives. If there are things scattered around the map for them to do for points, have them do those. If not, then move them in to close combat to cause chaos around where your Juggernauts are fighting. If something tries to run away, all of your fast movers should jump it immediately to prevent its escape. If you can annoy your opponent enough with that they start shooting at your fast movers, that is fire that isn't going in to your Juggernauts, allowing you to live longer and kill more. Every turn that both Juggernauts are active and at full firepower is good for you, try to use your fast movers to extend that time as long as possible.

Sample List

This sample list is built off of Hell's Horses availability (though with no Era restrictions), but would fit fairly well thematically for most Clans, and for IlClan era Mercenaries.
  1. Blood Asp B, 4/5 skills. 2885 BV
  2. Warhammer IIC, 4/5 skills. 2570 BV
  3. Phantom E, 4/4 skills. 1073 BV
  4. Fire Moth H, 4/5 skills, 779 BV
  5. Locust 1Vb, 4/5 skills, 642 BV
This list is a fairly platonic Double Juggernaut list, with a pair of extremely high quality assault mechs backed by a swarm of murderously efficient fast movers.

Blood Asp B

Second Dieron Blood Asp B config conversion. Credit: Perigrin

The Blood Asp B has rapidly become my favorite Blood Asp configuration and I use it a ton. It carries a gauss rifle, paired LRM-20s with Artemis IV, quad MPLs, and an ECM. The ECM provides coverage against enemy Artemis, which is particularly good against enemy ATM mechs. The Blood Asp B is also completely heat neutral and can rip off all of this damage every turn without fail, with a potential max hit of 83 damage if the cluster charts cooperate, but somewhere around 70 is more common. It is also easy to custom convert, the Black Remnant Blood Asp is most of the way there, and if you chop the lower arms off of it and replace them with the lower arms from a Crossbow, you get a completely WYSIWYG Blood Asp B.

Fire Everything while splitting aggro/incoming shots with the other Juggernaut. This is an exceptionally easy mech to use, there is literally no thought required.

This is only one half of the equation though.

Warhammer IIC

Warhammer IIC. credit: porble

One of the game's best assault mechs for 34 years and counting, the Warhammer IIC shouldn't need introduction. I'm still gonna do it though. Carrying a pair of Clan ER PPCs, a quintet of medium pulse lasers, and an SRM-6, the Warhammer IIC technically hits for less damage than the Blood Asp due to some heat concerns, but at 62 possible damage on its heat-efficient firing profile, it has more than enough.

That heat efficient profile is everything but one of the PPCs. This puts you at -1 plus movement heat, which is perfectly acceptable. Drop the SRM every 5 turns. You should only be firing the second PPC if you are past 12 hexes, the MPLs give higher expected damage from the moment they are in range on. Between this and the Blood Asp you have an incredibly dangerous amount of damage output at any range below 7 hexes, and good enough output beyond that.

The quickest way to victory is directly through the enemy frontline, and these mechs should be capable of getting you there.

Phantom E and Locust 1Vb

Lyran Guards Locust. Credit: Jack Hunter

These mechs are being lumped together due to having nearly identical roles. These are both pulse laser armed fast moving mechs with acceptable durability, though the Phantom E is by far the better of the two. These mechs are fantastic at getting critical hits on enemy mechs with open hit locations, particularly mechs that are running away from your frontline for whatever reason. In addition, they have the accuracy bonuses to chase down and destroy enemy light mechs. Ideally though, they are doing objectives. If there are points to capture, buildings to scan, signals to trace, or VIPs to extract, these mechs have sufficient movement and sufficiently low price to go and score those points for you. The Phantom should peel off to actually engage with the enemy first, it has a higher damage output and a generally better statline for direct combat, the Locust should remain doing objectives as long as possible. Both mechs are heat neutral, so take literally every shot you can.

Fire Moth H

Clan Wolf Fire Moth. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Fire Moth H is a mech we have talked about quite a bit, and while it is usually a second best compared to the P, it shines in this list. The Phantom and Locust are covering your light mech hunting duties, and the Juggernauts should be drawing aggro. The H is quite possibly the game's best backstabber and kill-securing mech. Hang back or do objectives around the edges of the map, but the moment you see a low TMM enemy assault mech or heavy mech, blitz in with MASC and try to backstab it. If an enemy is fleeing from the Juggernauts and has broken line of sight to them, dash in to remind them that they are not safe and cannot escape. The H is very vulnerable to enemy light mech hunters and pulse boats, but if you have an enemy big boy with low armor and low TMM, it is well worth trading your 779 BV light mech for an enemy heavy or assault that costs 3 times as much.

The reason you can be so aggressive in trading and backstabbing with the Fire Moth in this list is the presence of other fast movers. None of your maneuver elements are precious to you because you have so many, and you can afford to play for high-value trades with the Fire Moth as a result.

Summary of List Tactics

In short, the Blood Asp and Warhammer both move forwards, covering each other's back arcs as best as possible. They take the most direct possible line towards the enemy frontline, and begin focus firing whatever they are most likely to kill. I would recommend aiming at mechs with uncased ammo, mechs with IS XL engines, and then high firepower mechs, in that order.

Meanwhile, your light mechs buzz around the outside doing objectives and scoring points. If you see an opportunity to engage a damaged enemy target or enemy light mech without putting yourself at too much risk or giving up too many objective points, take it. Look to trade the Fire Moth H for a high value enemy target as early as possible, likely turn 3-4. It is likely that your opponent will back a damaged mech down away from the Juggernauts around that time, and that is the perfect moment to go for the trade.

Played perfectly, most enemy lists will struggle to put down both assault mechs before losing more BV than you have, and you can go wide enough on most missions to out-score any list with insufficient light mechs.

If both Juggernauts get headshotted and die anyways, that's BattleTech. At least you have two of them, so it has to happen twice.

Final Thoughts

While this list is heavy on pulse lasers, they are not required. There are plenty of high quality fast moving mechs without them, such as the various Mercurys and non-pulse Locusts, and the Juggernauts could be Hellstars, Kodiaks, Iron Cheetah A/Ds or Kingfisher Cs to cut back on pulse, if that is needed. This list should be capable of winning most games if played well, and while it requires a bit more thought and judgement than the beatdown list, it still is fairly easy to play. Just keep your Juggernauts together and keep your light mechs annoyingly present and you should do well.
