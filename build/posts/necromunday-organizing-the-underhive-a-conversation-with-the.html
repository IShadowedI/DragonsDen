Necromunday: Organizing the Underhive - A Conversation with the Munda Manager Team

July 27th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Munda Manager is a gem of a community-driven app, streamlining gang building and campaign tracking for Necromunda ‘23 with plans to support the rapidly approaching Necromunda ‘26. One of a small number of projects picking up where the venerable YakTribe left off, Munda Manager has expanded to include tools for arbitrators and players alike, allowing them to customize nearly anything within the game, including equipment, fighters, skills, gangs, entire trading posts, and full-fledged campaigns. I was excited to chat with some of the folks behind the project, as Munda Manager has improved my building and gaming experience in just about every way.

Djidiouf, joesoes, and Tizdale are leads on the project, but they are by no means alone in building Munda Manager. A host of passionate players have joined the team and contributed to the app and its success, including YukiTheSnowFox, Levariel, T_H_E_S_E_U_S, Age, Boschi, Mundastories, Carl R Johnston, LeperColony, GenFailure, Pyriand, HochuChaya, Clicky, TheJosh, Professor Bleep Bloop, HiveScum Wonka, Zaoshu, Steady.

The team has a Patreon account for Necromundans looking to offer additional support for their work. You can also find them on Discord and Instagram, where you are encouraged to share photos of your gangers and their respective profiles for the team to feature on their social feeds.

This conversation was conducted over Discord between April and July of 2026, and has been condensed, reordered, and clarified for your reading pleasure.

Hive Market Minis: So first things first: who are each of you, and what initially brought you together to work on Munda Manager?

joesoes: I’m Johan, and a friend of mine lured me into the game roughly two years ago. So in comparison, I’m still the newbie and still ask silly rules questions.

When we started playing the Underhells campaign, I felt that many were struggling with the gang manager and starting to lose interest. There were some discussions going on in the Underhive Discord server regarding a replacement to YakTribe where people thought “how hard could it be? It’s just an advanced Excel table”. I started looking into creating something similar, mainly to keep my hobby and gaming group alive. This was in September 2024.

Djidiouf: Heya! I'm Djidiouf, or Antoine, and I've been in the hobby for roughly 30 years, starting with Warhammer Fantasy Battle, Warhammer 40,000, Necromunda and Mordheim. Necromunda was the one that captivated me the most: having a bunch of lowlifes fighting for their survival, with terrain everywhere and a huge focus on storytelling and custom content!

Djidiouf's own Enforcers gang in Munda Manager. Credit: Djidiouf

After two years of YakTribe's gang management not being updated, I started looking into how I could create my own tool. But being in IT (I'm a DevOps) I knew that a tool like this for a game like Necromunda is a colossal task. I started playing around with building an app, but doing it all alone was making me freeze in place, as I could see just how difficult that kind of endeavor was.

Tizdale: So I’m Tizdale, or Peter in real life. I first got wind of the project around December 20, 2024 when I ran into a post by joesoes that got me interested.

I've been playing Necromunda for about two and a half years now, and 40K before that for about a year and a half until I got completely fed up with the meta shifts. I saw someone doing a campaign at our club and saw the fun they were collectively having at their own demise to roaming horrors, and I was sold.

The first campaign I played in was run in YakTribe of course, and for my second campaign I wanted to play Spyrers. Seeing as there was zero support in any way or form for them in YakTribe, migrating to Munda Manager and getting it up and populated was also hugely to my own benefit.

The post that got Tizdale interested. Credit: Tizdale

joesoes: Remembering that Dji had chipped into the discussion, I reached out to get some feedback and thoughts on the app, and later on he joined the project. Then came Tiz, who brought the community part. Both have been super helpful and essential to the progress of Munda Manager in their own ways and roles.

Djidiouf: At the end of 2024, when I saw that joesoes was very serious about his app, it pushed me to put more effort into my own app. I was making some progress on the infrastructure and design, but I didn't yet have anything concrete enough off the ground and I thought it would be better to join joesoes on his project. What a bliss it was to find folks who were like-minded and keen to create a tool together, with and for the community!

Since then, I've taken on a role more focused on feature implementation, development and the general direction of the app, making sure we build something solid while also allowing users a lot of freedom when it comes to potential house rules. I was also able to bring a lot of datasets for Tizdale to format for Munda Manager.

Tizdale: I found myself registering on mundamanager.com and sharing that link in a post. Then joesoes reached out to me in a DM asking me to remove it, as he didn't feel quite ready to share it…and a lot of people had already started registering. There was no Discord yet, so that was the first of my suggestions to get picked up, and joesoes made one. I was invited, added as an admin, and we started setting up some channels. That's where I first found out about Djidiouf, who had similarly approached joesoes to support the project.

We found a nice mix where I did the majority of the data entry in the early days. For project contributions, I consider myself to do a lot of the “spit out ideas and see what sticks.” I've reassured everyone that I don't mind if things don't get picked up, as it's the initial idea that gets our heads thinking and going in directions we might otherwise not have considered. We've got a good understanding in that sense.

Djidiouf: A few months in, we were joined by YukiTheSnowFox, who is a very active contributor and a wonderful human, as well as LeperColony and Steady.

Tizdale: Yuki's got a knack for data like I do, but is even more disciplined in getting it churned out as fast as possible

Djidiouf: A number of people joined the team or worked on Munda Manager over time, and all contributed to making it what it is today. Levariel, T_H_E_S_E_U_S, HiveScum Wonka, Clicky, GenFailure, Pyriand, HochuChaya, TheJosh, Professor Bleep Bloop, and Carl R Johnston of Underhive Art, and more.

Tizdale: We gained quite a rapid following and interest between December 20, 2024 and late January 2025. There was so much work done in just about six weeks that people already started to flock to the project in droves. We chose not to do a beta period, but to just go live with a disclaimer on the website banner until around early March 2025. At that point, we concluded there was sufficient support as a gang manager, and we'd almost surpassed the features and functionality available on YakTribe.

We saw a steady influx of new users and strong retention. I've had an easier time since then, as most of the data input is done and we only get a ticket here and there throughout the week. I shifted my focus toward user interaction and keeping both bug and data tickets under control to give joesoes and Djidiouf more breathing space.

Roxxy Riot and "Painful" Polly Styrene loaded up in Munda Manager. Credit: @rokksville

Roxxy Riot, Escher Gang Queen. Credit: @rokksville

Roxxy Riot, Escher Gang Queen. Credit: @rokksville

"Painful" Polly Styrene, Escher Gang Matriarch. Credit: @rokksville

HMM: Johan, you mentioned that you started looking into this mainly to keep your hobby and gaming group alive. Did you set yourself any guiding goals when you started the project?

joesoes: I set two goals when I started the project, based on my experience with YakTribe and my group using it. One, it should be mobile friendly. Two, it should be community driven, it should be open source, and it should not be dependent on me.

HMM: What would each of you say has been the biggest challenge for you personally with the project, and then for the project at large?

joesoes: The biggest challenge for me personally is definitely time. Having a four-year-old, work, and being engaged in my gaming group simply don't add up. Hopefully my Enforcer gang will be fully painted one day! As for the project, there have maybe been some technical challenges, but I would say finding people who want to contribute to the codebase, looping back to the goal of keeping it community driven, is the most challenging part.

Tizdale: Oof. I'll say having been quite fresh to the game. It's been a massively steep and fast learning curve, and Dji has kicked my butt all the way up that hill.

Dji commends me for my patience with users, but he has been quite patient with me and Joe as well, as we only later grasped concepts we didn't yet understand when first implementing them. Later we go, ‘Oooooh, so that's what you meant. We're sorry!’

Djidiouf: For me personally, it has been about managing time zones. I'm between eight and ten hours ahead of most of the team in Europe, so it can sometimes make participating in important discussions complicated. Being a night owl definitely helped!

The biggest challenge for the project, in my opinion, was back in 2025 when we were trying to get exposure and show how good the app was. We're not the biggest networkers, so it required an immense amount of work to try to get seen by potential users, especially in the UK scene. At times it felt like we were facing a wall. In the end, it was those users themselves who took the next step and started talking about Munda Manager to others.

A campaign map at work in Munda Manager. Credit: Djidiouf

HMM: I know I’ve personally set a dozen or more people up with MM at events! How has your experience with users been from when you first went public through now? What do you all see as the community reception thus far?

Tizdale: It's honestly quite amazing how positive everyone is. That said, we're incredibly responsive and have been very quick to fix issues, address missing data or even add features.

I like to think that a big part of that has been related to taking users on board in our journey from the start and being very open. People in general are very understanding and non-pushy in tickets. On our Discord, they're creating quite an engaging and lovely environment too. But toward Munda Manager itself, it's love all around for us.

I presume the vast majority of users can't quite fathom the amount of time and energy that goes into this project. I don't want to say it has ever felt ‘like a job,’ because it's way too hobby-related for me. I enjoy it more and don't consider it a burden. I'll guesstimate that I spend roughly 12 to 16 hours a week, with more early on. I suspect Dji has been spending more than 30, and during the start I suspect Joe was spending 20 to 30-plus hours a week.

Comparing that to our Patreon income, it's a financially very unrewarding and barely interesting endeavour. It really is the love for the community, and by the community, that keeps us going, next to having an awesome tool to run our own campaigns.

HMM: At the US Goonhammer Open in July of 2025, I would say roughly 60 percent of folks had their lists on Munda Manager. At the NOVA Open down in Washington DC last August, it was about 75 percent. At CoriusCon 4 in Berlin this past November, it was 95 percent Munda Manager users. The community adoption and usage is absolutely there.

There is just so much going on with the game, and it's not always officially structured in a way that makes perfect sense! Something Fowler and I were marveling at is where you've managed to make some of the newer systems seamless for us as players. It's easier to work out in Munda Manager than it is flipping back and forth through the book.

Tizdale: I introduced my daughters, seven and ten, more properly to Necromunda. I was shocked by how few questions I got from them while they were navigating Munda Manager. They've got their own accounts and gangs now.

Djidiouf: And I think many people don't even know the full extent of the features we have.

The Bloodswine Cult ready for close quarters combat. Credit: @tom_paint_n_build

Sow, Corpse Grinder Cult Butcher. Credit: @tom_paint_n_build

Swine's Cleaver, a Corpse Grinder Cult Cutter. Credit: @tom_paint_n_build

HMM: It's been interesting for me showcasing the extent of just the roster management. It's helping a lot of newer players skip digging through the books or NecroRAW for, say, random advancements and such.

Is there anything you're each just particularly proud of with it all?

Tizdale: Munda Manager app-wise, it would be Spyrer tiering as well as weapon accessories and attachments with their effect application.

On the part I can influence more, I think it's the lovely growth of the social network we've built up. We've also got a vastly underused guide on the site: the Munda Manager user guide.

joesoes: I am of course proud of the app as a whole and our team. For something app-specific, I need to pick the UX and UI in general. Maybe not especially proud of it, but the notifications feature was a thing where I really felt, ‘Oh, that's nice,’ when I got it to work properly.

Djidiouf: I'm particularly pleased with how open we are to new ideas, feedback and people joining the team. We're constantly listening to the community and are willing to work together. A few times we've received some harsh feedback, but we listened to it and made sure not to get set in our ways, instead using it to improve Munda Manager. It really is a collaborative effort.

I reckon that's what has led us toward becoming our own community of players and arbitrators discussing everything Necromunda together, and creating a space like this is not an easy task. I'm really happy with how it turned out. We've managed to foster an inclusive and friendly community where people help each other.

When it comes to a specific feature, I think one thing that really reflects that openness is how much effort we've put into making Munda Manager work well both on phones and tablets, while also making sure people can still print their gangs properly. We're very mobile-friendly, but we know a lot of players love bringing printed gang sheets to the table, so supporting both has always been important to us. I personally never use the printable roster or the checkboxes myself, but somehow I ended up being the one implementing them because they were important to some people.

Using a tablet to run Munda Manager while gaming. Credit: Djidiouf

HMM: They are, in fact, important to me! I have become more app-centric with time, though.

Now for the most important question. What are everyone's favorite gang or gangs?

Djidiouf: I didn't like Cawdor for a long time and ended up really loving playing them! Overall though, I reckon I'm a big fan of Escher and their chems, so those two are probably my favourites.

Tizdale: I've played Enforcers, Spyrers and, most recently, Van Saar. That said, I think I'm going to go with the religious nutbags: Cawdor. I've got a gang ready, but I've yet to play them.

joesoes: I've only played Enforcers, including Badzone, and Van Saar so far. I'm looking forward to diving into Outcasts soon-ish. Enforcers are my favorites so far!

HMM: Speaking of Enforcers, are you able to officially confirm that they are the most-played gang through Munda Manager?

Djidiouf: Palanite Enforcers are indeed the most-played gang! Ten percent of all gangs created or updated are Palanite Enforcers, followed by Venators. *between February and April of 2026.

Custom trading posts in Munda Manager. Credit: Djidiouf

HMM: Munda Manager feels very full-featured at this point. What is still on the roadmap?

Tizdale: Munda Manager is now in quite a solid, full-featured state, so we've got a lot more breathing space. We get to pick things up when we feel like they’re worthwhile rather than working to a time schedule, and we're capable of adding nice features and quality-of-life updates at a slower pace. Barring new releases and Games Workshop weaving in new features, there is now a light at the end of the tunnel for a while.

*Games Workshop did in fact announce the next edition of Necromunda since this conversation, and the Munda Manager team has been clear they will fully support both the legacy N23 we know and love as well as N26 as it rolls out.

Djidiouf: We're about to announce a new feature that was quietly deployed a few days ago, allowing players to record which fighters they have put Out of Action and view their full OOA history, including the fighters who put them Out of Action. Otherwise, better support for the Outlander campaign is coming!

With that, we’d like to thank Tizdale, joesoes, and Djidiouf for their time and the whole Munda Manager team for their efforts! Thank you as well to Necromunda community standouts @rokksville and @tom_paint_n_build for sharing their gangers with us! Have any questions or feedback?
