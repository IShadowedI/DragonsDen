Command Mass Armies in CityCrushers: Epic Battles

July 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Disclaimer: I was offered a chance to look at this game by my friend, Ben Skinner, one of the writers and miniature designer for the game.  

An 8mm scale game of combined arms, CityCrushers: Epic Battles is the second offering from Troublemaker Games. Their first foray into wargames was CityCrushers, a game of tactical combat between giant robots built of scraps brawling amidst the blasted ruins of cities on a dead world.

Now, they've added infantry, vehicles, and aircraft into the mix, as well as an entire new faction - the Novans, a human kingdom embroiled in a war against the Skinners, a simian species that celebrates violence and conflict.

A Novan army clashes with a horde of Skinners. (Credit - Troublemaker Games)

And that's about it for the lore - the game focuses heavily on offering a fun and accessible game play experience over reams of lore. In fact, the book presents a setting with practical anti-lore - with the soldiers involved in the conflict knowing they are doomed to repeat battles forever at the whims of colossal uncaring gods that command their moves and resolve their fates with dice. The details on both the Novans and the Skinners covers maybe a single page in the 36-page rulebook, which also included assembly instructions and two army lists.

What's in the Box?

CityCrushers: Epic Battles Boxed Game (RRP £120) contains two armies and a rulebook. It has no scenery or accessories (so no dice, tape measure/measuring stick, or tokens), which granted wouldn't fit in the box alongside all the sprues!

So. Many. Sprues. (Credit - hydriatus)

The contents build the following two forces:

Novans

  • 16 Infantry Units
  • 8 Infantry Fighting Vehicles
  • 2 Sniper Squads
  • 4 Battle Tanks  
  • 4 Howitzer Artillery Vehicles 
  • 4 Rocket Artillery Vehicles
  • 3 Weapon Conveyors 
Novan Infantry. (Credit - Troublemaker Games)

Skinners

  • 6 Leader Units
  • 6 Gobbler Units
  • 18 Infantry Units
  • 6 Field Gun Units
  • 6 Biker Units
  • 12 Trucks
  • 6 Scout Buggies
  • 4 City Crushers
  • 1 Capital Crusher
A Skinner patrol on the move. (Credit - Troublemaker Games)

These form a solid core force with room for some expansion - the game recommends armies between 75 and 150 points in size, with the Novans in the box adding up to 62 points, and the Skinners to 64 points.

The Rules

CityCrushers: Epic Battles operates on alternating activations - so during a round, a player will activate one of their formations, perform two actions (such as move and attack, or move and move), and then play will pass to their opponent, who will activate one of their formations, and so on. Who activates first is chosen by the player who finished deploying first in round 1, and then by the player who finishes activating all their formations first in subsequent rounds.

A mob of Skinners overwhelm a platoon of Novan Battle Tanks. (Credit - Troublemaker Games)

Formations are the game's equivalent of units, made up of a number of stands (bases of infantry and/or vehicles). Each stand has a profile covering it's movement, armour, weapon range, firepower, and any special rules associated with it.

Movement is measured in centimeters, with games being played on 6'x4' tables, with at least 12 terrain features. Whilst rules covering various types of terrain and the way it interacts with formations and stands is covered by the rulebook, there is no guidance on how large a terrain feature should be.

Combat, ranged and melee, is handled the same way. When a formation makes an attack, it must target a formation in melee with it, otherwise it can target any enemy formation in line of sight and range. Each stand has different firepower values at 0 cm, 20 cm, and whatever the stand's maximum range is. When making an attack, you pick your target formation, then add up the firepower of each stand, and compare it to the firepower chart in the rulebook to see how many dice you roll.

Then you roll that many D6s, with the target number being the armour value of the targeted stands in the enemy formation. Matching or exceeding this value removes the stand. The loss of stands generates stress markers on the formation, and once a formation has the same amount of markers as stands, it is considered Routed, and can only make Move or Rally (removing half it's stress markers) actions until it is no longer Routed.

And that's the bare bones of the gameplay!

How's It Compare?

Size comparison between Novan Superheavy Command Carrier and a Legions Imperialis Baneblade (Credit - Troublemaker Games)

Is the box good value for money? Let's quickly compare it to three other similarly scaled wargames!

Warhammer: The Horus Heresy – Legions Imperialis

Coming in at a similar price point (RRP: £129), this 8mm game in one of the most recognizable tabletop IPs has less models, but it does include dice, measuring sticks, tokens, and templates to offer a more rounded "in-a-box" offering. It's hardback rulebook has 240 pages, covering lore, rules, army lists and scenarios. It does bear mentioning that this box set does not come with scenery either.

Note: Troublemaker Games also make a set of scaled bases suitable for the game, as we reviewed in the past!

BattleTech: Alpha Strike

While there are several options when it comes to an equivalent "starter box" experience, for sake of comparison I will use the Battletech: Alpha Strike product as a comparison. As of July 2026, the BattleTech: Alpha Strike box set is $79.99, which converts to £59.48. It features 13 models, scenery, dice, a rulebook, novella, and setting primer. There are also card decks covering battlefield support to represent more than just the mechs on the battlefield. It does not come with a way to measure movement and weapon ranges.

Dropzone Commander

At the time of writing, there is no two-player starter set for Dropzone Commander as they gear up for the release of their new edition (Operation Artemis is more a mini campaign featuring one faction, the PHR). However an equivalent can be easily formed by purchasing two army boxes, which cost between £35-£40 each depending on the faction, and a Scenery Set such as the Ruinscape for £30. This provides a full 6x4 board with scenery and two starter armies of around 28 models each for about £110, without any measuring devices or dice.

Final Thoughts

A new entry into the mass battle wargaming scene, CityCrushers: Epic Battles emphasizes simple, quick, and fun gameplay. It has a lot of potential for further development, particularly alternate Novan kingdom forces, or other strange alien factions if Troublemaker Games products continue to overlap with Vanguard Miniatures (Eloi or Cybershadows would be particularly interesting to see in plastic I think!)
