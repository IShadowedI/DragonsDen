Games Industry News Roundup- November 3rd, 2026

November 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Frontline Gaming

ITC Officially Set to Retire in 2027

Pour one out for the ITC, as the Independent Tournament Circuit as we know it will officially be no more as of the 2027 season. In an announcement by Best Coast Pairings last week, the ITC will officially be rebranded as Warhammer Global Rankings following the end of the 2026 season at the World Championships of Warhammer, with some reconfiguring on how the points are allocated to boot.

The “end” of the ITC branding was announced by BCP in a non-chalant facebook post, where BCP wrote “We LOVE a good rankings ladder!  The Warhammer Global Rankings (WGR) track top competitors across individual, team, and faction/army rankings. Formerly known as ITC, WGR brings an updated points algorithm to keep competition exciting until the final dice are rolled!”

The ITC was originally founded by the team at Frontline Gaming, and its system of international player rankings based on event points dominated the competitive scene. Rather than try and beat them with a circuit of their own, Games Workshop instead elected to buy out the ITC entirely in 2024. From there, GW has taken several steps to mold the ITC away from its FLG roots and into something entirely its own, starting by changing the capstone event from the ITC away from the Las Vegas Open, crowning the World Championship of Warhammer as the marquee event of the competitive season instead.

With the upcoming name change, GW is now able to fully divorce the ITC from FLG into a system entirely its own, all while taking advantage of the ubiquity of the ITC circuit.

Alongside rebranding as the WGR, GW is adjusting how the points are weighed between smaller and bigger events. BCP writes in a comment on their announcement that “the largest events will still award the most points and smaller events will award less, but the distance in point allocations between the top and bottom was reduced,” and though we aren't exactly sure how large that gap might be, it does allow for players who cannot frequently travel to supermajor level events a fighting chance for high spots in the WGR.

BCP will remain as the main partner for event organization software and maintain the rankings with the new change, just as they were with the ITC.

Credit: BioWare/Electronic Arts

Mass Effect Shelved to Make Room For New Star Wars Battlefront

Last week the video game industry was rocked by the news that BioWare, one of the most famous game studios in the world, was shut down by EA and the new Mass Effect sequel the team was working on would be cancelled. Rumors as to why this happened were all over the place, but according to reporting from Jason Schrier at Bloomberg, the reasoning for the decision came down to money.

EA is in a financially tight spot following its acquisition by primarily Saudi Arabian backed private equity, with the company now responsible for $18 billion in debt post buyout. With this new pressure in mind, EA executives have been cutting costs wherever they decide that they can, and making much more considered decisions as to what titles they release.

According to Schrier’s sources, EA executives faced two multi-million dollar science fiction projects both being developed at potentially the same time. The first was the new Mass Effect game, and the other would be a new entry in the Star Wars: Battlefront series. Though the next entry in Battlefront hasn’t begun development, while Mass Effect 5 has been in the works for some time now, the executive leadership team decided that backing Star Wars was a much safer prospect than Mass Effect, and made the decision to cancel the game and dissolve Bioware to save on their bottom line.

While Mass Effect is one of the most beloved IPs in gaming, Schrier notes how betting on BioWare over the past decade has been difficult for the massive publisher, writing “what you have to understand is that because of EA's new massive debt load, predictability is more important than ever. EA needs predictable annual cash flow to service its debt. So a Battlefront 3 that hits its deadlines is more valuable than a Mass Effect that will likely be delayed multiple times.”

While it can certainly be argued that BioWare's development troubles post Dragon Age: Inquisition can be laid squarely at the feet of EA executives, it's still hard to argue that a potential new entry in the Battlefront series wouldn't have a far larger appeal than a new Mass Effect game, as the popularity of Mass Effect can’t hold a candle to Star Wars.

Electronic Arts has not made any statement at time of writing addressing Schriers report regarding BioWare’s closure.

Credit: Hasbro

Hasbro Announces New Wizards of the Coast President

Hasbro has finally selected a new president to lead Wizards of the Coast, after leaving the role vacant for several months following the departure of John Hight earlier this year.

The Hasbro Board announced last Monday that Dan Ayoub, the current Senior Vice President of Dungeons & Dragons, will be stepping into the role as president at Wizards. Ayoub has been with Wizards of the Coast since 2021, leaving his longtime role as executive producer and studio head at Halo developer 343 Studios to do so. Hasbro notes in their press release that Ayoub is the first internal candidate ever promoted to the role of President at Wizards.

“I'm honored to lead Wizards of the Coast and Hasbro Digital Games, and grateful to Chris and the leadership team for this opportunity,” Ayoub said in a press release announcing the news. “Magic and D&D have been part of my life for as long as I can remember, and the teams behind them are among the best in the world. We'll put fans first, respect the artists and creators who make these worlds come alive, and set an ambitious course for growth across tabletop and digital. I can't wait to get started.”

Ayoub prior experience in video game development is also likely a significant factor in his promotion, as Hasbro has attempted to expand its digital gaming presence with some notable high-profile games in the works, such as the original sci-fi game Exodus and a game based on Dungeons & Dragons, Warlock.

Ayoub steps in to a role that has been a revolving door at Wizards of the Coast over the past few years, with both of his predecessors serving relatively brief terms as president. John Hight and the president before him, Cynthia Williams, worked for two years as the company president before stepping down. While Hight left the role as president officially in September, continues to work at Wizards in an advisory role.

Credit: Corvus Belli

Corvus Belli Launches North American Online Store

While Infinity and Warcrow have long enjoyed an international fanbase, Corvus Belli’s distribution has been squarely locked in the company’s country of origin, Spain. While this is little issue for its European fanbase, American fans ordering directly from the Corvus Belli webstore have had to deal with long shipping times and potentially high import fees.

America’s long national Infinity nightmare is finally over though, as last week Corvus Belli announced a new updated US webstore to better serve its US fanbase.

Corvus Belli is partnering with Warsenal for the new webstore, which is live now, and will be shipping for US markets out of Warsenal’s Florida headquarters rather than overseas. According to Corvus Belli’s post, benefits that US customers can expect from this change include faster shipping times, lower shipping rates, no surprise fees, and prices in USD for all Corvus Belli products.

While the new collaboration with Warsenal is a gigantic step forward for Corvus Belli in the American market, there are still some potential hitches here. Namely, model production will remain overseas and will be sent to Warsenal for distribution, so tariffs and other trade issues could still cause price fluctuations in Corvus Belli products. Even with this caveat, this is a massive step for Infinity and Warcrow’s growth in the US, and makes the game vastly more accessible for both old and new fans alike.

Credit: WTC

The WTC Opens Negotiations for Games Workshop Official Collaboration

Breaking news out from the World Team Championship (WTC) this morning, as the leadership team behind one of the largest and most visible Warhammer events in the world announced on social media that its leadership board is moving forward with a potential official partnership with Games Workshop.

“We wanted to provide an update to our amazing WTC Community that the formal bid process for WTC 2027 has now concluded,” the WTC writes in the announcement post. “The WTC Organisation would like to thank all of the members from the bid committee for their support during this process (Matt, Malte, Sami, Francois and mhbanz) as well as all the cities that bidded (sic) to host (Talavera, Gilwice and Mechelen).”

“Most of you may have heard that there have also been discussions around exploring a collaboration with Games Workshop which was put forward and as a result was the most favoured.”

With the decision made to collaborate with Games Workshop in an official capacity, the next step for the WTC is to open negotiations. The WTC leadership board, supported by the Captain’s Council Chair representative, stated that there will be a six to eight week timeframe to work with Games Workshop on major terms of the agreement. Should both parties fail to come to terms, the WTC will instead remain independent and host its 2027 event in Mechelen, Belgium.

The exact level of control that Games Workshop would have over the WTC should an official partnership go through is currently unclear, and nothing is final yet while negotiations are ongoing. Bringing the WTC under Games Workshop in an official manner though is a huge step forward for the company’s presence in the competitive Warhammer space, and would be the most notable external acquisition for GW since their purchase of the ITC.

The WTC leadership team will be meeting with Games Workshop leaders in Barcelona during the World Championships of Warhammer to discuss further in person, and expects to have more news then regarding the partnership.
