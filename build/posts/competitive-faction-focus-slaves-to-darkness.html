Competitive Faction Focus: Slaves to Darkness

October 31st, 2026 via Goonhammer

In our Faction Focus series we take a look at the game’s factions and talk about their rules, their units, how they play, and what competitive lists for the faction look like. This is part of a larger series of articles in our Start Competing series. In this article we’re looking at Archaon’s brutal warhost, the Slaves to Darkness.

Archaon the Everchosen. Credit: Kevin Stillman

Why Should You Play Slaves to Darkness?

If you like aggressive, melee focused playstyles and swift, hard hitting units, but you also still want to have enough toughness to take a hit back, Slaves to Darkness are a perfect army for you. While every playstyle involves flinging big blocks of melee units at your opponent, there’s quite a bit of variety in each list - from hyper elite lists helmed by Archaon, to leaning into your best infantry hammers with Varangard and Chosen, to swarms of bodies with the Darkoath hordes, if you like the combat phase, you’ll always have something to look forward to.

This isn’t an all out offensive army though, there are still a whole bunch of tricks and technical things to learn if you want to master them. While they heavily favor aggression and have some fantastic offensive profiles to back it up, knowing how to actually execute your attacks is a key skill in playing this army, and the reward is cleaning your opponent right off the board.

Five Things You Need to Know

  • Pledges to Chaos are powerful. They provide significant unconditional bonuses to every unit, particularly the Pledge to Khorne giving +1 attack to melee profiles. Units are costed and statted accordingly due to this, and it’s worth remembering that when you evaluate how a unit performs - basically think of it like every non-unique unit can always just get +1 attack at some point if you chose.
  • Fighting is your biggest strength, and you must use that to win. That doesn’t mean you have to table opponents to win games, but when you hit, you need to hit hard enough to put your opponent on the back foot. For grindy lists, you still have to lean into playing aggressive: you can stand on circles, but you still have to be ready to brawl.
  • Screens are your friend. Whether you’re playing Darkoath and need to protect your Wilderfiends, or keeping your Knights and Varanguard from getting tagged, you have a variety of options to keep things from getting stuck in bad combats. You can’t waste time using your expensive units for scoring and battle tactics, so action dorks will always be handy.
  • Chosen and Varanguard will clear most things you point them at. You possess two of the strongest single-turn fighting units in the entire game due to having once per game “fight twice, fight last” abilities, letting you deal an absurd, dopamine filled amount of damage at one time.
  • Don’t get shot off the board. You have a lot of powerful centerpiece characters that are monsters, and aren’t able to hide away from shooting threats. Invest in things like Radiance of Dark Glory and keep command points aside to Rally, as the only way you can kill shooting units is by walking up and punching them in the face. Area terrain is your friend, as you have very little to lose from using it in this army.

Are There Any Must-Have Units to Start This Faction?

S2D have a pretty big roster, and thus have quite a few playstyle options. In general, we’ll look at both Darkoath and Warriors of Chaos separately here, even though you absolutely can make mixed lists.

First and foremost, let’s touch on the centerpiece characters. None of them are essential, but all of them are viable. Bel’akor is currently the most commonly seen one, who leans more towards board control focused lists, using his Dark Master ability, debuffs, and combat tricks to manipulate the game to get more favorable fights for your combat units. Archaon requires a full build-around, focusing on very fast lists to lean into his mobility and tech power, or herohammer lists to capitalize on his synergies with Bel’akor and friends. Abraxia is more of a brawler piece, taken in place of other fast cavalry like Knights since she requires a bit less support to function.

Darkoath is pretty straightforward actually, as most of them are things you’ll want to have. Darkoath Wilderfiends are the most important unit to have for Darkoath lists - you lack true melee hammers with them, so their ability to blast mortals out will be a ton of your damage output, and you’ll always want 2-3. Darkoath Marauders, Warchiefs, and Savagers are all essential as well. Fellriders are fast screens and skirmishers, so having a few on hand can be helpful as well. Gunnar Brand & Gunnar’s Oathbound also deserve a shout, as you get a few chaff units along with a decent wizard with retinue, which is the main way to get an actual Darkoath Wizard in the roster.

For Warriors of Chaos, there’s great news. Pretty much every “heavy metal armor guy” has a place in lists. For heroes, Sorcerer Lords, Chaos Lords on Daemonic Mounts, and Daemon Princes (for the Despoilers battle formation) are all staples. Chaos Lord on Karkadrak is also a solid choice, with the foot heroes being a bit more niche or just taken for their cheap costs. Every other Warriors of Chaos unit is a solid roleplayer, with most lists choosing between Varanguard or Chosen as a main hammer and filling out the rest of the list with avils, screens, and roleplayers. Knights are a great secondary hammer, while Warriors can fill a few different positions depending on if you run them reinforced or not. The new Scourge of Aqshy variant are incredibly strong and a reinforced unit is very difficult for your opponent to pick up.

Chaos Warriors. Credit: SRM

How Does This Faction Take Objectives and Score Battle Tactics?

For battle tactics, this is a combat focused army that runs pretty elite, so the “spread out and be mobile” type tactics can be a bit rougher. We often win through combat and primary scoring, so taking secondaries that closest align to that without having to stray too far away from your elite fighting core will be the best. Siege of Ashes is a great choice for pretty much every list. Burning for Vengeance is usually my second choice - while the first step is tricky on some battleplans, the rest of it is very easy if you can get past the initial hurdle. Blazing Onslaught is not a bad choice either, but you have to be a bit more intentional in taking options for shooting/charge phase damage when building your list. Flanking Firestorm is worth a shout as well, as you have a lot of agency over scoring it, but it requires you to spread out a lot more than most lists will do without intentionally planning for that strategy.

How Does This Faction Do Damage?

It’s almost easier to ask how you don't do damage in Slaves to Darkness; pretty much everything outside of the really fast chaff is at least somewhat fighty. It’s worth noting that this army has very little meaningful damage outside of fighting - you can get some Hero phase damage in here and there along with incremental shooting, but you’re planning around damage through combat. For Warriors of Chaos, literally every choice can fight well. Chosen, Varanguard, and Knights (with proper support) will be your primary damage dealers, while Warriors will often be there to take a hit and grind out fights. Ogroid Theridons are also a choice for a secondary hammer. Darkoath lists have it a bit tougher, relying more on reinforced Savagers and Wilderfiends to do a lot of the heavy lifting. It’s worth noting as well that the heroes in this faction are all also decently good at scrapping and will provide a good amount of damage as well.

Are There Any Combos to Build Around?

The combos in this army are more focused around good synergies than explicit powerful rules combos, although there are a few worth mentioning:

  • Bel’akor + any hero that can chain fight (like a Chaos Lord) + any unit that can chain fight with the hero (like Chaos Warriors). This gives a chance to use Deadly Trap on the hero as well as the main unit, effectively two chances to get basically Fights First on them.
  • Chaos Sorcerer Lord + Chosen/Varanguard. These units benefit a ton from a ward save, and this is a reliable way to get one without needing the Pledge investment.
  • Chaos Lord on Karkadrak + Deathmonger (Heroic Trait). This works with any hero, but it’s most common here since he’s the toughest and fightiest option that can take Eye of the Gods. This gives you a pretty reasonable way to threaten an Ascension pretty early and have a good missile unit.
  • “The Darkoath Core” - Darkoath Horde (Battle Formation) + Chieftain + 20 Mauraders + 20 Savagers + Wilderfiend. This is a single regiment that’s a great contingency for getting some bodies in your army, even if you don’t go all in on darkoath. Recursion, plenty of mortal wounds, and a hell of a lot of space on the board.
  • Despoilers (Battle Formation) + Daemon Prince (Pledge to Nurgle) + Scourge of Aqshy Chaos Warriors. The Daemon Prince can use Despoilers to give the Nurgle mark to the Warrior block at the end of your first turn, letting them easily reach a 5++ ward and having a really difficult block of wounds to shift.
  • Daemon Prince + Deathmonger (Heroic Trait). The Fight First on the Daemon Prince lasts for the full turn, cancelling out the Strikes Last you get from Deathmonger. This means you effectively get to hit at Strikes First and then “Strikes Normal” timings.

Note I didn’t mention every single chain fighting synergy, as those are pretty straightforward - but Theridons + Myrmidons and Chaos Lord on Daemonic Mounts + Knights are matches made in heaven.

Ogroid Myrmidon. Credit: SRM

Sample Lists

Slaves to Darkness is an army with currently a lot of list diversity, so this is absolutely not going to be a comprehensive list. Try these out, modify them, and experiment with other options to find what works for you.

Secondaries are scored by the Furies and Chariot, which can flexibly be screens against fast alpha strike lists. This is a variation of Roland from Woehammer’s list, which replaces the Karkadrak lord and chaff units for an extra Chaos Lord on Daemonic Mount and Knights to fight a bit better.

Final Thoughts

There are tons of options right now in this army, so I definitely suggest doing some experimenting to find out what works for you. Overall, S2D is a great choice for an army that has tough units that fight well. If you like combat phases, you enjoy brawling on objectives, and you love absurdly high damage numbers, you’ll have a ton of fun breaking these guys out on the table.
