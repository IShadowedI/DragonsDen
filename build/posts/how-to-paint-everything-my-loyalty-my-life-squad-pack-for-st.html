How to Paint Everything: My Loyalty, My Life Squad Pack for Star Wars Shatterpoint

November 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Morgan Elsbeth and her squad of Night Troopers have arrived to round out the villains from the Ahsoka streaming series. Once painted, this group of models should look pretty striking on the tabletop – Morgan in contrasting green and red, and the troopers with their patchwork armor.

Assembly and Basing

Assembly was on the easier side, with the only tricky thing being placing the troopers on the large bases so that their spacing looks even. The Night Troopers have an option to include wisps of smoke coming out of their armor, or not – I chose to do one base with the smoke, and one without. For Morgan Elsbeth, you can decide whether or not to add a wall of fire to the base. It’s not featured on the box art or in any of the promotional photos, but I thought it would add some dynamic color to the model, so I decided to include it.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers
Image credit: Jefferson Powers

I primed the models in Vallejo Black Surface Primer, and then I did the metal floor bases with my usual mix of greys. I started with a flat layer of Army Painter Deep Grey, followed by a drybrushed layer in Army Painter Ash Grey, and then a final drybrushed highlight in Reaper Misty Grey. I left Morgan Elsbeth’s wall of flame and Captain Enoch’s floor grating for now – those would involve some object source highlighting so I would wait to do them at the end.

Captain Enoch and the Night Troopers

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

They may sound like a ‘70s funk band, but they’re actually a squad of gruesome zombie Stormtroopers with armor that has been heavily repaired using everything from gold to what appears to be red electrical tape.

I wanted these troopers to have a slightly warmer look than regular Stormtroopers, so I started with a flat layer of Reaper Wolf Grey, doing two coats over the black primer. I then drybrushed all the armor areas in Reaper Polished Bone, but this still left it a little dark so I drybrushed a layer of Reaper Pure White to lighten everything up a bit. One of the reasons drybrushing is so great for highlights is that it lets a lot of the layer underneath show through, so the Pure White reads as a lighter warm grey when drybrushed over the Wolf Grey and Polished Bone.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

I picked out a few areas of armor – the face and top of helmet on one, a shoulder pad, chest plate and belt on another – to go over in Army Painter Gravelord Grey Speedpaint. It’s a shortcut but a pretty effective one, eliminating the need to do separate base layers and highlights for a relatively small part of a few of the models.

With that done, I brushed in a few blobs of Army Painter Bright Gold here and there, using the sculpted cracks in the armor to create shapes for the gold. Then I filled in the black areas between the armor plates (and also the weapons) in Army Painter Grim Black Speedpaint. I painted the red straps with Army Painter Slaughter Red Speedpaint.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

Finally, I gave each model a wash in Army Painter Strong Tone, mixed 2:1 with Speedpaint Medium. This was especially important in order to add shading to the gold areas. I then did a final drybrushed highlight in Army Painter Ancient Stone.

When painting the troops I looked at some reference from the streaming show, but I didn’t sweat the details too much – there were hundreds of them in the show and presumably each one was a little different, so this was an opportunity to be a little creative. Enoch, on the other hand, is a distinct individual so I made sure to look at a lot of reference and get all the details right.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

Enoch has more gold in his armor, and only a few grey patches. He doesn’t have any red straps, instead sporting a reddish purple skirt similar to what the clone wars-era ARC troopers wear.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

For Enoch's skirt, along with the spaces between the armor plates that are black on the rank-and-file troopers, I used a base layer of Army Painter Basilisk Red, followed by an Army Painter Dark Red Tone wash. I did the piping on the skirt on Army Painter Resplendent Red, and the belt and holster in Grim Black Speedpaint.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

Captain Enoch also has a pair of Imperial insignia on his helmet, which I approximated with a few dots of Resplendent Red.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers
Image credit: Jefferson Powers

As mentioned earlier, I did one of the Night Trooper bases with the optional smoke coming out of the armor. I painted the wisps of smoke with Army Painter Ghillie Dew Speedpaint, then drybrushed the areas around the smoke with Army Painter Emerald Forest to add a green glow. I did something similar for the floor grating on Captain Enoch’s base, first filling in the grate with Army Painter Eternal Hunt and then drybrushing the surrounding floor and nearby parts of the figure in Emerald Forest.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

Morgan Elsbeth

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

The first thing I did with this model was the green flames on the base and sword. I started with a solid coat of Army Painter Eternal Hunt, then added a layer of Army Painter Shining Silver to the sword blade. After that I drybrushed some highlights in Army Painter Emerald Forest, but honestly the difference in tone wasn’t very noticeable. I would come back to that a little later.

Following this I filled in the skirt in Army Painter Matt Black, to touch up where painting the base and the green flames had splashed over.

Then I moved on to the head and hands, starting with a layer of Army Painter Ash Grey for the hair and Reaper Mountain Stone for the face and fingers. I then drybrushed highlights in Army Painter Grey Castle, and (after doing the eyes in white and black) added some brushed-on lighter highlights to the face and fingers in Reaper Polished Bone. Finally I lightly dotted in an approximation of her face markings in Mountain Stone.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

I filled in the tunic and sleeves with a flat layer of Army Painter Basilisk Red, also doing a very light drybrush over the skirt and belt, to provide just a hint of a reddish purple highlight. I painted the inner tunic in Army Painter Resplendent Red, and the clasp on the belt in Shining Silver. After letting all that dry, I covered the whole tunic area with a wash in Army Painter Dark Red Tone, mixed roughly 1:1 with Speedpaint Medium.

I painted in the handle of the sword in Army Painter Ancient Stone, then lightly drybrushed the front of the model in Army Painter Rainforest – the goal here was to add a glow from the green flames, so I focused the drybrushing on the front of the skirt, underside of the left arm and hand, and the right side of the head and torso, where light from the sword would fall.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

After all that I did a final drybrush over the entire model in Ancient Stone, to tone down the green a bit and add highlights to the back and left side where I didn’t have any source lighting.

Image credit: Jefferson Powers

Finishing Up

I painted the edges of the bases in Matt Black, and then did my two-step varnishing process...sort of. For Morgan I used my normal method, which is a layer of Army Painter Matt Varnish, thinned with a few drops of water, followed the next day by a layer of AK Interactive Ultra Matte, mixed 3:2 with Speedpaint Medium. The Army Painter Matt Varnish tends to dry a little on the semi-gloss side, which I don’t like for most models. However, I thought it suited the Stormtrooper Armor quite well, so I skipped the Ultra Matte for Enoch and the troops.

And that’s four more Shatterpoint models ready for the table. I’m looking forward to seeing what these characters add to the game...
