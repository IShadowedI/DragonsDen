BattleTech Mech Overview: Venom

October 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

This week in BattleTech we're taking a look at the Venom, also known as the fat Spider. It's a side-grade to the Spider, created during the Clan Invasion as an alternative because of high need (because apparently scaling up to make an entirely different mech that uses more advanced technology was the safer choice in case fighting slowed down than just making more spiders? IDK some of that old fluff is wonky). It looks nearly identical, with a set of winglets on the back being the easiest way to visually differentiate it from the Spider, and carries twice the armament while moving just as fast.

Like a Spider, the venom is very good at breaking any sort of mission that involves looting things, with an 8/12/8 movement profile, two hands, and few enough guns that it's generally relatively cheap. While there may be other mechs that are better in specific situations, both the Venom and Spider are safe choices that you can happily put into any list knowing they'll be able to contribute.

It's so similar to the spider that the recognition code stays the same, both 'mechs being SDR-xxx.

Simonson's Cutthroats Venom. Credit: Jack Hunter

Chassis

At 35 tons the Venom is 5 tons heavier than its spider progenitor. Across all variants it makes use of an XL engine to maintain the same 8/12/8 movement profile despite the increased weight, and uses ferro-fibrous armor to give it at least slightly more protection. It's not an omni, so we do see a couple changes across the variants, but the movement profile does remain the same.

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

Venom. Credit: Rockfish

SDR-9K

Venoms start out, like a Spider, with very marginal armor. 62 points is technically more armor than a Spider (which usually have about 53), but can't be described as anywhere near enough for comfort. Every front location has enough that an IS medium pulse won't go internal, and the torsos can take a clan medium pulse, but that's it. Six leg armor and eight leg structure, or seven torso armor and eight side torso structure are exactly not enough to be able to withstand a 15 damage hit. A just over 50% chance to have the mech rendered nonfunctional by a single solid hit is high enough that I'd need this mech to be less than 798 BV for me to be truly happy with it. It's well armed for the mobility with four medium pulses, but that is too many - 12 single heat sinks means it goes up by six heat at a run.

Jack's Rating: C-

SDR-9KA

This first variant trades one medium pulse (bringing it down to three) for 36 more pips of armor (bringing it up to 98 points). It's not a meaningful decrease in firepower, as it couldn't actually fire the fourth pulse without significantly overheating anyways. With at least 10 points of armor everywhere now it can actually take a couple hits without instantly evaporating. It goes up to 865 BV, and I'll happily pay that to have a functioning mech.

Jack's Rating: B

SDR-9KB

Learning the right lessons but doing them wrong, this variant also has three medium pulses and more armor than the stock variant. Instead of being happy with that, it doesn't have quite as much armor, so a 10-damage hit will go internal on the limbs. Two jump jets are dropped, giving it an 8/12/6 move profile. In exchange for those losses, it gets one extra heat sink, so it's neutral walking (yay..............) and...............a machine gun. Yay. Do not spend 769 BV on this.

Jack's Rating: D

Mercenary Venom. Credit: Jack Hunter

SDR-9KC

The third time is definitely the charm for upgrading the stock Venom. For the third time it has three pulses, but this time instead of making bad trades it has an endo-steel internal structure and switches to double heat sinks. It retains the full 8/12/8 move profile and is up to 119 points of armor - it can take a 15 point hit to the legs or CT without it going internal, and with at least 5 points of armor across each rear torso location it can get tapped by a medium laser without too much concern. It does have c3 if you happen to be using that, but even if you aren't it's a solid alternative to a Spider 9M. Same damage potential (if slightly less accurate), better heat management, and significantly more armor; though that all comes out to a relatively expensive 939 BV. Very good, but not quite great - I'd still use the spider whenever possible.

Jack's Rating: A-

SDR-9KE

Skipping over -9KD (maybe that variant code got left unused for another upgraded normal variant down the line), the -9KE gets extremely weird. We're back down to low armor coverage, this time at 80 points. Enough that a 15-point hit won't actually remove a location that matters, but not enough to actually be survivable. A partial wing brings the move profile up to 8/12/10, which is great and very useful, but then filling out the rest of the mech is an ER medium laser, ER flamer, TAG, bloodhound active probe, remote sensor dispenser, and four vehicular mine dispensers. The mine dispensers give you eight turns of dropping 10-point minefields at the beginning or end of your movement, which is vaguely helpful (I think in practice they aren't really strong enough to stop anyone from moving through a hex, and you can't mine multiple hexes in a row without sacrificing your TMM), and the remote sensors give you a really really bad way to spot for indirect LRM fire. The mech has TAG, just use that with semi-guided. This is definitely a mech that exists, and being able to jump 10 with hands and a probe does have utility, but it's definitely not worth 803 BV.

Jack's Rating: D

Final Thoughts

The Spider 9M makes evaluating the Venom hard, because they're essentially the same mech and nothing quite hits the same intersection of cheap, mobile, damaging, and useful that it does. The 9KC variant comes close if you want to pay more for a bit more durability, but you do lose accuracy on the lasers as well as it costing half again as much, so I can't say that it's a good option. Still, if you're stuck in a situation where you already have the Spider and can't bring a second, the 9KC is a good choice.
