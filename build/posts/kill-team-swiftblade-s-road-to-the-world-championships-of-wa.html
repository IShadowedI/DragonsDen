[Kill Team] Swiftblade's Road to the World Championships of Warhammer 2026

November 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Ah, the World Championships of Warhammer (WCW). The highest level of competitive play for Warhammer, the peak of the mountain. The place where the best of the best all come together, having won their spot at some of the largest events from all over the world, to determine each year who gets to be crowned champion. It’s the annual apex of the Warhammer world, the dream destination of thousands of players worldwide who play their hearts out to earn a spot at. And somehow, one of those bozos who earned that spot is me.

To say I’m excited for worlds feels like an understatement. Since winning Best Overall for Kill Team at the Dallas Open in May, the WCW has loomed large on my mind, truthfully playing at Worlds is just something I never thought I’d be able to do. I’d dreamed it, sure, but the actual reality of attending meant winning on a scale that I just didn’t know if I would be capable of. Then, to my own shock, I did. And then I was also lucky enough to be able to afford to go, the next immediate hurdle. The stars were lining up, the universe was clearing a path and handing me the mandate of heaven. Or, in this case, the Dandate of heaven.

Which means it's time to get to work. With Worlds now less than two weeks away at time of publishing, in this article I’m going to go over my goals for Kill Team the WCW, and how I’ve prepared for the event on both the hobby side and gaming side of things.

My WCW Goals and Team Choice

As much as I’d love to say, “My goal is to simply win the Kill Team World Championship of Warhammer,” I need to be realistic. I’m probably, if not almost certainly, not going to win the top spot at the WCW. I know my own skill level, as well as a general idea of what the skill level is to even seriously consider the possibility of winning the whole thing, and there's a sizable gap there. I’m not even trying to be harsh on myself here, rather I know that if I set unrealistic goals for myself and fail to achieve them, I’m going to ruin any chance of actually enjoying myself at WCW, which would be the biggest shame of all considering the effort it took to get here in the first place.

The WCW is going to be a pretty cutthroat event, with some truly excellent players from across the globe. Even telling myself, “My goal is to break even with my win loss record,” is potentially a very lofty one. While it’s wise to keep my mind open to whatever my record might end up being at Worlds, I still want to set a goal for myself at the event. Goals are healthy, they help drive us and give us something to grind towards and aspire to.

My goal for Worlds is simple: I want to feel like a competitor. I want to play well enough that even when I lose, I feel like I’m playing great games and not just getting steamrolled eleven times in a row. I want to play well enough that when I hop back on the flight to Texas from Barcelona, I left the event feeling like I deserved to be there at the WCW, and it wasn’t just some fluke that I got to go. This goal is maybe a bit more nebulous than most goals I’d set for myself, and also the idea that I somehow don’t “deserve” to go to Worlds after winning a golden ticket to do so is absurd. Still, I think its a good goal, and one that I can work towards through mindful practice.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

As for my team selection, I’ve entertained a few ideas. Vespid Stingwings are very strong right now, while also having a fun, aggressive, and fast playstyle. But as much as I like the team, I also have no real love for the T’au as a faction, and if this is my one chance to play Warhammer on the global stage, I want to do it with a faction that excites me from both a gameplay and lore standpoint. Exodite Dragon Masters would be a good choice too; they’re doing great in the metagame in general, I love elves, and my Dragon Master team is extremely well painted to boot. When I think about the potential brain drain of measuring all of those turn and sprint angles for eleven games in a row, though, I can already feel the headache coming on. Plus, I put the team down for a few months after some initial success early this year while I waited for some sort of clarification ruling on the team’s cover rules. The ruling finally came just a few weeks ago, but by then I’d missed my chance to get in reps with the team. Nemesis Claw would also be a great choice, but I haven’t played them much over the past few months; I’d be coming in way too rusty.

Really, there was only ever one realistic choice here, and that’s Murderwing, the team that ended up winning me Best Overall in the first place. I’m a Chaos dork at heart, I love these spiky evil goobers, and it feels right that the team that won me the ticket to begin with should be the team I play to see this thing through. Plus, my Murderwing look great, they’re fun to play, and with the extra inch added on to their boost charge distance they’re looking pretty spicy too. So, I mentally locked in my team, and got to work preparing.

Hobby Prep

The really nice thing about hobby prep for Worlds so far is that I really haven’t had to do that much of it. Since I took the team already to Dallas, where they scored well enough in painting to win me Best Overall, I could absolutely rest on my laurels here if I wanted too. Which, of course, I won’t, because I’m a maniac.

There’s two main pieces of hobby prep to do for my Murderwing prior to Worlds. The first is a larger task than the second, and also much more important, which is to paint a second Chaos Lord for the team.

I’ve already got one Chaos Lord ready for my Murderwing, my Lord with Relic Lightning Claws, and he’s been a faithful blender through many games, but if I’m going to Worlds I’d like to make sure that I have a more evenly rounded roster for my team, which means making sure I have the flexibility to field a Lord with a reliable shooting option with the plasma pistol.

So I got to work, and honestly I had a really great time painting the Chaos Lord with Jump Pack up a second time, which was a pleasant surprise. I think that since I wasn’t under quite as much of a time constraint, I was able to actually stop and smell the roses a bit and actually enjoy the painting process at a bit more leisurely pace, which is a nice change from “oh God I have an event time to panic paint”. Plus, with a head swap and by changing the color scheme on the cloth and tossing in some extra red bits, the power weapon Chaos Lord looks nice and distinct from my Relic Claw Lord. Both these guys are centerpiece models of the team, you want them to shine and stand out, and fortunately I think both do.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

The second piece of hobby prep, which I’m currently in the middle of, is touching the team up prior to Worlds. As those of you who read my interview after winning Best Overall might already be aware, I painted my Murderwing initially under a pretty severe time crunch and ended up having to leave quite a bit on the table with the finished product. Now, it wasn’t enough to keep me from winning, but still, I know those rivets aren’t painted and I can’t be out here showing up to Barcelona without every single detail attended to, right? Fortunately, I really got pretty close to getting everything 110% done with this team, so if I don’t get every touch up done in time, that’s fine. I’ll still have a great looking squad with me at WCW. The fun of Worlds is to push yourself, though, and making sure I get every model on this team as sharp as possible is an exciting part of that process.

Picture from my game against Joshua Lyons at the Texas Teams Tournament.

Gaming Prep

Gaming prep is where I feel like I need the most work prior to Worlds. Like I said, I want to feel like a competitor, and while having a very pretty Kill Team is a mark of personal pride, the paintjob won’t help me win games. I need to get out there and practice.

And practice I have. By my estimation, I’ll have played at least 35 games of Kill Team between Dallas and Barcelona next week, with around 25 of those on Murderwing alone. Alongside attending some events here locally, I’ve traveled out to College Station twice for events, and even made a day trip to San Antonio this past weekend to get in practice out there too. I want to make sure I get enough games in with this team that I’m comfortable with their rules, know their strengths and weaknesses, and can make good operative selections and game choices when I play with them.

Credit: Warhammer Community

This practice period has had its ups and downs. A few times, I’ve walked away from games or events feeling like I’m unstoppable, like I’ve got this in the bag, and other times I’ve spent days kicking myself for how I did. I can’t be too hard on myself here, though, as it turns out the Texas scene is full of some world class players, which means that basically every event I go to here is full of sharks and tough as nails. As much as I don’t love getting my ass kicked, it’s probably the best thing that can happen to me in terms of getting actual practice where I can analyze my mistakes and improve.

Looking back at my games, I feel like I’ve done a pretty good job at seeing my strengths as a player, while also identifying my weaknesses. Firstly, for strengths, I’ve been very focused on making sure I play the Crit Op well, usually making it my Primary Op. I figure that if I can score well on Crit Op while strangling my opponent out of it, I can pretty comfortably win games as long as I don’t get blown out on Kill Op or Tac Op. This has helped me win quite a few very tight games, where my focus on winning the Crit Op has narrowly won me out against great opponents. It’s also shaped my playstyle, as I’ve noticed that my gameplan in most games is to play very reactively, using the strength of Murderwing’s mobility, offensive capabilities, and plenty of access to stun to lie in wait for my opponent to move up into my threat range before pouncing.

Brood Brothers Kill Team. Credit: NotThatHenryC

Where I’ve struggled is against hordes, particularly the horde teams with excellent shooting. Which, as it turns out, is several of them. My lie-in-wait playstyle just does not work nearly as well here, as if you let teams lie Inquisitorial Agents or Brood Brothers operate without putting pressure on them they end up basically having their way running the board, and build momentum to the point where they become too difficult to effectively stop in later turning points. I’ve spoken with a few friends about this, and their advice is to play more aggressively against these teams and worry less about taking early losses. Even if their meltagun drops a marine, maybe two, once Murderwing hits their lines they become extraordinarily dangerous, and the best way to keep myself safe in the late game is to make sure I kill the enemy operatives that can murk a Space Marine in the early game.

Focusing on getting some extra practice into horde teams to shore up that matchup is a big focus of my last minute pre-worlds games, just to make sure I can build a solid game plan I can lean on. With one last event and a handful more practice games left to squeeze in, I feel pretty solid. Especially if I can get past this horde team mental block, I think I’ve got what it takes to play some tight as nails games at Worlds and walk away from each table feeling like I did my best. Maybe even win a few of those games to boot. That would be nice.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Final Thoughts

I feel completely abuzz with Kill Team energy with the World Championships just around the corner. Win or lose, being able to go and play at the highest level of Kill Team is an incredible honor, especially since it’s on the global stage. I’m excited to spend the weekend with my Tabletop Battles and Texas Kill Team friends, as well as meet new people and make new friends from around the globe. You never know what life has in store, this could be my one and only chance to attend the WCW, and I intend to make the most of it.

That's all for now from me, I need to hunker down and get this last minute practice and prep before I fly out to Barcelona in just a few short days' time. If you’re going to Worlds yourself, stop by and say hi, I’d love to meet you! Otherwise, thank you for listening, and I can’t wait to share my experiences at the World Championship very soon!
