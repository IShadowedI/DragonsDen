Commander Focus: Pia, Aether Ascetic, Erosion, & Jyoti, Moag Ancient

September 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Erosion is one of perhaps the most fascinating natural phenomena that you can observe with the naked eye. Whether it’s the strange outcroppings and boulders suspended in America’s deserts, or the River Rhine tracing its form from the Alps to the North Sea, in ways both grandiose and minuscule the wear on our world is always visible. Erosion affects different materials differently, however, leading to strange columns of sandstone, or jutting diversion of a stream, and when the base layer of any given material grows weak enough the winds & waters will take the path of least resistance, pushing through once-solid stone, or settled sands. This is an article about erosion, not in the geological sense, but in Magic, and in Commander—this is about the simplification of tutor chains, and wear of enamel caps on the grinding teeth of this game. This is about Pia, Aether Ascetic. Pia’s one of the most exciting cards to arrive in Reality Fracture, and in a set rife with flashy Mythic Sphinges, colorshifted spins on competitive staples, and literal Tarmogoyf. Her effect is fairly simple: When she enters, you can pitch a card and search an Enchantment. This would be strong on a White or Black card, certainly, as tutors can smooth combo decks’ lines and allow toolbox strategies to find the pieces they need at the right time, but Pia is only as exciting as she is because...she’s Green. Not only is she the very first Enchantment tutor that has a Color identity of Monogreen (which isn’t an overcosted omnitutor), Pia also comes in at a cool 3 mana, being fairly inexpensive to weave into lines. So inexpensive, and so accessible, in fact, that she’s already begun to erode the limestone walls between known combos, and playability. The density of Green’s Creature Tutor suite is such that in any Gx deck without White/Black, you can just about find a given Enchantment as well or better than if you were in a color dedicated to finding the card type. There’s a critique which comes to mind, from FIRE Design-era Magic, that ‘Green can do anything, so long as it’s on a Creature’, and that feels eerily true here. With all that out of the way, let’s take the path of least resistance, and smooth our way towards Simic Enchantment combos, with a Commander newly-invigorated by Pia’s printing: Jyoti, Moag Ancient.

"Search your library for an enchantment card..."

Prior to the reveal of Pia, the best way to find Enchantments in something like Simic or Temur was by way of Intuition piles, where you’d choose the card you actually want, and two means of getting it back in hand. This could be your standard Regrowth, but as time’s gone on more flexible options like Bala Ged Recovery & Dryad’s Revival came along. There's a crop of issues that this previous path had though, least of all being the price tag (for some) and Game Changer status (for all) when it comes to Intuition. Things got a bit better once Gifts Ungiven was unbanned, but that's another Game Changer, whose opportunity cost was far from negligible to include. For this reason, Commanders whose win conditions were derived from Enchantments essentially needed to be White, Black, or potentially Red, if you intended to use a Reality Scramble build alongside things like Role tokens. If you were Simic, finding an Intruder Alarm or Food Chain was down to overpaying for something like Reweave as a worse-than Scramble enabler, or starting at B3 with Intuition. There's a host of Commanders this stymied, but the two I want to call out specifically are Berta, Wise Extrapolator as well as our focus today, of Jyoti.

She's Ghirapur's mayor, she's everyone's mayor.

Each of these, in spite of very minimal mechanical crossover, function as incredibly similar cards in my mind. Each goes close-to infinite with a single Enchantment, and fully infinite with access to some third piece that has ample redundancy. For Berta, Intruder Alarm and her constantly activating for X=0 leads to a repeatable action of creating a 0/0 Fractal, it dying immediately, and her untapping; this doesn't lead anywhere on its own, but alongside any number of cards which care about tokens entering, Creatures dying, etc it presents a pretty quick win. Jyoti's a bit more resilient, because she functionally produces mana and scales with being removed, but is definitely slower to win. The Elemental loves to see a Food Chain, which can (alongside the Dryad Arbor tokens produced per cast) gets close to infinite casts of your Commander, if you can somehow reduce the price tag of Commander Tax, create additional tokens, or provide your board Haste (such that the Arbor Elves can tap before being fed into the woodchipper). In either case, looking at decklists for them on Moxfield & Archidekt currently include these powerful cards, but no compelling ways to find them. In that sense, each falls victim to one of the least-fun things a combo deck can be, for all parties involved: Inconsistent. If you open your combo piece, congrats, you're threatening a win way ahead of schedule, and will be evaluated by the table as such in future games with the deck. If you don't, however, you're playing far more of a midrange game, but being focused down as though you were a combo deck. Adding consistency to either of these strategies not only increases the power floor for the deck in a given game, but also dodges these social woes which might otherwise mar one's experience with the Commander. This article's on Jyoti, but many of the lessons shown off can be equally applied to Berta.

Jyo-ti the World

So, Jyoti—what's the actual gameplan? Step one is fairly simple: Find Pia, Aether Ascetic, and use her to find one of two-ish Enchantments, being Rhystic Study or Food Chain. Step two is to get out Jyoti, and use her as a bit of ramp, maybe double up on a Landfall trigger in a given turn, before then finding a way to get her off of the field. This can be via Food Chain, if we feel the combo is close-enough at hand as to make Rhystic Study not high enough value, or something like an Altar, being Ashnod's Altar & Phyrexian Altar. These have the added bonus of being mechanisms to get Pia off the field, at which point we can do things like get her back from the graveyard to replay, or even jam a Body Double. Where things get dicey is the next part, because the path the deck takes when you're actually trying to combo off varies wildly based on available tools. If you're making extra Creatures to toss away from Food Chain when cycling through Jyoti ETBs, such as with Springheart Nantuko, then you only need a single Enchantment tutor really to get going—though it is immensely funny Nantuko is able to be found via Pia and our Creature tutors. What an accessible little gentleman. There's also the option of creating the opportunity for your Arbor Elves to tap for mana, such as via Lightning Greaves, Shang-Chi, Master of Kung Fu, or Concordant Crossroads. There is a fourth option there, in Thousand-Year Elixir, but with it being a bit expensive at 3 mana and not tutorable with what we'd want to play, it's been cut. What remains is either extremely accessible (Shang-Chi's a Creature, Crossroads is an Enchantment) or extremely good. Finally, Commander Tax. This is normally {2} on top of the existing cost, meaning that with Food Chain, Jyoti at a baseline produces 1 fewer mana than she rises in price, with the extra Dryad Arbor token made per cast. Therefore, you begin to break even once you do things like allow them to tap for mana, or make additional tokens, but another mechanism now enabled by Pia is in reducing the Tax to {1}...or less.

They call it the Simic Combine because you combine things and-

This is by way of Myth Unbound, my favorite card you're probably not playing in enough decks. This sees play in nearly all Partner brews including Green, but for Jyoti, what it does is much the same as our other enablers, with the added bonus of drawing a card every cycle. It also makes use of another subtheme in the deck: Enchantment clones. We're on essentially all options available to Simic to copy Enchantments, from the obvious like Copy Enchantment itself, and Estrid's Invocation, to more peculiar paths like Moritte of the Frost & Flash Photography. This is in service of two things: Generating a truly insurmountable amount of value ala cEDH by copying a Rhystic Study, or doubling up on a combo enabler, such as Myth Unbound. This can even be things like Springheart Nantuko, or Leyline of Abundance, allowing all these pieces to serve as 'wild cards' in terms of our endgame. Better yet, you can usually also target your opponent's Enchantments, meaning we're far less liable to groan at a Rhystic without one of our own.

Mamma Pia!

A Dancing Queen, often seen, should have been printed in the 20-teens.

The die is cast, and our gameplan is fully explained, but how consistent is it? Well...Green has perhaps the best suite of tutors in the game, following closely behind Black, although in their majority the best options aren't Game Changers. Moreover, unlike Black, a lot of these tools toss the searched-for Creature onto the battlefield directly, leaving less room for countermagic. Chord of Calling, Green Sun's Zenith, & Nature's Rhythm are the big ones, with X=3 being the common pick. To that end, usually following the Jyoti turn where we've ramped twice, these are our next play. They can of course find Pia, but also cards like Lotus Cobra or Badgermole Cub as additional acceleration, or Savvy Trader to not only help Jyoti cycle faster but also reset a dead Pia/Mystic Remora. There's also tutors to hand, like Sylvan & Worldly Tutor, and Time of Need, which keys off of the fact that Pia is indeed Legendary. Also played are Neoform & Eldritch Evolution, one-time Birthing Pod effects which get us from things like our mana dorks into Pia, Pia into Savvy Trader, or Jyoti into Moritte.

This deck has a whopping ~16 paths to Pia, and Pia herself then opens up the rest of the cards we want to see. That's a cool ~80% to see Pia or a means to her in our first opening 8 cards, and counting the mulligan, 96%. That's a rate higher than even many 'Secret Commander' lists, whose entire purpose is to hinge the deck around a card not in the Command Zone. Turns out that Green being able to do or find anything so long as it passes through the elephant-sized needle's eye that is 'Creatures' makes for obscenely consistent tutor chains. The actual win comes from casting Jyoti (or Eternal Scourge) enough times to amass a big enough board, and then flex Concordant Crossroads to provide haste and swing out for lethal...made easier by something like the new Roiling Canopy, which results in a massive Jyoti to pump up our board. Simple, clean, compact, and able to be reset via Endurance if things get stuck in the yard..

Example Decklist: Jyoti, Chandra's Step-Sister

There's something that I want to touch on here, which is that the ramp package here isn't what you'd expect for a deck that has a 4 mana Commander, much less one which actually ramps you. Why wouldn't we be playing all the 2-mana ramp spells that allow us to do the 2-4-6 ramp chain that's become so common? For starters, Jyoti isn't necessarily a card we aim to play especially early, as we don't appreciate paying 6 for her after a board wipe; the Dryad Arbor tokens have a tendency to get caught in the crossfire of things, so we'd much rather run out value engines like Faerie Mastermind or similar, then plan to play Jyoti after our tutor chain is live. Therefore, my preference is in playing 1-mana dorks, like Delighted Halfling, as one of the luxuries Green is afforded compared to other colors. We're also on a few ways to pick up our Commander, should our combo be interrupted after casting her 20+ times, being Road of Return & Command Beacon. A lot of the tools used by Prossh, Skyraider of Kher (the OG Food Chain Commander) come home to roost here, but Jyoti's cheaper, more resilient, and has access to Rhystic.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

In terms of Bracket, part of why this Commander Focus is on Jyoti and not Berta is the fact that I'm not comfortable denying the fact Berta's a bit fast for Bracket 3. Prior to the printing of Pia, the combo was inconsistent enough as to not require we forcibly scale her up to B4...but now, being able to play 15-20 ways of chaining to Intruder Alarm means Jyoti is far more apt for the Bracket. Even so, the fact you'll have games where you copy Rhystic Study is cooler than what Berta'd be doing anyway, and I'm a sucker for Myth Unbound as a core piece of one's list. This can be pushed to Bracket 4, but to be quite honest a lot of B2 lists running Command Zone combos with Pia shouldn't even look at Bracket 2. Now there's no longer the shield of inconsistency to protect less powerful lists, and for that reason I'm less keen on powering down Jyoti, unless your intent is to kill via combat by boosting her with a Tifa Lockheart or Mightform Harmonizer.

Not Yugioh (Yet)

Alex Stone lends elegance to the MH3 portrait arts, even without a head to focus on.

Reality Fracture is a set which takes big swings, and Pia is certainly part of that. We've seen some absolutely heinous designs deployed in the set, at a greater density than any since probably Final Fantasy, and that's still a maybe. Entomb but for Lands, High Tide but for Mountains, and of course, Green's first Enchantment tutor. This is a powerful set, and one I'll likely spend time diving into far beyond its release, especially with Star Trek thus far not inspiring confidence. In spite of all this power, I don't think Pia will be "problematic", even with my erosion metaphor, for the majority of the playerbase; where she's most obscene is in lists built by the Johnny-Spikes of the world, with combos planned two or even three steps ahead in the chain. For the majority of players, it'll be 'Oh sweet, I can tutor Rhystic Study!', and while that is certainly a problem it's not one of Pia, but of her targets. If anything, this might be part of what gets Food Chain back onto the Game Changers list, as it was recently relieved of its position. The thought there, per the committee, was that 'If you're playing Food Chain, you should be able to self-regulate', and I feel that downplays both the rising competence of the wider Commander community, and their self-control. For as much as I'm an advocate for deeply weird, cool combo decks, I'm also here to preach that cards like Food Chain, Yuriko, Urza, and even Ashnod's Altar should be Game Changers. Greater regulation by the letter of the law, and not the intent or vibe, is how we keep cards like Pia and sets like Reality Fracture from feeling like powercreep as opposed to added fun. Yugioh's long-since eroded the columns holding apart any two cards, and while our Aether Ascetic is a step in that direction, there's still plenty of sandbar left to chew through until all combos are intertwined.

Until next time, I can still hear you saying you would never break the chain.
