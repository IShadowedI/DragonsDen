Tabletop Battles Reviews: Custodes Support Battle Group

August 29th, 2026 via Goonhammer

More plastic reinforcements for the Custodes arrive for for preorder this weekend in the form of the Custodes Support Battle Group. This box contains three previously resin kits for The Ten Thousand, this release double dips into both Horus Heresy and 40K despite being branded as the former.

Our thanks to Games Workshop for sending us out a preview copy to review.

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought. Credit - Soggy

Soggy: I’ve said it before, and I’ll say it again. I have a type, and Games Workshop has my number. I like them thicc, and the Telemon Dreadnought was the undisputed thiccest unit out there until the Saturnine Dreadnought dropped.

Keewa: This guy (or gal) is chunky, my goodness. I can’t imagine what they were like in their previous lump o’ resin incarnation, but I’m glad they're in plastic now. The design is reasonably cool, I don’t really like the “shoulder pads” all that much, and the fringed cowboy jacket look of the dangling leather straps is very silly - if you, like me, find them to be a bit too daft or prefer the slightly more austere (in as much as Custodes model can ever look austere) style of the old model, it does seem like you can leave them off and their absence not be particularly obvious or jarring.

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought Sprue

Soggy: Spread across two full-sized sprues, I found it to be a straightforward build with no big gotchas or complications. For those who have built the Saturnine kit, it was a similar experience to it - albeit with slightly smaller pauldrons. While the legs come in two default poses with options for standing still or being mid-stride, there is a decent amount of flexibility available with several grooves on the knee joints to allow for different options. 

Likewise, the arms have good amounts of flexibility and are trivial to magnetise to enable you to swap out the different weapons the kit comes with: 

  • 2x Caestus fists with twin neutronium cascade projectors
  • Iliastus Accelerator
  • Arachnus Storm Cannon
  • Adrathic Desolator
wadding magnets with a wee bit of putty

I used 5x1mm magnets throughout, which were a perfect fit for the carapace weapon (Adeptus Titanicus, my beloved), they did the job elsewhere with a bit of glue or putty to hold them in place. The only thing I will note is that there aren’t quite enough couter pieces if you were to glue these on every weapon’s elbow joint - probably fine, as you will likely have a favourite pairing anyway. I forgot to even glue mine on in the end.

Keewa: I have.. Something of a reputation as someone who doesn’t enjoy building models very much. Is it accurate? I wouldn’t say so, but in the case of the Telemon, I actually found the whole process to be quite chill. I’m in the middle of moving apartments, so I only had time to pick one kit to assemble, and chose the Telemon based on the calculus that at least it would be funny for the reader to watch me suffer. The Telemon is set, quite cannily, into two large frames, joined together into one monster-frame - I usually snap these in half to make it a little less unwieldy. Building the trunk of the model, starting with the legs and progressing upwards, is quite straightforward - with nothing in particular to challenge the fat-fingered. Putting together the arm and elbow bits was a bit of a pain, but nothing compared to the struggle of putting together this fellow’s littler cousin.

I didn’t really understand what the difference is between the various guns; they have names like Caecilius Hortonic Devastator, so I just picked one somewhat at random to provide a counterpart to the fist. The instructions booklet doesn’t really give you any indication as to what is and isn’t a legal build; each weapon is interchangeable, and with a little finesse you can magnetise them, so presumably you could take any combination of guns and/or fists if you wanted. Although you have two fists, each of the guns is only on the sprue once, so you can’t double-up on a particular kind of gun. The model has a fixed pose (make sure you pay attention and put the legs on correctly; it’s entirely possible to end up with a model doing an awkward falling-forwards pose; ask me how I know), but if built correctly, it does appear that the top-mounted missile launcher can wiggle up and down; I don’t know why you would want to do this. I just glued it all together, in clear contravention of the stipulations of the instructions.

Gyrfalcon Jetbikes / Agamatus Custodians

NotThatHenryC: What's the fastest way to get yourself a coveted spot inside one of the awesome new Telemon Dreadnoghts? Well, why not fire yourself at an army of Daemons, sat on a cruise missile made of solid gold and marble, holding a sharp stick? 

The first thing you notice with these bikes is that each one is on a full-size sprue, with a whole lot of pieces on. You get four identical sprues in the box, meaning all your Agamatus are going to be pretty similar. 

Gyrfalcon Jetbike sprue

We do get a few options. The lance can be mounted on the jetbike’s hull or held in one of three positions. There are also four different guns, which is a surprise as we only have rules for three of them. Presumably a pdf will be along shortly to give us rules for the new Arachnus Volley Cannon. There aren’t bare heads in this kit and the helmet has a hose attaching it to the chest, meaning you can’t even turn it much. You do get three different plumes to stick on top though.

I found this an ok-ish build. No major mould lines, which was a relief, with so many pieces to clean up. It all fits perfectly together, though not all that easily, with limited locator pins for some reason. I had hoped the armour panels on the front could be painted separately but they’re integral to the structure so that wouldn’t have worked.

Internal attachment points are small on Gyrfalcon Jetbikes / Agamatus Custodians. Credit: NotThatHenryC

The guns are not designed to be interchangeable and are difficult to get in and out once the bike is built. You could trim the sides a bit, where they're designed to slot into the hull, to make this easier. It would be simple enough to magnetise them after that if you wanted. This is unfortunate as the guns are detailed and half submerged in the hull, making them difficult to paint in situe.

I also wanted to paint the rider separately as they are very detailed. Don’t glue the handlebar piece on if doing this, or you won’t be able to get the rider in and out. As is is I can just about remove the rider from the bike's left side for painting. There might be a better way that I haven't found, perhaps sticking the rider's legs to the bike and painting the upper body separately.

Thankfully when it does come time to paint, most of the jetbike is the jet, so I sprayed these Colour Forge Steelforge Silver, which saved a lot of time. I wanted a red marble on the flat areas so I first painted in the red, with a blended highlight. I then masked off the non-marble parts and attacked the bikes with Montana marble spray: black and then white.  

The messiest painting process ever: Montana marble spray. Credit: NotThatHenryC

I wasn't feeling too confident with this approach at the point of taking the above photo. Howeve, a layer of Blood Angel contrast paint over the marble blended the black and white a bit with the existing red, making the overall effect look less like silly string. I think the effect more or less works. One of the nice big transfers from the Custodes sheet will help a lot.

I'm sorry to say I haven't finished my bikes yet. Life and reviewing the new Journal got in the way, and trying to do all four as a batch was a mistake. This is how far I've got so far,

Loading...

I thought people might be interested to see a comparison with a 40k Dawneagle Jetbike I painted a little while back. The Gyrfalcon is quite a different design - longer obviously but also much less ornate. The middle part of a Dawneagle is a lot like a motorbike, including some exhausts on the sides for some reason, while the Gyrfalcon feels much more like a massive engine with a guy sat on it. As such, Gyrfalcons can probably be painted faster than Dawneagles if you don't go crazy and try to cover them in marble like I did.

Comparison of Gyrfalcon and Dawneagle Jetbikes. Credit: NotThatHenryC

Pallas Grav-attack

Pallas Grav-attack. Credit - Soggy

Soggy: God, these things still give me nightmares from Horus Heresy 2nd edition. 

If you’ve built any of the other plastic Custodes vehicles, being the smallest of them all is a similar experience to the others, however also somehow the one that annoyed me the most. The last  10% of the model took what felt like half of the time, my fat fingers weren’t the best at holding the cables on the engines in place.

Pallas Grav Attack sprue

As with the other Custodes vehicles, be very careful when gluing the nacelles to ensure a flush seam between the halves. This join is front and centre, so any mistakes are on display forever, plastic glue here is a must. 

It's been a decade or two since I've seen flash this bad on plastic kits

Weirdly, my kit had some horrible flashing on both the engine parts, which weren't present on the other demo sprues, so I’m not sure how much to make noise about this, it was easy enough to remove but quite rare to see on a modern  kit.

Weapons-wise, while you could probably use magnets here to alternate between the options, there weren't any cavities in place to use and I’ve opted for blu-tack in the meantime which does the trick!

Custodes Support Battle Group. Credit - Warhammer Community

As Good as Gold

It's nice to see Games Workshop follow up earlier plastic Custodes wave with another. It significantly lowers the barrier to entry on these armies for anyone wanting to pick them up. If the last battlegroup box is anything to go by, the hard part will be trying to get your hands on them - although inevitably they will see individual releases later down the line.
