[40k] 11th Edition Faction Pack Review: Adepta Sororitas

June 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With a new edition of Warhammer 40K comes a whole new bunch of Detachments, and we’re handling them in groups to make it easier to separate the wheat from the chaff, at least for now. We’ll go through the relevant changes to the Datasheets and rules at play, then see how the Detachments themselves stand up.

Thank you again to Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of these rules.

Sisters have had a very solid run through 10th - they had a fantastic Index and a great Codex that suffered slightly from excessive early points nerfs. However, these were eased back and they recovered to a great spot, and continued putting up good results with Hallowed Martyrs and a few other builds for the rest of the Edition. Will they thrive in 11th? Let's find out.

Datasheet Changes

  • Daemonifuge: Mysterious Saviours wording is updated to reflect the 11th edition terminology. Heroic intervention cost is -1CP rather than 0, and does not prevent other uses of the stratagem in the same phase.
  • Dominion Squad: Reactive move trigger distance is reduced to 8”, in line with similar reactive abilities elsewhere
  • Dialogus: Loses Leader. Gains Support.
  • Dogmata: Loses Leader. Gains Support.
  • Hopitaller: Loses Leader. Gains Support.
  • Imagifier: Loses Leader. Gains Support.

Rule Changes

  • Devout Fanaticism Stratagem changed to use the surge move core rule.

Available Detachments

In addition to three new Detachments, Sisters retain access to all of their prior detachments, including Champions of Faith, at these prices and dispositions.

3 DP Detachments
  • Hallowed Martyrs (Priority Assets)
  • Bringers of Flame (Purge the Foe)
2 DP Detachments
  • Army of Faith (Take and Hold)
  • Penitent Host (Take and Hold)
  • Champions of Faith (Disruption)
1 DP Detachments
  • Chorus of Condemnation (Reconnaissance)
  • Sacred Champions (Take and Hold)
  • Sanctified Orators (Purge the Foe)

The limited roster of Sisters Detachments necessitates specific builds to get access to each disposition, but each seems well-suited to what the Detachment already wants to be doing. The broad utility of Hallowed Martyrs lends itself to the single-action focus of Priority Assets, while Bringers of Flame is well suited to go forward and kill with a quickness, and presumably doesn’t need (or would overly benefit from) the assistance on offer from the new 1DP Detachments.

Chorus of Condemnation

Credit: Evan "Felime" Siefring

Chorus of Condemnation is all about using your Flying units to support shooting pieces, and using Exorcists to boost the output of your Flying units. It’s a cool concept that echoes some of the early Indirect-focused Bringers of Flame builds from 10th edition, but with a tighter focus on utility rather than output. Is that enough to make it worthwhile? Let's dig in.

Detachment Rule

Each Infantry Fly unit in your army can select one visible enemy unit within 12” and increase its detection range by 3” in your shooting phase (via a Condemned status, so it doesn't stack).

This seems merely okay. In most cases the practical impact will be boosting detection range for a unit that’s gone to ground from 12” to 15”, which isn’t nothing, particularly since wide-ranging access to Assault weapons is in the 3DP Bringers of Flame, but whether this is valuable enough to invest in multiple Fly units remains to be seen. Worth noting that Junith has Fly, so any unit she joins will gain the Detachment benefits (as would a Jump Pack Canoness attached to a unit of Repentia using the enhancement in Penitent Host, if you’re a certain kind of lunatic).

Enhancements

  • Clarion of Urgency: Canoness with Jump Pack only. Allows her unit to return to strategic reserves at the end of your opponent’s turn if unengaged. Always handy to have. This lets you get Army of Faith’s best mobility tool in other Detachments, or pick up two Jump Pack units in Army of Faith by using the stratagem elsewhere.
  • Symphonic Payload (Upgrade): This goes on Exorcists, and lets them reroll the number of shots they get with their weapon. This helps to solve one of the biggest issues with Exorcists, which is that they’re highly susceptible to variance on the shot count. Is this enough to make Exorcists viable? Maybe. The stratagem set is certainly making a push in that direction.

Stratagems

  • Inspirational Battle Canticles (1CP): At the start of the command phase (not just yours), pick a unit within 6” of an Infantry Fly unit to become un-Battle-shocked. Not exciting, but at base Ld7 Sisters units are likely to be Battle-shocked quite a bit in 11th edition, so this will get used.
  • Harmonised Exorcism (1CP): When an Exorcist is selected to shoot, pick one target visible to and within 9” of a friendly Infantry Fly unit, and receive +1 to hit against that target. This does nothing to help indirect fire, so this assumes you’ve driven your Exorcist out to fire at a target directly and want to invest into its output. Given the game-bending potential of CP, this is thoroughly underwhelming - it’s only slightly better than just CP-rerolling a hit roll in most cases.
  • Devastating Reprise (1CP): After shooting with an Exorcist, select a friendly Infantry Fly to gain Devastating Wounds against one of the targets hit by the Exorcist (excluding Monster/Vehicle units). At last, an interesting stratagem. It’s a bit awkward to set up, but the payout is quite good. The obvious use-case is a unit of Seraphim with hand flamers, but joining Junith to a unit of Retributors would also be interesting.

Is It Good?

This Detachment does have a clear identity going for it, with jump pack units and Exorcists working in harmony to boost morale and blast your foes. The idea is interesting. As a faction, Sisters are generally too elite to be tossing Fly units forward for buffs, so a concentrated list will build around repositioning Seraphim to a weak flank, supporting them with indirect fire and the Devastating Reprise stratagem, and then repositioning to do it again elsewhere.

As a package, the game-plan doesn’t appear to have a clear partner among the other available Detachments. Additional detection range may be useful for a slow-moving firebase in Army of Faith, but it’s not clear why that would be superior to simply taking Bringers of Flame, which would have more speed and more output.

Additionally, concentrating output into expensive units like Exorcists and a brick of Seraphim seems to be against-plan if you’re making use of the Reconnaissance disposition, which is loaded with action-based primary missions. That said, if you want Reconnaissance this is the only way to get it for the faction.

Chorus of Condemnation is an interesting toolbox, but given how straightforward and powerful the other two 1DP options are, we don’t expect this to see much play.

Sacred Champions

Credit: Greg Narro

This one is all about Celestian Sacresants. Similar to Champions of Faith, the rules and stratagems are all focused on that one datasheet, and as you’d expect it has the REVEREND keyword, which prevents you from stacking both Detachments together.

Detachment Rule

Rather than the pick-three mechanic from Champions of Faith, this Detachment simply gives all of your Celestian Sacresant units +1BS and +1WS. If you’re all-in on Sacresants, this is a great choice as it gives them the best part of Champions of Faith while still offering the benefit from your other Detachment pick. As mentioned above, it can’t be combined with CoF to create “super-Sacresants”, but as you’ll see there’s more than enough to make this attractive.

Enhancements

  • Writ of Compunction (Upgrade): Celestian Sacresants unit only, +1OC. The other part of the Champions of Faith Detachment rule, now with a points cost.
  • Perfervid Haste: Any Adepta Sororitas model. Her unit gets +1”M. It’s good but not game-changing. Potentially handy on any number of units, not just Sacresants.

Stratagems

  • Sanctified Blows (1CP): In the fight phase when you select a Celestian Sacresant unit to fight, you can give that unit +1A and +1S. The warcom preview buried the lede here, because this stratagem is bananas. The new 11e rules mean that you can’t stack stratagems on one unit, but you won’t need to because this is more of an output buff than any other Detachment can offer.
  • Faithful Fortitude (1CP): Any phase, when a Celestian Sacresant unit suffers a mortal wound, gain a 5+ Feel No Pain against mortal wounds until the end of the phase. Nice to have when it matters, though obviously doesn't matter if you already have a Hospitaller.
  • Unflinching Determination (1CP): Used when a Celestian Sacresant unit is selected to fall back or advance. That unit gains assault and can still shoot and charge. The power of miracle dice to boost the value of Advance + Charge meant that the capability was safely locked to Penitent Units in the Penitent Host Detachment for all of 10th edition, where it had limited impact. Keeping it fixed to specific datasheets is probably for the best, but Sacresants are where it was going to be used anyway. This is an incredible boost.

Is It Good?

This is an excellent Detachment. The newfound ability to double-attach characters to Sacresants along with this Detachment opens up some crazy options. The power on offer forces a genuine question about why you would ever run anything else. The likely answer is that this Detachment is Take and Hold, and the keyword overlap means it can only be paired with Army of Faith or Penitent Host, which are both also Take and Hold. Sacresants are the most durable Sisters unit when appropriately invested into, but they’re not that durable and the supporting resources are expensive, so it may turn out that leaning on them hard doesn’t leave room for a solid Take and Hold gameplan. Then again, maybe you’ll just score when they’re dead.

Unless you find yourself really excited about running Disruption, it does seem safe to say that Champions of Faith is a hard sell with Sacred Champions in play. The combination of always-on WS/BS boost, a better output stratagem, and the power of advance + fallback + charge is almost enough on its own, and the balance of Army of Faith or even Penitent Host is almost certainly better than the rest of what Champions has to offer (although Champions does combine nicely with the Hagiomnifex). Perhaps the power of 6” pile-in/consolidate will tempt technical players, or perhaps Disruption turns out to be a strong counter-meta disposition choice, but as it stands the power of advance + charge Sacresants that hit almost twice as hard as they do anywhere else will be hard to pass up if you’re bringing Sacresants at all.

Presumably the existence of this Detachment will also warp the pricing of the Sacresant datasheet and supporting cast as well, so it may turn out that this is an albatross around the neck of the faction, but we hope not.

Sanctified Orators

Canoness Preceptor Theshea Collumo (Credit: Thien Nguyendo)

This is an interesting one, because the rules text is negligible and the rules impact is significant. One middling rule, and one excellent unit upgrade. No stratagems. None. Nada. Zip. Zero. It’s ok, you’ll still run this.

Detachment Rule

This Detachment has a single enhancement, and taking it doesn’t count towards your total enhancement limit:

  • Friendly Adepta Sororitas Character units get +1 Ld.

It’s generally unwise to load up too heavily on enhancements anyway, so you’re probably not worried about the first part, but Sisters are predominantly a Ld7 army, even on leader characters like the Canoness and Palatine, so this will come up often. Additionally, pushing Morvenn Vahl to Ld5 better represents her station as a High Lord of Terra, in line with other notably indefatigable luminaries like…the Space Marine dude with the hourglass. Sisters have several abilities that work on leadership checks, so this is stronger than it first appears.

Enhancements

  • Hagiomnifex (Upgrade): An upgrade for non-Penitent Character units, which allows you once per turn to activate one of five different abilities at the start of a phase:
    • Increase the detection range of enemy units by 6” while this unit is shooting
    • Automatically pass battle-shock tests
    • Gain +1” movement speed
    • Gain +1S to their attacks
    • Enemy attacks with strength higher than this unit’s toughness get -1 to wound rolls.

This is an incredibly powerful upgrade, and if you take this Detachment you’re almost certainly taking it three times (since you’re not getting anything else). One of those uses is going on Morvenn Vahl and her Paragon Warsuits (pending settling the outstanding question if Attached Epic Hero units can have Upgrades), and the other two will vary based on what Detachment you’re running. +1S opens up several interesting breakpoints, -1 to wound will come up often since Sisters are all T3, and the increased detection range is surprisingly useful, particularly since merely having access to it can prevent cheeky rapid ingresses into spots that depend on hidden to protect the unit.

Is It Good?

Yes, for two reasons. The enhancement itself is quite good, and helps get more from Vahl’s unit, which looks to still be a faction centerpiece in 11th. The combination of universally applicable defensive ability and choose-the-most-relevant offensive ability means that any unit with a character is potentially a good target. And since Sisters characters have historically been priced at a premium relative to other factions, protecting your investment will probably remain important in 11th edition.

Perhaps more importantly (but much less interesting), Sanctified Orators gives Sisters access to Purge the Foe. If you want to hit the table with a prayer on your lips and murder in your heart, this is the best way to avoid distractions like “mission play” or “game plans”. Early indications suggest “just go kill them” is the best competitive advice, so if you want Purge the Foe and you’re not running Bringers of Flame, this will complement an existing Army of Faith or Champions of Faith build, both of which will usually include Vahl and two units with attached characters.

If Sacresants turn out to be the unit-du-jour of 11e launch, the Hagiomnifex is perhaps the best argument in favor of Champions of Faith, granting access to Purge and closing the power gap with Sacred Champions. Penitent Host also benefits, if for no other reason than to give the enhancement to Vahl (Penitent units can’t take it), and Purge fits well with the Penitent Host gameplan.

Final Thoughts

Competitively this is a strong set of rules, with one Detachment bringing a new playstyle, one that’s universally helpful to an existing gameplan, and one that supercharges an already-strong datasheet. It’s unlikely that anyone will take all three 1DP Detachments simultaneously, but each of the new ones has something to offer their potential pairings. Meanwhile, the universal utility of Hallowed Martyrs and the straightforward “fast gun good” power of Bringers of Flame keep them in contention even at 3DP pricing. There’s a lot of new tools on offer and new archetypes to explore, which is a great place to be as the edition kicks off.
