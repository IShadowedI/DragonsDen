[Warmachine] Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms: August 2026

September 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our monthly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each month our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the month’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

Welcome back, Warmachine fans, to Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms! Leading into NOVA, the last major event in the US that informs the global WTC meta we see that games played and winrates don't always tell the same story. Fane and other over performing newer armies soared in popularity without dominating top finishes. New hotness will always weigh higher as more people seek to try out but nothing beats good ol' grit and faction mastery. How did these savants break the mold? Well read on and find out!

As we start, a quick review of the filters we use for the data pulled here. For this series, we are only looking at ranked tournament games and are excluding online only events.

August Stats

In August we saw 3004 games played at 66 events. The Danish Masters, Warmanchester, and Lone Star Championship were the highlights of the month and had a variety of surprises bucking our usual meta trends.

Note- All of the Legacy factions currently unavailable as a part of Warmachine 3D are grouped together in the “Legacy Armies” section, due to their lower number of games played per faction and lack of widespread availability.

Games Played

Similar to our previous article, no faction breaks the 10% danger threshold of over-representation but more factions are getting close to that line. The factions of Old Umbrey, Grymkin, Kithguard, Fane of Nero, and Shadowflame Shard are all sitting at 8% or more, and not so coincidentally are also all Fury fueled factions. Due to a quirk of releases many of the new or newly updated factions have been fury based and the older of these factions represented have that extra little output that just feel better into the current meta. Wide scenarios split tools requiring more individual models to out-perform where necessary. The non-Grymkin 3D printable factions are the only entries that feel below the curve play rate wise however so its not all doom and gloom. Just indicators worth watching out for as we start getting to WTC and the end of the year.

Faction Win Rates

The Iron Kingdoms continue to be exsanguinated by the new (ancient) kids on the block but in far less concerning numbers than last month. While the Fane of Nero playstyle is fairly alien compared to even Infernals or Grymkin, people are slowly adjusting to deal with their fangtastic set of rules. Grymkin is the other offender over the ideal 55% winrate max. This shows that despite the House and Old Witch combination of nerfs; the faction still had a deep well to draw on to leverage their 2.5 effective feats per game via their Arcana cards. Kithguard and Infernals both plummeted from their positive status since last we checked and similarly Storm Legion has lost some of that new update edge. Despite that, all 3 armies are still well within healthy variance. Brineblood on the other hand dropped like a stone this month and is the biggest surprise to move downward compared to their dominance earlier in the year. The nerf to their premier gun caster in Firequill and the raising of other ships has not helped these salty swashbuckler's tide.

Necrofactorium has been down this year but not out. The actual damage output of the army has stayed devastating but the loss of the Spell Rack severely limited their access to easy MAT fixing (not the only fixing just the simplest with Carnage) and delivery methods leaving them weak to the influx of gunlines. After the mid year update that bumped up Mechanithralls and knocked down the primary gun casters, Necrofactorium gained their new Swarm Warden UA and the Helslinger Phantom leading Egernet to lean hard into the fundamental Cryx idea: Its okay to get hit so long as you can hit harder back. Khythos was the primary list leveraging the power of his defensive feat and the recursion of Revive and the double Necroharvester to constantly top up the thralls and control scenario. Two Arc Nodes extend the threat of powerful tools like Dead Weight and Scything Touch to lock down and eliminate pin point targets respectively and immediately force his opponents into difficult to navigate positions. Sepsira came out in the finals to shut down the heavy ARM of winter korps, positioned with the devastating combo of the Hellraker and Hellslinger Phantom to heavily limit the output of enemy cohorts and subsequently the armies that lean on them freeing up Sepsira to pressure units and solos.

Winter Korps at the start of the year were positioned to be the most menacing of the focus factions. Buffs to units, cohorts, solos, and casters all smoothing out play lines and pumping up potential raw damage, but finishes like this are rare. The lack of dominance has been the direct result of the wide scenarios pulling apart the castle style Winter Korps prefers. Baranova having CTRL 16 helps to ease that burden quite a bit. Soloing the list throughout the event, Olov primarily won by dominating the board early, most likely with an early and incredibly aggressive use of Baranova’s Feat. Shutting down your opponent’s speed or ability to really engage in combat while you shoot them from safety with impunity is an incredibly solid strategy that only lost to Necrofactorium’s ability to mitigate a large portion of that threat without having to rely on charging or speed thanks to the placing effect of the hell raker and the control provided by the Hellslinger that isn't a spell letting it apply debuffs through banishing ward. If the Savryn list ever came out it was probably to attempt to mulch more aggressive anti-gun armies thanks to the unmitigated damage the list could output at close range thanks to both the melee and ranged output of the one two punch Invictus + Behemoth and Kill Order bringing the warrior models to similar damage heights.

Shadowflame Shard has been a top army all year and its been pretty obviously off the back of Lylyth and their whole package. Easily able to dance in and out of danger with a crippling spray attack with a fish-able Crit Stagger effect on top of a speed control Feat, like Baranova, which is backed by similarly scary cohorts sporting additional powerful sprays and melee threat. Thanks to the personal output of Lylyth, the raw damage potential granted by their spells in Silence of Death and Deadeye, and the movement tricks in both Run and Gun and Slipstream to be in relative safety, even after a mid year nerf this is the premier way to play Khymera. None of Sebastian’s games were particularly fast until his loss showcasing the precise and deliberate nature of the list. Same as Winter Korps though the second list never saw play, but presumably it leans on Vallyx's raw melee threat, the aggressive output of both the Trikhymerix and Vypex backed by Belighul, and assistance from the units to bully hard in the middle. The only loss was to the Khymera mirror where presumably his opponent knew what to play around and how to position to kill Lylyth.

Brad Park is back at the podium title fight again to show us that a small faction is by no means a simple one and that dedication backed by a deep well of tools can carve success into a wide meta. Breaking the mold a bit, much of his success throughout the year has been on the back of the incredibly technical and back breaking Thexus list but for Lone Star he exclusively relied on his Khythos list instead. Built on a solid foundation defensive units and offensive tools like LOS ignoring spray attacks and damage improvement with scything touch on top of the rest of his control laden kit. This met with another top podium staple Alex Kosma. Equipped with the Grymkin mainstay of Old Witch and the freshly nerfed but no less efficient Slaughterhouse; Alex instead chose to bring his off list The Heretic to this contest of giants. The damage tools of that list is more dispersed across the army with more Dread Rots, multiple hard to execute heavies, and many more guns to whittle down the oncoming horde of drudges. Who came out on top? Which 3D Printed faction proved their older but sharpened tools were superior?

Result: Alex Kosma Victory - Scenario Win - 18 to 13

Credit: Steamforged Games

First Place: (Alex Kosma - Grymkin)

Archetype: Witch’s Cottage, Heretic Aggro

Lists: See Showdown

We managed to interview Alex himself on his thoughts going into the face off:

"I will say my Dark Operation matchup knowledge was weak going into that one, so I was going on the intuition that it’d be Khythos just on Thexus having some difficulty landing his high-impact spells. The other is Grymkin generally can struggle on scenario if you clear all their chaff. Not hard for Khythos at all, and it’s particularly punishing for Witch, as there’s very few bodies before she can’t really cover the board anymore.

"Scenario being Fault Line definitely heightens that concern.

"Heretic has better spell control with native Sacred Ward and two Cage Ragers, and his crackback can be punishing. The board having a ton of scatter obstructions and small LoS blockers wasn’t a major consideration. Honestly slightly better for me than a lot of other possibilities given LoS ignoring sprays.

"So default plan is identify which unit type will be the game-winner and which will be fodder to initiate engagement.

"In this situation it’s the Dread Rots that go under the bus. Losing them makes killing Invictus harder, but the sprays are what’s going to be what plows the army under, so positioning for the Holden unit activating under Fortune’s Path and wiping every warrior model in control was the bigger priority."

Grymkin has some insane denial both on their models and through their Arcana, but those models can be load-bearing to make the plan work. Alex sought to line up those tools perfectly against an equally skilled master caster who knew just as well where to apply pressure. The scenario score says it all in how hard fought the game ended up being for the Texas Tournament crown.
