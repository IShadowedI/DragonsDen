[40k] Competitive Innovations in 11th: Make Some Space, Marines pt.1

November 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

After last weeks somewhat alarming Dark Angels and Iron Hands avalanche, the combined forces of "literally anyone else" have done a bit better this week, so there's more faction diversity to unpack, and some impressive finishes for less common factions. There still absolutely are Marines galore as well, but we'll take what we can get right now.

Archetype

Drop Scion Combined

Thoughts

In third place (but still undefeated) we have a Guard build where someone has finally managed to take proper advantage of Combined Arms dropping to 2DP. Adding in Bridgehead ensures that drop Scions hit hard, and the combination of hit boosts, multiple sources of bonus AP and Lethal Hits if you're up against Terminators provides a level of adaptability that Guard have been short of. Being able to re-load a Scion squad with Reinforcements is also great (even if they lose the Command Squad), and On My Position allows you to maximally punish the foe for each unit they wipe. One Dorn is the main nod to the normal armoued might the Guard project, giving one deterrance piece that stops enemies being too disrespectful of longer-ranged firepower. Really glad to see someone making some guard stalwarts actually work, congratulations Clément.

Thoughts

Tricksy Aeldari face the firepower of the T'au in today's Showdown, both sides competing to Sabotage early, and Sabotage often. Unit-wise, this matchup feels decent for the Aeldari - they have plenty of reach, good output into key targets in the T'au list, and also benefit from the T'au not having brought any Starscythes, the bane of your average elf. However, the mission is horrendous for the Aeldari - the small, single central objective is a nightmare for them against a shooting build, especially with pressure to continuously interact with it. It also reduces the value of their two powerful flamer units, as it's hard to stage these somewhere that's relatively safe that still has good Overwatch lines on where the foe needs to be. Unfortunately, they're also not fantastically able to try and force the tempo of the game, because while the Tau's guns don't line up great into the elves, they'll still butcher them if they're overexposed, and that will ramp up in the late game.

From the Aeldari side, I think you plausibly need to play the objectives very aggressively instead. The T'au here don't really have any way of forcing their way onto the central objective if it's zoned out with engagement range in movement (outside the Twin Lance, but that's an expensive way to use them), so by cycling units forward agressively, and double Actioning every turn, it's plausibly possible for the elves to sell their lives dearly enough to build up a lead, even if it's very painful in lives. Their stuff that can body block the middle is a little more expendable than the T'au's, which helps, and they're also somewhat better set up to try and push points off the enemy Expansion if the T'au try and block the centre back.

It's high-risk, but I think it's the best plan available, and the Aeldari here gave things a real go, only very narrowly losing to the T'au, with essentially one Action's worth of VP in it.
