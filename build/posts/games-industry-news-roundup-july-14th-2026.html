Games Industry News Roundup- July 14th, 2026

July 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Obsidian Entertainment/Bethesda Softworks

New Fallout Game from Obsidian Confirmed After Xbox Layoffs

We’re starting things off this week with a real monkey’s paw of a news story. 

Last week’s brutal wave of layoffs that saw 1,600 employees let go across various Xbox Studios, including at venerable RPG developer Obsidian Entertainment. With at least a quarter of their staff cut, Obsidian was forced to cancel multiple projects in development and shift studio focus to creating a new Fallout game. 

According to a report from Bloomberg, Obsidian’s loss of around 60-70 employees in the first Xbox layoff wave left the company unable to continue work on several projects which were yet to be publicly announced. Among the titles confirmed for cancellation was a brand new IP with a similar feel and tone to the Fallout universe directed by studio design director Josh Sawyer.

Now, Xbox leadership has reportedly refocused the Obsidian team to begin work on a new Fallout game, with Sawyer in the director’s seat. This will be the first time Obsidian has worked on a Fallout game since 2010’s Fallout: New Vegas, a fan favorite of the series and a central focus in the most recent season of Amazon Prime’s Fallout TV adaptation. 

In a small sliver of good news, fans of the Avowed and Pillars of Eternity series can take a long sigh of relief. Though initially reported from Bloomberg that a planned sequel to Avowed was scrapped by Xbox, later reports have confirmed that this isn’t the case, and a small team at Obsidian is continuing work on the game while the rest of the studio works on Fallout.

Credit: Games Workshop

Warhammer Q&A Confirms New Rules Coming to Warcry

It’s been all quiet on the Warcry front for some time, but it looks like that silence has finally been broken with some potentially exciting updates for Warcry fans, with the most recent Warhammer Q&A stating that new Warcry rules are on the way. 

In a live Q&A session held last week, Warhammer presenters Eddie Eccles and Adam Troke answered a slew of questions regarding the releases teased at the Big Summer Preview livestream event held earlier this month. During the show, the pair addressed a question that had been submitted by multiple people, asking for a status update on the long dormant Age of Sigmar skirmish game, Warcry.

“We didn't cover Warcry in the preview. We will have some Warcry news for you quite soon,” Eccles responded. “One thing we can confirm today is there are some new Warcry rules on the way and they will cover, amongst other things, the new Ogor Mawtribes miniatures.”

While the exact extent for these plans for Warcry are still currently unknown, the skirmish game is in dire need for a shot in the arm from Games Workshop. Warcry’s been in the middle of a brutal content drought, with its last dedicated model release happening nearly two years ago. Games Workshop released a matched play update with new Helsmiths of Hashut rules in November of last year for free, but has since been quiet about any plans to support the skirmish game, leading to many fans wondering about the fate of the game and fearing the worst.   

Credit: Spiel des Jahres

JinxO Wins Spiel des Jahres 2026

The word association game JinxO won the 2026 Spiel des Jahres award, board gaming's most prestigious and long-standing award.

Spiel des Jahres was created in 1979 as a way to celebrate German language board game excellence, and has since grown into the highest board game award in the international community, with three different categories. Spiel des Jahres focuses on a best general audience game, while Kinderspiel focuses on board games specifically aimed at younger audiences and Kinnerspiel awarding more rules-intensive strategy games. Games are submitted for consideration by various game development teams and reviewed by a panel of industry insiders from around the globe, who decide on a list of finalist nominees and eventually winners. Not only does the award bring prestige to the games and teams behind them, but the exposure and notoriety brought from a Spiel win has been found to directly correlate with increased games sales.   

Developed by Martin Aang, the game revolves around players secretly writing down a list of words on a panel following a prompt, hoping that their selections match at least one other player. If no other players share any of the words, no points are scored, but if you share words with just one player, you score additional points. 

JinxO’s victory is the second time in a row where the winner has been from an Asian country, with Martin Aang hailing from Indonesia, and breaks a three year hot streak for cooperative games claiming the title.

Another notable story from this year's award ceremony was Reiner Knizia’s Kinnerspiel win with his tile-laying game Rebirth. With this, Knizia became the first ever developer to win the Spiel “triple crown”, having previously won the Spiel des Jahres award and Kinderspiel des Jahres award for two of his games back in 2008.  

Credit: WTC

WTC Releases Statement Regarding Players Hiding Lists

In a rare move for the popular international tournament, the World Team Championship of Warhammer (WTC) committee has made a rare statement regarding a recent controversy in the world of Competitive Warhammer 40k, asking players to not hide their lists prior to the WTC.

In an effort to conceal their lists from potential competitors prior to the event, and maintain an element of surprise while still getting important practice, several players who plan to attend the WTC have taken to hiding their lists on Best Coast Pairings at events where they would be otherwise public to view for BCP subscribers. This action has sparked a controversy in the larger community of competitive Warhammer players, with most condemning the act as unsportsmanlike on platforms such as Reddit.

The discourse around hiding lists has become loud enough that the WTC has elected to address it directly in a social media post, asking that tournament organizers don’t allow for WTC attendees to hide their lists at events. 

“We've been apprised that at some recent events, lists for attending WTC players have been hidden from the community and even attendees from the same event,” the WTC writes. “At the WTC, one of our pillars is that we believe everyone is entitled to be treated fairly and equally. We would petition tournament organizers out there to subscribe to that same principle and to not accord WTC players any more privileges than any of their other attendees as there shouldn't be different rules for different players at any given event. There are ample opportunities for WTC-calibre players to find opportunities to test their lists and measure their worth without the need for secrecy there-of at public events.”

While the WTC has taken a public stance against hiding lists with this statement, it notably doesn’t lay out any punishment for those who decide to continue the practice, leaving enforcement of this request up to local organizers.

This year’s WTC is scheduled to take place between August 11th-16th in Mechelen, Belgium.

Credit: Games Done Quick

Games Done Quick Cancels SNK Sponsorship Due to Company’s Saudi Ties

The charity speedrunning organization, Games Done Quick (GDQ), abruptly shut down its planned partner stream with Metal Slug developer SNK due to the company's ties to Saudi Arabia. 

The planned stream event was to celebrate the Metal Slug franchise, which turned 30 this year. The announcement was quick to receive vocal condemnation by fans of GDQ, who pointed out that while SNK is a Japanese studio, it is majority owned by the MiSK Foundation, which is run by the Saudi Arabian government. 

Shortly after the announcement, GDQ cancelled the planned SNK stream, and put out a statement on social media. 

“We have heard the concerns from our community regarding this partnership, specifically the company’s majority ownership by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, and the human rights concerns tied to the Saudi government,” GDQ writes. “We will not accept the funds from this sponsorship or continue to work with this sponsor again. GDQ is committed to supporting human rights and inclusivity, and we recognize that this partnership conflicted with those values.”

GDQ also apologized for not catching SNK’s ties to the Saudi government prior to the announcement, writing “We failed to conduct the level of review our community should expect from us and that was an oversight we deeply regret and take full responsibility for. We especially want to apologize to the runners and our host on this stream, who had nothing to do with this decision and whose runs were disrupted as a result. This isn't a reflection on them and we are grateful for their understanding.”

The Summer GDQ event was held from July 5th-11th, and raised $2.4 million for the international charity Doctors without Borders.
