BattleTechnical: Mook Mechs

October 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer

A few weeks back I wrote about how to build interesting forces as a GM (game master, not General Motors, for those of you that were confused). A key part of that is picking units that give the players an interesting game and present a challenge without being too likely to knock players out early. At the same time, both sides of the table are going to get bored if you throw nothing but 5 damage hits at people – it cuts down on threat analysis and decision making, ending up with every game feeling similar. To avoid this, I tend to categorize mook mechs into a couple different groups, and make sure to include some of each in my forces.

The categories I tend to put mechs in are:

  • Brawlers
  • Flashbulbs
  • Annoyances
  • Objective-grabbers

Not all of these are going to feature in every force, and some mechs will split across multiple categories, but by mixing these I can generally create forces that are engaging to play against without ever being a true problem for players. In each of these categories I’m going to include a couple ilClan-era mechs and why I’d field them.

Dawn Guard Crusader. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Brawler

These mechs are ones that are lower on damage and higher on durability. Generally they aren’t all the way up on durability like I might take in a competitive list, and I want to avoid standard engines here unless I can use risk of ammo explosion to help speed up death. It’s where heavies and assaults (and some thicker mediums) end up. When I want to represent a siege force (attacking or defending) I usually end up sticking a few more of these in, while I’ll include fewer if I’m making a recon or striker force.

Crusader CRD-5M

Crusaders are well known for ammo bombs, and this one is no exception. With an XL engine and ammo in both side torsos (and the right arm) there are a multitude of ways for it to die, but it’s got enough armor that it won’t happen instantly. Damage is decent out of the pair of LRM 15s, but kept to 5 point groupings, and there’s good counterplay against it by getting inside minimum range.

Wolverine WVR-9R

This wolverine is a pretty decent mech without any crippling flaws, but it also isn’t doing anything tremendously well. It does moderate damage at moderate range with moderate mobility, so it can be planned around by both you and the players. One side torso is empty other than the engine, and the other is stacked full of ammo, so it’s not too hard to take out. It also rewards good play by the players – with its main gun in the right arm, it offers canny players where they can engage it without getting shot back.

Amarok

A mech that I’d never otherwise recommend using, this makes a great campaign filler. At 100 tons, moving up to 7 hexes, it’s threatening at a glance. It’s covered in hardened armor, which again makes it threatening at a glance. It’s got an OK weapon load, but doesn’t have the heat to effectively use it – and a PPC capacitor is mostly wasted potential. With only 56% coverage by the hardened armor, it gets the drawbacks (move speed reduction and PSR penalty) without actually gaining much of a durability benefit other than specifically to headshots. It’s not going to take any longer than an Atlas to die, and again rewards players who are smart enough to go for kicks and position to avoid being attacked by torso mounted weapons.

Cougar. Credit: porble

The Flashbulb

Almost the opposite of brawlers, these are mechs that do a lot of damage for their durability and often come with heat problems. Having mechs with heat problems is great, because it gives you options depending on how well players are doing – you can always throw out a bunch of fire on not-great shots, then slow down from the overheat and give the players an opportunity to remove a mech from the board. You can find them in every era and techbase, but they’re extremely common among clan mechs.

Cougar I

Most cougars are going to fall into this category, but this one does it particularly well. Improved heavy lasers are a real threat, but one without a lot of range, and firing both spikes this slow light mech up to at least 10 heat. A cougar that only has six MP should be a dead cougar.

Shadow Cat J

The flash here is mostly what’s likely to happen to the pilot. It’s got decent total damage, so it can’t be totally ignored, but every single weapon is explosive and it doesn’t have a whole lot of armor. It’s quite liable to take a couple hits and then have the pilot pass out, which can be good or bad – good if you want to let the players salvage a shadow cat, less good if you don’t.

Warhammer WHM-11T

While looking like a warhammer at a glance, this one picks up a bad move profile, enough weapons to overheat pretty badly on an alpha strike or let you do consistent moderate damage (in 10 point hits, but having some of those in a force is good as long as you don’t go super heavy on them – you want players to feel some concern), and a glaring weakness in under-armored legs. I’ve mentioned having specific weaknesses in a couple of these mechs, and I think it’s a very valuable thing to have on your OpFor mechs as it gives the players something to reward paying attention and thinking about their play.

Panther. Credit: Rockfish

The Annoyance

Annoyances are units that exist to do a bit of damage, just enough to be noticed, but are a little too durable or a little too long ranged to be a target where the players want to focus all their fire. I don’t put them in every force, because they absolutely do get annoying and can tilt the game if the players don’t have a way to deal with them, but they’re a perfect unit if players are bringing a mix of forces and have some fast units. Position them away from your main force and the players have something for their fast mechs to do without the risk of diving right into some higher damage heavies.

Panther PNT-12K

This applies equally to just about every panther; they’re all perfect annoyance units. They have one shot for 10 damage, enough to notice but not enough generally to prioritize if there are other things to shoot, and will hang out at range. Make sure that it doesn’t engage until some of the brawlers are in the fight and keep it isolated, it’ll provide a good target for any fast player units to safely participate.

LRM Carrier

While I’ve mostly talked about mechs here, LRM carriers are something that are always worth considering. They’ve got high potential damage, so players won’t want to just let them shoot, but if you set them up for indirect fire they’re usually very inaccurate, making their actual damage output relatively low. With thin armor and little movement they’re again something great for fast player units to engage while staying out of a main brawl.

Sentinel STN-6S

The sentinel (in most of its forms) is a mech with moderate speed, moderate armor, and moderate damage. This has decent range, but is putting out hardly any damage, making it another good way to fill out a force. It doesn’t have any accuracy buffs (and even has a couple weapons with substantial minimum range), making it a perfect target for faster units to go engage, If it’s left alone it’ll eventually wear down player units, so they don’t want to ignore it completely.

Simonson's Cutthroats Locust. Credit: Jack Hunter

Objective Grabbers

These are the mechs I’m least likely to field in most forces – ones that really exist just to do objectives. Usually cheap, fast, and lightly armed, they can be described as fast annoyances. I usually don’t field many of them because I want the players to be the force with specific objectives rather than being on the defensive side. Still, sometimes they can be helpful to fill in a force, even if just to give the impression of numbers without adding anything effective (be careful that doing this doesn’t cause problems with initiative sinking though).

Locust LCT-7V2

By being faster than anything my players are likely fielding I can use this to get them to think a turn or two in advance and plan where it’s likely to go. The weapon mix on it is a mess, so if players fail to plan effectively it’s not going to just tear them apart (don’t use a Locust IIC).

Stinger STG-6R

8/12/8 is the farthest jumping profile I want to field in most games, and this does it with minimal armor and minimal damage. I do want to have this speed on something designed for objectives because there does need to be a challenge to the players – a 6/9/6 objective grabber is going to die too easily to present a mission that has any real challenge, but 8/12/8 will require them to do a little more planning around where it’s likely to go and how to kill it, while still being something they can deal with even without having a force designed around killing fast stuff.

Final Thoughts

I use mechs from these categories to fill out most of my OpFors, but not all the units in them. Depending on the number of players, I’ll almost always have a few mechs that fall outside these guidelines, either because they have one or two bigger guns, more cohesive weapon loads, or are just harder to kill. Those pull double duty – they keep the force from being something that’s too easy to deal with, and they give the players salvage choices that they’re actually going to want to use.
