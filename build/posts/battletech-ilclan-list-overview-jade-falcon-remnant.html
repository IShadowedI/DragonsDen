BattleTech IlClan List Overview: Jade Falcon Remnant

September 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Howdy everyone and welcome to List Overview. BattleTech has a great many factions in it, with 31 factions with IlClan availability lists on Master Unit List. These factions all have their quirks, idiosyncrasies, oddball unique units, and long-running fandom stereotypes about their playstyles. As a new player it can be somewhat hard to figure out what a Jade Falcon list is supposed to be like, what a Clan Protectorate list looks like, what much of anything looks like. In addition, many players have somewhat of an odd opinion that a list is either competitively viable or its fluffy, faction appropriate, and lore friendly. The idea that a list can be both very strong and extremely on theme for a faction seems to escape a lot of people. Today we are going to demonstrate this by looking at an on-theme, powerful list for the Jade Falcon Remnant, one of the most interesting factions come IlClan (in my humble opinion).

Lore Primer

The Jade Falcon Epsilon Galaxy forces from Battletech Aces: Scouring Sands. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Jade Falcon Remnant, also sometimes called the Sudeten Falcons or Jiyi’s Falcons, are a splinter group of Clan Jade Falcon, rejecting their clan’s defeat by Clan Wolf and acting with rather un-clanlike wisdom and tact to secure their position and their ownership of the planet Sudeten. The Jade Falcon Remnant are in the unique position of having more mechs than pilots, desperately trying to fill their ranks. This includes straight up stealing a class of cadets from the Ghost Bears in quite possibly one of the funniest raids in all of BattleTech fiction. Khan Jiyi Chistu is a really fantastic character in one of BattleTech’s most interesting subsettings (The Hinterlands), and the Jade Falcon Remnant (or at least part of a Jade Falcon Remnant its unclear) star as the opposing force in the BattleTech Aces: Scouring Sands box. 

You might be interested in playing the Jade Falcon Remnant if you want to have some of the best color schemes in the game, if you hate Clan Wolf almost as much as I do, and/or if you really like Thunderbolts, Thors, and Turkinas.

Availability

The Jade Falcon Remnants have one of the best Mech availabilities in the IlClan era, with access to best-in-class OmniMechs like the Butcherbird(Ion Sparrow), Phantom, Stormcrow, Turkina, Mad Dog(Vulture) MkIV, Loki MkII, and Blood Asp. In addition, the Jade Falcon Remnants have access to several high quality and exclusive (or near exclusive) standard BattleMechs, such as the Thunderbolt C 2, Shrike 2, Thunderbolt IIC, and the Jade Hawk. You will struggle to build a wall of steel or any other strategy that hinges on large amounts of cheap armor, but the Jade Falcon Remnant is extremely good at playing either high speed cavalry beatdown strategies with their strong stable of 5/8 heavy mechs, or, as we will demonstrate today, at simply doing so much damage so quickly that many lists simply cannot withstand them for long enough for it to matter.

The List

Our thematic Scale 4 (8000 BV) list today consists of the following mechs:

  • Turkina H, 4/5, 2712 BV
  • Thunderbolt C 2, 4/5, 2170 BV
  • Phantom H, 4/5, 1178 BV
  • Locust IIC 4, 3/5, 954 BV
  • Butcherbird (Ion Sparrow) C, 4/4, 978 BV

Coming in at a grand total of 7992 BV, this list combines high speed “Buzzsaw” strikers with a solid, high damage heavy mech and one of the game’s premium centerpiece assault mechs.

Turkina H

Clan Jade Falcon Turkina B. Credit: Valk

The Turkina H is a high quality assault mech, weighing 95 tons and moving 3/5/3 with a frightening weapon load consisting of paired UAC/10s, paired MPLs, and a trio of heavy medium lasers. All of these weapons are helped by the Targeting Computer mounted, bizarrely, in the right arm. The Turkina H has effectively no heat issues, only going up 1+movement on a run. 

You need to deploy your centerpiece carefully here, as 3/5/3 is just not a particularly impressive movement profile. The jump jets can help with getting in to a firing position, but with so much damage tied up in heavy medium lasers you want to be pushing forwards to get in the fight as quickly as possible. Find an open line that gets you to the middle of the battlefield in a timely fashion and slam the Turkina down it. Our wobbly idiot bird son will take his time getting there and is no good at all for objectives, but if you can leverage the thick armor and great close ranged firepower you should be able to find some good success with him.

Thunderbolt C 2

Thunderbolt. Credit: porble

A mech that is, in IlClan, exclusive to the Jade Falcon Remnant, the Thunderbolt C 2 has rapidly become one of my favorites. It carries a good armor load, a standard engine, moves 4/6/0, and has a pretty fantastic weapon set. An ERPPC is the only concession to true long range, with the rest of the mech being set up for close range brawling with 4 ER Medium Lasers, a streak SRM 6, 2 ER Small Lasers for a good reason, a pair of Small Pulse Lasers, and an AMS. While in the past we have ragged on AMS somewhat, in the new core rules AMS is quite good, massively reducing the damage output of missile weapons. As ATMs and Clan LRMs are some of the game’s premium ways to puke damage, this damage reduction is very noticeable against things that would otherwise be natural predators of the Thunderbolt C 2. 

With a good firing pattern of “Everything but the PPC”, the Thunderbolt C 2 really wants to get to close combat and stick there. Put it on the same ingress line as the Turkina H, but try to not outpace Mr Turkey too much. A standard engine is nice, but 65 tons is not heavy enough to take aggro from an entire enemy list for very long. Neither of these mechs are particularly subtle, but they are easy to use. Front Towards Enemy. Which is something I should really paint on the mini-fridge that is the chest of a Thunderbolt.

Locust IIC 4 and Phantom H

Jade Falcon Epsilon Galaxy Locust IIC 4. Credit: Jack Hunter

Putting these mechs together because they have very nearly the same weapon load. The Phantom is much, much more durable as well as faster, but the Locust IIC is cheaper. Both carry 6 heavy small lasers as their primary weapons, with the Phantom backing that up with an MPL and a flamer. The Locust IIC 4 instead has an ER medium laser, which is a bit of a bummer but shouldn’t make much of a difference in practice. These mechs are very simple in use cases, they want to get next to something and puke 43 points of laser damage into it. That said, within that simple use case there are a few things to keep in mind.

The key one is to keep it in your pants. These mechs do a LOT of damage and they are very, very fast, but this can lead you towards poor play. You do not want these mechs to engage early, you only want these mechs to get involved in the fight once your Turkina and Thunderbolt are already taking fire. After that you want to make absolutely sure that the slow movers remain at all times the more tempting targets, ensuring 4 TMM (or 3 TMM plus Terrain) on your strike package. If they are the best or only shot an enemy has, they will die immediately. Most players are not going to be able to ignore a Turkina they are hitting on 7s to risk firing at a Phantom on 10s, but if the shots approach being equal you will rapidly find yourself down half your mechs. 

In addition, the Phantom is your only fast mover with hands, so depending on the scenario you might end up sending it off to do objectives. If this is the case you need to be even more careful with your Locust, as you can rapidly end up without a significant ground-bound backstabbing force.

Butcherbird (Ion Sparrow) C

Clan Jade Falcon Ion Sparrow Prime. Credit: Valk.

I am going to call this the Butcherbird from here, because that name is cooler. The Butcherbird C is one of the game’s best light mechs (and was voted such in our article on the topic by multiple people). Carrying 6 ER small lasers slaved to a Tcomp with a 8/12/10 movement profile, the Butcherbird C does whatever the hell it wants. Ideally it wants to mirror the Locust IIC and the Phantom, going for backstabs and picking off targets who are tied up tangling with the heavy hitters. Beyond that it has much more agency than either of the other strikers, being capable of rapidly responding to literally anything and being more than capable as a light mech hunter, freely bullying offending Spiders and Fire Moths.

Assuming the scenario doesn’t care about having hands, this is also your premium objective dork. 10 hexes of jump is just incredible mobility. Even without any EWAR equipment, probes, ECM, the like, that speed on its own will cover a good chunk of the core book scenarios, particularly against a slower opponent. If the scenario doesn’t require attention, 30 damage at -1 to hit is 30 damage at -1 to hit, go mess some people up.

Gameplan

At this point you should be seeing what this list’s overall gameplan and win condition is. The light mechs hang back while the big boys push up to the center of the map to take control of the center, and after a fight has begun in mid around them the strikers descend and snap at the heels and flanks of any of the enemy’s mechs that seem vulnerable. If anything tries to back down or get away from the frontline, dive it with the fast movers and see how they like over 100 points of laser damage coming in while they are vulnerable.

This list is capable of horrifying damage output and can play many objectives fairly well due to the 3 high speed assets. Your goal should be to apply that horrifying damage to melt down the enemy’s heavies and assaults, trading positively into them by virtue of not giving them enough time to extract their BV out of your list. You are capable of doing more than 220 points of damage in a single turn, more damage than the majority of heavy mechs have armor. If they try to outrange you and refuse to go to the middle of the board try to dive any snipers or long range specialists with your light mechs. While doing this, push onto their side of the map and pin them up against the board edge while Turkina-ing all over the place.

It isn’t an undefeatable list though. If your enemy invests in a few premium light mech hunters (Gunsmith, Cicada 4A, etc) you can quickly lose your mobility advantage if you aren’t careful. A traditional wall of steel might also give you some issues, assuming your opponent invested in precision autocannons and pulse lasers. A Precision AC/20 to any part of your light strikers will likely kill or cripple them, and LPLs are similarly threatening. After the light mechs are gone, you are only a few unlucky rolls away from being out of the game and a well made wall of steel will make a lot of rolls. It isn’t a hard counter, but it can be very hard to approach with your fast movers.

That said, this all assuming a general melee with no real objectives. So, how does this list play the Core Book Missions?

Core Missions

Jade Falcon force from the Battletech Core Box. Credit: Jack Hunter

The core missions are divided into two categories. They are either broadly just deathmatches with funky deployment, or they are ones that have objective points to control in various ways. The kill missions will be pretty easy for this list and you can mostly play them as above, but the objective missions all require a bit more thought.

For Deathmatches, the core book has:

  • Ambush - If you roll attacker, divide the slow from the fast and put the slow on the side with the quicker route to center
  • Annihilation - Literally just a deathmatch do whatever
  • Dominate - Kill mission, but you can leave the board from the enemy's side to score as if you had killed your own mech that just moved off the table. Do this if a mech is damaged to the point you aren't getting any value out of it.
  • Extraction - If you roll defender, put your big boys in the middle and move the fast movers on. Play as normal
  • Focal Point - Technically an objective mission but this is a straight forwards king of the hill mission. Move your big mechs on the fastest line to the point, try to score it turn 1 with your fast movers if you can before backing them down.
  • General Melee - Slightly funny kill mission. Put your big mechs in the center and play as normal.

As for Objective missions, things are a bit more diverse. The book has the following objective centric missions:

  • Control the Field - Straight forwards objective sitting mission. Goal should be to sit the Turkina on whichever backfield objective has a stronger position for it, as the UAC/10s and MPLs give it acceptable range. The thunderbolt will want to push to the objective opposite it, supported by your fast movers. Once you are racking up points from that, send your striker package to try to claim the other enemy objective while the Turkina (hopefully) keeps the enemy off of its objective. You need to sacrifice your other home objective, you do not have the numbers to hold both.
  • Hold The Line - Straight forwards objective mission. Put the Turkina on the objective closest to your board edge (or the more defensible one if multiple are equidistant) and shove everything else forwards onto whichever other objective seems easier to take. Make sure your Turkina is in a position to support them with its big guns.
  • Objective Raid - I hate this mission. Pray that you don't get scammed by potential objectives while your little mechs skitter around scanning. Move your big mechs up to whatever objectives are closest, and if you roll Mobile objectives try to use the Thunderbolt and Phantom to pull objectives into a ball next to your Turkina.
  • Sensor Check - Run next to the enemy with your light mechs and scan them. Once you have scanned enough that you feel you have done enough, pull your light mechs off the table. Try to distract anything with pulse or precision with your big mechs to avoid total light mech elimination.
  • Steel Rain - Roll attacker. If you don't roll attacker, drop the big mechs first and the light mechs second. Get your bigs onto a pair of objectives that are near each other, and have the Phantom and Thunderbolt consolidate objectives as in Objective Raid. If you roll attacker, try to get the Phantom off the board with an objective ASAP. This will get your racking up points for minimum cost while you try to focus down whatever enemy unit/concentration seems the most vulnerable.
  • Urban Sweep - Run your light mechs around and scan all the objectives. Push your big boys up an obvious line and kill anything fast enough to scan well. Killing probe mechs is also a good idea. This mission is somewhat non-interactive, you both just spin to win and see who actually wins VP from the objectives.

Final Thoughts

This list is trying to use some Falcon specific/iconic units while still having a coherent focus. Thors, Thor IIs, Lokis, and Loki MkIIs are well and good, but it can be difficult to build an effective list around them given their low damage for BV. The Turkina is an excellent centerpiece and it commands attention at the table while being the biggest dumbest bird to ever live. The Thunderbolt C 2 is a mech that I desperately need to be on more availabilities, it is not fair that a heavy mech this good is stuck on Jade Falcon.

Clan forces at Scale 4 (8000 BV) struggle sometimes to fill a full star...if you want to only use big mechs. The Clan Light and Medium mech stable is in general very strong in IlClan across all Clans, and the Falcon Remnant have a particularly good selection. The Phantom on its own very nearly makes a top tier fast mover stable on its own, with the Butcherbird, Hammerhead, and Stormcrow just adding on to it. You need to really consider taking lighter and somewhat thinner assets at 8000 compared to, say, 10000, but you can still squeeze in any big boy fairly easily. When your cheap mechs are some of the best in the game, its not a problem to splurge on the best of the really expensive ones.

Overall I really am happy with this list and I think you could put in some very good results with it. This is just a starting point and my word is not law (yet) and I make no claim that this is the only way to play Falcons, but it is one of the better ways to play Clans in general at 8K as a result of the above mentioned very high quality of fast, cheap Clan mechs come IlClan. I’d love to hear any thoughts, opinions, expansions, or alterations to this list.
