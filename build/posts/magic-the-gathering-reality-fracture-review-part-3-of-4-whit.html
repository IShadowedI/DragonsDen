Magic: The Gathering Reality Fracture Review, Part 3 of 4 - White, Blue, and Black Cards

October 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer
At the crossroads of Jace’s rampant egomania and ‘what if I drew a fucked up, evil version of the Hamburger Helper’ lies Reality Fracture, Magic’s newest set. This is the first Planeswalkers-matter set since 2019’s War of the Spark, or should we say Planeswalker, singular, as Jace is the set’s focal point. Reality Fracture is filled with powerful cards and their alternate universe counterparts, offering both novel mechanical takes on beloved characters, and imaginative ‘what-if’ scenarios for Vorthoses. To that end, this set is built for pick-2 draft, with both versions of many characters appearing alongside one another in the same pack. After a couple of middling releases, this set is probably the most powerful since Avatar, and we expect a host of multi-format staples to solidify once players get their hands on Reality Fracture. Today we’re covering the set’s white, blue, and black cards.

Reality Fracture officially releases to tabletop on October 2nd, and on Magic: The Gathering Arena on September 29th.

White

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ajani Resolute

Saffgor: 2 mana Planeswalkers are a rarity as it is, and good ones even moreso. I think this is more Wrenn than Tibalt, though, and seems like a great pickup for lifegain strategies.

BPhillipYork: This should be gaining loyalty counters pretty fast even in a commander game, maybe even fast enough to deal with random flyers and such hitting it.  It's a neat design and I'd like to see more planeswalkers be viable.

FromTheShire: Now that's one hell of a card. Your average lifegain deck generates what I would ballpark as 3 triggers per game action by any player so you can expect Ajani to be a constant thorn in the side if he's not dealt with. By the end of the game you're going to wish they were popping the ultimate rather than a never ending supply of ever growing beaters.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Campus Crier

Saffgor: This is lowkey one of the best Empower enablers, being something you can mill over and activate in the yard for cheap, which then provides a target for your Reality Scrambles or Entrust the Sparks. Popping this on the opponent's End Step and untapping with your Scramble effect is very real. It matters less in Commander, but in a 60 card version of the combo I think it has a key role. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Danitha, Sword of Hope

BPhillipYork: Solid enabler for two kinds of decks, or both combined. Really useful for Equipment decks which don't have a ton of good card draw sources, and kind of neat to have protecting your creatures also have some card draw attached for replacement.

FromTheShire: She's not as good as Sram, but that's a high bar to clear, and you'll likely be happy to get the redundancy. Plus she has the upside of also drawing off of pump and protection spells so you may occasionally get a second draw per turn cycle, though it won't be a regular occurance.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Enlightened Confidant

Saffgor: The game is a bit too fast to make this wildly compelling, and that’s a sad thing. This should be good, I feel it in my bones, but it probably isn’t.

BPhillipYork: This is a really nice payoff for lifegain decks. Decent creature to have lifegain on top of that to set up it's own trigger, though a bit flimsy.

FromTheShire: It's definitely better in 60 card than Commander, but we do already have some lifegain decks there that will be very happy to see it. There's no world where it even comes close to eclipsing Bob since it is a build around rather than being generically powerful but I like it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Flickering Hound

Saffgor: The trigger isn’t my concern with this card, it’s the targets. Plenty of ways to spam out Creatures, but it’s much harder to make this go mana-neutral or mana-positive, by virtue of it not being able to target Artifacts (e.g. mana rocks). Still good, but it’s no kitten.

BPhillipYork: There's some obvious comparison to Displacer Kitten here but it's nowhere near as good. Still a fun value play, and if you have flash you can use it to dodge creature removal pretty simply, and if you want to use it to set up a combo it definitely can go infinite, just takes a bit of elaborate planning.

FromTheShire: I mean the actual comparison here is doing combo nonsense Peregrine Drake and Co. style. We did it guys, we broke Palinchron. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Germinate Recruits

Saffgor: Hello endgame for lifegain decks. I mean, yeah this does push to kill the table if you’ve gained infinite or infinite enough life. The fact they enter untapped means doing it for blockers is brutal, and copying this with a soul sister effect onboard has cumulative results. Again, this is an outlet for infinite lifegain, which otherwise does nothing, given 'not losing' doesn't win on its own.

BPhillipYork: This seems fine as a low tier payoff for a lot of lifegain, and there are a few weird tricks to exchange life totals or regain all your life or something like that.

FromTheShire: In Standard this feels like a whole lot of power for a very reasonable investment, at instant speed too. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ghalta the Immovable

Saffgor: This is probably the best ‘mana value matters’ Commander for Monowhite, in the same vein as Lord of the Eagles, Gorex, the Tombshell, and your choice of Ghalta. Heck, new 5c Karn is also in that field. You could be cute and try to build the whole cycle, but once you’re past Sultai colors I foresee only disappointment. There's just not enough worthwhile payoffs, and just White isn't enough for butts-matter.

BPhillipYork: Another nice enabler for a toughness deck archetype, and a solid creature for it, you could build around it if you wanted but there are better options.

FromTheShire: Excellent redundancy for toughness matters deck, giving the full attack package all in one. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Gideon's Memorial

Saffgor: This is like an Infinity Stone-level mana rock for superfriends decks, sheesh. It obviously doesn’t see play elsewhere, but it’s a fantastic addition to one archetype specifically. More cards like that, please.

BPhillipYork: Pretty nice for support for token decks, with some solid secondary options, and also a nice mana rock for planeswalker, especially gideon decks where it's going to ramp you and buff your tokens.

FromTheShire: We'll see where it shakes out in the long run but can confirm I have seen this card before being summarily beaten to death on the ladder several times so far. It really does a lot, ramping you into walkers who make tokens which are then hitting harder and always on defense for the crack back. And then if you draw multiples it becomes removal? Yikes. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Guiding Hydra

Saffgor: Worse Ouroboroid, but still maybe good enough? I don’t rate this highly.

BPhillipYork: Pretty nice slowplay way to buff your go-wide strategy over a couple of turns, especially if you have a couple of ways to add more tokens to keep it going.

FromTheShire: You know how Mikaeus, the Lunarch sees next to no play? Yeah neither is this outside of maaaybe Hydra typal. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Kindred Judgement

Saffgor: 7 mana for this is just too high, as combining a protection spell with a wipe does about the same thing with each component being individually far better.

BPhillipYork: There's getting to be a fair number of these kindred centric wipes and when it reaches a threshold to be able to rely on, it suddenly makes for powerful one-sided board clears to support tribal decks, which is fairly nasty. Great in Angel tribal in particular, and thematic.

FromTheShire: I'm always a big fan of one sided board wipes. It's narrow since it wants to be in a typal deck but I have a ton of those so let's go. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Liliana the Faultless

Saffgor: They combined two of the best White one-drops into a single card, and it’s pretty great, to nobody’s surprise! Yes, it’s worse than Soul Warden at using opponent’s turns, yes, it’s worse than Mother of Runes at protecting key threats, but it’s both in the ways that matter. In decks that want both effects, maybe even in the Command Zone, she’s wonderful.

BPhillipYork: A Mother of Runes and a Soul's Attendant stapled together, which is a pretty nice two for one.

FromTheShire: Mother of Hinterland Sanctifier? Yeah I'm all about it. Only giving hexproof not protection is a definite downgrade but when you have 2 playables stapled on to one card who's complaining. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lyra, Archangel of Dawn

Saffgor: I have no interest in typal, least of all Angels, but this does provide its effect at the cheapest we've seen it, for as ubiquitous as Lyra's synergies are.

BPhillipYork: This gets really dangerous quickly in Angel tribal if it drops early, and it's fairly costed so just a solid enabler for a kindred deck.

FromTheShire: Do you have a Giada, Font of Hope deck? Then this goes in it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Repurposed Enforcer

Saffgor: If you have 8 bodies, it gets you to Kasmina loyalty. If that’s over two turns, maybe that’s still good enough.

BPhillipYork: Potentially you can use this to insta-ult Jace planeswalkers, which is pretty nuts honestly. Not quite Doubling Season insta ult goodness since people still get to interact before you go to combat, potentially really strong nonetheless.

FromTheShire: I mean you'd have to have made a token copy of a Jace with an ultimate first since the empower only loads up tokens, but I'm sure it's doable. It gets you a burst of loyalty but having to attack to do it means you're probably not repeating it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Return to the Light Realms

Saffgor: I am not the target audience for this spell, give it to Timmy. I see this, and it's an uncastable piece of cardboard that wastes a Mythic slot in limited.

BPhillipYork: So it's super Replenish, but it costs 9 mana, which is a lot of mana. But pretty easily you can win the game if you just put the right pieces in your yard.

FromTheShire: Um Actually it's a super Rise of the Dark Realms or Brilliant Restoration. Just like the original it's a fun card that should result in you winning the game when you cast it if you're doing it at all correctly. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Teyo, Lightshield Expert

Saffgor: If this were printed a year ago I’d have called the ability to ETB put a Loyalty counter on something spooky, but between this and Mystery Booster: Commander Wizards has ramped the ability to turbo through Planeswalkers up substantially. To that end, Teyo is probably worse than the new option for Monowhite, Worzel, the Protector.

BPhillipYork: I guess the way to think about a card like this is it's really just meant to sit in your hand as a creature and planeswalker protection spell, and has the upside of, once it's done that, it sits around as a 1/1.  It's quite a bit better than non-creature protection, since it's usually easier to counter non-creature spells, and usually easier to stop them from being cast.

FromTheShire: Potentially useful piece of protection in Standard. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Thalia, the Survivor

Saffgor: Being one-sided is pretty great, honestly. I've seen talk of it even as far as cEDH, and while I'm not quite that bullish, you do cause opponents to hemorrhage mana in stack wars.

BPhillipYork: Solid stax piece, if a bit pricy, but since it doesn't affect you that makes it pretty powerful.

FromTheShire: The only way I can see this being a thing is cheating it in somehow, in formats where you want a piece like this you don't want to spend 4 mana for it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tomik, Orzhov Lawmage

BPhillipYork: As a way of protecting your planeswalkers its interesting. I'm not sure if it's really enough to limit it to one creature per combat, though I suppose his ability to hand out flying also means you can throw a big fattie in the way, to provide more deterrence. But it's hard to bet on a planeswalker if it's losing even 2 loyalty per round simply as a result of 1/1s hitting it.

FromTheShire: I like it as a tech piece if you're going to try and make superfriends work in Commander. Anything you can do to slow down incoming attacks is really useful because otherwise people will always feel free to swing at your walkers politically. You still need some good blockers as well but if you can stick it maybe you have a chance.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Way of the Mentor

Saffgor: What I said with Teyo applies, it’s crazy they’re trusting players with this degree of Planeswalker turbo capabilities. Have you guys read some of the ultimates available? Insane card, but probably not even the best Way Enchantment for its role...for that, we’ll need to wait for Black. It's going to require some doing, but expect to see emblems ahead of schedule.

BPhillipYork: This one seems really nuts as a way to make your planeswalkers pop off. Have a couple in play, drop creatures to gain a few life triggers, other people playing creatures causes life triggers, and so on. It's a solid way to actually play planeswalkers in Commander, which I'm excited to see if they can survive in "real games".

FromTheShire: Extremely good way to ramp up your walkers, this might be playable even if it only said target planeswalker. It doesn't though, so buckle the hell up. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion.

Saffgor: Good dog. Surely Jace wouldn't ruin this lil guy's life.

BPhillipYork: It's a good dog, that's for sure.

FromTheShire: Hardened Scales on the goodest boy. A little pricey but he's doing his best.

Blue

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Chandra, Chill of Compliance

Saffgor: To be quite honest, I’m not feeling Blue Chandra. Her abilities are all fine, and have merit in 60-card formats, but there’s nothing which protects her while building Loyalty counters. To that end, she seems fragile, and the power associated with that fragility isn’t as high as say, The Theorist in this set.

BPhillipYork: Well the emblem is obviously strong, and not unrealistic to get to, though it's 3 turns of +1 with no interference, which is rough. With all the loyalty gain triggers running around pretty doable.

FromTheShire: Solid rate, lots of modes, and they're all pretty good. Most likely this is mainly going to be a surveil engine but stunning things is going to come in handy at times. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Countersculpt

Saffgor: This goes in the theoretical Jace Monoblue deck for Standard with Machinations, Theorist, Reality Sculptor, etc.

BPhillipYork: I'm starting to think this set might be a little overly Jace centric.

FromTheShire: It wants a very specific deck but if it materializes straight up Counterspell in Standard is pretty wild. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Cruel Calculations

Saffgor: Better than it looks, especially in formats where Archive Trap is a very real card. Still, the floor is abysmal, and it kind of needed to be Instant-speed to see use in the above scenario, so you need to be extremely sure you can get some value out of the card before adding it to decks.

BPhillipYork: This is a huge payoff for a mill deck, including a self-mill deck, and can easily draw you a huge hand. The limitation of sorcery speed is really big, it would probably be too good as an instant, but then you could draw of your opponents self mill as well. A strong card draw with the right deck.

FromTheShire: Man good thing there aren't any decks running around with things like Kitsune's Technique or Singularity Rupture. Nope, not a one. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Diviner of Victory

Saffgor: This is the ideal opening play for the UW Scry/Surveil tempo deck in Standard which is arriving in Reality Fracture, and honestly it’s pretty good. Bouncing gets better if the game is faster, as it becomes functional removal if the opponent never has the chance to recast the card you send back to hand, so treating this as a nontraditional aggro list seems wise.

BPhillipYork:  Decent value card. Fun with the various ways to auto-prepare, or with decks that flicker a lot or things like that to just mass stall your opponents.

FromTheShire: Really nice tempo piece. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Fblthp, Impossibly Lost

Saffgor: Higher floor, lower ceiling than peers in the Lab Man space. I can see this in Tymna, the Weaver decks as a secondary Thoracle which doesn’t feel terrible to have in hand.

BPhillipYork: Well super complex way to win the game, dangerous and fun, so I really like it. Either build around him and deck yourself or else just include him as a win con. Love living on the edge decks that put themselves down to 0 cards, though in this case you could do one card in library (if you are a coward).

FromTheShire: Speaking of little guys doing their best, you go Fblthp. You win that game. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Geist of Saint Thalia

Saffgor: Kind of a crazy card to print for how simple she is, and in decks which want her, Thalia is either broader or less color-intensive than similar options. Great to see.

BPhillipYork: Wow that is crazy strong cost reduction. Just really good.

FromTheShire: On the heels of banning Gran-Gran I'm sure this will be totally fine. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jace, Reality Sculptor

Saffgor: Jace gets back to his Monoblue roots here, with a shockingly fast pace to kill in the right deck. If you play him on turn 5, he’s Empowering 5 and going to 6, then next turn let’s say 5 and going to 7, for 17, which means Jace’s Machinations on the opponent’s turn to add 8 and allow for an Instant-speed activation gets you to exactly his 0-cost ability’s 25 loyalty requirement. If you can survive long enough to tempo your opponent out, this is a great win condition, even in Standard.

FromTheShire: Even if you're not hitting the combo kill, this is another piece that adds a hell of a lot of loyalty to a Jace token, like enough that it may instantly no longer be worth it for your opponent to try and kill it. And if they do decide to swing at it, it's buying you quite a bit of life.

BPhillipYork: Well that is an interesting finisher ult, and doable but tough to pull off in Commander. With the -3 you can actually possibly protect a bunch of planeswalkers, but if you combine it with The Chain Veil it's pretty doable to save up some Treasures and pop off. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jace's Machinations

Saffgor: Doesn’t look like it immediately, but this is one of the best cards in the set. For Pioneer and earlier, it enables the Kasmina, Enigma Sage combo I covered on Puzzlebox, and in Standard it helps make Jace, Reality Sculptor function. This card won’t be played fairly, and that’s okay, because boy are its interactions cool.

FromTheShire: Yeah this is a banger. Even leaving aside everything else, 3 mana for 8 loyalty on the token is so, so much. It doesn't actually exist in a vacuum though, and there are some really good ways to take advantage of that big burst as mentioned above.

BPhillipYork: This seems like a great way to get a Jace ult off by casting this on an opponents turn, and up-ticking everything and then going off. It's still going to be tough protecting planeswalkers but this makes it a little more viable. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lyra, Tolarian Archangel

Saffgor: Counting all End Steps is pretty snazzy, but 3 cards is a challenging ask for a payoff of a single 3/3. I don’t think this gets there.

FromTheShire: Generally speaking if you can repeatedly draw at least 3 cards per turn, you don't really need a non-hasty 3/3 on top of it to win the game.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty decent in some ways, though it's also kind of a win more card. If you're already repeatedly drawing 3 cards per turn, do you really need Angels? Still pretty decent to have a way to pump mana into her if you stall out to get more card draw. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Precise Redaction

FromTheShire: This is a Standard pick, especially since it gives blue a way to fight colors it normally wouldn't. Usually this would read red and green, but the switch opens up choices for decks like Spellementals or Jeskai Lessons to have play against Orzhov Lifegain or Mardu Discard out of the sideboard. A nice safety valve to see.

BPhillipYork: This kind of conditional counterspell isn't something I'd ever recommend using in Commander, there's just too many better options.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Samut, Tyrant of Naktamun

Saffgor: The vast majority of people do not play Commander at a high enough power level for this to be good in their decks. I’m opening with that statement, because I believe it to be true, but in cEDH and actual B4, this thing is a menace. Samut makes the best decks better, is reasonably tutorable, and even helps you stop wins by immediately stuffing the countermagic war. In Bracket 3 or below, this probably stops a removal spell, or a single protection effect. Scant few decks where this matters unless you know for a fact people will be fighting over the stack.

FromTheShire: I'm still of the opinion that this can be quite valuable in B3 for making sure your counters and removal always just work, but yes at higher power levels is where it really shines. Or maybe like burns with an inner fire because god damn. In 60 card this has the potential to be insane too, probably why it's a 2/1 so it dies to Orcish Bowmasters to rein it in a bit.

BPhillipYork: At first to me this card seems deeply problematic, but it's not really. Functionally it's just kind of like Grand Abolisher or something like that. For sure it's a bit strange to see something like this in blue, since it gives you the option to really shut down your opponents by just countering something and no-one can respond. Nonetheless I think it's a really powerful card for cEDH deck and is probably going to be pretty meta defining for a while as everyone running blue includes it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Seasoned Cryomancer

Saffgor: Pretty sick card for Ux midrange in Standard, although in Commander you’ll probably only see it as a way to draw via an ability from the yard, as a win against Rule of Law effects. Card is quite fair, and a close-to Seasoned Pyromancer being in Standard and potentially not even being good is wild to me.

FromTheShire: It certainly feels like it should see play but it's not immediately obvious where. Maybe in Reanimator or Excruciator to take advantage of the discard in addition to buying you time to execute your plan? I could almost see it having more play in Pioneer in Modern than in Standard despite the power.

BPhillipYork: Two card selection and dropping a couple of stun counters is nice, but doesn't really do that much unless you're going to be flickering it or something. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Sphinx of False Conclusions

Saffgor: It’s a fine midrange card for Ux decks in Standard, but I don’t think it makes the cut for the deck it needed to, which is Dimir Midrange.

FromTheShire: Kind of a fine, relatively fair card in this day and age which feels kind of wild to say. It is a pretty decently costed flyer that offers card advantage and some recursion and yet it doesn't feel like it's going to get there. Maybe a couple of copies somewhere, though I certainly wouldn't be shocked if it found a true home at some point.

BPhillipYork: Kind of neat to have flash on a Sphinx and then a draw and discard trigger, the create a token copy when it dies is also pretty interesting. I could see that trigger showing up and becoming deciduous and or evergreen though it's a fair amount of value to put onto a creature. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Sphinx's Approach

Saffgor: I have so much to say about Sphinx’s Approach—I’d estimate 3500ish words worth, judging by my coverage of it last week. This isn’t just the best ‘play any number’ card they’ve ever printed (and it is), but also an enabler for some ridiculously cool storm turns, courtesy of cards like Sphinx of the Chimes & Sphinx of the Second Sun. This card had me in the tank for hours after its reveal, and after a literal sleepless night turning over Exchange of Words lines with Immortus, Master of Eternity in my head, it’s already entered my paper stable of decks I’m bringing to FNMs/conventions. I don’t usually gel with this type of card, but Sphinx’s Approach is a very special design, and one you ought to try out.

FromTheShire: Not a lot to add on this one as it has been so thoroughly covered already, so go read the article instead! I can vouch that it's a good one.

BPhillipYork: Wow that's fairly, uh, nutso. Sort of, and pretty obviously, like Dragon's Approach. I sort of hate that this card is going to blatantly spike in price and stay up there unless they reprint it repeatedly. There are a ton of interesting Sphinxes with powerful abilities, and this is an instant, so you can easily be casting it to have fast Sphinxes popping out right before your turn, and it's a card draw card on top of it. There's also some cards that trigger off copies of the same card, more so than the ripple trick, I think Locket of Yesterday's is why this card has two blue mana in it's casting cost instead of 2 colorless, or else you could be drawing 2 for 1 blue mana which would be pretty gross. In any case this will be a fun card to build decks around. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Theorist's Proxy

Saffgor: It’s worse than Malevolent Hermit, probably, but that’s okay. A bit of redundancy for when you jam key spells is fine, and the blocker+Jace token make it go card-neutral.

FromTheShire: Interestingly it seems like a few people are testing this out in the sideboard for Legacy Show and Tell and Sneak and Show, and in Simic Ritual in Modern. The appeal is certainly there with the ability.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty nice to force through a spell and pump your Jace or get a new Jace token, solid utility creature. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Theorist, Jace Beleren

Saffgor:This isn’t The One Ring, but a lot of times Jace will feel like it. Come down, remove the likely means of dealing with this Planeswalker for the first turn rotation via the -2, and then draw a card every turn he survives. The idea of a card draw engine which insulates itself is known to be strong, and this is very much along those lines. Great, spooky card.

FromTheShire: Well it's a whole lot of draw, let me tell you. This is one of the cards that people are trying to accelerate into with Gideon's Memorial, and boy does the game feel over if you don't have a way to deal with it right away. They can start pumping out vigilant 2/1's or bounce your best threat, and the whole time they're also out drawing you so good luck connecting with it.

BPhillipYork: That's definitely a ton of card draw, if you can defend it with cards like Moat or Sphere of Safety, then it'll go pretty bonkers. But that's really building a whole deck around defending planeswalkers. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Variable Chaser

Saffgor: I’ve warmed up to this card a great deal since its reveal—while not mandatory, being access to a Wheel of Fortune-lite you can blink to reset is very strong, and it’s a solid attacker/blocker as well. I think this sneaks into most decks playing Displacer Kitten in 2 or fewer colors, but I’m okay being wrong about that.

FromTheShire: It's well known to be a good effect as long as you are set up to take advantage of it, even better if you can do it repeatedly.

BPhillipYork: Wow another prepared Wheel of Fortune, basically. Yes, it's slightly less good since it's optional, but wow. Only costing 3 mana is pretty nuts for something like this, yes it's 3 more to wheel, but just having it sit around is pretty solid. It's also an okay creature on its own, with flying and prowess. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Way of the Mind Sculptor

Saffgor: Most of the Ways are pretty sick, and this is no exception. It’s perhaps overcosted a tad, but so long as you’re drawing 4-5 cards from it I think it’s a worthwhile inclusion.

FromTheShire: It's definitely not cheap but it's quite the effect, especially if you can get it going on multiple walkers.

BPhillipYork: Definitely a really strong way to get more value out of planeswalkers, though a bit pricy.

Black

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Bloodline Recollector

Saffgor: ‘Each End Step’ is horrifying here, in a way I’m rather excited for. 3 deaths isn’t especially easy, but playing this after a board wipe could be a backbreaking line, on top of its use in token strategies to funnel useless 1/1s into cards in hand. If you’re ever triggering this 2x times a turn cycle, you’re in a winning position. By my money, it’s better than Emeritus of Ideation, but worse than Variable Chaser.

FromTheShire: Three creatures dying isn't nothing, but in aristocrats or Fleshbag Marauder style board control decks it should be doable, and then you get that sweet, sweet draw. Very powerful if you can get it going.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty nasty. Yes, it's 3 life, but in aristocrats decks, it's pretty doable to trigger over and over. If you pair this with cards that punish you for drawing it gets pretty mean fast. Or you can just be gaining lots of life, and use it to turbo draw cards.
Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Darklight Phoenix

Saffgor: This obviously does some crazy stuff with Breath of Fury, but so does Arclight Phoenix, and that’s never been an appetizing line. Instead, Darklight is far more like a take on Vengevine, and likely goes in the similar Modern deck of Dredge, over another recursive threat. Card’s good, but will it get to the point where Arclight was, headlining its own archetype in multiple formats? I don’t think so.

FromTheShire: It's a bigger ask than Arclight Phoenix but still quite achievable, this seems like it's going places. A shell hasn't been fleshed out yet that I have seen but we have certainly established that the raw power of this effect is very high.

BPhillipYork: This is solid for sacrifice and aristocrats decks, just a recurring creature. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dark Matter Manipulator

Saffgor: In formats with better self-mill this guy can swing for 3 on turn 2, but I feel as though he doesn’t scale quickly enough to matter. Like a vastly worse Serra Ascendant in Commander.

FromTheShire: Extremely solid amount of self mill to get you started with your one drop, and while it may not routinely get bigger than a 3/2 there will be games where you're looking at a 1 mana 7/2 which is quite the rate.

BPhillipYork: Solid self mill card. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Extrapolate the Impossible

Saffgor: Not Commander playable, literally, but in Modern you can play enough redundant Pestermite-style effects to have this be a tutor for that. Twin feels like this thing's only potential home. Any combo piece for whom there’s substantial redundancy, this finds.

FromTheShire: Completely dead in Commander, this is an incredible tutor in 60 card. 2 mana to get one of 2 removal spells, or board wipes, or literally whatever else you need is excellent.

BPhillipYork: Well no wish boards in Commander, or it'd be fun. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Garruk, Veiled Butcher

Saffgor: A decade ago, this would be one of the best Planeswalkers of all time. A few years ago, it would have been good. Now? It’s fine. As the topend to a probably BGx midrange shell it gets rid of things in four ways, none of which are fast enough for the current meta.

FromTheShire: A bit on the costly side, but exiling creatures that would hit the yard can be extremely useful. It tries quite hard to protect itself, with all 3 modes offering it in some capacity. Doesn't go in just any deck but for midrange or control I could see including a couple of copies.

BPhillipYork: This is an interest stax planeswalker, and another replace death with exile. His +2 is solid, and the sacrifice is solid too, so all around pretty useful, but you either need a way to protect him or to get instant value out of him. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Gideon the Oathless

Saffgor: This guy punishes opponents well, but the damage likely won’t add up fast enough to matter in Commander. In Standard, he could maybe see Sideboard play as an answer to go-wide? Just not impressive.

FromTheShire: Always good to get more redundancy of this type of effect, and tacking on planeswalker hate is nice for Standard. Brutal against the token decks, or my dumb Goblins lists. I don't know that he does enough into the rest of the meta to see much play though, might be more of a sideboard card. Or it could be that pushing through 3-4 extra damage over the course of a game is just what you need.

BPhillipYork: Stax Gideon is interesting, though this is just a repeated ping, for 3-mana and with the ward this is very defensible choice to just grind your opponents down. Shutting down planeswalkers is fine but not very relevant in Commander. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lich's Relic

Saffgor: Baffled this is a Rare. Genuinely feels out of place in the set, even if it’s probably decent all things considered.

FromTheShire: Primarily a removal spell that also happens to have an Equipment stapled on to it, you're never casting this without the kicker. Especially with it being able to hit planeswalkers as well it's an excellent rate in a full Commander pod.

BPhillipYork: This is a weird card to me. 3 mana to destroy 3 creatures or planeswalkers and you get a mediocre piece of equipment out of it? And if you have a way to recur equipment it gets even meaner, since it's an enters play trigger as opposed to some kind of additional casting cost you can just recur with various Goblin or Boros tricks. So, really strong. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Liliana the Repentant

Saffgor: One of the better cards in the whole set, Lili here mills you fast, and I mean really fast. No ‘nontoken’ in that textbox means you can absolutely tear through the yard, and combined with a Bridge from Below there’s even some combos to consider. At her very worse though, she’s one of the best self-mill enablers in the game, with an inefficient reanimate for the lategame.

FromTheShire: Most likely you're not going to activate her exhaust ability in Standard but if things go pear shaped it's not a completely wild ask. More importantly, she does a heck of a job of filling your yard, though it's possible that since she doesn't trigger off of herself she doesn't make the cut with how many good pieces those decks already have. It really feels like she should though.

BPhillipYork: Good self-mill, presumably set up to reanimate something specific. For Commander that's a bit dicey given the 1 of format and large deck size, but if you've got a number of useful targets, or just want to fill your yard it's a low cost way to keep milling away. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Loot, the Anomaly

Saffgor: This is the kind of little freak that Reality Fracture is all about. He’s not good, but you can definitely Agatha’s a Hyalopterous Lemure under him and swing out for game. It’s not about being good though, it’s about being weird, and in that domain Black Loot excels.

FromTheShire: This is an interesting commander to do lower tier Voltron with, especially since it gives you a reason to play with Hyalopterous Lemure and Viscid Lemures, noted cards I'm sure you're all familiar with. Stuff them in an Agatha's Soul Cauldron and now you're one shot K.O.-ing people. Dumb? Yes. Fun? Also yes.

BPhillipYork: Yay for weird rules conundrums. There's definitely lots of ways to pump down his power, and sort of a fun thing to build, if not amazingly good. Sort of strange that WotC have devolved to the point where they just have a card that's "the McGuffin". Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Mabel, Bitter Recluse

Saffgor: If this said permanent, we'd be talking, but as it stands Mabel's just not going to compare with something like Vampire Hexmage.

FromTheShire: I'm on the fence with this one, she's a rattlesnake that can also snipe planeswalkers for one mana, but without knowing how the meta is going to shake out it's hard to say if that will be worth much. Maybe she offers some upside as a ninjutsu piece.

BPhillipYork: There's a few fun things you could remove with this, things that uptick with negative counters, or use it in conjunction with blight. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Multiply by Zero

Saffgor: I'll buck the trend and say this is mediocre, about comparable to a Go for the Throat. Most things that have Indestructible in Commander are Voltron-adjacent, and probably also have counters or Equipment/Auras.

FromTheShire: Solid removal spell with the upside of being able to deal with indestructible creatures, I like it.

BPhillipYork: Wow that's really strong creature control. Really just removes anything that is targetable, basically can't be protected. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Overwrite the Multiverse

Saffgor: Another way to Empower Jace for 8+ for Kasmina, or push you forward for Reality Sculptor. Great card if you’re in the colors for it, and playing towards one of those endgames. Bad elsewhere.

FromTheShire: A mass exile sweeper in black is awesome to get access to. Oh, and I guess it also makes a big Jace token after, sure.

BPhillipYork: Well that's quite a strong board clear, though expensive. I feel like overwriting the universe should really require all 5 colors, and to have it be pure black is just kind of lazy. Like WotC doesn't understand the implications of their own IP, but given the profusion of nonsensical and lazy Universes Beyond, then obviously they don't, or they just don't really care. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Rise of the Deathbringer

Saffgor: 5 mana Instant-speed -3/-3 is a ruthless profile for control, even disregarding the draw mode. Being able to hold up mana for a not-insignificant board wipe like that will dunk midrange, and maybe even aggro if you’re on a little ramp. The draw mode is worse than the original Return of the Wildspeaker, but I think this card on the whole is better.

FromTheShire: Massive amounts of card draw or a mini board wipe at instant speed, what's not to like?

BPhillipYork: This is a fractured version of Return of the Wildspeaker and it's probably stronger, though regular return is a solid finisher, this is a nice modal spell too. -3/-3 is generally going to clear almost everything off the board, or you can just card draw. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Sanctum Lurker

Saffgor: I’m shocked this isn’t Legendary, because it feels like the kind of wacky text they’d aim to place on a Commander. That being said, as a card this requires your opponents play Creature removal and not simply bowl over your Planeswalkers with damage, and that’s reasonable insulation for something like a Gideon of the Trials. You could probably get some mileage out of creating a ‘Secret Commander’ deck with this at the helm, though the actual Commander would need to put in some work, like Aminatou.

FromTheShire: Horror is right. If you can actually stick this and protect it you immediately turn off the whole issue with planeswalkers in Commander, namely that they usually are cast, activate once, and then die. Notably loyalty can't go negative, so no matter how hard they get hit your walkers will happily keep sitting at 0, ready to use either the plus granted here or their built in ones to start ticking right back up into problematic territory.

BPhillipYork: Yeah I don't like this really, it just overwrites the rules of the game and it has potential to just let you keep stax planeswalkers sitting around. Some of the best early ones cost only 3 like Ashiok, Dream Render, and if they just won't die then that's really rough. Thankfully it's a creature so relatively easy to remove. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Teyo, Diamondblade Mage

Saffgor: Giving weird Enchantments Deathtouch can result in repeatable, Instant-speed boardwipes. That’s extremely cool, as is the capacity to turbo out Black Planeswalkers’ ultimates, which tend to win the game on resolution. The issue stems from the fact that Black Teyo is 4 mana, unlike his 2-mana White counterpart, which is a shame. Were this cheaper, I’d honestly be writing a Commander Focus on the guy, because this effect genuinely is novel. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance

Saffgor: This is just worse than other Tinybones-es, though in Standard it could perhaps be a piece for discard?

FromTheShire: If you build around him Tinybones is going to be more than a pocket sized nuisance. Even when he drops he's making 3 people discard a card and dealing 3 damage, and then his discard is not keyed to only when opponents discard, so if you want to you can teach your opponents all about cleanup step looping strategies as you ping them to death.

BPhillipYork: Wow. Really solid 3-drop, pinging people for discarding and doing it when he enters. All the Tinybones in conjunction are starting to get really mean and have a lot of synergy. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Vraska's Final Mercy

Saffgor: {B}{B} cost and Empower 6 are both really unfortunate breakpoints, which likely precludes this card from being played in the Islands-matter Jace shell. Good in Limited, at least.

FromTheShire: Sorcery speed is a bummer, but the rate on killing a planeswalker or making a big Jace token is quite good.

BPhillipYork: Good creature control for 2 mana, and there's modal empowerment of Jace if you want that. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Way of the Necromancer

Saffgor: My pick for the best Way Enchantment, Way of the Necromancer is absolutely wild. Tons of Black Planeswalkers make chaff bodies you can throw under an Altar, and Bx decks in general have an aristocrats bent. This is cheap, at bare minimum likely cantrips, and can help you push for endgames with deathloops or ultimates way ahead of schedule. It’s shocking they’re printing this card, and it goes to show the expected power of Planeswalkers in modern Magic is defined by Commander, and not 60-card. In the format that is aggressively hostile to the card type, the designers are doing their damndest to make them playable. Is this really what it takes?

FromTheShire: Good Christmas that's a lot of counters. Even if you only have a few walkers out using their abilities to create creature tokens which you can then use to block to protect your squad and refill their loyalty it means it's going to be an uphill climb to deal with your board. However, we're in black, aka the color of sacrificing and reanimation loops, and so in reality this is going to read as 'Ultimate each planeswalker you control', and that tends to win games.

BPhillipYork: This is one of the ways to easily ult off by sacrificing a bunch of creatures for value to ult out something you just dropped, like a Liliana Vess, in which case everything just pops back into play.
