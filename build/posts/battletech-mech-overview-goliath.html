BattleTech Mech Overview: Goliath

November 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, MechWarriors! This week we're talking about the walking tank which is the Goliath, an 80-ton quad mech which dates back to the earliest days of BattleTech!

Goliath. Credit: Valk.

That's right, we're not talking about StarCraft mecha, roided-out Necromundans, or an industrial vehicle commandeered by Genestealer Cultists, we're talking about the other-other-other Goliath, the one with four legs! If you haven't dealt with quad mechs before, I recommend taking a quick look at the pros and cons recapped in my Scorpion Overview, though the Goliath stands in a somewhat different position than the Scorpion simply by virtue of being slower.

80 tons isn't as awkward an interstitial weight bracket as 40 or 60 are, but the Goliath doesn't carry its weight as well as it might, largely on the back of art-adherence inertia giving it a pair of LRM 10s as secondary weapons in many of its variants. Those... aren't the most impressive weapons for a mech to be strongly associated with, though at least they can deploy Thunder Missile minefields still under Core Rules, unlike the poor LRM 5.

Anyway, let's see how Goliaths stack up against our TTB ranking system!

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘Mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

GOL-1H

That's the Terran Hegemony H, not the Marian Hegemony H; we are not starting with a funny rocket variant here. No, this is the original Goliath! It moves 4/6 and mounts a pretty good load of armor, notably thick on the rear and a bit light on the front legs (apparently due to construction rules at the time treating quad front legs as arms!). For weapons we have a PPC, a pair of LRM 10s, and a pair of machine guns, cooled by 17 single heat sinks. It builds 1+ movement heat on an alpha strike, definitely manageable for a long-range IntroTech mech.

At 1,442 BV this mech is aggressively mediocre. There aren't many non-Primitive BattleMechs which carry this much armor for this cheap, and the Awesome 8R is the only one of those which can really compete with the GOL-1H for range, but this mech is extremely vulnerable within its minimum ranges, and I don't really want to be paying this much for Assault Mech armor and structure only to leave my Goliath firmly in the back line, not when it's competing with stuff like the Charger 1A5, Banshee 3MC, or even BLR-1G-DC.

Oh, and the LRM ammo is in the CT. It's no longer an instant-death button there with Core's changes to ammo explosions, but it's still a bit uncomfy!

Lynn's Rating: D+.

GOL-2H

...okay, this one is the funny rocket variant, though it originated from Circinus this time!

The Goliath 2H takes a 1H, swaps to an incrementally thicker coat of ferro-fibrous armor, and drops the MGs and their ammo for six Rocket Launcher 10s. The full set of six RL 10s swaps one-for-one for the LRMs + PPC in terms of heat build, so what you're gaining here is basically the ability to threaten something inside your LRM minimum range with a boatload of inaccurate damage once, in exchange for your ability to deal with the Perfidious Conventional Infantryman.

I'd say in most cases that's a slight improvement, but you pay for it (and the slight armor bump) with a shift up to 1,583 BV. I'm not particularly impressed.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

GOL-3L

Alphabetic order has already taken us from the Star League to the Early Republic! This Capellan variant is a weird one. An up-rated XL engine gets it to 5/8 speed, and it mounts a full coat of Stealth Armor. The requisite ECM suite is stuffed into the head via a Small Cockpit, which is at least less-bad on a quad than a biped. Weapons swap to an ER PPC, two MML 7s (two of their three tons of ammo CASE'd in the left torso, the third free-balling in the CT), and an MPL in the CT. A base 10 Double Heat Sinks are... suboptimal for this weapons load, I'll say. There are workarounds, but if you're at range with the Stealth running you're basically just shooting two MML 7s if you don't want to get into heat penalties, which isn't impressive for 1,708 BV.

The main issues here are Stealth being overcosted in BV alongside the use of Stealth a distance-closing tool. The 3L has vastly improved close-range performance and the speed to reach short range, but once it gets there the Stealth Armor is just wasted BV. I don't hate this mech, it's extremely funny and has some genuine upsides relative to other Goliaths, but I can't quite love the total package.

Lynn's Rating: C-.

Titan Tactical Brigade Goliath. Credit: Jack Hunter

GOL-3M

The Helm Renaissance variant, the 3M takes a 1H, swaps to an XL engine, and trades the PPC for a Gauss Rifle and an incrementally thicker coat of Standard Armor. Notably, it does not drop any of its heat sinks in this trade, so it's suddenly very over-sinked. The XL + Gauss + LRM 10 eat all the crit space in the right torso, so the right torso MG gets pushed to the CT, where it's joined by a ton of inert Gauss ammo, and all the LRM ammo moves to the CASE'd LT... though unfortunately the MG ammo also doubles to two full tons in the process!

This is 1,537 BV, and that's not the worst rate for a Gauss on a well-armored 4/6 chassis. The 3M's certainly usable, but it's not exciting.

Lynn's Rating: C.

GOL-3M2

This is exactly a 3M which drops both MGs and their ammo for Small Pulse Lasers, no other changes. This does go to 1,631 BV without really giving the mech enough close-range defense to deter much of anyone, but it is an objective improvement.

Lynn's Rating: C, but a better C than the 3M's C.

GOL-3S

This Civil-War-era Lyran variant uses a Light Engine and thins the armor (particularly on the rear torsos and the rear legs). Where does the weight go? Well, it drops down to one LRM 10, tacks Artemis IV onto it, and swaps everything else for an LB 20-X autocannon, an ER Large Laser, and three ER Medium Lasers (one in the head and one in each front leg). Both tons of LRM 10 ammo and 2/4ths of the LBX ammo are in the CASE'd RT, but the other two tons of autocannon rounds are split across the rear legs. Heat sinks are a base 10 DHS, so firing the LB 20-X and the three ERMLs together builds 1+ Movement when targets get in close.

This makes a very clear decision to swap long-range ability for short-range threat. 4/6 movement with an assault mech's armor should be enough to get this mech to that short range, and its damage output is decent, if not stellar, for 1,792 BV, but its low-ish speed and nature as a quad means it would be easy to outmaneuver, and there are a lot of explosives in here.

Lynn's Rating: C-.

GOL-4S

This uses the same Light Fusion Engine, Standard Everything Else, and heat sinks that the 3S uses, but makes changes to most everything else. It's got a Heavy Gauss Rifle, one LRM 10 (no Artemis this time), and two ERMLs (in the RT). Both side torsos are CASE'd and the HGauss ammo is all crammed into the rear legs, giving some crit padding, and armor coverage is back up to near-full, though it's heavier on the front and thinner on the rear than the early Goliaths. 1,912 BV.

This one I like. It's gotten a little bit comparatively worse with Core, since firing Heavy Gauss on the move is easier for bipeds now, and it's got a touch of a range band problem (the HGauss hitting Short at the LRM's Minimum, the ERMLs hitting Short at the HGauss's Minimum), and it's another Goliath which hates close combat, but it has good output at a six hex range, can weather a good amount of hate, and a Fully Armed And Operational Heavy Gauss Rifle is just always fun (except when you miss repeatedly and have to confine an ever-increasing number of dice to dice jail, anyway).

As an aside, weapon explosion damage is all applied in the location where the critical hit occurred, if the weapon's critical slots spill over into a different location. This means that any LT Heavy Gauss crit is contained by CASE, but if either CT Heavy Gauss crit slot is the first slot hit, you're looking at 20 damage unshielded to the CT... not lethal on a TAC, but definitely a big ouch.

Lynn's Rating: C+.

Dawn Guard Goliath. Credit: Jack Hunter

GOL-5D

While the Lyrans were developing the 3S and 4S in the Civil War, this is what the Davions were doing with their Goliaths. It's got full armor (albeit with the same 8/12/8 rear coverage the 4S has), a standard engine, and a weapons load featuring a RAC/5, four ERMLs (one in the head and three in the RT), a targeting computer, and four MGs, two front-facing in the CT and two rear-facing in the rear legs. Three tons of RAC/5 ammo and a half ton of MG ammo live in a CASE-protected bin in the LT, next to the RAC. 12 DHS mean an alpha strike with the RAC at full spin only builds 2+movement heat.

BV is up to 1,975 with this one and range is down, but I like this one... I just wish its rear armor was a little thicker, given quads' propensity for getting backstabbed. Still, it's arguably the best Goliath we've seen so far, even though it's also the first that can't do damage in any cluster larger than 5.

Lynn's Rating: C+.

GOL-5W

W is for Word of Blake! This one has the same Standard Fusion Engine and armor layout as the 5D, but the armor swaps to Stealth, powered by a Guardian ECM in the CT. A Small Cockpit makes room for a C3i computer in the head, while all of the weapons move to the RT, two Plasma Rifles and three regular Medium Lasers. Fifteen DHS give this a very coherent heat curve: Either you build Movement Heat firing both Plasma Rifles with Stealth running or you turn the Stealth off and Alpha Strike for Movement -1. Drop something or other when you need to avoid penalties, preferably one of the MLs.

This is shockingly sane for the Blakists. Putting every single weapon in a single location is a little eyebrow-raising, but it's a full-armored mech with no explosives, at least that location isn't going to fall off easily, and you can get an alright charge off with this chassis if you need to (although if the RT is gone, so is the Right Front Leg, which doesn't exactly help).

BV is 2,006 for less overall damage output than either the 4S or 5D, but I think I'd be willing to pay it, possibly even in a network.

Lynn's Rating: B-.

GOL-6H

Good lord, this is the kind of weapons list that looks less sane than it is.

Another Periphery special, the 6H takes a 1H, normalizes the legs to 29 armor each, installs a Light Engine, swaps the PPC for a Heavy PPC, leaves the LRM 10s alone, swaps both MGs for six LMGs in two Arrays of three each, and mounts a B-Pod (explosive point-blank protection against Battle Armor leg / swarm attacks) in each leg. 12 Double Heat Sinks mean this builds +1 heat on a running alpha strike. All the ammo is CASE'd in the LT.

This is basically everything a 1H is but more, with an increased price tag to match, going to 1,681. It's still extremely poorly-defended against anything inside its minimum ranges that isn't infantry, keeping its bulk back from the front line. This kind of mech works better in lore than in the average game, IMO.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

Marik Purple Goliath. Credit: Peri

(Peri, friend, is that a Ral Partha Unseen Goliath with its entire lower body on backwards? Because that's what it looks like.)

GOL-6M

Heyyy, the Quad Turret is here! And it's in the Core Rules now, and tech levels aren't a thing anymore, so TOs won't blanket-ban this for no good reason! Long story short on Quad Turrets is that the weapons in one of a quad mech's side torso locations can be mounted to a turret so they can point in any direction, which goes a long way toward answering quads' weakness to being outflanked. The turret does eat up some tonnage, and of course there's limited space for weapons in a single side torso, and there's a crit slot associated with the turret itself, with the turret jamming in the last direction it pointed in if that slot gets hit, but I still think they're a major boon to the viability of quad mechs.

I think this is one of the Goliaths that does the best job of "being a Goliath," though its speed is a problem, and it definitely lives in the shadow of the fact that it later gets a Clan-Spec upgrade with a targeting computer and ATMs. It's got the same armor layout as the 1H and still carries a standard engine, but it slows to 3/5 movement and picks up a Compact Gyro. For weapons, it mounts a gauss rifle and an MML 7 in its turret, with a second MML 7 in the other torso, alongside the CASE-protected ammo. The legs mount an odd checkerboard pattern of lasers, with the Front Right and Rear Left mounting an ERML each and the Front Left and Rear Right mounting a Small VSP each, with both lasers in the front legs pointing forward and both lasers in the rear legs pointing back. Heat is basically no concern unless you're firing both the front and rear lasers simultaneously, and even then it's manageable.

At longer range this is just a weird, slow side-grade on the 3M, but it can defend itself up close to a significantly greater degree than most Goliaths, and I think that's overall worth the price bump to 1,742 BV.

Lynn's Rating: B-.

GOL-7C

This Dark Age Goliath marries near-full Light Ferro-Fibrous armor coverage (notably keeping the rear side torsos at the 10 pip threshold while still thickening the front side torso armor) with a Light Fusion Engine back at the normal 4/6 speed and a new weapons load of an Ultra AC/10, two ER Medium Lasers, and two Streak SRM 6s. With 12 DHS you only go up +4 heat on a running alpha strike if both Streaks lock.

There's a LOT of ammo in this mech for the weapons it mounts (two tons of Streak 6, four tons of Ultra 10), but it's all protected by CASE II. This weapons load feels like it wants to be on a slightly faster mech, but in my experience 4/6 assault mechs usually don't have a problem getting in close when there are objectives in play.

As always, you need to be cagey in close combat with a quad that doesn't have a turret, and this one will set you back 1,790 BV, but this seems like a solid entry in the "budget assault" category.

Lynn's Rating: B.

GOL-7K

This Kuritan Dark Age variant mounts a Light Engine with a Supercharger for a 4/6[8] profile and an almost-but-not-quite-full coat of standard armor. For weapons it has a Heavy PPC with a Capacitor, two MRM 10s with Apollo Fire Control, two machine guns, and five M-Pods. Heat is slightly spicy if you fire a charged HPPC shot alongside the MRMs, but not really a concern.

M-Pods are funny Solaris weapons which are basically one-shot LB 15-Xs with point-blank range. I absolutely adore them, but they are strange to see on this (not Solaris) mech, to say the least. I'll note that I see nothing to indicate that Pod weapons don't have facing (even though the smaller pod weapons are built to attack things in their own hex, and M-Pods are still capable of doing this), so even though two of the 7K's M-Pods are mounted on the rear legs, since they aren't noted as rear-facing I can only guess that they still fire forward. I should also note that, as I mentioned in the Whitworth Overview and its comments section, Saturation Mode is basically useless on MRM 10s, so the Apollo here is just giving us normal cluster rolls.

I want to like this mech, but the Heavy PPC is a bizarre non-bo with everything else that's going on here. The MRMs and M-Pods and MGs and Supercharger say "I want to rush up in your face," but the biggest weapon here has a minimum range that kicks in at the MRMs' Short range. With PPC Capacitor and Supercharger tax, you're looking at 1,901 BV for this, and I just don't think it's worth that.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

GOL-7R

In the running for "most boring Recognition Guide variant," the Dark-Age-era 7R uses a Light Fusion Engine to buy weight for a near-full coat of Light Ferro-Fibrous armor, a Gauss Rifle, a pair of ERMLs, the customary normal-ass LRM 10s, and CASE II on both side torsos, with base 10 DHS meaning it builds +1 heat on a running alpha strike.

It's a 3M2 but better at fighting anything other than the Perfidious Conventional Infantryman. It costs 1,888 BV. God I'm tired of LRM 10s by this point.

Lynn's Rating: C+.

Goliath C

The headline here is that the Wolf Empire refit a BattleMech other than the Hunchback without completely screwing it up.

I know, I'm as surprised as you are!

Take a 6M. Strip all the weapons. Put a Gauss Rifle, an ER Large Laser, and a Micro Pulse Laser, all Clan grade, in the turret. Put an ATM 9 in the other side torso and a CERML in the head. All four legs get B-Pods. Both side torsos get CASE II, protecting the Gauss and all the ammo (except the single ton of inert Gauss ammo that gets shoved down the rear left pant leg). Shove a Targeting Computer in the CT.

This isn't perfect - it only has two tons of ammo for the ATM (take Standard and HE), it's still slow and slightly light on armor (mostly on the front legs and the front side torsos), and it builds 2+ Movement heat without the Micro Pulse firing - but it's a really solid refit with capabilities well worth its most-expensive-Goliath 2,227 BV price tag. Its photo is in the yearbook next to the 6M as "The Cooler 6M."

Lynn's Rating: A-.

Goliath. Credit: Rockfish

Final Thoughts

80-ton assault mechs. Quad mechs. Mechs that are associated with the Free Worlds League. Mechs with a distinctive (if silly) look.

The Goliath sits at the center of such a manifold Venn Diagram of what I like, and I have tried so hard to love it, but damn do the unenhanced Spheroid LRM 10s of it all let a ton of its variants down. They're absolutely playable, and get noticeably better once the Civil War (and, out of universe, Project Phoenix) hits, but they don't feel noteworthy relative to peer competition except where they step outside the lines of what a basic Goliath-shaped mech looks like at least a little.

Until next time, MechWarriors, don't forget to walk your big tank-shaped dog!
