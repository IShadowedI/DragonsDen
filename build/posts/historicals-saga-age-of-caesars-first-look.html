Historicals: SAGA Age of Caesars First Look

July 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

The newest SAGA age is here from Studio Tomahawk, and we (Bair and Lenoon) are very very excited. It's time to don your hamata, squamata, plumata, or segmentata, grab your pilum and get down to business with our first look at this new, decidedly Roman, age. You'll note in this article we're pretty light on model photos - amazingly, not many of us have ancients armies! This is shortly about to change, because this book is absolutely fantastic.

This is a very, very Roman book, with eight new boards (and two Old Friends New Enemies) charting the Roman Empire and it's enemies from somewhere around 100BCE to 300ADish. This is an age of heavy infantry, and 5 out of the 8* of the boards can take (in one way or another) the signature unit of the book: Legionaries. Legionaries sit somewhere between hearthguard and warriors - armour 5/5, aggression 1 and six bodies to a point (three to generate a Saga dice), they’re the beating heart of three factions and a nice bonus for another two. Lots of special rules play off the Legionary unit type, and they work well to represent the organisation, fighting ability and flexibility of the Roman legionary. 

*more or less - either natively, via ruses or something eerily similar 

It’s not just infantry either, as the majority of boards include the heaviest cavalry in Saga, the mighty Cataphracts. Age of Caesars has both camel and horse varieties and they hit like a brick, especially in boards set up around them. If your board doesn’t let you natively take cataphracts guess what? There’s mercenaries that fill the gap. Every army bar rebels against Rome can, somehow, take very very heavy cavalry. 

Making up the trifecta, the manuballista (portable ballista) is also found in a surprising number of lists, either as a core unit or brought in via ruses. You’ll need it too - nearly everyone in this book gets access to some kind of unit in very heavy armour, and here’s one of your can openers (Dacians are *all* can opener, all the time). 

What’s Good in This Book?

Theming

There's a lot of clever use of SAGA abilities and core rules to get across just enough of the flavour of how the factions fought in real life to give you a satisfying historicals glow. Roman auxilia will do best on the flanks, Dacians love a bit of siege warfare, the Germans are terrifying monsters from beyond the fringes of empire, it’s all very clever stuff that gives this book a real hefty feel to it.

Faction Range

This is one that’s good in every SAGA age, but Caesars takes it a bit further. There’s not only a lot of variation between boards (even the Roman factions fight differently), but a lot you can do within them too. With great support from Ruses, most of the armies here can select radically different units, pivoting between mounted, foot and occasional monsters (elephants or naked guys).

Innovative Mechanics

It wouldn’t be a SAGA age without picking a few boards that break most of the core rules, and here we’ve got extra (very flavourful) mechanics galore. Nothing too difficult to wrap your head around but terror, leaders, formations and rampant corruption all play with your processing in interesting new ways, forcing you to focus in on ability hierarchies and order of operations.

We’ll touch on this below but one faction making use of two different kinds of SAGA dice at once allowing you a lot of control and flexibility in what you’re doing is especially interesting. 

Internal Balance Between Factions

This will take a few games to really be absolutely sure of but it seems that every faction does their own thing and has some counter play to what other factions are doing at the same time. None of the factions stick out to me as being above the rest for what should be a very even, and very interesting, playing field. There’s a lof of high armour units and a lot of heavy cavalry but there are also a lot of abilities to give extra attacks, gain bonuses to attack dice, and so on. Dacians especially are the “can opener” faction with heavy weapons and battle board abilities that will make it even easier to take out those heavily armoured foes.

What’s Not So Good?

Age of Caesars is a little light on some of the fan favourite extras - Mercenaries and Old Friends New Enemies! With only two OFNE factions, both using the Gauls from Age of Hannibal (with no mention of a single village holding out against the might of Rome), most of the heavy lifting for weird and unique army composition is instead found in the main battle boards.

Credit: Jackie Daytona

Mercenaries might not be as thin on the ground as I thought initially (7 units), but they feel a bit unimaginative - mostly a version of slightly choppier warriors, and dogs. There’s nothing (I think, I’m happy to be corrected) quite as cool and weird as mercenaries in some of the other ages. This feels extra light following on from Age of Chivalry which had the largest number of mercenary units of any supplement before it. 

Faction Overviews

Caesar’s Legions

Republican Roman Velites. Credit: MommaNegan

The most flexible and internally variable board in the book, The Caesar’s Legions represent the dying days of the republic after the transition from the quadruple-line maniple system (as seen in Age of Hannibal) right up until Augustus. That means you have a core of Legionaries, and options to take “Allied” troops from one of three Roman regions - Africa, Asia (effectively the Hellenic world) and Gaul. Each brings something substantially different - peltasts and sarissas from Greece, Elephants and Mounted Archers from Africa and mounted Hearthguard and Warriors for Gaul. Just about any (and every) weapon option can be picked up here, letting you tailor your list to how you like to play while keeping an iron core of legionaries. Unusually you also have three options for leader - a Consul (standard warlord), Tribune (multiple activations but can’t fight) and a Legate (somewhere between the two). 

Key Ability: Sesterce

Aside from all the options, your key mechanic is rampant corruption, letting you put sesterce (money!) down on your units to give them access to one-time boosts to Saga abilities. The Boosts are very, very good - the board generally looks solid, but with the support of naked corruption, the legions hit very very hard and shrug off a lot of attacks.

Key Unit: Legionaries

Imperial Romans

My first "test" legionaries on a home-made 1x4 sabot bases. The card-stock upper is almost exactly the same thickness as the fender washers on which the minis are based, so everything bleeds well and looks great on the table. (credit: Ilor)

Studio tomahawk clearly looked across at GW for these ones, because the imperial Roman board lays like space marines (or maybe marines play like romans). You set chapter tactics(formations) and your elite legions march up the board putting everyone to the pilum or gladius. This looks like a very strong, very simple board where a block of legionaries anchors flanking auxilia and centurion heroes keep it all together. The formation rule and saga abilities that key off it make you tough, elite and very saga dice efficient. If you’re playing an Age of Caesars event, expect to face imperial romans, and know how to counter them (Dacians is probably the answer). 

Key Ability: Formations

Imperial Romans are able to set formations that affect multiple units if set up correctly, giving them board-wide buffs like maneuver closer than L, or free shooting attacks before a charge. They’re very, very good and provide a solid and dice efficient way to support an area of the board.

Key Unit: Legionaries

Dacians

Dacians Attacking a roman fort, from Trajan's Column. Credit: Wikipedia

As I wrote earlier these are can openers, when Legionaries and Cataphracts are the cans they’re opening. They get heavy weapons, cataphracts, composite bow warriors, and can even take up to two manuballistas for good measure. While many units are limited to just one and you need to take as many heavy weapon warriors as no-option warriors, you’re going to end up with a very cool mix of units on the board that each get to make use of battle board abilities amplifying their killing potential. They have abilities that add automatic hits, give +1 bonus to attacks with bows, forcing your opponent to re-roll successful defense dice and just flat out removing enemy attack dice too. These guys are all about killing as quickly as possible, objectives can come later. Their other ruse unit is a pack of warrior dogs (8 models) with aggression 2 when attacking and Determination allowing them to wander the flanks on their own to good effect drawing out enemy shooting. 

Key Ability: Siege Tactics

A two dice uncommon and rare advanced saga ability as an activation that lets you shoot with a unit and then charge the unit you just shot at that generates no fatigue if you managed to kill a model with the shooting attack. The enemy is not allowed to close ranks and suffers a further -1 to their attack dice as well! A great combination for when you want to charge in with heavy weapon warriors or hearthguard effectively bringing their armour back up to the normal value while removing their ability to be safe. 

Key unit: Heavy Weapon Warriors 

Germans

Ancient Germanic Warriors. Credit: Victrix

The big bad barbarians across the Rhine are a fast, hard hitting combat board with a leader focused mechanic that powers up their saga abilities to unleash genuine bloody hell. Battle board abilities are straightforward, powerful and hit like a sack full of legionary heads after the Teutoburg forest incident. You’ll hit War Cry every turn, Relentless Hunt to get your entire army within charge range then Cherusci Treason to gain a terrifying amount of rerolls in melee. Rinse, repeat and there’ll be bugger all left of the enemy at the end. Facing a German board without a chunky ranged deterrent is going to be, as the Romans said, tempus culus stridens.

Key Ability: Relentless Hunt

As a combat-heavy army, the Germans need mobility and Relentless hunt provides it in spades. While giving everyone S isn’t the same as giving several units a free big move (like the Norman board, for instance), it’s important that it applies to everyone, because you want everyone in combat at once! You’re using this not just to make charges, but to make multiple charges into the same unit, getting close enough that a standard M of movement can get two units into a single enemy one in succession. Wipe them out and leave no survivors, fatigue bomb their neighbours and then charge in there too!

Key Unit: Fanatics

Rebels Against Rome

A truly unique faction here, with no hearthguard, no warlord and the potential to take absolutely crap civilians (the Follower unit) in groups of 18! There's some really interesting stuff here in the form of Leaders (almost-warlords who can be brought back into units after death), flexible warrior units and giant, fairly poor units of Followers. The board is interesting - there's a lot of potential to recycle your dice, chuck out surprisingly effective melee rounds and, occasionally, bring back your dead leaders into newly victorious units. However, and confession time here: This looks interesting but my brain is too smooth to process it. I wonder how competitive this one will be - either there's something here I'm not seeing (extremely possible), or this is an interesting and unique faction, but one that will have the crap kicked out of it on the tabletop.

Key Ability: Freed from the Yoke of Tyranny

There's a couple that could be key ones, but the big one for me is Freed from the Yoke of Tyranny, allowing you for the low low cost of one common or uncommon dice, recycle two inactive saga dice onto your board when the enemy warlord activates or uses we obey. You'll always be able to pop a dice on this, and it'll be a very rare opponent that doesn't do one or the other, letting you stack the board in your turn and then put dice on key relevant abilities in your opponents.

Key Unit: Warriors (Bair), Followers (Lenoon)

Parthians

A section of the Parthian Monument in the Ephesos Museum in Vienna, likely commemorating the Roman-Parthian War of 161-166. Credit: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 License

Shoot-Move-Shoot with the original masters of shooting a bow from a horse while riding away. The Parthians are all about cavalry, with Cataphracts (Horse and Camel versions), mounted composite bow warriors and camel-mounted ammo bearers. There’s a lot of support here to push a particular way of playing - disrupt and thin out with shooting, then crack out the Cataphracts to clean up. In my view it's a precision instrument that will take some real finesse to get the best out of, with incredibly hard hearthguard and highly mobile but very squishy warriors. As always with a majority composite bow armed faction, mastering how the bows work is the first step to mastering the board. With a ruse you can even take an elephant or the best Legionary unit in the book since it’s 8 models instead of only 6 and they have Determination.

Key Ability: Loss of Leadership 

This is, for me, the best ability on the board as it’ll punish your opponent for spreading out. Pick an enemy unit more than M away from Warlord and within M of any of your composite bow units (like your elephant!) and they count as having the Mercenaries rule for the turn. This is an Orders or Orders/Reaction ability. Yes it’ll use up your Rare dice but switching off advanced saga abilities from a unit you want to pick on is incredibly powerful at the right time.

Key Unit: Arrow Bearers

A camel based, determination-equipped, support piece for your archers, the arrow bearer provides a small but solid bonus to each shooting attack within range. Shooting attacks tend to throw out fewer dice than close combat, so any boost is appreciated. Arrow Bearers could also be incredibly good ways to pick up objectives, and will be surprisingly durable with resilience (1). They’re also incredibly cheap to take, you get up to three of them, and each only replaces one.

Sarmatians

Sarmatians in Scale armour. Credit: WIkipedia

Another cavalry-focused army and board, the Sarmatians were an ancient Iranian nomadic steppe people, enemies of the Roman Empire often employed as mercenaries themselves. It’s also entirely historically accurate to have an army of Sarmatians of mixed men and women, or entirely of women. In game your warlord must be mounted on a horse with cataphract armour and your hearthguard are equipped the same. Warriors are also mounted (or not) either with composite bows or just melee weapons and levies have to have bows. By far this is the simplest force selection of any warband in this book! What you end up with is a terrifying mounted force with a battle board that is ruthlessly offensive and rewards a mix of cataphract and non-cataphract units for maximum efficiency. You likely end up spending 3 points on hearthguard, 2 on warriors with composite bows, and 1 levy unit to have some infantry on the board. 

Key Ability: Iron Barding

While many abilities on this board add to your killing capabilities this one helps your cataphract units actually arrive in enemy lines. Costing two common dice it’s very easy to get and can be used in either your turn or your opponents and lasts for the whole turn giving every single one of your Cataphract units +1 bonus to defence dice against shooting attacks and cancelling the first casualty they would suffer in melee. That keeps your Warlord and Hearthguard much safer as they move across the board and even hardier in combat. Use in your opponents turn early game and then in your own for safer combats when charging in. 

Key Unit: Hearthguard Cataphracts

The whole board is really built around this unit being as effective as possible. Similar to playing Milites Christi in the Age of Crusades you’re going to be investing heavily into your Hearthguard. 

Palmyrans

Palmyran Empress Zenobia, from Bad Squiddo. Annie, please make a camel mounted version!

The Palmyrans are probably up there for the shortest-lived polity ever to receive a SAGA Board (the War of the Roses guys give them a good go though), and here they’re represented by a kind of half way house between Romans and Eastern factions. There’s a lot of flexibility in what you can take, and their unique mechanic is to mix Eastern and Roman dice on the board. The same abilities can work two different ways depending on the dice you’re using, effectively giving you a double-sized saga board with a huge range of abilities. Eastern dice tend to move you, Roman dice tend to hurt the opponent. You’ll need to plan, carefully, what you’re going to use at the start of the game, allowing you an extra level of customisation above and beyond the usual unit size and equipment. Palmyran players who know their board and what abilities to use in each match up might be very hard to beat! 

Key Ability: Respect for Tradition

Using an Eastern dice to activate a unit with ranged weapons for a move, generating no fatigue if that move ends more than M from all enemy units. Use this on a unit of mounted composite bow warriors and you’re easily moving into range, shooting, and moving back to safety while generating zero fatigue on your unit, incredibly strong! Or you can use a common Roman dice to instead activate a unit without ranged weapons for a shooting attack of range M that generates no fatigue; being able to soften up an enemy unit before charging in with a unit that doesn’t normally have ranged weapons is always going to be good since it helps keep your unit safer. 

Key Unit: Warriors with Composite Bows and Mount (Camels) 

First off it’s just super cool to take warriors with composite bows on camels alongside cataphract camels. Second, the reason this unit is great is it meshes with the board well and for two ruse points you can give one unit of them Determination for the whole game, an incredibly strong ability on a composite bow unit that will be making great use of Respect for Tradition mentioned above! 

Alea SAGA Iacta Est

This is, unquestionably, a hugely flavourful and interesting Saga age book - possibly the best one so far in terms of theming and mechanical variety. It's an excellent on-ramp to Saga too, with a wide range of different styles of army within the one book, and allows you to pick up some of the most common miniature ranges (Romans - who doesn't make them?) before expanding out into the complementary ages of Hannibal and Alexander. We can't wait to get this one on the table - remember though, victory goes to the one who takes pains!
