Tabletop Battles Reviews Warhammer The Old World: Arcane Journal River of Bones

October 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Falcon: As 2026 comes to an end, James Workshop has blessed us Old World players with our final Arcane Journal of the year, and at first glance it is a bit of a mixed bag. River of Bones has a surprising amount of content, with roughly 25 pages of lore and 20 pages of rules including two and a half new Armies of Infamy, the return of Mysterious terrain from previous editions (now called Enchanted), new cursed core magic items, and more Wood Elf kindreds. Yet, other than a couple of highlights it feels like this book might be the dreaded reverse Sum41: All Filler No Killer. That is a deep cut just for you Canadian millennials out there. Who knows though, just like my opinion on Sum41, I’ve been wrong before; so let’s dive into it and y’all can be the judge.

An Enchanting Addition

Previous editions of Warhammer Fantasy Battle prominently featured mystery terrain features, where forests, rivers, and ruins would alter the state of the game once the first model toed into their borders. Trees could be filled with snakes or awaken and slap unfortunate souls that chose to move too quickly through them. Rivers could enrage or heal the models that waded into their depths. Ruins could be haunted or cast random spells. For players that loved the anarchy, the narrative, or the layer of challenge they added to the game they were well-enjoyed, while others hated how they could tip the scales because they hated fun. Well, now all of that is back with the Enchanted terrain rule. This optional rule for your games causes all natural terrain features on the board to potentially have an additional effect that is rolled for when the first model enters the terrain piece. Unlike previous editions where there were separate tables for the different terrain types you could expect, enchanted terrain utilizes a singular 2d6 table for all of them and has effects that range from permanent reductions to movement speed, to ward saves, to abject violence being meted out on the poor souls looking for a place to hide. If you’re looking to occasionally add an extra layer of spice to your games this is a welcome little tweak that I highly recommend.

Why Are We Here?

Liam: The story is great here; Settra is rampaging north and it out just to really show the forces of the Bretonnian who's actually boss, continuing the story from previous books. He rampages through Renegade Princes and fends off an army of Dwarfs without breaking sweat. While some of his lieutenants may not always fair so well Settra is very much untroubled by any of it and marches to the foot of Louen Keep ready to enforce his will. What is also great is that at one point he sends off a High Priest to investigate some Tombs, as you do, but the High Priest ends up coming out the other side and finds the boarder of Athel Loren, he doesn't want to enter, but it's just too tempting, he can't help himself so in he goes, against his better judgement.

There's loads of additional background material on the forces of the Nehekhara filling us in on how they want to collect knowledge and despite their leaders massive lifespans haven't remotely began to quench that first. I'm not usually a big "reading" guy for the most part but I'm enjoyed reading this and it's certainly fed into the cannon inside of head for how these armies work and grow over time.

We then head back to the forces of Ashurtak and their foray into Athel Loren. I'm not going to go into lots of details here because it's for you to enjoy but I wondered why the book was called "The River of Blood," and after reading this I'm no longer left asking this question.

On Barrowed Time

Peter: Next up in this tome, we find essentially two Armies of Infamy for the Tomb Kings related to the Barrow Kings. The first of which, the Champions of Nehekhara, is Battle Marches exclusive, which is very interesting as it opens up more options for people trying to play with the limited points in that format. One of the problems with the Battle March format for grand army Tomb Kings players, is their need for a Hierophant as well as a general and how that shoehorns your lists in order to make it remotely possible. This list follows all of the normal Grand Army composition requirements for the Tomb Kings but removes the option of taking actual Tomb Kings and High Priests along with their 1+ needs, instead letting you lead your army with a Royal Herald or Necrotect should you desire

(though you may still choose to take a Prince or Mortuary Priest).

In addition to this, Royal Heralds in a Champions of Nehekhara battle march army gain the Honour Thy King special rule. This rule grants any unit joined by a Royal Herald either a +1 to their movement speed, reroll hit rolls of 1 in the combat phase, or +D3 to their initiative. It should be noted that this rule cannot affect a unit also affected by My Will Be Done.

Finally, if you elect to run a Champions of Nehekhara list in your Battle March games and have a Royal Herald or Tomb Prince as your general with no Mortuary Priest to back them up, your general gains the Arch Hierophant special rule, allowing them to attempt to resurrect infantry or cavalry units in your command phase though they are much worse at doing it than their clerics.

All that said, if Battle Marches are not your jam, the Barrow Kingdoms Army of Infamy is here to quench your unending thirst for subjugating the sands. This AoI, representing the disparate Barrow Kingdoms that exist near the Skull mountains, requires you to take 1+ Tomb Prince or Royal Herald rather than a Tomb King, as well has 1+ High Priest or Mortuary priest. Note that should you use that 1+ on a High Priest, you cannot take any further, as they are both a 1+ and 0-1 in this list which could be confusing on first read through. It would also lock you out from bringing a Tomb King, so you must choose wisely when building out your characters in this AoI.

In core, you are required to take 1+ units of Skeleton Archers or Warriors, as well as however many Skeleton Skirmishers, Horsemen, Horse Archers or Chariots you desire, and up to 1 unit of Tomb Guard. The big units you’ll be missing out on if you choose this army over the others are in Special and Rare as Ushabti and the Casket of Souls (and Carrion if

you’re into that kind of thing) disappear, replaced with the Winged Warsphinx from the last TK aligned Arcane Journal.

As far as special rules go, the Barrow Kingdoms have 5, though 3 are simply GW’s new way of creating specialist units for a faction without building out an entirely new datasheet for an existing unit with 1 or 2 extra rules. Battle Wizards is the first rule here, and the main reason to consider running this army composition over any of the others. In addition to their normal Lores of Magic, Liche Priests of the Barrow Kingdoms may know spells from the lores of Battle and Dark. If you’re looking to run a more aggressive Tom Guard list in a post-1.5.3 world, this is probably where you’re going to do it. Getting access to Frenzy and Hatred from Dark magic or Arcane Urgency/Oaken Shield on your big brick or Sphinxes on top of the benefits given by a Necrotect can make for a scary elite unit that can protect your mid-table and project a threat that is hard to avoid.

Finest Charioteers and Noble Cavalry are next. The first of these rules allow you to upgrade 0-2 of your Skeleton Chariot units for +6 points per model, giving them +1 initiative, First Charge, Khopesh and most importantly a 5+ regeneration save. Given how cheap Chariots have become in recent FAQs, this is a pretty spicy upgrade to have in your back pocket and opens up some pretty devastating flank potential. The cavalry upgrade grants 0-2 of your Skeleton Horsemen units regeneration 5+, a weapon skill of 3 and the Khopesh special rule for 3ppm. Liam: In my head these are both great upgrades, especially the chariots. I'm a big fan of this way of "boosting" units to give new options rather than just making a new version of similar things. I know these are top tier upgrades but they're good upgrades and something I'm looking forward to seeing on the table.

Skeleton Skirmishers also see a cool upgrade in this AoI. For +3ppm, 0-2 units may be

upgraded to Shades of Death, making them Ethereal and giving their bows the Magical Attacks special rule. Liam: It goes without saying just how ridiculously game breaking this upgrade could be. Magic Weapons are limited to a handful of models in most armies and while Magic Missiles exist they're still not easily going to kill 20 models with a 6+ regeneration save. I haven't had real chance to put this on the table yet but I'm fairly sure that nearly every time I write a list for this ends up with me putting in 1x20, if not 2x20 Skeleton Skirmishers just to pose opponents problems.

Falcon: The final special rule for the Barrow Kingdoms, Vassals of the Great Land, gives all Royal Heralds in a Barrow Kingdoms list the Honour thy King special rule we spoke about in the Battle March army of infamy on top of allowing your army general the option to ride a Necrolith Bone Dragon as a mount even if they normally would not be allowed to.

All-in-all, from a purely competitive perspective I do not think this pushes out the Royal Host or Grand Army in terms of power as of the writing of this article, but there are some really interesting things here to set you apart. Access to Dark and Battle Magic are big deals though they are hampered a bit by the restrictions on High Priests in the army.

The Woods Are Lovely, Dark and Deep

While the Tomb Kings side of this journal feels a bit lackluster, particularly given the range

refresh being released along with these rules, Wood Elf players have a few more things to be excited about with two new and improved monsters, a truly dedicated forest spirit Army of Infamy, and most importantly for those of you looking to make the most of your lists in grindier games, new kindreds.

The Spirits of the Forest Army of Infamy is up first, and while it lacks the bevy of special rules other Armies of Infamy have had, it still manages to create a brand new way to play your models through a unique army composition list. In terms of what you are allowed to bring, your character choices are locked to 0-1 Ancient or Gargantuan Ancient Treeman per 1000 points, 0-1 Spellweavers, and any number of Glade Captains, Spellsingers or Branchwraiths. No Shadowdancers, Dragonlords, or Wayfinders make their way into these lists.

At Core, Dryads are a mandatory piece at 1+ units per army with Glade Guard and Deepwood Scouts listed at 1 per 1000pts. The big piece here for players is that a single unit of Treekin, sporting their fresh new Toughness 6 duds, can be slotted into your core so long as your general is a Treeman of some kind.

The Special slot sees Deepwood Scouts, Eternal Guard, more Glade Guard, Wardancers,

Wildwood Rangers, Tree Kin, Glade Riders and Sisters of the Thorn as option, while Rare (at a heaping 33% of your list if you want it) is taken up by the old Treeman or 0-1 Gargantuan Treemen per 1000 points.

This list does have some serious potential, though it runs pretty slow with no kitted Glade Lords to project combat threat. That said, having access to a T6 unit in your core slot and the potential of running 4 Toughness 6+ monsters (we’ll get to those shortly) leads to some unique opportunities for a faction normally reliant on evasiveness, and could be a real winner in team play in particular.

For special rules, the Spirits of the Forest have just two. Deepwood Ambush is the first and is relatively simple. Dryads and Treekin may have the Ambushers special rule for +1 point per model and Branchwraiths and all varieties of Treeman may have it for +10 points. Additionally, any unit coming in from ambush may choose to enter play completely within the boundaries of a forest so long as they are not within 6 inches of an enemy model. I’m not gonna lie, this is a flavorful and sassy ability if only situationally useful. Unlike the Green Knight that just needs an inch of space to pop up and choose violence, 6 inches is a pretty big gap to maintain and can be easily countered with positional play. That said, it is another thing opponents will need to think about whenever you toss a unit into ambush and there is a world where ambushing a chunk (or all of if you are an absolute madman) of your army will save you points regardless of the fickleness of the ability. Liam: There is a big difference between fitting in a single base and an entire unit of Dryads or a Treeman.

Falcon: The second rule is Blessings of the Elder Wood. This rule grants access to a unique set of what are essentially magic items with their own allotment, just for Forest Spirit characters in this Army of Infamy. Any Forest Spirit character in this army of infamy may select 1 of the blessings, though the new Gargantuan Treeman and it’s Ancient Gargantuan kin may take 1 regardless of the army they’re found in. They are:

Blessings of the Oak. This 50 point blessing is pretty stellar, granting the bearer Armoured Hide (1) and immunity to Killing Blow, Monster Slayer, and Multiple Wounds (X), instead suffering just a single wound if hit with any of those abilities. Bumping your Treemen up to a 3+ Save AND granting them this kind of protection will keep them up and moving for way longer than they have any right to and I would expect to see this upgrade in every Forest Spirit list from now until eternity.

Blessings of the Ash. At 45 points, this is the other big boy blessing to be had in the list,

granting it’s bearer a +1 to their regeneration saves (getting those Treemen to a very respectable 4+) AND forcing enemy models to reroll any natural to hit rolls of 6 both in combat and at range.

Blessings of the Willow. For 40 points, this upgrade grants its bearer Magic Resistance (-2) and provides them with a 5+ Ward save against Magical Attacks.

Blessings of the Yew. Finally, the Yew Tree is a 30 point blessing that allows its bearer to stand back up after losing its last wound on a roll of a 2+. On a 2-5 the bearer will have 1 wound remaining, on a 6 they will gain D3. This one is pretty lacklustre, I generally dislike when rules like this have an additional failure point when you are paying additional points for them.

Liam: This is really weird because these are all good, and if you could buy them for your monster/character in a lot of armies you'd nearly always pick one up, even the Blessing of the Yew while not amazing means you can be a little bit more reckless with your characters. But the problem here is that the Blessing of the Oak is ridiculously good. I mean it's so far ahead of the rest that it might as well cost 80pts and only be available on the Character version. I'm not totally sold we'll see loads of these on the table but what I can be sure of is that if we do they'll nearly all be Blessed by the Oak.

Last March of the Ents

The Gargantuan Treeman and Gargantuan Treeman Ancient are the new additions to the Wood Elf arsenal and may be taken in the Spirits of the Forest AoI as listed above or as a 0-1 Character option for the Ancient and 0-1 Rare option for the plain jane gargantuan model in the Wood Elf Realms Grand Army.

As far as Behemoths go, these fellas are a bit beefier than their smaller cousins coming in at Strength 6, Toughness 7, and with 7 wounds compared to the normal Treeman and Ancient’s 6 Toughness and 5 wounds. The Gargantuan Treeman also boasts 6 attacks at Weapon Skill 6 and is a lvl 1 wizard with access to Battle Magic, Elementalism, and the Lore of the Wilds. The Ancient Gargantuan sacrifices 2 Weapon Skill and 2 Attacks in exchange for being a lvl 2 Wizard and having access to 100 points of Forest Spites compared the Gargantuan Treeman’s 50. On top of all of this, both new Treemen get bumped to D6 Stomp attacks and trade the old Strangleroot attack for a MUCH more impressive 24 inch Str 3 AP -1 (Str 6, Multiple Wounds D3+1 in the centre) Stone Thrower attack using the 5 inch template with the Move and Shoot special rule. Lastly, these Treemen are on 100x150 bases which indicates they are to be played with their Age of Sigmar (8th Ed WHFB) models or perhaps a new model coming down the line, given just how small the original Treeman would look on that absurd base.

The only real problem with these guys is the price tag. At 300 base points for the Gargantuan Treeman, 325 for the Ancient and the effectively mandatory 45-50 point upgrades to help ensure their survival against the seemingly infinite cannons, ogre blades, and wrecked flails that permeate the Old World, it can be difficult to find space for these brutes in a meta that values higher movement speed, swiftstride, and core units that can play the objective game. Also, the loss of attacks and weapon skill is an insane drop in value to be paying 25 points extra and only getting 1 lvl of wizardry in return.

Dreaded Kindreds

When the Wood Elf Arcane Journal was released, one of the high-points was the incredibly

powerful list of kindred upgrades for your elven characters and the same is true of this release as Wood Elf players gain access to a further 6 to elevate your lists. Unlike the previous release, these ‘kinbands’ are actually for your units of Glade Guard, Glade Riders, Eternal Guard, Wildwood Rangers, and Deepwood Scouts rather than the characters and a few are flat out fire.

Note that these kinbands are 1 per unit but are repeatable throughout your list and only one of them applies any further restrictions on your army.

Kinbands of Cavaroc (+15pts per Unit): Grants the unit +1 to the movement and the

Vanguard Special rule. Not a terrible upgrade, but there are better ones for sure.

Kinbands of Argwylon (+10pts per Unit): Grants the unit Magic Resistance (-1). Very cheap access to MR (-1) is nice if you have the points.

Kinbands of Athylwyth (+2pts per Model): Units with this upgrade Reroll To-Hit rolls of 1 in shooting and combat, you cannot have more than 1 unit with the Tree Spirit rule in your list per 1000 points. Rerolls are hard to come by in this edition and this penalty is a very minor one as most Grand Army lists are only running 1-2 units of Tree Spirits anyway.

Kinbands of Modryn (+2pts per Model): Units with this upgrade Reroll To-Wound rolls of 1 during the Shooting Phase. This upgrade being locked to the shooting phase is really its only weakness and is a strong selection if you’re going all in on say a unit of Glade Guard, though there is probably one better.

Kinbands of Cythral (+1pt per Model): Models with this upgrade automatically pass

dangerous terrain tests and receive a 5+ ward save against any unit with the troop type

Monster. This is an excellent upgrade for the units of Wildwood Rangers we sometimes see

tossed into competitive lists as it greatly upgrades their survivability when doing the thing they do best.

Kinbands of Torgovann (+1pt per Model): The greatest of the kinbands and it is not even close in my opinion. Models in a unit with this upgrade select a non-magical weapon they are equipped, that weapon has its armour piercing characteristic improved by 1. Asrai longbows are already decent threats in a wood elf list, giving them an additional pip of AP is a huge upgrade for a single point. I feel we’ll be seeing this tossed on a number of units with abandon.

Barrow Relics

The last bit of love in this journal are the new core magic items made available to every non-dwarven faction in the times before. Like the last Arcane Journal, these items are cursed, and so they all come with some kind of negative effect that theoretically brings down their overall cost. Unlike the last Arcane Journal, there aren't really as many bangers as were to be found in the March of Chaos.

Liam: It's worth noting that these are new Common magic items and can be taken by anyone. I'm all in on new random magic items in books when they've thought of ones they'd like to add to the game.

Falcon: Still, there are a couple of situationally sweet treats for the right connoisseurs. Of the 18 items available, here are the standouts:

The Briar Bow. For 25 points, this Range 30, Str 3, AP -1 bow has some real potential in the right hands. When this bow successfully strikes an enemy unit, roll an artillery die to determine how many hits the unit actually takes. On a misfire result, the bearer of the bow is hit once instead and the bow is destroyed. The potential upside here is quite powerful, with each bowshot having the potential to really neuter enemy units and the downside is well priced as a single str 3 hit is relatively easy to survive under most circumstances.

The Armour of Fate. At 35 points, this piece of Heavy Armour has a lot of potential in the right list. Infantry or Cavalry Only. Bearers of this armour reroll all failed armour save rolls (suck it dawnstone), if the reroll result is a natural 1, they take a strength 5 hit with no armour save permitted. Any build that can reliably get down to a 2 or 3+ armour save is going to love adding this piece to their repetoire, as any WHFB player that ever got stuck in with a Saurus Cowboy in the age of 8th edition will be sure to testify.

Robes of Phakth. This 50 point enchanted item seems a bit pricy at first especially as it is infantry only, but it grants the bearer the Ethereal, Fly(8) and Loner special rules. There are a number of dedicated combat characters out there that become extremely difficult to deal with with the extra layer of protection Ethereal provides and there is just enough room here for a Flail of Wrecks and Charmed Shield to round out the build in basically every army that wants it.

The Tome of Oppression. This item actually isn't particularly good outside of Legacy factions but it is unique and for that it deserves some credit. For 40 points, this arcane item may be used in place of a dispel attempt once per turn. Instead of attempting to beat an enemy's casting result, the bearer and the caster both roll a d6 and add their toughness value. If the bearer's result is greater, the spell is dispelled and the enemy wizard suffers a Str 4 hit with an AP value of -1. If the casting wizard is successful, it is the bearer that takes that hit. Units like Troll Hags, Chaos Dwarf Sorcerers and Slaughtermasters, can get a lot of mileage out of this item.

Final Thoughts

The River of Bones has a lot of content to offer, though much of it is bogged down in middling rules. That said, as I typed out this review I became more enamored with bits that I originally disliked. While I don't necessarily think the Armies of Infamy in this book are particularly strong, there is something here. The kinbands are a strict upgrade to just about every wood elf list, a couple of the magic items will potentially shake up what has been a stale meta, and I'm curious to see if the Gargantuan Treemen will make any kind of waves.

Liam: It's not a bad book, and I think my initial impression of there being very little here has kind of fallen away. I think that they're clearly trying to add a little and often to lots of different factions in Warhammer: The Old World and it's through this that we're going to learn about the world and it's inhabitants. I think we'll see a fair amount of the TK AOI but needs some skill to get right and applying the Ethereal units in the key locations and while the Gargantuan Treemen won't fit every style of Wood Elf list there's certainly something in taking a T6 unit in your core, multiple T6 monsters and a couple of T7 ones and just saying "can you deal with this?" in the same way Knights posed that question in 40k for years. Will everyone be overjoyed by the additions? Unlikely. But I'm excited to try them out.
