Tabletop Battles Reviews: Warhammer The Old World Arcane Journal - The Doom of Lord Carrard

November 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

"In Ancient Nehakhara, the living must bow down before the dead." - Sir Varo of Brionne

In a move which has taken many by surprise, Games Workshop has decided we weren't done with Warhammer: The Old World in 2026 and has released another Arcane Journal following up from the River of Bones Arcane Journal last month. This book focuses on the clashes in the in the Badlands and down into the Land of the Dead as Lord Carrard takes his Questing Force to take on the forces of Nehekhara head on. As always thank you to Games Workshop for sending us a preview of this book so we could bring this to you on Pre-Order day.

In this book we have a bunch of new scenario's for you to play following through this campaign, with both Battle March and "Standard" Games of Warhammer: The Old World on display. With games of 3 various sizes (2x600, 1x1250, 1x2000) letting you follow the campaign from head to tail. Then we've got rules for expanding your Bretonnian Army around both the Questing Host Army of Infamy and then some Pledges of Honour which add another layer to this Bretonnian onion. These are then follows-on by the Khemri Dynasties which do a similar job and let you theme your lists (even more) around specific rules of Nekakhara Past.

A Tale as Old as Time

The Lore in this book follows two distinct paths. The first is, as the name suggests, Lord Carrard's journey from Bretonnian to the Boarder Princes to meet up with a disgraced Knight, Sir Lothar. From there they head down into the Badlands and as the title of the book suggests this might not go exactly to plan, but that wasn't entirely all the armies of the dead's doing.

The second trail is more background information about the Lands of the Nekekhara and it's history, going into more detail of the Gods they worshipped and the Dynasty's which formed over the years from Settra, through to Rasetra, Khetep, Lahmizzar, Lakhahaz and finally Alkhazzar followed by his descendant Alcadizaar and into what we're now in, the Seventh Dynasty, the Dynasty of the Dead.

Let's Go on a Quest

The first set of rules contained in the book are for the Questing Host Army of Infamy which sees you form an army around a host of Knights who are heading out to complete their own set tasks for their ascension. You're allowed 0-1 Baron of Prophetess per 1000 and 0-1 Outcast Wizard, full stop, it doesn't scale. In addition you're allowed Paladins, Damsels and Sergeants-at-Arms up to 50% of the force.

In Core we see 25% as a minimum requirement with 1 unit of Questing Knights per 1000 making up the core of the force with 1+ units of Knights of the Realm on Foot or Mounted bulking out the force and then Men-at-Arms, Peasant Bowmen and interesting Squires all into Core. The addition of Squires in core could prompt some Skirmishing Avoidance style lists but it's pretty hard to do that effectively when you've still got 3 units of Knights in the list at 2000pts. Special see's yet more Questing Knights alongside Mounted Yoeman, 0-1 units of Pegasus Knights (again no scaling) and 0-1 Knights Errant and 0-2 Battle Pilgrims who are both per 1000pts, again at the standard 50%.

Finally we have Rare which is up to 25% with Grail Knights and 0-1 Border Princes Brigands being the main choices here. These Questing Knights don't believe in long range fire support it seems. As usual you're allowed to upgrade a Paladin to a Battle Standard Bearer, but no Mercs have made their way into this force.

So what do you get for these restrictions? Acts of Gallantry allows Grail Reluquae within 12" of a model with the Questing Vow (who isn't fleeing) to gain the Immune to Psychology and Unbreakable special rules which are nice allowing them to really commit to being that roadblock allowing your Hammers to counter. Campaign Yoeman allows 0-1 unit of Men-at-Arms to be upgraded at +1pts to get Ws3 and the Veteran rule which seems a bargain. Holy Weapons gives all non-magical Great Weapons an additional Armour Bane up to Armour Bane (2) and Magical Attacks which is great considering last months Tomb King AoI ended up bring mass ethereal troops back into the game.

Questing Hero's grants the ability for the characters in the list who have a Chivalrous Vow to pick up a Noble Pledge of Honour and gives them way to regain back their Blessing should they ever lose it. Finally Warriors of Faith improves said blessing for any Questing Knight giving them a constant 5+ Ward, even against weaker attacks!

What I'd Do for You, My Lady

Noble Pledges of Honour follow up, these additional rules can be taken by characters (with a Vow) in this AoI or by any character with the Questing Vow in any other Bretonnian Army. Maybe we'll see some Questing Knights on the table now 'eh?

These are free upgrades and range from "Of course I'll take it" to "Please No, don't make me". Pledge of Conquest for example gives the character an additional 50VP's if it ends the battle in your opponents deployment zone, which is all upside with no real downside while Pledge of the Hunter grants your character the Monster Slayer special rule (Amazing) but they can no longer purchase a Magic Weapon (boo - but can still take Virtues) and if they fail to slay a monster during the game then your opponent gets 150VPs! As I said some of these are great and some are wild - but what's important is that they allow you to theme a list and build it more in the style you want. These will be great for campaign play when you know (with your opponent) what's coming but in matched play events some of them do feel a little bit like rolling the dice with your matchups, especially those with downsides. In those scenarios I think ones like Pledge of Wrath will just be in every army because it's something you should achieve and just give you a benefit with no real downside.

Units of Questing Knights (or any Knight unit in the Questing AoI) can also take Pledge of Honour but choose from a different list. These follow a similar trend and some give small bonuses you might remember at the end of the game while others can bite you in the backside. Pledge of Liberation grants you 50VPs if said unit is fully within your opponents Deployment Zone at the end of the game with no downside but Pledge of Honourable Warfare can end up granting your opponents 100VPs if you ever fail to win combat against opponents units with Missile Weapons, but it does give the unit Furious Charge to make that target easier.

Never-Sleeping Armies

The Tomb Kings get their next set of upgrades in this book with the Dynastic Rulers section. Here you pick an army, including any AoIs, and say it comes from a specific Dynasty. In doing so your characters get a bonus to them, but at the same time you get additional restrictions or bonuses applied to the army as a whole. I'm not a fan of the way this is presented in the book, it has you flipping back and forth between pages trying to work out what is better in combination as the character and unit amendments or restrictions are on different sections and unless I'm missing something I don't see why you couldn't put all of the rules effecting the First Dynasty for example on the same page next to each other. There are a bunch of rules here so I'm going to just break down a couple of examples.

Khemri - The First Dynasty

This gives Tomb Kings and Princes +1Ws, +1I and +1AP of any None-Magical Weapon they wield. However you can't ever decline challenges. However if you pick this then you are limited to 1 Tomb King per army and only 1 unit who's type is 'monster', 'monstrous infantry' or 'monstrous cavalry' per 1000pts. In effect meaning that you're playing with Skeleton Infantry of all it's various types and none of the newer "toys" which Tombs Kings have had added to their lists over their lifespan. However in addition to this you can upgrade any number of Skeleton Warriors to be Khemrian Spearmen for +1pts per model, this gives them Ws3 and means they have to be equipped with thrusting spears and light armour.

Zandri - The Third Dynasty

Choosing this Dynasty means that Tomb Kings, Princes and Heralds who are mounted on chariots (they don't all have to be, but if they are) gain the First Charge special rule and may re-roll any rolls of 1 when making Dangerous Terrain Tests. In addition their chariots Impact Hits also gain AP-1 which is a big impact. The restrictions around this are that 1+ character (not including the General) must be mounted on a Skeleton Chariot per 1000pts and Skeleton Chariots become 1+ per 1000pts. In addition any unit of Skeleton Chariots and Tomb Guard Chariots can be updated to be Zandrain Charioteers which means they also gain AP-1 on their Impact Hits and gain that same Dangerous Terrain test reroll, which is well worth every point of upgrade.

As you can see these are small changes, and I'm torn between them being nice little flavorful additions and yet another thing to remember at the table. I think I'm in the first camp but I know I'm going to forget something at an important point and it's going to cost me a game. It might not be till 2028, but I know it's coming at some point. For what it's worth the 2nd Dynasty - Raestra is themed around the Priests, the 4th Dynasty - Lybaras is around Archers (dangerous I know - but they have thought about it), the 5th Dynasty - Quatar is around the smaller constructs and the 6th Dynasty - Bhagar is around those shiny new Skeleton Calvary models.

If you want to have a look at the others you'll want to pick up the book when it's released but I think there's enough here for Tomb King Players to want to pick it up and benefit from some of the additional elements here without a shadow of a doubt.

Final Thoughts

I go back and forth a little over this book. The rules which are in here are 1 Army of Infamy, then some rules which I don't expect will be used loads by Bretonnian players (due to lack of Questing Knights on the table - but this might help that) and then just some bonuses for Tomb King players who want to theme an army even more than a normal AoI might do. But at the same time these rules and additions are pretty well thought through and while some of them are never going to be used in competitive play due to matchup roulette they are fun, and a lot of the time that's what certain sections of the player base (myself included) can forget. Not every new addition to the game needs to be game or Meta warping, sometimes you just want a cool addition to a new force or a new narrative hook to start a new Battle March force, and I think the Questing Force and the Dynasty's do that really well.
