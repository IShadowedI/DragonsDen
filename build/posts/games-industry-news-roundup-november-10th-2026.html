Games Industry News Roundup- November 10th, 2026

November 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Arkane Studios

Arkane Union Accuses Microsoft of Intentionally Sabotaging Studio

The fate of fan favorite Immersive Sim studio, Arkane, has been negotiating for it’s life with Microsoft to find a new buyer following Microsoft's massive corporate restructure earlier this year. Initially, things had looked alright for Arkane, as French union laws gave them extra time at the table with Microsoft representatives to discuss a potential buyer or how to spin off, but it was announced last month that these talks had failed to find an interested party. Now, even more news has been leaking out from the tense talks between Arkane and Microsoft, as Arkane’s union representatives accuse Microsoft of sabotage.

According to a statement made by the Le Syndicat des Travailleureuses du Jeu Vidéo (STJV), Microsoft is looking to potential close Arkane due to “economic reasons”, but claims that Microsoft themselves caused the money problems Arkane faces through acts of intentional sabotage, including a 19 month hiring freeze that delayed work on the game significantly.

“The truth is that Microsoft based its decision only on stone cold and inhuman logic, in which our lives, projects and dreams are voided, so we can better serve Microsoft’s short term financial goals,” the STJV writes. “In the wake of thousands of yearly firings, studio closure to which you can now add the tenth of thousands of voluntary buyout offered by the company, the decision to close Arkane is solely the brutal application of a never-ending search for maximum profit optimization.”

“Arkane is profitable. Just not profitable enough for Microsoft.”

Arkane claims that their current project, Blade, is nearly complete, and it would be able to ship the game in short order should an interested buyer be found prior to the negotiation deadline in December. One of those potentially interested buyers is reportedly Ubisoft, who’s looking to bring in additional third party studios as a part of its own corporate restructuring.

The STJV is also fighting for making sure the workers have a say in their potential new ownership in their talks with Microsoft, as well as making sure that should the studio face closure, every member of the Arkane team will receive a sizable redundancy payment and redeployment support for the Arkane team.

Microsoft has not made any statement at time of writing regarding the accusations from the STJV, or regarding the potential deal with Ubisoft.

Credit: Unity

Unity Reportedly in Talks for Buyout by Same Groups Who Bought EA

The multi-billion dollar buyout of EA earlier this year is one of the most consequential stories in recent gaming history, with ripple effects that are sure to be felt for years to come. For EA’s new owners however, the world might not be enough, and according to reporting from Reuters the private equity firm Silver Lake and the Saudi Arabia Public Investment Fund (PIF) have set their sights on a new prize: Unity Engine. 

Reuters reported in an article last week that several confidential sources from within Unity approached the publication and told them that Unity had been approached by Silver lake and the PIF several times over the prior weeks, and that there’s been ongoing talks for a leveraged buyout the company in a similar fashion to how the two companies were able to purchase EA.

Silver Lake and the PIF reportedly even presented a potential value to propose to Unity leadership based on the company’s most recent earnings, with a potential deal valuing at around $25 billion.

According to Reuter’s sources, the leadership at Unity hasn’t made a decision yet, but is carefully considering its options as well as consulting with several investment banks. Reuters says that the terms may change, and emphasizes no deal has been finalized.

While not nearly as massive of a purchase as EA, Unity’s presence in the gaming world is fairly far reaching, particularly in the mobile gaming market with extremely popular games such as Pokémon GO.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Warmachine Shows Off New Faction, Announces Plans to Sunset Old Factions

Steamforged Games hosted Lock & Load North America last week in St. Louis, a weekend long official convention and celebration of all things Warmachine which included multiple tournaments, casual and narrative play events, painting classes and lore seminars. Aside from the Iron Gauntlet capstone event for Warmachine’s competitive season, the biggest event of the weekend was the preview show, which was streamed live on the Warmachine YouTube channel and gave us our very first look at some of the biggest upcoming releases for Warmachine.

The biggest reveal of the entire show was our very first look at the next faction releasing for Warmachine, the Orgoth Reaper Covenant. This faction of Warwitches uses the eldritch and terrible magic of the Orgoth to summon and control nightmarish constructs. According to what was stated in the preview, the Reaper Covenant will act as a tough force that can march up the board while taking a beating, supported by an immensely powerful magical gunline, and is designed to be easier for newer players to get into the game with.

Alongside the new faction, some exciting releases for currently existing factions include the return of the Harbinger of Menoth and the Avatar of Menoth, the full reveal of the Fane Gargantuan, the Tyrant Nocturnys, and a new 2 player starter box that pits the forces of Khador and the Reaper Covenant against each other, which will also include 3d printed terrain and a battle mat to help players get started.

As for the state of the game itself, a significant change was announced during the preview show. Steamforged stated in the preview that it plans to begin the slow and careful retirement of the old legacy factions for the game, cycling out the old models from the game’s original run that are no longer in production. The plan for this process is to announce which factions will sunset with one year's warning before they’re removed from the Prime tournament format, with a focus on first cycling out factions that already have an equivalent mark IV army available, such as Khador and Cygnar.

For more on these reveals, the VOD of the preview show is available on the official Warmachine YouTube channel.

Credit: Disney/EA/Bit Reactor

Star Wars Zero Company Developers Layoff Staff After Returning Team from Furlough

The recent return from furlough of many members of Star Wars: Zero Company development studio Bit Reactor was a really sweet story, wasn’t it? One of the better feel good stories of the year huh? Well toss all that on the ground, because it the video game industry baby, there can be no creation without destruction, and according to several LinkedIn posts from former Bit Reactor staff it looks as though the studio has gone through a wave of staff layoffs.

According to Star Wars news site Bespin Bulletin, the layoffs are affecting many of the staff members who were not brought back to Bit Reactor from furlough, and as of November 9th the site reports that ten members of the Bit Reactor Staff have confirmed that they’ve been let go from the company, with November 6th acting as their last day at the studio.

“I am immensely proud of the work that I, the UI/UX team, and the team at large have accomplished. I hope that in this tumultous industry that my coworkers (and myself) find placement,” UI Engineer Tyler Eichden wrote on his LinkedIn, confirming he was one of those let go.

As of the time of this writing, neither EA or Bit Reactor has made any official statement regarding these layoffs.

Restoration Games Brings Back Several Popular Warhammer Board Games

Ending on some very good news in the world of board game preservation, with Games Workshop and Restoration Games announcing a multi-year licensing deal last week to bring back several of GW’s classic board games, reimagined to fit into a new setting based on the Talisman IP.

According to the press release, Restoration Games is planning to re-adapt three GW classics: Chaos in the Old World, Space Hulk: Death Angel, and Warrior Knights, with Chaos in the Old World the first of the three to release. All three will be reimagined out of the Warhammer universe and into the Talisman IP, with Chaos in the Old World becoming Devastation in the Dread World. Eric Lang, the original designer for Chaos in the Old World, is returning to help build Devastation in the Dread World.

“I think Chaos in the Old World is my most requested reprint from the most dedicated fans,” Lang said in the press release. “Restoration is the perfect match to bring this game into 2026. I started this project excited, now I’m beyond thrilled.”

“Since I started the company, the idea of working with Games Workshop has been a goal I could not have imagined was possible,” writes Justin D. Jacobson, owner of Restoration Games. “These games have that signature spice that only a Games Workshop setting can offer. Gritty, grungy, brutal, and explosively fun. Getting to work on them now is a dream come true.”

Restoration Games plans to bring Devastation in the Dread World to crowdfunding early in 2027, with a retail release to shortly follow pending the successful fundraising campaign.
