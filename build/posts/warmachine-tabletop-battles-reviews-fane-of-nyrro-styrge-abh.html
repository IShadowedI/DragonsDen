[Warmachine] Tabletop Battles Reviews: Fane of Nyrro Styrge Abhorreth

September 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Eldritch Priests of the Fane of Nyrro have long plundered the dark catacombs of the Eversael for dangerous and profane beasts to subjugate to their will, and the Styrge Abhorreths are some of the largest and most dangerous of their kind. These gigantic monstrosities rampage across the battlefield commanded by magic wielding riders, who combine the raw physical strength of their mount with their command over the arcane for deadly effect.

After being teased in August’s Autumn preview, the rules for the Styrge Abhorreth are finally here, with a combination of both deadly combat prowess from the gigantic mount with a strong mix of spells from the rider, drawing from nearby warbeasts to fuel its arcane powers. In today’s article, we will be going over the rules for the newest addition to the Fane of Nyrro roster, some of the synergies we see with the Abbhoreth inside the faction, as well as some sample lists and overall thoughts on this beautiful beastie.

Before we begin, we would like to thank the team at Steamforged Games for giving us early access to these rules for this review.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Rules Overview

The Styrge Abhorreth rocks a fairly impressive statline befitting such a gigantic monster. Coming in at ten points, the Abhorreth is fast at SPD 7, accurate for Fane with a MAT and AAT of 7, and tough to shift with DEF 12, ARM 18 and 24 Boxes. Alongside these stats, the Abhorreth has a nice mix of keywords, with some obvious ones like Fly and Undead alongside some welcome surprises like Cavalry, Unstoppable, and as a special treat Trample. Lastly, an easy to miss but very important detail is that the Abhorreth is an Eldritch model, which is important for several casters like Hysene and Vorsalys.

Beyond its statline, the Abhorreth has an interesting mix of rules that allows for the model to act as both a magical support piece with access to three different spells, and as a powerful bruiser with 2 POW 16 RNG 2 Talons and a POW 14 RNG 2 Staff with Dispel, and can draw Fury from friendly warbeasts within 12” to empower both of these functions. The Abhorreth also has Reposition 3”, as a treat.

The main gimmick to the Abhorreth lies in using Agitator and Harnessed Rage. Both of these abilities allow you to add Fury onto nearby warbeasts to either cast spells from the Abhorreth's spell list or boost the attack or damage rolls of its attacks. Boosting will add one Fury to a nearby beast of your choice, while casting a spell will require you to add Fury equal to the cost of the spell used, which for all three spells is two.

The Abhorreth can cast three spells per turn, and has three spells to choose from. The first is Embittered Hail, a POW 12 RNG 10 arcane attack that pushes the model hit (not damaged, just hit) d6” away from the spells point of origin and reduces their speed by 2”. Its next two spells are support spells, with Eternal Torment preventing Torment continuous effects within the Abhorreth’s control range of 12” from expiring and Warped Darkness reducing the range of enemy ranged attacks targeting a friendly model within 2” of the Abhorreth by 2”.

While the ability to cast some strong spells and boost some nasty damage is eyebrow-raising at first glance, it’s tempered by the need to use nearby warbeasts as a battery. Since you cannot exceed a beast's Fury with either Agitator or Harnessed Rage, the more you pump into the Abhorreth, the less you can do with your beasts, and if the Abhorreth has no beasts to draw from its own effectiveness is greatly reduced. To get the most out of the Abhorreth, you’ll want to make sure that you’re balancing how much juice you’re pumping into it from your beasts, while leaving enough for your beasts to do what they need to do each turn.

Credit: Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson

Synergies

Hysene: The Styrge Abhorreth might be the best model for Hysene since the Strygon Rider. Not only is it a fast and well-armored warrior model capable of benefiting from Dash and Storm Rager, but it’s also an Eldritch model able to benefit from Hysene’s feat and Hunger abilities. If the Abhorreth kills an enemy model while affected by Hysene’s feat, it will be ARM 21 even before the Hysene’s other spells come into play. Although it doesn’t have as devastating a Brutal Strike as the Strygon Rider, it is able to get much more reach than the Rider with Murderous Impulse attacks and it’s able to boost every initial and extra attack so long as it has nearby beasts to stack fury points on. This can expand the danger area of the Abhorreth to threaten whole units and makes it a strong consideration in any Hysene army.

Ashmael: Ashmael doesn’t bring a great deal to benefit the Abhorreth besides his feat and Mirage given the Abhorreth’s lack of critical effects, but fortunately the Abhorreth provides some useful tools for Ashmael. Misdirection combined with Warped Darkness means it’s much more difficult to displace or remove the Abhorreth at range, while Embittered Hail provides a flexible relatively long-range tool to push enemy models into Desecrated Idols range to pounce on them with the rest of your force.

Vorsalys: Voralys and the Abhorreth have lots of overlap with each other. As the army’s largest non-caster Eldritch model, it’s ideal for contesting elements for Dominion and its built-in resilience as well as Warped Darkness make it a more resilient contesting piece than most Eldritch models in the roster. On the offensive side, the Abhorreth can benefit from Arcane Winds and Unholy Blessing as well as use Illimitable Power, turning it into a solid spell sniper under the Auricant. The push and speed debuff from Embittered Hail can combo nicely with Humble to effectively remove a melee centric model from consideration for a turn, and the rider isn’t afraid to get in there with multiple boosted damage rolls in cases where Vorsalys needs to deal some damage. They bring a lot to each other and I expect to see them paired often.

Nymara: While Nymara doesn’t attain huge benefits from what the Abhorreth has to offer, what she offers herself is a horde of bats for the Abhorreth to tap for power and relatively fewer consequences for doing so. Nymara seldom minds when her beasts are filled up since they can provide fuel for Dark Valkyrie or managed into a good position via Slingshot or Feral Onslaught. Warped Darkness is never a bad tool to have and Embittered Hail can be good for eliminating or pushing away Shield Guard models to allow Nymara to make a play with her javelin. And besides, who doesn’t just love the idea of a swarm of giant bats and bat riders swooping down from above?

Harasheth: Harasheth can give the Abhorreth some extra armor at the cost of 4 Hunger, but it’s arguable that he finds the least use in the Abhorreth given that he can use Hex Blast to dispel, would often rather take channeling bats or units that can benefit from Profane Resurrection, and can handle shooting well with Expendable Pawn. However, Harasheth also finds particular value out of Eternal Torment. By building Torment turn-over-turn with no possibility of expiration, Harasheth can gain a lot of Hunger with Afflicted Wind and set up a devastating Feast of Torment with a little preparation. Comparatively niche, but Harasheth is built different among the Fane casters.

Lanyssa Ryssyl: Hey, this isn’t a real caster! While true, the synergies are worth bringing up because Benkei and Sasha are faction warbeasts. While you may not always want to build fury on your Strygons or Vordaks to cast spells, building fury on Benkei and Sasha is a much more appetizing proposition since Lanyssa can Subdue 3 of it away almost immediately and she’s generally looking to leach back 3-4 fury from her cats each turn. If you want your Abhorreth to cast more spells without necessarily compromising your fury economy, the Final Hunt may be a strong consideration.

Swiftblade: Would it surprise anyone reading this that I played exclusively mono-green big monster decks when I played Magic? No? That’s what I thought.

As the name implies, this list has the subtlety of an oncoming freight train. It wants to get in your face quickly and hit you exceptionally hard. With Withering Darkness and Deceleration, I can keep myself relatively safe from shooting until I get into charge range, and I can use Eternal Torment alongside the Stalkers Torment ammo to generate a good amount of Hunger quickly. The Abhorreth is just a hilariously good target for Storm Rager and Dash, it can cover a ridiculous distance really quickly and just murk some poor bozos in its way, and under Hysene’s feat becomes just nasty to shift at ARM 23 if it kills something. with Lanyssa plus her pets, the Strygon Rider, and Hysene herself also offering very capable damage output too. This list loves big, beefy targets that it can crack wide open, and ideally also addresses some of Hysene’s biggest problems with hunger generation. As an added bonus, the Abhorreth’s extra large base makes it a great (if expensive) target for Shadow Shift if I absolutely need to get out of dodge, especially if the big beast was about to die anyways.

Blake: This list takes Vorsalys’ control elements and turns them up to 11. With the Abhorreth having plenty of juice to cast all the spells it needs and Warping Darkness catching most things that Blinding Light won’t, there will be plenty of cover to advance up your hard-hitting models in Lanyssa’s battlegroup and the Klyvenesh and go for kills when you’re ready. With plenty of post-combat movement in Reposition and Crimson Ballet and being able to blunt enemy counterattacks with Humble, Wither, Thunderbolt and Embittered Hail, this army can set up multiple turns of alpha strikes and lose very little in the bargain.

Ashmael is the master of applying the proper pressure at the precise points to really cripple enemy plans, but sometimes he needs a bit more safety. This list leverages his Stolen Fate hunger ability and Dissected Essence spell to their fullest by playing a hard and harsh scenario denial game. On top of the classic Lesser Eldritch and Strygon Rider combo to fish for Crit Stationary and scalpel out a key piece, there are a plethora of ways to disrupt enemy scoring. Be it The Forsaken who can bully most edge flank objectives, Blood Sirens with the Overseer UA to be truly obnoxious to remove, or the Abhorreth’s Embittered Hail for shoving this list will help you control the flow of scoring at every angle while delivering your control pieces with the help of Ashmael’s Misdirect and the Abhorreth’s Warped Darkness.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Overall Thoughts

Swiftblade: My first thought is, rules aside, holy crap what a beautiful model. I’ve really got to hand it to the design team here, they’ve been absolutely knocking it out of the park with the most recent releases, and I’d want one of these for my shelf regardless of the rules.

It does help though that I do think the rules are pretty good. I think it rides the line well between having rules that are interesting and strong without dipping into becoming oppressive, with clear limitations on it’s overall power level thanks to needing Fury from nearby warbeasts. It’s in the sweet spot of where you want a model to be in the larger scheme of power level, where it’s not going to pop up in every list, but the lists that it does appear in will make a meaningful impact.

Generally, I think the Abhorreth is best when you’re able to leverage both its magical support ability as well as its strength as a combat beatstick. In each of those categories individually, I think the Abhorreth falters behind other options Fane has access to, like the Strygon Rider or Thelmara. But if you want to get you a solo that can do both, then the Abhorreth is your giant bat.

Blake: When this model was first previewed, it gave me immediate Blighted Nyss Sorceress vibes. For those newer to the game, the Sorceress was an older cavalry solo model in the Legion of Everblight (see the Ravens of War Army of Legend) that cast spells from the back of its flying mount and was primarily there to support the other winged elements of the army. This model is that ideal fully realized, being a powerful fighter in its own right in keeping with the Eldritch aesthetic and bringing some powerful options to the force to help close the distance or make more distance.

While the Abhorreth looks very good on paper (and as a model, cause wow), I think it will need some practice for players to adjust to how she might impact the fury/frenzy management in the army. Her maximum potential output will mean at least 6 points of fury hanging out on your beasts, which is not something to trivially write off. I think players will find a balance with experience as to which beasts to max out and when to get the most from her devastating spellcasting and superlative combat solo potential.

MasterBall: Gorgeous sculpt aside (I'm just not the target demographic for bats on bats on bats) there's a lot of interesting things going on with this latest solo. The idea to use resources of other models to fuel its spells is a novel one and I broadly enjoy when SFG messes with the existing mechanisms of the game rather than hand waving in wholly new stuff. I think this will see play in most list pairs for Warped Darkness alone attached to a body with multiple solid initials, but the perks are hard to fit. Support tax in this army is already at an all time high and while it's hard to trivially remove it is still only as tanky as a standard light cohort. A triumph of design; I hope to see more of this sort of tool for future armies over some of the other things that already exist in Fane that will need to be tuned down in January.

Final Thoughts

Combining arcane mastery with big, beefy claws, the Styrge Abhorreth is a gnarly addition to the Fane of Nyrro roster. It will require players to carefully manage their fury to make sure that they don’t stumble over their own feet trying to power up the Abhorreth and get the most out of their beasts, so the skill ceiling on the Abhorreth is quite high. If you can wrap your head around it though, the Abhorreth looks to be a real workhorse of a solo that can wear quite a few different hats on top of its gigantic, grotesque head.
