[40k] Competitive Innovations in 11th: Rolling Need pt.1

August 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

Welcome back to Competitive Innovations in 11th edition! Your regular host, James "One_Wing" Grover is out this week. And so is Tom "lowest of men" Lowman. So instead you just get me! I'd like to think I bring a certain amount of charm and wit to the table that they don't, at the expense of any sense of "class" or "propriety."

This weekend's events represents the first weekend of games following the July 2026 balance update, and hoo boy did that one do a number on the competitive landscape. It feels like Purge the Foe disappeared overnight as an option, as it's no longer really attached to any good Detachments and they removed it from almost every 1 DP Detachment in the game. What we're left with is a weekend in which there were zero winning lists running either Purge the Foe or Disruption. This feels like a bad sign - we've gone from having four viable Dispositions to three. I personally played the Take and Hold mirror in all three of my day one games at the Lone Star Open.

Looking at the data from competitive events over the past four weeks shows off this particular trend. Namely, that players have (accurately) figured out that Disruption just kind of sucks, and now that Purge is really hard to get in a good list, the disposition now makes up about 1/6 of the field, while Take and Hold and Priority Assets lists now make up more than 60%, each accounting for about 31% of the field.

This ultimately highlights the problems with the July balance update, and ultimately the detachment/disposition system as it currently stands: Dispositions are very strongly tied to the strength of the Detachments they're paired with. Players aren't going to take a bad detachment for a good disposition, but they can play around a mediocre disposition if the detachment is particularly strong (as long as it's not Disruption, it seems). This means that making Purge unavailable to good detachments means that it's just not an option for most of the game's armies when played competitively. Rather than Purge being fixed, it's just now not showing up, and the result is that players get in a lot of mirrors with Take & Hold or Priority Assets.

So if you're a competitive player looking to practice for a singles event, my advice is to get comfortable playing against Take and Hold and Priority Assets. If you can shore up your matchup into those dispositions, you'll likely knock out more than half the games you play at an event.

Thoughts

BLOODCRUSHERS, BABY! What a time to be alive. The ability to fall back and charge with Bloodcrushers makes them much more versatile and the ability to give them MOBILE with the Warp-Riders Stratagem is essentially a must-have to make this whole thing possible.

I asked Mike for his thoughts on the list and his matchup in the final round against Drukhari, and this is what he had to say:

Mike: The Purge The Foe vs Recon matchup goes one of two ways. The first is that the Purge player plays cagey and the Recon player is able to play defensively. That’s a boring, drawish game.

The second option is that the Purge The Foe player shoves all-in and tries to table the Recon player. That’s a fun, quick game. That game also happens to be heavily Purge The Foe favored, especially if the Purge player goes first. That means the Purge player has 2 turns to try to kill as much as they can before the Recon player can even start doing their action to start scoring Primary.

His Drukhari list was built around being uninteractive, with 2 units of Scourges that could fire and fade back to safety, and the ability for high-damage melee bricks to jump out of a transport, delete a unit, and then jump back into a transport. I didn’t want to play a 5-round game of attrition vs a strong player with a list that would win an attrition war. That further incentivized me to rush him early.

Andrew is a really strong player (and a friend), but when I got to the table, I knew what I had to do.

All of that is to say that I deployed on the line, literally advanced everything turn 1, used the Cavalcade Of Chaos stratagem to bring in my Bloodcrushers turn 1, and prepared to go all-in turn 2 after killing some screens. Andrew took a decent chunk out of my army and I lost around ~10 Bloodcrushers, but by turn 2 I was able to charge my entire army in and kill most of his real damage dealers. Andrew smartly basically left his deployment zone and started playing the flanks to scrap for points, but the Blood Legion all-in on turn 2 took out enough of the fragile elves that they could never regain any momentum. It’s always fun playing Andrew (or any of my teammates on the Mile High Militarum) but this matchup definitely felt Blood Legion favored. All I had to do was avoid any glaring mistakes (which isn’t guaranteed by the sixth round of a GT!) and execute the gameplan to end up on the podium.

Thanks, Mike and congrats on the podium finish.
