11th Edition Detachment Focus: Flyblown Host (Updated August 17, 2026)

October 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

In our Detachment Focus series we take a deep dive into an army’s Detachments, covering what’s in them, how they play, and how they’ll fit into the broader meta and your games. In this Detachment Focus we’re looking at the Flyblown Host from the Death Guard.

Originally introduced in the Grotmas 2024 update, the Flyblown Host Detachment received a major overhaul with the release of 11th edition, dropping down to 1 DP and gaining a series of new tricks. This Detachment focuses primarily on your Plague Marines units, giving them some movement tricks and upgrades that can boost their output.

Changelog

  • 10-23-2026 (Current): Updated for post-Q3 Dataslate changes
  • 8-17-2026: Updated for the 11th edition changes
  • 6-25-2025: Updated to reflect FAQ changes
  • 4-26-2025: Updated for the 10th edition Codex
  • 12-1-2024: Initial Publication

Detachment Cost: 1 DP

Force Disposition: Reconnaissance

Unique Tag: FLYBLOWN

The Flyblown Host Detachment focuses purely on buffing Plague Marines, and as a result, its power and utility will largely fluctuate with the power and utility of that unit. The rule here generally lets you give Infiltrators to a number of your units, which can be a solid boon if you're trying to deal with a unit which otherwise struggles to move up the table without a transport. The enhancements here are a solid help as well, with two upgrades for your Plague Marine units on offer. As a Reconnaissance Detachment, this one can push you in some weird directions, but you'll likely be taking a different Disposition from your other Detachment to offset that.

Detachment Rule: Verminous Haze

In the Declare Battle Formations step, you can pick up to two friendly PLAGUE MARINES units to gain Infiltrators.

This is a solid ability to drop on a unit of durable infantry with a 5" move. It helps you position forward and take objectives early while setting up for aggressive turn 1 moves if you're up against an opponent unlikely to punish you for said moves. Of course the big downside to this is that the current layouts often make Infiltrating a massive risk - finding safe spots can be really tricky and if you go second you’re likely to lose your Plague Marines before they can do anything for you. Playing aggressively will often cost you, especially if you end up going second. It can be very tempting to be aggressive with your Plague Marines units, but if you play it too aggressive you’ll just be throwing their points away. That said, if you do have a good spot to drop them, then finding good spots or getting of first turn charges can be a huge deal.

Note that you pick these units long after you’ve picked and attached Leaders, so you can pick a unit with a Leader/Support character to gain Infiltrators.

Credit: Gerald Miller

Enhancements

Both of these are upgrades for Plague Marine units.

  • Insectile Murmuration (Upgrade, 15 points). Goes on a unit of Plague Marines. When the unit targets a unit within Contagion range of a friendly unit, those attacks re-roll wound rolls of 1. This is just very good, as it essentially gives you re-rolls of 1 to wound all the time in melee. 15 Points can be a bit much but if you take this you want this on a big unit of Plague Marines, supported by a pair of characters.
  • Plagueveil (Upgrade, 15 points). Goes on a unit of Plague Marines. Gives them -3” Detection Range. This is cute, but a lot less useful. If you’re taking this Detachment, your Plague Marines are going to be infiltrating forward, in your opponent’s face. This has its best value on a unit in your backfield, holding a home objective, but you don’t use Plague Marines for that, you use Poxwalkers. There's some synergy with your ability to Infiltrate; if you put your Plague Marines behind heavy cover in a forward position this can drop you to a 9" Detection range and help you stay protected even if you've deployed aggressively. That said, you still need to watch out for transports and vehicles which can just close the gap and shoot you in the face.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Stratagems

  • Nauseating Paroxysms (1 CP). Used at the start of the Fight phase on an Engaged Plague Marines unit. Pick a unit they’re engaged with and the enemy has to make a battle-shock roll, with -1 to the roll. This is better than it looks, in part because Plague Marines can take an Icon of Despair, which gives enemy units within 6” -1 to their Leadership. This means they’ll be taking tests at -2 when you use this, and those odds are much, much better. Can be a good way to turn off your opponents’ ability to use Stratagems. That said, this will require you to take an icon, which isn’t the ideal loadout - though it's better now that bolters give you S5, AP-1 shooting. It feels a lot better to take one, anyways. Theoretically this feels geared towards a world where you're using Scabrous Soulrot to give an enemy unit in Contagion Range -1 Leadership as well but at that point you're giving up the faction's best ability (-1 to enemy saves) for a shot at battle-shocking one unit. Just don't.
  • Droning Horror (1 CP). Used in the Shooting phase, when a Plague Marines unit shoots. That unit can re-roll hit rolls of 1, and if their target is within half range, they can re-roll wound rolls of 1. This is a solid little upgrade, and can be nice when you’re fishing for 5+ crits with meltaguns, plasma guns, and blight launchers on a unit with a Biologus Putrifier. That said, it isn’t that big a boost, and Plague Marine shooting (especially the shooting that rolls to hit) isn’t great, so this isn’t something you’re going to use often, in part because you often take plague spewers/sprayers with Plague Marines, and you can get re-rolls to wound with the Enhancement.
  • Eye of the Swarm (1 CP). Used in your Shooting phase when a friendly Plague Marines unit shoots. That unit’s ranged attacks have [CLOSE QUARTERS]. This on the other hand, is straight fire, and great for just shooting your way out of combat when your turn comes around. You may not be able to fall back and shoot/charge, but just killing the unit in combat with you is a fine consolation. Fire it off and push through your enemy into the next unit.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Playing This Detachment

Remember when we said the value of this Detachment fluctuates with the strength of Plague Marines? Well, Plague Marines aren't particularly great right now. They managed to dodge some point hikes in the latest balance update thanks to already being T6, and their bolters are better, but you're still not taking bolters if you can avoid it and being able to Infiltrate isn't as good as it sounds when it can often mean getting blown off the board on the first turn.

The other challenge here is Reconnaissance. It's not really a Disposition Death Guard and Plague Marine-heavy armies want to play with, so you'll want to pick up an alternative with your second Detachment. There your options are:

  • Shamblerot Vectorium - This does nothing for you, and Disruption is a worse disposition. If you're running Shamblerot, run Paragons with it instead.
  • Tallyband Summoners - This one is more focused on the idea of taking a lot of big, nasty threats (Rotigus, Mortarion, Great Unclean One), with Plague Marine bricks to support them as surprisingly fast units to spread contagion with an extra 3" to their range. Like Shamblerot, only adds Disruption, making it more of a teams pick.
  • Mortarion's Hammer - This one's going to focus more on putting your Plague Marines in a land raider or another transport, then driving said transport through a wall. Big advantage here is getting Purge the Foe as your Disposition.
  • Champions of Contagion - The best secondary option, this one actually focuses on the characters who will be attaching to your Plague Marines. Gives you Take and Hold.

If you're running this Detachment, it'll likely be with Champions of Contagion, which will let you switch contagions mid-game for your army. That can be cute if you're using something like Nauseating Paroxysms to cause battle-shock tests and mess with enemy leadership but the reality is that you just want -1 to saves (Rattlejoint Ague) most if not all of the time. After all, dead units can't pass Leadership tests. The real gems in this Detachment for you are the Malignance Magnified (re-roll hits and wounds against a unit below starting strength), and Grotesque Fortitude (+2 Toughness for a phase), both of which help you get more out of your 10-model Plague Marine units.

Strengths

  • Plague Marines. This Detachment does a crazy good job buffing Plague Marines, and Infiltrating them can be very scary.
  • Eye of the Swarm. Shooting your way out of combat is huge, and helps solve a key problem for Plague Marines.

Weaknesses

  • Plague Marines. Plague Marines aren't great right now - a little too expensive, a little too weak in the output department. This Detachment is only as good as they are useful at any given moment.

Final Thoughts

If you're running Death Guard and want to take big bricks of Plague Marines, this is the Detachment for you. You're likely going to pair it with Champions of Contagion, as that both encourages you to take characters who attach to Plague Marines and also gives you more options to play with on the table.
