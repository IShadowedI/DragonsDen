Tabletop Battles Reviews: BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4

October 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

I am going to start this review off with a very important caveat: I am not Brazilian.

But by the grace of God, I am a fan of boomer shooters, and the BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER series (the caps are important) is one of the best boomer shooter series on the market. Although this review is specifically about 4, the entire series spans 2 games that are available on itch.io, and 3 and 4, which are currently on Steam. When I was offered the chance to review 4, I really had very little idea of what I was getting into, and within 5 minutes of playing 4, I stopped and immediately went to get the other 3 games to properly enjoy the newest one.

What I experienced is a game that is a love letter on multiple levels: to genre, to games, to music, and to Brazilian culture. There are certainly other Boomer Shooters you can buy and play, but none of them are going to give you the pure unadulterated joy that BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4: DEMONS FROM THE PORTAL TO HELL I OPENED ARE STILL IN THE FAVELA AND NOW I HAVE TO SAVE MAMADAS FROM RONALDO will.

A code for BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 was provided by the publisher; however, I purchased 1-3 on my own. 

BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 opens with almost inscrutable text on a loud, garish background that immediately asks you if you want to learn about new movement tech. Good news: being Brazilian has gifted you with the ability to double jump, and also you can air dash. The movement and speed of BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 are key to understanding just why this game is so much fun: it doesn't have sliding or parkour in the sense that more modernized game engines might allow, but the sheer mobility you have in this game approaches levels that maybe only ULTRAKILL pulls off as well. The jumps and dashes are responsive, and the kinetic energy in the game sucked me in to the point that I turned down multiple invitations to play my current mainstay game, Deadlock, to instead sink 6 hours into playing this series. Sorry friends, now you know why I kept dodging you.

One of my favorite memories from college was sitting in my dorm room playing Serious Sam while my roommate blasted Ludacris on a particularly cool Fall day. We received a noise complaint that we ignored and ordered pizza that probably tasted terrible, and we capped the day off by blowing off our Astronomy course that we both later had to withdraw from because we missed an exam on that exact day that was worth 20% of our grade. I use this memory lightly because it is one of the pinnacles of gaming memories and joy for me, and BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4: DEMONS FROM THE PORTAL TO HELL I OPENED ARE STILL IN THE FAVELA AND NOW I HAVE TO SAVE MAMADAS FROM RONALDO is the closest I have felt to that memory in over 20 years. To veer into gaming review terminology for a few moments, the game has tight controls, fast gameplay, a sense of speed and style, and energetic music that helps push you to continue playing. To stop sounding like people trying to avoid using the word "fun", this game is pure, unadulterated fun that costs less than 10 dollars.

I like Boomer Shooters, probably too much. My first PC gaming experience was Wolfenstein 3D, and I remember being so excited that my mom let me play Duke Nukem 3D; it was even my first online gaming experience, which was some of the most jank garbage ever.  BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 does, to the point that it makes me feel a similar electricity to playing Ultrakill for the first time did, which is something that all good Boomer Shooters do: they remind me of what it was like to beat Doom II, Wolfenstein, Duke Nukem, and all the rest. The game is garish, EXCEPTIONALLY loud, and both feels great to shoot and move in. The music is bumping and frenetic, and everything is so Brazilian that even barely understanding any of the jokes, references, or touches somehow wraps around to making perfect sense. Perhaps it also just works because BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 is not trying to sell me on cinematic realism, narrative, or high fidelity graphics, but instead on the base principles of what a Boomer Shooter is, what Quake is, and then handing me dual drink bottles to shoot people with and saying have fun with a wink.

I played on the hardest difficulty for the review, and I found that the game was a solid challenge but still a bit of a breeze if you're comfortable with these types of games; that isn't to say it was easy, and the autosave feature might be one of the best things the game offers because it makes retrying areas smooth; I died a lot. Like, a lot a lot, but it never felt particularly frustrating when I did so; I remember playing through a lot of the modern Doom games feeling occasional moments of annoyance at how the fights were designed, but also the fact that dying would often create a pretty unsatisfying loop of downtime. Not here; death here is just about as simple as hitting Y and trying it again, which I was more than happy to do. After I finished my playthrough, I gave the other difficulties a shot to see what the game would be like at various levels, and although I do think the easier ones are a little too easy, I do appreciate that they still resulted in the sense of speed and energy the game wanted to present. I mentioned that I enjoy Boomer Shooters, and something that I think it often misunderstood with modern titles is that "harder" means the game should be exceptionally punishing or even tiring, turning enemies into bullet sponges and battles into grueling endurance fights, while making easy difficulties almost walking simulators. When I was younger, I played on easier difficulties and often used cheat codes because the games were just hard, even on lower difficulties, which meant that they were tuned better to what type of experience level I was expected to have going in; as a kid who had never played a shooter at all in my life, the easier Wolfenstein difficulties were still hard, and I feel that if you are not that familiar or comfortable with Boomer Shooters but want to try them, BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4 makes sure players can experience what the game is doing without punishing them by giving them an extremely reduced version of the "real" game, nor does it force them to play at a difficulty they won't enjoy.

I will say I didn't get to do much with the multiplayer; there are options to play cooperatively and also deathmatch, and I did try deathmatch a few times which was fun, but it wasn't something I've spent a lot of time with in my time with BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER BEFORE 4; I might give it another shot, but for me, the real joy is running, jumping, and gunning through the main campaign. Of course, as the game is less than 10 dollars, the fact that it even has a functional and fun multiplayer at all is magical, really. There is also a rocket jump feature and the game encourages you to replay it, particularly with rocket jumping and otherwise, to pursue specific secrets and challenges; like most older Boomer Shooters, some of the puzzles are obtuse and fairly opaque, really asking players to think a little outside of what is being asked to solve them, but I didn't find myself struggling forever on them, which was something I appreciated; hunting for the single hidden pixel that signals a secret or similar other things I've done in other games is one of the things I like the least in Boomer Shooters, because they break the speed and sense of kinetic energy that the games thrive on.

Perhaps my only regret with the game is that I just don't know enough about Brazilian culture to understand the jokes and references that are present; I recognize some things and people I've picked up through osmosis from having Brazilian friends, but there is a lot going on here and sometimes it is going on so fast that perhaps being familiar with it wouldn't help anyway. This isn't a knock against the game, though, but more a knock against the human experience; I can't know or understand everything, and it is frustrating! But what I can recognize is that this is a game made by people that love their cultural identity, rather than one that is mocking and stereotyping it. And that sense of love, even taken to comedic extremes and absurd weapons like bottles of guarana drink, a sandal, or a shower, feels fun.

I love this game. I think that part is very obvious, but I want to state that the other 3 games are equally as good, and I really do suggest giving them a shot and playing through the series before you get to 4. If you really don't care and just want to know what the hell I'm talking about when I say that double jumping and air dashing because you shoved fireworks up your ass feels great while you shoot enemies with two bottles of Guarana Golly that work like automatic pistols before collecting a key that opens a giant black texture that reads ENTER HERE, then just start with 4 on Steam and have an amazing time for a few hours. I think the game absolutely sings as a single playthrough experience, so clear a few hours for yourself, put on some headphones (or torture those around you) and consider taking a trip to Brazil.
