Malifaux July 2026 Errata - The Tabletop Battles Hot Take

July 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Beware!  I live!

get it? Credit: Williams Electronics

Malifaux Fourth Edition has been out for a little while now - long enough to get a sense of what's working and what's not.  I don't know that anyone expected an errata this soon, but surprise!  Wyrd dropped one a few days ago.  They are still planning to release Gaining Grounds 1, as well as core-rules errata, in September, but they decided to get the jump on the model errata at this time too.

Before we jump in, it's worth shouting out Wyrd's presentation of the errata.  This is one of the most comprehensive and detailed communications I've ever seen from a miniature company accompanying a balance pass, and if this is the standard going forward, that's a very very good thing.  The page is well-organized, presents the updated cards and changelog prominently, and includes a very detailed (and color-coded!) description of both 1) their general principles governing how they make errata decisions and 2) how those principles were applied in these specific cases.

What's more, they directly addressed the models that did not receive errata but who might have expected to, and explained why they got no changes this time.  In a couple cases - Monk and Plague - they plan to do a more comprehensive set of revisions later, but as both keywords are unusually small by model count, they'd like to bring them up to full strength before they start making changes.

This is great!  I love this level of communication.  I don't agree with all of the decisions Wyrd made in this errata, but seeing their reasoning makes me feel much better about the process as a whole.  Good job, Wyrd!

Before we get into this, a note: A chunk of this errata was "clean up," standardizing how abilities work and renaming abilities that had the same name as another ability.  If a model's rules did not substantively change, I will not be discussing it here.

Arcanists

Kaeris, Reborn's crew card uses the Power Bar mechanic to model Fervor, her devotion to the Burning Man.  Unlike most power bars, this one advances very slowly, but never goes down, and provides stacking effects as it increases.  Unfortunately, the action needed to advance it, Call to the Burning Man, sucks, and it imposes a real cost on you just to make your crew do its thing.

The errata updated Call to the Burning Man, increasing its range, giving you a card draw instead of a heal, adding a small teleport, and making it a Signature action.  It's much less costly to spend a Soulstone and an AP doing this now. In addition, you are no longer limited to doing it once per turn, though your Fervor can't go higher than the turn number - so if you miss a turn, you can catch up next turn.

As a tradeoff, at full Fervor the "burning-spread" portion of the crew card ability is slightly weaker, but overall this ability should be much easier to max out and start using.

Otherwise it's just clean-ups here, making sure Banasuva's ability doesn't damage himself for no reason.

Bayou

Two nerfs and a buff here.

Buffs

First, the good news: Alphonse's rules all work now!  Specifically, his Warning Growl matches everyone else's, and his Oops, Too Hard trigger on Toss now kills the target at the end of its movement instead of the beginning.  This is actually a significant buff, because now you can do things like spend three stones and three AP (remember, he treats Glowy tokens as, effectively, Fast tokens) to throw three Stuffed Piglets, inflicting 6 AOE damage in the form of six individual pulses of 1 (i.e. effectively irreducible except against Shielded/Cover) while also drawing six cards.  This is very legal, and very cool.  I have a game in a bit more than an hour from time of writing in which I'm running Alphonse and two Taxidermists.  I will update this article with how it turned out.

gettttttttt dunked on! Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Nerfs

Some bad news: Piglets have been locked to Minion (3) instead of (4) for production reasons, apparently, which is kind of an annoying reason to nerf Sooey.  But more importantly, Squish and Squash have been, well, squashed.  Not only did her attack get a TN (meaning you can't just relent against it forever), not only did her signature action go to once per turn and see a TN increase, not only did the trigger on her signature action change to requiring a stone, but she also lost Ramming Speed.

pour out three: two for the frogs and one for the rider. good night sweet princess. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Squish and Squash was definitely too good but this is an absurd number of nerfs and basically nukes the model out of existence.  Oh well, RIP.  Fun while it lasted.  Any three of these would have been fine, all five together is sending a clear signal that she's not supposed to be competitively viable.

Explorer's Society

Nerfs

We all saw it coming, and the Society has the largest single share of substantive errata so far (well, I guess technically it's tied, since the Rex is technically a Neverborn model and the Fall Into Darkness change isn't supposed to be a substantive nerf but just alignment to an already-issued FAQ).

The Affluenza Brigade has definitely been terrorizing the tables a bit, and the worst offender is Maxine Agassiz, Renowned.  Given how insanely strong her crew is, the change appears modest, but I think it's the perfect size.  Her crew card action, Patterns in Portals, no longer allows a free Interact.

That's it, but that's a hefty change. The free Interact was bananas, giving you an unprecedented amount of scheming flexibility; it was simply impossible to defend against her scheming because, at any time, any of her models could active, walk, and use Patterns to fling an ally 12" and let them interact for free, with no interaction possible. Now it's still an incredible placement tool but it doesn't also give you a bunch of free interacts, so you have to sequence your activations much more carefully.  This also means that you will not be able to use the ability nearly as often, since a big part of the power of it was that the free Interact immediately started to refill your power bar.  I think this was the right level of touch and EVS will still be very strong without being oppressive.

did i love playing EVS? yes. am i sad about this? not even a little bit. Credit: Wyrd Games

The other obvious targets were the OOK/Versatile all-stars: the Emissary, Eva Havenhand and the Alleyway Echo. At least two of these three showed up in almost every list, and that's a sure sign something has to change.

Starting with the lion: his Stalwart Protector now has a 4" range, but is only once per activation. I think we all saw this coming; in an edition when most damage mitigation is once per activation, having an aoe form of mitigation that wasn't stood out. In practice, this means that a model being protected by the lion can expect to take an additional 1-2 damage per activation that it's being focused down, which will add up fast.  If you are cheating defensively and/or your opponent is lobbing attacks opportunistically, rather than in a dedicated way, you may not notice the change at all, but the game's best beaters are all capable of making 3+ 3 damage attacks per activation, and you will definitely notice that. This also makes the lion itself a lot more vulnerable.

he's still gonna be lion around causing trouble. Credit: Wyrd Games 

Also, he's an inch slower, but if you're playing him right you will barely notice, since he can still teleport to friendlies within 6", and his 50mm base means that's a lot of displacement.  You should not have any problems keeping up with him.

Moving on, Eva and the Echo have both been made Loyal, which I think most people saw coming, but the Echo's Secret Passage is no longer a signature action, which I think most people certainly did not.  At a glance, that seems excessive, now that it's locked into the DUA crew.  I get not wanting to have two different signature-Secret-Passage models in the same keyword, but I think they should have given the Echo a little something back in return - maybe increasing the range of Claustrophobia, giving it a built-in Mask for On Your Heels, or replacing Dismember with a real trigger that does something remotely relevant for the crew. Right now it basically jumps, charges, and maybe swings twice.  That's... meh for 9 stones.

the weird blood guy just showed up on two many scientific expeditions. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

these operatives ain't loyal? well, this one is. Credit: Wyrd Games

Oh, and the Malisaurus Rex went up to 10 stones. It's an Apex model, we're talking about it here. Nothing much to talk about. If you don't think it's been worth ten stones this whole time, you're wrong and you should feel bad.

Buffs?!

There's one more very unexpected errata to talk about, though. Seeker is in a bit of a rough spot - none of its models are that exciting except for Cayce Jones, and both of its Master titles are pretty weak.  So Wyrd decided to fundamentally rewrite Jedza, Everlasting.

Some things are staying the same: she still uses Death Tokens, which tack on a free Execute to friendly models' damaging attacks. Her models still get one when they kill a non-peon enemy.  She still shares Lamp Markers and Life Tokens with her original form.  And she still has a beefy 3 irreducible damage attack. (And Unimpeded, of course).  But that's about it.

Let's start with her crew card. Her rules all have the same names, but that's about it.  Dredge Up used to be a Unique-only, once-per-activation action that targeted a friendly Lamp marker within 8" and let the acting model make a melee swing through it, optionally spending a Soulstone for a +twist and the suit of its choice. Now it's just a passive rule: all Seekers can, once per activation, draw line of sight for an attack through a friendly Lamp within 8".  There are some really, really strong ranged and magic attacks in this keyword: Avalanche, Permafrost Breath, Hunt by Moonlight.  What's more, because this isn't an action anymore, you can now Charge and then make your swing through a Lamp that you just Charged into range of.

muffled "oh hell yeah" audible in the distance. Credit: Wyrd Games

Lamp Markers on the table are much more dangerous now, which is good because this keyword veritably pukes them out.

What's more, Font of the Everlasting has changed in an incredibly amusing way.  No more can you cash in Death Tokens for healing; now, you can choose at start of game to get any number of free Soulstones, up to the number of models in your crew, but at a cost.  Each one costs you an Everlasting Debt token that can never be removed; when a model with such a token dies, you not only don't infuse a Soulstone, you lose two.  That's a significant cost, and probably enough that you will very rarely use this ability on anyone but Jedza herself - even counting the extra, that's net -2 soulstones for each model you lose, and this Jedza can't no-sell friendly deaths the way Wanderer can. That said, it's very funny, and there's basically no cost to putting this on Jedza herself.  Also, this crew gulps stones, as you will shortly see, and so it's nice to have extras.  I suppose you could go ham with this ability and just plan to spend all of your stones as fast as humanly possible, before you start losing Debt-ridden models.  You can't have negative soulstones.

Anyways, moving to Everlasting's stat card: buckle up. Jedza's stats are the same, except she's gone from 13 health to 6.  When something like that happens it behooves you to read her card very closely. And here is the reason: when (not if) she dies, you can spend a Soulstone to heal to full and gain a Death token.  You can do this any number of times per turn, and the wording means that even if you already have a Death token it still works (you just don't double up).  Anti-healing tech is rare, and she's probably a dead drop into, say, Kastore, but short of that, good luck ever putting this woman down.  It's not even a Demise, so stuff like Killer Late Fees has no effect!

really loud OH HELL YEAH right next to your ear. Credit: Wyrd Games

She has also lost Weight of the Grave, i.e. third edition's Drain Life, for Light of the Grave - she can always draw LoS for her non-general Tactical Actions through friendly Lamp Markers within 8".  What Tactical Actions? Well...

Moving to her back of card, we'll start with her attack, since it has changed the least.  It's still a 2" reach stat 6, damage 3 irreducible attack.  She still has Mangle.  It's the other triggers that have changed.

spark status: drained. Credit: Wyrd Games

She can't give herself Life tokens anymore, but as we just discussed, she doesn't need them.  Instead, she has Siphon Essence, which fuels her Soulstone hunger and also pairs well with Mangle, since they have the same suit and each one only functions in situations where the other one doesn't.  She's also replaced Death Denied, which let her cash in Death Tokens for +2 damage instead of the Execute effect, for Strange Eons, a Soulstone-powered trigger that deals 2 damage to Jedza herself and lets her swing again.  Notably missing: any once-per-activation or -turn limit.

Yeah, you can swing six times if you keep hitting.

See what I mean about Soulstones?  She's going to be absolutely chugging them, but if you can keep her fed, you have an unkillable beatstick that ignores all forms of damage reduction and strips your hand and/or Soulstones while she's killing you.

Moving to Tacticals, she keeps her summon - at half its former range, but that's ok, because you're drawing LoS through a Lamp Marker, aren't you?  And it no longer requires a Life or Death token to function.  It just... happens. She no longer has Thread Unwoven, which was really just a complex way to turn a Life Token into a Death Token, and now has On Your Heels, which is a fun way to fling her into combat with something that needs to die.

She has traded out Waypoint, her somewhat-jank Lamp-generating Signature action, for Deadly Illumination, a vastly superior version that 1) doesn't require a friendly model to anchor the new Lamp to and 2) hands out a free Death token, not even requiring a raise or trigger, just for the cost of admission. Also, insult to injury, the TN actually went down. And it has a trigger to give you a Life Token, which as noted, is less essential now that she's unkillable, but it will save you a stone here and there by requiring one more attack to trigger her regeneration.

Finally, she has a brand new fourth action, and it's a doozy.  Another signature, On Deadly Ground pushes an ally a modest 3" for a bargain-basement TN, and gives it an Aura (Hazardous) token to boot. Plus, it has a Soulstone trigger to let the pushed model attack at a +twist! You won't often have spare Soulstones, but this lets you do very funny things like push the Damned into someone, have it swing for 2 damage plus a possible Puncture or Mental Trauma, and then have the 2" push that's part of its attack sequence resolve and deal an additional damage to the enemy due to the Hazarous aura.  Get wrecked!

Although, I think what I'll probably use this for most often is to nudget Mr. Goo into someone so I'm swinging Death Punches into them at stat 7.

Overall, I really like this new Jedza.  I think she opens up some cool possibilities for the Seeker keyword.  I still think you bring Cayce Jones - he's just insanely strong, even when you are pressured for stones - but I can see an argument for leaving him at home in return for something like the Hand of Janus, which gives you some ranged punch, or the Emissary to protect your squishy little Grave Goo until it can do its job.

Guild

Ok, so back to the quick hits. Guild is in a decent place overall with one glaring exception: the Nightcrawler Network is wayyyyyy too good at disruption and scheming/counterscheming, mostly because The Network means you can functionally put Camerabots basically anywhere they might be useful.  Also he's Damage 3 for some reason.

the rise of streaming services has been tough on him. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Anyways, he is now Loyal, so other Guild crews will have to find their own do-everything scheme runner.  For good measure, he's gone up a stone, and The Network has been changed: now the Camerabot must be in range, not just the terrain it's "attached" to.  That means functionally 1) it has a greatly reduced ranged compared to before and 2) it still has a colossal range since you can place a Camerabot 8" away, then draw LOS through it to place another one 8" from there, plus base width. He'll still be hired by every Guard and Journalist list from now until the heat death of the universe, which is fine.

Neverborn

Team Purple has been putting up some great results too, so it shouldn't be surprising that they're getting bad-touched.  First on the list is the Syzygy Sisters, who have been even more omnipresent than the Alleyway Echo.  A triple tap here: they're down to Spd 6, they can no longer declare actions on their Upgrades as signatures, and Fallow Night has been renamed and is once per activation.

they are still both filthy and gorgeous. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

The net result of this is that the Sisters aren't going to be solo-scoring "drop two markers" Schemes like Ensnare and Detonate, and their offensive output is a bit reined in since they can't attack more than twice per activation (at least with their real attacks).  They're still good, but they aren't clearly superior to every in-keyword alternative.

The other aspect of purple that needed to be looked at is Euripedes, whose crew cards are just way to full of stuff.  Wyrd wrote a little mea culpa here - Savage is a small keyword, with just nine models, and so they pushed it a little hard in testing.  Sometimes you push things too hard and they break.

The changes are purely to the crew cards here, which is a good place to start.  First, across both cards, Numb has been nerfed a bit - it used to make a model functionally not exist for Scheming purposes, but now Numb models can still bodyguard markers they're standing on and their Don't Turn Your Back is still live. So there's a bit more interaction possible. Both titles got a second tap as well - Euripedes, Hierophant's Frozen Vigor, the "push an ally and give them an Insight token" signature action, went from 5" to 3" of movement. And Euripedes, Old One Eye's Giant's Skin simply disappeared entirely, while Ancient Winds, the signature attack that pushed targets and sometimes gave them Frozen Solid tokens, is once per activation.

oh no, whatever will you do with your 2" less movement on your signature action that your whole crew gets that also gives out insight? Credit: Wyrd Games

at least you can kill 'em now. Credit: Wyrd Games

you can be a little more comfortably numb. Credit: Wyrd Games

Even all of this might not be enough because The Land Yields is still one of the most insane abilities ever printed onto a crew card, but Wyrd says that with Informants leaving the pool in a couple of months, they want to try leaving it in for now.  I think they may regret that, but we'll see.

Oh, and the Malisaurus costs 10 stones, as discussed.

Outcasts

Freikorps Scouts have Sniper now.

wheeee! Credit: Wyrd Games

That's literally it.

Resurrectionists

Gang Green has been underperforming a bit, so it's mixed news here.

Nerfs

First, the bad stuff: Asura Roten makes way, way too many zombies.  Sorry, that's just how it is. The number of zombies she makes, when compared against what might reasonably be expected given her cost, durability, other functionality, and the difficult of making them, is, considered objectively, elevated above the ideal level of zombies for her to be making.

Now she makes fewer zombies.  Her Return of the Living Dead (now Dawn of the Dead) has a higher TN and is once per activation.  You cannot simply cheat two 9s and get 6 zombies anymore.  Forget about it.

fewer zombies, more... well, not more anything. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Also, Graveyard Dust now just does +1 damage when you cash in a zombie instead of +2.  You'll still use it.

I think Asura is much more of a sometimes food now.  This is a serious nerf, but the model's ceiling was so high, it still has room to be good.  My concern is that green isn't doing so hot, and Asura was kind of load-bearing, so we'll see what they can put together without her.

Buffs

Ah, Seamus!  An iconic Master with a brand new lease on life this edition (see my Redchapel crew focus for more details).  While Seamus, the Last Breath has been performing, his more dapper and refined alter ego AKA Sebastian Baker has not.  So there's a pretty significant rework here.

First, as with Jedza, the crew card. Pounce is the same as it was, but he's swapped out Vicious Mockery, the ability that let his models cash in Entranced tokens on enemy models to put that enemy on a -twist to resist attacks, for the new ability "A Favor, Darling..." What this means is that Entranced tokens on enemies function a little bit like Numb tokens: they turn off enemies' ability to disrupt your Interact actions via engagement or standing on markers.

if you're so Entranced by me, then surely you Don't Mind Me just dropping a scheme marker right here... Credit: Wyrd Games

The crew card has also picked up an action, Hide the Bodies, a once-per-activation signature tactical available to unique Redchapel models that plops down a Remains marker anywhere within 6" and eats all enemy markers within 1" of the dropped marker.  This is very strong, mostly as AOE marker remove, and is going to really screw some crews to the wall.

Looking at his card, he has changed much less than Jedza.  His stats are identical, as is his front of card, although he's lost Bask in the Spotlight (the ability that let him heal when nearby enemies became Entranced) and gained Beguile, which gives him a frankly colossal 12" aura in which enemies Entranced models can't Interact.  That's a biiiiig deal and makes him incredibly annoying to deal with on, say, Boundary Dispute.

let's call it Hyper-Chatty. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

His Bladed Bouquet has traded Coordinated Attack for Execute. That's it - the real changes are in his other actions. Grandiose Promises keeps its name, but that's about it.  At launch, it was a bit awkward - a short-ranged cast attack that did hefty damage and let you push your target's nearby friends away while letting your own engaged models swing on the target.  Cute in concept, but a lot of hoops to jump through.

It's a lot simpler now, but much easier to get value out of.  The range has doubled from 6" to 12", and while the damage has gone down to 2, it now Entrances the target (or deals the original 3 damage if the target is already Entranced).  A ranged source of Entrance is important for this crew now that Seamus has that big bubble of no-Interacting while Entranced.  There are also three very nice triggers here: "You Dropped That" removes all enemy Markers near the target, Smooth Talker spreads out Entranced, and it even has Coordinated Attack for a bite of additional damage.

This attack is likely going to be your bread and butter.  Tiri having a 12" range Earthquake that turns off Scheming is a big part of what makes her good, and this is sort of similar (though enemies can walk out of it, or attack you to drop their Entranced). It's more of a tax than an absolute prohibition, but that's pretty strong in and of itself. The attack also has some ranged anti-Scheming potential, which is always useful.

Finally, Seamus's Special Arrangements tactical signature action has become an attack, and also does something totally different.  It's only got a 6" range, but it's potent when it lands: you pick one effect from a menu, plus one per raise.  The effects are:

  • Move the target 6"
  • Drop a Scheme Marker touching the target
  • If enemy, make the target Craven.

This is much more similar to Tiri's attack, though the shorter range is a significant downside.  However, it can also hit friendlies, and that's where the sauce is.  Eagle-eyed readers might spot that "move 6" and drop a Scheme Marker" is a bargain-basement version of Maxine's just-nerfed crew card action - though, since it needs a raise, you won't be able to relent if you want this effect.  But hitting an enemy and getting even one raise is going to be brutal.  Hell, it's fine even without a raise: just dropping a Scheme Marker in base contact with a friendly 6" away is worth the price of admission.  The triggers are the same as they were, so you can still summon Dead Dandies if that's really something you want to do.

Overall, this version of Seamus is much more scheme-focused than his original.  Without Secret Passage he won't be able to dart around the board dropping Schemes at will, but he can disrupt enemy scheming and place markers a good distance away through prudent use of his signature.

Ten Thunders

Lots of changes here, too, almost matching Explorer's Society.

Nerfs

We all know that Red Library is a bit too good right now. Wyrd specifically noted that Linh Ly, Storyteller is on their radar, but because they have to hit Story of Suzaku, they're leaving her alone for the moment.  That could change if this change doesn't do the trick.

For the uninitiated, Storyteller gives her Story models a once-per-turn Demise to discard a card and heal 3.  That's way too good with the Hard to Kill Suzaku; if it's at 2 hp you effectively have to hit it a minimum of 4 times in one activation to kill it.  So it has lost Hard to Kill for a new Demise, Rite of Rebirth, that lets it eat a nearby Rift Marker to heal 1 when it would die.  This preserves (or maybe even increases) its durability in Oni while cutting it substantially in Red Library - an elegant fix.

dying and coming back is a lot more on-brand for a phoenix than not dying in the first place, tbh. Credit: Wyrd Games

The other big problem in Orange is Mei Feng.  Everyone who has played against her knows it, and she's in contention for best Master in the game right now. The other candidate, Maxine, ate a nerf, so this was coming; it's just not fun to have Mei Feng launch herself across the table and murder you from infinity inches away.

Some significant changes here.  The biggest is to her crew card: Ride the Rails ate a triple nerf.  First of all, you must have a Scrap Marker to target to declare the action, and second of all, it's once per activation. Now you can't just spend your first activation spamming out Scrap with a Metal Gamin to set up a railroad for the rest of your squad. And finally, the attack you get from the Defend the Line trigger no longer comes with a free +twist.  Why did it ever?

it's called "ride the rails" not "spew out lumps of jagged metal everywhere." Credit: Wyrd Games

Because this risks you being shut out of Scrap entirely, she's gained a new crew card action, Restart the Line, which lets you just drop a Scrap Marker within 6" if there are none on the table.  Hopefully you never have to take this!

Mei Feng herself has also been toned down.  Constant Motion requires you to successfully resolve a trigger, not just declare it.  Lots of people didn't even know that that worked, but yes, the way timing works you declare triggers before determining duel success, so you could declare triggers on every action you took whether they succeeded or not.  And Tiger Strike got doubly changed: first, it places Mei in contact with the targeted ally, rather than the marker they just made, which knocks about 7" off of her displacement; second, you now place or attack.

she's still going to kick your face off, she just needs to jog a little first. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

I think Mei crews' playstyle will have to change now that they can't just roundhouse kick your head off from the next zip code, but she's still mobile and deadly, with a solid crew, so she'll be fine.

Finally, Yasunori's Fan the Storm went up one point of TN and is now once per activation.  Probably fine; mobility in this game overall is pretty high, it doesn't need to be that much higher.

a little less pinball, a little more killing. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Buffs

But it ain't all bad news!  Asami has, historically, been tough for Wyrd to balance, seesawing between garbage and terrifying. In her current iteration, Asami, Oni Mother struggled from having Rift Marker-centric gameplay built on a pretty tough Rift Marker economy.  Especially given how many of your Rift Marker actions and abilities require you to remove a Rift, you had to spend a lot of AP to keep parity.

i like the flavor of this one, too; as they flicker out of existence they tear a little exit wound in reality. Credit: Wyrd Games

Now, all dying Oni models leave a Rift Marker behind.  It may not seem like much, but Asami's Flickering summons all now leave a Rift behind when they pop out of existence, and that will really add up.

The changes to Asami's card are more substantial. Beyond's Reward has been reworked; you don't get to eat Flickering friendlies for Soulstones anymore, but you do get a Rift Marker every Asami activation just for showing up. It also pulses out a test for Slow, giving you a free, slightly weaker version of the Repellant Passage trigger (which used to cost a stone!).

it's a "hmmmmmmmm" from me, chief. Credit: Wyrd Games

Credit: Wyrd Games

Asami has lost Open the Gate, which she no longer needs and her signature has moved to Demonic Tresses. Tresses has gained a fourth trigger, "Feed!", which lets a targeted friendly attack, giving her a lot more aggressive punch.  More significant, though, is the change to From the Maw: for two Soulstones, the Infuse Substance trigger means you show up without a Summon token.

I'll be honest: I'm a little nervous about this.  Summons that don't have all of the natural downsides of Summons are, historically, a pretty dangerous thing.  There's a natural balancing element here in that Asami's Summons Flicker and therefore never stick around more than a turn, but still, being able to interact with Strategy markers is a big deal.

What's Not Here

This is a pretty comprehensive errata, which is great to see.  Wyrd acknowledges that there is more to come - specifically, for Monk and Plague - so we'll have to keep an eye on how things develop.

Still, there are a few notable omissions, some of which we may see next time. Everyone will have their own list, of course, but off the top of my head, I can think of a few particular models and Keywords that I'd like to see get some attention.

The headliner, of course, is Resurrectionists as a whole, who I think will be struggling even more after this update.  It's impossible to fully balance a game as large and complex as Malifaux, but the stinky boys and girls have had a rough go of it in 4th Edition.

In terms of crews that aren't over- or under-performing but could use a look, I think Kin suffered a bit from some late changes in the open beta and is pretty polarizing, although I would be shocked if her win rate is excessive, especially with one of her best pieces nuked from orbit. I think the power budget could afford to be spread out a bit there.  Ophelia, Overloaded also does nothing interesting right now.

I also am not a fan of the play pattern Rusty Alyce, Trigger Happy has - giving her summons a signature action to eat a Scheme Marker for a Soulstone is kinda nuts, and the restrictions just aren't enough to really let you safely scheme against her.

Finally, I think we're due for an overall cleanup to some of the core rules.  The Leave Your Mark FAQ ruling is still pretty insane and I can't wait for it to either get fixed or rotate out.

But these are quibbles.  It's a brave new world out there!  What do you think?  Is this errata going to make you rethink the way you build crews?  Are there any models who have been gathering dust but who might now make the cut for you?  Sound off in the comments
