August 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

After Wednesday's article I decided to dig in a little bit more to the data, with some help from StatCheck's Jeremy Atkinson, who pulled this data on win rates by Disposition.

This is pretty even, with the glaring exception of Disruption, which just isn't very good, particularly into Priority assets, where it only wins 42% of the time. The only positive Disruption matchup appears to be into Purge the Foe, but that's also within expected ranges and because Disruption just isn't taken often, it likely won't be on a Purge player's radar.

On Wednesday we also looked at the distribution of Dispositions among lists, and found that Take and Hold and Priority Assets each accounted for about 30% of the field, making up more than 60% of the total field. If we restricted that to just players finishing in the top 8 last weekend, that remains true, but with some important shifts:

Disposition Distribution, Top 8 players, week 31 2026
  • Take and Hold - 38.4%
  • Priority Assets - 28.6%
  • Reconnaissance - 14.3%
  • Purge the Foe - 12.5%
  • Disruption - 6.3%

I was surprised to learn that top players are more likely to take Disruption, but less shocked to see Take and Hold as the overwhelming favorite among them, given how many good Detachments offer it as a disposition.

Thoughts

I had Garrett write this one up, since he plays Space Wolves and needed the reps doing list analysis on his preferred faction.

Garrett: This is an interesting one, and puts the Take and Hold mirror with two brick-based, durable armies to the test. These are similar play styles, with 4W bricks of durable dudes who can leverage up-downs, with similar reactive moves, melee output, and numbers of units. Interestingly, both of these make some solid efforts to counter the other’s buffs as well. Lions have easy access to +1 to hit and wound, negating some of the durability of Wolf Guard Terminators, while the one unattached Wolf Guard Terminators squad creates a real problem for the Allarus Terminators, who rely on their rerolls to get work done. On the fringes of the Terminator slap fight, we also see the unstoppable force of the Wolf Guard Headtakers against the immovable object of the Wardens, which also brings to light one important dynamic of this matchup - rolling 4+ is absolutely a thing.

These two armies are, on paper, fairly evenly matched, and Mario managed to get a full 20-0 in the fifth round, after facing two other similar Custodes armies and getting the same score. So either Mario is the avatar of the pagan god of 4++, or there was probably some skill involved. While similar on paper and similar in tricks, to me the real difference maker here was likely Wolves' ability to push tempo in a way that the Custodes just couldn’t hang with. With turn one Deep Strikes, double Rapid Ingress, Heroic shenanigans, and buffed charges, the Wolves are able to get stuck in, and cause problems early, with Terminators, while having Headtakers and their incredible output in the wings, without ever giving the opponent time to breath. Once the Custodes bricks begin to break, they quickly run out of the output needed to put a dent into Wolf Guard, especially if the Sons of Fenris can pounce early before the Lions positioning buffs come into play. This was likely exacerbated by Yannick’s list packing slightly smaller squads of guys, and the stress that the Bjorn/Logan (neither of whom is a slouch at killing Custodian guard, either) combo creates on CP economy, and once that dam breaks, it is going to roll to a 20-0 quickly.

Great work by Mario, I love to see my favorite boys win.

Thoughts

The Exorcist makes a comeback! I asked my buddy TwoHorse to talk about this one, because it's way easier to farm out this list analysis to faction experts than to try and write it myself. This is probably why I'm always just losing my first game into a particular army each edition, but what can you do. I've copied his Discord message pretty much verbatim here because that was funnier than rewriting it.

TwoHorse: Hell yeah this list slaps. It’s basically the old-school 10e bringers plan with less stuff. No triumph in favor of more tanks, but you don’t have to cross the board all at once if you’re on Priority Assets. The CoC detachment is just for the exorcist upgrade; the list has no fly girls so the detachment rule and two thirds of the stratagems do nothing. But that’s enough because the exorcist lets you finish off whatever the castigators spike down into (and they will spike down, because castigators are really swingy). Fraye helps with that too by tagging something with lethal hits so all the heavy bolters have a better chance of contributing into hard targets.

There's tons of flamers for chaff between the Immolators/dominions, and the split dominions give you screening for the center objective with their reactive move so they or the solo Canoness can mash the PA button for 5 turns while you kill them on their side of the board if they don’t shove you T1. The detachment rule is assault; none of the strats do anything except armor of contempt. It’s just playing honest Warhammer. Imagine Company of Hunters but worse in every way except the one that matters: drip.
