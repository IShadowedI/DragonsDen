StarCraft: Tabletop Miniatures Game Wave 2 Reveal - The Tabletop Battles Hot Take

August 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

StarCraft the Miniatures Game showed off their next wave of releases. Wave 2 focuses around heavy support, adding Big Guys for Terran, Protoss, and Zerg. In addition we’re also getting a proper release with rules for Zeratul, which has been their event promo mini for a while now. We’ll be going through the releases today and taking a look at the rules they showed off (although they’ve noted they aren’t final) and giving some opinions on them.

Siege Tank

Norman: I’m very stoked to see these guys ready to roll out. This will be the first unit in the game to actually use the Blast Template rules (although the ravager accomplishes something similar) and it’ll be interesting to see if that shakes up how folks approach playing the game and making lists. While you can’t deny the lethality here, with a 5+ armor and it requiring an upgrade and a CP to transform to siege mode you’re absolutely paying for the privilege. Do I think that you should try to run two of these or even just one and a Goliath? Absolutely not. Am I gonna do it anyway? Absolutely. Also very excited that you can build it in either siege or normal mode and while it's a bit disappointing you can’t transform it, it makes sense as that would be a nightmare for painting, playing, and everything else.

The elephant in the room here is the two sources of indirect fire that’s being introduced here, both from the Smart Shells upgrade and the Coordinated Strike ability that comes with the Siege Mode upgrade. On their face these are incredibly strong abilities when paired with the killing power. That said I think it’ll be ok if things are priced appropriately and because this game is so focused around teleporting around the table that getting a line on a Siege Tank shouldn’t be too hard. That said, we’re just gonna have to play with it to see what's up.

Condit: I'm never a huge fan of indirect fire in wargames, and ever since I first heard this game was being developed I've been worried about how everybody's favorite sci-fi tank would wind up being implement. Thankfully, I think Smart Shells likely manages to find a good balance. It's a solid ability, but not only is it a once-per-game effect, it also uses the Indirect Fire trait, which will let your target take an Evade roll if you can't see it. Add in an additional -1 to hit if you're using the extra range it offers and you have an ability that, while unquestionably powerful, has some tradeoffs that will make it interesting to play as and around.

That said, I am a bit concerned about Coordinated Fire. Letting the tank ignore range as long as it has a spotter and reach out and touch something on the other side of the board without allowing an Evade roll might wind up feeling oppressive, especially when you remember that Aftershock Rounds means it's dropping a pie-plate full of 2-damage attacks on a lot of units. Granted, the fact that you have to be sieged up to use it combined with the multiple ways most opponents will have to redeploy to punish you for sieging in a bad spot will require some skill to get the most use out of this. But the fact that you can use Coordinated Strike to ready a cross-table attack in the same activation that you sieged up could wind up being a real pain to deal with. We'll have to see how it plays out, but we'll be keeping an eye on this one.

Norman: The new Factory is also a nice add. It's nothing mind blowing and clearly is there to combo with the Siege Tank, it's a good ability and can pair nicely with the goliath if you take the big missiles.

Ravager

AshC: Ravagers look like they’re really going to open a completely new dynamic in how Zerg can play. At the moment all I’ve seen people working with is massed Roaches or massed Zerglings, and the Ravager looks like it’s going to support both archetypes (but especially melee options) while also opening up more options for combined arms.  It’s going to be a consistent 1 BM tax, but (with upgrades) that’s 2 tokens coming down that hit everything in a ~4.6” radius for 5 hits each. Doesn’t seem like much, but those hits can also put down a -1 Armour debuff, meaning your Zerglings will get to crumple pretty much anything they touch. I’m not particularly enthused about indirect coming into the game, but I think something as broadcast as Ravagers are is the best way to go about it, since there’s direct counterplay besides maximising space within coherency.

The main issue I’m seeing is timing the Corrosive Bile, since you do it in the Movement Phase. You’re going to be broadcasting where your blasts are going to go, which means it’s really important to weigh it against gaining priority. What I’m looking at as the normal play pattern might be two Ravagers in a unit dropping one Corrosive Bile towards two different units with Zerglings lined up to charge both. Whichever doesn’t run away gets charged and locked down, and if neither runs away the world’s your oyster.

I’m also not sure if you ever want to run more than a single unit of these, though you almost definitely want to run them in pairs. Upgrades wise I think you almost always take Bloated Bile Ducts and Potent Bile, and pretty much never take Burrow Ambush, since that ends your activation and stops you from popping off a Corrosive Bile. On top of that they already have a reposition ability, so that’s a 1 BM tax turn 1 to position them forwards: the turn that’s most likely to have you not use Corrosive Bile in the first place.

Cocoon is interesting since it doesn’t look to provide BM at the moment, the first card so far that doesn’t generate any faction resource at all. Seems like it’s a card that’s designed to support the Ravager/Zergling pairing, since you get to respawn Zergling variants (though not Kraptors, boo hiss) or take your utility Roach unit and swap one out for a Ravager. I’m not sure how useful a single Ravager is going to be, especially since it won’t have any upgrades, but at a minimum you’re gaining a supply and an independent screening unit that can hold an objective.

Immortal

Condit: It's so nice to see my big, beautiful baby finally take the field. The forces available to Protoss in the launch wave left the faction feeling somewhat incomplete, but I've been holding out hope that the Immortal will fill in those missing roles. And honestly? It delivers. Not only does it provide some punishing anti-Armoured weaponry, it also gives you a nice tanky piece to sit in the middle of the board and dare your opponent to come deal with it.

Thanks to its Improved Barrier ability, its already-impressive 6 shield points are going to be that much tougher for your opponents to chew through, and either of its weapons are solid choices, depending on what you need from it. The stock Photon Disruptors will let you throw some reasonably reliable anti-Armoured hate a little further downrange than your opponent might expect, while upgrading to Phase Disruptors will make you a little deadlier into all targets at the cost of having to work a little closer in. Its two passive ability upgrades each complement one of those weapons nicely: For The Ancients adds Precision (1) to attacks more than 8" away to help mitigate the hit penalty from Long Range on the Photon Disruptors, while Fury Unyielding will let you assert total dominance over a mission marker by blasting anyone foolish enough to contest it clear out of the Koprulu Sector.

Its last upgrade, Shield Overcharge, might be my favorite, though. It costs 1PE and only works as long as it still has shields, but it gives your Immortal Tough (2) against a roll, great for situations where your opponent biffed their surge roll (or just didn't have the right keyword). Of course, if you find yourself needing to get rid of some surge dice, you can always look to the Power Field tactics card, but the new Robotics Facility included with your Immortal strikes a balance, offering both Tough (1) and Dodge (1) on a friendly Mechanical Ground unit. It's a nice ability to have in your back pocket, and the 2 Elite slots will let you bring both an Immortal and a pair of Stalkers to make use of it.

Zeratul and Nezarim Watchers

Condit: The Dark Prelate finally has rules, and also brings along some for the Nerazim forces backing him up. Zeratul himself is more or less what you were likely expecting, gaining Hidden any time he resolves any action and sporting a 1PE repeatable reaction to push his nearly-always-available 5+ Evade roll up to a respectable 4+ when someone manages to get close enough to attack him. Add in a Place (6) effect, a speed debuff ability to lock down opponents, and a couple ways to juice his melee output, and he seems like he'll deliver on the melee assassin fantasy everyone was hoping to see from him. Unfortunately (for Protoss players, anyway) he's the only Dark Templar that will be able to hit the table for now, but he's plenty cool in his own right.

If you're looking to field Zeratul's Nerazim, their faction card is also what you'll want to see. You can flip it to give a Biological unit the Hidden status, which is a useful trick to help keep a key unit of Adepts or Zealots safe before you're ready to engage them. Or if you'd rather lean into the "unseen assassin" angle, you can use it when a friendly unit loses Hidden to boost both its speed and the power of its next attack. We'll see how this faction shakes out compared to the other two released so far, but if nothing else it's absolutely dripping with theme and really helps sell the fantasy of playing as Zeratul's exiled Protoss brethren.

Zeratul also brings along his Nerazim Watchers, a special Adept replacement unit. Its Glaive Cannon ranged attack picks up Pinpoint, and it keeps Psionic Transfer, but everything else here is new. Instead of adding Precision to enemies nearby, this unit's Shade token strips the Hidden status from any enemies within 6". Adding insult to injury, they can spend 1PE in any phase to gain Hidden until they perform another action. Adding another way to reveal Hidden units is an interesting trick for this unit to have, and while it's likely not enough to justify taking Zeratul or the Nerazim faction, if you wanted those options anyway, these guys are probably worth a look.

Finally, there's the Void Seeker tactical card. It's unique, locked to the Nerazim subfaction, and only offers a single Core slot. In exchange you can flip it to either give a unit the Hidden status or to return an unengaged unit to reserves and immediately deploy it again. Hidden is cool and all, but that second ability is truly wild, and will often feel like it's allowing you to get two tactics cards' worth of power out of flipping a single card. If you're playing Nerazim, you're taking this. Full stop.

Overall Thoughts

Norman: We love big models here don’t we folks. Lots of cool releases coming down the pipe along with a pleasant surprise of a whole new subfaction for Protoss. I’m happy to see some more curve balls entering the game as these additions will add some significant reasons to bring more anti armor and more puzzles to solve on the table.

Condit: A nice selection of solid centerpiece models helps round out each of the factions, and the Nerazim faction offers an interesting spin on the Protoss units you're already used to seeing. Each of the new units does a good job of filling a role their respective armies were missing while still helping ensure that there's meaningful distinctions between them. I fully expect to see a good number of these units taking the field, if only because they're all really cool looking models with some abilities that you can't find anywhere else. And if that winds up being the case, it could offer a new lease on life to several units and loadout options with the Surge: Armoured ability.
