Mechanicus II: The Tabletop Battles Review

May 29th, 2026 via Goonhammer
What makes a game 'iconic'? It's a bold question to ask when starting a review of a brand-new game, but I feel the designation rings true for Mechanicus, the antecedent game to our subject today. The original Mechanicus gave us one of the best Warhammer game soundtracks to date, on top of being richly quoteable. "From the moment I understood the weakness of my flesh...", that line? That's from Mechanicus, and the lead author for both games, Black Library's own Ben Counter. Few factions in the 41st millennium are blessed with a game that captures their theme & tone, with so many little touches that pierced the very heart of what the Adeptus Mechanicus are about.



I say all of this to compare it to Mechanicus II, which resembles the predecessor more in name than in spirit. For those who were fans of the original Mechanicus, there's really just surface-level similarities to many of the game's systems, with its scope & story being greatly increased—a choice that causes friction with the gameplay. Many facets have been expanded-upon, with the customization technically being far deeper, and unit selection across both of the game's playable factions technically having greater depth, but the ways in which these truths need to be qualified will make up the lion's share of my complaints about Mechanicus II. As a bit of background, I 100%ed the first installment, recommended it to a number of friends, and own a cool ~4000pts of Admech in plastic; I speak as someone enfranchised with the medium here, and it's important to note that Mechanicus II is a solid introduction for someone entirely new to the 40k universe. The narrative of the game is perhaps its brightest point, as alongside a huge update in graphics, the game's presentation is just fantastic. It's no AAA title, but I have zero complaints with the way things look. How they sound, that's a different story.

Sights & Sounds



To get it out of the way, musically this is a staggering disappointment, especially given the composer—Guillaume David—is the same as the first game. I'd love to know if the direction he received was radically different, or whether the prior installment was too punchy, too intrusive with everything else going on, but man. The fact you can purchase Mechanicus' soundtrack for use in its sequel as day 1 DLC is telling, and not unlike Total War Warhammer's blood DLC, it shows there's at least a bit of acknowledgement that something's missing in the core experience with its absence. Thus far, I've put in ~25 hours into the game, completing the Mechanicus campaign, with a few hours into that of the Necron, so most of my opinions are informed by that experience. In terms of the vocal performances there's been a shockingly wide divide among folks I've spoken with as to whether those of the Cult Mechanicus ought to speak in binharic, or understandable Low Gothic (which I'll refer to as 'English'). Not to bring up Mechanicus I again, but one of the tiny touches that likely spawned as a cost-savings was to have only the Necrons speak in English, with the Cultmech characters you controlled communicating through wonderfully crunchy technological chatter. The default option is for everyone to use English in Mechanicus II, with an option to return to the previous division, only added following an outcry after the game's demo released. That said, the number of chatter sequences is minimal, and clearly tacked-on as a panicked addition. I'm of two minds here, because on the one hand, the vocal performances are great! Regardless of your choice, during skirmishes you'll hear the English performances, and they were nearly good enough to get me to switch away from binharic. On the other hand, thematically, making your protagonists more alien than the literal robots is a wonderful bit of juxtaposition. Of note is that the number of lines available to that of the Necrons is far lower, with none of their leader characters having essentially any lines compared to the returning named characters from Mechanicus I.



You'll be getting very familiar with these spoken roles, because unlike the first game there's no true mix-and-match customization, instead having every skirmish led by a leader, 5 for each side. These are the previous questgivers from the original game, now striding onto the field themselves; throughout the game, you'll upgrade them bit by bit, unlocking different weapons & abilities that allow for some wildly disparate build paths. The issue is that, by tying so tightly the function of the gameplay to your named characters, every mission requires they survive every fight. Instead of sallying out with your Skitarii to crush the enemy, you're usually hidden from sight, only poking out to secure kills for fear of a restart. That sucks, and is very un-40k. Instead of building your crack squad of specialized tech-adepts, with a few select troops accompanying them, Mechanicus II is all about the rank & file you bring to combat, with substantial focus placed on things like cover & resource generation. I don't dislike any of the leaders we get to play with, mind you, but exchanging true customization (including a Dawn of War-style army color tool) for playing as a named character feels too eerily similar to actual 40k for my liking; it's the same sensation of a Chapter Master you built from scratch, versus running Dante. Neither is the wrong option, but it's a choice made in contrast to the precedent set by Mechanicus I.

Robots & Resources



Speaking of, now that the conflict of focus is truly planetary in scale, rather than just a series of tombs, some other systems have changed to compensate. Instead of being on a timer, and needing to expend resources and actions to destroy potentially-valuable control panels to delay said ticking clock, Mechanicus II's sense of urgency comes in the form of pop-up missions you...complete, when they come up. Because your leaders no longer use the same currency to upgrade as your troops cost to deploy, once you're through the early game anything but the hardest setting of Mechanicus II lacks a stressed economy. Missions provide tokens to be spent on the leaders used, so those you don't play as fall behind, meaning you're basically just alternating between 2-3 options in a given campaign. The importance of movement is also reduced, no longer needing to gather it from pylons for the use of your best weaponry. Instead, each troop generates Cognition (the currency during skirmishes themselves) by performing an appropriate battlefield role: Rangers shoot at a distance, Servitors get smacked, etc. This is different from how Necrons generate their own skirmish resource, Dominion, which is instead achieved by stacking damage in quite a cool but straightforward fashion. The issue is mostly that generating these resources doesn't require risk, or an opportunity cost; if you're doing the thing you already ought to be, you'll usually have enough Cognition/Dominion during a combat to tie you over.

As for something I won't draw any comparisons to the first game about, let's talk Necrons. Necrons function as you would expect, being slow, implacable killing machines that get spookier the longer they're online. While the leaders do individually scale, like with the Admech campaign, their special upgrades are shared among the court, meaning you can't easily double up on critical buffs (which is a good thing). Your unit selection is also different, with each leader having their own troops to command; if you're playing Hehki, you're all about scarabs & the canoptek side of Necrons, while Obasis is Triarchs for days. You don't simply take the same squads on every mission, and while the leaders themselves aren't as differentiated as with the Admech campaign, the fact their troops are locked makes them overall feel moreso. The troop-centric nature of this game is likely why. The gameplay in Mechanicus II feels more resonant with the story & tone of the Necrons, with pivotal lords leading swarms of soulless automata, and it shines as a bright spot in the otherwise solid if not extraordinary gameplay loop. I can already tell you I'm having more fun with them than I had with the Admech campaign (outside of Electro-Priests, huge shoutout).

Highs & Lows



The original Mechanicus is in many ways closer to a longform roguelite than it is anything else, while its sequel is firmly in the narrative strategy game world. The end goal of the original was to complete missions with no deaths, and no time added to your in-game clock, with the resource of the world beneath your feet awakening providing a suitable push-and-pull. That urgency is absent from Mechanicus II, because it's got a story to tell, and won't let any systems or player failures get in the way (at least on the Normal/Hard difficulties). So, how is the story? I would say...ambitious, and broadly in a positive way. There's plenty to love if you're a fan of the Necrons or Adeptus Mechanicus, but a surprising number of inclusions beyond that made their way in, from an intercession by the Iron Hands, to a chunk of the game being dedicated to the Leagues of Votann in their first non-mobile video game debut. Not to get too specific (as it is actually worth the experience) the fact core members of the game's cast of characters are ingloriously killed early on, or betray you, works. Both the ranks of the Admech & Necron dynasties have messy politics, and the palpable frustration of navigating those hierarchies remains a great through-line for the story's entirety. The writing for Magos Faustinius especially is fantastic, with him herding cats and being the solely reasonable voice for much of the Admech campaign. I do think the tone is a bit goofier than the first, with the insults hurled between characters like Videx & Scaevola being more bantery than bitter, but that's also true of the game as a whole—lest we forget the Necrons taking themselves too seriously is a point of purposeful comedy on their end. The fact the dialogue is intended to be read aloud by the voice actors means there's fewer instances of binharic screed being the way jokes are conveyed, but we've already addressed that fact is rife with discourse.

So where does that leave us? Mechanicus II has traded width of character customization for depth, freedom in combat for regimented roles, and systems to encourage friction & risk for a far better story. I would contend it's the worse game purely on the merit of its gameplay, but as a package, there's still a lot to like. From a technical perspective, though, the game badly needs a patch. There's bugged achievements, bugged unit upgrades, soft locks, and the optimization is such that some players can't run the game without their GPUs throttling to avoid an overheat; this is a solid-looking game, but isn't so gorgeous as to require fans at maximum to run without a CTD. While the gameplay of the Necrons is likely the better half, their treatment in terms of voice acting and achievements/music is lacking comparatively. The only leader in the game not to have a dedicated capstone achievement is a Necron, as petty an example as that is. I ran into a few small bugs, but nothing as bad as you'll see in the current negative reviews on Steam, yikes.



On a semi-related note, the UI for the game feels poorly-thought-out, with many buffs and terms being thrown about with no explanation until after they become active. My third-most-used leader, Rho, cared about stacking something called 'Data Choir', and for the life of me I still don't entirely understand where I could find at any given time how much I had. The capstone unlock ability on my absolute-favorite leader, Videx? Sometimes, it just wouldn't go off. This isn't really an acceptable state to launch as a 1.0, and it feels like Mechanicus II needed a couple more months in the tank, and likely shouldn't have come out with Warhammer Skulls. There are things here which, if accounted-for, could be interesting as opposed to frustrating: When you unlock more unit types, fewer of what you already have unlocked show up. As someone who was firmly committed to running solely Cult Mechanicus forces, being the guy who brings ~30-40 Electro-Priests to a GT, this needed to be addressed in some tooltip, somewhere. There's a host of minor quibbles like this with how the systems are presented, but once I got the hang of (most) keywords & abilities combat flowed fairly well.

Final Thoughts



Many of the problems inherent to Mechanicus II stem from what I'd term as good-natured hubris on the part of the developers, of being too confident in the successes from the first game. Believing the character recognition would be strong enough from a few portraits in Mechanicus I, to replace player-designed tech-priests? Now having the ability to go fully voice-acted, and initially canning the binharic? These are decisions among others that lead to a game which is so unlike its predecessor, it can't coast on any of its goodwill without unfortunate comparison. It's not a bad game, but in being so close to greatness and somehow flubbing it, my heart aches all the more. Luckily, these are things which can be fixed in patches & DLC, so my fingers are crossed that the mixed reception inspires changes for the better, and not abandonment. We've heard little from the developers outside of a few messages in their Discord, and a bugfix post on Steam.

So, what are the positives?
  • The Necron campaign is a highlight, but the story of the game on the whole serves as a great introduction to 40k's world
  • The presentation is a treat, and the units & weaponry feel appropriately dangerous
  • Unit variety has increased substantially from Mechanicus I, with two campaigns' worth of content to explore on top of an epilogue. Your playtime should easily eclipse ~50 hours.
And what could be improved upon?
  • Many of the best aspects of the original were letdowns here, so if you've played Mechanicus, this has some notable downgrades
  • Leader-focused gameplay is a pretty big miss, with true customization reduced on top of not being able to actively use your flashy characters
  • The game is rife with bugs and has poor optimization, especially for a modern release

The Verdict

Outside of its presentation, Mechanicus II doesn't measure up to the original, at least not currently. There's a solid game here, but this is one of the most disappointing sequels I've played in recent memory, so if you were anticipating the grand return of Magos Faustinius, you're probably in for a letdown. Once a few of the kinks get sorted out, it's definitely worth revisiting, and if the game receives ongoing support and tangible DLC, the scaffolding is here for a fantastic game—so long as they understand why the first title was as iconic as it was. Go play the original Mechanicus, you won't regret it.
