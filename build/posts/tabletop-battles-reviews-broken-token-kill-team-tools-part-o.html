Tabletop Battles Reviews: Broken Token Kill Team Tools, Part One - Complete Token Set, Dashboard, and Card Stand

June 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Something that isn’t really told to players when they first pick up Kill Team is that it’s a very token heavy game, more so than any other game that Games Workshop produces. There’s going to be a whole lot of them on any given board at any given time. While GW does provide cardboard tokens for the game, there’s plenty to be desired for a more premium product, as well as a demand for game aids that GW doesn’t provide, like objective control markers or game trackers. 

Enter Broken Token Gear, whose online store is dedicated to an impressive library of tokens, tools, and game aids for Kill Team. Alexis, who operates Broken Token Gear, was kind enough to provide us here at Tabletop Battles the entire library of products from his store for review, which is expansive enough that we’ve decided it best to split this review up into three parts to spare my delicate fingers from carpal tunnel. 

In this first part, we will be covering the Complete Token Set, Dashboard, and Card Stand from Broken Token Gear. We would like to thank Alexis for providing us these items for review.

Complete Token Set

Credit: Broken Token Gear

The Complete Token Set comes with ninety tokens and two clear plastic containers. The tokens are designed to cover the basic tokens needed for any game of Kill Team, such as Engage/Conceal orders, equipment, and special statuses like stun or injured. Additionally, the set comes with tokens for both Crit Ops and Tac Ops in Approved Ops 2025. Let it not be said that Broken Token was anything but through here; it is a whole carnival of tokens, and all of them are double sided.

Competing with the standard cardboard tokens produced by Games Workshop means providing a high quality token that is both durable and will look nice on the tabletop, and boy howdy are Broken Token’s set both of these things. The material used for these tokens are a nice matte black acrylic with high contrast UV print, which means they won’t shatter into pieces if they drop from the table and the print won’t rub off with sustained use. It also helps the premium feel of this set  that the black acrylic alongside the orange and white print looks sleek as hell on a tabletop. It's clear here that in designing this token, the first priority for this item was producing a high quality item.

An aspect of this commitment to quality is that these tokens come shipped with a plastic film on each side, to protect them from scuffs during the shipping process. This is more of an inconvenience than a real problem with the tokens, but it does mean that they aren’t really “ready to go” out of the box. Removing the film is real easy, it takes maybe thirty seconds to do one, but multiplied by ninety and removing all the plastic will probably take the better part of an hour. It's perfect work to do with a hot drink while catching up on a show or podcast.

Credit: Broken Token Gear

The tokens come with two clear plastic containers for storage, with dividers and compartment doors, as well as a sticker set that can be placed on each compartment to denote what kind of item is inside (such as Engage/Conceal orders, Tac Op tokens, Crit Op tokens, and so on). These containers look nice, feel sturdy, and are small enough to not crowd a table. My only complaint is that the size of these containers is a double edged sword; you can, technically, fit all of the tokens in one box, but it is very tight. Splitting the tokens between both containers feels better, and makes organization between the tokens easier, which does make me wish that the complete set came with one larger container, rather than two smaller ones. 

My only other criticism of the token set is the lack of visual aid to distinguish which token represents what. The basic stuff is very simple to distinguish, even with some design changes it's pretty clear what represents Conceal and Engage, or the equipment options. The Tac Op and Crit Ops are trickier, especially since they don’t have a clear analogue in the GW cardboard tokens to draw from. Sitting down with all the Ops in front of you, it’s not a terrifically difficult task to sus out what token is what, but having an included manual (or even a digital PDF guide) would be much appreciated.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Overall, these tokens are great. They look excellent on the table, they feel nice, and they cover just about everything you’d need a token for outside of faction-specific stuff. Broken Token’s Complete Token Set is an easy recommendation for someone who wants to upgrade the presentation of their Kill Team on the table, all while keeping track of every Op currently in the game. As any good product does, it left me excited for future token sets from Broken Token Gear, be they faction-specific or for the next iteration of Approved Ops.   

Dashboard

In particularly intense matches of Kill Team, it can be hard to keep track of all of your scoring while making the best decisions possible with your operatives. While the Tabletop Battles App offers an excellent digital tracking tool for Kill Team games, there are folks who prefer something physical to help them keep score. The Kill Team Dashboard by Broken Token is designed for just that sort of player.

Like the tokens set, the Dashboard is made of a high quality acrylic material, which looks nice, plus some rubber feet so it doesn’t slip and slide around the table. However, it is a bit thin compared to other products offered by Broken Token Gear. Mine is already sagging in the middle after some light use, so you’ll need to be a bit more delicate with it. The Dashboard goes for an urban ruins design print, with white and orange numbers and lettering for high visibility. Unlike the tokens, which took a good bit of time to get set up and ready to go, the dashboard is real easy. Just peel the film off the back, and it’s ready for business. 

As far as being a tracking tool, it's very straightforward, with slots for dice that can be used to track multiple scoring items such as crit op, tac op, turning point, and auxiliary items like Communion points or pain tokens. These dice slots are about right for medium sized dice, like the faction dice that Games Workshop likes to sell or chessex dice. There are also three slots at the top of the set that can be used to hold the cards for crit op, tac op, and kill op. These slots work great for unsleeved cards, but I found that when I tried to use my Approved Ops cards, which are sleeved with standard tarot size sleeves, that the sleeve made the card too wide for the slots.

The best feature of the Broken Token dashboard is the Kill Op counter. The Dashboard comes with a set of strips for Kill Op scoring based on the number of operatives, from 5 operatives to 14 operatives, and using these strips is as simple as finding the strip you need and placing it on the dashboard. Helpfully, the Kill Op strips are duplicated, so you’re still covered if both you and your opponent have the same number of operatives. I find that having such an easy guide to what the Kill Op breakpoints are without needing to check the card makes keeping track of Kill Op scoring much easier during the game, which is a big advantage the Broken Token Gear Dashboard has over other Kill Team dashboards on the market.

I like this Dashboard, though I’m probably the most lukewarm on it out of the three products we’re reviewing today. While it looks good on the table without taking too much space, I’m disappointed about the slots not being wide enough for sleeves, as well as how delicate it is compared to the other Broken Token Gear stuff I’ve looked over so far. It’s hard to undersell how much I like the Kill Op strips though, they’re absolutely the standout feature for this Dashboard and it’s main selling point. If you struggle to keep track of Kill Op during a game, this is absolutely the dashboard for you.  

Card Stand

The Card Stand is the simplest of the three Broken Token Gear products in this article, but it should be noted that I’m not a very organized person. I’ve spent probably more time than I’d care to admit trying to find a card to reference at the table, and I’ve even lost a card or two along the way trying to shuffle from table to table at events. The Card Stand offers an elegant solution to this organization hang up, offering a one stop shop for organizing all your faction cards in one spot on the table.

The Card Stand itself is made of the same material as the dashboard, though it is larger and doesn’t have the same rubber feet. To compensate, the Card Stand is thicker, meaning it won’t bend and feels much sturdier than the Dashboard while still having plenty of room to hold cards. An added bonus here is that these slots are absolutely big enough for sleeved cards too, which is a huge upgrade over the Dashboard. The Card Stand comes in two designs, the urban ruins or hazard stripes, and as a Chaos loving bozo at heart I’m personally more drawn to the hazard stripe design.

The actual layout of the Card Stand is well done, and clearly put together by someone familiar with the game rather than someone trying to make a quick buck. There are plenty of spots for Operatives (thirteen total, plus an extra Aux card slot to reach 14), as well as five equipment slots, a faction rule slot, and an area for strategy ploys. Note that it’s just strategy ploys, not ploys in general, with the idea here being to track only the active strategy ploys for the Turning Point rather than hold all your ploys in one place. I think this is the correct call here, as trying to also fit all the firefight ploys here means you’d be making the Card Stand a good bit bigger, at which point I think it stops being compact and easy to fit on tables. 

As an organizational aid, I think the Card Stand is great. It keeps all the reference material you need on the table right in front of you in clear terms, and cuts down the amount of time you’ll need to spend hunting down cards to reference just what exactly they do. If you’re as disorganized as me, this is great stuff. 

Final Thoughts

While I’m lukewarm overall about the Dashboard outside of its innovative use of Kill Ops stips, I’m a big fan of the Token Set and the Card Stand. All three of these have a premium look on the table, but the Card Stand and Token set have the added bonus of durability to back that up. If you’re in the market for ways to make your tokens look nicer or help you stay better organized during games of Kill Team, you’ll get plenty of mileage out of what Broken Token Gear has to offer here.

Next time, we will reviewing Broken Token’s products focused on measuring markers and operatives on the Killzone, like the objective markers and smoke sets, the control range circles, and the Snap Bracelet Ruler.
