[40k] 11th Edition Faction Pack Review: Necrons

June 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With a new edition of Warhammer 40K comes a whole new bunch of Detachments, and we’re handling them in groups to make it easier to separate the wheat from the chaff, at least for now. We’ll go through the relevant changes to the Datasheets and rules at play, then see how the Detachments themselves stand up.

Thank you again to Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of these rules.

Necrons have been consistently fantastic across 10th Edition, and should be ready to hit the ground running in 11th. They have access to all high durability tarpits, strong brawling threats, and good ranged firepower, plus a decent well of cheap utility models, so everything is set for them to thrive.

They also, like everyone, get new Detachments! Let’s take a look and find out if any of them will challenge the already considerable arsenal Necrons have access to.

Datasheet Changes

  • All hull-having Vehicles gain FRAME.
  • All four regular-flavour Crypteks become Support, along with Orikan.
  • Transcendent C’tan and Ophydian Destroyers have the set-up distance for their reposition abilities changed to 8”.
  • Doom Scythes get set to OC and M “-”, in line with all Aircraft.

The Night Scythe also gets a complete overhaul:

  • It’s not an Aircraft any more, just a weird-shaped Vehicle.
  • It’s now M14”.
  • It gains the Hover rule, so doesn’t pay for using FLY.
  • It has FRAME.
  • It gets a rule allowing units to re-embark within it even if they Disembarked from it this turn.

The sad bit for Necrons here is that solo Psychomancers are gone, which is rough as they would be really, really good in 11th. You now need a Warrior or Immortal unit to attach them to, which can be a tough sell without them providing any unit buffs.

The good bit is the Night Scythe, because it whips now. It still has its turn 1 Deep Strike ability, but can actually operate on the table like a normal unit after that, and if you’re worried about your opponent screening out drops you can choose to set it up on the table and zip about.

Rule Changes

  • None

Available Detachments

Necrons keep all of their existing Detachments, with none getting slimmed down to 1DP versions. This means they now have access to the following:

  • Awakened Dynasty - 3DP
  • Canoptek Court - 3DP
  • Starshatter Arsenal - 3DP
  • Annihilation Legion - 2DP
  • Obeisance Phalanx - 2DP
  • Hypercrypt Legion - 2DP
  • Cursed Legion - 2DP
  • Pantheon of Woe - 2DP
  • Cryptek Conclave - 2DP
  • Hand of the Dynasty (new) - 1DP
  • Skyshroud Spearhead (new) - 1DP
  • The Phaeron’s Armoury (new) - 1DP

The big winners from the great Detachment-pointening are Cursed Legion and Hypercrypt Legion. As we'll see with a lot of factions, the sweet spot to be in for 11th is to be one of the Detachments that was good enough to win events, but not the best of the best for your faction, as these tend to squeak through at 2DP, letting them add an extra option.

Both of these, here, feel like exceptional combinations with Hand of the Dynasty, and Cursed Legion/Hand of the Dynasty has, in fact, been the build I've been maining in our internal games of 11th to prepare reviews.

Hand of the Dynasty

Necron Warriors. Credit: Pendulin

This is a Detachment for boosting Warriors and Immortals, and it’s really, really good.

Detachment Rule

This Detachment has the Dynasty tag, and cannot be taken alongside another Dynasty detachment.

Warrior and Immortal units have Assault and can Advance/Action. Great stuff here, significantly boosting the mobility and utility of the Battleline contingent you’ve got on the table. Even with tesla Immortals (and you’re gonna be taking some of those) adding Assault to the weapons on Royal Wardens/Plasmancers meaningfully helps them with their damage output.

Enhancements

  • Enlivened Sentinels (Upgrade): Give a Necron Warrior unit Scout 5”. Further amping up the mobility here, this is solid for pushing a big Warrior brick up the table, and at least worth considering on smaller units because of how many of the 11th Edition maps look. On a lot of them, this will let you push 10 Warriors onto your Expansion straight away, where they will almost certainly be Hidden from most foes, but instantly shut down any plans from Action Dispositions to do stuff on your Expansion turn 1.
  • Tools of Dominion (Upgrade): Gives Immortals Rapid Fire 1. Yes I would like to have an extra 10 tesla shots from the unit I’m chucking out the side of a Night Scythe, thank you so much for asking. Hanging a bunch of Immortals round with Szeras is great with this too.

Stratagems

  • Dominance Protocols (1CP): Used in either Command Phase to provide a unit of Immortals with +1OC till the end of the turn. This kind of Stratagem doesn’t come up very often, but it’s the perfect ace to have up your sleeve because you only press the button on it when it guarantees some level of Primary swing.
  • Will of the Conqueror (1CP): Secures an objective that a Warrior or Immortal unit is holding at the end of your Movement Phase. Lack of good ObSec has been one of Necrons only real problems for all of 10th, and good news, that’s over now. You can also have fun with this using a Night Scythe - chuck a unit out, secure the Central Objective, pull them back in, and now the enemy has to come to you.
  • Nanosaturation (1CP): After an enemy unit shoots at your Immortals or Warriors, your unit can Snap Shoot back. This is a bit more niche, because it doesn’t do anything for you that Overwatch wouldn’t, but occasionally you’ll want to do both. I guess it also provides a way of Snap Shooting an enemy unit that was Hidden in the enemy Movement Phase, but has now revealed itself by shooting.

Is It Good?

Hell yeah it is. This is an extremely good set of abilities, and the first thing I’m going to be doing once the DP costs are out is working out what this goes with.

Skyshroud Spearhead

Necron Converted Tomb Blades

This detachment supports Necron Mounted units of all stripes. The Detachment Rule provides an OK buff to Tomb Blades, but the real value is the Stratagems and an Upgrade providing a really good set of boosts for both those and Lokhust Destroyers.

Detachment Rule - Transdimensional Deployment

Tomb Blades get Deep Strike, and in any turn where they made an Ingress Move they get +1 to hit rolls. This is…an odd choice because it has enormous anti-synergy with several aspects of the Tomb Blade datasheet. They already have Scout, which is often a better way for them to get where they need to, and they can’t use their move-shoot-move after arriving from Ingress, so you’re sacrificing their protection if you bring them in like this. A full particle beamer unit with nebuloscopes and +1 to hit popping in is appreciably nasty into some targets, so you could try that, but this isn’t a huge draw.

Enhancements

  • Recursive Reanimation (Upgrade): Gives Tomb Blades +1 to their Reanimation Protocols. This better be really cheap because Tomb Blades tend to go from alive to dead at speed.
  • Deepening Madness (Upgrade): Gives a Destroyer Cult Mounted unit Assault. This is pretty great, helping Destroyers get where they need to be. Regular Lokhusts seem massively improved with 11th’s rules, so this is actively quite appealing.

Stratagems

  • Omnilocked Strafing (1CP): Gives a Mounted unit Fall Back and Shoot. Fantastic for Lokhusts, getting stuck in combat is a real challenge for them, and this protects them from that.
  • Swift as Death (1CP): Gives a Mounted unit a d3+3” reactive move when an enemy unit ends within 8”. Again, really, really good, ideal for a Heavy Lokhust unit nosing onto terrain then escaping later. You can be very tactical with these in 11th as well - with the minimum 4”, you can place your unit on an objective so that if the enemy wants to move onto it, they have to trigger this and give you enough distance to get off.
  • Evasive Protocols (1CP): Provides -1 to Wound against shooting attacks with S greater than T for a Mounted unit. You’d rather not get shot at all using the above, but this can be clutch if you have to get caught, and bigger Lokhust units have a lot of wounds to push through.

Is It Good?

Yeah it’s fine. It’s quite resource intensive, but Lokhusts do seem really good in 11th, and there are some nice boosts for them here. You could plausibly go for bigger Tomb Blade units as alpha threats as well, so there’s definitely some utility here.

The Phaeron’s Armoury

Monolith with Death Rays. Credit: Rockfish

This Detachment wants you to bring huge metal boxes to the table. Big metal boxes.

Detachment Rule

This detachment has the Hypercrypt tag and cannot be combined with another Hypercrypt detachment.

Necron TITANIC FLY units get +6” M. This is a frankly hilarious level of acceleration to throw on these, allowing them to truly rocket around the table. The post-Ingress reach on a Monolith is enormous, and it might allow you to meaningfully use a Vault or Obelisk that starts on the table.

Enhancements

  • Prelocation Optimiser: When the bearer’s unit is selected to shoot in a turn that they were set up using a Monolith’s Eternity Gate, they get your choice of Sustained Hits 1 or Lethal Hits. Nice to have the choice here, as most Necron units have one or the other already, and you can certainly ramp up the damage on units with Plasmancers this way. Heavily dependent on how much it costs to set up, of course.
  • Mortality Shroud (Upgrade): Give an Obelisk an 8” aura that forces enemy units that are below Starting Strength to take a Battle-shock test in your opponent’s Command Phase. I am…having something of an existential crisis because this Detachment really wants me to take an Obelisk and it might be semi-real? The big problem with the Obelisk is that it doesn’t do anywhere near enough that’s pro-active, putting a giant aura of extra morale tests until the enemy kills it might actually do it? Helps that this Detachment has a solid output boost for them as well.

Stratagems

  • Subsurface Quantumweave (1CP): Armour of contempt (-1 to incoming AP for a volley of attacks), but for TITANIC FLY units. They all have a 2+ save, that’s the best place to drop Armour of Contempt-style abilities, so yeah this is good.
  • Particle Pulse (1CP): Pick a Visible enemy unit within 12” of a TITANIC FLY unit at the start of your shooting phase, and inflict +3” detection range on them. Niche (because you could, at this point, just shoot them with your big box), but fine to have access to.
  • Cosmic Storm (1CP): +1AP to Tesla Spheres when an Obelisk or Monolith shoots. Legitimately a very good upgrade, particularly for the Obelisk because it can chip enemy FLY units like a champion.

Is It Good?

I mean…look. It is 100% going to depend on what the price tags are on the various big boxes. In a world of Defilers, the 10th Edition price on a Monolith looks completely absurd, so if they come down great, and this makes me want to try an Obelisk more than anything has since I started playing again in 8th (the edition where the community voted it the worst Codex Datasheet in the game). This is highly unlikely to be the best option available to you, but it’s pretty funny and does push the things it targets hard.

Final Thoughts

Generally good news for the Necrons. Night Scythes seem real now, and Hand of the Phaeron is one of the better 1DP detachments across the whole suite of 11th. Love to be a Necron player.
