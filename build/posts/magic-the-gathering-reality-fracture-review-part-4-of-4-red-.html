Magic: The Gathering Reality Fracture Review, Part 4 of 4 - Red, Green, and Commander Cards

October 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
At the crossroads of Jace’s rampant egomania and ‘what if I drew a fucked up, evil version of the Hamburger Helper’ lies Reality Fracture, Magic’s newest set. This is the first Planeswalkers-matter set since 2019’s War of the Spark, or should we say Planeswalker, singular, as Jace is the set’s focal point. Reality Fracture is filled with powerful cards and their alternate universe counterparts, offering both novel mechanical takes on beloved characters, and imaginative ‘what-if’ scenarios for Vorthoses. To that end, this set is built for pick-2 draft, with both versions of many characters appearing alongside one another in the same pack. After a couple of middling releases, this set is probably the most powerful since Avatar, and we expect a host of multi-format staples to solidify once players get their hands on Reality Fracture. Today we’re covering the set’s red, green, and Commander companion cards.

Reality Fracture officially releases to tabletop on October 2nd, and on Magic: The Gathering Arena on September 29th.

Red

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ajani's Anguish

Saffgor: What a deeply fascinating midrange card. Obviously that archetype appreciates flexible removal, and being able to punish the opponent for chumping their efficient attackers, but in all of its modes (finish something off with damage, Banefire-lite, 1-mana Trample enabler, etc) it’s never the most efficient pick. If there’s a compelling way to give this Flash, that’s going to be what pushes this over the edge in 60-card formats.

BPhillipYork: Fireball + Trample for your creatures is pretty neat, and of course you can always just give it X=0 if all you need is trample, which is probably the best way to think of it - R for trample and colorless for some upside. Some funny shenanigans with the various doublers and redirectors.

FromTheShire: Cheap trample access in red, the aggro color, is excellent. Having a scalable burn spell tacked on is even better. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ajani Unrelenting

Saffgor: This is a great top end for Superfriends in Commander, allowing you to turn your board of walkers into aggressive pushes via his incidental tokens. That archetype isn’t competitive, per say, but it is popular, so I reckon Ajani gets plenty of love even if he’s just a good card in a lukewarm strategy.

BPhillipYork: Strange design for a planeswalker but a sort of self wheel is pretty powerful at -2 and creating tokens whenever you activate a loyalty ability could go fairly nuts.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Craterclaw Colossus

Saffgor: There’s one facet to this card which vastly improves it compared to its peers in Craterhoof Behemoth & Moonshaker Cavalry: It’s an Artifact. That allows it to be cheated and revived far easier, even if it checking for Artifacts vs Creatures likely means your board is smaller. Still, in the right decks this is just going to be better Craterhoof, so if you’d prefer to end the game via turning sideways in a single turn rather than comboing off (even if the result is the same) this is a good tool to have in your belt.

BPhillipYork: I'm trying to think what this card reminds me of, and it's just not coming to light. There must be something sort of like this already in the game I'm thinking of but ah well. Trample and +X equally to artifacts is pretty great when it's an artifact and between mana rocks and Treasures you can really rack them up quickly. 7 is a lot of mana but this is an obvious finisher.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Curse-Marred Demon

Saffgor: Gamble on a reasonable body is great to see, especially given Red can ‘reroll’ their tutor via Red’s suite of cheap temporary clones. If you just want the Gamble, this is a little high on the curve, but for any format where a 4/4 Flampler for 4 has merit this’ll feel great.

BPhillipYork: This demon has a Gamble addiction and we should get him some help. But Gamble for 3 more mana that gives you a 4/4 flyer with trample is pretty solid.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Draconic Visitor

Saffgor: This is the card which makes Ganax, Astral Hunter one of the best scramble decks out there, being able to go infinite with a Red Background Commander and being remarkably accessible via Reality Scramble etc, but there’s a caveat: If you lack an outlet or source of damage/wincon, you draw the game out and lose, in most tournament scenarios. Neither Visitor nor Ganax is optional, so you need a way to win or stop the combo before popping off. For that reason it’s a hair less potent than it initially reads, but is still a card which may bring Ganax to fringe cEDH.

BPhillipYork: It's sort of weird to infer things about the stories from sets from the cards. Back in the day that's really all you had, little flavor texts and card names to figure out what was going on, a paragraph or two on the booster box maybe. It was actually really kind of neat, it gave collecting all the cards a flavor bonus too. In any case, this is a card with some fairly bonkers potential, mostly in Dragon decks. There's various Dragon enablers, doublers, and things that make entering Dragons ping, and this can turn every Treasure or Clue you make into a Dragon. It's like good in a take it to a tournament way, but it's a rad fun card for brackets 2 and low 3.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Face Yourself

Saffgor: I dislike splashy spells like this which are predicated on your opponents’ resources, but I also understand myself not to be the target audience. This is a Timmy card, and for Timmies it’ll feel cool to cast, probably.

BPhillipYork: So copy everything someone has, and then keep them if you have a walker. This is really too pricy for what it does, so it ends up just being a fun haymaker, but the likeliest outcome of this should be a combo that ends the game. Otherwise it just dumps a bunch of creatures into play, but the most likely player to use it is one who is running a lot of creatures so it'll be rare you're copying someone else's creatures for 7 mana.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Identity Echo

Saffgor: I've been one of the biggest proponents for bringing Scramble/Polymorph to the masses in Commander, so I've gotta love this card, right? Not...exactly. See, the upfront cost is brutal, being one of the least efficient means of polying a Creature you'd potentially play, and only mildly efficient on the Planeswalker front compared to its peers. The fact this can hit both means you're doubly restricted during deckbuilding, and I think the juice isn't worth the squeeze. For the new Jace precon though, yeah this is a shoo-in.

BPhillipYork: Planeswalker polymorph is interesting. Most fun thing I think to do with this is to just run planeswalkers and then to use token creatures to get them, though for 4 mana to cast and 3 mana to use it's pretty pricy. You could pair with things like Mana Echoes and go totally crazy, dumping out your whole deck in one turn in a kindred deck.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jiang Yanggu, Alone

Saffgor: Jiang uses some new wording technology that’s debuting in Reality Fracture, allowing you to attack three different players with single creatures to triple-dip on the ‘attacks alone’ rider. This isn’t a retroactive change, but going forward, I’d expect to see this phrasing crop up in more designs. Jiang’s been on my list to talk about in a full focus, as the degree to which his damage ramps up alongside extra combats is close to unheard-of. Cool card, 5 mana sucks, but the textbox may still be worth it in Commander.

BPhillipYork: You could potentially really pump something up a lot with this if you've already used Wheel of Fortune or something like that. Also the sad man losing his dog and going John Wick should have like, Focus, Commitment, and Will, and those should be reflected.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Koth, the Geomancer

Saffgor: Folks love Landfall, and Mountains-matter has been waiting for a worthy Commander for decades at this point. Koth swoops in with the save, coming as both combo enabler and damage outlet, value engine, and all-around great synergy piece. In spite of only being an Uncommon, he strikes me as Red’s answer to Nissa, Resurgent Animist, and that’s high praise.

BPhillipYork: Love it. Love land damage decks. Not a great commander but awesome as an enabler. You can do some crazy stuff with this, various ways to recur a lot of lands at once, or sacrifice a bunch then grab a bunch from your yard.

FromTheShire: Great for pushing damage through, great for adding a little extra mana. I'm all about it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Master of Barbs

Saffgor: Spooky card for aggro to get, especially with the Lizards-matter subtheme that remains from Bloomburrow still around. I think this may be the piece to revitalize that, if the power level dips down far enough to bring the Bloomburrow typal strategies back to the forefront.

BPhillipYork: This seems good in Standard or whatever it's called but not Commander.

FromTheShire: Super nice piece for Rakdos Lizards and Burn decks, this dude will push a game to its conclusion in a hurry if not dealt with. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

No Admittance

Saffgor: This is mentioned for being Red’s cheapest way (aside from Way of the Pyromancer) to Empower, something eminently relevant for the color that has access to Reality Scramble. Reality Fracture opens up the causeway for layered Scramble/Polymorph deckbuilding, as Planeswalker tokens being everywhere means your Scramble target for a Karn or Lukka no longer need be in the Command Zone. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pompous Battlemage

Saffgor: Really interesting sidegrade to the 1-mana Prowess options in Red. Coming with its own enabler is sweet, and in any format with Fiery Temper this outputs a shocking amount of damage rather quickly. Maybe even with Blazing Rootwalla, in Modern?

BPhillipYork: IMO not enough upside unless you have a way to re-prepare him or flicker or something, or maybe want to jack up the storm count or cast from not-hand. I guess I'm saying definitely not in a vanilla red deck but if it leverages your other abilities in some way then good.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pyre Rhymer

Saffgor: One of two huge gets for Mountains-matter decks in the set, the other being Koth, and it’s great to see. There’s many good reasons to jam a ton of Mountains, such as Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle, and letting them tap for extra mana ala High Tide is just fantastic. The fact Red is rife with ways to copy the Prepared Spell means that you can really abuse this, and have Mountains tapping for 5+ mana during your combo turn with relative ease. Strong pieces for niche archetypes is what good Commander games are made of.

BPhillipYork: High Tide is back and it's an elemental sorcerer. Crazy.

FromTheShire: Fortunately this is on a 3 mana creature but even so giving the Rituals and Fork color access to a High Tide is wildly strong. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Samut, Hazoret's Champion

Saffgor: While unexciting at its face, Samut is one of the cheapest unconditional Haste enablers that’s one-sided, and being Legendary is fairly easy to find in something like Sisay. I think this ends up being far stronger than it looks from an initial read, even if it won’t be found in the Command Zone.

BPhillipYork: Well this is a very solid way to give all your creatures haste. And they actually have haste, as opposed to like, acting like they do, which will let you trigger certain things that otherwise wouldn't.

FromTheShire: Haste is super strong, and getting it both board wide and crucially one-sided for just 2 mana is excellent. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Stingcaster Mage

Saffgor: Snapcaster Mage is one of the best Creatures of all time, and while this lacking Flash greatly hampers its flexibility, especially in higher-power formats, it’s hard to turn one’s nose up at Flashback with a body.

BPhillipYork: It's good for constructed I'm sure, in Commander I'd likely pass.

FromTheShire: It's no Snapcaster by a long shot, but this is still immediately being tested in basically every format where it is legal. We'll see how that goes since this is much more buying back things mostly fairly on your turn instead of making a surprise blocker and a removal spell, but adding a hasty attacker plus burn or card advantage spell still has a ton of upside. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tomik, Izzet Sparkmage

Saffgor: The effect isn’t new, but the cost is—Tomik here is cheap for the effect you get, and for that reason likely slots in over his peers. Decks that want damage increasers are almost always aggro-adjacent, and any chance to lower the curve for aggro decks is one you’ll take.

BPhillipYork: This sort of thing can get pretty bonkers, especially for only 2 mana. It's almost too threatening and small-bodied and cheap though, so it'll likely eat some removal.

FromTheShire: Very nice piece for the tokens + Warleader's Call deck, as well as various Burn and Pinger decks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Way of the Pyromancer

Saffgor: Correct me if I’m wrong, but this seems like a fantastic 2-mana rock if you have any means of protecting Jace. Makes mana or Surveils the turn it comes down, and thereafter can break even on card advantage. This is better than it looks.

BPhillipYork: These enchantments that give planeswalkers new loyalty abilities are really neat, and I like how they interact with the game, but I'm not sure how good they are.

Green

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Carnivorous Cultivator

Saffgor: In a lot of ways, this is a better Life from the Loam, a phrase I never thought would escape my lips. Overstatted, functionally evasive, and with the capacity to set itself up, this is a one-time tutor and also value-over-time...so long as you connect. I think most Commander games will have points at which you can’t, but on the whole I’d expect this to work writ large. Using this to dump Dakmor Salvage is just nasty.

BPhillipYork: This is neat card design, it's balanced and solid but not amazing. Letting you recur one land a turn is good and letting you tutor a land to your yard is good and a bit risky, so all around very nice well balanced card.

FromTheShire: Being able to tutor out ANY land is always an excellent effect, and in a lot of the decks that run it you're going to have ways to replay lands from the yard so even if you can't connect with him you're still getting it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Fblthp, Knows the Way

Saffgor: Wildly funny that playing Snow Basics vs Basics lets you double-dip, but really that's the only remarkable aspect.

BPhillipYork: Grabbing up to 12 lands is a lot, though 14 mana is also a lot of mana. I thought he was lost or something. Now I'm lost. In any case, solid card for 5-color decks to get some mana fixing in.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Gardenize

Saffgor: I’ve gone back and forth on this, and I think my current stance is that, so long as you can get a single counter on it the turn this card comes down, it’s pretty good. That could be via a sac outlet, something like a delayed Sakura-Tribe Elder, etc, but you really can’t afford this to be not providing mana the first real rotation at most Bracket 3 game paces, as a benchmark.

BPhillipYork: I mean it's the set theme so it's fine to just have these cards that are color-twisted versions of earlier cards. Mana is so much easier for green to generate that I think this card is kind of less good in Green, but it's still pretty solid especially in scenarios like a Black/Green or Black/Green/white aristocrats deck, in which case it might get kind of absurd.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Garruk, Curse Breaker

Saffgor: Let’s break this down---you’ve got a 5 mana 4/4 that draws a card, and next turn if you can protect a Planeswalker, you get to functionally add 2+ mana. You also have an Overrun...which also applies for your opponents, towards your opponents. You can even just look at this like a ~3 mana card to weave into a turn, or less if you have a Land(s) that tap for 2+ mana. You might be able to tell, but literally all of these modes are playable, and having the option to pick between them is unbelievable; Green Garruk here is a Planeswalker built from the ground-up for the Commander age, and it shows. You should be playing this card in fair Gx decks if you have any chance of drawing 1-2 cards from his passive, as really the only archetype where he provides 0 value is combo, when compared to something like Nissa, Who Shakes the World at 5.

BPhillipYork: Pretty all around solid planeswalker with good abilities and a good passive and some interplay between his loyalty abilities.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ghalta the Unstoppable

BPhillipYork:  It's a big dinosaur that hands out trample. It's fun. I'm not sure the people writing these flavor texts really know what apex means though, or think about it. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Heartwood Crafter

Saffgor: This is the best 1-mana dork since Delighted Halfling, and it’s a shame it’s not technically Monogreen. Being able to play this on one, Soul Tether on 2, and then a 5 mana Commander on 3 off of a single card is insanity. If you have an RGx 5 mana Commander you’re fond of, like I don’t know, Pantlaza, Sun-Favored, this should be the first card in that deck after Sol Ring. I’m not kidding.

BPhillipYork: Well that is super goofy to me to see a green Elven Artificer. Making Heartwood tokens can get completely out of control, and this is a sort of mana dork, and one that can get a commander out, so pretty all around strong. So strong it's basically an auto-include in any green+red and any other colors Commander deck with at least a 5 CMV commander, since it lets you get him out 2 turns sooner.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hexhaven Invigorator

Saffgor: In Modern, where we have Gut Shot and now Mine Collapse, it is entirely possible to kill your opponent the turn this comes down. Is that line weak to removal? Yes. Is it sick as hell? Also yes. As a version of Scapeshift that gets to incidentally run far more Creature removal, for its synergy with the combo, I feel as though this might just work out.

BPhillipYork: Well that's fairly nasty. All these 4 of one mana color creatures are pretty bonkers, and frankly Phyrexian Obliterator is still probably the nastiest but this has many ways it can be abused to just get all your lands out so quickly.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hungering Puppetbeast

Saffgor: The flexibility on this is great in Standard midrange, and with Heartwood Crafter itself being able to ramp on 1 and 3 into this, there’s a very clear curve that emerges for Green within the internal logic of Reality Fracture. It’s a bit fair, but it’s also straightforward enough to work.

BPhillipYork: What even is a puppet beast supposed to be. I hate when it's just like, random amalgam of nouns and then some nasty looking thing. Magic IP is so lazy. Anyway, 5/5 fine, Heartwood token okay, sac to yeah, fine it's a mediocre beater, don't bother. Unless your deck is Pinocchio themed or something I guess.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jiang Yanggu, Never Alone

Saffgor: The untap is neat, and can do some cool things with a token copy of Strionic Resonator, but Monogreen isn’t the identity for whom this effect extracts maximum value.

BPhillipYork: Is this before he got his dog back or killed or what. Anyway, untapping all those Heartwood tokens for example is pretty fun, plenty of token-able mana dorks or ways to make your tokens dorks.So potentially pretty good. Never alone would make me think the dog is pretty hard to get rid of but it's just a vanilla 3/3. Like it seems like it should come back at the end step and then untap tokens. Oh well.

Loot, the Nexus

I've made that work in draft already, and it's shockingly competent. Aside from that, it’s less interesting than its Black counterpart.

BPhillipYork: Yeah that can generate a finite but a lot of mana so that's always fun. For decks that build up quickly this can get pretty funny in terms of just generating all the mana.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Marwyn, the Preserver

Saffgor: I intend to cover her in a full article this week, but suffice to say a better Groundskeeper is absolutely wild to have in the Command Zone. Basic check people with repeated Boseijus, activate this 4+ times per turn cycle, heck this can still just jam the Six combo with Lion’s Eye Diamond if you so desire. Not only that, but in providing your Lands with Hexproof, you can make use of Aura ramp without risk of opposing removal...or be nasty and turbo into The Tabernacle of Pendrell Vale/Glacial Chasm. Fantastic Commander, and likely a sleeper from the set.

BPhillipYork: Returning lands from the yard is always nice, gets you tons of value out of fetchlands alone. 2 mana for that and 2 mana to ensure you never miss a land drop is very worth it.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Omnipresence

Saffgor: Yes yes, Green can ramp more easily into this so it’s okay the free casts are conditional, but I’m going to say it, even actual Omniscence isn’t an especially good card. It enables you to pop off, sure, but it’s not a wincon in of itself. This is worse, and highly board-dependent at that. Not a good card, and barely a cool card.

BPhillipYork: This is obviously dangerous if it lands and sticks, but it costs 8 already and then it lets you cast other stuff for free. If you have 8 mana just win the game.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pia, Aether Ascetic

Saffgor: It’s genuinely kind of wild they printed this card. I’ve spoken about Pia at length in my Jyoti Commander Focus, but the long and short is that it allows decks with Green, and without White or Black, to access Enchantment-based combos via abundant Creature tutors. There’s Food Chain, Intruder Alarm, etc that all serve as key pieces many decks couldn’t previously access without Intuition or deep draw, and I fully expect to see things like Berta, Wise Extrapolator sneaking up the fringes of cEDH due to Pia’s existence.

BPhillipYork: Super goofy and color pie breaking to let green search for enchantments. It's basically just default amazing since that's something that green can't do normally, and you can put this in the Command Zone if you want, flicker it, mulch it etc.. Tutors are good.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Puppet Crafting

Saffgor: Deeply interesting to get another piece of animation in Green, something I covered all the way back in my Maester Seymour Puzzlebox. Turn 2 5/5 Gingerbrute is a real consideration, and this being able to come back in a stalled-out game is nice for super hard control strategies where ~2 copies of this are the only wincon. There's also the hilarity that is animating your Background as a Background Commander, so its 'Commander Creatures have...' abilities double up.

BPhillipYork: Okay, this is just another color pie breaker with recursion stapled on top, but not really good for much by itself.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Simulacrum Shaper

Saffgor: If you want to ramp at 3 mana, e.g. going 1 mana dork into a Kodama’s Reach, this just ends up being better. It’s fine to powercreep Solemn Simulacrum in the color that never played him anyway.

BPhillipYork: Basically just another basic ramp and draw card, pretty similar to several already existing cards. This one doesn't have a built in sacrifice making it a little harder to get full value out of, but maybe you want to sacrifice it for some other valuable thing in which case it gets even more valuable.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Titanbones, Towering Heart

Saffgor: Any time there’s an effect which triggers for free, even if it’s just some incidental lifegain on discard, it’s worth taking notes. This card does some interesting things with Marina Vendrell’s Grimoire, and Tortured Existence, but lacks any obvious superlative combo at present. Don’t forget this card exists, however, because it’s ripe for a lifegain payoff that loots.

BPhillipYork: This can definitely get huge very fast. It has no built in trample or evasion so it's just a beater, but white and green can set up a lot of lifegain triggers and just be going off, so it's great for that sort of deck as yet another huge threat.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Verdant Kraken

Saffgor: Love the Wayne Reynolds art, and heck, love the card too. It’s on the pricy side, so you’ll want to cheat it out somehow, but this is essentially a Koma that can turn its value into mana. Any deck playing a Koma-like card in the 99 should find room for this as a sidegrade.

BPhillipYork: Wow this is hugely expensive but also going to make a ton of walking land tokens, so a big midrange beast that also ramps up the threat level. Especially given how many finishers can turn those 3/3 basically vanilla Forest lands into big creatures with trample or infect or something.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Way of the Paradox

Saffgor: Superfriends decks breathing heavy about this one, good lord that’s a lot of Land drops. You do of course still need to have the Lands in hand to make use of it, but the acceleration this can provide is profound.

BPhillipYork: Planeswalkers causing ramp is really dangerous, so neat card.

Commander Deck

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dack Fayden, Helping Hand

Saffgor: Dack is certainly the most powerful Monowhite Commander we’ve seen this year, perhaps second only to The Queen of Dale. He operates, at the highest level, very much like Godo, Bandit Warlord, where on resolution there is a ‘correct’ set of ~5 Creatures you can play to win the game 100% of the time if uninterrupted. White has a few problems compared to Godo, namely that Red has far better acceleration, and slamming a turn 2/3 Godo is fairly common. The benefit, however, is that Dack wins through many of your own stax pieces, so jamming cards like Rule of Law or Deafening Silence early can assure a win alongside Silence. At the lower power levels, Dack is about free Eldrazi, and handing them to your opponents, likely with intent to then Homeward Path them back, and that’s a blast too. It’s rare we get a Commander that pleases people across the power curve, but this guy walks that knife-edge perfectly.

BPhillipYork: I love this. Totally fun card. Goading a ton of weird creatures, or maybe they are hatebears, or who knows. Just a goofy fun card. I'm sure there's like "good" things you can do with it but I think it's ideal as just a card that forces everyone to fight.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ob Nixilis, the Ascended

Saffgor: Compared to Dack, White Ob is a disappointment. Worse payoff, worse floor, expensive, just downright discouraging. The only good thing about this card is the art, which is admittedly quite nice.

BPhillipYork: That's an expensive but powerful Angel that sort of wraths and with lifegain will generate a horde for you very quick.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Teferi's Reproach

Saffgor: I'm already lower on Teferi's Protection than most, and this is a pretty stark downgrade...right? Don't be fooled, this card isn't great, but if you're trying to stuff a combo player this is one of the best ways to do it. The only downside is that this doesn't prevent them from winning the game, ala Everybody Lives-esque cards.

BPhillipYork: Okay. I guess this shuts someone down, and it certainly will also stop someone who just dumped out a Craterhoof Behemoth or that new Craterfoot artifact thing. I mostly think Teferi's Protection is better, but I guess if you wanted two of them, this sort of works that way (maybe).

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Avacyn, Angel of Horror

Saffgor: Avacyn in the Command Zone is essentially a Valgavoth turned inward, where instead of disrupting opponents you’re looking to build your own implacable slow loop of death triggers. That’s fine, but Avacyn is too expensive in my eyes to justify that, even in Monoblack. As a reanimation target, she’s a bit better, and via her delayed trigger can dodge many of the nastiest exile triggers to come back to live in the End Step. It’s a cool card with a strong effect, but is going to be priced-out of anything but reanimator or like, Kaalia.

BPhillipYork:  Hilarious. Huge reanimator. Great for battlecruiser.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jhoira, Weatherlight Corsair

Saffgor: This whole precon couldn’t print a new Commander under mana value 5, and disregarding Jace every option is 6+ mana. Jhoira suffers from being a member of a Polymorph-matters deck, because the moment you put her in the Command Zone she falls apart. If ever you need to pay 8 mana for this card, you’ll be putting on clown makeup. There is one cool thing you can do though: Prune the opposing Library. Via Bitter Ordeal, Earwig Squad, Sadistic Sacrament, etc you can build an anti-tutor deck that stuffs combo strategies and picks away at things like mana rocks, saving only juicy targets for Jhoira. I don't know how realistic that is but good lord does it sound sweet.

BPhillipYork: Pretty expensive but fun card, and cool card design. If you have some life gain this may net you some fun cards. People really love it when you take their huge creatures too.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Venser, Fervent Forger

Saffgor: Venser does a lot, but at his core he’s a piece in the 99 of RWx Donate decks that provides a compelling win condition. If you play, for example, Iroh, Tea Master and hand your opponent a Felidar Guardian, Venser can enter, make two copies, and then repeat ad nauseum for infinite hasty attackers. This style of deck traditionally lacks a good combo finish, but boy does Venser fix that. Beyond that, making two Lands for Landfall is cool, and this works well with all the forced turn-ends, but if I’m being honest Venser exists to make donation decks actually have an endgame. That’s a great thing for one of Commander’s durdliest archetypes.

BPhillipYork: I love the idea of flashing this in to win the game when someone else tries to, which alone makes it pretty great. There's plenty of ways to copy him once he's on the field too, and even if they die faster than instantly, the triggers still go on the stack.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Niv-Mizzet, Ghost Council

Saffgor: Spooky Niv brings a new archetype to high-power Orzhov: Doomsday. Niv excels at cracking a Doomsday pile, with Soul Spike as the top card cracking the rest for free, and from there you can find wins in a few ways. On top of that, even as a generic value Commander Niv has promise—as all his iterations do. Still, I’ll stand firm on the ground that this is best played as a Doomsday Commander, and a remarkably fun & cool one at that.

BPhillipYork: Wow that is a crazy card. Glad it costs 6 because that's absolutely bonkers card draw. There's so many ways to constantly be gaining life, if you translate that into card draw this gets insanely dangerous.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tamiyo, Upriser Crowned

Saffgor: What a strange little card. Tamiyo is statted to be an aggressive, Voltron-style threat, with fantastic keywords for killing your opponents. The rest of her textbox though is all about...slow card advantage, and disincentivizing people from attacking you? I guess the thought is that Tamiyo isn’t something you want attacking you if you steal The Monarch, but at 6 mana and Haste she’ll be turning sideways regardless. This is a disjointed design.

BPhillipYork: I'm glad to see a monarch card that punishes people for de-monarching you. So many monarch cards just buff you for being the monarch, which means nobody is likely to let you keep it.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Ur-Sphinx

Saffgor: Eminence is a horrifying bit of text to see, but I think The Ur-Sphinx isn’t going to be causing issues. Sphinges have quite a bit less internal synergy, and across the board have less support (especially low along the mana curve). The free mana discount is still spooky, and enables a few loops with Colorless Changelings & Unesh, Criosphinx Sovereign, but that’s nothing Inalla and Co. weren’t already doing. Just don’t build this as a Sphinx’s Approach deck, I’m begging you. That card can do so much better.

BPhillipYork: This is way better than The Ur-Dragon though it's kind of strange. Reducing the cost of sphinxes, which are generally hugely expensive, to me is pretty dumb. Also I think the idea of their being an Ur-Sphinx is dumb, and it not being 5 color is also dumb. Sphinxes are weird mysterious creatures of secrets and contradiction and riddles. Having an original Ur-Sphinx is so reductive. There should be like 5 competing Ur-Sphinxes or something.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Nissa, Leyline Tamer

Saffgor: Nongreen Landfall is neat, and as the alt Commander to a Polymorph-matters deck this is about what I’d expect, but yeesh 7 mana is a lot. I think that precludes it from playability, especially with no inherent protection.

BPhillipYork: This is bonkers. So good with fetch lands. Which are good in and of themselves. Easy to generate insane creature dumping with this.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jace, Multiverse Architect

Saffgor: I never thought I’d see the day we get a Polymorph-matters Commander as the face of a precon. Jace appeals to my sensibilities a great deal, enabling you to turbo out big, specific Creatures & Planeswalkers by way of playing a highly-tailored 1-3 card set, and token generators. The latter is assisted by Empower Jace cards in Reality Fracture itself, but I think Jace is going to be a bit of a new player trap on the whole. Building for Polymorph is no easy thing, and the vast majority of targets you’d want to flip into via Jace rot in hand; learning you want as few targets as possible isn’t something this precon teaches, nor something it’s easy for new players to learn.

BPhillipYork: Built in polymorph is pretty strong. Jace tax seems pretty weak, but all in all you can easily build around this to turbo into something fun. Bottoming cards you don't need is nice, though you can end up not really wanting to do it. All in all a pretty interesting commander and or planeswalker to just run.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Darksteel Angel

Saffgor: Admittedly a very funny card to turn into a Moon by way of Princess Yue, but I don't think this gets there across the board. Maybe Giada?

BPhillipYork: Okay well yeah sure it's fun if not good and another instance of you can't lose, and this one also combos with Devoted Druid. Because once you have 9 mana, you can make infinite green.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Ginger, Queen of Sweets

Saffgor: Compared to Tamiyo, also in the Commander precon, this is what I expect out of a Monarch Commander. Ginger is effectively just Marchesa with food theming, and with the added bonus that she spawns tokens every upkeep, letting you accrue a massive board pretty quickly. 6 mana vs 4 does matter, as does losing your colors, but if you like Marchesa, Ginger’s a reasonable analogue.

BPhillipYork: I sort of like that this exists, but I want there to be more support for a Food Golem deck of which she can be the commander.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Memnarch, the Warden

Saffgor: That art is positively striking. The card itself doesn’t belong in the Command Zone, but as a reanimation or cheat target he seems absolutely fantastic in the right deck. Not much more to say.

BPhillipYork: Well that is obviously a ton of card draw, but its 10 mana so it's like, kind of whatever.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Omnath, Locus of the Void

Saffgor: This being 7 mana is kind of brutal compared to Final Fantasy’s Ultima, Origin of Oblivion, and Omnath both combos with less and lacks the capacity to threaten opposing Lands. He’s cool, but Ultima just does the job better if you squint and play a Horizon Stone.

BPhillipYork: A predictable but fun version of Omnath, I wonder where they will go with this next. I like the idea of putting this in an Eldrazi deck just because, it lets you bank mana and generates mana so it's like, whatever, just more big dumb colorless stuff.

That wraps up our look at the new cards of Reality Fracture. We’ll be back with regularly scheduled content later this week.
