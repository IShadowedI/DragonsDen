Kings of War Fracture Focus: Basileans - For the Glory of the Hegemon!

August 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer

G’day Tabletop Battles readers! After a few weeks marinating with the new Kings of War Fracture book, we are excited to share our opinions regarding the most fanatical human faction in the Kings of War world - the Basileans. As we shared in our Fracture book preview, the Basilean army list received a lot of exciting updates, from expansions to their lore to a whole swathe of new Command Orders and many interesting developments to the army list. Let’s dive in and see why the Hegemon of Basilea is worth fighting for!

Basileans armies are made up of a core of human soldiers. Credit: Mantic.

The Lore

The Basileans are the fantasy Eastern Roman Empire (aka the Byzantine Empire) that continued on after Primovantor (the fantasy Roman Empire) literally drowned due to magic-induced climate change. Like the Roman Empire, Primovantor was a vast grand empire of humans, famed for its well-equipped and well-trained legions and ruled over by the Hegemon (the Emperor).

The comparisons to the Romans fall apart, however, once we get to the true formation of Basilea as it is in current Kings of War. Unlike the Romans, when the Basileans were facing annihilation at the hands of ravening hordes and they prayed to the gods, the gods (the Shining Ones) listened and intervened. Warrior angels known as Elohi swept down from the heavens, and celestial powers were bestowed upon the heroes of Basilea. Where in our history Constantinople fell, the City of the Golden Horn of Basilea grew and prospered.

The dedication of the Basileans to the Shining Ones changed the culture of the empire forever. Orders of paladin knights and sisterhoods began forming to defend the faith, which were quickly brought into the military machine of the Basileans to supplement the legions. War priests wielded the celestial power of the gods, healing and purging in equal measure. To the dismay of all the enemies of Basilea, even the Elohi and Phoenixes regularly support the Basilean armies.

The Phoenix is considered one of the great Mantic models in the Basilean range. Photo credit Mantic Games

Despite the prosperity of Basilea, it has a constant need to maintain its fighting forces thanks to its proximity to the hellish Abyss. It does not help that within Basilea there is a schism of religious doctrine, and it threatens to destabilize the region. The whole problem is based on what to do about the Wicked Ones of the Abyss, the evil counterparts to the Shining Ones.

Traditionally, the Basileans were all for the detainment or even destruction of the Wicked Ones; however, recently there has been a growing movement to see the Shining Ones and Wicked Ones reunite and reform as the Celestians they once were. No one is certain this is possible, but some of the Shining Ones think they are strong enough to overcome their Wicked halves, and that’s enough for their supporters.

This is all covered in the Fracture book; however, to really get an insight into the Basileans, you can read the Sacred Ark series of books by Mark Barber which bring the world to life, starting with Steps to Deliverance. The books cover all manner of viewpoints throughout the empire, from common foot soldiers, to paladins (Orion), and even a half-human half-elohi known as Aestelle (both of which feature in the new army list). These books show just how good and bad the Basileans are.

Steps to Deliverance is the first novel in a series about the Basileans, featuring Orion and Aestelle. Credit: Mantic

Command Orders

For the Glory of the Hegemon

For the Glory of the Hegemon is a simple enough order; charging unit gains Elite (Combat). It’s good enough by itself but where it really shines is when combined with a Bane Chant from the War Priest. Giving some Paladin Foot Knights Elite AND Crushing Strength (+1) will turn them from mild threat to vengeance of the Shinings Ones made manifest. Will you get this combination off every time you need it? Unlikely, but when it does happen, the Abyss will cower (and your opponent will shudder). The name is also very flavourful, and one should definitely bellow it (politely) in a Hollywood Roman accent (see Gladiator [2000]).

Cleanse

Cleanse gives a unit Iron Resolve (+1), which is not shabby by itself but is not exactly as good as a Heal. This reflects a general reduction in Command Orders that directly heal units; instead the healing is conditional on it actually surviving a Nerve Test.

The real boon of Cleanse is that it removes any ongoing effects of spells, such as Weakness, Frozen, Scorched Earth or Hex. Many armies' entire strategy relies on reducing the effectiveness of enemy units, and Cleanse is one of the only means to overturn it. Paladin Knights are being Hindered by Scorched Earth, not anymore! This won’t always be a useful Command Order, but it is invaluable in the right situation.

Aegis of Kolosu

Now first off, everyone is wondering who or what is a Kolosu. Answer: It's the mountain upon which the Shining Ones reside in Basilea; essentially Mount Olympus. Hopefully that helps.

Regarding the command order itself, it bestows Faithful units with +1 Defence against ranged attacks in the front facing. This is almost identical to the old Forces of the Abyss Command Order but differs in one key way - the units it can affect.

The Forces of the Abyss order was limited to Upper Circle units, of which there were not many to choose from and none of them were the ones you wanted to protect from shooting. In contrast, the Aegis of Kolosu can affect Faithful units which are almost all the units in the army list; Men-at-arms, Paladins, Sisterhood. The fact that it is also issued on a roll of 4+ rather than the Abyssal’s 5+ makes it much more reliable. Prime targets are Paladin Foot Knights and Paladin Knights, getting their Defence to a mighty 6+ against shooting.

Borne by Angels

If the sole purpose of Command Orders was to bring out the uniqueness of each army, then Borne by Angels is perfect. A Infantry or Large Infantry unit gets Fly when moving (not charging). Angels sweeping down in a blaze of bright light and whole units of Paladins fly across the battlefield.

Blocking terrain in the way? No problem! Need to fix up some bad deployment? Just fly everything around until it fits! Enemy in the way? Not anymore! This will be the Command Order that will surprise opponents the most, as units move into places they weren’t supposed to get to.

Ogre Palace Guard can be potentially flown across the battlefield by angels... which would look quite funny considering an Elohi is slightly smaller in stature. Photo credit Mantic Games

Fell the Beast

For the simple minded (such as yours truly), Fell the Beast is the most satisfying of the new Basilean Command Orders; an Infantry or Cavalry unit gets Slayer (+nD3 - Combat), ‘n’ being the number of successful dice rolled in the issuing. The fact that this is PLUS Slayer is what makes it nice and spicy, because there are a few powerful units in the Basilean army list that have Slayer (D6) already, and adding another 1-3 D3 on top of that can turn that unit into a real can-opener! We cannot wait to have that one battle (it will likely only happen once) where our Paladin Monster Slayers have Slayer 12.

Spells

Holy Lance

The new spell exclusively available to the Basileans in Fracture is Holy Lance, a literal laser beam of holy radiant damage that starts off strong and tapers with distance. It has Piercing (1) and Shattering (1), and at 12” and below gets to roll 9 dice to hit. If going for 24” you get 7 dice and up to 36” you get 5 dice.

At this point in 4th edition, all offensive damage spells must be compared to Blizzard (3), and the Holy Lance compares favourably depending on the situation simply because it is not classed as a Magic Missile. It hits on 4+ no matter what (with the exception of Spellward), and means that your War Priest need not sit still to get that bonus to shooting. It can keep up with the army and issue the great Basilean Command Orders. Obviously the range from target will determine if it is better or worse than Blizzard (3) in terms of damage output, but overall Holy Lance works better with the army. If you really want to shoot the enemy off, then just take another War Priest with Blizzard (3) and have the best of both worlds.

Core and Auxiliary

Paladin Monster Slayers

The Paladin Foot Knights of the Basileans are already pretty tasty, but running the Defense 4+ variant in order to get Crushing Strength (2) is a bit precarious for a regiment of Nerve 17+. Paladin Monster Slayers fix this problem by giving you the horde of infantry, pushing their nerve to a very respectable 24+ and giving them Slayer (D6) to boot. This is the unit that will terrify your opponent, and this is the unit you should do everything in your power to keep healthy, with all the Heal, Iron Resolve, Cleanse, and Celestial Restoration you can muster. Then when it gets into combat hit it with the one-two combo that is Bane Chant and For the Glory of the Hegemon (or Fell the Beast, depending on the enemy) and watch the enemy break like a fart in the wind.

Of course, there remains the difficulties inherent in most hordes of infantry. It can be unwieldy. It’s a major target for ranged attacks. It’s a lot of points. But Orcs players happily invest in hordes of Greatax, and they are on massive Heavy Infantry bases, so if anything this is easy mode, especially with the awesome Command Orders. And unlike Orcs, in Basileans, Infantry can fly…

Specialists

Order of the Abyssal Hunt

That’s not it for massive hordes in the Basileans, because they get something even bigger: The cavalry horde, in the form of the Order of the Abyssal Hunt. This unit has been taken from the former Order of the Brothermark, which in the lore were steadily being absorbed by the Basilean armies. Now they are represented as a giant cavalry horde (which is the same size as an Orc horde) with a very decent profile. Where regular Paladin Knights have Thunderous Charge (3), the Order has Crushing Strength (1) and Thunderous Charge (2). Like the Monster Slayers they come with Slayer (D6), which makes them situationally devastating (sorry Ogres). The real downside to this unit versus the regular knights is that by being a Unique unit, they are unable to take magic items, the key one being Sir Jesse’s Boots of Striding, to ensure at least one charge they make is not Hindered. That is unfortunately the fate of most cavalry hordes, charging while Hindered, so at least they have Crushing Strength (1) to help with that. This is definitely a unit to take because you love the idea of massive cavalry charges, but not if you want the most efficient use of points (360 of them!) or an easy to use unit.

Champions

Aestelle

Coming straight from the Sacred Ark series of books, Aestelle is one of the main characters, an ex-Sisterhood initiate with Elohi blood in her veins. She’s a decent close-combat hero with 5 Melee 3+ attacks with Crushing Strength (1), Elite, and Vicious. She even has a bow attack, but alas no Elite and Vicious for that. The main downside is she lacks Inspiring, which would make including her a difficult choice were it not for the Aura (Iron Resolve (+1)). Everything in the army has Iron Resolve, and bumping that up to a baseline of (2) is nothing to sneeze at. Combined with the Cleanse Command Order and suddenly units are healing 3 damage every time they pass a Nerve Test. This doesn’t make her auto-include, but it at least means she has a use despite the lack of Inspiring.

Aestelle is available as an STL from the Mantic Vault and myminifactory. Credit: Mantic Games

Orion

Orion is a character followed throughout the Sacred Ark books, from his tragic beginnings as a hopeless squire, to the battles as the implacable paladin he became. This is represented in Kings of War as one heck of a combat character. His basic stats are pretty standard among combat characters - Speed 8, Melee 3+, Defense 5+, 5 attacks with Elite and Crushing Strength (2). There’s no doubt Orion can do some damage, but he excels in a manner few other heroes truly excel - fighting other heroes.

It’s no secret that Duellist in fourth edition is a rather lackluster rule. It grants a bonus number of attacks when attacking a hero unit, typically just D3. Getting 1 to 3 extra attacks is hardly enough to take down most enemy heroes, and most often the kinds of units that have Duellist lack Inspiring, making them even less desirable a choice. Orion not only has Duellist (D3+2), giving him the potential of hitting with 10 attacks, but he also has the unique special rule Disarm. If he hits an enemy hero, they suffer from the effect of Weakness. Couple this with the fact Orion has Inspiring and we have a unit worth having!

Orion is available as an STL from Mantic Vault and myminifactory. Credit: Mantic Games

Warlords

Abbess

The first of the Warlords of the Basileans to be tweaked slightly, the Abbess’s stats haven’t changed, but the way she affects the overall army has. Now she touts an Aura (Brutal (+1 Sisterhood only)) as well as making Sisterhood Panther Lancers into Core choices. In short, if you want an army full of Sisterhood units, then this is the Warlord for you. Unfortunately, it means that outside of that niche, she has not much applicability compared to the other Warlords. She might be the cheapest Warlord, but certainly not the most useful.

High Paladin

Like the Abbess, the High Paladin hasn’t changed much, but crucially has gained a unique ability called Holy Edict: The ability to issue Command Orders up to 15”. Considering how good, but in some cases situational, the Basilean Command Orders are, having a Warlord capable of covering a 30” bubble of the army is incredibly useful. On top of that, the High Paladin makes regiments of Paladin Knights Core choices, freeing up those Specialist slots for units like the Ogre Palace Guard. Almost any Basilean army can benefit from having the High Paladin as a Warlord. Strangely, when the High Paladin mounts a dragon, they lose the Holy Edict ability. Maybe it’s hard to hear the commands with all that wing flapping and roaring.

Samacris Mother of Phoenixes

Samacris, the spellcaster Elohi hero, has also changed subtly. She no longer interacts with the Elohi, but rather the Phoenix, granting them the ability to take the spell Martyr’s Prayer (5). This spell enables the transfer of damage from a friendly unit to the caster for each success rolled, essentially the Phoenix can soak up damage. This spell was previously available only to the War Priest, who has a pretty low ability to soak up damage before expiring. The Phoenix on the other hand not only has much higher Nerve, but also boasts Regeneration (4+), making it capable of taking advantage of this spell in a way no other unit could.

Samacris might cost a lot of points, but her utility cannot be denied. Credit: Mantic Games

Gnaeus Sallustis

Gnaeus disappeared at the advent of fourth edition, but now he’s back with a very happy new model. He’s an exceptional combat hero with 7 attacks with Crushing Strength (2), a hefty Nerve 17+ with Iron Resolve (1) and Fearless, and Speed 9. He cannot be ignored but he is also quite hard to remove. This would already be a nice package for the 240 points he costs, but he also has Master of the Field, the ability to issue two Command Orders a turn. One Warlord, two Command Orders, incredible value.

Gnaeus is a very good warlord for any Basilean army. Credit: Mantic Games

My Army List: The Silversaints of San Michon

Over the course of writing this article, I quickly found a lot to like about the Basilean army list. It’s got a lot of simple humans just trying to fight the evil of the world, but enhanced with holy magic that can really make them all superhuman.

This got me inspired to start a Basilean army with the idea of recreating the forces that feature in the book series Empire of the Vampire by Jay Kristoff. If you haven’t read Empire of the Vampire, I heartily recommend it for all that appreciate an epic set in a post-apocalyptic medieval vampire world. In fact, I want to thank the hosts of Unplugged Radio (a Kings of War podcast) for mentioning it.

Empire of the Vampire, an excellent book series for any lover of dark fantasy. Can also recommend the audiobook. Credit: Jay Kristoff

The whole reason for mentioning the Empire of the Vampire is the holy order of half-vampire vampire slayers called Silversaints that feature in the book. Their vampire fathers have imbued them with unholy strength, and the Order of San Michon collects these half-vampires and trains them to be vampire slayers, lest they instead give in to the sanguine hunger. When I saw the new unit of Paladin Monster Slayers, I knew these to be my Silversaints.

The rest of the army simply came together to reinforce the theme. Paladin Knights, Men-at-arms, Sisterhood, all of these are found in the world of Empire of the Vampire. The only unit that isn’t present in that world are Elohi, so my army is notably lacking the more angelic units on offer in the Basilean army list. I will likely introduce them with time (mayhaps if Mantic remakes them?), but for now they are out.

The central power of this army are the two hordes - the horde of Paladin Monster Slayers and the Order of the Abyssal Hunt. The army needs to act as a delivery mechanism for these hordes to ensure I get value on investment.

For Monster Slayers there are the Men-at-arms regiments (both the spear and crossbow wielding variants), which will precede the Monster Slayers in a manner similar to the Roman quincunx formation. This formation enables the independent units to support each other when engaged, the only tricky part is that this takes a lot of space to work optimally and not every table’s terrain will be so facilitating.

On that note of space, the cavalry horde that is the Order of the Abyssal Hunt will be the hardest to use well. Units like Orion and the Gur Panthers will be escorting them, tying up enemy units, but it might not be enough. Of course, it will be difficult to avoid terrain on the charge, but thankfully the Abyssal Hunt has that critical Crushing Strength to help them. Watch this space, as this is the part of the list that will change the most.

Finally there’s the ranged pressure to force engagements. Most of the army is not fast, but the two cannons, crossbowmen, Sisterhood Scouts and the Holy Lance of the War Priest should be enough to get the enemy moving.

It’s not a typical Basilean army, but it is one I am very excited to make. All the units will be Mantic with the exception of the Paladin Monster Slayers, which I have taken to Titancraft to make some Silversaint models. Silversaints are so called because of the silver tattoos that adorn their body, so I am in for a ridiculous level of free hand (which is not something I typically do).

What armies do you, dear readers, have in mind? One festooned with Elohi and Phoenixes? Or perhaps it’s all about getting as many Ogre Palace Guard? Let me know on the socials, I am keen to read them!
