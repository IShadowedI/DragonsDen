Archetype

Heavy Carnival

Thoughts

We start today with an unusually heavy variant of Carnival of Excess, utilising a range of big threats and far fewer of the utility pieces the detachment is known for. It also utilises the Elegant Brutes detachment solely for the Take and Hold Disposition, aiming to force the opponent into a brutal confrontation over central objectives. It should be pretty well placed to win that, given how brutal the concentration of violence it has access to is, and this list is also unusually well placed to survive the Dataslate. It takes a hit, but Carnival was one of the builds most targeted by the update, and this version dodges the level of impact more conventional versions will see. It honesly also looks like a tonne of fun, always been a fan of more out there monster mash setups, so congratulations Jean-Luc, and I hope this keeps serving you well.

Archetype

Triple Threat Tsons

Thoughts

Another curveball in undefeated second place, this list makes that rare (at least up till now, Ork Codex incoming) move of using three 1DP detachments, using the buffs to Mutants and Robots to anchor the build, and then the solid utility/deterrent pieces from Ritual of Regeneration to keep the build on the table and scoring. The power of the Robots in combination with the hammer threat of the Terminators from Ingress will force foes to cede quite a bit of the battlefield to the Thousand Sons, making Disruption an unusually solid prospect, and that will only get better with some of the map changes from the update. Complete left field stuff here, love to see it, well done David.

Dreadknights and Purifiers

Thoughts

Grey Knights break through to a win here, utilising lots of Purifiers and Dreadknights to evaporate the foe from key objectives, and a small number of Terminators to then lock them in. I'm pretty firm in my opinions that Grey Knights needed a bit of help in the Dataslate, despite the success here, but they do potentially have some very solid tools to take down Orks, so I can imagine a world where that book being popular gives them a little bit of a bump. I guess we'll see soon enough, congratulations to Hugh for making them work as they are.

Thoughts

Chris has been kind enough to send us a comprehensive writeup of both his list, and the final game. Let's find out how such an unusual build holds together:

Chris: This list was built to work off of the Disruption missions by keeping primary scores as low as possible, using my mobility to either punish my opponents primary or to press hard to end games early.  The list works in three aspects to best accomplish the goal.  The scoring element of the list uses two squads of chaos cultists to both screen the backfield against opponents and maintain space to deepstrike using Warpstrike Champions defensively if needed. Working in concert, the unit of Red Corsairs as well as the Bestmen are there for early game scoring.  With how popular Take and Hold is as a disposition, it's important to have a setup that can get me 15 primary points turn one if I go first, giving me a huge advantage from the outset. The final element of scoring rests in the solo Terminator Lord, who deepstrikes alongside a Terminator block to trigger free Siegebreaker Strike for Ignore Cover, and provides a late game action piece that can be challenging to remove effectively.

The second aspect of the list is the two main hammer thrusts in the form of the two ten-man Terminator squads with Sorcetors.  Each of these squads are kitted out for maximum firepower with bolters and autocannons.  One of the sorcerers has Ashur’s Binding Runes to further provide a hefty turn one threat.  Just having the threat present in the list of a turn one deepstrike makes opponents play games differently.  The sorcerer also provides some very nice utility to the squads such as access to Death Hex (the ability to grant extra AP to a unit) as well as re-roll charges.  When this combines with the Portal of Spite stratagem it gives the unit a 7” charge re-rolling.  A turn one Deep Strike from tenTterminators shooting and clogging a backfield can be crippling for some armies.  The second terminator squad becomes a turn 2-3 threat to engage in concert with the other combat units to overwhelm the opponent.  Both of the units benefit from the Cabal of Chaos giving the units access to healing, as well as the shooting bonus strength which helps with toughness breakpoints.

The final aspect of the list is the two melee haymakers from the Huron Terminator squad and Kravek's Mulitators.  Each of these has a different role that helps shore up weaknesses in the list.  Huron’s squad swaps autocannons for heavy flamers to help with horde clearance as well as having an overwatch threat to deny opponents secondaries.  Huron’s aura ability can not be understated here either, as in a very elite list such as this an aura of +1 OC is huge when trying to deny primary.  Since, most Disruption missions only require the player to hold a single objective, this allows Huron to deny central objectives against other dispositions who need to hold 2 or more objectives.  Kravek is the best answer to Defilers and knights.  This list has a weakness for mass tough hulls and Kravek gives an answer to at least take out one threat to protect the terminators.

The Final Game: 

In the final game of the event I was facing off against Astra Militarum running Mechanized Assault and Abhuman detail.  Since my opponent had a Stormlord full of Ogryn I had to respect its threat ranges, as with Rapid Fire a squad could (and later in the game did) disembark and  maul an entire terminator squad.  We were playing on layout B and I got the first turn, which is usually a good thing for my army but my opponent deployed very defensively giving me no targetss turn one for aggression. I did get Secure as an easy turn one Secondary, and held and Forward Position.

My opponent also played a very defensive turn one, only sending out five Ratlings to retake the central objective and staged his Bullgryn in offensive positons, after which I returned my 2nd Terminator squad, and Kravek to deepstrike.  On my turn two I drew Beacon and Center Ground.  Seeing my opponent place his Bullgryn so far forward, I decided now was the moment to strike.  Both of my Terminator squads arrived with line of sight on the Bullgryn squads, and both also made the charge, killing one Bullgryn unit outright, and leaving the second squad to just 2 models left.  In the center my Red Corsair Raiders also killed the ratlings, unlocking their improved OC.

On my opponents turn he prepared for the crack back. The Terminator squad on my opponents expansion faced full Ogryn and Scion squads, while the others were assailed by two Scion squads (one of which was led by Yarrick) and the Stormlord.  While my Terminator squad on the objective was reduced to just two models, and the other to just the Sorcerer alone, neither squad died fully, denying my opponent his Secondary and Primary for turn 2.  My opponent made the decision to urgently try to score points, and he charged Yarrick’s squad into my Red Corsair Raiders, I used my -1 AP stratagem and only lost two models, and then killed 6 scions back, allowing the remaining Raiders to hold the objective thanks to being OC3 each.

On my turn three I brought in Huron and Kraveks units and sealed the deal, making a 12” charge onto my opponents home and a 10” charge to destroy his Ogryn squad.  At the end of my turn three my opponent was left with just A stormlord and a smattering of weakened units testing Battle-shock, and that was it.

Result

Chaos Space Marines (Warpstrike Champions/Cabal of Chaos) Victory - 100 - 50

Thoughts

Bike and Allarus Custodes vs. Ravener-heavy Invasion Fleet here, playing a Take and Hold mirror that's sure to get extremely violent. Asset-wise, the key units on each side feel like the Raveners (obviously, super killy and quite durable with a 5+ Feel No Pain available) and primary-denying Hormagaunts for the Tyranids, and the Wardens and Venatari for the Custodes. The Allarus are fine here, and enjoy getting to do re-rolls against the Raveners, but they're also pretty vulnerable to getting devoured by said snakes. The Wardens are a very different kettle of fish, as the AP reduction from Valerian combines with their once-per-game Feel No Pain to create something that can plausibly face tank a full Ravener brick, then cause some problems in return. The Venatari, meanwhile, will kill pretty much whatever they touch after ingress, and the Tyranids don't have that many alpha units, so have to respect them.

Good tools on both sides, and also some ways to work around them. Maleceptors help with the Warden problem, as if they snipe a few off with D3 shots, the overall threat presented by the unit diminishes considerably. In the other direction, the Allarus into Hormagaunts feels like overkill, but that doesn't stop them turning them into dust the second they so much as look in that direction, and just in general the Custodes enjoy being surprisingly near parity on activations. That said, into Invasion Fleet (with access to the Feel No Pain stratagem), the small Bike units are less useful than they'd normally be, as they won't reliably do enough to justify their price tag. The last big potential curveball here is Shadow - more activations makes the Custodes less likely to suffer a total blowout here, but if they've already lagged behind, it could be a real killing blow.

Who wins, then? I think what probably ends up sealing this here is the free double Ingress the two Ravener units can do. Tyranids winning this game reliably needs both Ravener units to make good charges, but it turns out Ingress > 10" movement makes that pretty comfortable to set up, and I don't think there's quite anything the Custodes can do to stop it. Being aggressive with the bikes (since they won't be that useful otherwise) can maybe delay this somewhat, but I doubt it's enough, and the bugs were triumphant.
