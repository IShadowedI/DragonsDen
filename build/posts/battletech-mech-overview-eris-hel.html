BattleTech Mech Overview: Eris/Hel

November 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

First released in plastic in the Core Box, the Eris is a new take on being a simple trooper medium mech. Like the Griffin or Wolverine, it’s got adequate mobility, armor, and firepower – armor is let down slightly by being 50 tons instead of the classic 55 ton trooper, but it’s still more than adequate. It’s got an interesting backstory, as it was first created as the Hel by an industrial mech manufacturer expanding into battlemechs, but they ended up selling the specifications elsewhere when they were unable to get buyers. Strange, since this is a pretty good mech. Amusingly, as record sheets do exist for the Hel, it can be used as a work-around in a tournament that limits you to only having a single copy of a mech variant – the Eris ERS-2N and Hel HL-1 are identical in all but name.

Jade Falcon Eris. Credit: Jack Hunter

Chassis

The Eris is a 50 ton medium mech with all three variants moving 5/8/5 or 5/8/7 with a partial wing. It’s a straightforward design, an XL engine, endo-steel structure, and standard armor combine to give it enough crit slots for a decent armament while not being excessively limited on tonnage. Armor layout is good, everything is armored past the key 15-pip breakpoint, and at 22 armor the legs aren’t getting taken off too easily. I do wish that the rear side torsos had 6 pips of armor instead of 5, but I don’t think there’s anywhere I’d pull armor away from to make that happen.

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘Mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

Broken Arrows Eris ERS-2N. Credit: Valk

ERS-2H

A heavy PPC, paired ER medium lasers, and a streak SRM 6 rack give this the ability to do a little bit of everything, even if it doesn’t excel at anything. The XL engine renders it vulnerable to torso destruction, but it’s fully armored so that’s not a big concern to me – it’s still going to take enough firepower to bring down that you’ll usually get good value out of it. At 1,674 BV it makes a great filler unit for a force, especially as the left arm has hand actuators, useful for any Core Rules missions that use mobile objectives.

Jack’s Rating: B

ERS-2N

Superficially similar to the 2H, the 2N adds a partial wing to boost mobility up to 5/8/7, and swaps the weapon load out for a snub-nose PPC, pair of MML 5s, and trio of small x-pulse lasers. While it does go up four heat on a run, you’re not usually going to be firing the PPC and small pulses at the same time, and only going up four still leaves you below any heat penalties if you do need to. It also only goes up one heat on a full jump with snub + MMLs (or neutral with pulse + MMLs), so you can jump and fire reasonably effectively (not that jumping and firing will have you hitting much). Generally I think this is a more useful weapon load than the 2H. You do lose out on the headcap potential from the heavy PPC, but you’ve got more missile tubes, can pack them with infernos or acid SRMs, and the small pulses give you a bit more ability to deal with light harassers. It’s also a drop down to 1,400 BV, making it easier to fit into a list.

Jack’s Rating: B+

Eris. Credit: Jack Hunter

ERS-3R

Normally I’d call this some periphery bullshit, but it appears to be made solely by the Free Worlds League. Like the 2N it has a partial wing for 5/8/7 mobility, and actually picks up an extra nine points of armor. It does have three ER medium lasers, but most of the armament is the 24 rocket launcher 10s it carries. Mostly I think you fire two of these per turn along with your medium lasers while keeping an eye out for anything that falls down and can’t get up, shuts down, passes out, or is otherwise immobilized – and then you have two choices. Either unload everything, probably trading the Eris for their mech, or skip your lasers to fire 12 of the rocket launchers. In the first situation, you’ve got 89 heat, and sinking 23 per turn will take three turns before you even have a chance to turn on again. In the second you’ll go up to 15 heat, only needing to avoid shutdown on a 4+, and can jump away the next turn. I don’t really think either is a strategy you want to count on for winning games, and 1,868 BV is way more than I’d pay for this, but it’s pretty funny.

Jack’s Rating: D+

Hel HL-1

As mentioned in the intro, this is identical in every way except the name to the Eris ERS-2H. It’s got a different entry on MUL, so it can dodge around chassis/variant limitations in an event.

Hel HL-2

The Hl-2 swaps the heavy PPC of the ERS-2H/HL-1 for a pair of large lasers. I’m neutral on this swap – it’s more theoretical damage, higher chance to put at least some damage through, and gets rid of the awkward minimum range that overlaps with your SRMs. On the other hand, putting out a 15 point hit is very useful. It’s 1,549 BV and compares very closely to the Starslayer STY-3C. I would generally start out with the ERS-2H in my list, but if I need that hundred-odd BV I’ll swap down to the HL-2 without complaint.

Jack’s Rating: B

Hastati Sentinels Eris. Credit: Jack Hunter

Conclusion

It’s a very simple mech that’s hard to go wrong with. The rocket variant is much more funny than good, which is pretty typical, but all three of the other variants make for good simple troopers. Take whichever one you have BV available for and you’ll get an easy to use reliable mech.
