Tabletop Battles Reviews: Horus Heresy Falchion, Whirlwind and Spartan

May 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Behold, the benevolent Games Workshop has provided us with more tanks, yet again! This time we are granted a Falchion super-heavy tank, a Whirlwind missile tank, and a Spartan with different guns.

Due to the modular way that Games Workshop has designed heresy kits, these vehicles are all really existing tanks plus a new sprue or two rather than totally new creations. We’ll look at how these tanks are different from previous versions and the options you get for building them.

Thanks to Games Workshop for sending us these tanks to play with in order to review.

The Falchion

Meltabombed: Oh boy oh boy! Long have I desired to have a Falchion for my Blood Angels, and now I can finally realize that dream! Unfortunately, I was unable to paint said tank into those colors; not only is this a hefty build that is worth lavishing the time on, I am also in the throes of teaching a tiny human how to sleep, so my free time (and rest!) are at a premium. If anyone has suggestions for suitably Blood Angel-y themed names for this beast, drop a comment or @ in the discord, I’d love to hear some!

As someone who struggled through building the hybrid plastic-resin super heavies of yesteryear (in my case, a Glaive), I can say for sure that this was a miles better experience! As with any plastic GW Heresy tank, take your time, dry fit everything before gluing, and be ready with the sprue goo or putty to do some gap filling. Alternatively, you could go the scale modelers route and add some welding or manufacturing texture to the tank instead; it’s a great canvas for it! As with any center-piece model, this one is worth taking your time on. That said, there are a few things in particular you should watch out for. 

Credit: Meltabombed

Credit: Meltabombed

The only different parts on this kit from the Fellblade (and, I'm told, the Glaive) are the front fuselage part and the neutron-wave cannon housing that goes on top. It also comes with the full array of new sponson weapons, which I’m sure we’ll talk about more in the Spartan portion of this article. 

Credit: Meltabombed

I had the tank fully assembled for photos (sans treads, I always paint these separately and leave them off my tanks until final weathering steps), but a LOT of pieces on this are only Blu-tacked in. The smoke stacks, fuel barrels, sponsons, smoke launchers, coolant pipes, little blinky light cable thingies, tank commander, and front weapon shield are all stuck on for separate painting; I figured it would be a disaster to try to paint the tank with them all on there. I’m not normally one for this amount of sub-assembly, but on a beastly project like this one, I think it’s worth the extra time/effort. The Blood Angel resin upgrade doors are just dry-fit in place, nice and snug. The barrels are stuck in place with a tiny drop of super glue so they weren’t saggy for the photos and could easily be snapped off later once I’m ready for them.  

Credit: Meltabombed

Which brings us to the biggest issue of this build and something I think will prove a headache for many; the laser cannon barrels. The each barrel is 8 total pieces, with a trio of inner barrel parts, a trio of outer barrel parts, and then a piece for each end. I think you would be hard-pressed to not have gaps to fill on these. The trap lies where the gate marks are on the outer barrel parts (part N33 in the instructions); if you are a little too overzealous with filing the sprue gates away, you will be making a huge headache for yourself in the future as you are grinding away the plastic the joins the parts around the inner barrel. My advice would be to trim the gates as close as possible with only nippers and/or a knife, then leave them alone. Alternatively, be ready with some plastic putty to sand them smooth afterwards. For the ribbing around the ends of the barrels, just…do your best. These are gonna be rough, and I’m not sure I have any advice other than sprue goo or plastic putty to join the seams then carefully sand and scrape them smooth later. 

Credit: Meltabombed

I can’t say I understand what goes into designing a sprue, but why on earth are these split like this? On probably the most prominent part of the kit? It took me at least two separate nights of a couple hours work each to get these to this point, and I have more I’d like to do (again, center-piece model; it needs to be perfect) and are a big part of why I ran out of time to try to put some paint on this behemoth. This aspect of the build alone takes this from a B+/A build down to a C/C- for me; it’s so demoralizing to have to sink so much time into the main feature of a kit just due to bad design. It’s still an otherwise fine build and it is still miles better than the previous iteration, but like, damn guys…you’ve been warned. 

Max: It really can't be emphasized enough how annoying this is on an otherwise really nice model. The design team have done a great job in the past with hiding seams but these barrels are a horrible build experience.

Falchion Subassemblies. Credit: Jack Hunter

Jack: My barrels were a bit cleaner, but still have very clear seams. It's disappointing that they're right at the top of the barrels, even flipping it 180 degrees so there's a seam at 60 degrees and 300 degrees on the top half would be a lot cleaner. I ended up with a huge number of sub-assemblies, for access, color, or easy weathering. Most interesting to call out is that on the laser destroyer sponsons it's easy to leave both side barrels off until after painting, which saves a ton of awkward angles to get at the inside of the barrels like you would've needed to on quad lascannon sponsons.

Painting

Meltabombed: As I said I was not able to get any paint on this monster in the time I had, but to be honest I'm not sure I would have wanted to. I probably could have gotten a layer of primer down, or possibly an initial pre-shade, but this is not the project to rush on; this is a tank to take your time on and lovingly render every detail. Some might say that it's a waste of time on a tank you wont use in 85% of your games, but to them I say this is why no one will remember your name! A giant center-piece Super Heavy like the Falchion is exactly the place to waste hobby hours on things nobody will ever see but YOU know are there. If nothing else, it's a badge of honor to sit in your case and look pretty, what better time than on a Super Heavy to push yourself as a hobbyist/painter? If you'd be interested in seeing a series about how I tackle such a massive project, drop a comment or an @ in the discord; I'm sure I could be convinced!

Whirlwind for Horus Heresy. Credit: NotThatHenryC

The Whirlwind

Assembly

The Whirlwind is obviously a Rhino with a box full of missiles on top. It builds exactly as a normal Rhino would until you stick a plate on top for the turret, and the missile launcher. Here's the sprue with the extra stuff.

Whirlwind sprue pic. Credit: NotThatHenryC

They give you a Predator-style front but I decided to use the rhino’s two front doors, as they have the same armour value. As a bonus, this also means I can use the vehicle as a Rhino if I want, but I'm not sure that'll ever happen. I just wanted to emphasize that this isn't an up-armoured hull.

The launcher itself goes together well. The curved corner plates are a single piece, meaning they’ve avoided having a seam along there. They leave a few gaps in the top after joining, which you fill with some pieces that represent vents and stuff. This all works fine without any gaps or prominent sprue gates to clean up. 

The turret rotates but isn’t intended to tilt up and down. There are tabs in the joint to keep it in place and their positioning makes them a bit awkward to remove. I cut them out anyway and stuck in a couple of pairs of 2x1mm magnets to make a hinge. This was easy enough to do if, like me, you like doing stuff like this for no reason at all.

Loading...


You get all the pieces to make a rhino, so I left the top plate unglued. It sits there very snugly. I did the same for the cupolas so I can swap various options. I think a havoc launcher might be the go-to, though if you’re just hiding the Whirlwind that isn’t needed.

Painting

I treated this Whirlwind as a test vehicle for a Sons of Horus army I’m starting on, so my recipe was a bit experimental. I started with a spray of Colour Forge Renegade Green on the hull, while the cupolas and launcher got matt black.

Loading...


The hull is painted using sponges to stipple on a gradient of Sons of Horus Green to Lupercal Green. I then did the edges in Sybarite Green. I think it might be good to stopple on some Sybarite Green in places to get a stronger transition, so I’ll try that in future. This isn’t bad at all though and I like how dark it is.

The black bits are similar. There’s Dark Reaper and Thunderhawk Blue on there, edged with Blue Horror. I washed this back in places with Black Templar, using Black Legion where I wanted it to be properly dark, like on the sky/earth line on the launcher.

I used a mixture of the transfers in the box with a couple from the Sons of Horus sheet, blended in using micro sol. The big eye on the back is a little weird and might be better against a black background, so maybe I’ll paint the door black. It’s probably fine though.

Whirlwind becomes a Rhino. Credit: NotThatHenryC

I haven't glued the roof part on and I've got the doors, so in theory this can work as a Rhino too. If you want another Rhino for your army then there's a reasonably good argument for getting something like a Whirlwind or Scorpius instead, to have the option of some shooty support.

Spartan Prometheus. Credit: realSnice

The Spartan

Soggy: Last and probably least from this wave of releases is the Spartan Prometheus, not in terms of size, quality or utility, but in terms of novelty. 

Spartan Prometheus Sprues. Credit: Soggy

The Spartan Prometheus is made from the existing four plastic Spartan sprues, vehicle accessory sprue and a single sprue which differs - the sponsons. The sponsons being the laser heavy destroyer ( which many people proxy with the lascannon arrays anyway) and the Gravis Heavy Bolter arrays. This sprue was first seen only relatively recently with the plastic Fellblade release. 

Salamanders Spartan Prometheus. Credit - Soggy

While we're glad to have the option, this genuinely could have been a case where the sprue was made available via direct order similar to the Dreadnought weapon sprues, particularly when Spartans are often singular in a given army - the biggest and best party bus fit for a Primarch and their entourage.

Meltabombed: Occupied as I was with the Falchion, I did not have a chance to build another Spartan, but I have been wanting some bolter sponsons for already existing Spartans, so they are a welcome addition to my armory! Bolters are cool. All my homies love bolters. 

I agree with Soggy; this feels like it could have been a direct order sprue rather than a repackage, and honestly it probably will be at some point once these don’t sell. If you’re starting a new army from scratch and you REALLY need those bolter sponsons and dont have a set from one of your super-heavies sitting around you can borrow, sure. But I’m sure there are still cheap Age of Darkness box Spartans floating around that you could pick up cheaper. 

Assembly

As a result, the assembly here is nothing that anyone who picked up the Age of Darkness box isn't mostly familiar with already. That said if you are new to the system, or if your Age of Darkness box still resides in your pile of potential, read on. 

The Spartan may just look like an XL land raider, and well, you aren't wrong. It's just like building one, just slightly bigger - and by that I mean it's a very straightforward build with no surprises. 

Spartan Prometheus WIP. Credit: Soggy

I've always left the sides separate to the main hull when it comes time for subassemblies, which I find makes it easier to work with and mask if I'm feeling that way. Due to being pressed for time with this review I left the tracks on the sprue - to let me prime and drybrush them in place. This largely worked, although on a centerpiece model I wouldn't recommend it due to the inconsistencies with the sprue contact points.

Salamanders Spartan Prometheus. Credit - Soggy

Roll Out

We're all suckers for big tanks as they scratch some long lost childhood memory of toy soldiers. Otherwise this wave consists of largely predictable releases filling out the range of large vehicle kits available on plastic, some of which could have just been an upgrade sprue - nice to have for completeness sake.
