Narrative Forge: Five Leagues from the Mortal Realms - Part 1

November 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Our “Narrative Forge” hobby articles exist to encourage stretching yourself out in the hobby - and that includes trying new games and rules. But those can be quite a commitment... unless you use what you already have! Today, Hydriatus takes a look at using Five Leagues From The Borderlands forge a narrative for your Age of Sigmar collection! This first part covers the character creation and setting setup for a campaign. 

Released in 2022, the 3rd edition of Five Leagues From The Borderlands offers a comprehensive set of solo rules to play out the adventures of a warband in a frontier setting. Designed for model agnostic play on a 2'x2' or 3'x3' board, it offers an RPG-lite experience with a procedurally generated campaign world.

Perfect for our purposes - forging a narrative to go with an Age of Sigmar army!

Five Leagues From The Borderlands Rulebook. (Credit - Modiphius)

I've recently been working on a Slaves to Darkness force, using a mix of Age of Sigmar, Old World, and third party miniatures to fit my theme of a Darkoath Tribe hailing from the frozen plains of Bjarl in the Realm of Ghur - whose story we will flesh out with the help of a solo Five Leagues From The Borderlands campaign!

What We Need

To begin, we'll need:

  • The Five Leagues From The Borderlands rulebook.
  • Enough miniatures to represent your warband and some foes. Any two-player starter set will furnish you with enough, or the equivalent of a Warcry box for your warband and a  Spearhead for your foes.
  • A board and scenery - while officially the game is designed for 2'x2' or 3'x3', it works just as well on a Spearhead board! You will need way more scenery than a Spearhead set comes with though.

The Rules

Playing the game itself is simple - characters roll for initiative at the start of a round, with the result and their Agility determining their order of activation alongside the enemies. Models first move and then either perform a combat action or a non-combat action (like running or casting magic). After every model has activated, there is a tracking phase during which morale checks are carried out and reinforcements appear.

Combat involves rolling a D6, adding the model's Combat Skill, and meeting/beating a target number. If successful, you then roll another D6 and compare it to the target's armor - if you match the number, the target is stunned. If you beat the number, you roll another D6 and compare that to the target's Toughness - if you beat it, they are removed from play. If you meet the number or roll under, they are wounded and stunned, and on a natural 1 they are just stunned.

(Stunned figures have penalties in melee, making it easier to wound and slay them with a follow up. If a wounded model would become wounded again, they are removed from play.)

Magic is handled differently - you roll 2D6 plus the model's Casting Skill, and try to meet or beat the target number associated with the spell you want to cast. This works the same as background skills, which you can make during the campaign phase to try to obtain resources or information or otherwise progress your adventure.

The Characters

In Five Leagues From The Borderlands, you create a skirmish warband who wander around a region dealing with random encounters (tabletop battles).

Your warband consists of 6 characters, represented by suitable miniatures - 4 heroes and 2 followers. Each character has a stat line consisting of Agility, Speed, Combat Skill, Toughness, and Casting Ability (if they are the Mystic archetype). They also have a number of skills based on their origin and background, which you can roll against to help with preparing for a battle or recovering after one.

Origins and backgrounds also grant various stat increases, special abilities, equipment, or skills. Wealth and equipment is generated individually for each character, but can be pooled and reallocated.

Once the 4 heroes are generated, you pick one to be your Avatar, which grants it more Will and Luck, as well as some randomly generated backstory. This is, for all intents and purposes, your warband leader.

As Five Leagues is based on low-fantasy, fairly small encounters, it makes sense to build your warband out of a single unit option. As I was building up my army for games of Spearhead at first, I chose to base my warband on my eventual unit of Darkoath Savagers. As the basic Savagers miniatures did not match the winter theme I was going for, I turned to mostly using Old World Chaos Marauders as a basis, with some Darkoath models from Warhammer Underworlds mixed in to represent the models supposed to be on different sized bases within the unit. And because I wanted a clear leader, I chose to add my Darkoath Warqueen as well - because I think the model looks cool and makes a nice focal point for a warband.

Vizhna and her companions embark upon the Path to Glory! (Credit - Hydriatus)

Character Creation

With my miniatures chosen, it was time to figure out how to represent them within the game itself.

Vizhna, the Inheritor

First of all, the leader, the Warqueen Vhizna - who I named after a quick glance at a Darkoath name generator I found online! A quick glance at the available character creation options helped me figure out how best to represent her on the table - a Human Zealot.

In Five Leagues, Humans get a bonus to Speech tests, and are the only origin capable of attaining more than one point of Will - an in-game resource that can be spent during battles for various one-off bonuses and effects. Perfect to represent a "main character". Meanwhile, the Zealot background (only available to Humans) makes it likely for a character to start with extra Luck, as if they are favored by some Dark Power...

Each background has a series of D20 tables you can roll on to to determine the character's Capabilities, Mentality, Possessions, and Training. I took my D20, made some rolls, and got the following results:

  • Agility Increase
  • +1XP
  • Basic Weapon
  • 1 Skill (Leadership)

From these results, we can see Vizhna is more focused on dexterity and mobility than raw power. She also has some experience, implying she has been prepared for this adventure - trained by a parent, an elder of the tribe or current leader perhaps? She brings a fairly basic weapon with her to add to the stash, but clearly knows how to command warriors and convince them to join her cause - maybe she was a unit leader during previous raids, and now undertakes a quest to prove herself to the tribe that she can command a raid herself?

Vizhna. (Credit - Hydriatus)

As she is my "main character", she is designated the Avatar and gains a free point of Luck and Will, before rolling on the Avatar History table and getting the "Old Map" result. This adds a Hidden Delve (dungeon) to our eventual region map - and just like that, we have a storyline and campaign goal! Our hero will have to decipher the old map, locate the dungeon, and plunder it for whatever treasures lie within!

Vizhna is a warrior of the Blood Claw tribe, who has been on her share of raids. Now however, the time has come for her to take her first steps on the Path to Glory. During her last raid, she uncovered a curious map written in a foreign scrawl. Swearing an Oath to discover the map's secrets, she sets out with a small group of companions to earn the attention of the Gods... 

Taulveg, the Smith

Next, my proxy Darkoath Savager Slaughterborn, Dendrel from Brand's Oathbound. Essentially a unit champion for the Savagers, I chose to make my Slaughterborn a smith instead, responsible for issuing out the finest weapons to proven warriors, and the unit being his bodyguards. As this campaign is a prequel of sorts to my army's lore, he would be accompanying Vizhma to collect equipment for the tribe, and maybe even find a worthy relic or two to inspire his own work.

Wanting some variety, I looked at my character creation options and found the Preen - foul-tempered avians with a bonus to Crafting tests. They also are forced to take the Townsfolk background, which is focused around wealth. Perfect for my purposes.

I rolled on the relevant tables, and ended up with the following:

  • Speed Increase
  • +1 XP
  • 3 Gold marks
  • 1 Skill (Battlewise)
Taulveg. (Credit - Hydriatus)

That struck me as a someone probably falling under the influence of Slaanesh, swift to get to grips with the foe (although Khorne would also work in terms of narrative)! Taulveg clearly has spent time in battle before, and maybe was part of Vizhna's unit before she took her Oath and embarked on this quest. He was must also have been a successful raider, amassing a small personal fortune, and knew how to read the tide of battle.

(Although with such a speed, I probably could have substituted a mounted model instead and themed it as a fellrider!)

Taulveg is one of the few smiths in the Blood Claws. While most of the tribe's equipment come from raids, they still require craftsmen to maintain and repair it equipment - and Taulveg has an eye for the material that makes him perfectly suited for such a role. Having served under Vizhna's command in past raids, he has come along on the adventure out of loyalty, and a chance to claim a share of the spoils the mysterious map is bound to lead Vizhna to...

Ikherd, the God-Speaker

The Mortal Realms are a very magical setting, so it seemed obvious to include some kind of spellcasting character - and a Savager unit does come with a God-speaker! Besides, a potential Warqueen on a quest is bound to draw the Gods' attention, so it simply made sense to have her accompanied by someone who could interpret signs from the Four.

This particular model is from the Warhammer: Underworlds Gnarlspirit Pack, a collection of very nice Ghur-themed Darkoath who only need a little conversion work to make suitable for the winter climes my army was themed around!

In the interest of exploring all the options available, I chose a Fey-Blood origin for the mystic, as it comes with a Foresight ability and an aura preventing opponents from gaining bonuses against the character. Ideal to represent the foreboding aura of someone who dabbles in Chaos magic!

Ikherd. (Credit- Hydriatus)

My rolls on the Mystic tables gave me the following:

  • Casting Increase
  • +1 XP
  • Mystic Item (2 Congealed Strands)
  • Alchemy Skill

Clearly, this is someone who hasn't been on as many raids as the others, but rather stayed back in the village making poultices and salves. However, clearly they are favored by Tzeentch if they find casting spells easier than others.

Speaking of spells, I rolled for the 3 starting spells and got:

  • Distract
  • Premonition
  • Forsee

Then for my 2 choices, I selected

  • Heal
  • Guidance

Ikherd clearly is a seer of some kind!

Part of the coterie that attends the tribe's shaman, Ikherd was a lowly assistant entrusted with the medical care of the wounded after each raid. After he proved capable of pre-empting shortages in supplies, the tribe's shaman recognized the man's aptitude for magic and taught him how to commune with spirits. His insight and foretelling has become quite respected within the Blood Claws, so none challenged or questioned him when he declared his intent to accompany Vizhna to see her Oath carried out. 

Drifa, the Unbound

For my final character, I chose to make another melee-focused one as the Savager unit had one more non-basic model - the Wrathtouched. I used another proxy from the  Gnarlspirit Pack. It made a nice looking veteran monster hunter, fitting the aesthetic of my totally-not-Norscans.

Drifa. (Credit - Hydriatus)

This time, I chose a Duskling origin, which grants the Counter Attack ability against undead, a reroll to their attack on the charge, and a distrust of magic - so someone definitively choosing Khorne as their patron. Maybe a survivor of the Necroquake, or a veteran of battles against the Ossiarch Bonereapers who have been settling the continent of Bjarl.

The only background available to Dusklings is outsider, which focuses on self-sufficient survival. My rolls on the subtables this time gave me the following:

  • Toughness Increase
  • +1 XP
  • Fine Basic Weapon
  • 2 skills - Speech, Expertise

So a real veteran compared to everyone else in the warband, harder to wound, bearing a finely honed axe, and recognized for her skills at carousing and avoiding natural hazards.

Hailing from a tribe that was wiped out by the undead, Drifa bears an ever-burning hatred of those who serve Nagash. Though she survived the attack that saw her tribe annihilated and made her way west, upon reaching the territory of the Blood Claws she was treated as an outsider. Denied an opportunity to raid, she has joined Vizhna on her journey in exchange for a promise to be taken along on future raids should the Oath-quest be successful. 

Followers

In addition to the 4 heroes, a warband has 2 followers - minor supporting characters who may through their deeds one day become heroes of note themselves. These have a basic statline, and count as having the human origin, but receive no stat improvements. Rolling on the Follower table grants me the following personalities:

  • Studious Apprentice
  • Brave Villager

These made me think of two tribe members, probably unblooded who haven't even been on their first raid yet.

Finally, we have the warband's stash - their equipment and collected treasure.

  • 6 Gold (can be spent in settlements on equipment or further followers)
  • 2 Silvertree Leaf (used to speed-up post-battle healing)

The World

With our warband done, it was time to generate the world - or at least the setting of our narrative campaign.As I mentioned earlier, my Age of Sigmar Slaves to Darkness army is based on the continent of Bjarl, in the realm of Ghur - and I am using primarily the Old World Chaos Marauders as my models due to preferring their sculpts over the Darkoath for my rank-and-file winter warrior aesthetic.

A quick check on Lexicanum gave me a map of Bjarl to use as a basis - but as Bjarl is a whole continent, I was somewhat spoilt for choice! I decided to come back to this stage after I had more an idea of what would be required on the map itself, and proceeded through the next steps of setting up a campaign for Five Leagues.

Ghurish Heartlands. (Credit - Games Workshop)

While any options can be chosen to make the kind of campaign you want to play, I chose to randomly generate all my options and see what I could make out of them. The first roll I made was to determine who settled the region I would be exploring was - and rolled low, so there would be only two settlements, a Village (with a market town specialization) and a town (with the Manor specialization).

Well, the number is fitting considering Bjarl is often described as a brutal place, mostly inhabited by nomadic Chaos and Ogor tribes. Still, a village established enough to be recognized a fledgling trade hub in Ghur... well, to me, that seemed to indicate a port town, somewhere the nomads could gather and trade every couple of seasons - a place where harvested beast parts would be exchanged for rare worked metal tools and weapons.

As for a community gathered around the manor of a local noble, which has the mechanical effect of allowing you to repair 2 items rather than one per visit... well, that sounded like a place of exiles, gathered around a disgraced noble who was forced south. Probably the ruler of the small port village as well!

Next, I determined the threats facing the region. Again, I chose to randomly determine these rather than choose. My results were:

  • The Whispers from Beyond (undead and cultists)
  • The Ice-heart Court (tree-folk and fey)
  • The Faceless Kingdom (disciplined military forces)

...I don't know about you, but to me that sounds like:

  • Anything from Grand Alliance: Death
  • Sylvaneth
  • Cities of Sigmar / Stormcast Eternals
A soldier from Coldfort pays their respects to a fallen comrade. (Credit - Hydriatus)

From there, I quickly browsed through Lexicanum again and found the two Bonereaper settlements in eastern Bjarl, and a Sigmarite stronghold in the north-west Bjarl known as Coldfort. And I had already generated an entire town of people gathered around the leadership of an exiled noble, it made sense to be closer to Coldfort. That meant the north-west of Bjarl. Glancing at a map of the Ghurish Heartlands, I found the place for my campaign - the Bloodwhiten Fjords...

...and that's all we have space for in this article! Join me next time, when the Campaign gets going and I provide some examples of play!
