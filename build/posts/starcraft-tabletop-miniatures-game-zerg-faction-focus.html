StarCraft Tabletop Miniatures Game Zerg Faction Focus

May 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Zerg are a collection of species of aliens which accomplish everything through Evolution™. Blessed with Purity of Essence by the ancient Xel’naga, the Zerg assimilate other species’ essence to create a variety of Zerg strains and bioforms to accomplish their goals. Said goals include obtaining more essence and expanding relentlessly throughout the Koprulu sector and elsewhere, so you could argue they’re basically another metaphor for late-stage capitalism (Just substitute capital for essence).

Starcraft Tabletop Miniatures Game

How They Play

Zerg units tend to be really really good at doing one thing in particular and absolutely awful at doing anything else. They’re in possession of a powerful toolbox filled with very specific tools that are deployed at need.

Mechanics

Creep

Creep is a mechanic unique to the Zerg. It’s a superorganism that the Zerg spread wherever they go. It’s how the Zerg bind their colonies together, and its psionically-active nature means that the Swarm can empower its lifeforms more effectively on creep.

In game, that translates to the status On Creep, which is granted when any model of yours is within 6 inches of a Creep Tumour or an Omega Worm. On Creep does little by itself, but turns on or strengthens a bunch of abilities of various Zerg units.

Burrow

Burrow is another Zerg specific mechanic. Broadly speaking, what it does is grant Hidden. However, there are a few other effects. The primary benefits are that Burrowed units count as size 0, meaning even size 1 terrain will block vision of you. Additionally, Burrowed units can make an Evade roll against any attack, even if your opponent has removed Hidden from you. They’re also Hidden, so unless your opponent removes that effect, you’re also untargetable by shooting from outside of 4” and immune to Impact.

However, Burrowed units are Supply 0 for Disengage checks, cannot control or contest objectives, cannot make any attacks, do not block movement (though they still have an engagement range), can’t charge, and any move (unless you have a special ability that states otherwise) immediately breaks Burrow. This means that in combat, you can’t even make melee attacks unless you first Close Ranks, which removes the Burrow status.

All this means is that Burrowed is completely defensive and doesn’t even let you properly steal objectives. What it does is let you be sneaky and force your opponents to either use resources (to remove Hidden) or move units near you, allow you to counter-attack with additional units or by unburrowing in other turns (explained later).

Burrow Ambush

A common upgrade among Zerg units, Burrow Ambush lets you Deploy a unit 18” from your deployment line (including from an Omega Worm!). Since Zerg lack movement tricks like Place(X) and double moves from Zealot Legs or Barracks moves, this is how you get units up the board quickly.

Devastating Charge

Strictly speaking, not unique to Zerg. It is, however, far more ubiquitous among Zerg units than in Protoss or Terran. Even the humble Zergling does IMPACT damage, and the Zerg faction has more access to ways to buff Devastating Charges.

Cards

Faction Cards

Faction cards generate 1 Biomass. They also require you to take a Creep type, which determines what buffs your army gets for being On Creep.

Kerrigan’s Swarm - Faction

The legions of the Queen of Blades are her tools, driven forward by her lust for vengeance and leashed to her psionic might. 

In game, Kerrigan’s Swarm gives you 1 Hero Slot, 2 Elite Slots, and 3 Core Slots. You get to summon Kerrigan’s Omega Worm to get access to the Nydus network, plus an any-phase buff for +1 movement and Precision (1) on your first attack. The buffs last the entire turn, so that’s +1 movement, +1 charge, and one automatic hit on either shooting or fighting for a unit of your choice. Pretty solid.

Kerrigan’s Swarm also gets access to Malignant Creep and Kerrigan Swarm Raptors, which are more damage-oriented. An excellent pick for someone who wants to seize victory by hammering their opponent with overwhelming damage.

Zerg Swarm - Faction

The unified Zerg swarm, (presumably) under the command of the Overmind. The “default” pick for Zerg.

In game, the Zerg Swarm gives you 1 Elite Slot, 3 Core Slots, and 1 Support Slot. You get a defensive reaction for +1 to Evade (For Zergings, that’s a whopping 3+ Evade), as well as a Movement Phase ability to burrow any unit that’s unengaged. The burrow is an out of sequence burrow, which means that you can move a unit up, have your opponent react to that movement to shoot them, then burrow that unit to hide from shooting. This works especially well if you can bait out Detection elsewhere first, letting you reveal a Nasty Surprise.

Zerg Queen (looking mighty fine)

Creep Types

As the Zerg, you’re obligated to pick one type of creep. Creep is an aura generated by Creep Tumours, which are tokens placed on the battlefield with Stay In Place and Displacement. Being within 6” of a Creep Tumour grants ON CREEP. Enemy units ending a mode within 1” of a Tumour removes it after they finish moving, which means Creep Tumours can (slightly) block enemy model placement. Displacement means they can’t block the unit, since the Leading Model can just shunt the Tumour(s) to the side, but it can make their model placement awkward afterwards.

Creep cards DO NOT generate Biomass.

Accelerating Creep (Unique) - Creep

Simple but powerful. If you choose this, your units ON CREEP get +1 to their Speed. This affects charges as well. In tabletop games, movement is king, so this is an excellent default option.

Malignant Creep (Unique) - Creep

For when damage is more important than speed. If you choose this type of creep, your units ON CREEP get +1 to their Devastating Charge rolls. While this isn’t huge on some units that already do IMPACT damage on a 3+, that’s a whopping 50% buff for Zergling variants.

Zerg Roaches

Cards (1 BM)

All cards in this category generate one Biomass.

Evolution Chamber

1 Core Slot. Tough (1) in any phase, or +1 to their IMPACT rolls in the Assault Phase. Tough boosts your durability, which is always great. IMPACT damage is great; if you charge before your target shoots, you can reduce incoming damage. The more damage you do, the less shoots at you.

Hatchery

1 Support Slot. In the movement phase, you can use this to burrow your active ground unit if unengaged (and doesn’t take an action, so disengage + burrow is an option), or drop a Creep Tumour within 6” of another Creep Tumour or your entry edge.

Hydralisk Den

2 Elite Slots. All the better for taking more Hydralisks. Gives one unit Precision (1) in shooting. Not bad, but you’re mostly taking this for the slots.

Lair (Unique)

1 Elite Slot and 1 Core Slot. The advanced version of a Hatchery with the same creep tumour ability. Instead of a burrow, this lets you give Instant to a unit’s Close Combat Weapons. Currently this is in Any Phase, but that’s probably an error. Just be sure not to use this on a shooting attack by mistake!

Overlord (Unique)

1 Hero Slot, 1 Elite Slot, 1 Core Slot. Your method of taking Kerrigan in Zerg Swarm. Has a similar ability to the Hatchery and Lair with Creep Tumours, but works in the Assault Phase for a potential surprise (or a response to something unexpected after you Pass). You also get access to a signaled end-of-round Deploy. You get to drop a token anywhere on the board 10” away from any Enemy model, and then after scoring you put a unit’s Lead model base to base with your token and deploy them normally. Hold some Supply and drop Roaches or a big Zergling blob early and reinforce them with Burrow Ambush, or maybe in a position to punish a response to your Omega Worm.

Overseer (Unique)

1 Support Slot. In the Movement Phase lets you remove Hidden from enemy units within 6” of a token you put down, or used to give a unit a better charge reroll (2d6 drop lowest). Notably, the added D6 to charge is after the roll, so you can see the result of your first die before deciding on using this. This is also how Zerg removes Hidden (other than someone walking next to an Omega Worm the turn after it’s deployed) so taking an Overseer is borderline mandatory.

Roach Warren

1 Core Slot. Lets you unburrow a unit in the Assault Phase. Useful for unburrowing Zerglings or Roaches to charge or shoot after an opponent drops an Observer/Overseer/Orbital Command token.

Spawning Pool

1 Core Slot. Precision (2) in melee. Excellent on Zerglings or Kerrigan, since they hit on 4s.

Cards (2 BM)

All cards in this category generate two Biomass.

Spawning Pool (Six Pool) (Unique)

2 Core Slots. Like its cousin, Precision (2) in melee. However, you also get to Deploy one Zergling unit off any Table Edge, so long as they’re out of 10” of an Enemy model. Note that this is the basic Zergling, so no Raptors or Swarmlings. Unfortunately, this means you land a charge on a 6+, not great odds. Accelerating Creep + Kerrigan’s Swarm gets you a 50% chance to stick the charge. Add an Overseer for a mighty 75% chance to land a charge (and half the time, you don’t even need to add the Overseer!). It’s a bit of a commitment, but creates a threat opponents can’t ignore.

Compared to two Spawning Pools, you lose flexibility in Biomass use, a second use of Precision (2), but gain an additional ability. At the time of writing, it’s also cheaper than two Spawning Pools.


Credit: SecretlyPaul

Units

Heroes

Kerrigan

The Queen of Blades; the personification of the Swarm. Once a Terran Ghost and a Psionic so powerful they had to rewrite the scales for her, she was betrayed by Arcturus Mengsk and assimilated by the Swarm to become its avatar. Compared to the other starting heroes, she’s squishier than Artanis and much shorter range than Raynor, but comes with a kit that rewards skilled planning. She has Speed 7, with 5+ Armour and 6+ Evade and 9 HP. As with all Heroes so far, he’s 1 Supply, but is treated as 2 for Objectives and Disengages.

In the Movement Phase, she has two active abilities that cost 1 Biomass each. The first Activates an enemy unit within 12”, preventing them from moving or using abilities that target an Active unit. The second allows her to tune her defences to a specific enemy unit within 18”, granting her Evade rolls at +2 to all attacks made by that unit. This functionally lets her pick a unit and ignore almost 60% of their damage output - and that’s even before you use any reactions!

In the Assault Phase, she has an 8” ranged attack with 5 shots, hitting on 3s with D3 Surge against both Light and Armoured targets for 1 damage each. Not the worst, but certainly not the best. She also has Devastating Charge (4) on 4+s, which again is perfectly fine. What makes her stand out is Leaping Strike, letting her Place (6) if she’s unengaged. This lets her Shoot before getting into combat, or land into combat from over 13” away (and from places she can’t be shot, too!).

In Combat, she gets 6 attacks that hit on 4s for 2 damage each. This has Critical Hit (2), meaning she hammers any target (including Heroes) for 4 damage automatically as long as she lands two hits. With Spawning Pool active, she’s got decent odds of popping any Hero in a single swing if they don’t have any defensive buffs up.

Kerrigan’s a complex piece to play, since so much of her kit is dedicated to dealing with one thing at a time. However, she’s an unmatched control piece with the ability to potentially completely shut down a unit for an entire turn.

Credit: SecretlyPaul

Supports

Queen

Utility Queen! Barely fast enough to walk in off a board edge, these Armoured chonkers cost 1 supply and scoot along with a 4” move, 4+ Armour, no Evade, and 9 HP each.

Starting out, Queens can do the following in any phase: cleanse debuffs within 4” when they’re applied, block 2 incoming damage on any friendly unit (including Structures!) as a reaction, or spawn a Creep Tumour that’s in base-to-base contact with herself. While all of this is pretty expensive, each Queen can discount the use of one ability by one once per round as long as there are 7 friendly models within 6” of herself. Since she’s friendly to herself, that means 6 other models within 6”. Functionally, this means Zerglings, since all our other units come in 1s, 2s, 3s, and 4s.

In shooting, she’s got a pretty alright loadout. 4 shots hitting on 4s with Surge D3 against Light at 2 damage each means she kills a pair of Marines on average (though at only 6”, that’s tough to reach). Against Flying units, she has the same attack but with twice the range (12”) and Surge against both Light and Armoured. On the charge, she gets IMPACT (4) 3+, averaging 3 hits. In melee, she’s a pretty pitiful 3 attacks hitting on 4s at 1 damage each, so try and make sure she’s the one charging since her charge will do far more damage than her melee.

Queens have two upgrade options, one of which is basically mandatory. The first gives her +2 speed while On Creep. A 50% movement buff on the unit spreading creep while on creep seems like a no-brainer. The other one is a movement phase active ability that gives a unit within 6” (LoS not required) +1 Supply for Objectives or Controlling and Contesting Mission Markers. Useful, but its relevance depends on your overall strategy. Objective control only matters if your opponents aren’t dead, after all!

Credit: SecretlyPaul

Core

Zergling - Zergling Variant

Ling Rush. 6Pool. Speedlings. Zerglings are iconic units of the Zerg, and for good reason. They come in 12s for 1 Supply or 18 for 2 Supply. They’re fast with 4/8 move, 6+ Armour and 4+ Evade, and 1 HP each. They’re your core skirmish unit, meant to hammer enemy lines quickly and bog them down until your heavy hitters arrive (though they’re pretty good at hitting things themselves).

A coherency of 4” means that they have the same functional movement as a 5” move unit (though a bit less charge speed). They can also get 2D6 drop lowest on the charge, though this needs to be used before you roll, unlike the Overseer. After sticking the charge, they resolve IMPACT (1) on 5+. IMPACT (1) doesn’t sound like much until you roll 18 dice with Malignant Creep and Evolution Chamber support to average 12 IMPACT hits.

In melee, they get 2 attacks each, hitting on 4s with D3 Surge against Light for 1 damage each. Good for cleaning out squishy light units, not so great into anything else.

Zerglings can also be upgraded with Burrow Ambush (see above), Precision (2), and/or better claws that Surge against both Armoured and Light.

Zerglings do a little bit of everything for you. They let your Queens drop Creep Tumours for free early, trade efficiently with skirmish units, and can be upgraded to deal with more heavily armoured enemies. On the flip side, they’re easily choke-pointed and die in droves.

Swarming - Zergling Variant

A specialised Zergling variant. They come in 18s for 1 supply, and have the same stats as Zerglings barring a 5+ Evade. They have the same coherency rule, same charge bonus rule, and same IMPACT damage. They’re even worse in melee, hitting on 5s with no Surge, and the only upgrade they get access to is Burrow Ambush.

What sets them apart is their ability to Respawn models: For one biomass, you get 2 Swarmlings back, or 3 if you’re ON CREEP. Respawned models come back in base-to-base contact with other models, so you’re getting at most about 1” of extra movement, but that can be enough to flip a point. Additionally, while their melee is anemic, their charge is just as good as normal Zerglings, meaning a good charge from Swarmlings can crush through a Marine unit just as well as Zerglings.

Roach - Roach Variant

The reason everyone built Missile Turrets back in the day. These are your general purpose Roaches; their variants shift specialties. They come in 3s for 1 Supply. Movement 4/7 move, 3+ Armour and 5+ Evade, and 4 HP each.

Roaches are the only unit type with an innate ability to burrow. As long as they’re unengaged, any of them can burrow/unburrow for 2 BM. Pricey compared to equivalent abilities on cards, but understandable since it’s in any phase and no list-building opportunity cost. It’s important that they can do this innately since they also Heal (2) if they’re Burrowed at the start of their activation. Best case scenario? They kill someone in melee, spend 2 to burrow, and then heal up on your next turn without having sacrificed any actions for it.

They have the near ubiquitous Devastating Charge ability for IMPACT (2) 4+, with a pretty mediocre 8” shooting attack at 3 shots hitting on 3s for 1 damage without surge. In melee they’re not much better, getting 2 swings each hitting on 4s for 1 damage without Surge.

Roaches can get a pretty nifty set of upgrades for low cost. As the premiere burrow unit, they get Burrow Ambush. Tunneling Claws lets you Move and Run while Burrowed without losing Burrowed, and lets you phase through other units’ bases. They can get a 1 BM upgrade to get +1 Speed or +2 if ON CREEP. Finally, they can upgrade their spit with Hydriodic Bile to get Surge D3+1 against Light armour.

Between being Armoured and a 3+ save, Roaches are incredibly tanky to shooting that isn’t specialised at taking them out. While Surges can shut their best defences down, having a 3+ base means Marines and Zealots struggle to take them out while their spit mows the light targets down.

Corpsers and Roachlings - Roach Variant

Baby’s first Summoner. They’ve got the generic Roach statline, with similar baseline abilities. In short, they’re slightly worse at shooting (2 shots instead of 3), slightly better at charging (IMPACT (3) instead of 2) and better in melee (3 attacks instead of 2, with Surge D3+1 against Light). They have the same suite of upgrades, too. So far, this just looks like a slightly more melee oriented Roach, undeserving of the massive cost increase (70 Minerals at time of writing!). What sets Corpsers apart is the ability to SUMMON Roachlings once per game for 2 Biomass.

Roachlings cost 0 supply, can’t be burrowed, can’t shoot, die to a stiff breeze, and have 2 attacks each on 4s for 1 damage with no Surge. On the surface, they’re pretty crap since they can’t run around and contest objectives or do damage. What they do get you is an amazing distraction or screening piece. First off, when summoned, a Leading Roachling is set up base-to-base with a Corpser. They’re immediately Activated, so no additional movement for them. They can, however, still charge during the Assault phase. If their parent Corpser unit is around, the Roachlings must activate once the Corpser unit has finished their activation, effectively giving you a double-activation. 

Roachlings are effective for a select few tasks, but boy are they effective at what they do. You can summon them to charge Marauders/Stalkers after their movement to tie them down; you can give them 1 Supply with an upgraded Queen to force an opponent to spend time dealing with them, soaking a charge or an entire unit’s shooting. You can run them into a chokepoint to bodyblock opponents. In short, they’re terrible and useless, which makes them fantastic and useful since you don’t need to care about their survival, only what they can do to make your opponent’s life harder. Ask not what the Swarm can do for Roachlings - ask what your Roachlings can do for the Swarm!

Vile - Roach Variant

“What if Roach but more gun?” must have been how Viles were conceived. They’ve got the generic Roach statline, with similar baseline abilities.  They’re slightly better at shooting (4 shots instead of 3) and slightly worse at charging (IMPACT (1) instead of 2). Again, they have the same upgrade suite, so if ever you were to upgrade a Roach variant with Hydriodic Bile, these are your best option.

Their main gimmick other than better shooting is a reaction to Shooting. If an enemy unit declares a Shooting attack within 8” of a Vile, you can spend 1 BM to DEBUFF that unit with -1 to hit. Statistically especially useful against Stalkers, Goliaths, and Hydralisks.

If you’re unsure of which Roach variant to take, Viles are probably your best default pick since they’re only slightly more expensive than a base Roach with better bang for your buck in terms of upgrades.

Credit: SecretlyPaul

Elites

Hydralisk

So iconic they lived on the Brood War main menu. Hydralisks come in 2s for 2 Supply and 4s for 3 Supply. They’ve got movement 4/8, 5+ Armour and 5+ Evade, and 4 HP each. Their movement is supplemented with 4” coherency, though honestly they need it because of how massive their bases are.

Their primary role is shooting, and boy do they shoot. They shoot at 12” at both Ground and Flying, 3 attacks each hitting on 3s, with Surge D3+1 against both Light and Armoured targets for a whopping 2 damage per shot. They also come with a reaction which lets them sit comfortably out of danger; if another friendly unit within 10” is shot they can make a move directly towards the attacker, which might be enough to pull them into range for immediate retaliation (or to wipe out a different squad). In melee they’re anemic, getting 2 swings each hitting on 4s for 1 damage without Surge.

Hydralisks have four possible upgrades, all of which are Passive. They can, of course, Burrow Ambush. They can buy Long Range (16”) for their ranged attack, which makes them stronger as turrets. Defensively, they can gain TOUGH (1) for the first armour roll of each Activation, meaning they automatically pass one armour save per unit attacking them. Additionally, Lurking grants you Evade against the first ranged attack targeting them each round, with a +1 buff if they’re ON CREEP as long as they’re Stationary (meaning as long as they didn’t Move or Lunge this turn). Effectively, they can cut incoming damage in half, meaning they’re less vulnerable if you lost the First Player token.

The expected play pattern for Hydralisks seems to be to Burrow Ambush them forward (or deploy them off an Overlord) before having them sit semi-stationary (mostly moving using Lunge once their main threats are dealt with) as a turret. If there’s High Ground for them to sit on, even better.

Raptor - Zergling Variant

What if Zergling, but faster? Raptors have almost the same statline as Zerglings, gaining +1 on their movement for 5/9 Speed. They share the 4” coherency, their melee profile, charge buff, Devastating Charge, and upgrade suite.

They have three advantages over regular Zerglings: The first is the increased speed. The second is that they can ignore IMPASSABLE TERRAIN of size 4 or less as well as change Elevation freely. Finally, they can spend 1 BM to boost their IMPACT HITS by 1, increasing IMPACT efficiency by 50%. All in all: just better Zerglings.

Kerrigan Swarm Raptor - Zergling Variant

The Kraptor. Taking the idea of the Raptor and elevating it even higher. Kerrigan Swarm Raptors (or Kraptors for short) are a UNIQUE unit that only Kerrigan’s Swarm can take. They’re what happens when you stuff the power of two Raptors into a single body and let them loose. They’ve got the Raptor statline but with a 5+ Armour and 2 HP each, making them substantially more durable. Despite coming in 6s, they still have a 4” coherency, making them exceptionally fast.

They share the Raptor’s ability to ignore terrain and to spend 1 BM for +1 on IMPACT HITS. Unlike standard Zerglings and Raptors, Kraptors get IMPACT (2) 5+, meaning despite having only 6 models they still hit like 12. Additionally, instead of 2D6 pick the highest, their Charge buff is a flat +2 to the charge. This means they can guarantee charges from over 8” away (the most common attack range in the game) and reliably hit charges of 11+.

In melee, they unfortunately have only 2 attacks each. On the other hand, their attacks hit on 3s, are INSTANT, with Surge D6 against both Light and Armoured. Their main downside is not being able to Burrow Ambush, but since you can only take them in Kerrigan’s Swarm, they can just pop out of an Omega Worm anyway.


Credit: SecretlyPaul

Other

Omega Worm

Kerrigan’s special ride through the Nydus Network. This unit can only be summoned through the Kerrigan’s Swarm card and cannot be deployed by regular means. Once summoned, the Omega Worm doesn’t move. It has a 5+ Armour with 10 HP.

As a Structure, your Omega Worm can’t contest objectives, can’t activate, and can’t perform actions. You also can’t use abilities on it unless the ability specifies it works on Structures. At the moment, this means your only way to protect it is using a Queen’s Transfusion.

On the turn it’s placed, the Omega Worm has no abilities other than Structure while it sets up.

Afterwards, it does three things. It provides detection in 6”, acting as a deterrent to Burrowing units and Stalkers. It also spreads a 6” bubble of Creep. Finally, two Supply worth of units per turn can treat it as an Entry Edge, and if any leading model ends a move touching it, they can hop back into reserves.

Combos

Burrow Tricks

Zerg Swarm: Allows you to hide in response to opponent’s movements or after they’ve already dropped Detection somewhere else.

Roaches & Variants: Burrow, tunnel forward. In a subsequent turn, unburrow in Assault Phase and shoot/charge. This is also sort of replicable by any other unit using…

Zerg Swarm/Hatchery + Roach Warren: Using either Zerg Swarm or a Hatchery, you can burrow a unit. Even if detection is dropped on them, burrowed units always benefit from Evade. In your Assault Phase, use your Roach Warren for a Nasty Surprise and shoot them/charge them.

Zerg Swarm + Hydralisk Lurking

Pretty self explanatory. Zerg Swarm gives you +1 Evade, pushing Hydralisks to a staggering 3+ Evade.

Absent Father Manoeuvre

Burrow Ambush some Corpsers forward, summon an Omega Worm within 4” of one model. Next turn, summon some Roachlings and jump the Corpsers into the Omega Worm so the Roachlings can now activate independently. What does this accomplish? Not much, it just has a hilarious name.

Kerrigan Raptor Yeet

Drop your Omega Worm somewhere that’s awkward for units to shoot it. Next turn, pop some Kerrigan Raptors out on Accelerating Creep with Kerrigan’s Swarm active and use Leap for a horrific 9+D6 charge that completely ignores elevation and terrain.

And that's a wrap for Zerg and their multitude of gribbly guys. Overall, Zerg are a hyper specialized toolbox that'll reward players for really understanding what their army can do well and the weaknesses of their opponent's lists. We expect to see Zerg do just fine when stacked against other factions, especially if the player piloting them knows their in's and out's.
