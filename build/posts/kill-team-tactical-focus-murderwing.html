[Kill Team] Tactical Focus: Murderwing

September 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In this series of Kill Team articles we take a deep dive into one of the available teams, analyzing each team's strengths and weaknesses, how to set up important plays, and ultimately what to consider to pilot the team to victory. Note that this article assumes you already have knowledge of the basic rules for each team, and will reference abilities and ploys from that team throughout. If you want to learn more about the rules for each team you can find the most up to date version of the rules on the Warhammer Community Website here. In this article, we’re covering Murderwing.

From the heights of darkness descend the merciless killers of the Murderwing, Kill Team’s premiere jump pack warriors. Combining the flexibility and the combat prowess of Astartes with the movement flexibility provided by their jump packs, Murderwing is a team that excels at leaping from staging area to staging area, striking their prey from the most opportune angle. Their roster of operatives and their ploys are some of the best in the game, such as the Chaos Lord and Wings of Darkness, giving you as a Murderwing player plenty of options for both playing the mission and, well, murdering when the time is right. Today, we will go over some of the most important tactics to know and options to consider with these skyborne menaces, to help you master Murderwing.

Basic Overview

Right off the bat, Murderwing, like all of the other Space Marine teams in the game, rock some of the best defensive and offensive capabilities available thanks to their stat lines and Astartes special rule. Defensively, Murderwing operatives are 14 wounds base (15 on the Chaos Lord) with a 3+ armor save, making them difficult to deal with with any shooting without the piercing special rule and giving you plenty of wiggle room to survive some chip damage. Offensively, Murderwing operatives have 3 APL and can shoot or fight twice in the same activation. The combat weapons of Murderwing (outside the Skysear) are all 5 attacks with at least a 4 / 5 damage profile, which is a real workhorse, and though the shoot twice is restricted only to bolt weapons, a bolt pistol shooting twice can do some good damage just from sheer weight of dice. 

This is all before even considering the team’s marquee ability, Boost. Boost gives Murderwing some of the best mobility options in the game, letting them ignore terrain, operatives, or vertical distance when making moves with a boat provided you aren’t under vantage terrain while you do so. While most other teams have to carefully consider how to get around things like razorwire or tall Volkus ruins, Murderwing operatives will zip right past them, giving you some excellent opportunities to set up non-reciprocal charges or just appear out of nowhere to take out an enemy operative who wandered too close.

Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

All together, the Murderwing package encourages you to play like a bird of prey, which makes sense for a team full of Chaos Raptors. Murderwing has very few tools to stick around in a brawl, unlike other Astartes teams, and prolonged combats tend to go badly even with how deadly your operatives are. Playing Murderwing well requires you to focus on making sure you're bringing the right tools for the job, and effectively staging your threats so that you can strike at the perfect moment without leaving yourself exposed. 

Team Strengths and Weaknesses

The biggest strength this team has is easily Boost, especially when playing on an open killzone like Volkus. Even without the extra 2” of movement from charges, Boost provides excellent mobility options for Murderwing, and when paired with the ploy Wings of Darkness they become the fastest team in the game, being able to move 12” in one activation with a move plus dash, ignoring any piece of terrain or model that happens to be in the way. Plus, Boost makes it exceptionally easy to stage non-reciprocal charge threats when paired with things like vantage or equipment. Being able to stay behind equipment or on vantage safely while projecting threat is key to success with this team, and fortunately Boost makes it very easy to do.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

This mobility is paired well with both Murderwing’s offensive capabilities and excellent access to stun. While the team has plenty of beaters, the standout king of slicing and dicing is the Chaos Lord, especially with his relic lightning claws weapon option. While this loadout sacrifices any ranged output, it turns the Chaos Lord into a blender of death, spitting out 5 attacks at 4/6 damage with Ceaseless, Lethal 5+, and Rending. If you’re getting a Balanced reroll from predators above, the Chaos Lord averages around four crits every time they fight. Coupled with some chip damage from Clawed Armor and an extra damage boost from Boons of Damnation, the Chaos Lord is very capable of dealing with a 14 wound space marine in two swings. While the other operatives aren’t quite as dangerous as the Lord, the team still has multiple very dangerous combat weapons to choose from, making the team one of the most dangerous teams around should they get the charge.

Murderwing is also notable for its wide access to easy stun abilities from the Vox Scream equipment as well as from the Shrieker. In addition to this, both of these stuns can be used while engaged in combat, and as icing on the cake the Shrieker’s stun from Shriek is guaranteed to go off every time with some chip damage as a cherry on top, though there is the caveat that neither of these abilities can be used form conceal. You could even potentially take Stun Grenades on top of all of these options, giving the team just a silly amount of options when it comes to being able to reduce enemy APL.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

The biggest weakness of Murderwing is, surprisingly for a team of power armor clad transhuman warriors, its glass jaw. Murderwing works best when playing as the predator, but has very few good options should the team become the prey. While power armor and strong combat profiles should keep you reasonably safe, it's very common for a few bad dice rolls to quickly snowball into disaster for your operatives, especially since you cannot ignore injury outside of the standard Raptor operative nor can you heal. Murderwing players need to be careful about their positioning, as a poorly placed operative or badly timed charge can easily cost you a model.   

Standout Tactics

The first thing to really consider on the road to mastery with Murderwing is operative selection. The team is absolutely spoiled for choice when it comes to operative and loadout selection for any given matchup, which means making sure you’re taking the right tools for the job is all the more paramount. 

The only operative who’s mandatory is the Chaos Lord, and while there's a few cases where you may want to take him with a plasma pistol and power weapon, the sheer deadliness and reliability of the relic lightning claws is hard to beat. The Chaos Lord is also the tankiest operative too, since you can use Boons defensively to reduce incoming damage alongside Instill Fear to highly reduce enemy damage once he does commit to a fight. Since he is both so tanky and deadly, the Lord is best used later on in a game to seal the deal rather than a first wave fastball, as once he commits he’s such a dangerous and valuable operative that opponents will likely commit hard to removing him. His effectiveness is much improved when your opponent is running out of steam and just lacks the options to deal with the Chaos Lord, letting him run roughshod through enemy lines.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

For the rest of the lineup, you have five options from a long list of great operatives. The only dud here is the Raptor, since following the most recent Dataslate his ability to ignore movement modifiers while boosting isn’t nearly as important as it once was. There are a few go-to picks that are almost always going to go on your roster from the selection of available operatives, rounded out by a few flex spots. The operatives you’ll almost always take are the Champion (for CP generation and generally good all around damage output), the Skysear (for access to strong shooting with either the meltagun or plasma gun), the Shrieker (for reliable APL reduction, unless your opponent can ignore it), and the Warp Talon (for consistent combat output and threat projection with the Warp marker). The Champion and the Skysear have some equipment options to consider as well, adding even more flexibility. Generally, I always give the Champ a plasma pistol, and then go with a power fist for teams with 8 wound elf teams and a power weapon for all of the others. As for the choice between meltagun and plasma gun, it really depends on what the matchup calls for. Generally, the plasma gun’s longer range will do more to keep your opponent honest and is best against teams where you’re outactivated, and the meltagun is just an ultra-reliable elite killer and is the best bet into other power armor teams. With that said, be careful trying to get into a shootout with a dedicated shooting team. Even with a plasma gunner, you won’t win, and in these cases it may be worth considering dropping the Skysear for extra combat bodies.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

As for the remaining selections, the Curseclaw is the best of the bunch, despite having the least dangerous weapon (a chainsword equivalent with rending). The obvious standout ability is Snatch, especially since it can be both used from conceal and can combo with Wings of Darkness for some wild grabs from downtown. An underrated part of his kit though is his Frenzied Attack ability, since that lets him resolve an attack before he dies if killed in combat, making it tough to trade up into him. The Huntmaster is a good pick into elite teams, since he brings an extra power weapon to the table, and the Depredator’s 4+ to hit on his Great Chainaxe makes him pretty unreliable, he’s handy into horde teams with his Carving Blow and high damage profile on the axe. He also makes for a great operative to toss in first, since he can charge and still do damage with Carving Blow while not initiating a fight back in return. Combo this with a stun to keep an enemy from falling back (or falling back and shooting if they have 3 APL), your opponent may be forced to reroute their plans to deal with the axe-weilding maniac now loose in their line. 

Once these operatives are on the field, consider where best to place them in order to deny threats to your opponent while opening up threats for your team. Prior to the first dataslate, both Malicious Narcissism and the 8” charge threat from Boost made this trivially easy. Now that Narcissism doesn’t guarantee you last activation anymore and boost is capped at 6” though, this is trickier to do, but by no means impossible.

Credit: Warhammer Community

On Volkus, the best thing you can do to stage effectively is to stack up on the vantage and body-block your opponent from climbing up. With just a few operatives hanging out near the edge of a ruin, it's easy to make it so that your opponent can’t shoot you, nor can they fit their base anywhere for a successful charge, giving you all the time in the world to wait for the right moment to either strike out and take out an operative or claim an objective. On Tomb World and other killzones without vantage to hide on, this is much harder to do, but not impossible. You’ll need to carefully consider your equipment placement and “build the maze”, putting down items like barricades and razorwire to both deny your opponent from moving freely while giving you ample opportunity to boost in. Remember, Murderwing loves to fight, but doesn’t like to retaliate; make sure you do everything you can to decide the flow of combat. This often means that you will need to play patiently, playing more opportunistically rather than full send aggression.

Don’t forget that, in a pinch, you can also bravely and boldly run away with Long Forgotten Honor, but while it's an extremely strong ploy for getting out of dodge, it's important to be mindful of its limitations. First, you need to use a crit to use it, which means in most situations where you’re retaliating you’ll actually need two crits to use it since most opponents will just parry out your first hit if they know you’re going to fly away. Secondly, make sure that when you do fall back, to do so somewhere safe from further retaliation (preferably up onto some vantage). Successfully fleeing a fight you were going to lose only to die from a bolt pistol to the face from a Space Marine not only feels bad, but it's also a wasted use of a ploy. In short, be mindful of where you position your operatives if you think they’ll need to use Long Forgotten Honor to stay alive, and be aware that it isn’t guaranteed to work, so leaning too hard on it to bail you out of a sticky situation can lead to heartbreak.  

Tac Ops and Crit Ops

Credit: TheChirurgeon

For Approved Ops 2026, your three best options are Retrieve, Flank, and Dominate. Retrieve is the least exciting of the three options, especially since you’re more than likely scoring four on it rather than the full six, but it pairs really well into the team's opportunistic playstyle. With Retrieve, you can kick the scoring can down the road all the way until the end of the game before literally swooping in and scoring it, made all the easier by being so spoiled for choice on APL reduction. 

Flank is a bit more interesting, especially on Volkus, where stopping Murderwing from scoring flank can be a real hassle. With options to stall your activations from Malicious Narcissism and zip all the way across the Killzone with Wings of Darkness, you can wait until it's safe and then zip into a quadrant to score. The Curseclaw is a premier operative for scoring Flank thanks to Snatch, since he can fly over and grab an enemy operative to use them for a mobile tactical hug as shooting protection. Just be careful using Flank on close quarters battlefields, where Murderwing’s mobility is dampened, and make sure that by picking Flank, you aren’t just drip-feeding your opponent easy kills all day long. Consider taking Smoke Grenades and leaning into charge hugs to stay safe from shooting.

Finally, Dominate is a good option if you’re expecting a knock down, drag out fight, but be sure to consider Murderwings relative fragility. Because you don’t have great defensive options, many teams with access to Search and Destroy are probably going to pick Dominate into you, and since the new Dominate update you’ll drop two tokens for each Murderwing operative killed. If your opponent can take Dominate, assume they’re going to pick it into you until proven otherwise. If you select Dominate, don’t get greedy, and stick to taking safe kills that won’t leave you in a compromised position, or waiting until the end of the turning point three or four to make a big Dominate play and immediately cash in.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

The excellent mobility and APL reduction tools available to Murderwing give the team a generally rock solid Crit Op game. In many cases, you can fly in from complete safety to seize an objective right out from an opponent, either by utilizing a stun or just tearing them to shreds. Plus, the Warp Talon is an outstanding central objective bully with Slice the Veil. By placing the warp marker near or on that objective, you’re able to both project threat on the objective for scoring as well as threat to any enemy operative who wanders in to close, all from complete safety while the Warp Talon is off the Killzone. Plus, if the center objective becomes too hot, you can always deploy him in your drop zone to keep him safe and even help bolster a flank. 

Matchup Considerations

Kill Team Tomb World - credit Warhammer Community

Weirdly, the biggest matchup consideration for Murderwing isn’t even the team it’s facing, but rather the Killzone it’s facing them on, as boy howdy is there a noticeable power disparity for the team depending on if you’re playing on an open map with vantage, like Volkus, or a close quarters map like Tomb World, which neuters many of the team's best mobility tricks.

Vantage is by far and away the easiest way for Murderwing to stage effectively and set up threats that the opponent cannot deal with, alongside playing nicely with many of the team’s ploys. Without vantage or big terrain to hide behind like the Volkus ruins, boost’s overall utility plummets, and the team will need to work much harder to stage up properly. It’s not impossible though; you’ll just need to be far more considerate about taking equipment that can block off parts of the killzone while setting up equipment carefully to make effective launchpads for your operatives from safe positions. It can help to build layers of equipment as well on Close Quarters maps to really leverage the effectiveness of boost, especially since razorwire is a much longer than the other equipment options and is outstanding for cutting off parts of the battlefield.

Vespid. Credit: Rockfish

As far as the teams to watch out for, the scariest teams for Murderwing to face up against are the teams that have tools for easily dealing with elites. Since Murderwing lacks a strong suite of durability tools, strong piercing weapons will cut down an exposed operative really quickly, or injure them and drastically reduce their combat effectiveness. Teams like Kasrkin, Vespids, Hand of the Archon, and both Necron teams are particularly nasty to deal with, since a small mistake can ruin your day fast. Against these teams, slow down the game and lean hard into the opportunistic playstyle of the team. If you strike out, you’ll get melted. Be patient, and focus on scoring points by using APL tools to dominate crit op and safely score retrieve while playing keepaway from their shooting as much as possible. 

Piloting the Team to Victory

Credit: Sky Serpent

A team name like Murderwing gives off the impression that Murderwing’s number one goal is to go screaming in, jump packs blazing, and slice apart every poor soul that happens to be in your way. Sometimes, the team is exactly about that, and with operatives like the Chaos Lord you are certainly very good at slicing and dicing, but that isn’t the most effective mindset for playing this team. It’s fitting that a team full of Raptors work best when you play them like real world raptors, biding your time from on high until you strike decisively like a bird of prey. 

While mobility and combat deadliness can sometimes just bulldoze over other teams, especially since you can rely on the strength of being an Astartes with a deep wound pool, great armor, and excellent action economy, more often than not you’ll be most rewarded by exercising patience and restraint with Murderwing. Make a game plan that makes it very difficult for your opponent to play passively by staging up on parts of the map that they can’t quite reach you easily, and then leverage your ability to reliably stun your opponent or be pretty much exactly where you need to be with Boost to dominate the Crit Op and the Tac Op. Strike out when your opponent is forced to intervene to stop your scoring, focusing first on the operatives that can most threaten you with dangerous weapons before sending in the Lord and his pals to deal with whatever remains. Playing Murderwing well might feel like you’re losing most of the game while you stall for the right moment, and then all of the sudden seizing the game like a bolt from the black. It can be difficult to bring together, especially on close quarters maps, but when it does, you feel like a merciless predator worthy of the Murderwing.

Final Thoughts

Fast, exceptionally dangerous in a fight, and with plenty of tools to mess with your opponents action economy and ability to score, Murderwing are a team that plays with a deadly finesse style rather than the overwhelming hammer of most other elite teams. Play cagey and pick your engagements, stay in the shadows and focus on scoring until your opponent is forced to make plays they don’t want to make in order to deal with you, and then launch your attacks from safe staging areas. They may not be the meta terrors that they were on their release, but make no mistake that when piloted well, there's still plenty of terror to be had with this team.
