Games from the Crypt #24 - Return of the Vampire

October 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

It's time to go back into the Games Crypt once more this fine October for another deadly Halloween delight. And this time it's another freshly exhumed reprint coming to us courtesy Renegade Game Studios, who have a new edition of 2010's Dungeons & Dragons: Castle Ravenloft coming up in February 2027. I was a little surprised to see this one make a comeback not only because it was a fairly extensive production with tons of minis (crappy, but still), dungeon tiles, cards, and the fact that there several other games in this particular D&D product line that were quite a bit more compelling than this one was- regardless of the fact that the gothic horror setting of Ravenloft is my all-time favorite D&D realm.

But let's look back to 2010, when this game first hit. It made quite an impact at the time, and in retrospect I think it was mainly because at the time the only real and current competition on the market for an old school dungeon-crawl board game was Fantasy Fiight's Descent: Journeys in the Dark. But this was a D&D branded, borderline mass-market design that was reasonably priced, self-contained, and ultra simple despite being on the very much not ultra simple 4th edition D&D rule set. It had that X factor charm, that sense that this is a game that kids will buy and play with their friends in their parents' basements or around a kitchen table. More hardcore gamers decried its very basic, simple rules and whiff of a campaign structure and leveling mechanics. But the beer and pretzels crowd glommed right on to what this game was selling.

At the time, I was very much one of its defenders - I thought it captured all the most important elements of a D&D-themed dungeon romp. Hero classes, traps, treasure, monsters galore, and D20 based combat that was fun and quick. The monsters ran on a very basic system that described tactics in an if-than triaging scheme so no DM was required, which distinguished it from its most notable antecedent, HeroQuest and its contemporary Descent. The game was even worth playing solitaire, at a time when solo gaming was really kind of starting to emerge as a significant concern. It was pitched somewhere between Gygax's original Dungeon! board game and the more scenario-based play of subsequent dungeon crawlers. There was text to read to get you into the Ravenloft vibe and the miniature bats, werewolves, hags, and ghosts (despite being crappy as noted) gave it a "box of toys" feel. I'm sure many copies of this game were sold simply to jump-start DM miniature collections.

This was one of those games that everybody was playing for a minute back in 2010, but I found that it was actually maybe a bit too stripped down. The Ravenloft atmosphere wasn't particularly strong with generic dungeon tiles, even when going face to face with Strahd himself in an epic showdown. These days this sense of something missing would mean that an expansion would inevitably trundle onto the market to add things to the chassis. But not so with the line of SKUs that Castle Ravenloft sired. Instead, they made more games based on the core system.

And frankly speaking, all of them were better and the design team refined and iterated on the core mechanics effectively. They all added more narrative, better progression, and up to a point better miniatures (before they completely fell off a cliff in that regard). At one time I had like five or six of these massive titles in their massive boxes, with Wrath of Ashardalon remaining to my mind the standout, offering the best of what these games had to offer.

Looking over Renegade's announcement and online materials for the Castle Ravenloft reprint I have to say that it is somewhat disappointing that the upcoming release is really just kind of a visual assets update with a price tag that is literally twice what it was back in the day. Sure, even at $100 there's a lot in the box - but there's also some 16 years worth of similar games out now that offer more for less.

Regardless of my criticism, I still have a fondness for this game. It's still a solid, appealing dungeoncrawl that you can play with virtually anyone and complete a scenario in an hour. Kids tend to love it and get really into it, which is always a huge positive in my book. And it's always great to see more Ravenloft on shelves, especially at Halloween - except that it's really more of a Valentine's release this time, isn't it?
