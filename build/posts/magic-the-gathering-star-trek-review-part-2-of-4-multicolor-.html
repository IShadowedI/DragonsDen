Magic: The Gathering Star Trek Review, Part 2 of 4 – Multicolor & Colorless Cards

November 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Space, the final frontier. These are the voyages of the starship Enterprise...as well as around 34 other new Spaceraft. Our continuing mission: To explore in detail the ramifications of newly-printed cardboard; to speculate upon their effects on formats such as Commander, Standard, & Modern; to boldly joke about the 43 new Creature types printed in this set unlikely ever to be seen again! Yes, Star Trek has arrived to Magic, and with it a host of new cards sure to shake things up. Most impactful is the full realization of Spacecraft as an Artifact subtype, more than doubling the number thusly printed. Today we're covering the set's multicolor and colorless cards.

Star Trek officially releases to tabletop on November 13th, and on Magic: The Gathering Arena on November 10th.

Multicolor

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Borg Cube

Saffgor: This card is indeed strong, but has one clear caveat—its ability does not carry over if the Cube re-enters, is copied, etc. This is also a linked ability, meaning that if you give this Escape or Delve somehow, those cards cannot be Assimilated. What this card doesn't say keeps it from being truly potent, but its actual text is quite good. Just don't overthink this one, it has guardrails.

BPhillipYork:  This is honestly really disappointing, the Borg Cube took on the whole Federation fleet, this would lose to the Defiant, which it... did not. It really feels like it should get +1/+1 for each charge counter it has one it or something. The exile and then assimilate is nice, but for 7 mana it's not that impressive. Granted it's basically another Meteor Golem type effect, but it requires 2 colored mana.

FromTheShire: Especially with the colored mana cost you're not likely to be casting this for free but a decent reduction is reasonable. The ability is also solid, though it's not exactly quick.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The City on the Edge of Forever

Saffgor: I kind of wish this could sit in the Command Zone, or otherwise be more findable, because the ability to stop things from phasing in is rather unique. There's a ton of removal which becomes all the more permanent with that effect onboard, and the card itself is at worse some card draw and a temporary board clear.

BPhillipYork: This is kind of crazy, like if you can protect that admittedly 0/1 creature, it just locks your opponents boards out. And it's 5 mana, which is more than a basic wrath, but only 1 more. Potentially really strong asymmetric board wipe if you have a plan for it.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dal R'El, Augment Orphan

Saffgor: Gruul Lotus Cobra in the Command Zone with a mana outlet to draw the deck alongside a Drownyard Temple? Shoot, yeah this seems incredibly potent. It's likely only going to get better once we see the eventual X-Men set with Mutant support, so keep Dal in mind.

BPhillipYork: Well, good another Lotus Cobra. Landfall wasn't strong enough already. This is really great for, like, good decks. With fetch lands alone it's a massive boost early to build some momentum and if you are fetching more lands or ramping aggressively it gets really gross.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dathon, His Heart Open

Saffgor: Appearing in the sole episode of Star Trek I've actually seen, this guy is a very cool take on Temur. Sagas isn't something the identity has had a Commander for thus far (explicitly, anyway) and that activated ability being for any Enchantment means things like Gardenize or Simic Ascendancy are very real here. Saffgor, His Interest Piqued.

FromTheShire: Temur enchantments seems like a neat deck to build. I'm not sure I'd actually go the Saga route since I still tend to dislike them in Commander, and I'm not sure you'll cast enough of them for the 2 damage per to do more than annoy your opponents and draw aggro, but maybe that's actually a viable choice once you put it all together.

BPhillipYork: I'm really glad they referenced this, one of the better Star Trek episodes. A Temur enchantress that also deals damage when you play Sagas is fun, and great to build around. The ability to place counters is also really useful in random ways. Fun card.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hit It!

Saffgor: Aside from the weird Day 0 errata which doesn't change this card's functional text, it's cool to have such a versatile ramp piece. Haste is great, mill is great, and the fact this will usually be able to find a nonbasic means it can help colorfix in RGx lists. I expect to see this wherever Haste is relevant, in the appropriate colors.

FromTheShire: Now we're talking. Rhythm of the Wild has seen play in a bunch of formats, and it's not for making your creatures uncounterable. Haste is excellent, and having ramp stapled on to get to your big threats faster is a great bonus.

BPhillipYork: This is fine but Gruul really has better all haste sources to use.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Inner Light

Saffgor: It's a bit slow, but my goodness that's a lot of value. At minimum a reanimation, probably some card draw, and possibly some lifegain and a Treasure. If you expect to see every chapter, and do something with it, this card is backbreaking.

BPhillipYork: I really sort of hate that second Saga effect whenever I see it. Potentially you can like, slap double strike on something or else generate extra combat steps, but it's usually just draw 1 card potentially, which isn't very significant. Yet it seems to show up a lot on Sagas and such, I don't know why. Anyway, another great episode, okay card.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Kira Nerys, Firebrand

Saffgor: Star Trek's greatest achievement by my mark is the introduction of compelling Spacecraft in the Command Zone, and multiple cool Boros options. Kira is the standout for that latter camp, and is utterly bonkers when built around. Sneak Attack, blink, tokens, you name it; she's got a number of viable build paths at all power levels, and I expect to sit across from her at the LGS and MagicCons.

FromTheShire: Well that is just terrifying to sit down across from. Even worse than hasty threats are ones that enter attacking. It doesn't have to be attacking the same player as the attacking creature either, so this is a great way to cheat things in around defensive enchantments and the like.

BPhillipYork: The ability to have creatures enter after blockers are declared is pretty nasty, but it's also pretty transparent, so it's going to lead to people not wanting to be attacked by you, and lead to your creatures just being blown up. Definitely you can like, Sneak Attack out a Blightsteel Colossus and kill someone, but once you do that nobody is going to suffer you to attack them.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Mirror Universe Intrusion

Saffgor: If you rate Imposter Mech reasonably, this is along those lines. You'll rarely kick this, but having the option is nice I suppose. Really though, this exists to snag opposing value bodies and tutors.

BPhillipYork: I love this. I love the whole trope of, oh the evil version just has facial hair. And of course, the mirror universe version is the same but menacing. Chef's kiss. It's pretty good for just cloning your opponents commander or other super useful creature, and the kicker is only good if they have a creature based deck.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Nog, Ambitious Cadet

Saffgor: Build-your-own Icetill Explorer, sure. The real issue stems from the fact that, should Nog ever be removed, you are so wildly far behind. Paying in installments feels great until you need to do it twice. That said, using this with Agatha's Soul Cauldron and Creatures that are already Scouts? That has potential!

BPhillipYork: 6 mana for additional land and play lands from the yard, effectively. It's fine, but it also curves kind of badly. If you drop him turn 2, unless you managed a dork on turn 1, then turn 3 you are forced to pay the 1 and probably can't pay the 3, so you're on a 2-turn delay to get what you came for.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Sarek, Federation Ambassador

Saffgor: This just seems like a pain to track with not that much payoff. Ooh, a fair win condition if I do the thing, spooky. No thanks.

BPhillipYork: There are some goofy creatures that have a bunch of creature types, like Curious Farm Animals or Stonework Packbeast that come to mind, so if you want to make like a weird bad deck that is sort of fun. Run stuff from the D&D set and play a lot of cards that don't usually see play, he's good for that. Otherwise, probably pass.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Seven of Nine

Saffgor: Seven is the defacto Dilemma Commander unless you want to play 5c, and she's a decent one (if not a bit boring). I talked about Dilemmas at length in a recent Puzzlebox, so my full thoughts are there, but the real shock is how fair Seven actually ends up being, unless you do something silly with Pyramids.

BPhillipYork: Well this is potentially pretty strong, though it's really just an enabler for the true dilemma commander Captain Benjamin Sisko.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Warp Speed

Saffgor: They had to make this Simic identity I guess, what a shame. This is an absolutely tremendous spin on Spelunking, and the ability to translate it into advantage later is very welcome.

FromTheShire: Pretty much any card that makes your lands enter untapped has seen play somewhere because it's a wildly powerful ability. You may remember there was even a whole Modern deck about it. I'm not sure if there is enough that dovetails with this in Standard outside of Evendo, Waking Haven to be a real thing just yet, but I could see it possibly swapping in for The Wandering Minstrel in Pioneer Scapeshift since it doesn't die to Fatal Push.

BPhillipYork: Personally I don't think making lands enter untapped is all that exciting, most of the lands that enter tapped are bad. Cards that make bad cards less bad is not really my thing, but there are things you can do, especially with the bounce lands, and that's where I could see this being good.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Benjamin Sisko

Saffgor: There's a few ways to face infinite Dilemmas, but honestly this reads more like the mandated 'play all the cards in the set' 5c Commander. I guess we need to have it so fans of the property can slop it up, but I dunno, this one really doesn't spark joy.

BPhillipYork: Well this is obviously the build a dilemma deck commander, and I think a deck with tons of charms and stuff is a fun way to play. It's not super good, but it'll let you interact with the board a lot in different ways.

Colorless

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Combadge

Saffgor: Combadge isn't impressive, but the fact it makes a token Equipment matters immensely for a new Commander from the set, Gwyndala, the Progeny. That's where you'll see it, and it's pretty fantastic with her.

BPhillipYork: So, uh, 7 mana to make something unblockable. Well, it's really 7 mana to make 2 things unblockable, but even so.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Deep Space 9

Saffgor: It never becomes a Creature so alas, it cannot be your Commander. Regardless, this does some asinine things when cloned, in much the same vein as Gyruda. If this resolves, things get nasty, but ultimately it is just a big stupid Artifact.

FromTheShire: It's slow but it's pretty funny in a Primal Surge style deck. It's so expensive you definitely better be cheating it out somehow.

BPhillipYork: It's not like, super good, but it's a fun kind of, generate a ton of colorless mana, and then who knows what will show up. Probably Eldrazi.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Replicator

Saffgor: Things that make token copies will be playing this, as generally the upfront cost and/or opportunity cost of cloning something is more than the repeated cost of Replicator. Great thing to use with the New Planet tokens in the set as well.

FromTheShire: That's a lot of words to say Populate unless you're doing something unique to make token copies of noncreatures.

BPhillipYork: Making Food tokens isn't very exciting, but there are lots of ways to copy permanent spells now and this would let you make even more copies, which could get really nasty. So it's a great further enabler for a deck based on making token versions of things that don't normally have them.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class

Saffgor: This is a reasonable Spacecraft and will see you at minimum one additional card per turn, but where this might be enjoyed as a Commander is in some sort of Proliferate+Flash enabler deck. If you can add counters on opponents' turns, and Flash the cards you exile out, it becomes a cool value engine.

BPhillipYork: This seems fine, there's way you can get around the sorcery limitation, such as proliferate, and that'll let you play more stuff off the top, and certainly tap a creature to get the ability to play the top card of your deck is solid enough.

Land

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Starfleet Academy

Saffgor: You need to control 7+ types for this to start going mana-positive, and that's both fairly easy, and fairly difficult. I think in any Creature-heavy deck that isn't typal, you can probably turn this on in a meaningful capacity, but you're probably already way ahead on board at that point.

FromTheShire: The vast majority of the time this is probably not going to pay off, and will instead warp your deck trying to jump through hoops.

BPhillipYork: That's a very solid land, enters untapped and potentially makes a lot of mana, and unlike Gaea's Cradle variants it's not only green.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Anomalyland Cycle

Saffgor: These seem...pretty damn good. They remain mana-neutral, colorfix and get you a Landfall trigger, and many of their effects are pretty much at-rate for Land abilities. Green one is the stand-out, but Artificial Anomaly isn't too far off of Ramunap Ruins, and that card was banned in Standard.

FromTheShire: The crucial difference is that Ruins sacrificed a different land than itself, and was red. That made it perfect for pushing through the last few points of damage with aggro red after the board stabilized, and with Artificial Anomaly being in black I don't think it's as useful. Black has good ways to remove creatures so that board stall doesn't happen, and right now the most successful Rakdos deck is Lizards, which I can assure you in my games has either won or lost by the time you are at this plus 4 other lands. As a cycle they're not bad, I think we're more likely to see one or two ofs in a few decks rather than being major players.

BPhillipYork: These are fine, not terribly exciting, though I guess if you have the ability to play from your yard and the game stalls out you can just keep using them over and over, and end up with a lot of new planet tokens. I could see using Artificial Anomaly as a way of dealing consistent damage by having the ability to play lands from your graveyard in the right deck.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Dilemmaland Cycle

Saffgor: These being slowlands means they likely go more in Commander than 60-card, but Quark's Bar being an extra, potentially-untapped ping on a Red Land is frightening. Great to see a Rare Land cycle that works in Monocolor, but these not being fetchable like their Surveilland counterparts is a shame.

BPhillipYork: Quark's Bar is potentially usable in Worldgorger Dragon / Animate Dead loops as a damage source, and it's a slight improvement on the other ones that are deserts, other than that though these are all kind of meh to me for general usage.

Commander

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Borg Queen

Saffgor: This seems...mediocre. Theft in general is always a tough thing because you lose resources as players are eliminated, and The Borg Queen clearly wants you to be a board-based midrange deck. She can't even mill your opponents out, as the rest get put back rather than remaining in exile. Just not a great card, unfortunately, especially at a whopping 7 mana.

BPhillipYork: Well, there she is. Commander for your Borg deck, and she does something on ETB which is nice, since I think with a built in anthem and that ability to assimilate every attack she's going to be blown up a lot.  At 7 mana you're probably not casting her a lot of times per game.

FromTheShire: For 7 mana you don't even get to hit each opponent? Not great, Jim.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Commander Spock

Saffgor: 'Random bullshit go' is a strange theme for the logic-themed character Spock is, but with all the tutor-to-top effects in Bant, it might be less random than you think. Spock can crack tutor piles quite well in fact, especially if we're making our own little pile with Congregation at Dawn. He scales in power based on targets to hit, which means he's viable across a number of brackets, too—the more I think on this card, the more I like it.

BPhillipYork: This is really solid. Kind of weird, but basically at a minimum it's draw a card, and then gets better from there. Bant ramps really well and can fill out lots of differently named lands fast, to make this a lot more powerful. And you can just flicker him a bunch.

FromTheShire: Solid effect, but only getting one trigger per turn unless you're flickering and needing to survive combat as a 3/5 isn't outstanding. The fact it keys off of the lands you have in play means you're not doing the normal plan of trying to cheat out something giant with your free cast either so it's basically a value play unless you're stacking combo pieces on the top.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jean-Luc Picard, Luminary

Saffgor: It goes without saying, but rest in peace to the wonderful Patrick Stewart. It is a shame his face Commander card is mediocre. We'll talk about it in a bit, but placing counters on Spacecraft is worse than simply building your deck with enough high-power Creatures to turbo-Station them, and that draw trigger is shockingly restrained. He's fine, but we can do better.

BPhillipYork: Drawing off 3 cost mana rocks is pretty great, and leads to an overabundance of resources scenario that get's really dangerous. A Jeskai artifact commander like this is basically just an improvement over Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain or at least a strong variant, and it's not that hard to get some artifact cost reduction and just draw out your deck.

FromTheShire: Yeah I think this deck ends up being built not to care about Spacecraft unfortunately, it's just a pile of artifact cost reducers and huge payoffs since you have 'everything is a cantrip' in the Command Zone and storm/combo decks love that.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Worf, Defiant Commander

Saffgor: I think Worf just ends up being too fair, as even with extra combats he's less mana-efficient for a similar role to the Mardu Zuko. Not compelling.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty solid. With things like melee and myriad you can be attacking everyone all the time and just keep rolling out more and more aggression, which is very Worf-y. Actually it's not, Worf's main job on Star Trek was to get thrown around to prove how strong some alien was. But it's who Worf wanted to be.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Alexander, Ship's Fool

Saffgor: Oh this guy is just wonderful. A Boros Vial-Smasher of sorts, with some group hug/slug elements, and mana-value matters. We're going to be untapping this guy, placing big stuff on top, just having a blast. They were incredibly generous to make this an Instant-speed tap ability!

BPhillipYork: That's fun.

FromTheShire: Now this I like. Random damage and political tool for the ultimate "hey guys it's not my fault you took damage and look at that scary Blightsteel Steve just drew" table manipulation. If you're a bard at heart, I think you're going to have a blast with this guy.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Janeway, Endgame

Saffgor: Officer typal is probably just better done by the 5c Sisko, and that unblockable ability is laughably inefficient. No thanks.

BPhillipYork: This is fun, not great but lots of ways to make Officers and throwing them away for more card draw is very thematic.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Captain Kirk, Boldly Going

Saffgor: Tutoring up a pretty good Spacecraft which he in turn helps to turbo out and pump, which in turn causes it to draw more cards? I mean, shoot, this guy does everything. Just make sure that the Enterprise doesn't get exiled, and Kirk here takes care of the rest. The Spock precon knows how to design a Commander, and probably has two of the best options on its own!

BPhillipYork: Crazy ramp commander really. That particular Enterprise is really good, and uh, goes really well with crazy ramp. Kind of weird to have a pure value commander, though you could definitely make a Spacecraft tribal deck which would be kind of fun.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Chief Kyle, Transporter Tech

Saffgor: Seeing 'Kyle' on a Magic card makes my hackles raise, but for what it's worth he's quite strong. Combining Landfall & blink with so many Creatures like sad robot which find Lands is sure to lead to an outpouring of value.

BPhillipYork: This is an okay variant for Brago, King Eternal, a little slower but maybe less dangerous, and of course you can just run both. It can be a value play early that turns into a win con later which is pretty solid.

FromTheShire: Definitely goes in the 99 of other decks but easily triggered repeatable blink is great.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Cmdr. Troi, Outgoing Empath

Saffgor: You just know that abbreviation is because they couldn't fit 'Commander', and that's just a little funny. Troi is one of a few different candidate for Izzet politics/group hug, along with Red Death & Itazura, but my main concern is that she might just kingmake a bit too often.

BPhillipYork: This seems potentially really dangerous, but kind of a fun way to play. Obviously you'd really want some way to see into their hand first (which seems like what she should actually do but here we are). Anyway, unpredictable but interesting commander.

FromTheShire: This is super interesting, and a great excuse to play things like Telepathy, Zur's Weirding, and Glasses of Urza. It also means she scales super well to whatever the power level of the table is which I like as well.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Collective Override

Saffgor: Uh...wow, okay, this is about as pushed as a Fog-like can be. This will blow out a lot of opponents, and does a pretty good Jeska's Will impression alongside an Altar/KCI if you're swinging out.

BPhillipYork: Well this seems really good for like, the Borg deck. And potentially good for any kind of go-wide deck, though blue isn't the most common color for go wide, it's definitely viable, especially Esper.

FromTheShire: Sheesh. It doesn't do a full Arachnogenesis or Inkshield unfortunately so most likely the creatures are all dying as chump blockers if you're the one getting attacked, but that's some damn spicy card draw. And if you're the one doing the attacking, or one opponent is attacking another. this becomes an absolute house.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hugh, First of Many

Saffgor: Replicate is a delightful little additional cost, which means reductions pay for its colorless component. Anything with Affinity for Artifacts is usually able to overflow some of its discount, and in practice have "Replicate {U}", so that's really where Hugh's home is. Great potential here, and while it might not cut it in cEDH you can sure make a ton of Frogmites.

BPhillipYork: That's potentially really nuts. Like it's super expensive, but with cost reduction it gets really cheap suddenly, and you can make a horde or go nuts with mana rocks and triggers (Disciple of the Vault type of thing).

FromTheShire: Fortunate it goes in a deck with lots of cost reducers or else you would never actually be able to pay for the replicate, but once you can it's a banger.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Jurati, Cordial Queen

Saffgor: Token-copy your Artifacts, copy your clones, profit. I wish she had evasion, but I won't turn my nose up at another clone-adjacent Commander that comes in low along the curve.

BPhillipYork: This seems a bit of a win-more type card, but if you build around it, a particularly with some kind of ETB heavy creatures. Using something like Gingerbrute to swing through so you can copy a Sad Robot via a Borg scientist is kind of funny.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Kor, Cause of Vengeance

Saffgor: The fact the Klingon payoffs also state 'Warrior' goes to show why typal strategies in UB that necessitate their own type are doomed to fail. I don't know dude, can we not? There's not enough Klingons, and there's better Warrior Commanders.

BPhillipYork: This isn't all that unique, so it's fine, but not all that exciting. But certainly if you want a Mardu deck around this or just to slot it into a Mardu deck it's fine.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Locutus of Borg

Saffgor: This guy goes exponential, and being in Blue you've got Extra Turns to contend with. Much like crabs, every archetype returns to Edric Flying Men eventually.

BPhillipYork: Well that's potentially a lot of tokens, after a while. Obviously all the Borg work pretty well with each other.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Pavel Chekov, First Posting

Saffgor: Holy shit, Abundance in the Command Zone. Uhh, wow, yeah that's wild to see, and both stops you from ever flooding out...and enables combos. I don't expect him to be popular, but there are some fun '96 Lands and some combo pieces' lists to brew here.

BPhillipYork: This isn't super exciting but you can do things with it or just include it in a deck where you say use a lot of land tutors so you don't ever want to draw land.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lieutenant Hikaru Sulu

Saffgor: Killer first ability, and unblockable when you need it helps certain payoffs get in. Give this guy a Banishing Knack and let him go to town with an untap effect.

BPhillipYork: This is fun, a bit expensive, but a nice color combo and it lets you swing through with something you used his explore to ramp to get going.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Command Chair

Saffgor: I think you need at least an 8-mana Commander to make this worthwhile, and that's a fairly slim pool for a 6-mana Artifact. Having to control them as well is another hurdle, so unless you intend to cheat out a Dargo this likely stays in the binder.

BPhillipYork: It's a big mana rock that's really too expensive but some people are into that kind of thing.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Guardian of Forever

Saffgor: A slow Nim Deathmantle of sorts, it's cool to see Colorless have an aristocrats-ish blink-ish Commander now that comes in so low along the curve.

BPhillipYork: Well it's kind of a neat looping sacrifice, really expensive, and also slow, so it's not like you can really abuse it. Though you could use it to avoid removal everyone will see that coming.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

K'Vort-Class Raider

Saffgor: Hexproof like that is nice to see, and given any Commander can play it I expect the Raider to show up, even if it isn't flashy.

BPhillipYork: Well giving creatures your control hexproof during your turn for purely colorless mana is a pretty nice effect to let you do your thing, that seems like that main use of this.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Queen's Diamond

Saffgor: Is this my favorite card in the set? It's certainly close, if I'm to be honest. The Diamond has shades of the now-banned Jeweled Lotus, helping to turbo out Commanders with not too many colors (ideally 2 or fewer) by way of translating power on board into a chunk of restricted mana. This has a much higher ceiling than the Lotus, but a much fairer floor, although the safety it provides to kill-on-sight Commanders or those with obnoxious costs is profound. Heck, it's a pretty good blocker if you don't need to recast your Commander regardless. It might just be the kind of person I am, but every deck I've built post-Star Trek has found room for the Diamond, and that tells me it likely has legs.

BPhillipYork: This is more what I expected a Borg ship to be like, and I like the idea of being able to afford command tax, so nice to see this. It's really something you can include in any color deck having nothing to do with Borg. Having haste and vigilance is also a nice upside so you can just swing away and then use it for mana later. You can also do something crazy with this and Doubling Cube.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

S.S. Botany Bay

Saffgor: Cross off 'Reanimator' from your Colorless Commander archetype list, because this nails that task. A bit of grave hate, a cheap upfront cost, and being in an identity known for its massive beaters. Seems like a slam dunk, if you can actually get the necessary targets in yard.

BPhillipYork: Wow that's a strange but effective reanimator, and also a way to trim your opponents' graveyards when you want. I sort of like the idea of including this as a troll card, that you're just hitting yards with and not really exiling anything and everyone is holding up removal to keep it from reanimating something dangerous.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Starfleet Communicator

Saffgor: Gwyndala use case aside, this is just a ton of draw and ramp if we're being honest. With a pair of attackers suited up, you've basically cast two Growth Spiral on attack, and I'm pretty happy with that. Makes for some genuinely obscene tokens to copy too, as three attackers with these gets out of hand exponentially.

BPhillipYork: This is a more useful communicator, and there's lots of ways to make more copies, which seems like a fun thing. Replicate this with Hugh, First of Many and things can get pretty nuts pretty fast.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Starship Enterprise

Saffgor: This card is attached at the hip to Bant Kirk, and I think its only real use case beyond him is in counter-matters strategies like Maester Seymour & Felisa, Fang of Silverquill. Seems great there though, so who am I to judge? Lovely card.

BPhillipYork: This is one of the stronger spacecraft in my opinion and of course Kirk fetches it which is pretty nice.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Defiant, Mirror Migrant

Saffgor: Fantastic protection piece and a well-overstatted attacker/blocker once Stationed up. This goes in every Commander-reliant strategy you can imagine, and with it being Colorless that's a great number of them. Pick up copies while they're cheap.

BPhillipYork: This is a nice way to save something and then you've got an okay hitter if you want that.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Enterprise-C, Time-Tossed

Saffgor: The only real stellar thing this does is serve as a remarkably funny card to clone. Legend Rule-ing a clone wheels you twice, and that's a stupid amount of discards & draws if there are external payoffs. Not great outside of that.

BPhillipYork: There are some things that care about how many you drew or discarded this turn, and this is a solid way to trigger that a lot, but outside of that I probably wouldn't run this just for pure value in a deck that doesn't specifically leverage what it does.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Farragut, Bold Explorer

Saffgor: I've got a Focus cooking for this one, because giving Colorless access to a Sneak Attack on Landfall is already amazing, and this one even replaces the advantage you'd have otherwise lost. The Farragut, so long as you have enough ramp, is a frighteningly powerful Landfall payoff...and given Landfall requires Lands, which produce mana, you probably have enough ramp to make this spooky.

BPhillipYork: This is fun, especially if you run a ton of ETBs and ramp, it's just ramp into crazy more ramp. It fits well with Kirk and the Enterprise and lots of lands entering and things that fetch lands. Of course it also lets you cheat out huge creatures for a single shot, it's basically Sneak Attack for every color which is insane.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

U.S.S. Prometheus, Prototype

Saffgor: Add 'Voltron' to that Colorless Archetype checklist please. I mean, shoot, this does just kind of do exactly what you want if that's the goal. A hair expensive, but in Bracket 2 you can definitely score some kills.

BPhillipYork: This effect is nice in Commander, if you can pump it you can deal a lot of damage really fast. People will certainly see that coming so you'll want some way to protect it.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Shore Leave Planet

Saffgor: It's weird to print this a set after Hall of Echoes, not going to lie. Feels like some designer miscommunication. If you were playing Hall, play this as well. If you weren't, don't.

BPhillipYork: Copying a creature is nice sometimes, but I don't know that you really want it from lands. It's sort of like a temp clone but it effectively costs you 6, so are you really going to be able to do something if you've already burned through 6 mana?
