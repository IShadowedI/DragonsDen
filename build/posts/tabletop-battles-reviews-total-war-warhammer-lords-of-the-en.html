Tabletop Battles Reviews: Total War Warhammer Lords of the End Times

September 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The phrase “End Times” is a loaded one to many of the folks who frequent this website. It refers to the sequence of events that led to the shuttering of the world of Warhammer Fantasy which recently returned as Warhammer Old World. Fantasy contained a lot of beloved characters, many done dirty by the wrap up of their stories all happening quickly and in sequence. Because of that, and the killing of an entire game system, the End Times left a bad taste in a lot of long time player’s mouths. After 10 years, the Total War: Warhammer series is doing their own End Times with a new DLC release, and no doubt are looking to have a better landing than their tabletop predecessors.

Thank you to SEGA for providing us with access to the Lord of the End Times DLC. 

What You’re Getting

The Lords of the End Times is a pack of 4 DLCs, each containing a legendary lord for Total War Warhammer 3. Each of these DLCs can be played stand alone, meaning you can play the Nagash campaign without owning Total War Warhammer 3 or any other DLC which is pretty exciting. Each Lord is part of a preexisting faction (or a collection of them) and provides their own twist on their established formulas. Available is Boris Todbringer (Empire), Glottkin (Warriors of Chaos), Thanquol (Skaven), and Nagash (Kinda Tomb Kings I guess maybe but really all the undead factions at once; it's kinda nuts).

The Lords

Boris offers some more melee focused troops to the Empire roster. Sworn to the god Ulfric, his guys are largely wolf themed and focused around either anchoring the line or hitting your opponent hard. There’s not a ton to write home about here, as mostly everything has a comparative unit that’s already in the roster (shield infantry but elite, knights but more durable) but what’s here fills out the Empire roster to let you build any sort of army you can imagine. You can have your gunline, scrappy blitz infantry, or a tech-y magic focused force. The new units are a nice mix of low, mid and high tier so you’ll see a lot of new stuff across all steps of your campaign.

Mechanically, Boris focuses around his Temple of Ulfric, which can give you some faction-wide effects that can bolster your forces and the Defense of the Drakenwald system that has you fighting on going beastmen attacks as the campaign goes on. The latter is the narrative focus of the campaign, as Boris preps for the ongoing chaos incursions he’ll be looking to settle his grudge he has with Kazarak One-Eye in a climatic cinematic battle. All the lords have a quest like this and little narrative blurbs breadcrumbed along the way which helps guide the player towards different goals.

Next up, The Glottkin are somewhat your standard warriors of Chaos flair. You get access to some new fast moving cheap infantry in the form of Flayerkin, a new giant, and a slew of Blightking variants. This is one thing a lot of folks may have complaints about in this DLC; there are a lot of reskins. That said, the design around these reskins is definitely not slapdash. For example, in addition to the above units, the Glottkin get access to four legendary heroes, three of which -- the Maggoth Lords -- are reskins of each other, but each has a distinct skill tree and battlefield role and one can be used as a legendary lord when you unlock them. While they may look similar you’ll be using them very differently and can theme entire armies around each one of them.

Mechanically the Glottkin have the standard Warriors of Chaos systems of vassalizing Norsca fractions by roughing them up and using those vassals to fund your war into forces of Order. Special to them though are the Gifts of Nurgle, which are buffs you can give to Lords and Heroes that can do things like spread plagues or change the actions they can do on the campaign map, and the Gardens of Corruption, which basically give you a settlement from the Nurgle daemon faction, giving you access to their roster and plagues. With the Warriors of Chaos recruitment system and the summoned daemons you get from the Gardens you can pop up a formidable force in a turn assuming you can financially support it, allowing you to make lightning assaults wherever you need them. 

I spent the most time with the Glottkin in my time prepping for the review, upgrading marauders all the way to Blightkings and achieving their short victory by conquering Altdorf. The narrative beats and the way the game guides you along an “ideal” path is a welcome change of pace from other Immortal Empires campaigns, giving you enough short term goals to keep you engaged beyond just attacking the next settlement. In battles, the Glottkin are an absolute terror as well. Once you get some regeneration on the trio they’re almost impossible to take down.

Where the Glottkin may be familiar, Nagash is altogether different. Nagash uses the full rosters from all the undead factions in the game. You want an army of grave guard backed up by Vampire Coast artillery? You can do that. You want a monster mash of all the undead big guys? Absolutely. Getting all the best heroes together for a single entity doomstack? You got it. In addition, you can confederate most of the big factions as well as some fresher faces via the Mortarch system, allowing you to pay some money after a set of conditions are met to automatically grab all their assets.

You also get the most insane tech system ever introduced in this series via the Black Pyramid. If you’ve played Path of Exile or Final Fantasy X, this will be familiar. It's a sphere grid. The thing that's messed up is that every single node on this thing is useful and a lot are transformative to how your units function. This is probably the most impressive thing in the set of DLCs; if you’re looking to get just one lord, pick Nagash for this reason alone.

On the battlefield, Nagash is also a monster in his own right. With access to the new lore of Undeath and the massive buffs you can get to his spell casting he can out-heal and out damage most things in front of him. He’s no slouch in combat either, as long as you keep him healed up (which is trivial with his abilities) he can solo entire low tier settlement garrisons. His new units add some nice spice to existing undead rosters, with spirit hosts giving you a new type of chaff that are a fantastic tarpit if your opponent doesn’t have magical attacks, Morghast providing fast moving monstrous infantry that can hang for a while unsupported and the Khemric Titan which is a big bug made of rocks that shoot lasers.

Last-last but not least-least is everyone's favorite grey seer; Thanquol. Mechanically, Thanquol isn’t too different from other Skaven lords, but gets an additional mechanic called Master Plans. These plans can be a bit confusing on their face but in practice are pretty straight forward. Basically you assign one of your four underlings to do a plan; this can be something like massing a garbage army to attack a settlement, stealing warpstone (Thanquol’s special currency) and money from another faction, or doing some more specialist stuff like summoning one of the new Verminlords. Once you start your plan, every turn your underling will draw some “schemes” from a deck that will determine what you’re getting out of your plan, the longer you let the plan cook though, “complications” will arise that can do things like kill your massing units or remove money from your pot. The nice thing is these plans are relatively cheap to enact so you can keep spamming them.

On the battlefield Thanquol is mostly unimpressive until he gets his mount Boneripper, at which point he becomes a ranged (or melee if you’re inclined) powerhouse. If you’ve ever used warpflamers and said “wow these are pretty good” but bemoaned their speed and inability to hang in melee, Thanquol does it all. The same can be said for his additions to the roster. The headliners are the Stormfiend variants that come in 6 flavors: fire, machine gun, poison wind launchers, grinder fist, big spiky balls, electro fists. These are all reskins of each other, but the utility added to the Skaven roster for a durable weapons platform or brawler unit that can be in sustained combat can’t be denied. In addition, there’s stuff like the low tier Pusbags or the high tier Warp-Doom Magma Cannon that adds a nice bit of variety between high and low tier units. In addition, Skaven can now recruit Verminlords, the greater daemons of the faction, to lead armies. These give some pretty amazing buffs and are strong combatants in their own right.

Closing Time

All of the above isn’t a comprehensive list of what’s in this DLC, but even then there’s plenty of meat on this bone. And that doesn’t even take into account the 9.0 update that’s releasing alongside it with a ton of quality of life improvements and Neferatta. Overall though what I imagine folks will want to know is “Is this worth it?” If you’re a veteran of the series looking for something new, this is absolutely a great get. Each lord brings exactly what you’re looking for and a ton of cool mechanics to sink your teeth into. If you’re looking to hop into the Total War Warhammer ecosystem with this DLC though… I’ll be honest these are some of the most complex characters and factions that are in the game. All of them have mechanics you’ll have to respect or you can easily bork your save, and even when I knew what I was looking at, things like the Black Pyramid and the Master Plans had me scratching my head a bit. If you’re up to the challenge and love reading A Lot when diving into a strategy game, there’s a lot to chew on here, but ironically the End Times are gonna demand a lot of your time. 

All that said there’s so much love here that if you’re just coming in as a person who loves Warhammer you’ll find a lot to like. When Gotrek and Felix showed up and ruined my Master Plan with Thanquol where I was trying to steal from Nuln, it felt incredibly apt. Nagash snowballing into an unbeatable force and casting a billion spells truly felt like he does on the tabletop. The characters all ooze with personality, and clearly feel like they belong in the world they inhabit. Even with 20ish hours under my belt in this DLC alone, I can tell you my time here isn’t quite at its end.
