[40K] Competitive Innovations in 11th: Hark, Angels pt.1

November 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

The World Championships looms ever closer, and right now Space Marines look poised to claim it. All of Iron Hands, Ultrawatch, Dark Angels and Space Wolves are tearing up the metagame, and although there's still sparks of defiance, this week they're firmly on top. Will the tables turn once the best of the best unleash their super secret tech? Not long till we find out. For now, let's survey the power-armoued devastation.

Thoughts

We start as we mean to go on today with some Dark Angels representation, here repping a big pile of Speeders with bonus alpha strike potential via Thundercowl Turbines. Nasty. The list already has a tonne of anti-tank, so packing in some insurance against things like 20-Berzerker bricks seems fine, sure. Dark Angels wildly outperformed everyone else on win rate this weekend, even if I think the most powerful Wolves and Iron Hands builds are probably better, but even amongst all these successes this was the single biggest win, so congratulations Jack!

Archetype

Double Telemon Custodes

Thoughts

Alongside the Marine cousins they view with deep suspicion, Custodes have been picking up the pace this week, with the highest non-Marine win rate as people start to really figure them out. As this has been happening we've seen lists without as many Dreadnoughts do well, but you absolutely can still just slam Dreadnoughts and Allarus onto the table and go wild. This combination of tools gives you a great ability to push into pretty much all Marine builds, though dropping the Caladius that you normally see reduces threat projection against some forces. Teching for the Marine matchup makes sense though - emerging evidence is that the Custodes book is extremely real, but because of its low drop count it suffers into enemies that are just too efficient, which the best Marines absolutely are. This build feels right for that metagame, and though it couldn't quite pin down the Dark Angels above, it did smash through several other powerful Marine lists with Steve at the helm.

Thoughts

Well done Max.

Thoughts

The Imperium's most elite forces face off, presumably arguing over who gets to fumigate the basement of the Imperial Palace for the next century. In the shiny silver corner, a Purifier-heavy build of Warpbane, going for a second full squad as well as Crowe's, which is currently the most popular option for Warpbane. This is because when storm bolters are real guns, it turns out that being able to fire a whole squad of them with full hit and wound re-rolls is pretty good in most matchups.

Not so much this one though - this has the potential to be fairly rough. The good-ish news for the Grey Knights is that Custodes don't have the drops to screen out their teleporting nonsense, especially if they prioritise clearing the Sisters, so they can leverage mobility very effectively. They're also well set up to take the Terminators off the board if given a chance, though Trajann's sheer durability may well bounce them.

The bad news is the rest of this army, which is very resilient to their offence, and perfectly equipped to send them back to Titan. Double Caladius is a disaster for the Grey Knights - unless they get turbo-lucky with the razorbacks, these are never dying, and threaten to obliterate a Dreadknight cleanly each time they shoot. Meanwhile, the Galatus dreads are fantastic into everything that they're facing, and hard to put down. Archetype

Thoughts

As the title suggests, this is the build of Shamblerot that excelled at the Paris Wargame Expo a little while ago, dropping the standard Defiler and also using the flex slot for Plague Marines and Plaguecasters in Rhinos. This gives the list a more mid-weight piece it can skirmish with compared to the polarised Poxwalker or Engine options you normally have, and also makes locking down objectives and reliably hitting Disruption Primary much easier. The Knight still gives you plenty of long-ranged firepower, which is helped by the Plague Marines being able to tag bonus AP onto a more distant target. This version also loses a lot less from the change to Shambling Wall than Defiler versions, and it wouldn't shock me if we saw it creep further up in popularity. Fundamentally, the Plague Marine datasheet is an absolute steal at 90pts, so congratulations to Marc for putting them to work.
