August 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I talked about prep and travel to the 2026 Lone Star Open from Frontline Gaming. I've been to that event a few times but took a couple of years off recently when the timing of it just didn't jibe with my schedule. This year I went back with the wife and kid in tow.

The big change here is dropping a Rhino and a Tzaangor Shaman for a second Forgefiend, and I've moved to Rubricae Phalanx from Hexwarp + Ritual. This also means I'm not on Purge anymore, and that'll end up being a bigger loss than I expected, as Take and Hold doesn't quite fit the "go really hard early and often" strategy I tended to benefit from with Purge. But what it does have is a better detachment rule (+1 to saves against D1 attacks), with access to a better "fall back and shoot/charge" stratagem, which did a lot of work for me at Tacoma.

I didn't quite finish my second Forgefiend, but I did get him done enough to play with comfortably. Of course, I immediately forgot my display board back at home on our way out the door, which meant I was more or less hopeless for paint scoring at the event. So not much to be done there, but it means I won't be waiting around to find out the results of paint scoring or best overall.

We arrived at the hotel in Allen on Friday night and more or less turned in that night without hanging out. It had been a relatively long day for us and it's a four-hour drive up to the venue in Dallas from Houston - not crazy, but long enough that you feel it. I said hi to a couple of Houston regulars before we hit the hay.

As an event, the Lone Star Open is always really well run. The location is fantastic - it's a large, well lit event space with a ton of space, the hotel food is good, and there's a nice little downtown area across the street from the hotel where you can get decent food. They always have some decent vendor support, and Jake and the Frontline Gaming crew do a good job running the event and handling judge calls - the system they have puts QR codes on each table to call a judge, letting you call one more discreetly, which is cool.

Bret's big challenge is that he fundamentally lacks the firepower he needs to handle the Scarabs, and his infantry are dead weight against the small flamer squads. And this is one of the few matchups where the detachment rule matters, giving me good save buffs against FRFSF shooting. That said, he does have infiltrators and scouts, which leads me to put one of my Terminator squads on the table in case I have to go second and don't want to get locked out of deep strike placement if he spreads out aggressively.

This was Take and Hold Layout A, and all of the day 1 games were Layout A. That would end up meaning I had to play the same mission/layout - Take & Hold vs. Take & Hold - three times. That was kind of brutal.

My general plan was to let Bret screen his backfield, then drop a terminator unit into the expansion objective on the lower right, in front of him where I can immediately drop a tank and force Bret to fight over his expansion while I dominate mine and the middle. That worked really well, and I don't think he expected to see my unit drop there - he needed to screen me out of it.

My Terminators would ultimately get whittled down in that corner but spend most of the game forcing him to commit to fighting them while I take out the rest of his army at midtable. The ensuing scrum means he falls behind early on primary scoring and never catches up.

Result: 96-44, Win

I ended up skipping lunch - I had a decent breakfast in the hotel with my son - and went straight into round 2.

In a hilariously rough twist of fate, this is the second time I'd end up playing against a list that was inexplicably heavy on Sisters of Silence at Dallas. That said, Brian was a better player than I anticipated by a considerable amount, and while this list was a nightmare for me in particular, he also went 5-1 at the event, so it has legs even when it isn't playing against Thousand Sons.

It's also very good into Thousand Sons, though. Those sisters have a 3+ Feel No Pain against psychic attacks and mortal wounds, and are just more than capable of spiking saves against my regular attacks regardless. The Allarus are also an issue, with an insane number of attacks. They're the bricks I need to counter with my own terminators, but unlike most Custodes lists which run a small number of units this one more resembles a horde and can easily spread out and zone me out.

I end up going first, which I don't really want, and pushing forward to try and avoid early T1 charges from Sisters sword units which can move 9" and advance and charge. I avoid the T1 charge into my Forgefiend, but otherwise that was the last charge Brian missed. He'd end up hitting all of his deep strike charges, including a longer bomb from his Allarus into my ingressed Scarabs, going 11" to tag them and do real damage.

The killer was when I just bounced utterly off his Sisters units as they passed an insane number of 4+ armor saves against my flamers to stay on the table at midtable, keeping me off scoring primary for longer than I needed. At the same time, his reserve Sword Sisters hit their 9" charge into my Forgefiend on the expansion and spiked their attacks to kill it, and that was more or less the game.

Result: 65-100, Loss

That was a relatively brutal loss, but I'm not too choked up about it. It's a tough list for me - melee heavy with a lot of movement and DS threats - and the army's bullshit inbuilt psyker protections meant that I was always going to be fighting an uphill battle. It's the kind of game you can't really plan for because if sisters of silence lists become big in the meta, you just don't play Thousand Sons.

Melee lists are always a tough challenge for the Thousand Sons, who really don't want to be tied up in melee, particularly against the kinds of units which can do 3-damage attacks and trivially put T5 targets on 2+ to hit with [LANCE]. My goal in this one was to clear out the Vindicators early and then hopefully pick off the other units a little at a time.

This one started off really strong for me as I was able to push the Vindicators down to 1 and 2 wounds, respectively - but then I kind of stalled from there. Some rough dice on my end and a bad choice gambit splitting fire meant I left too many units alive, allowing Terrell to keep both Vindicators firing - albeit at -1 to hit - and harassing my units. The Sanguinary Guard were a particular problem, as the combo of -1 to hit, -1 to wound, and a 2+ save really made them a chore to deal with.

I had some particularly bad saves with my the terminator unit I dropped onto Terrell's expansion and lost a large number of them before I wanted to. My saving grace this game was that Terrell played too conservatively, holding back units early instead of committing to that kind of screaming assault he needed. He mounted a very strong comeback on rounds 3 and 4, pushing his guard and captains to mid-table, but by that point I was too far ahead of him on primary scoring for him to make up the difference. I managed to squeak out of this one but it was closer than the score indicated. Mid-game there Terrell really spiked some attacks into me and I thought he might have it until his luck ran out the following turn.

Result: 85-67, Win

2-1 isn't how I like to start events but it also means I'm free to head home after round 5 on Sunday, making for an early return. That's great because the missus was very not likely to want to stay past noon. We ended up meeting up with Thomas "Goatboy" Reidy and his wife and kids and going out to dinner at Grimaldi's with our friends Max and Brandon. The pizza was very solid and because I needed to get a kid back to the room, I turned in earlier than usual.

Round 4: vs. Chaos Knights.

This was certainly a game that I played. I underestimated how much damage I'd need to put into an Atrapos when it was running a 4+ invulnerable save and a 6+ Feel No Pain, and some bad luck on the saves meant that my Scarabs only managed to chip 12 wounds off it in their first volley. That wasn't insurmountable but I also failed two Heroic Interventions this game - both under 6" - with key units that would have really changed the flow and outcome of the game. As it was, I fell behind on primary early to Priority Assets and was never able to catch up.

Result: Loss, 73-93

Going 2-2 isn't great but it carried the massive advantage of being able to go home even earlier. So we grabbed lunch with Thomas and his family before saying goodbye and heading home. The drive back was pretty uneventful and we performed the customary Texas ritual of stopping at one of the two Buc-ee's between Dallas and Houston for gas and food. Getting home by 5pm made it all worth it and after the amount of travel and Warhammer I've been putting in.

Final Thoughts

I'm really not sold on Rubricae Phalanx. Ultimately the +1 to saves bonus just didn't feel great and I think on the balance I'd have been better off having access to dev wounds via Grand Coven. My next stop is likely to swap over to Grand Coven, which will mean killing a unit of Scarabs in favor of Magnus. That said, I'm also not averse to trying out Death Guard again - I really liked the list I played into at Tacoma a few weeks ago and it may have legs. I need to figure out what I'm bringing to Challengers Cup in September, and I only have a couple of weeks to do it.

See you next Thursday.
