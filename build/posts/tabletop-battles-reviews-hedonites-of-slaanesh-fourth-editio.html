Tabletop Battles Reviews: Hedonites of Slaanesh Fourth Edition Battletome

June 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer
As always we’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of the Battletome. We have also been provided with a provisional Battle Profile list for the army to use when writing the review, but please note that these points are subject to change until they are published on Warhammer Community.

Slaanesh has had a lot of ups and downs in the past few editions. Toward the end of 2nd edition it was the most busted Battletome that had come out at that time, and it never lived that one down. Since then it's slowly tried to dig itself back out of the gutter with a new mechanic around tempting your opponent with offers of power that may blow back in their face.

The current Faction Pack version has put up some decent tournament results, but the Temptation Dice mechanic is very difficult to use properly. Since you no longer offer your opponent the dice when you want, and they can use it when they want it gives them a lot more power over those (potential) auto 6s. Can Slaanesh find new levels of depravity to conquer the battlefield?

Battle Traits

Like many of the recent Battletomes, GW has taken a more radical departure from the faction pack, eschewing some longstanding battle traits in favor of some new flavors. For Slaanesh, this means Temptation dice are gone, which is a bit of a shame since it was one of the more easily identifiable flavor wins, but the new system has its perks.

Credit: Games Workshop

Instead we have the Paragon system. Each turn, if you have fewer than three units with the Paragon keyword, you can give it to a unit who doesn't have the keyword (many of the Unique Heroes come with it on their warscroll, and it does count toward the limit). This unlocks a number of Temptation keyworded battle traits for them, or makes certain ones better. However, Slaanesh is fickle and if a Paragon makes a fool of themselves, Slaanesh loses interest in their champion and they lose the keyword immediately. This includes:

  • Taking 3 or more damage from a non-Attack ability
  • Miscasting a spell
  • Making an unmodified Charge roll of 5 or less

Now, the good news is this after any rerolls. So you have the opportunity to save face if you roll an unfortunate Charge roll. You cannot give Paragon status back to a unit that already lost it but Unique units cannot lose their status, whether it came with the Warscroll or not. Sigvald already proved himself, he doesn't have to do it again.

In case you were trying to be clever and stack your list full of Paragon Unique Heroes to bypass the limit, it won't work. The designers commentary specifically calls this out and forces you to remove units from the field until you have exactly 3.

Now what exactly does this unlock? A lot, actually. All the keyworded Temptation abilities are Once Per Turn (Army) and each Paragon unit can only use one Temptation ability per turn, so you have to be choosy, but you do have options. First we have Sensuous Arcana, if a non-Unique Paragon Wizard successfully casts a spell, they immediately can make all friendly wizards within 12" of them gain +1 to cast, making other casts even better.

In either movement phase we get Cutting Barbs, which can either make an enemy unit within 12" +1 to be hit or give them a control score of 1. If this unit is a Paragon, you get to choose, if they're not a Paragon, your opponent picks. This is clever, obviously this incentivizes you to use the Paragon system but if you have to improvise, neither debuff is great for the opponent so you still kind of win out.

Next for the shooting phase, we have Beguiling Glance. A Hero can pick a unit within 12" and then force them to either end their charge no further from that Hero or subtract 2 from their charge. Like Cutting Barbs, this is a "If Paragon you pick, if not a Paragon they pick". Ability. While it definitely seems more useful in the enemy turn, it works in both, so it can be used to deter counter-charges and is particularly effective with the Lord of Hubris to force a no win scenario.

In your charge phase, non-Unique Paragon units that successfully charged can use Chief of Revels granting a +2" charge aura. Simple and sweet, does the job.

Finally in combat, you get two traits that can be used in either fight phase: Excess of Agility is a simple 12" -1 to be hit Aura only usable by a Paragon and Enthralling Vanity is the final "If Paragon you pick, if not a Paragon they pick" ability where you pick an enemy unit and either get Crit (2 hits) against them with all attacks, or they lose their weapon abilities. Unlike the other 2 you probably want to have a Paragon be using this since it can be very easy for the opponent to pick the lesser of two evils and walk away mostly intact.

Overall, I dig the new ability. It's a lot more intuitive than Temptation dice  to the opponent which either required you to slow down play to ask players if they want to use them in 3rd, or would often go forgotten in 4th (and were often more trouble for the Slaanesh player than they were worth).

The challenge is in the fact that your units may lose Paragon status very abruptly and force you to improvise, as the loss in status happens immediately. You're also limited to 3 Paragon units at a time, who can each only use one of these abilities. There are 6 total, and 3 can be used by non-Paragon (albeit by allowing your opponent to choose which fate they'd prefer) so you have to weigh if you want to use as many as possible, or whether you would prefer to force a certain debuff upon them instead.

It also means the end of Euphoric as a keyword, which means Crit (2 Hits) (and the upgraded Crit Mortals) are harder to come by, as are Run and Shoot/Charge. This means the army isn't quite as fast it was, and you may have to be more cagey about when you attack.

Battle Formations

As always, we have four options here and they're good options. Depraved Carnival, 3 units get a free D6" of movement at the end of both player's turns. They can move while in combat, but can't move into combat. This is a crazy deal and may be the strongest but even 3 free out of phase moves every turn has some stiff competition.

Artisans of Torment gives a combat phase command ability, Sadistic Tendencies. Target a friendly unit and an enemy they're in combat with, get +1 attack but you have to swing everything at them. Statistically better than all out attack (And no downside along with it) if you really want something dead and have an aggressive melee list this isn't bad.

Godseeker Cavalry gives Run and Charge but only to units within 9" of the battlefield edge. Run and Charge is almost always good, but the value of this is going to vary wildly based on the mission which you naturally won't know ahead of time.

The final one Lurid Dreamers might be the most technically interesting one of the bunch. At the end of the enemy shooting phase, you get a free teleport for one unit that took damage this turn, outside of 9". Shooting heavy armies may have to assess if it's worth potentially giving you a free teleport onto an open objective, and even armies who lack shooting but have that random hero with a 12" gun that they shoot just because there's no downside may have to rethink that.

Heroic Traits

Let's look at the more straightforward ones first. Dillettante is a once per game teleport in your movement phase, outside of 9" of the enemy. Solid, nobody regrets a free teleport. Unseemly Vitality heals D3 wounds at the end of each turn and it's fine. Probably good on a Keeper of Secrets with their high wound total but it's not very exciting is it?

The fun one is Centre of Attention. In the Hero phase, choose an enemy unit within 12" of this Hero and on a 3+ that enemy unit has -1 to hit all other friendly units, and all your stuff gets +1 to hit them as long as that hero remains within 12" of the center. Seems very fun on a Keeper of Secrets to draw units in to fight them, or on an infantry hero who is shielded by other friendly units to make them impossible to actually fight.

Artefacts

Bashful Visage is a 6" aura that makes friendly units not visible to enemy units more than 12" away. Put that on a Keeper of Secrets and that's a pretty generous bubble to keep your Archers hidden or to allow some units to move up the field without being shot at.

Crown of Ur-Slaanesh improves upon the Scourge of Ghyran Celebrity Warlord trait and allows you to bring back any friendly unit once per game for a command point. Your stuff is pretty fragile, so a welcome choice.

Finally The Rod of Misrule let's a unit wholly within 12" use Power Through even if their wounds are lower than the target. Definitely helps in a pinch, sometimes you charge and your dice just eat shit and your units are now left in combat and do not want to be there. Why not just leave? Or, reposition your stuff onto an objective after fighting.

While all three artefacts are good, the crown and rod cost points while Bashful Visage is free, so that may affect what you can fit in.

Spell Lore

Hysterical Frenzy (CV6) is your bread and butter unlimited spell, giving a friendly unit wholly within 12" +1 to wound. It's good it's unlimited too, since most of your stuff wounds on a 4+, you'll probably cast this every turn you can.

Last Ecstasy (CV6) is your detonation spell. When a model dies, roll a die and on a 4+ do a mortal wound. If it's a monster, roll 10 dice. Not as good as some similar spells, since it doesn't scale very well (especially for Non-Monster Heroes) but if you know a unit is likely to die soon and have nothing better to cast, why not?

Finally is Overwhelming Acquiescence (CV7) makes a return but instead of +1 to wound (since Hysterical Frenzy does that to 1 unit instead) it gives all attacks Crit (2 Hits) against them instead. With Euphoric gone this is your best way to get Crit 2 hits back mostly consistently.

Credit: Liebot - https://instagram.com/liebot_pics

Units

Like so many Age of Sigmar Battletomes at this point, the Hero section is fat, so we'll break things down a bit. Like most Chaos Battletomes you got your Daemons and your Mortals, but they play along pretty well now and don't have the keyword divide past tomes had. We're just breaking them up for ease of reading, not that you have to focus on one or the other.

Also, for some bookkeeping there are some units banished to Legends This includes the Chariot kit (Herald of Exalted Chariot, Hellflayer and Seeker Chariot) and the much older cavalry unit Hellstriders. They will be valid to play for the next year, but remain here unchanged.

Daemon Monster Heroes

Even dividing the heroes by Daemon and Mortal there's still a ton of Daemons. So let's go into the Monster ones first. So let's start with the arguable posterchild for the Daemon side, Shalaxi Hellbane, who got a bit of an upgrade here. Shalaxi starts as a Paragon and keeps her killer stat block from the Battlepack. Her Paramount Hunter reaction has had its ranged improved to 12", which makes charging near them an even more dangerous proposition as they can counter-charge right into them for free. The warscroll spell gives First Strike, which makes it way more versatile. While they lost their -1 to be hit, that's a lot easier to make up with battle traits, so instead they can retreat and charge, making them far more difficult to lock down. While they lost their Fight Twice ability, they picked up An Exquisite Trophy which fits their Hero hunting flavor a lot better. They get Crit (2 Hits) on a 5+ against a target hero and if they kill them, all friendly units within 12" get +1 to attack. So if they can get an early opening against a Hero, it can push things in your direction and cause a cascading effect.

Similarly, the generic Keeper of Secrets had their -1 to be hit changed to a Retreat and Charge. While their sword lost a swing, it's also 3 damage now, far more befitting of a giant Monster hero. They kept their warscroll spell Cacophonic Choir, but you may choose any number of targets (but it's a -1 to cast for each target past the first), and you don't have to roll above their control characteristic. This makes it a decently flexible ability for throwing out some piddly Mortals, or hitting a few units. It kept Locus of Slaanesh to bring back a dead Daemon unit, which was probably a major reason you brought it. Dark Temptations is back but offers a choice: either offensive buffs (+1 to hit and Crit (2 hits)) or Defensive (-1 to wound and rend for the enemy). Since the +1 damage is built in now, this makes it a really nice well rounded ability that can adapt to different situations whether you think you'll swing first or second.

The twins, Dexcessa and Synessa have been rewritten. Previously, attempts have been made to try and make it so they played off each other, but they're simply too expensive to try and run both so the synergy has been broken and they're arguably much improved for it as it really lets them flex their roles. Dexcessa is the combat sibling, and they are scary. Their new combat profile adds an extra attack and Charge. Fleeting Dance of Death has been changed to the ability to charge out of combat, an incredibly powerful tool that makes them difficult to lock down permanently (especially since she can Fly!), they can follow this up with You! Amuse Us! to let's a Paragon fight after them. Then, Redolence of Violence has been changed for what is arguably the better, rolling 2D6 at end of turn (Adding 2 if they charged) and if they roll higher than the target's health characteristic, automatically slay a model. Send them in to combat, fight a unit, have another friendly Paragon fight, and then Redolence the accompanying enemy hero to wipe out the whole thing.

Synessa wraps things up as the more arcane twin, but more about disrupting your opponent's abilities. They have a warscroll ability where if they can roll higher than an enemy wizard's Power Level, the enemy Wizard gets -1 to cast for the rest of the round and Synessa gets +1 Power Level. They warscroll spell hits enemies with a -1 to attack while a combat ability allows them to inflict -1 to wound on a unit with lower Control that charged Synessa this turn (and they have the requisite 5 Control Monsters do). All in all a very solid debuff control piece that is going to very annoying to deal with.

Also, while I did say the book seems to be moving away from a Dexcess/Synessa team up, there is an Army of Renown for people who still hold a candle for this, and we'll get to that at the end!

The Thricefold Discord. Credit: Keewa

Daemon Infantry Heroes

The Thricefold Discord returns from 3rd edition Purgatory, one of the Underworlds Warbands they elected to rescue and return to Age of Sigmar. It's a rare multi-model Hero unit instead of infantry now and comes with a suite of buffs. The Thricefold Court ability gives one of 3 buffs, of which you must choose a different one each turn (You can pick the same one twice, but not the same twice in a row):
  • -1 to Power Level on visible enemy Wizard or Priest
  • This unit, which comes with built in Crit (2 Hits), scores Crits on 5+
  • A friendly unit wholly within 12" gets 3 extra dice when Rallying.

It also comes with a Warscroll spell which either adds +2 to Movement of a friendly unit wholly within 12", or does X mortal wounds to an enemy unit within 12", where X is the number of models in this unit. A reliable 3 Mortals isn't bad, and who doesn't want Slaanesh to go faster? I quite like this unit.

Next, The Masque has always had a theme, to be a frustrating piece that requires far more resources to kill than it costs to try and slow your opponent in the early game. However, its warscroll has rewritten pretty much every time to try and capture that theme in some novel way and this time is no different. This time it doesn't have a deployment phase teleport, but it does have some very fun movement abilities. In the movement phase it can:

  • Pick an enemy unit within 6" and make all attack rolls from 1-4 automatically fail.
  • Move 2D3", it can dance through enemy units and end in combat, allowing it to bypass chaff and lock down enemy heroes.
  • Shut off commands for a unit within 6" while it is within combat with the Masque

1 and 3 operate similarly, and reducing combat efficiency, and you pretty much can't go wrong with either depending on what the unit in question is. The real clincher is your foes cannot retreat from it, so they really are stuck with it and can't just ignore it. As it always has, at 120 points you're basically taking this banking on the fact that it'll cause an annoyance for opponents far greater cost than it since its kill output is only so-so.

Sylll'esske, the Vengeful Alliance basically copies its Scourge of Ghyran variant, but was made a lot more flexible. Their warscroll spell now lets you target heroes, and their end of turn ability Violent Offerings now includes Unique heroes and Heroes with Enhancements under its umbrella for a permanent Power Level boost. This makes it a lot more versatile in multiple lists than before but it is now a gnarly 250 points which is a hard sell.

The Infernal Enrapturess still is trying to disrupt spellcasters, but does it in a more straightforward way, unbinding spells as if it were a Wizard and dealing D3 Mortals when it does. It's new ability Maestro of Discordance lets you roll a number of dice equal to the number of models in an enemy unit within 18", deal a Mortal for each 6, and then heal or return a number of models equal to the damage dealt. This is honestly pretty solid for dealing with reinforced 20 man blocks, since most armies have to cast a spell to do this - and often without the healing.

Finally the Contorted Epitome occupies a new strange Control role. You can pick an enemy unit within 18" and on a 3+ they become so infatuated they cannot move further away (And can't teleport either). Even better, if they're within 6" this effect is automatic. It also applies to pile-ins, so thanks to their 4+ ward you can do shenanigans to make pile-ins a lot harder. It also gets its own Banish ability, and when successful nearby Wizards get 3D6 to cast a Manifestion. Not bad if you rely on your own manifestations.

Sybarite Heroes

Naturally, let's look at the new hero first, the Lord of Hysteria. A really good companion with Twinsouls/Painbringers the Lord of Hubris may have competition. His Temptation ability gives +1 Attacks to a friendly Sybarite Unit wholly within 12", it's free if they're a Paragon, but still a 3+ if they're not. They also add Anti-Charge to everything wholly within 12", making them excellent at holding a line against charging armies.

Glutos Orscallion has had a ground up rewrite. He is a Paragon, naturally. His combat profile is way, way better now and is actually terrifying, both in shooting and melee absolutely loaded with Damage 3 attacks. His Gorge on Excess can now also bring back models instead of healing, which increases its versatility. The biggest change is that he no longer gives out new buffs each round a la Daughters of Khaine, but has a list of Feasts. He can eat one at the start of either player's turn, but cannot eat the same meal twice so you have to save it for something important. The buffs include:
  • Hide all friendly units wholly within 12" from enemy units more than 12" away.
  • RUN abilities (so Advance and Redeploy, usually) can count 1-3 as a 4.
  • +1 to Unbinding and Banishment rolls for Glutos
  • D6 Mortals on every unit (friend and foe) within combat range.

These buffs are...fine. I don't dislike them, but it's less impactful than a huge aura that gets increasingly better over time. The fact he's  more of a combat monster than before might balance it out.

Sigvald is terrifying. He's a Paragon out of the gate, naturally. He also has Crit (2 Hits) on his warscroll now, meaning he can start swinging out of the box. He also gives it to all friendly units wholly within 12" of him as well, so he's not just an autonomous beat stick anymore. He kept the Strike First when charging, and number of attacks equal to charge roll. He also picked up a temptation ability similar to the Lord of Hubris, where they must dump all attacks into him or take D3+3 Mortals which he then heals. The only thing he lost was that he no longer bypasses wards - but he does cause a -1 to enemy ward rolls which in most cases is enough.

The Lord of Hubris bought more into his gimmick. As before, he can either give an enemy Strike First and they must swing all attacks into the Lord but that now comes with a -1 to hit. Alternatively the enemy can ignore this, but they get Strike-Last and friendly units get +1 to damage against them. This is a temptation ability, so you can't stack it with other debuffs from him so be careful there. Then the Lord gets the standard "I fight, then a friendly unit fights with a +1 to hit" common to Foot Lords now, which makes him even more dangerous to confront. Lord of Hubris was one of the best Heroes in the army and he really just got better.

His cousin the Lord of Pain went back to the drawing board. He picked up an extra rend but worse Wound so, win some lose some. He does have an interesting new ability which lets him inflict 1 mortal wound on another unit in the hero phase and grant them the ability to charge even after Retreating or Running. After losing the Euphoric buff, this is potentially very desirable...if it didn't come with a 3+. Not a complete dealbreaker, but a huge hit to its viability.

Finally the reliable Shardspeaker is your generic foot wizard. Unfortunately its best ability has been hamstrung. The -1 to save is now a CV7 Warscroll spell which requires you to be a Paragon to cast. You can help this out, it has an ability to deal 2 Damage to itself to gain a +2 to cast, but again it's one of those nasty 3+ abilities. Why make you pay a cost and also make it possibly fail?

Credit: RichyP

Infantry

Daemonettes seem to finally have found a useful niche. They get to be Riverblade understudies, moving D3+3" after charging and are able to move through units. They picked up Anti-Infantry for their claw attacks too, so even though they don't get the bonus from charging the ability to use them as a tech piece to snag a foot hero hiding behind chaff is actually a pretty good argument for taking a block of them now, even if the movement is not super reliable.

Probably the biggest sigh of relief is for Fiendbloods. They stole the Scourge of Ghyran warscroll, which was one of the "must take" warscrolls for the army. For the uninitiated, they go into deep strike and can then drop in in either player's movement phase. This makes them a lot more difficult to anticipate and punishes leaving objectives behind, or wizards without something to screen them out from behind. They also get a free 3" move anytime an enemy unit uses a command within 12" (that can end in combat!) and have a solid stat profile to back up their deep strikes.

The Blissbarb Archers moved in a strange new direction. They got their shooting range reduced by 3" to 15", and without Euphoric they don't get access to run and shoot quite so easily. Their tokens now, instead of -1 to be hit by ranged fire, give a 6+ ward and go away on ward rolls of 1. They also picked up a new ability in the shooting phase that gives them Shoot in Combat and Crit (Auto Wound) for the turn, but they don't need to be in combat for that part. It's a very strange suite of abilities that seem to want them to play a bit more aggressively, and even being tagged into combat lets them keep shooting. That said, they're still a 6+ Save 1 wound unit so they likely would collapse very quickly if that were to happen anyway.

Last, let's look at the two beefier infantry. Painbringers and Twinsouls basically got their roles swapped. This sort of makes sense because the faction pack version read like they got the two mixed up in printing and it had to go out like that. I mean, Painbringers have shields! So as such, they picked up the 5+ Ward for not charging, and also punish enemy attacks by dealing a mortal wound every time they roll a one to hit. It's fine, but not as good as -1 attack. The Twinsouls are a bit more unique at being more offense-orientated, they get extra rend if they're damaged or at half size, so that's a bit tricky to navigate as they only have 2 wounds. They also get +1 damage if fighting an enemy with a higher control characteristic (Theirs is 1, so it's going to activate quite a bit).

Cavalry

The 3 surviving Cavalry units all had their speed bumped up to a 14" which is very appreciated. This helps Seekers a lot, since they got a new ability to make a Move at end of turn. You can't pair it with Power Through, but if you don't have the CP to spare, or don't have a valid target, it's one way to get the free movement.

Blissbarb Seekers get a retreat and shoot, which is alright. They're still awfully short range, but with the improved movement and the shorter range of the foot Blissbarbs, there might be a play there. Slickblade Seekers bring some comfortable familiarity by being unchanged, just a solid workhorse infantry.

Beasts

Only one, the Fiends return with their Anti-Magic prowess and got a bit better at it. They still inflict -1 to Cast and Unbind within 12". They no longer inflict Strike-Last, unfortunately, but can inflict D3 bad mortals to every enemy unit within 6" that didn't charge or fight that turn, rerolling if its a Priest or Wizard. This works pretty well as a deterrent to keep buff heroes from chilling outside of combat where they can't be reached.

Endless Spells

The Wheels of Excruciation are now a bit more direct. Their previous ability to deal mortals to enemies they flew over could be difficult to set up, and only really hurt enemies with 5s or 6s to save, which were hordes anyway. Now they get an attack with Charge, with Rend 1 and a proper Wound roll of 2+ now, and deal D6 Mortals to one unit at end of turn. A bit more flexible in what they can hit, but more of a blunt instrument.

The Mesmerising Mirror was simplified, now only dealing Mortals to an enemy unit that doesn't move closer to it. In exchange though, the damage was bumped from D3 to D6, and the range for viable targets was bumped from 12" to 18". Probably the nastiest surprise in there is that it no longer is just triggered in the movement phase but any MOVE ability, including redeploys. Try and redeploy away, now you just took 4 mortal wounds!

Last we have the Dreadful Visage but it's less of a Beat stick now, since the Wheels fill that role. Instead, it can target an enemy unit within 12" in the Hero phase and until it is banished or destroyed they cannot use Run abilities (which includes Redeploys!) and take D6 mortals when they retreat instead of D3.

Overall, this is a great suite of movement control pieces, which are Slaanesh's bread and butter. It makes them feel a lot more clever and versatile in their use and it's a lore I'd happily take along when playing the army.

Terrain

The Terrain piece is...interesting. The Fane of Slaanesh lets you pick an enemy unit in your Hero phase within 18". On a 3+, as long as it stays "in range" of the terrain piece their weapons are treated as Companion weapons. Said range starts at 9" and increases every round.

Giving the Companion trait is an odd approach. In short, it shuts off any positive modifiers to Hit, Wound, Attacks, Rend or Damage unless stated otherwise. This means it's either going to do nothing or absolutely cripple an army. If the enemy army relies on a lot of  buffs to their attacks, then it's going do a lot. If the Warscroll is just generically good (and of course, All Out Attack still works), they may not even notice.

This means you kinda have to know a lot about your opponent's army to know what the best target might be. But hey, it's a terrain piece, it's free, when in doubt just target the scariest thing and hope it helps.

Credit: Charlie A

Rules of Renown

As always we got 2 Armies of Renown and 2 Regiments. In a nice shake up, neither of these are trying to "bring back" mechanics they killed in the Battletome like a lot of other books have been doing and both do their own things.

Army of Renown - Decadent Host

Decadent Host is for those who prefer mortals, and importantly, want Sigvald to really shine.

In the Decadent Host you can bring:

  • Sigvald
  • Any Sybarite unite that isn't Glutos
  • Up to 1 Daemonette unit
  • Up to 1 Contorted Epitome

Surprisingly, Sigvald is not a mandatory take but everything in the army assumes he is involved and wants to make him as cool as possible so you will take him.

Perfection Made Manifest lets Sigvald deal D3 mortals to a unit he's in combat with in the Hero phase, then for each model slain from this ability, add 2 to his attack characteristic. This is mostly to help him if he gets bogged down in chaff the previous turn and needs a way to boost his attacks since he may not be able to charge back out. During the combat phase he has Petulant Wrath to deal D6 mortals to a unit he's in combat with, then reposition in combat with that unit, allowing him to move to a better striking point (say right into the hero right behind them?). He also has an eye-watering 2+ Ward bodyguard rule near Sybarites and Infernal Handmaidens is a cute ability to boost his Daemonette Groupies, giving them +1 to wound while they're within 3" of him. With their 4+ to wound that is exactly what they need. Finally, if at the end of the Turn he's not on an objective he can trigger Important People Have Important Places To Be, and move 2D6" as long as he ends on an objective. Sigvald does not waste his time on Rabble.

Even if after all that he dies, he gets to come back in your movement phase, wholly within 6" of the Contorted Epitome. This bypasses 2 usual rules, one he doesn't have to be outside of 9" of the enemy, just outside of combat, and he can do this multiple times.

Onto the enhancements, they still are all about Sigvald looking awesome. The Heroic Trait Protege lets Sigvald return to the battlefield after being destroyed as if that hero was a Contorted Epitome. Huge for getting him back into the front lines and not respawning back at base. The Mirror Plate artefact reduces the bearer's Attacks to 1, but Sigvald's Control jumps to 20. Put it on something you don't much care about whether or not it fights, like a wizard.

The two spells only work on Sigvald. Diverting Excruciation heals him for D3 while Visions of Eternal Bliss let him crit on 5+s. And then you have access to the Slaanesh Manifestions but those aren't for buffing Sigvald so who cares?

What's the verdict? Seems good! Like, it's going to be deceptively difficult to pilot well, because while it turns Sigvald into an absolutely terror that many lists will not be able to mess with, it doesn't really do anything else for the rest of the army. Most of the Slaanesh units have pretty poor baseline stats, so they will struggle to take down anything of significant power without buffs. It's basically just playing the Sigvald game and hoping for the best, but that said this does make him very strong and I do not want to undersell how scary he will be to deal with if opponent doesn't have proper ways to delay him.

Army of Renown - Court of the Godlings

The other Army of Renown tries to get both Synessa and Dexcessa together as a power duo. Lists can include:

  • Synessa (Must be included)
  • Dexcessa (Must be included)
  • Any non-unique Hedonites Daemon Unit
  • Dexcessa and Synessa must be in seperate regiments

You're probably wondering what's up with that last one. The vibe of the Army is that Synessa and Dexcessa are in a constant sibling rivalry and when one does better than the other, the other tries to compensate, and that includes the units in their regiment.

The first ability The Voice and the Talon gives both twins the Twin keyword. This is mostly to clean up book keeping, since every ability going forward references a "Twin" so it's just a faster way to say "Synessa or Dexcessa".

The More Glorious Slaughter gives you an idea of what we're in for. At the start of the turn, if one Twin destroyed a unit in the previous turn and the other did not, the one who did not (and their entire regiment) gets +1 to hit for the whole turn. With The Longer Shadow if one twin is contesting an objective you control and the other isn't, the twin who is not's regiment gets +5 to control scores.

Once either twin is damaged, The Grand Visage kicks in, giving the less wounded twin a 4+ Ward. In the Charge Phase The Swifter Surge makes it so if one twin charged last turn and the other didn't, the one who didnt's regiment gets 3D6 charge. For everyone!

Finally, The More Cunning Ruse breaks tradition so that if they're both within 12" of each other at the end of the turn, one can teleport away. Stealing an objective or just getting to safety, leaving the twin with the 4+ ward to fight.

The heroic trait Tethered to the Twins lets you bring back one of the dead twins within 9" of the bearer of their trait, similar to Sigvald's heroic trait. The Triplet Trinket gives -1 to be hit to a Twin and the bearer if they're within 3" of that twin, and adds -1 to wound if both Twins are within 3" of the bearer.

There are 3 spells, plus the manifestations. Essence Transfusion lets you deal damage to one of the twins (any amount, your pick!) and then heal the other twin for twice that amount. This is basically a way to shuffle wounds around if one twin is getting very badly injured, or tip the balance so the one you want is getting the 4+ Ward. Sibilant Whispers lets you pick an enemy unit within 18" and as long as that unit can see one twin they halve their movement, and if they can see both they only get to roll 1D6 for charges. Finally Ecstatic Furore adds +1 to wound to a unit's combat attacks. Not even just the twins, any unit. Very appreciated since many units in Slaanesh are rocking 4+ Wound rolls.

I love this detachment. It's extremely fun creating a mini game where you want to balance out using both twins, tapping them in and out to keep the other one motivated. It will involve a lot of tracking, so have tokens, but it's such a great idea and I think both Armies of Renown were a huge success here in finding a play pattern that feels so wildly outside the core book.

Regiments of Renown

Both Regiments of Renown can be taken by any Chaos army that isn't Khorne. First up is Mistclad Revelers. This includes:

  • 1 Shardspeaker of Slaanesh
  • 10 Blissbarb Archers

This is extremely straightforward. They simply can enter in reserves and then deep strike in. Big problem, and I'm not sure if this is an oversight, but the Shardspeaker needs to be a Paragon to use its warscroll spell. Which mean it effectively does nothing. The warscroll spell, if usable, might be a mark toward using this but as it stands you get a mediocre shooting unit in a grand alliance with some very strong shooting armies.

The other is The Accursed Reflection which includes:

  • 1 Contorted Epitome
  • 1 Wheels of Excruciation
  • 1 Mesmerising Mirror
  • 1 Dreadful Visage

Now, as written you do get to deploy with all the manifestations on the field, but they will probably get Banished in short order due to the +2 your opponent is getting to remove them. Still, that's castings wasted to deal with them. Meanwhile the Contorted Epitome is here to bring them back if they're gone.

I actually think there's some play here. The Mesmerising Mirror and the Dreadful Visage are both really strong control pieces. The Mesmerising Mirror forces opponents to go in directions they don't want or take some mortal wounds, while Dreadful Visage locks them down and makes it more difficult to leave combat.

Final Thoughts

I really like this book. While the loss of Temptation Dice at first felt like a real blow to the Army's flavor, I think the new stuff is really engaging. It creates an army with a very technical, control-y flavor that will probably take a lot of time to master all of it's nuances. Very appropriately if it lines up correctly, it's going to feel absolutely ecstatic.
