Getting Started with Star Wars Legion: Shadow Collective

July 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone, to another episode in our Star Wars Legion series, where we cover the fundamentals of the game! This time, we're taking a look at one of the core factions of the game, the Shadow Collective.

Thank you to Asmodee for sponsoring a series of articles on Star Wars: Legion.

What Is the Shadow Collective?

Ever since Obi Wan pulled a fruit ninja on him on Naboo, Maul, formerly Darth, was fueled by hatred and plotted and schemed on how to get vengeance. Knowing Obi Wan had previous "friendship" with Duchess Satine of Mandalore, he worked with her opposition, the Deathwatch. In an honor duel for the ruling seat of Mandalore, Maul slew Pre Vizla and took the Darksaber and command of Deathwatch. Bo-Katan Kryze, sister to Satine, took those loyal to her and ran to the Republic for assistance. This entire plot had been crafted to lure Obi-Wan Kenobi to lead troops to Mandalore, where Maul would get his vengeance. 

All the while, Maul had been collaborating (more coercing) other alien crime families like Crimson Dawn, Black Sun, and the Pyke Syndicate, to work together and reinforced smuggling lines for spice and weapons across the Outer Rim and the Galaxy. Together with Pirates, and those Mandalorians of Clan Saxon loyal to Maul, the Shadow Collective is a scary force to bring to bear on the tabletop.

Credit: Atomic Mass Games

Essential Media

Episode 1: The Phantom Menace: Dark and mysterious, Darth Maul is introduced as Darth Sidious’s apprentice, hunting Jedi Qui-Gon Jinn and a young Obi Wan Kenobi as they discover a child with an extremely high midichlorian count, Anakin Skywalker. He’s not a pivotal character until the last 20 minutes of the movie, and that 2v1 is some of the best lightsaber fighting this side of Otoh Gunga. Not to mention Duel of the Fates, John Williams you beautiful bastard. 

The Clone Wars: “From the dead, an old enemy has awoken, seeking vengeance." - Master Yoda, TCW. For fans of the aura-farming zabrak from that one movie with the gungans, he returns in one of the most fleshed out story arcs throughout multiple seasons. We see how his hatred kept him alive while living off of scraps, and his rise from a throw away villain to a fleshed out crime boss and leader of a Mandalorian uprising. The Clone Wars is a must watch, just for Maul’s story alone, and he’s one of my favorite characters till this day.

Maul: Shadow Lord: In the still hot ashes of the Republic, we see an older and wiser Maul struggling to keep his crime empire together while keeping himself and his followers out of the spotlight from the real Empire. This show also shows a great dynamic of Jedi post Order 66, and how they became allies of convenience with one of the greatest dual saber wielders. The show is great fan service for fans of Maul, myself included, and I’m excited to see where they take season 2. 

How Do They Play?

Shadow Collective is an interesting bunch, and interact differently than the Big 4 (Republic, CIS, Empire, Rebels) in a lot of ways. For starters, most -- and by most I mean 75% of your units -- have Independent: [Action], meaning if they do not have a face up order token, they get free tokens at the start of the activation phase. Those units want to be played out of the Order Pool, unless it's a big turn where you can give them tokens with command cards or they don’t need to move. 

Maul is also a beast with his Juyo Mastery, able to make three actions (no more than two moves and one attack) and coming with a host of cracked command cards. Be careful with him, however; I’ve had him die to a WL0-5 before I could use Witch Magik to heal him next turn.

Shadow Collective - Credit Bair

Strengths

Independent: As previously mentioned, most Shadow Collective units have Independent: [Action], usually Aim 1 or Dodge one. Organic token generation without spending actions is incredibly strong and elevates the attack and defense of your units, as long as you don’t give them orders. 

Strong Units: Even without command card abilities, the dice pools of the Black Sun Enforcers, Pyke Syndicate Foot Soldiers, and even your Weequay Pirates are no slouch. The Pirates’ pistols also have the keyword Versatile, meaning they can make ranged attacks out of melee at different units. Mandalorian Super Commandos are also great units at Range 2 to melee, and are quite tanky with red saves and surging to block build in. 

Weaknesses

Poor Courage: Seems to be a common negative across the galaxy, and the mercenaries are also afflicted. Most units have 1 courage, meaning they panic after being shot at from a single Suppressive weapon. Both Pykes and Black Suns can increase their bravery by 1 by taking the Capo or Vigo respectively, but 

Affiliations: To piggy back off of poor courage, all of your corp unit choices have Self Preservation. That means they can only use the bravery of other commanders with the same affiliation, and Maul does not have the same affiliation as a Black Sun Enforcer squad. Depending on which alien crime family you lean into, it’s worth it to bring the Pyke Capo and Black Sun Vigo as individual units to support your units with a bravery bubble. The leaders aren’t super strong, but it's a 42 or 50 point unit to prevent your others from panicking.

Bag Game Play: This one isn't so much of a weakness, but it is definitely a learning curve for newer players to Mercenaries and those with the Independent keyword. At the core of Legion, you want to give your units order so they can do things, and it's difficult to willingly choose to let your unit's order go into the Order Pool. This can lead to out activations out of order than how you wanted them to go, but it comes with experience on when to give Independent units an order vs let it go to the Order Pool. The concept of Order Control can still apply in Shadow Collective, specifically if you keep your bag to 6 Corps tokens and order everything else.

How Do I Get Into Them?

This one is going to be a little difficult. As of writing, there is no “Starter Box” for Shadow Collective. There is the Leaders of the Shadow Collective box which includes Gar Saxon Commander, Maul Operative (with Dark Saber upgrade to make him commander) and Rook Cast unit upgrade. The only other units that are available to purchase from AMG are the WL0-5 Tank, Mandalorian Super Commandos, Weequay Pirates, and the AA-5 Speeder Bus.

What Else Do They Have?

Outside of Pirates and Mandalorians, the Syndicate operates on spice, and the Pyke Syndicate and Black Suns are most known to dabble in the trade of the sparkly dust. 

Black Suns are some of the hardest hitting infantry in the game with that Det-Pack Enforcer, and their Scatter Gun, while expensive, does wonders with that Pierce 1. Pyke Syndicate Foot Soldiers are also really survivable, with an Independent: Dodge 1, a built in Outmaneuver (use dodges on crit results) and Danger Sense: 2 (adding extra save dice for each suppression on them). They can also bring an Electro-Whip to shock and immobilize your opponents and take their lunch money. 

I do want to mention Swoop Bike Riders, my beloved. Fast and hard hitting, they zip across the board and can make Overrun attacks on units the move tool or base passes over. Also, with the new models for our Bounty Hunter friends, both Bossk and Cad Bane can be run in Shadow Collective as Operatives.

Disclaimer however, Pyke Syndicate, Black Sun Enforcers, and even Swoop Bikes are not available for purchase from AMG directly. If you can still find copies at your FLGS, by all means, they are entirely worth it. Those models are still good, even if the cards are not, and you can always print off the updated cards on AMG’s website. If you can’t, you can always live out your inner mercenary life and haggle with others on 2nd hand resell lots, and hope your luck doesn't run dry.

Battle Forces

Currently, the only reason Shadow Collective works as a playable army is through a Battle Force. They are technically a “Mercenary” faction, being made up of entirely Mercenary Units, but do get a few special rules to make them function as a “cohesive” force on the table. If you want to learn more about the Shadow Collective Battle Force rules and other Battle Forces, check out here: Star Wars Legion: Intro to Battle Forces - Tabletop Battles

However, if you like grabbing loot and making credits, do I have the Mercenary Battle Force for you; The Ohnaka Gang. Remember that charismatic pirate Hondo from TCW? He gets to lead a battle force of Weequay Pirates, WL0-5 Speeder Tank, and Swoop Bikes, and it’s a ton of fun. A little pricey on the real world model cost, mostly because you can run 4, count ‘em 4, WL0-5 Tanks! I’ve been having a ton of fun with Ohnaka Gang, and I can count on two hand the number of times my opponents have gasped when I’ve said I’m bringing 4 tanks to the game. I smell profit!

Final Thoughts

Maul is a good enough reason to play Shadow Collective alone, and that has been my driving factor to bringing him to the tabletop. His seedy band of gangs and mercenaries can be a ton of fun when you just give Maul orders every turn, and gamble on who to activate next from the Order Pool. 

While you still can cobble an army together from what's available currently, a lot of the harder hitting units in Shadow Collective are still waiting for their unit reprint. This makes it harder for newer players to get started with this amazing faction, either forcing them to look to 2nd hand used armies or 3rd party proxy models. Fingers crossed those repacks are coming soon, I’m tired of borrowing my friend’s Swoop Bike Riders for events, even if his models manage to perform better than mine.

Hope to see you all across the table, and if I do, be scared of Maul.
