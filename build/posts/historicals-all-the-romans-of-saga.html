Historicals: All the Romans of SAGA

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With SAGA: Age of Caesars finally here, it's time to tackle the thorniest problem in buying and playing Roman miniatures: the bewildering diversity of Romes! We have, depending on your definition of Roman, five SAGA Ages where you can find a Roman Army list, and each one could (or should) have different miniatures associated with it. Luckily, if a miniatures manufacturer makes Historicals, it very very probably makes some Romans. Imperial, Republican, and Late Romans are massively popular ranges, so get out there and find the exact minis you want by trawling through all of our favourite sites. If you're diving into the classical world for the first time with Age of Caesars it can be a very confusing mix of loricas, legions and ages, so let's dive in and look at which miniatures match each battle board.

Age of Hannibal - Republican Romans

Most of us probably had our first encounter with Republican Roman armies through Rome: Total War, and, if you did, the cries of Hastati! Principes! Triarii! will be burned into your brain. From around 300BC to 100BC, the Roman army fought virtually everyone in the Mediterranean - Carthage, the Greek states, other Italians - until a slow organisational evolution transformed it into something much closer to what we usually think of as the Legion. This army was, to a great extent, stratified by social class, with three ranks of Legion infantry, wealthier cavalry and allied Infantry. Equipment and armour slowly changed throughout the period, with Greek-style Bronze Cuirasses and Italian short swords replaced by chainmail and the classic Gladius and Pilum over the 2nd century BC. Soldiers had to supply their own equipment though, so presumably this would have been fairly heterodox throughout!

Hastati, with a carthaginian head. From Republican Romans (1) Credit: Lenoon

Age of Hannibal features The Republic of Rome, a flexible board with a balance of offensive and defensive abilities with a strong focus on units of warriors. Your army must have more units of warriors on foot than levies, and more than twice the number of warriors on foot than hearthguard. Nicely, this sticks very close to the organisation of the Republican army, giving you a thematic and accurate-for-SAGA force.

Velites - Conscripts from the (near) poorest class of Romans, lightly and inexpensively armed. They were deployed as light infantry, and in SAGA terms will end up being your Javelin-equipped Levy.

Hastati - The younger conscripts, the Hastati (literally "spear bearers") were deployed at the front line of the legion. Armed with spears (later, pila), they would most likely have had a pectoral plate, helmet and shield as their major protection. These are your Warriors on Foot.

Principes - Slightly older (20s to 30s) conscripts, the Principes were the second line of the legion, most likely armed in a very similar way to the Hastati. These are more Warriors on Foot.

Triarii - The oldest cohort (30s to 40s), the Triarii were a heavy reserve force rather than the all-conquering spear gods they end up in the Total War series. These are your Hearthguard on Foot.

Equites - The cavalry of the Roman Republic was provided by those rich enough to support their own horse, forming the upper levels of the conscripted citizenry. They were paid more, treated better and died in occasionally society-destabilising numbers. They operated as heavy cavalry, with greek-style breastplates, small shields and spears or hefty two-handed lances. In Age of Hannibal, you can have one unit of Warriors on Horses.

Socii - The Socii were allied and tributary soldiers, called upon by the Republic to bolster the Roman line. When called from Italian states it's probable that they operated and were equipped much the same as the legion - but why not go a little further afield and use other classical age models with different kit? These would be a good place to represent Mercenary Units. 

Where to Buy Republican Romans

Lots of manufacturers make Republican Romans, due to the all-powerful allure of refighting the Punic Wars. The easiest place to go though, is Victrix, for the simple and convincing reason that you can pick up a single bag of miniatures and you're done. Victrix makes three Republican Infantry sets - Rome's Legions of the Republic 1, 2 and Allied Italian Legions. These are, more or less, the same set with different armour. All three allow you to make a rounded SAGA list for Age of Hannibal, with 36 Legionaries that can be Hastati or Principes (with 8 Triarii thrown in there too), 18 Velites and a smattering of command. The differences are in armour styles. If you want to model the First Punic War pick up Legions of the Republic 2 (pectoral armour), the second Punic War go for Legions 1 (chainmail), and if you'd like to look less strictly Roman and more like one of the other Italian city states, go for Allied Legions.

Victrix Republican Romans - Credit: Victrix

One bag will get you an entire SAGA army, but if you'd like to go further and bring along a few more options, Victrix also do Republican Cavalry which forms 1.5 points of Warriors.

Age of Caesars - Many Romes, One Emperor (Sometimes)

The Age of Caesars spans from slightly before there was a Caesar to around the 3rd century AD, and while the Roman army did vary and change significantly over the period, this is the classic, classical, image of the Roman Legion. Roman Citizens served as legionaries, eventually as a volunteer, professional army, with non-citizen soldiers serving as Auxilia and soldiers from outside the boundaries of the empire forming units of Numeri. Age of Caesars includes the Imperial Roman Army (around 30BC to somewhere in the 3-400s), the Caesarian/Late Republican Legions (slightly earlier) and the Palmyran Romans (270-273AD) Over the period, the equipment of Legionaries slowly changed, though reduced to 28mm scale these changes are mostly minor.

Romans! (credit: Ilor)

Legionaries - Organised into centuries (~80 fighting men) and cohorts (480), the Legionaries were heavily armed, very heavily armoured and fought predominantly as infantry. They were the hard core of the Roman army, and are represented by Legionaries in Age of Caesars.

Auxilia - Auxilia were non-citizen residents of the Roman empire, who could win citizenship by surviving a 25 year term (god knows what % of recruits managed that!). They provided archers, slingers, cavalry, javelinmen, elephants, and spearmen to the Army. They will be Warriors (No special equipment, mounted, ranged, and elephant) and occasionally Levy in Age of Caesars.

Numeri - Hired/pledged soldiers from beyond the empire, Numeri were armed with whatever their group usually had! This is a good opportunity to pick up some non-roman units for use as Mercenaries, Levy or Warriors. 

Where to Buy Caesarian Romans

This is where the armour nerdery kicks in! Late Republican and Caesar's legions (Julius connotation) were organised similarly to the Imperial Legions (Augustus onward connotation). The vast majority of Mini manufacturers use a shorthand visual difference: Chainmail vs Segmented armour.

Warlord Caesarian Romans

Chainmail, the Lorica hamata, was increasingly common in Roman armies throughout the Second Century BC, becoming standard equipment by around 120BC. It is used all the way through the Imperial Roman period, so picking up chainmail-armoured legionaries will do you from 100BC to 300AD, if you can ignore that most Caesarian Romans have Coolus helmets (you can). Warlord Games have a nice box of Legionaries in lorica hamata with Julius Caesar era helmets which will make you exactly 4 points of Legionaries. For Ranged or Mounted Warriors and Levy, head back to Victrix, who have an excellent range of Auxilia in Cavalry, Archer and Infantry forms. If you want to pick up some metals to go along with these, Wargames Foundry have a fantastically characterful range where you can pick up some incredibly grizzled Centurions.

Where to Buy Imperial Romans

The classic legionary image, the Imperial Romans represent the height of the Pax Romana, the period of Roman-enforced economic and cultural hegemony over the European and North African world. In real life it was likely equipment was never quite as homogenous as on the table, but why not lean into it! The lorica segmentata, the banded/segmented armour that endures as the most famous image of the Roman legionary, is introduced some time around the BC/AD border, and becomes at least fairly widespread quite quickly. Accompanying it is the Imperial Helmet, oblong scutum (shield) and heavy pilum, making for an Asterix-style image of Roman equipment perfection!

Hell yeah, Victrix Romans (credit: Ilor)

Imperial Romans are available virtually everywhere miniatures are sold, but again, if you want that single bag draw, Victrix' new Imperial Roman Warband gets you a full 6 point army as long as you want all-Legionaries and a ballista! Otherwise, Victrix Early Imperial Roman Legionaries (Advancing/Attacking) each get you 4 points of Legionaries, which can be easily supported via Auxilia for ranged or mounted Warriors and Levies.

Where to Buy Palmyran Romans

The Palmyrans provide a slightly different take on Roman equipment, with the short lived indepedent Palmyran Empire arising out of a slow disintegration of centralised control over the empire during the 3rd Century. They're styled in SAGA as a blend of Eastern and Roman battle boards, which likely goes at least some way to representing the complex blend of identities that emerged, and supported independence, out on the Eastern fringe of the Empire. While Palmyra didn't last very long, there's a surprising amount of miniatures available. While the kitbasher might want to pick up some Imperial Roman kits alongside Persian or Sassanid plastics, you probably want to go with Armorum and Aquila Miniatures who have a solid range, while Essex Miniatures have a good range of Persians and Romans to mix and match from - though beware these are 25mm rather than 28.

Age of Invasions - Romans

Age of Invasions picks up story with the Romans in SAGA, with a focus on the late 3rd to 5th century AD. Age of Invasions portrays the Roman Empire at bay, holding on where it can as the systems underpinning the western half of the empire begin to fall apart. This happened in different places at different times, Age of Invasions slightly squashes this together into a single battle board with a strong focus on momentum through the Impetus ability. It's a very defensive board that rewards planning and organisation, which is why I can't get my head around it at all.

The late Roman army was split into smaller, more flexible, units, with the Legions - once mighty if cumbersome behemoths - brought into line with the smaller Auxilia legions. Over the period Age of Invasions depicts, the idea of the "Border Force", the Limitanei, became more prominent, with garrisons holding down an increasingly tenuous border. Equipment differentiation begins to grade into each other and Roman armies become less standardised and more influenced by their local area. Trousers replace tunics and kilts, especially on colder borders, hamata returns to replace the segmentata and older helmet styles are replaced by ridge, nasal and cavalry helmets. The armies become increasingly regional and complex, but can be very roughly classed into:

Comitatenses and Limitanei - The inheritors of the Legionary heavy infantry, who fought with darts, sword and typically oval shield. Comitatenses refers to soldiers of the mobile armies (as contrasted to the Limitanei, the garrison forces), who were possibly a bit better equipped, armed and trained, used as a trouble shooting (literally) force when customs disputes, raids and migrations overwhelmed the Limtanei. Ranging from Heavy Infantry to medium, ranged or non ranged, These are your Warriors, Warriors with Bows and potentially Levy.

Equites - The Late Roman period saw a resurgence of Heavy Cavalry, back to prominence after the Legionary core of the Imperial period was seen to be too slow to react to the various border threats. These could vary from Legionary-style cavalry all the way up to Cataphracts, incredibly heavily armoured cavalrymen clad head to hoof in mail. In Age of Invasions, these form your Hearthguard on Horse and Hearthguard Cataphracts

Foederati - Troops recruited from treaty-bound nations and tribes around the edges of the Empire. Goths, Saxons, Franks and the like could all go from sometime mercenaries to treaty partners to enemies fluidly! Foederati (which doesn't mean Foe as in enemy, interestingly), were often groups of "barbarian" mercenaries with whatever equipment the Romans could hire at the time. These could be all sorts of units in SAGA terms, from just about anywhere in the late Roman world - Hearthguard could make your army represent one of the "Barbarian" Emperors, Warriors could be a mix of elite soldiers and foreign specialists, levy would show a mass conscription to meet a crisis and mercenaries can add some unusual rules and models to an otherwise standard force.

Where to Buy Late Romans

Armoured Goth warriors, who will also look good as late romans. Credit: Lupe

Another area where everyone makes models, Late Romans have the huge advantage of being fairly heterogenous in terms of kit and equipment, so mixing ranges has a huge pull. Victrix have a lovely late Roman set that will do you well but I am a relentless kitbasher so my eye is drawn to the Wargames Atlantic Decline and Fall range. There's quite a few kits here - two types of Roman armour, Goths, an army builder set we reviewed positively, and light cavalry. The exciting bit is that all of these come with the same arm, waist and head joints, so you can buy a ton of different kits, chop all the bits off the sprue and kitbash anything you like, from very regular Romans to foederati armed with whatever's left in the bargain bin of the Constantinople storehouses. Great fun!

Age of Vikings - Last Romans

After 300 years of slowly, and then quickly, falling apart, the Romans of Age of Invasions, battered and bloody, crop up again as the Last Romans in Age of Vikings. This is a good bit of confusing naming, because by some accounts they're not "Roman" - Rome is ruled by someone very very different at this point - and they're certainly not the last. The Last Romans is a combined arms board in an era of charging at the enemy waving a sword about, using heavy shooting power and reserve forces of hearthguard and warriors to kill whatever makes it through. Your warriors, hearthguard and levy all have access to ranged weapons, so you can shoot, feint and strategise your way through the barbarians beyond the Theodosian walls.

Byzantine cavalry. So hot right now. Credit - Rick H

The Byzantine army went through almost as many rejigs as the Classical Roman one did, but from this point on the Legions are long gone, and the core unit of organisation is much more feudal - the Theme. Themes (Themata) were regional centres with associated armies, largely concentrated in Anatolia as the last true bastion of Eastern Roman power that could plausibly withstand something like "a load of people turn up and start burning things down". The standing army, as opposed to whatever guys the Empire could hire, was organised in the Tagmata system, slightly smaller and weaker individually than the legions. The age of Vikings board represents an Eastern Empire forced onto the defensive, carving up territory into more easily defended subdivisions, waiting for the last ride of the Emperors across the Balkans (spoiler for Age of Crusades there!). Your armies will be made of:

Tagmata Soldiers - Your Hearthguard, mounted on horses and blending centuries of lessons learned from Cataphracts and Eastern horsemen, these are very clearly a transition between the heavily armed shock cavalry of the late empire and the mail armoured proto-knight we know from Norman and Carolingian sources. Last Roman hearthguard can hit like a brick, but there's always very few of them!

Welcome also to the hearthguard club the Varangian Guard! Finally, the Eastern Empire gets in on the big hairy guys with big axes trend with the traveling mercenary/bodyguards/kingmakers/Western Europeans of the Varangians. Armed with Heavy weapons, they form a hard hitting deterrent for anyone trying to charge your archers.

Theme Soldiers - These are your bog-standard warriors, who may or may not be professional fighters. They're armed with spears, shields, darts and lamellar armour, looking very much like a continuation of the Late Roman soldier, or bows. These form your warriors.

Psiloi - Light skirmishers, retaining all the low status and basic equipment of their ancestors. These add, in sufficient numbers, serious short and medium ranged firepower to the Last Romans, in addition to being generally annoying and skirmishing out front. These are your levy.

Where to Buy Last Romans

Byzantine Mounted Archers from Footsore.

These Last Romans can still be represented with Late Roman troops so everything above applies. If you want to stick to plastic, mix up Late Roman and Byzantine (see below) plastics to add some heterodox themes. Otherwise, you’re looking for Early Byzantine miniatures, or suitably rough looking Byzantines. Footsore, Gripping Beast and Aventine all have large ranges.

Age of Crusades - Byzantines

A Byzantine army is a formidable sight. Credit - Rick H, London SAGA Mead Hall

The Byzantine army age of crusades lets you take your Eastern Romans right up to the bitter end, with a board that brings elements of Invasions and Vikings with it. You’ll need to plan ahead with Domestikos, rely on close cooperation between units and try to keep your warriors alive with stacks of defence dice as long as possible. All the while you want to be harassing, moving and firing and generally annoying the slower latins and avoiding the faster Islamic armies. To pull that off, you have the 11th century Byzantine army to help you:

Hearthguard - Mounted lancers that might look suspiciously like Normans or Anglo Danes on Balkan horses, because they absolutely were! Mixed in there will be retainers and Eastern Empire equipped knight-equivalents, but a man in mail on a horse can be from anywhere! Varangians still hang around, and receive a massive influx of Anglo Dane nobility after 1066.

Warriors - Again, mostly mounted (probably more in the game than in life!), these are the final evolution of late Roman cavalry, merged with the tactics and experiences of fighting Islamic armies on the Anatolian border.

Levy - Still Psiloi, these are the farmers, smallholders, townsmen and occasional professional foot soldiers that your Doux has levied from his immediate area.

There’s still a lot of evolution to go even in this late period. After taking a massive battering at the battle of mamzikert, the Eastern Roman Empire has its (almost) last hurrah just before the Crusades. The reforms of the Kommennos killed off the last lingering remnants of the themes and legions, replacing them with something that for our purposes looks much like the medieval armies of a century or two later: professional soldiers, feudal retainers and specialist mercenaries. The redesigned Byzantine Army manages to take back a bit of the old imperial stomping ground of the Balkans, and even calls in the wild and uncouth successors to the western empire for a bit of religious warfare the emperor is absolutely sure he can control. I’m not sure it was quite as all mounted as the SAGA board make out, but lighter troops and mounted archers were definitely a key part of the army. While it’s on the way out, the Empire still manages another three hundred and a bit years in one form or another, but here we have the last gasp of the SAGA Romans.

Where to Buy Byzantines

You might not need many, but it's always good to have some spearmen. Byzantine Spearmen from Fireforge Miniatures.

Byzantines have a bewildering amount of representation in metal miniatures, but for plastics go with Fireforge. Some of the sculpts might be a little chunky, but there's a very solid range here of infantry, light and heavy cavalry and - for now - they're the only people in the market!

If you want to go down the Byzantine armies as a collection of troops on retainer, you can go to Victrix for Norman and Islamic Infantry - mixing up the sets will make for some great Anatolian troops  Armenians, and Constantinople-based Normans.

Oops! Too Many Romes

Of course, this is history - and more than that, it's Roman history - so nothing is ever that simple. There are far, far more Romes than that. On a very generous basis you can make a claim for a whole lot more, so in the interests of completeness here's a very, very brief line on just some of the major contenders for, "Oh man there's too many Romes":

Age of Invasions - Goths

Are the Romans the people who rule Rome, the leaders educated by Rome or the people who claim to be the Roman Emperors? Whatever the case, Theodoric and the Ostrogoths have a solid claim to it. Ruling over Italy - and claiming the Imperial title - from 493 to 526, Theoderic recieved the previously defunct vestments of the Western Imperial throne from the Eastern Emperor, making the Ostrogoths another Roman board in all but name. This board is all combat all the time, representing a wave of experienced troops crashing into already exhausted enemies.

Age of Vikings - Carolingians

Another hairy guy who technically kind of ruled a version of the Roman Empire, Charlemagne and the Carolingians certainly told everyone they were the inheritors of the Roman Empire, helped by the fact dodgy Pope Leo III crowned him as Emperor in 800AD. The Carolingian board is all about organisation, striking at weak points and overwhelming in detail. The key ability Proelium acts a little like Late Roman impetus, a nice thematic link to the inheritance Charlemagne claimed.

Age of Chivalry - Papacy

If the Roman Emperor lives in Rome, is richer than God and runs a world spanning empire from an Italian throne, then the papacy is surely the western Roman Empire by any other name? The Papal States turn up in Age of Chivalry as a variant for the Condotierri board, where the unique quirk is a lot of shooting and the ability to reroll SAGA dice en masse.

Age of Chivalry - Germany

is the Roman Empire whatever people say is the Roman Empire? I’m not going to make the classic joke but if so, the Germans of the age of Chivalry are the latest - and last -  Rome in SAGA. The German board inherits elements of the Gothic one in Invasions - tire your enemy out, manage your own fatigue and finally win the argument of what’s Roman, Holy and an Empire when everyone else is too tired to carry on!

No, No More Romes

It's really quite fantastic that SAGA lets you play Rome all the way from the Punic War to the 4th Crusade, with boards that work to at least the vague feel and theme of what was happening in the Empire at the time. There are - of course - many more SAGA boards that could claim to be Roman, without even touching on the Sultanate of Rum, Romania, the mess that is Britain and a succession of new-Romes (nationalist connotation). But, with a Rome for nearly every age, the gauntlet is down - who among you can fight to become the ultimate Augustus from 300Bc to 1450AD, and keep the flame of the Romanitas burning on the SAGA tables?
