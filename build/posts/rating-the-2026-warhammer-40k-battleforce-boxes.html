Rating the 2026 Warhammer 40K Battleforce Boxes

November 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Games Workshop has prepared a selection of big honkin' Battleforces, just in time for the holidays. These are typically big collections of slightly older models, offered at a moderate discount and presenting a solid start to a new army, or a healthy expansion of an existing one. We'll break down each of these sets, figuring out how much you're saving, how useful they are, and roughly how many points they total up to.

Space Marines - Indomitus Task Force ($255)

Contents:

  • 1 Captain in Terminator Armour
  • 1 Chaplain
  • 10 Intercessors
  • 5 Assault Intercessors with Jump Packs
  • 5 Terminators
  • 1 Redemptor Dreadnought

Contents MSRP: $384

Value saved: $129

Points Cost: 775

This set serves as a solid core to a Marine force of just about any flavor, with most of its constituent pieces finding their way into nearly every Marine army - be it casual or competitive. The Terminator Captain is a good fit for the associated Terminator squad, a good thing since Terminators are rather good right now. Conversely, the Chaplain's sort of an odd inclusion. He can only attach to the squad of Intercessors who don't really want to be in melee, but this isn't a Combat Patrol, so it's not like it's meant to be played out of the box anyway. He can still find a home in plenty of other units should your Marine army have any dedicated melee dudes. Those Intercessors are kind of struggling to find a place now that they don't have Objective Secured, but Intercessors waver in and out of viability often enough that they'll find a place into armies again before long. Jump Pack Intercessors are a great scoring and bullying piece, and the Redemptor is a nice anchor, providing a beefy midfield brawler. This set is a solid start to a Marine army or a great group of reinforcements for an existing one, even if most Marine players already have Intercessors falling out of their ears from past releases. Get this and the Chapter upgrade sprue of your choice and go to town.

Final Verdict: Buy this box.

Blood Angels - Angelus Assault Force ($210)

Contents:

  • 1 Commander Dante
  • 1 Blood Angels Chaplain with Jump Pack
  • 3 Sanguinary Guard
  • 5 Death Company Marines (Assault Intercessors with Jump Packs)
  • 1 Death Company Dreadnouoght (Brutalis Dreadnought)
  • 1 Blood Angels upgrade sprue

Contents MSRP: $345.50

Value saved: $90

Points Cost: 645

On the flipside from the vanilla Marine box, the Angelus Assault Force has a dinkier footprint and isn't as broadly appealing. Dante is a great model, but Blood Angels players will already have him. The Chaplain is stylish but quite old, dating back to the Ancient's Blade release for Blood Angels back in 2016, and as a result is scrawnier than his Primaris compatriots. I'm honestly surprised they didn't include Lemartes or the new Jump Chaplain instead. Sanguinary Guard are a good pick, even if it's only three, and the last units are mostly relying on that Blood Angels upgrade sprue for their identity. The Jump Pack crew can be built as regular Assault Intercessors or Death Company, both of which have a good home in Blood Angels armies, but at only five men, there aren't enough of them to lean into. The Death Company Dreadnought is a fun toy as well, and actually has the movement to keep up with the flyboys pretty well. For existing Blood Angels players, this will go down about as well as a glass of Dante's piss (it's canon shut up) but for newer ones, there's some value if you don't have Dante yet. Regardless, there's better ways to get into Blood Angels (or Space Marines as a whole) and this set really pales in comparison to the vanilla Marine Battleforce. At least it's cheaper.

Final Verdict: Pass unless you're brand new to Blood Angels, or pick up the Combat Patrol instead.

Orks - Armoured Blitzmob ($255)

Contents:

  • 1 Warboss in Mega-Armour
  • 1 Battlewagon
  • 1 Deff Dread
  • 3 Meganobz
  • 10 Ork Boyz

Contents MSRP: $389

Value saved: $134

Points Cost: 560

This is another good box of army staples, even if many of the kits are getting pretty long in the toof. The Warboss and Meganobz are obviously a great combo to put in the Battlewagon and roll forward, though you could build the Battlewagon as a Gunwagon should you so choose. Either way, it's a hardy vehicle that finds its way into loads of lists. The Deff Dread is a surprisingly durable distraction Carnifex that your opponent will have to deal with, one way or another, while any Ork army without sufficient Boyz just isn't an Ork army at all in my eyes. All of these units are useful, whether you're just putting together a burgeoning mob or adding to a years-long Waaagh! of your own.

Final Verdict: Pretend I wrote "buy this box" but in an annoying Ork Mockney way.

Space Wolves - Champions of Fenris ($255)

Contents:

  • Logan Grimnar
  • Arjac Rockfist
  • 15 Wolf Guard Terminators

Contents MSRP: $340.50

Value saved: $85.50

Points Cost: 835

Few things activate my neurons like Terminators, and this box has a buttload of them. As mentioned before, Terminators are in a great place right now, especially so for Wolf Guard Terminators. If one were so inclined to simply have some Wolf-flavored Tactical/Assault Terminators instead, they'd also be a great base for those. Either way, these are a great choice to run either in 5 or 10 man squads, and you've got a good pair of characters to lead them. Logan Grimnar is an excellent leader, giving his unit all three Doctrines for maximum flexibility and hitting like a truck on his own. Arjac Rockfist isn't quite as broadly useful but still kicks ass in his own right, giving his squad a 4+ fight on death as well as being a beatstick of his own. Unless you already have both characters, this box is an excellent pick, and even if you already have Arjac, he's a great basis for a Terminator character conversion.

Final Verdict: Buy unless you have both characters.

Chaos Space Marines - Cult of Destruction ($255)

Contents:

  • 1 Warpsmith
  • 1 Defiler
  • 1 Venomcrawler
  • 2 Obliterators
  • 5 Havocs
  • 3 Mutilators

Contents MSRP: $419.50

Value saved: $164.50

Points Cost: 945

If you want a Defiler at all this holiday season, this is kinda the only way to get one. They're remarkably good units, with at least one showing up in every list. The Obliterators/Venomcrawler sprue from Shadowspear is sometimes tricky to get a hold of, and both units have been rather good over the past few editions. Mutilators are a bit more niche but have some use in Warpstrike Champions and a few other melee-centric Detachments, even if they compete with a lot of other gnarly melee units in the faction. The Warpsmith is kind of a nice to have in this set, though topping off and buffing the vehicles here is handy. Lastly, the Havocs, well, they're cool models at least. Infantry weapons platforms like them tend to die very, very quickly, but you're already saving a load of money here so what's one kinda dubious unit? Maybe if they've been good this year Santa will buff them, who knows.

The biggest surprise is that Games Workshop didn't go even more Iron Warriors-heavy with the flavor here, but this ensures just about any Chaos player will find plenty of useful stuff inside. I don't think this is the best set for a new Chaos player as it isn't really covering the army's basics (though just getting a squad of Legionnaires and Cultists in addition will do that), but for any Chaos player looking to add to their collection it's an excellent buy, mostly full of excellent units.

Final Verdict: Buy if you want a Defiler.

Necrons - Disciples of the Nekrosor ($255)

Contents:

  • 1 C'tan Shard of the Nightbringer
  • 1 Nekrosor Ammentar
  • 3 Ophydian Destroyers
  • 1 Canoptek Doomstalker
  • 10 Necron Warriors
  • 3 Canoptek Scarab Swarms

Contents MSRP: $377.50

Value saved: $122.50

Points Cost: 890

Despite having the single most intimidating model of this entire collection of Battleforces, I do think this is a great start to a Necron army. The Nightbringer is iconic, while also being genuinely great on the table, and Nekrosor is a fun character who still finds his ways into loads of lists. Ophydian Destroyers are a great action unit, especially in Cursed Legion. The Doomstalker, Warriors, and attendant Scarab Swarms are kind of an odd fit, not really fitting with the C'tan or Destroyer vibes, but are a good basic building block for Necron lists. The only drawback with the Warriors and Doomstalker is that existing Necron players might already have gotten their fill of them from 9th edition's starter sets. The Nightbringer also makes up a significant portion of this box's value while being a model you can only take 1 of in an army. It's a little unfocused, but a solid selection of units that any Necron player should have in their collection.

Final Verdict: Buy if you don't have the Nightbringer, otherwise skip.

Chaos Knights - Hellhunt Lance ($255)

Contents:

  • 1 Knight Ruinator/Abominant/Rampager/Desecrator
  • 4 War Dog Stalkers/Brigands/Karnivores

Contents MSRP: $408

Value saved: $153

Points Cost: 865

I pointed this one out as the advertised Ruinator/Stalkers combo, but you could easily crank that up almost 100 points more by just building out other options. These are all units a Chaos Knights player can get a load of mileage out of, with oodles of options should they want to build them differently. Big Chaos Knights aren't too hot at the moment, and the best of them, the Despoiler, is one you can't build with this set. Similarly, this doesn't build War Dog Executioners, so if you wanted to run the best versions of Knights big and small, you'll need to find some other source of weapons. While that might sound negative, rules come and go, and the days of Karnivores and Rampagers running rampant may be back before too long. It's a good value buy for a bunch of units you'll likely end up using, whether you're an old Chaos Knight Robot Jock or a newcomer to the faction.

Final Verdict: Buy this one.

Final Verdict

While not all sets are created equal, this year's crop is genuinely pretty solid. The only stinker in the lot is Blood Angels, and even then, that set isn't truly awful, just not as good as the rest. Otherwise, I'm pretty hot on these, and wouldn't be upset if that Indomitus Task Force was under the tree on Christmas. Hint, hint.
