Mayday, Miss Marcy! Billy Joel Edition

November 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

So dear readers, where do you fall on the side of setting up for the holidays (if you observe them)? Are you already decorated, are you half-decorated, or are you waiting till after next Thursday to set up your tree and lights? Here in my household, I just retrieved our tree out of storage, which is a bit earlier than normal, but I think we could all use a little bit of cheer this year and so I'm going to set up my plastic, glowing mood lamp before the end of the weekend. While I wait to do that and recover, that means we have some time for questions! And as you know, here on Mayday, Miss Marcy! we take your questions and provide them with the best answers we can. Are you curious how to have your own questions tackled in this very column? Well, read on:

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

And now, on to the questions!

Heart Attack-ack-ack-ack-ack, You Oughta Know By Now!

Dear Miss Marcy,

This question can be a bit heavy at first sight so I want to start with the fact that I recently had, and am recovering from, a heart attack. Thankfully I was prepared and have been keeping up with my doctors and so the possibility was known and treatment looks great. The issue that I'm actually writing in about, though, is that I've been told that my hobbying might need to take a back seat to my physical therapy and other activities.

I realize that being alive is more important than painting miniatures, but I really do enjoy the time I spend with my hobby and I'm not thrilled to have to consider cutting down on it. I was wondering if you have any ideas of how I could maybe combine them or make a convincing argument?

Made the Save

Dear Made the Save,

Aside from inspiring this week's theme, congrats and great to hear that you are recovering well. I'm sure that must have been pretty scary, and honestly a fear that I think a lot of people have is that one, sudden health issue that you can't always plan for or expect. In your situation, it sounds like things turned out for the best and I'm glad to hear you'll recover well.

I have a feeling that you might be worrying about your hobby a bit much, though, or at least, I get a sense that the issue is that your hobbying probably involves sitting hunched over in a chair or on a stool and not moving for many hours. Is that about right? Because that's likely the aspect you'll have to consider working around. And I honestly get it; while it is easy to say things like "get a standing desk", that doesn't always solve the issue, and it also isn't functionally the best way to go about building or working on a hobby; from recently working on my gunpla kits, I've found myself having a hard time building them standing up the whole time myself.

What I would suggest then is treating the hobby aspect of what you're doing as a combination of work and recreation. That means you should still be doing the sorts of physical therapy and physical treatments you would do if you were working from home: standing up every 20 minutes, stretching, moving your body, focusing your eyes on distant objects for 20 seconds, etc., all of that sort of thing, so that your body stays active and not curled up and sedentary. I suspect that if you were to do that or express that is your intent, your caretakers or people worried about your sedentary aspects will be more at ease; plus it really is just better for you to do! Good luck with the recovery!

Movin' Out

Dear Marcy,

I'm moving out of where I was living by the end of 2026. I am not going to say much else other than that but to say that I am happy to be moving. My question is that I'm not sure what to do with a majority of my collection of games and miniatures. Moving them is going to be, frankly, a pain in the ass, and I don't know if I'll have much use for or ability to use all, or even most, of them. I have tried selling some of them but that is going slow and I have a deadline. I don't want to just dump them at Goodwill or something, as I'd rather just throw them away at that rate.

Movin On

Dear Movin On,

Check your local library and see if they have a board game group. They likely can't buy the games from you but they would very likely be happy to rehome them; and be sure to check for the library group, not the library itself, but you could ask them too. My local library has a board game group that is always happy to take games to test at their board game night, and whatever happens to the games after that is no longer your problem. If you're really wanting to sell them, there are some places like Reddit that are good, but you're going to have to juggle packaging and shipping them. Since you're on a deadline, I think a local group donation is the best thing you can do. You should also check in with any schools near you that might have a club, or frankly, any local clubs.

As for the miniatures, that might be harder. I think you may have to settle on either selling them or otherwise just taking them with you, if value is a concern; they aren't going to be a very easy thing to place, and miniatures do carry a pretty big investment cost that makes giving them away feel bad. That said, I do say see if there's any groups in your area or players you know that could either take them off your hands cheaper, or maybe even help rehome them to newer players or people who would be curious.

A sad part of this is that you may never know what happens to the things you give away after you do so, but the thing to remember is that is no longer your problem; if you really don't care what happens to them, just throw them away. Yes, it sounds wasteful, but if you bring opened board games or assembled miniatures to something like Goodwill, there's a chance that's what they'll do with them too. I suggest looking for clubs or local groups first.

Just the Way You Are

Dear Marcy,

I am lonely. There isn't a more poetic way to say it. I am single, in my 40s, and my family are mostly gone. I have a job that I enjoy but when I am on my own I do not have a lot of luck in expanding my social circles, either romantically or socially. I go to my local LGS to play, but most of the players there are either married guys with families or college kids half my age. I can play games and have a good time but I have never really made any new or lasting friendships.

Is there anything I can do? I don't want to sound desperate but I am just struggling with what, frankly, sounds embarrassing to write: I don't know how to make friends as an employed adult.

Alone but Not Forgotten

Dear Alone,

I really do think that there's a big problem of adults who are stranded without the ability to make friends that has mostly been engineered by societal and economic forces to make that happen. While "male loneliness epidemic" often reeks of incel speak, there is something to be said of the fact that it can be incredibly hard for adults to make and maintain new, interpersonal friendships. You don't mention trying dating apps, but we all know that those tend to be an almost fruitless game of fishing. And as you noted, going to your local LGS gets you socialization, but not friendship, which is something that requires multiple people to invest into with time and effort. In a lot of cases, friendships happen naturally, or perhaps, unnaturally: usually created by the combination of time and place as much as they are personality meshing.

So, I would say that you really aren't doing anything wrong. The frustrating thing is that it means you also aren't doing anything wrong, which makes the problem hard to fix. If anything, the biggest thing I could suggest is not writing off the younger players you're meeting. Yes, they may be in their 20s, but you're sharing an activity and there is probably more you have in common with them than you think. There's going to be a gulf but writing them off simply due to age is going to make it harder.

As for the other options, I really do wonder if you've considered looking into events run in places that are not explicitly for playing games; for example a coffee house near me does a One Shot night where people gather to experience TTRPGs even if they've never played them. You seem to have established a routine for yourself, but unless you break up or shake up that routine, you are essentially hoping for the solution to fall into your lap. Instead, you may need to think outside of the box for places where you can combine your interests with more explicitly social gatherings, especially those that may be more likely to be attended by people closer to your age.

Just remember that having people in your life in any capacity is better than none, and that humans are social creatures; we crave some form of connection with others, even if it is small. Don't give up on it, but remember that you're you, so be you. Find places to be you, and you'll find people who like who you are; from there, it really is just about nurturing and trial and error, and I wish you luck.
