[Warmachine] Going NOVA: Sea Raiders Invade Virginia

September 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer

I spent most of this long Labor Day weekend trekking down to the Warmachine events at NOVA, the Northern Virginia tabletop convention.  This superlative convention hosted in Washington DC hosts all sorts of tabletop games and Warmachine was well-attended this year.  Boasting two Steamrollers, an Iron Gauntlet Qualifier, a team tournament and a narrative event over the course of four days, there was truly something for everyone who wanted to bring focus and fury to the table.  Special acknowledgements to Tried & True for running such a successful series of events and of course all of their contributors in terrain donations, streaming, prize support and running/judging the main and side events to keep things running smooth.

Credit: Blake

I played in three different events over the course of the weekend: The Thursday Night Last Chance Steamroller, the Iron Gauntlet Qualifier (games Friday and Sunday) and the Nova Team Tournament on team Donut Holes.  Besides having some awesome games and immersing myself in this great community, my intent was to hone my WTC list contenders to a knife's edge.  Thus, for simplicity purposes, I brought all of the same lists to each event:

Thursday Night: The Last Chance Everyone's Qualified Steamroller

Longshanks here.

Despite the name, the only last chance in this steamroller was a chance to practice before the next day's Iron Gauntlet.  I intended to use this event for warm-ups/practice and dropped after two rounds, as I figured extra sleep would be more valuable for the next day of the Iron Gauntlet

Round 1: Butcher vs. Stormcraw (Kithguard) on Wolves At Our Heels

This game was fairly well defined around a large obstruction in the middle that bisected the 50mm objectives.  My opponent went second and chose the more defensive side that allowed his Guard Tower to go behind the obstruction.  Stormcraw and his heavier elements (Steelbacks, Ramhead and his Mistborn) favored the left side of the table with some lighter elements covering the right and the Scrappers and Throat Cutters in ambush, deploying opposite my left side Rhoks and right side warjacks, with Constance and Gallant and my Assault Reavers up the middle.

My first turn was spent running up aggressively as Butcher does.  Stormcraw feated on turn 1 to attempt to aggressively leverage his ambush threats on the first scoring turn, but unfortunately my opponent underestimated how aggressive I was going to be.  Butcher feated top of 2 targeting my Rhoks with Horn of Woe in order for them to charge, take out Ramhead, and then reposition to surround his left-side Trapdoor and prevent it from being used to Ambush.  My right side models took out his Spirit Shamans on his 40mm as well as his Sidewinder, with the Assault Reavers moving up to take down the Guard Tower and surround his remaining trapdoors along with my repositioning Barragers.  Constance and Gallant moved up to ensure they could benefit from as much Requiem as possible for Stormcraw's counterattack.

Credit: Blake

Stormcraw's feat was unimpressive by design, forcing him to change plans by deploying the Scrappers to threaten my 40mm objective while the Throat Cutters deployed to his own.  The Steelbacks killed several of the Rhok Harriers used to kill Ramhead, enough so that the Scrappers could move to take my 40mm.  Stormcraw and the Mistborn moved up to take down my Assault Reavers, although he had no mechanism for moving Constance out of contesting range.  The Jungle Troll moved up to intercept my warjacks on the other side of the obstruction.

Turn 3 saw Constance at maximum Requiem go in on Stormcraw, the Mistborn, and a single Kithguard Medic.  The Mistborn was the only transfer target, so my intention was to deal as much damage as possible to Stormcraw and maybe force a few transfers to the Mistborn, who would also be suffering damage, followed by Careful Recon to get Constance out of the way to make room for Gallant to try to finish off Stormcraw.  It didn't come to that, however, as Constance's attacks (1 initial attack and 3 Flashing Blades, all with Divine Inspiration and Boosted Damage) quickly cut through his transfers, with Constance's final strike showing me triple 6s for a photo finish.

The photo finish!  Credit: Blake

Round 2: Butcher vs. Craghorn (Kithguard) on Two Fronts

Was not expecting two Kithguard games in one day, especially against my fellow Donut Holes teammate!  I didn't know Craghorn especially well except as the ambush specialist, which certainly made me worry a bit on Two Fronts.  Fortunately, I got to go second and choose a relatively secure 40mm point as well as making the flag terrain a large obstruction near it, making it difficult for either of us to secure and ensuring that the game would be more closely decided by the 50mm objectives, where I felt I had an advantage.

After initial trapdoor entries, he had Throat Cutters, a unit of Kithguard Infantry, 2 Mortar Teams and a Sidewinder lying in wait.  His turn 2 was unexpectedly aggressive with his other Kithguard Infantry unit going to a charge on my Assault Reavers to apply Countermeasures to my army, though his poor dice and my use of Last Word limited the actual damage.  My turn 2 consisted of Butcher wiping out the infantry in the middle and feating on the Rhoks once again to attempt to eliminate his Steelbacks with just an advance since he kept them out of line of sight for charge attacks, though they only killed two.  Constance and Gallant moved to aggressively contest his 50mm with my Flail Tyrant playing backup.

His Turn 3 feat turn brought on his Kithguard Infantry off the side to kill one of my Rhok units as well as my Barragers on the right side while the Throat Cutters and Sidewinder emerged on the left to weaken Gallant enough that Ramhead was able to kill it, although he was unable to finish off Constance.  My counterattack got the Rhoks into the Kithguard infantry to kill most of them with lucky throws, while Butcher himself dove into the Throat Cutters and killed them all to control the far side 50mm while my Tyrant killed Ramhead with Redline and Hand of Destruction.  At that point, only token contesting with his remaining ambushing weapon crew was possible and I took it on scenario.

Shout-out to the crazy late-night 3-0s in that one.  For my part, I was getting a little shut-eye in preparation for...

Friday and Sunday: NOVA Iron Gauntlet Qualifier

Longshanks here.

Round 1: Butcher vs. Vallyx (Shadowflame Shard) on High Stakes

Playing against another Donut Holes teammate, this game quickly turned aggressive as we constantly fought over the middle objective.  He quickly committed his Trikhymaerex to kill Gallant and secure the 50mm, which I counter-took with a feated Tyrant, meaning that the game devolved into a slog where control of the 50mm was trading back and forth.

My opponent's scenario terrain was a wall template that was fairly close to the middle of the table and so my opponent secured it aggressively with Incarnate Knights and ultimately Vallyx personally.  It was also the first piece to "explode" and represented the first significant scenario lead of the game.  Butcher took on the right side of the board, killing a Vypex and his Talons that committed to kill a Tyrant and Constance while keeping a safe distance from Vallyx.  My Barragers on my further-back scenario terrain point scored it more consistently than his easily contestable scenario piece as his ambushing Wind Strikers were his only real contesting models and they were dispatched in quick order.

The game went a full seven turns and all three of the scenario terrain pieces "exploded" during the game, the final one being my own piece at the bottom of turn 7, which I had anticipated and had used Duck and Cover to Dig In my Barragers the turn earlier.  A wild back-and-forth game that gave me no time to rest before I jumped straight into round 2.

Round 2: Kishtaar vs. Bohdan (Old Umbrey) on Trench Warfare

Bohdan got to go second on Trench Warfare, which is a significant advantage, but I had played into Old Umbrey enough to know that Kishtaar has ridiculous advantage into this matchup, as Bohdan's shooting defenses are nearly nonexistent and it's difficult for him to apply Incite profitably without risking sudden death.  My Tyrants moved up to protect my cache and Arcane Vortex meant he couldn't manipulate a charge onto them with Winged Nuisance.  He committed his Primeval to the left side 50mm and committed his Feral to take the right side, while the Starved took the middle in hopes that the obstruction cover would save it.

Credit: Blake

My feat barrage killed the Starved, the Liegeman Ursans on the right and exhausted all of his Shield Guard bears, forcing a fairly complete collapse of the right side of the board apart from the Hounds and Feral, who did work under Incite buffs to kill a Tyrant and the Execrators.  Being left with fewer attacks meant that my remaining Assault Reavers on that side were able to jump on the Trench Warfare Scenario Terrain at a critical moment and pull ahead in points.  Bohdan focused his influence more centrally and on the right side, which meant the left side was a slower grind with his Liegeman Cavalry and Primeval unable to finish off my Sure Foot Assault Reavers and dispatching the Primeval over 2 turns of shooting.  Ultimately, once I finished off the Primeval and Feral and Bohdan's feat was exhausted, there was nothing left to keep him out of the crosshairs of my remaining shooting.

Round 3: Butcher vs. Tyrus (House Kallyss) on Fault Lines

This match was streamed live, although perhaps it's better if it wasn't.  The Tyrus list was the 30 soulless model variety, backed up by three Ghasts to enable Flank, Lanyssa and her pet cats, and essential support to strike fast and hard with the swarm of dudes.  I went second and watched the wave of Soulless run up to me, though keeping a healthy distance from Butcher.  As Butcher could kill them all himself, but cannot outthreat them between the Dusk speed buffs and his army's innate resistance to Hunter's Mark, I did a risky play, giving Butcher Stand Your Ground and riding up to the forefront of the army on full focus camp backed up by the Revenant Champion to threaten his 40mm objectives, daring him to commit or retreat his masses of elves and counting on my surrounding models, good stats, and spell immunity to limit his damage.  Still, as the old saying goes: "The risk was calculated, but man am I bad at math."

It was at this point that Orsus regretted everything.  Credit: The Ice King

The factor that I did NOT account for in this plan was forgetting about Chiller on the cats, which saw Benkei run in first to apply it to Butcher.  I also didn't account for Abyssal Gate, which Tyrus used to teleport my Revenant Champion out of Guard Dog range.  This is something I could have accounted for with better positioning or simply having Constance apply Aegis to it, but I was duly punished for my carelessness.  A Ghast followed by 2 units of Stir The Blood Soulless Blademasters came in at me and finished Butcher off with probably 3 attacks to spare.

Round 4: Kishtaar vs. Vilkul (Winter Korps) on Wolves At Our Heels

A third Donut Holes teammate battle!  This one was an interesting game as Kishtaar and Vilkul are both fairly fragile casters and so it's a very "who blinks first" style of game.  Thus, we kept our distance from each other.  Going second on scenario effectively gave me the first strike, which I used to kill Razor and most of his Shock Trooper units.  His counterattack with the Hounds, Krueger and Gallant was brutal, setting me back a Tyrant, Barragers, and most of my Reavers.  The middle was a trading game with a Tyrant going in to kill Gallant (with help from Silence of Death and Dark Shroud), dying to Connie's vengeance, before finally being brought down by another Tyrant.  Kishtaar put in overtime killing the Hounds and Zaccheaus while Vilkul mostly stood near her own 40mm and was instrumental in bringing down the Execrators and removing models from my scenario terrain with her thrown axes.

Once the Hounds were finally dead, I ran a unit of Witch Coven to my 40mm and finally started accumulating points on it.  Thanks to my contesting models on the left, he wasn't able to follow suit and I ended up scoring the 3 VP "jackpot" of Wolves.  Kishtaar moved up the board laterally keeping her distance from Vilkul and set about ending a turn inside my opponent's scenario forest, killing the scoring Battle Mechanik inside it and the last Shock Trooper Gunner that could possibly contest her now insurmountable scenario lead.

And here's where I screwed up.  Following Kishtaar finishing off any models that could contest her scenario advantage, I decided to protect her by spending her last two focus on Sure Foot.  It was sort of a reactive thing regardless as I had ensured that she was not within Vilkul's 18" melee death range.  I had neglected to consider her 19" walk-Cyclone-throw axes into Stealth death range and her Blessed axes meant I had trade my overboost protection for effectively nothing.  This cost me the game as Vilkul nailed her axe attacks and overkilled me by 5 boxes, snatching victory from the jaws of defeat.  A harsh, but important lesson for me.

Round 5 (Sunday): Butcher vs. Dekathus (Necrofactorium) on Two Fronts

This was an interesting one since Dekathus effectively camped the 50mm side of the field with Hades and a unit of Mechanithrall Brutes, while the rest of his troops swarmed the 40mm side.  I went second and took as scenario terrain the rubble in the middle. I got an early lead bottom of 2 with my Redlined Harpoon Tyrant able to drag in Hades and then finish it off with my Flail Tyrant, but my attempts to wipe out a unit of Criterions with my Rhoks and Assault Reavers ended in failure and my opponent was very fastidious in not giving Butcher any easy charge targets around the obstruction he was cagily moving around.

My opportunity came from the true MVP of the game, the Harpoon Tyrant, who managed to find and pull in a Criterion in the other unit without killing it.  This game Butcher the charge target he needed to get distance, surf into a nearby Night Terror behind a wall, and from there into the rest of his troops, racking up a brutal 14 model kill count and talking total control of the 40mm objectives.

Credit: Tried & True

Dekathus and the Brute Thralls eventually committed and, over the course of two turns, they were able to kill Gallant, but stopped short of finishing off Constance.  By then, there was simply no more material to contest with and I scored the most army points of any game I would play that weekend.

I did decide to drop out at this point to avoid undue pressure related to making my flight home at the end of the event.  I did get to witness some of the final games played, including the final match between Grymkin and Convergence that ultimately ended in a Grymkin victory.  Congratulations to Cody Demunn for piloting Old Witch to a perfect record over 6 grueling rounds.

Saturday: NOVA Warmachine Team Tournament

Longshanks here.

Round 1: Butcher vs. Craghorn (Kithguard) on Trench Warfare

Our opening round into Team Absolute Destruction had me take on a pairing I had a fairly fresh experience into, although this Craghorn list was notably different from the Thursday night one.  He traded Ramhead and the weapon crews for Klangor, Chomper and the Scrappers and played very aggressively by comparison to my last Craghorn game.

His turn 2 Feat and Ambushes saw Chomper emerge to take out Gallant while the Scrappers and Steelbacks went for my Rhoks and front line jacks.  His Sidewinder and Kithguard infantry played defense for his other objectives.  The Scrappers didn't deal much damage through my Shield Guards though (since the Shield Guard models were outside Sneak Attack range) and my counterattack with Butcher had him kill all the Scrappers and 2 Steelbacks, while the feat tokens enabled my Tyrants and other Rhok unit to kill Klangor and Chomper.  Constance counterattacked into a few Kithguard infantry before being shot down and my Harpoon Tyrant that helped kill Chomper managed to hold the line and avoid being shot down by the Throat Cutters long enough that I could secure the right side 50mm indefinitely and start moving troops into taking his 40mm once I removed the Guard Tower and Tunnel Rats.  In the end, despite the early gambit to remove Gallant, he didn't have enough gas in the tank to play out the attrition game.

Round 2: Kishtaar vs. Vorsalys (Fane of Nyrro) on Payload

We received the pair-up into Peyton's Make-A-Wish foundation, who ultimately took 3rd, where I played against the titular team captain, rocking an army of vampires and sporting a count's cape to let you know this was serious business

Pictured: Serious Business.  Credit: Tried & True, used with Peyton's permission

I did have some innate advantages here as the scenario was Payload and I forced Peyton to go second, meaning that his opportunity to generate pre-conflict Dominion hunger was limited and my Arcane Vortex protection was total.  I feated top of 2 and was able to Ricochet off Gallant to kill Merciless in order to trigger Hell Hunters to ensure all 3 Tyrants had valuable targets for their Quad-Bolters.  Peyton had expected me to remove Sybaris from the table (it was behind cover that was no defense against my Hunter Tyrant), but also lost the Strygon and two Klyveneesh as well as a handful of Merciless in the bargain, though my attempts to chip damage Gallant mostly ended in failure.

Vorsalys' feat counterattack so him fearlessly go up to his 40mm, though he couldn't quite clear all the Assault Reavers off the point.  Most of the rest of the Merciless were killed by Last Words, but they cleared the way for Constance to take out the Execrator unit, which in turn left Gallant and his Strygon Rider able to charge and kill one of my Tyrants and damage enough, with a healthy application of Blinding Light on various places.

It was here Peyton made a critical error: He failed to reposition Gallant back within 3" of Constance.  At only one focus and Weakened after killing the Execrators, Kishtaar easily dispatched her and my remaining Tyrants (one Blinded, but with the Shredder melee weapon) killing the Strygon Rider and the last Klyvenesh.  With very little material left, Peyton went for an assassination on Kishtaar, using Perdition to get within range where he could blast her with multiple Blinding Lights.  Thanks to some poor dice, he needed to spend more fury than anticipated to get range on Kishtaar, giving him 3 spells worth of boosted POW 10 damage roles into a 1 focus Kishtaar with Sure Foot.  It was a close thing, but the final spell, with Vorsalys' final fury point earmarked for damage, rolled snake eyes.  The loss didn't hurt Peyton's team much, as they took the round, but it was an excellent matchup into a situation where I felt initially like I had every advantage and turned into a close game despite some errors.  Fane are certainly a force to be reckoned with.

Round 3: Butcher vs. Mordikaar (Exalted) on High Stakes

This team matchup was the Donut Holes vs the Molke Princess Posse.  I'm told this team matchup carries some significance for Dungeon Crawler Carl fans.

By fortune, I got another stream table match, and this one painted Butcher in a much more flattering light.  He took incredibly defensive approaches controlling his side objectives while Butcher threatened to get to his 40mm if he was careless.  There was an obstruction right next to the 50mm that essentially allowed both of us to contest the 50mm from near-perfect safety and each of us were waiting for the other to make the first move.  I took that advantage, finding an angle where my Harpoon Tyrant could drag his Titan Gladiator into range of my other heavies, though I had to commit both Gallant and my Flail Tyrant to finish it off.  He responded by sending in his Aradus Soldier to kill the Harpoon Tyrant and flooding the center with feated Immortals.

My Tyrant was able to clear out by Trampling away, managing to kill the one Immortal actually engaging Gallant and freeing him up to charge and deal some damage to the Soldier.  Most importantly, this freed up space for Butcher to land in the middle of the tightly-packed Exalted army.  It was a little nerve-wracking as I mostly needed 6s to hit the targets there between the Feat, the Extoller UAs, and the Swarm Soldier and only 1 reroll into Poltergeist meant I had very little margin for error, but the dice did not fail me.  Butcher carved an unstoppable swathe of destruction through the middle of the board, culminating in killing Aberration in 3 hits (including Vision) and ending the turn with 14 Blood-Quenched stacks.  It's weird that it happened twice.

Before... Credit: The Ice King ...and after. Credit: The Ice King

Mordikaar's insult-to-injury continued when he failed to hit my Rhok Harrier with a boosted spray, leaving him unable to Void Walk to safety.  Although Butcher got Paralyzed by the Krea, he simply walked up to it and killed it, carved through a couple of Paingivers, and then 2-shot Mordikaar for the win.  Certainly got to put on a show for the stream and end on a high note as the team defeated the Princess Posse (though still ended below them in the final standings based on individual game wins).

Final Thoughts

So, what does this say about Sea Raiders as we approach the terminus of the gaming season for the year?  I think that they're in a fairly competitive place.  They certainly have weaknesses (I was happy that I didn't have to face the likes of Caine or Rosko), but they can play into a wide variety of matchups, have some neat tricks and dark horse factors that might surprise opponents ("Kishtaar's feat lasts a ROUND?") and, in the case of Butcher blowing through whole armies by himself, are simply just fun to play.  I've heard it said that Sea Raiders are on SFG's list for some form of rework to give them a greater faction identity, and while I look forward to that, I hope they understand that between all the tricks and unique mechanics, that a pure stats army can be fun, good for the game, and a good entry recommendation for newer players who want to run the original conquerors of the Iron Kingdoms.
