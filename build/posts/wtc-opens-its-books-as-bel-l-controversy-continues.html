WTC Opens Its Books as Belül Controversy Continues

November 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The ongoing saga around the World Team Championship (WTC) and former organizer Işık Belül continued this week as both groups exchanged additional comments on the 2025 Leoben event’s finances and the decision to end Belül’s role. The exchange follows the WTC’s November 10 statement, which disputed Belül’s October account and released a comparison of the initial Leoben budget outline against what the current team describes as the reported result. You can find our reporting on Belül's initial statement here.

For anyone looking for a quick recap of the story so far and an introduction who these people are and why this matters, look below.

A Quick Primer - Click to Expand

The WTC is the World Team Championship, an independent events organizer formerly run by Tom Adriany, Işık Belül, François Lesestre, Christian Lord, Fernando Kerubus, Michael Banz, and Neil Kerr. The WTC is responsible for running the annual Warhammer 40k tournament of the same name as part of a larger event that often includes other games, such as Kill Team, X-Wing, and Age of Sigmar (notably a singles event and not the world team championships for that game). The event is normally held in late summer, and in past years has typically been held in Mechelen, Belgium. In 2025 the event was relocated to Leoben, Austria under the guidance of Işık Belül and reportedly lost money due to a number of factors. In 2026 the event was moved back to Mechelen and Işık Belül was removed from his position at the WTC. The two sides have had public disagreements since his removal, with each claiming the other is responsible for the loss of revenue in 2025 and is unfit to run the organization.

In Belül’s original statement, the WTC co-founder argued that remaining WTC management is “unfit for purpose” and urged team captains to consider moving the event. He linked his removal to ongoing disagreements over a business relationship with Belül’s company centering around terrain for the event, the failed 2024 effort to form an Austrian company (which he contends led to further loss of revenue from missed state subsidies), and demands from other WTC managers that he publicly take accountability for perceived failures of the 2025 Leoben event in a manner he rejected. He also claimed that terrain-licensing revenue and certain sponsorships stayed in Belgium rather than supporting Leoben, and that Kill Team and X-Wing were effectively subsidized by the 40K event.

On November 10, the WTC released its own statement, signed by Tom Adriany, Francois, Christian, Fernando, Michael Banz, and former WTC organizer Neil Kerr. In that statement, the group listed conflicts of interest, a lack of accountability, a breach of trust related to Leoben financial reporting, and interpersonal issues as the basis for ending their working relationship with Belül. They disputed that the absence of Kill Team and X-Wing was the main cause of the Leoben shortfall and released a spreadsheet comparing the initial budget (which projected a “real expected profit” range of €75,000–€125,000) to later figures. The statement described the comparison as the team’s assessment of Belül’s outline. It is important to note that what WTC released in the November 10 statement is not an independent review, and the underlying actuals have not been released alongside it. Kerr left the organization in 2023 and has had no formal role for three years; neither the statement nor subsequent comments have explained his inclusion as a signatory.

In comment provided to Tabletop Battles, Adriany claimed the net reported loss from Leoben was “5,000–6,000 euros” and confirmed that this figure does not match any total in the released spreadsheets. He described these documents as incomplete and “quickly hobbled [sic] together when WTC leadership insisted on some accountability from Isik's end” once they heard of a deficit against the earlier profit projection and after the roughly €26,000 bar budget had been removed from the event financial reporting. Adriany confirmed that the “conclusions” column in the comparison spreadsheet is annotated by the WTC team, written in response to a financial debrief shared by the captains council, rather than material from Belül. No calculation supporting the €5,000–€6,000 figure has been provided.

One major point of focus is the event’s second weekend, during which the WTC had planned to host other games and events, including Kill Team, X-Wing, an Age of Sigmar singles event, and a painting competition. Adriany attributed the plan to fill the hall with roughly 400 people across multiple systems to Belül and a collaborator named Kevin, stating that Games Workshop leadership had not requested it (there is a notion that the 40k publisher might have requested some level of exclusivity for the teams event). Notably, these events did not occur - Kill Team and X-Wing ran separate events in Mechelen, while the Age of Sigmar event and painting competition were scrapped entirely. According to Adriany, the events that actually ran that weekend were small— “an 18 man beer and pretzels doubles event, a 59 player WH40K singles event, and an 18-man AOS event” —and he argued that Kill Team and X-Wing would have seen similarly low turnout if forced to Leoben. He said the side events that did occur that weekend contributed roughly as much as those systems would have.

In response, Belül has prepared a lengthy point-by-point reply, which he provided to Tabletop Battles in response to a request for comment. He claims he already accepted responsibility for the Leoben outcome in the “Lessons Learned” document sent to management and the Captains Council at the end of March, including failing to recognize in time that the side events would not succeed. He maintains that Kill Team and X-Wing “did have part in the WTC 2025 financial loss, not the entire thing but definitely partially,” particularly because the Austrian company was never formed (Belül claims that not forming the company meant losing out on a large amount of revenue in government subsidies), and partially because the organization carried dual sets of costs. He claims that the decision to hand bar revenue and costs to the venue was made collectively in a meeting during the event year, with any profits to be donated to a future WTC organization. Belül primarily lists undercharged tickets (team tickets cost €900 in 2025 once lunches were removed - on which see more below - versus €1,800 in 2026), the absence of Kill Team and X-Wing, cancelled side events, and unrealized government subsidies as the key contributing factors to the Leoben event generating a loss.

The spreadsheets, however, provide a significant amount of data that can be used to interrogate some of the claims and counter-claims. Although on the surface there are eye-catching cost overruns and failures to realise revenues, there are also several places where the WTC’s presentation does not align cleanly with the underlying financial data. The net loss on ticket sales compared to budget is €13,845, which the WTC team attributes to the Warmaster GT underperforming and the failure of the AoS and painting events to launch, while claiming that Kill Team and X-Wing revenue was compensated for by other ticket sales. However, the lost revenue from those two events is projected at €13,450 - almost exactly the net shortfall. The secondary hotel position shows a budgeted net contribution of roughly €9,000 becoming a €15,000 cost. Public hotel-reservation announcements made by the WTC for the second week during the lead-up to Leoben show substantial bookings at Hotel Kongress and a sold-out Living Campus, which stand in contrast to the spreadsheets’ description of secondary hotel income as having not materialized.

WTC Leoben Hotel Booking Announcement, April 24 2025
WTC Leoben Living Campus Announcement, May 16, 2025

The expenses spreadsheet itemizes costs by hotel, with costs for the Living Campus listed as rising by €9,000, and Hotel Kongress incurring zero cost, which would still have left a profitable overall position thanks to €90,000 hotel income. What mostly wiped this out were costs from a third accommodation option, Hotel Kindler, where an expected €9,000 cost became €15,000, and no income is logged, making for an overall €24,000 loss compared to budget. The contemporaneous hotel booking updates simply show this hotel as “available” where both Kongress and Living Campus were filling up (with the Living Campus later listed as sold out), even for the week of the 40K WTC itself. This plausibly suggests over-optimistic projections for room sales, but it’s also far more explicable than it’s presented as in the WTC accounts.

This also highlights the potential compounding effects of lower player numbers. Even if the separate Kill Team and X-Wing events held in Mechelen were profitable, lost revenue from the hotel bookings and additional venue costs strengthens the case that keeping these events separate hurt the Leoben event. However, there are also indications that the impact of some logistical challenges were simply underestimated; the player shuttle, budgeted at €4,000 with a note that it might be fully subsidized, is listed at €11,630.

The treatment of food and player/team lunches is particularly difficult to reconcile. The original budget listed 40K team tickets at €1,200, and the income analysis shows that lunches were essentially charged at €15 per-person, per-diem. For teams of nine people (assuming the inclusion of a coach), once lunch was included the loaded price would have been approximately €1,740, but the event only charged €1,350 for those tickets, a total difference of roughly €16,000 based on the 42 teams which attended. The WTC annotations in the comparison notes that ticket prices were lowered because of the high projected profit; neither side has identified who made that decision.

As a result of the decision for lunch costs to be folded into ticket prices, the comparison then shows both higher ticket income and also a large catering expense. The WTC’s analysis does not make clear that the ticket revenue is not being compared on a like for like basis, and presents the €30,000 of “missed income” for food sales without adequately explaining that this has been separately accounted for in the ticket sales section. The document’s explanation that ticket overperformance for the core 40k teams event offset the projected Kill Team and X-Wing shortfall is therefore hard to substantiate, as it appears that the claimed overperformance is primarily driven by changing where the “food sales” revenue is accounted for.

Belül has separately noted that team ticket prices were lower in 2025 than in 2026 once lunches were removed. Decisions on lunch also appear to have some overlap with the decision to allow the venue to take on the operation and profits from the bar, a major point of contention between the accounts. The WTC account presents this as a major loss, while Belül indicates that costs of operating a bar often overrun, making the loss less impactful (though the original estimate presents it as a potential significant revenue source). However, delegating the food to the venue appears to have worked very well for the event - the financials indicate they were simply charged a unit cost of €13 per meal compared to the €15 they rolled into ticket prices; if that is accurate, it provided a small amount of headroom for staff catering, and seems to have flexibly adapted to the final player numbers. It does also again highlight that the comparison between tickets sold is not a like-for-like comparison; a significant contributor to the 40k overperformance against expected revenue were additional tickets for team staff, of which the majority of the cost was for catering.

For the WTC’s part, the initial budget outline itself contains multiple unsupported estimates, including a subsidy total of €21,250 accompanied by an unelaborated guess that the final amount would reach €30,000. Multiple line items are annotated as “Generous estimate from [past event]”, and one €500 cost line is labeled simply ‘thrash’ with a note that reads ‘dont ask.’ The WTC’s comparison document also lists €763 under miscellaneous costs for ‘Treatment for team,’ noted as massages for judges plus breakfasts, dinners, and lunches. Neither the WTC statement nor Belül’s reply explains the contractual relationship between Alpine Cup GmbH (the Austrian-domiciled company which we understand actually ran the event), the venue, and the WTC (which consists of two Belgian entities, a non-profit and a company), or which party ultimately bore responsibility for these results. The failed/stalled effort to form a WTC-controlled Austrian company is cited by Belül as a reason government subsidies did not materialize; the WTC statement does not address that timeline. It also appears to have made some degree of conflict of interest in the running of the event effectively inevitable without strong contractual controls addressing Belül’s dual position with the WTC and Alpine Cup.

The materials released to-date show that Belül’s original budget outline contained significant, overly optimistic estimates and that the side events for the second weekend which he projected did not materialize; these are outcomes for which Belül has accepted responsibility in the Lessons Learned document. The final stated financial position is also not easy to reconcile with the figures in the spreadsheet. At the same time, the WTC’s comparison spreadsheet and November 10 statement present the results in ways that do not align cleanly with the underlying financials provided - most notably in the treatment of the Kill Team and X-Wing revenue, the food and ticket figures, and the secondary hotels - while failing to address the lack of documented oversight on key decisions (such as the ‘don’t ask’ line item), the failure to form the Austrian company in time for expected subsidies, and the related-party structure through which the event was run. The net effect is an account that assigns primary responsibility to Belül while the released figures and process gaps indicate broader failures of planning and supervision.

Captains considering future hosting decisions still lack a complete final accounting for 2026. On November 3 the WTC announced that it had concluded the bid process for 2027 and would begin negotiations with Games Workshop to act as a hosting partner (note: These negotiations are being initiated by the WTC - Games Workshop has not made an offer or bid to host); should those negotiations not succeed in the next six to eight weeks, the bid would go to Mechelen. In the captains’ ranking, 34 of 45 participating nations placed the Games Workshop option first.
