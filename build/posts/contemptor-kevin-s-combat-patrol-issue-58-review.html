Contemptor Kevin's Combat Patrol Issue 58 Review

July 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The final issue of the "regular" 9th Edition era Leagues of Votann Combat Patrol arrives this week, with Issue #58. This contains the final sprue of the Cthonian Berserks, as well as lore materials and gaming materials. How do these gaming materials fare now that the game and ruleset they were based on are over? Let's find out. As always, thank you to Tabletop Battles and Hachette for the opportunity to review these models, magazines, and materials.  Also, as an American when I write this: Happy Independence Day to all who celebrate!

Hobby Materials

Ymyr Conglomerate Cthonian Beserk. Credit: Corrode

We get the final sprue from the Cthonian Berserks box, plus the associated bases (4 32mm and 1 50mm for the Berserk with Robot Friend). This lets us build the last two Berserks (three of them plus the Robot Buddy having been in last week's issue), and have a set of models ready to go bust some skulls. The first part of the issue is a paint guide for the Berserks. Nowadays, these paint guides usually focus on painting the entire unit from basecoating to shades to highlight. Because the Berserks don't believe in shirts, the issue has us undercoat them with Wraithbone. Honestly, this strikes me as a good idea if you're not actually using a spray primer. The subscription scheme involves a lot of yellow fabric, and while Averland Sunset is a good yellow paint, it is still a yellow paint with all the disadvantages of that specific color. The only disagreement where I think things look weird (in the issue) is having the skin color be the Wraithbone undercoat with two coats of Reikland Fleshshade. As someone who used to use a similar process when I was getting started, I abandoned it (for the most part) with my Space Marines in favor of using very reds and whites mixed together. The color turned out to be slightly "off" for Caucasian skin, in my experience. That being said, it is not inappropriate for the Berserks based on the Narrative Materials.

Narrative Materials

We only get one lore article in this week's issue, but it is a lengthy four page article discussing the League Territories. We learn that the Leagues of Votann have vast interstellar empires within the core of the Milky Way Galaxy. The Leagues tend to use ident-beacons to mark their territory, and generally other Leagues will leave them alone. The Leagues will, however, happily seize territory that happens to not be held by another League and kill anyone who resists. Unusually for a Warhammer 40K faction, they will not necessarily slaughter those who lack the strength to resist.

Black Templars vs. Leagues of Votann at the Squig City Casino Royale GT. Credit: SRM and Jason

We get a map of the Kinspace, the majority which is within the Circumnuclear Disk surrounding Sagittarius A. This sector is filled with resource-rich star systems, but tempered because of the proximity to a supermassive black hole and the usual danger of the Warhammer 40K. It is an area of dangerous radiation, unstable anomalies (spatial and temporal), and wild empiric currents. This level of terror keeps the Kin on their toes, and why they are so aggressive in resource acquisition: the unpredictable and violent nature of their home space can get them sucked into gravitational oblivion at the slightest misstep.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Gaming Materials

We have two more Campaign missions this week; the first is Aeldari v. Orks. Using the Magazine Rules, the mission is played on Forward Outpost, where the Aeldari and Orks are trying to wreck the other side's bases (the Aeldari because they need the Orks disabled while they search for the spirit stones of missing Aeldari, and the Orks because it's fun). In 11th Edition, the two Combat Patrols would each be Purge the Foe, and would feature the high volume of Ork shooting against the fewer but far more accurate and powerful Aeldari shooting.

The second campaign mission is the Chaos Space Marines versus the Orks. The Heretic Astartes find the Orks to be really irritating because they are in their way, and the Orks...well, they're still the Orks. They want to krump gitz, and the Ruinous Powers of Chaos are very much gitz. The magazine has the two sides battle under Combat Patrol mission Scorched Earth, while the 11th Edition version would pit Chaos Space Marine Disruption against the Ork's Purge the Foe.

The quickplay scenario for this issue sounds like fun: Scenario 35: Hunt them Down. A squad of Cthonian Berserks are exploring a derelict space hulk, they learn the hard way that the Tyranids are afoot! Within a large chamber, 5 units of lone Berserks face off against two units of 1 Von Ryan's Leapers and 3 units of 2 Termagants. This is a simple hack and slash mission, with only the datasheet abilities and stats to carry the day. The Berserks get feel no pain and fight on death against the Von Ryan's Leapers, which seems helpful for the hack and slash.

The Final Verdict

Another solid issue of Combat Patrol Magazine, doing what the subscription does best. We get some hobby materials, some interesting storyline and insight behind why we paint our models the way we do, and a way to play with out new toys and let them smash into each other. Indeed, the campaign system is still fun and with some minor tweaks (using force disposition and RNG to determine missions instead of the issue), you can still run the campaign system as-is.  The magazine gives you 110% of what you paid for, and if you need Cthonian Berserks for your Votann army this issue (and #57) give them to you at a greater than 50% discount, if you can find them for retail price.

Until next time Combat Patrolers!
