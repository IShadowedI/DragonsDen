Turn Order: Paper Minis for TTRPGs - The Artists and Creators

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

What Are Paper Minis?

Paper Miniatures sometimes also known as “standees” are an old school form of TTRPG miniature. The ones I’m talking about are perhaps even simpler than some of the really old ones that predate inkjet printing by a few decades. There are videos out on the internet of Peter Cushing (yes Tarkin) painting toy soldiers made of compressed drawing paper. What I’m writing about today is a bit more modern than that though I promise.

Selection of Paper Minis from PrintableHereos.com and PaperForgeMinis.com

What Advantages and Disadvantages Do They Have?

Paper Miniatures are cheap and quick to make in large numbers to a high level of quality. If your party is facing a horde of Goblins or Kobolds next week getting said horde printed and ready is something doable in a single day from start to finish. I know this website has plenty of people that love to paint miniatures but when you’re trying to run a sandbox as a GM being able to react to the players between sessions is something I value highly, and these minis look better than any goblin army I’d paint in a week or two (heck even in a month).

They have some of the same downsides as plastic or resin minis (bought or printed) though, some of them avoidable. For instance I tend to print my own minis with their bases attached, folding them into the required shape. The space required to store minis like this is something Warhammer playing readers of this website are probably all too aware of. Many paper mini users instead print their minis flat and use slot bases to stand them up for us, letting the mini itself be stored flat in something like a TCG Binder. To save space and to fit larger maps into a smaller amount of table space I actually print my minis at 15mm scale (more on that later) a size that slot bases aren’t sold at. I also use a monitor to display maps on in my games so I’m perhaps a bit overly cautious about the impact of a plastic base on the screen’s surface over time. It might be a simplistic understanding of materials science but “harder material wins” is the level I understand and I want the winning material to be the screen not my minis. The big disadvantage though you can only avoid so much with them is that they are still made or paper (or card) and therefore not going to outlast a plastic mini with regular use. In my experience I’ve gotten several years out of my players' minis without needing to replace them though, with careful use and storage I wouldn’t call them fragile.

Size though I think is an advantage I’m surprised I don’t see talked about more by 3D printing TTRPG players. There are lots of minis you can buy for games like D&D, but they’re almost exclusively at the 28mm scale. Maybe the UK dining table is just a bit smaller than the average but fitting character sheets, dice trays, note books, and a decently sized map on the table at that scale quickly presented itself as one of the first challenges I faced as a new GM a few years ago.

Early Days of GMing - Credit Vre’kais

Roughly 20 x 8 squares and I’ve almost crossed the entire width of my table at the usual 1” grid scale. I really wanted to fit a more meaningful amount of space on my table and it occurred to me that I could make my minis any size I want.

Paper minis on a screen showing a VTT Map - Credit Vrekais

This 18.5” portable screen fits a 27 x 15 15mm grid in less space than two pieces of A4 paper.

One last big advantage I’ve found of Paper Minis is how easy they can be to adjust or edit if the exact mini you need doesn’t exist yet. You might not have even realised it but you’ve seen one already in this article!

Modified mini created using minis from PrintableHeroes.com

This Tent Mimic is actually a Tent mini crossed with a barrel Mimic’s teeth and tongue, with a smattering of eyes for good measure.

What Providers Are There?

I won’t even pretend that this list is exhaustive, it’s not even close, but they’re the creators I’ve used the most as well as some I’m aware of with different styles.

Printable Heroes & Paper Forge

Printable Heroes and Paper Forge are probably my first go for 99%+ of my mini needs, with a catalogue that as their names suggests doesn’t just cover monsters, there’s a vast array of player character art in here to choose from as well. What I really like about these two though is how compatible their styles are when used together. To some extent they’ve also tended to not overlap on more niche things if the other has one already which is a breath of fresh air in this competitive world we live in. They both also have some of the most helpful and friendly discord communities I’ve joined. Here’s a bunch of player characters I printed and whilst I think most people might be able to figure out which is which given enough scrutiny it’s not jarring enough to be an issue.

Paper minis designed by PrintableHeroes.com and PaperForgeMinis.com, printed by Vre'Kais

Both of these creators offer a large selection of mini art for free with variants and some very specific minis being only available to subscribers of their respective Patreons. Considering both have offerings starting as low as $1/month though neither are especially expensive and both put out minis at a decent rate usually several per month on average.

Paper minis designed by PrintableHeroes.com and PaperForgeMinis.com, printed by Vre'Kais

PrintableHeroes current long term goal is to try to cover the entire D&D monster manual but the latest release on their website was No.1174 and there’s only roughly 300 monsters in that book so some side quests have certainly been taken on their way to achieving that goal. Between these two providers though I’ve very rarely not been able to find something I need or that I can uses as a proxy.

There's also a great community for modifying the minis using parts of other minis. For instance I’m running a campaign of Curse of Strahd Reloaded currently and reached out for help finding a reasonable proxy for “The Strix” a large bird construct with Manticore stats and abilities. Someone in the Printable Heroes discord actually “kit bashed” me something that was almost perfect using parts of several different minis combined into something new.

Uses Paper minis designed by PrintableHeroes.com and combined by CapZylo

Hero Forge

Sometimes though I have to admit that these providers can’t provide for every single character fantasy, so for those I’ve turned to using exports from Hero Forge. There’s a few different approaches you can take here though, I’ve tried both doing front and back images separately and lining them up as close as I can, but also more recently accepted defeat and mirrored a single image front and back. Perspective on a 3D model causes the size of elements from each view to vary quite considerably. When I was still cutting minis out by hand this wasn’t a big issue but since swapping to a digital cutter those differences are very difficult to work around.

Selection of Paper Minis including some using Hero Forge exports - photo by VreKais

I’ve also resorted to combining art from Hero Forge and Printable Heroes when necessary, a combination that worked far better than I actually ever expected considering the very different styles. In this case it was needing a PC mini for both mounted and unmounted combat, as their Drake beast companion was a key part of the character’s identity that I definitely wanted to see on the table.

Combination of Hero Forge mini and PrintableHeroes.com art, photo by Vrekais.

To create this I actually posed the character mini in Hero Forge on a motorcycle, and then edited that out of the image to pose him on top of the Drake.

Other Artists

I’ve covered the art providers I’ve used but there are loads more I’ve seen and want to make sure I mentioned a few of them here.

Trash Mob Minis

Credit Trashmob Minis Patreon

An incrementally more cartoony style than Printable Heroes and Paperforge, these minis certainly have a lot of charm with bright bold primary colours and distinctive designs. They're probably the first provider I’d recommend for games aimed at a younger audience.

Paper Mage

Credit Paper Mage Patreon

At the other end of the illustrated style spectrum Paper Mage has a few hundred designs available. I haven’t used them myself but they are highly recommended in the paper miniatures communities I frequent.

This barely scratches the surface of the creators out there designing mini art for almost any vibe or setting now. I’ve asked around the communities I’m in for more suggestions and here’s a quick list of those recommendations.

  • David Okum (Okumarts)
  • Yasashii Kyojin Studio
  • Lichfest
  • PaperMini
  • Kev's Lounge
  • HUMAN PERSON

I’ve had a great time enhancing my games with these amazing artists' help over the last few years and I hope this quick tour of what’s available inspires more people to give them a try! I’ll be back soon with a guide on how I print, cut, and prepare my minis soon!
