Getting Started With Trickcal: Chibi Go

August 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Longtime readers of the site probably know that I play a lot of games; I’ve spent a good majority of my life playing them, writing about them, thinking about them, and occasionally working on them. I’ve spent over a year on this very Website writing about Umamusume: Pretty Derby, and there are a lot of games I regularly play and enjoy that I love the thought of sharing with others; particularly, I think the mobile/phone/tablet gaming scene has a lot of wheat to separate from chaff, with a lot of really great games that exist in an ecosystem that isn’t always easy to discern, discover, or frankly trust. Earlier this year, I happened to see a particular little creature show up a lot on my various socials: Bluesky, Xiaohongshu, Instagram, YouTube, everywhere I looked I kept seeing this cute, pathetic sort of… thing.

This thing.

I had to know what it was, where it came from, and why it kept yelling “좋아요” (Joayo/Cuayo), and that’s how I discovered a game that has ever since occupied a constant place in my mind. I love this game, I think more people should love this game, and hopefully I can convince you to give it a try. That game? Trickcal: Chibi Go, a creation of Epid Games and published by Bili Bili for Android and iOS platforms, is a charming, silly, fun, and surprisingly thoughtful world that sucks you in with cute chibi characters and goofy mechanics like being able to pull their cheeks (even during cutscenes), delivers a surprisingly deep auto-battler system that actually seems to know how to “auto”, and the part that has kept me around: one of the best worlds I’ve seen built and realized in a game lately.

We’re On A Mission From God

As Master of the Order, you have a house, and lots of devoted followers. Who are all weird.

Let me start with that world building before getting into how to play the game and what I find so enjoyable about it. In Trickcal, players take on the role of The Master (교주 or Gyoshu in Korean,which can be read as either “cult leader” or “religious leader”), a faceless and genderless stand-in character that you get to name when you start the game. There really isn’t anything known about “Gyoshu” except that they are from Earth and have somehow found themselves in the world called Elias, inhabited by various races: Sprites, Witches, Werebeasts, Phantoms, Dragons, Elementals, and Elves, who also come from outside of Elias (and have a lot of trauma about Earth and Santa). Gyoshu really has no powers, special abilities, or superheroic abilities, but they do have a few things to their name: human reasoning, size, and a gun.

Ner (left) is the long suffering advisor to Erpin (right) who only really wants to eat bread. Or literally anything, frankly.

Your immediate introduction to the world of Elias come as the Sprite kingdom is leading a rebellion against the gluttonous and dim-witted Queen, Erpin, and her long-suffering advisor and leader of the Church of Yggdrasil, Ner. I was mostly just along for the ride to check the game out when the initial prologue started talking about multi-level-marketing schemes and debating whether insects that are squashed go to the “Weekend Ranch” or “Weekend Farm”, establishing that Elias (apparently) has no concept of death. I’m going to level with you, dear reader: this made me bust out laughing at how stupid the conversation became, and from there, I ended up finding myself hooked.

Everyone in this story is some form of a moron, but endearingly. This is Pyrrha, a dragon who makes fake gold, because... she's the Pyrite Dragon, so she's also a loan shark. Of course.

Trickcal is a testament to worldbuilding, frankly, and perhaps the somewhat complicated history of Trickcal: Chibi Go’s development is part of that reason. There was a webnovel called “The Trickcal” which predated any games, and another game called “Trickcal” which shut down very early in service; Trickcal: Chibi Go is functionally the third attempt to bring the concept to life, and a lot of that development shows in just how fully realized the characters and world they inhabit are. I mostly expected the game to be silly, and it is, comically so, but the more I played the more I was pulled into the mystery and charm of the game: Yggdrasil is sick, dying, or possibly already dead, there are numerous threats to Elias and its relative peace, and somehow Gyoshu finds themselves at the center of all of it. As players meet various characters, play through their stories, and experience the new events that expand on the story, I also found that Trickcal does something that I have criticized other mobile games for in the past: it has not just horizontal depth of constant updates, but it has vertical depth, as characters change, impact the story, and the story does not simply forget their existence the moment someone more popular or interesting is introduced.

The writing in this game is honestly so funny at times it catches me off guard.

I know that sounds like odd praise, perhaps, if you’re not overly familiar with mobile games, but Trickcal’s uses of foreshadowing, character depth, and urging players to remember even very small details as the story develops makes Elias feel like an actual world that you’re interacting with, rather than a simple power fantasy where your character solves all problems and nothing is ever at stake. Perhaps my favorite example of this are the mysterious “Eldynes”, a group of 7 sisters who all seem to be separated by some event players are unaware of, and their slow meetings through the story provide a lot of flavor and strife as simply being sisters doesn’t mean they’ll reconnect or get along, let alone recognize one another; without going too far into spoiling the story events, the interactions of the Eldyne sisters alone makes for compelling and at times heartwrenching drama in a game that seems like it should just be silly on the surface.

Set Expectations to Whimsy

Trickcal is nothing if not colorful.

That balance in narrative that Trickcal strikes is maybe the best part of its whimsical nature: it loves to go for laugh out loud jokes and situations, while also tempering those with emotional stakes and character growth that the player is not actually all that relevant to solving; something else I loved about the game is that Gyoshu exists, but is not the character present in every story or event, perhaps even explicitly to the detriment of what is going on: without the presence of a “wise outsider” like in a lot of games, things can and do go wrong, leaving the pieces to be solved… maybe.

Trickcal has one consistent message: ACAB.

That isn’t to say that every event is depressing or ends badly; a lot of them are silly, too, and the whimsy of Trickcal is the thing that keeps me coming back to it. I love the art design, the cute characters, the colorful world, the little references and attention to detail that are worked into the game; the developers at Epid have a big love for cinema, mythology, and literature that shows up in surprising ways. Even the aforementioned cheek pinching mechanic just seems completely silly, and the sudden proliferation of Speaki into defacto mascot of the game just fits in with the cute and relaxing vibe the game wants to give off, which it then counterbalances with the deeper narrative aspects and developments.

Epid really loves little cute references, such as this Breaking Bad one.

The Global version of the game, which is the one I’m playing, is quite a bit behind the Korean version of the game, which also gives me a little foresight into what is coming down the pipe, and almost everything I ever hear about from Korean friends playing the game is that the best is yet to come, which is… oddly exciting? So many games that have development/release gaps often go through cycles of “oh you’ll hate this”, such as Umamusume’s community maligned Trackblazer era. All I ever hear about Trickcal is excitement on excitement for the next story, the next bits of character development and progress, and even the bits I don’t fully understand but hear about have me curious; the game successfully sucks you in if you let it, and I’ve not regretted a single second spent with the characters and world.

Elves can't catch a break, even in Elias.

But how does it play? Well…!

Accepting the Mantle of Leadership

Trickcal is an auto-battler, in the sense that players do not control every single aspect of combat in a game. You select certain upgrades and developments mid-battle, set your teams up pre-battle, and can control the use of various skills and abilities during the course of a battle, but otherwise your chibis will do all of the fighting for you. At times auto-battlers tend to carry the stigma of being simple or boring, games that play themselves, but I find that Trickcal really seems to show that isn’t the case. There are numerous ways in which the game wants your input and strategizing, while also allowing you to take a backseat during farming or routine sorts of maintenance style tasks you might do while playing.

Generally, you'll make teams of 6, like this.

Teams are traditionally built of 6 characters, although some modes allow for 9, which are usually boss raids that can be fairly hectic and “end game” in their challenge. Team building rewards utilizing various characters of the same “Emotion”, or color, ranging from Purple (Depressed), Yellow (Vivacious), Innocent (Green), Composed (Blue) and Madness (Red). One of the more interesting wrinkles is seeing how these various Emotions manifest in the way the characters themselves act or behave; there are certainly characters you’ll encounter who don’t seem all that Composed, and others who you wouldn’t believe are “Mad” (read: angry), but…let’s just say that part is flavor.

Sometimes, you'll even make teams of 9!

The more of each type you have in a party, the bigger the bonuses are that you get, working in pairs: 2, 4, 6, in increasing bonuses. Mixed teams are also possible, such as 2/4, or in Raid style content, 2/4/2/1, 4/4/1, 2/2/2/1, and so forth. Generally, though, you’ll want to focus on trying to build teams of single Emotions to get the biggest buffs. If you’re a brand new player who intends to pick the game up from reading this: Pick a single Emotion and build that team, with Depressed being one of the best teams to build around, especially if you obtain the character Yomi. The bonuses are fairly easy to understand, in that 2 of a type gives 6%, 4 gives 28%, and 6 gives a whopping 66% increase to HP and Damage. Even just running 4 of a single Emotion can go a long way to allowing a newer player to make their way through difficult content as they build their roster and overall collection of Apostles to work under them.

Your Apostles need skills, gear, and crayons to be their best selves.

I’ll take this time to mention that Trickcal: Chibi Go is a gacha game, in that the characters, cards (equipment and runes) are pulled from corresponding banners that refresh regularly. I know that many people have complicated feelings towards gacha games and mechanics, so I wanted to take a moment to explain how Trickcal’s works: after pulling 200 times (150 for cards) on an Apostle banner, players can cash in to obtain the Apostle; the only difference here are Eldynes, who are generally more powerful characters that are occasionally added to the game and require 300 to “cash in”; that pity system carries over from banner to banner, and is one of the more generous versions of this mechanic I’ve seen. I will ALSO say that the Trickcal banner rates can be a little brutal, so perhaps that’s balanced out by the ability to store up.

Clearing challenging content like Dreamworlds isn't even that hard, nor does it take a "deluxe" team!

Generally, though, I have found Trickcal is one of the most Free To Play (F2P) friendly games I’ve ever played. The developers give out free 3 star character items like literal candy, characters can be farmed from 2 different dungeons, and generally there’s no direct benefit to pulling numerous copies of a character for someone who isn’t overly interested in min-maxing characters; Epid’s CEO has mentioned he mostly wants people to enjoy the world of Trickcal, and it shows, because even finding reasons to give the game money can be difficult, frankly. Pulling doesn’t reward reckless spending, materials are never scarce, and skins are about the only thing that exists to make your game “prettier”, being totally optional. That doesn’t  mean the game does not have micro-transactions or gacha, though, so if those are inherently deal breakers, I don’t know if Trickcal: Chibi Go will change your mind on that, which is a totally valid position to take.

Exploring Elias

If you do decide to make the plunge and check out the game, there’s a lot to do, and yet also all the time in the world to do it. A “daily” session of Trickcal at bare minimum can take about 10 minutes maximum, but if you’re reading the stories and doing other aspects, you basically can play as much as you want and do whatever you want in it, in various increments. The main battle part of the game is available through “Crusade”, which includes various difficulties and scaling difficulty to test your team against, and also presents you the best way to procure various equipment materials for your Apostles. Dungeons offers various ways to get specific resources, like money, upgrade materials, Apostle certificates, and much more. There’s PVP, a rotating set of Raid style events (Dimension Clash and Elias Frontier), and 2 separate daily challenges; frankly, there’s almost too MUCH to do in the game.

I said it was consistent.

This doesn’t even account for the various stories: the main story itself, apostle stories, event stories, and one of the things I enjoy is that past events are added fairly quickly to the game, meaning that you can catch up on character stories you may have missed and events you didn’t get to play without a hassle or some sort of obtuse system; Events are added a few patches after they initially ran, but last I checked there are only a few missing since I started playing that have not yet made it to the theater to watch.

Events and Apostle stories let you get to know them better, such as Kommy's political beliefs. (based)

There’s also events that run for a few weeks, generally to introduce new characters to the game/story. Some are characters that haven’t made their full appearance or had their own time in the spotlight, and others are stories that help flesh out a character in more detail by showing how they interact with the world around them; as I said above, the world building is excellent and rewarding, and the character gimmicks are often hilarious and charming, like xXionx, a self-aware cringelord failwoman with a heart of gold, ED, who thinks the world around her is a gigantic lucid dream, Shoupan, a delivery person who hates cops as much as she hates going slow, and Kyarot, a somewhat clueless farmer who loves her plants to the point of committing violent crimes in their defense. I have to give the game credit in that I can’t say I can (yet) think of a single character I dislike, as antagonistic characters are often fleshed out to have more depth than you’d expect.

Trickcal Chibi Go to the Polls

I could honestly talk about the game more, but that would probably be beating a dead horse at this point; I’ll simply say that I love this game a lot, and I really do hope you’ll consider giving it a try. It is currently available on iOS and Android devices, but there’s no PC client yet; there are some ways you can try to play it on Android emulation like MuMu, and I’ve had some success with doing so (many of the screenshots you see here were taken using MuMu, actually). Recommending games can often be a bit of a gamble, because talking about how much you love something doesn’t always mean you can convince people to take the plunge with you, and in the event that it isn’t their thing, well, at least you tried.

Again, the amount of times this game says out of pocket things that make me laugh is pretty regular.

Trickcal’s colorful and cute whimsy is what sold it to me, and it has a very vibrant and fun community that love the game across the globe; one of the fun parts of the Speaki explosion is also the vast amount of creativity I’ve seen for the game, and perhaps it’s just relaxing to see a game that people from various parts of the world all enjoy together and share how much they love the game with one another; I am not trying to say that Trickcal is going to solve global political relations, but it does at least feel nice to occasionally see something bring people together instead of set them at one another’s throats.

xXionx describing your average discord server.

If you do end up trying the game, don’t fret if some of it seems overwhelming; I plan on revisiting the game and talking about how to build teams, challenge various difficult aspects of the raid systems, and more in the future, and maybe I’ll even see some of you around in the game! Until then, keep your Apostles well fed on candy and cake, and I hope you have even half as much fun in Elias as I do!
