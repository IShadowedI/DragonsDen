Commander Focus: Whales, Banishing Knack, & H.M.S. Bounty, Unlikely Timeship

November 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With a great number of modern Universes Beyond designs, there's a bit of a lag time that occurs between ones initial evaluation of the card, and actually considering its ramifications. A great many of these appeal to Melvin, perhaps annoyingly so, with characters like Seven of Nine, Unassimilated becoming a 7/9, or Shadowfax, Lord of Horses having Haste's reminder text called out. There's a knowing wink, a sensible chuckle, and then you move on with your day. To that end, a great deal of these newly-printed Commanders get lost in the shuffle following their debut, because these are largely surface-level jokes, or bits of trivia you might see on a shortform video interspersed with gambling ads. Very few of these have their true merits or horrors taken to their reasonable conclusion, and that's very much the case when we talk about the H.M.S. Bounty, Unlikely Timeship.

This is a marvelous Spacecraft from the Star Trek main set, which does the adorable thing of searching for a Whale when it enters, and allowing for an extra turn by way of new syntax, once per game. For a great many people, there was the immediate shock of 'A Whale?', before writing off the obvious tutor target of Dreamtide Whale as being neat but not extraordinary. Those who've played Pauper in the last year, however, had a different reaction. 'A Whale'—followed by an exclamation mark—as we realized something absolutely horrible was newly-enabled. Yes, the subject of today is the occasional Pauper darling Steelfin Whale, but it's far from the only Whale we'll be aiming to spot as we go sightseeing. There's actually two very real combos, both in Monoblue, enabled largely by Whales, both taking center stage in this list. Let's dive below the surface, and talk about them.

"...search your library for a Whale card"

This line of text on a 5-mana Commander isn't a thrilling way to start, if we're being purely objective. Whales in Magic are few and far between (at least until Nauctis), but the good news is that average card quality on them is relatively high. Sure there are some stinkers like Killer Whale but compared to quite a few of the 'tutor a niche creature type' Commanders Whale is a pretty solid one to draw upon. Take for example, before we talk about the more relevant combo pieces, our humble Ethereal Forager. This is usually a ~2 mana flying 3/3 that gets back 1-2 Instants or Sorceries, and given we want to flex cards like Planar Incision to flicker and repeat the Bounty's tutor trigger, we can grab the Forager early on to continue searching through. Dreamtide Whale is also of course a slam dunk, nearly Stationing our Commander on its own, or fully so if we get its Proliferate trigger off. If we wanted to dig a bit deeper, there's also Horned Loch-Whale and its associated Adventure to shove attackers down the drain, or Aethertide Whale as a means of generating infinite Storm count off of infinite colored mana. It isn't pretty, but you can even dig through your Library with infinite colored mana, Waker of Waves, and another "Whale", Barkform Harvester. Simply discard and bottom the Waker over and over, filtering through two cards at a time. That's right—Whales can draw through your deck, so long as you have infinite mana, and a way to repeatedly tutor the Waker. How hard can that be?

Josh Brolin is in the driver's seat of our Commander, or something. Did anyone see this movie?

This does bring up an important point though, which is that Changelings are Whales; that's fairly obvious, but given Blue has seen quite a few solid options printed with the ability, it's a huge boon for the Bounty. Omni-Changeling is a clear pick, as having a Clone to find when applicable is just great to see, and as mentioned Barkform Harvester can help us reset key combo pieces or tutor targets, should they end up in the yard. The funniest Changeling we're keen to find, however, is one I've touched on already in another Commander Focus with a typal subtheme: Orvar, the All-Form. Yes, Monoblue's fourth-most-popular Commander is a Whale, and given our density of blink effects he's often just a way to find more Whales, as we repeat the Enters trigger on the Bounty. Beyond Blinks however, one of the deck's essential combo pieces (both of them, even) are single-target Instants, which mean we get additional knock-on synergy beyond the blinks and protection spells we're already running.

The cards in question are a pair you've likely seen if you've played any competitive format, being Banishing Knack & Retraction Helix. These cards have a long history of going infinite, whether with Jeskai Ascendancy in Modern, Rona, Herald of Invasion/Valley Floodcaller in Pioneer, or our target, Pauper darling Steelfin Whale. The effect these Instants give to any untap-happy Creature allows them to go infinite in any number of ways, usually via a mana-positive rock such as Sol Ring, a Moxen, or even something like a Monolith. Moreover, even if we take these cards at their fairest, it probably results in bouncing every nonland our opponents control, so in a pinch they're like a build-your-own Cyclonic Rift. Not bad for a 1-mana Orvar trigger.

Station to Station

The Thin White Duke referred to a Beluga, presumably.

Steelfin Whale specifically is a rather interesting card for the Bounty, because while it of course does silly things with a Knack effect, it also just Stations up cards extremely well. This matters for the Bounty of course, but we're on more than a few ways to make use of the ample power behind some of our cetacean comrades. With this deck having quite a high density of Artifacts, we can use things like Steelfin or Dreamtide to turbo out the full effect of Uthros, Titanic Godcore, which in turn helps us go mana-positive on another very specific whale. This can also help enable the full might of what is likely the best pre-Star Trek Spacecraft, Uthros Research Craft. Once this is big enough, it's not only a draw engine and threatening attacker, but also big enough to Station our Commander, or load up the final main Station target, Queen's Diamond. Queen's Diamond is a card that seems absolutely insane in any deck that can take advantage of its restricted mana, the likes of which have either a Commander they expect to die over and over again, or be targeted down by the table. I wouldn't call the H.M.S. Bounty a 'kill-on-sight' Commander, but it does quite enjoy being sacrificed and recast to tutor once more. As mentioned, so long as we have infinite mana and infinite means of making the Bounty re-enter, we can draw through the deck via Waker & Barkform...and a great way of getting there can be making Omni-Changeling into a copy of our Commander, and recasting the original infinitely, putting it back in the Command Zone due to the Legend Rule each time. The conditions to allow for this seem asinine, but let me remind you, basically everything but the mana engine are Whales.

On the topic of Stationing however, I'd like to bring up something we aren't doing, which is abusing Luxior, Giada's Gift. In a more fair version of this list, we can effectively turn the Charge counters placed on our Commander into +1/+1 counters, leading to fairly quick kills with Commander Damage, especially if we've yet to take an Extra Turn that game. Funneling ~18 power worth of Station into the Bounty isn't very difficult, but it is a fairly fragile gameplan, and tends to be antithetical to our normal path of blinking the ship. I say all of this because—if you wanted a more fair version of the deck, for whom lower-power tables would be an option—that's an excellent path to go down. That's true of frankly any Spacecraft Commander, and something to keep in mind as the subtype proliferates throughout the format. This can be said as well for Genji Glove, which provides an extra combat on top of Double Strike, leading to a whopping 3 additional turns. These aren't especially safe bets though, and it can often be preferable to keep the Bounty or the Research Craft as noncreatures for as long as one can, because it allows you to better dodge board wipes. Knowing when you want to attack with the Bounty, or not, determines the accessibility of certain combo lines later in the game. This is because of the oddity that is Bounty's single use-ish Extra Turn.

Turn, Turn, Turn

Before touching upon the win conditions in detail, I first want to broach the topic of Extra Turns with the Bounty, because its trigger is fairly novel and we've not gone into detail on it thus far. When it connects with an opponent, you always draw a card, then if you haven't taken an Extra Turn that game, and control a Whale, a delayed trigger is created to take an Extra Turn. Notably, the actual turn occurring is not contingent on having taken no Extra Turns, only the initial trigger; therefore if you copy the trigger or cause it to trigger again, on a turn wherein you've not taken Extra Turns previously, you can take as many as you like. Of course if you're already taking infinite (or close enough) triggers or Extra Combats, the spare turns are putting a hat on a hat. This is doubly true in Bracket 3, where chaining turns together is forbidden by writ of the Bracket, so we're on just a single way to copy this trigger, being Peter Parker's Camera. There's a world where you could play & copy something like a Vantress Visions via Virtue of Knowledge, but I'm of the opinion that less is more, in terms of the Bounty's combat trigger. The more important thing it's actually doing is closer to simply being a once-per-game Haste enabler, if we really boil it down to how the turn gets used. Oftentimes, in order to secure the necessary pieces for our win, we'll either need to have a Steelfin Whale without summoning sickness, and/or get back a Knack effect via Ethereal Forager. Ultimately, you can just flex your first tutor as Dreamtide, Station up the ship + Proliferate, and treat its effect as a boneless Explore—a draw+Land drop. That's fine if you don't want to think about holding your turn as part of a combo.

There's one Whale in here we've not yet talked about, and it's the oldest one to see inclusion. I'm talking about boneless Palinchron itself, Great Whale. For a long time this was the 'budget' version played for redundancy alongside Peregrine Drake, but given its reserve list status it's crept up in spite of being worse than its contemporaries. The thing is, neither of those contemporaries were Whales. By way of either Orvar or Displacer Kitten, we can use Mind Games to generate infinite mana in most scenarios, via untapping our Lands repeatedly with additional copies or flickers. Mind Games as a card is especially cool as an enabler for this, because it can be found through the exact same pieces that we use to grab Knack effects, also being a 1-mana Blue Instant. Micromancer, Spellseeker, even Dizzy Spell can all either grab either a targeting, Buyback-able piece or Knack, depending on which is more advantageous. The largest factor determining which endgame you'll be shooting for is your available mana—Great Whale certainly has a higher ceiling, but requires you start things off by casting a 7-drop, to say nothing of Orvar, & Mind Games. Usually the Steelfin combo is available with 2-3 mana, due to Affinity and the nature of its combo pieces being either free or mana-positive. If you're strapped for resources, the latter is less plug-and-play (as it often generates colorless mana) but far more accessible at minimal mana. Do note it requires access to Haste and/or a turn, however.

They call it Knack II because there's two copies of the effect.

Just to make it clear, given these combos vary in their degree of esotericism:

  • Combo 1 requires a hasty/non-sick Steelfin Whale, any 0-mana Artifact/Artifact which goes mana positive e.g. Mana Vault, & a Knack effect (Banishing Knack/Retraction Helix). Target the Whale, bounce your Artifact, play it again to untap the Whale, rinse and repeat for infinite storm count/Artifacts entering/Blue Creature tapping/etc. Once infinite mana is achieved, repeat targeting the Bounty, to infinitely tutor Whales and dig through deck via Waker of Waves you reset to Library with Barkform Harvester. Finish with Brain Freeze->Faerie Mastermind activation.
  • Combo 2 requires Orvar/Displacer Kitten, Great Whale, & Mind Games. Cast and/or target your Whale with the Mind Games, either flickering it or copying it. Rinse and repeat for infinite mana if you have Lands which produce >4 mana, which can then be funneled into copying/flickering the Bounty for infinite tutors into the same endgame as Combo 1.
  • If for some reason you cannot bounce and/or flicker your Bounty, you can search for Omni-Changeling to become a copy of it, and infinitely cast & have it die to the Legend Rule. This can be paid for via Queen's Diamond, which in turn can be fed Station power via the copied/blinked Great Whale. Note also that any endgame with Brain Freeze->Faerie Mastermind can do just fine with one of the two, as we can stack infinite Mastermind draws with Barkform Harvester activations, thereby ensuring we always have a card to draw from bottom. Similarly, Brain Freeze and just waiting for opponents to draw is also fine, if all you have from your combo is infinite Storm count.
  • Finally, remember that Ethereal Forager can be used alongside a Haste enabler and/or Extra Turn in order to try again on a failed combo, or recur pieces which have gone to the yard. If for some reason you cannot Brain Freeze/loop Waker, Mastermind activations with cards in your yard can draw out opponents while Barkform Harvester is continuously shuffling back your pieces. For this reason, the Barkform/Waker package is the most important thing to not see exiled, as between the two you can 'fix' any issues that crop up outside of a Rest in Peace.

A Whale-Oiled Machine

Beyond Whales, combo pieces, & tutors, you don't have much in the 99. This is a shockingly trim list for how esoteric some of its core pieces are; your mana rocks all get to be mana-positive combo pieces we can use for Steelfin, our flicker pieces to reset our Commander are functionally tutors (and often protection), and things that would normally be cantrips get to be Whales, e.g. Waker of Waves as a 2 mana Consider. In terms of our ramp which isn't mana rocks that go positive, there's pieces like Renowned Weaponsmith & Vedalken Engineer, which each help us push for a turn 3 Bounty, on top of translating their mana into unrestricted resources by way of the untap ability on Grim Monolith & Basalt Monolith. Bilbo, Thief in the Night functionally reduces the cost of our Commander, and can re-up a blink from the yard to act as...another tutor. For a while, we even had Universal Automaton in here as a Whale for {1}, which would become {0} by virtue of either Etherium Sculptor or Shuri, Wakandan Inventor. That ended up being a bit unnecessary, as we really didn't want to play a true vanilla even if it could serve as a combo piece—far better was it to just make sure we mulliganed, Tezzeret-ed, or otherwise tutored something like a Sol Ring at some point.

The final few slots belong to non-Whale card advantage, and some odd ramp pieces. First and foremost, there's obviously Rhystic Study as the final Game Changer here (the other two being mana-positive rocks). This is supplemented with a Mirrormade, which can either be our Commander or another Rhystic, or even something like a Sol Ring. Faerie Mastermind is a great advantage engine, and even helps us ensure a win by way of Brain Freeze via its ability to force card draw around the table. New to the game, however, is Nyota Uhura, Communications, and I'm quite high on her! Talion on 2 does fantastic work, and coming down at one fewer mana really does a ton, even if you lack the life loss component. As for mana, Energy Tap makes great use of the fact a lot of our Whales cost a ton, with special mention to Ethereal Forager's 6-for-2 thanks to Delve. Dream Halls meanwhile exists to expedite our Great Whale lines, as nobody has time for the full 7 cost in this day and age. We can readily find and pitch spare Creatures thanks to, say it with me, our Commander's capacity to tutor Whales.

Example Decklist: H.M.S. Bounty, Steelfin Speedrunner

While we aren't a dedicated Proliferate strategy, the fact we often tutor Dreamtide fairly early does mean it's worth building around a few counter synergies. These are largely in the small-scale, such as duplicating the Shield counter from Diamond City or retaining Saprazzan Skerry for longer than expected. Two fantastic use cases exist however that put some real threat behind Dreamtide Whale being allowed to survive, namely The Theorist, Jace Beleren & Lost in the Maze. The Theorist is like a boneless The One Ring, but comes attached to some solid removal. If we're able to plus him enough to use it twice in a row, expensive Commanders just...aren't allowed to exist. Much the same can be said of Lost in Maze, whose Stun counters can be a real pain to handle, on top of the universal Hexproof access thanks to having several Station-able permanents at all points in the game. Speaking of tapping, Unctus, Grand Metatect is an absolute stand-out here, giving our Steelfin the ability to loot...although frankly, given Station we can bet on abusing the hell out of that ability boardwide. One last funny interaction is via Shuri, who can turn one of your other Artifacts into a Bounty on the turn you swing to get an Extra Turn...thereby getting you another one. It doesn't come up much (and hasn't, in testing) but when it does I'm ready to squeal in delight.

One last thing, regarding "Whales"—that is to say, Changelings. We're playing three, between Orvar, Barkform, & Omni-, and because we can find them readily it is within our best interest to run some off-typal synergies which are low enough in opportunity cost to be okay without controlling their respective type. Most notable among these is Mana Sculpt, a spell that kind of died on arrival from Secrets of Strixhaven, which is essentially Mana Drain's little brother. We have a vested interest in bulk Colorless mana, so while I'm normally bearish on Counterspells which cost 2 or more, these end up worthwhile most of the time. Think of them like slow rituals we can save our ass with. Beyond that, Trenzalore Clocktower comes up fairly frequently, as between Dreamtide and our endpoint combo untapping Lands infinitely, in the case of Great Whale, we can flex it as a bona fide Timetwist because a few of our Whales are Time Lords.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

I've actually taken the liberty of reducing this list's power from where it stood in earlier versions, by taking away both Rings of Brighthearth & Power Artifact. Each of these goes infinite with one or both of our Monoliths, and makes for an extremely concise combo by which we can accrue infinite mana. I want to be doing so via Whales, and as such simply included more tutors to compensate; if you wanted to power up the Bounty, being able to play more Game Changers (given we can't even afford a Cyc Rift or TOR, with all three slots being spoken-for) is a huge buff. Powering down is likewise fairly easy—as stated, you can just lean into the combat nature of the Commander, and treat it like a chunky flyer that enjoys tutoring one of a few different Equipment. Being 5 mana really allows you to scale your power based on fast mana, and it's parallel to the fact said fast mana serves as combo pieces. A slower Bounty isn't just slower, it's less consistent in the execution of its best win condition.

Don't be a Moby Dick

Ceran manages to make the whale as majestic & mysterious as it ought to be, helped by smaller versions of the giants flanking our focus.

When I first read this card, I saw it only as a way to break Steelfin Whale and take an Extra Turn, and I was already excited at just that prospect. In really digging into it, however, the H.M.S. Bounty continued to surprise me not only regarding its own flexibility, but the shocking depth of Whales' playability in Magic. In spite of not really being a Creature type you think about often, there's everything here to form a Sisay-style combo engine that fills gaps and solves for awkward gamestates. This is also the first-ever Spacecraft we've covered on Commander Focus, which is a triumph in its own right. I had felt initially that making the card type legal as Commander seemed unmotivated, but after seeing the options from Star Trek it makes quite a bit more sense; that's not to say I'm a fan of all the Spacecraft from the set (why are so many Colorless?) but the good ones are great. I've had a dedicated Spacecraft-matter deck in paper since the release of EOE, in the form of Maester Seymour, but frankly this might unseat it, or at the very least sit alongside it.

That is to say nothing of the fact that we have Nauctis, the Sunken Realm arriving next year, and with it I would assume a whole host of new Whales. We've seen them in promotional art, and given this is a card from the main set, it's perhaps possible Wizards is teeing up some kind of Standard Whale-oriented strategy. I don't take the Bounty as a Standard-playable card, as cool as it is, but I appreciate the potential. While not necessarily as complex as Princess Yue, to whom I always return to when mentioning flavor inadvertently leading to rich mechanical depth, the H.M.S. Bounty is a wildly novel Commander in the grand scheme of things. The type it touches on has had no support prior, and as such may have just become the defacto Knack deck for Blue in Commander. That alone is a huge get, but the fact not one but two combos exist for Whales, on top of a wholly-Whale mana outlet with infinite blinks & mana, is just obscene. It's the kind of thing that makes you wonder if the designers knew this, and moreover, makes you want to build this list in paper. At time of writing, the H.M.S. Bounty has 18 decks on EDHREC, with more sure to come.

Until next time, call me Ishmael.
