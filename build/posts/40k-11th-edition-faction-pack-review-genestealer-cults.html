[40k] 11th Edition Faction Pack Review: Genestealer Cults

June 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With a new edition of Warhammer 40K comes a whole new bunch of Detachments, and we’re handling them in groups to make it easier to separate the wheat from the chaff, at least for now. We’ll go through the relevant changes to the Datasheets and rules at play, then see how the Detachments themselves stand up.

Thank you again to Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of these rules.

Genestealer Cults had a choppy 10th Edition thanks to the Codex version of their Army Rule being very high variance. However, after this was re-worked to something more predictable they’ve been a consistent lurking threat in high-level play, so let’s find out how they’re looking going into 11th Edition.

Datasheet Changes

  • Biophagus: Lose the Leader ability and gain Support.
  • Biophagus: FAQ that unit is equipped with Alchemicus Familiar, as per last FAQ.
  • Clamavus: Lose the Leader ability and gain Support.
  • Locus: Lose the Leader ability and gain Support.
  • Nexos: Lose the Leader ability and gain Support.
  • Nexos: Battlefield Analysis ability reduces CP cost by 1CP, as per last FAQ.
  • Acolyte Hybrids with Hand Flamers: Demo charge range 8”, as per 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Acolyte Iconward: Summon the Cult ability reduced down to 8” from 9”.
  • Goliath Rockgrinder: Demo charge range 8”, as per 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Goliath Rockgrinder: Add the FRAME keyword (measure to hull).
  • Goliath Truck: Demo charge range 8”, as per 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Goliath Truck: Add the FRAME keyword.
  • Hybrid Metamorphs: Change to Brood Surge ability, to keyword the move as a surge move and align with 11th ed surge move definitions.
  • Kelermorph: Hypersensory Abilities distance decreased down to 8” from 9”.
  • Primus: Leading unit ability gives +1 to hit, as per 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Reductus Saboteur: Demo charge range 8”, as per 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Reductus Saboteur: Planted Explosives ability distance decreased down to 8” from 9”.
  • Sanctus: Creeping Shadow ability distance decreased down to 8” from 9”.
  • Sanctus: Cloaked Assassin ability changed to ignore snap shooting instead of overwatch. A net buff due to other snap shooting abilities.

Rule Changes

  • Army Rule - Cult Ambush: Resurgence Points remain, and are identical to the last Balance Dataslate from 10th ed. However, there is a major change due to the new character attachment rules. Reviving a unit via Cult Ambush requires every model in the unit to have the Cult Ambush Rule, and GSC characters do not have it. This means that if you attach a character to a unit it cannot be recycled via Cult Ambush because they do not separate when the last bodyguard model dies.  Keep rocking those 2 RP 5 model Genestealer ambushes - but if you attach the Patriarch, they can’t come back.
  • Cult Ambush Markers: Distance for your opponent to remove markers decreased down to 8” from 9”. Units arriving via Cult Ambush Markers now arrive via ingress to be in line with 11th ed ingress rules, which also decreases the distance away from enemy units down to 8” from 9”. Additionally, units in Cult Ambush can make an ingress move in the first battle round using this method.
  • Biosantic Broodsurge Detachment, Simulated Bio-surge Stratagem: Changed to be a flat +2 to charge, instead of +1 per target up to +3.
  • Biosantic Broodsurge Detachment, Predatory Instincts Enhancement: Changed wording for reduction in cost for Heroic Intervention stratagem to be -1CP instead of 0CP. Additionally, added rider that if this unit uses Heroic Intervention, it doesn’t prevent other units from doing so this phase (used to only let this unit do it an additional time if another unit did so beforehand).
  • Brood Brothers Auxilla, Adaptive Reprisal Enhancement: Changed wording for reduction in cost for Heroic Intervention stratagem to be -1CP instead of 0CP.
  • Brood Brothers Auxilla, In the Shadow of Iron Stratagem: Distance away from enemy units reduced down to 8” from 9”.
  • Brood Brothers Auxilla, Regimental Reinforcements Stratagem: Distance away from enemy units reduced down to 8” from 9”.
  • Brood Brothers Auxilla, Integrated Tactics Detachment Rule: Second paragraph restrictions for units allowed to be taken matches current FAQ.
  • Host Of Ascension Detachment, Primed and Readied Stratagem: Costs 2CP, as per last 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Host of Ascension Detachment, Return to the Shadows Stratagem: Used end of opponents fight phase, as per last 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Host of Ascension Detachment, Tunnel Crawlers Stratagem: Setup more than 6” away from enemy units, as per last 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Host of Ascension Detachment, Prowling Agitant Enhancement: Distance to activate reduced down to 8” from 9”.
  • Outlander Claw Detachment, Along Shadowed Trails Stratagem: Distance reduced down to 8” from 9”.
  • Outlander Claw Detachment, Rapid Takeover Detachment Rule: Bonus OC and sticky, as per the last 10th ed Balance Dataslate.
  • Xenocreed Congregation Detachment, The Downtrodden Rise Stratagem: Changed to be used at end of opponent’s movement phase, to align with 11th ed not having a Reinforcement Step.
  • Xenocreed Congregation Detachment, Tireless Fervour Stratagem: Wording updated to match 11th ed rules. Otherwise unchanged.
  • Xenocreed Congregation Detachment, Deeds That Speak to the Masses Enhancement: Start with 2 bonus resurgence points, as per the last 10th ed Balance Dataslate.

Available Detachments

Genestealer Cults retain all their Codex Detachments and the popular Final Day digital Detachment.

  • Host of Ascension - 3DP
  • Biosanctic Broodsurge - 2DP
  • Brood Brothers Auxilia - 2DP
  • Final Day - 2DP
  • Outlander Claw - 2DP
  • Xenocreed Congregation - 2DP

These are joined by three new 1DP options:

  • Heroes of the Uprising, focused on some of the elite warriors of the Cults.
  • Purestrain Broodswarm, bringing love for (you guessed it) Purestrains.
  • Xenocult Masses, buffing up Neophyte Hybrids.

Heroes of the Uprising

Kelermorph. Credit: Rockfish

This Detachment is all about the minor characters of the GSC range- some of our most memorable sculpts, these little guys provide a range of support roles and ‘assassin’ type functions in game. The Heroes Detachment aims to make them better at this and give you additional synergies with a 2 Detachment Point option.

Detachment Rule: Killer Reputation

The Killer Reputation rule gives you the following:

  • Friendly Kelemorph / Locus / Reductus Saboteur / Sanctus models gain KILLER keyword.
  • Friendly KILLER models' attacks gain rerolls of 1 to hit and wound.

This rule is a lovely little value add on the supporting case of GSC characters, making them all that little bit more dangerous and consistent. None of them are HUGE output in and of themselves, but GSC is an army that does damage by committee, so having these characters (one or two of which make it into most GSC lists already) be a bit more consistent is great. More fragile armies like Aeldari / Drukhari will really feel that boosted damage, and you can chip off harder targets more consistently too.

Enhancements

  • Gene-Tailored Toxins. This gives a Locus or Sanctus model +1 damage. Always nice to have, and the damage 3 dagger Sanctus with rerolls against his ‘prey’ target is particularly nice for early trading.
  • Contraband Munitions. This gives a Kelemorph or Saboteur +2 Strength on their ranged attacks. The Sabs demolition charge going to S11 is lovely, especially if combo-ing for rerolls to wound from a Goliath Truck.

Stratagems

  • Living Up to Legend (1 CP) - if a friendly KILLER unit destroys an enemy unit or character, you can un-battle-shock each visible friendly battle-shocked GSC unit within 12. This is quite nice in a world of persistent shock effects, and may prove clutch, but it’s rather situational too.
  • Surging Broodworship (1 CP) - Give a Killer unit DEVASTATING WOUNDS in shooting or fight phase. Great wherever you can access wound rerolls - as alluded to above, the big winners are the Sanctus against his prey target, and the Saboteur with Goliath wound rerolls. Very nice if you need to drop something at a key moment or take out an enemy character. (TwoHorse: Worth remembering that the melee Sanctus has Anti-Infantry 3+ on his knife, so this stratagem is absolutely devastating into 3W infantry when combined with the +1D enhancement.)
  • Loyal to the End (1 CP) - Fight phase when an enemy unit targets a friendly KILLER unit. If that KILLER unit is destroyed, then on a 1 that unit deals a mortal wound on death, and on a 2+ it can fight on death before it is removed. Great on the Locus and Sanctus for doing some damage even if the combat goes against you!

Is It Good?

This Detachment works nicely if you build decisively for it by integrating several of the KILLER characters, which is something almost any GSC Detachment can potentially do. It doesn’t unlock any new capabilities for the faction overall, but distributing output amongst multiple lone-op characters is an interesting hedge against concentrated effort by your opponent.

Certain detachments benefit more than others - Outlander Claw has historically enjoyed taking multiple Saboteurs or Kelemorphs, for example, while Biosanctic Broodsurge or Host of Ascension generally only took one or two. If it has a limitation it’s that such units are still incredibly fragile, and the 11th edition core rules incentivize your opponent to build a list that can bring guns across the board quickly. Leaning into the Detachment too heavily risks spreading yourself too thin, but that’s the sort of listbuilding tension that makes GSC interesting.

Purestrain Broodswarm

Purestrain Genestealers: Who's ready for razor hugs?
Credit: Pendulin

This Detachment on Purestrain Genestealers, providing mobility, tricks, and damage boosts as well. Purestrains do a lot of work as the Vanguard units in your army, skirmishing at the start of the game before the rest of your army gets stuck in, and the toolkit here amplifies their impact. It now shares a detachment keyword with the Biosanctic Broodsurge detachment, so you won’t be able to double-up to supercharge your Purestrains, but as you’ll see that’s a shrewd design choice.

Detachment Rule: Enemy Within

At the end of your Fight phase, friendly unengaged Purestrain Genestealer units can be placed into strategic reserves.

Repositioning is an excellent tool every time it shows up, but previously GSC have accessed it through either getting units killed or single-target stratagems, most often on ranged units. Being able to relocate Purestrains or threaten a future Rapid Ingress is a good way of pressuring the opponent to screen meticulously - which is already imperative against a GSC army. You can also use Ambush Markers to place respawned Purestrain units in more defensive positions and then pick them up if your opponent doesn’t give you a charge target. If you take this detachment, you’re likely taking three squads of Purestrains.

This detachment has the PURESTRAIN tag and can’t combo with another PURESTRAIN detachment.

Enhancements

  • Mark of the Children (Upgrade): This gives a Purestrain unit +1 Toughness and Strength, and a 4+ save. A nice buff to their survivability and threat, but you’re paying more per unit.
  • Talons of the Sire: Patriarch model only. This unit’s attacks can reroll wound rolls of 1. On a unit with 40 attacks and DEVASTATING WOUNDS, rerolls are always nice to have.

Stratagems

  • Lurk and Strike (1 CP): Gives a Purestrain Genestealers unit fall back and charge. Purestrains don’t often survive after making contact, but this helps defeat opponents who’ve learnt the cunning tactic of crashing a rhino into your Purestrains and then ignoring them.
  • Crawling Horror (1 CP): Start of your opponent’s movement phase - give a Purestrain Genestealers unit -6” detection range. This takes the detection range of the unit down to its minimum value of 9”. The timing ensures your opponent has an opportunity to move accordingly, but even with full knowledge this is a great way to keep 10 bodies with a Patriarch safe or force significant commitment from your opponent.
  • Inhuman Reactions (1 CP): A D3+3” reactive move when an opponent ends a move within 8” of you. Combined with the stratagem above, you can force an opponent to stand precisely between 8” and 9” if they want to engage a key unit, which is an awkward movement puzzle to solve.

Is It Good?

This is an interesting toolkit to bolt onto the existing Detachments. Repositioning multiple deep-strike units is great, all the stratagems are useful, and the enhancements are both interesting and could see use if the points cost is reasonable.

The core issue this will have is that Purestrains have few damage buffs available in most detachments. This allows you to load in 3 Purestrain units, potentially a Patriarch, and take advantage of the skirmishing toolkit to draw the opponent out early while maintaining late-game mobility with the reposition. This is a welcome solution to the classic problem from 10th edition that the Genestealer Cults often couldn’t get good use from their namesake unit.  Now if you want to bring a skirmishing frontline or a large melee hammer unit you have tools to do so alongside your baseline detachment.

Detachment Three: Xenocult Masses

Neophytes. Credit: Rockfish

This Detachment buffs the hordes of Neophyte models we have come to associate with GSC lists over the years. These little idiots are fantastic mission play pieces, with loads of bodies, OC and resurrection mechanics, and shocking damage output. This Detachment helps make them even better at what they do!

Detachment Rule: Hordes of the Faithful

In your Command phase, if a friendly Neophyte Hybrids unit is within a terrain area, that unit heals 3 wounds. A lovely little buff that means a Neophyte Hybrid unit on an objective is reviving D3+6 models a turn - yum. Crucially, this also means that if the Cult Icon in your unit is destroyed, you can use this to revive models anyway (and get your Cult Icon back).

This Detachment has the HOSTS tag and cannot be taken with another HOSTS Detachment.

Enhancements

  • Inspired to Greatness: Magus / Primus model only. This unit’s attacks can re-roll damage rolls. The Magus is pretty bad, and so is the Primus after his brutal nerfing last year. Given you probably want a Benefictus in your Neophytes, I don’t rate this much.  (TwoHorse: The core changes to 11th edition make both the Magus and the Primus much more relevant than they were in 10th, and a 20-brick of Neophytes usually has 6 variable-damage weapons in it. If the points are reasonable, this is worth a look.)
  • Devious Disguises (Upgrade): Neophyte Hybrids unit only. This unit gains -3” detection range. Making your Neophyte bricks harder to target is nice, as it combos well with the resurrection mechanic. I can see this working well on an OC drowning objective game.

Stratagems

  • Eyes of the Cult (1 CP): Start of your shooting phase. A Neophyte unit in a terrain area can give an enemy unit in that area +6” detection range. Nice for helping you dig your opponent out without exposing a more valuable unit.
  • Fanatical Hail (1 CP): Start of your shooting phase, give a Neophyte Hybrids unit re-roll hit rolls. This is EXCELLENT -  a massive damage boost to a Benefictus brick, which already has Lethal hits and access to Ignores Cover and +1 AP from the Ridgerunners. Particularly saucy in Final Day, which can give a Neophytes unit Sustained Hits as well.
  • Slunk from the Underbelly (1 CP): -1 AP for one of your Neophyte units in the shooting phase. This combos really well with the -3” detection range too to give your opponent an issue knocking you off of objectives. Fall short at range, and the brick is regrowing almost half at a time! (TwoHorse: This one feels a bit meh to me. Neophytes in cover weren’t surviving a hail of ap-1 shooting previously, and this is unlikely to change that. It’s neat, but I’m skeptical that it’ll come up.)

Is It Good?

Based on what works for GSC in the present day, this may be the immediate standout at release. Neophyte objective play is an engine of the army, and making them harder to kill aids this greatly. Rerolling hits on a Neophyte brick is also a very significant damage buff here, so the Detachment aids both your survivability AND damage in meaningful ways. Not being able to combo with Host of Ascension at 3DP is good for the game, but this offers a reason to run one or two big Neophyte bricks in almost any detachment - particularly Xenocreed, where the rules supported Neophyte utility but lacked a means of improving their output.

Final Thoughts

This is among the most interesting Faction Packs we’ve seen so far, and shows off the new Detachment system at its best. Each Detachment has synergies with multiple existing Detachments, and none of them appear to be wasted print or ludicrously powerful. This is probably because the 10th edition GSC Detachments were already heavily focused on specific datasheets, so the new Detachments create new possibilities rather than simply buffing units that were already in a previous list. We’re big fans of this Faction Pack, a welcome confluence of fluff and crunch that makes the game more interesting.
