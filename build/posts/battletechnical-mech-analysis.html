Battletechnical: Mech Analysis

June 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
After figuring out a game plan (of which Peri has written great examples of both pure aggression and big bruiser styles), one of the most important things you can do is figuring out exactly which mechs you’re going to bring. You can just read our mech championships to get an idea of some of the very best units, or read through our reviews, but there are going to be times where you want to take a look at a mech and quickly figure out if it’s any good for yourself. Maybe you’re playing in a campaign and got a few options of mechs to salvage, or you just really want to use that Ostwar in the Illician Lancers forcepack that we haven’t looked at yet. This process can guide you through analyzing a mech, and both get an idea of its power level and how to effectively run it on the table.

This isn’t a completely formulaic process – looking at the armor on a mech won’t tell me to lower the grade by half a point or anything. It’s judgement based, requiring context of use, and somewhat personal opinion. We all evaluate mechs based on the same criteria, but each of us will end up rating a mech slightly differently.

Step 1: Context

This is the easiest step, as we aren’t looking at the mech yet, just what kind of game we’re playing. Generally we want to look at four things: game size, map size, game format, and era. For most Tabletop Battles reviews we’re looking at games of 8-10k BV, on a standard double mapsheet/single neoprene map, playing the playtest (soon to be core) missions, and not being concerned about era availability. Game size is mostly going to effect how we feel about high battle value, as a mech at 3k or so is such a large chunk of a list it needs to be extra effective to be worth it. Map size changes how we think of speed – if you’re on an oversized map, you likely need faster mechs to interact with objectives potentially spread apart. The game format can be key. If you’re playing in a campaign, you might put extra emphasis on standard fusion engines over XL engines to get extra time to retreat mechs as they take heavy damage or might add value to energy-only mechs for longer missions or to protect from pilot hits. Playing with the playtest missions adds value to having an active probe, hands, and ECM in your list (though you may not need all in one single mech) to accommodate a variety of possible objectives. Finally, we need to consider the era. While a good mech is good regardless of era, and a bad mech isn’t really redeemed just because it’s surrounded by bad mechs (there are also good mechs in every era), it’s still important to consider that it’s OK to go a little slower if you’re playing in succession wars because you don’t need the high mobility to avoid pulse lasers, or that an early game may have more melee because it does more damage than other weapons on the mech.

Peri: One of the most significant things about context is that it is the aspect that depends the most on your local meta. Priorities can shift around quite a bit depending on what your local looks like. If you know that gauss mechs are extremely prevalent that might boost the anti-ballistic armors, or if pulse lasers are ubiquitous then reflective armor goes up in value. If you share a local with ATMs Georg, you should be taking an ECM, or maybe even an ECM and an AMS stacked together. There are a lot of pieces of specific use case equipment in BattleTech that get substantially better if you know your local tends to favor the strategies that they counter.

Liberty: Context is that little thing that a lot of people reading our reviews often forget. The light in which a ‘Mech is viewed can rapidly change based on what environment you’re using it in. As Peri said, if your local doesn’t work the way our template pans out then your priorities are indeed going to shift. Maybe you will want to pay some extra change on a ‘Mech to get specialty armor or bring along a bunch of Re-Engineered Lasers and the like.

The world in which you are interfacing with the game is going to color what you’re looking for, be it longevity of a campaign, the outstanding violence of a pickup game, the cunning brutality of a tournament, or a long running skirmish league, every ‘Mech has its place. Well, except the Phoenix Hawk 99… There’s no context where that thing’s helpful.

Step 2: The Chassis

I’m going to start by looking at the base aspects of the mech before considering the weapons. While the two are very related, I want to get a sense in my head of how it’s going to move and how it’s going to take damage before I start thinking about how it deals damage, especially because the movement abilities of the mech dictate how effectively the weapons can be used.

Going back to the example of an Ostwar, I’m going to look at the 3M variant. It’s a 65 tonner that moves 4/6 with nearly full armor coverage, so I don’t see anything immediately inherently wrong – that also describes a Thunderbolt, a mech we know can have successful variants. It’s useful as I go along to compare with other mechs I know, because I can make inferences from running those mechs to see how I might use this one. The armor layout here is pretty good – at least 20 points across every location in the front, important to keep an AC/20 from immediately going internal, and 7+ across the rear, which protects from a pulse laser. With the standard engine I know that this is going to be able to take a decent beating and keep functioning, so it starts off in a good place.

Peri: Generally speaking for the chassis there are some actual rules that you want to generally keep in mind. Anything more than about 700 BV needs to have 10 points of armor everywhere but the arms if at all possible. If you are investing any real BV in a mech, it has to be able to survive a Clan LPL. Personally, I don’t really think of armor in general amount, I think of it in breakpoints. 10, 15, 20, and 30 are all important breakpoints due to the amount of weapons that deal 5/10/15 damage clusters. If at all possible, you want to get over one of those breakpoints, because falling short of them will lead to crits coming in noticeably more often. The difference between 28 armor and 30 armor is one you can feel very palpably in game despite not being, objectively, that wide of a jump. 7 armor is also a vital breakpoint for rear armor, as that is the damage of a clan medium pulse laser, and CMPLs are pretty common backstabbing weapons.

XL engines are an interesting case. If you are getting something you couldn’t get without it, it can be a pretty good deal, and XL mechs with no explosives are often more durable than standard engine mechs with explosives in practical terms.

Liberty: Peri does a good job of summing up my feelings here. Breakpoints are quite important, as are the types of armor that you are getting. Location of ammo, or other explosives, is also of import. Armor is important but so is making sure that any one location being breached does not present the possible problem of a ‘Mech fully unmaking itself. CASE protecting bays in functional ways is a very large consideration for any ‘mech trying to make any use of them. Take, for example, that Stalker 5M I ragged on in the Stalker review and compare it to our friend the Ostwar here.

The Ostwar keeps all of its ammo in CASE protected torso bays that, while still dealing head hits like a son-uva-bitch, will not outright kill or fully cripple the ‘Mech should they go off. It will still be able to do something should one of the torsos enter low earth orbit. That Stalker on the other hand, will not. If it gets leg attacked and has its leg ammo pop then it will all but stop existing.

I personally quite like an XL engine ‘Mech when they are using the equipment well. Explosives are only so much of a problem if they are in a position to actually harm the engine, IE in the torsos with the engine slots rather than CASE, or CASE II, protected arms. If the weight savings of the engine are being used in helpful, competent ways then they are really just good. Removing the outlier of ‘both side torsos fell off and the machine just kept going’ that occasionally happens an old Royal Battlemaster is really only so much more survivable than a Warlord 2G when you consider that more often than not a machine that loses a side torso is either:

  • Extremely unlucky and just got drilled

  • In such a bad state that much more damage would have just crumpled the machine over with an SFE anyhow.

Step 3: The Weapons

Looking at the weapons I see an LRM 20 (with Artemis IV), two ER medium lasers, and two streak SRM 4s. Firing all of these generates 22 heat, and it sinks 24. At first glance great, it’s always going to be heat neutral, but looking at this weapon set that doesn’t matter. The LRM has minimum 6 range, so you’re going to be markedly inaccurate from 4 hexes in (effectively medium range), while the ER mediums are at short range there and your streak SRMs want to be at 3 hexes. This tells me I have two distinct weapon groups with not a lot of overlap – the LRM at range, and the medium lasers and SRMs in close. The medium lasers will be at medium if you can hold to exactly LRM short range, but that’s really not a reliable way to do damage.

Having those two separate weapon groups means I need enough heat sinking for one or the other, not both. This isn’t too bad because it’s only two tons being spent on extraneous heat sinking, but those are still tons that could’ve been used in design to give me a more viable weapon group for either range. On a bigger mech with more guns this would matter more – here it’s relatively minor, as dropping the 2 heat sinks isn’t enough to give us a heat BV discount, but with more guns I can usually save some BV by only being sunk for one range bracket at a time. We’ve also got 3 tons of ammo (18 turns) for the LRM, when I’m looking at games that almost always go to 8 turns. Again not a big deal, but for our games it adds some risk of an ammo explosion to that side torso with bins that you won’t be emptying during normal play.

Peri: I actually have a really simple rule here. For every 1000 BV, a mech should be capable of doing 15 damage at minimum in each of its viable range brackets. Ideally, you would hit 20 damage or better per 1000 BV, 15 is the minimum for viability. If you are doing more than this at your viable range brackets, you are in the money. Mechs like the Phantom C, Blood Asp B, most Kodiaks, and Locust 1Vb sail past this rate significantly and become extremely dangerous as a result. You will notice that an ATM-12 per 1000 BV happens to get you over this threshold, and that should explain why we tend to be so kind to mechs that carry one.

Liberty: Peri’s rule is a good one but on top of it you must remember something else that can stand as an important part of reviewing a Chassis: How is it doing that damage? The subject Ostwar is a 1550 ish BV ‘Mech that does 20 long and 26 short possible damage. This is quite acceptable for cost, about on target. But none of these damage groupings are in big hits, in fact the biggest they ever get is a 5 pointer.

There are two types of damage to take into consideration when looking over a ‘Mech: Hole-punching and Hole-exploiting. This is a very good mix of exploiting damage between the pile of 5 pointers and the pair of streaks. It’s not the best option for the price or tonnage in the category but it is a damned good brick that can do the job and stay alive for quite some time. Many ‘Mechs will have a good mix of both, LB-X Autocannons are quite useful for this as they can do both punching and exploiting damage, at least in the 10 and 20 classes.

Step 4: BV and the Mech's Role

At the last step I want to combine the weapons, the movement, and the durability to figure out what I can actually do with the mech, then look at BV to figure out if it’s worth bringing. It’s worth noting that BV is remarkably good at this – there are outliers that use clan large pulse, or are very fast chassis with very short ranged guns, but for most mechs BV pretty accurately represents its capacity. What matters to figure out if its worth the BV is to determine whether what it can do is actually effective.

Looking at this Ostwar, I’ve got a moderate damage long range weapon in the LRM 20 and moderate damage with some crit seeking in the short range package of ER mediums and SRMs. Moving 4/6, it’s not going to be able to dictate range on the fight, so having some long range weapons is good, but this is somewhere I want to have overlap in the weapon groups – I’m firing either the long range or the short range weapons, so either way I’m sitting there with BV not being used. Neither group is a particularly large amount of damage either, so this sits there as a mech that exists but doesn’t dictate the flow of the fight.

Thunderbolt. Credit: porble

As I noticed earlier, this feels a lot like a Thunderbolt, so I’m going to compare it with the TDR-7S, a variant that costs 25 BV more. They both exist in a pretty similar space, being a chunk of reasonably durable armor with slow movement and unexceptional firepower. The Thunderbolt, however, has an ER large laser to go with the LRMs, meaning I’m spending BV on a weapon I’m using all the time rather than just some of the time, and as such is able to bring more damage for the BV.

Ultimately, I end up looking at this Ostwar and thinking that it’s adequate, but isn’t doing anything particularly well and is outclassed in its role as a trooper – the only thing that really stands out is bringing an ECM, which will be valuable, but isn’t something unique enough that I’d want to bring this. At the same time, I’m not going to be tremendously disappointed when I bring it – the mech functions, I’m not sitting there unable to fire an obvious weapon group because I don’t have the heat capacity, or running all short range weapons on a mech that won’t be able to close into range to use them. I’ll end up rating it somewhere around a C+ - it’s not going to win you a game, but it’s not going to lose you a game either, and it doesn’t do anything particularly interesting.

Peri: My philosophy towards BV after a year of playing at tournaments and several years of BattleTech content on this site, is that you should never spend more than 1600 BV on a mech that is “Fine” or “Adequate”. If you are spending that much of a percentage of your list on a mech, it needs to be outright good, it can’t just be ok. There are tons of passable mechs in the 1500-1600 BV range on functionally every availability if you care about availability, and you can win games just by stacking them up until you run out of BV. Mechs that pass that BV range need to be doing high damage efficiently while having good durability to make up for the opportunity cost of not just grabbing another mech. Light and Medium mechs in the 2000-3000 BV range are almost never good because they lack the durability to make up for the cost of not just bringing another mech.

When it comes to the role of a mech, I am of the opinion that there are only really 5 mechs in BattleTech when you boil it down enough. Frontliners to walk forwards and trade hits, Backliners to use long range weapons to support the frontline while minimizing risk to themselves, Fast-Movers that flank, do objectives, and put firepower where you need it, Flexible mechs that can fit any role you need in a pinch (this is the domain of the 5/8 Clan Heavy Mech and the 4/6 IS mech with PPCs/LRMs and real, meaningful guns to cover inside the min ranges), and bad mechs that fail to find a real use case. All mechs are some variation on one of these roles, and that is how I tend to think about them as a result. The above mentioned Ostwar is a bit of an awkward mech. It wants to be flexible between Frontline and Backline use, but it is under-invested in either role leaving it behind the curve on damage at both long and short range. This is a common trend with Inner Sphere Flexible/Trooper(long running community term for the same concept) designs, they end up with damage that is too far below the curve to really pull their weight in either role.

Liberty: I am of two minds in role and BV. One side of me wants a tide of cheap steel that does alright damage and simply grinds the opponent down in a glacial drive that slowly but surely takes the field no matter the losses because there will always be more to come. The other… well the other wants to feed people to Kodiaks and laugh as they drown in their own viscera.

Both Peri and Jack are right, when it comes down to it if you’re spending north of 1600 on a ‘Mech that you look at and think ‘that sure is a ‘Mech’ then you’re paying too much. There is almost certainly something that does the job better. Peri is correct on the 5 ‘Mech classifications but I am of the opinion that they are missing a coveted 6th that, while perhaps a subset of ‘frontliner’ is quite important: The Buzzsaw. Something that acts as a forward centerpiece that pours terrible amounts of damage into something turn, after turn, after turn, after turn, until someone makes it stop.
