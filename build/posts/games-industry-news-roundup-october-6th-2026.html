Games Industry News Roundup- October 6th, 2026

October 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Wizards Announces Changes to Collector Booster Packs

The humble booster pack has been the backbone of trading card games for decades now, as both a way for fans to grow their card collection as well as the central focus of draft-style organized play events. With such widespread use, it’s no wonder that companies are still tweaking the exact contents of their boosters all this time later, and the card game juggernaut Wizards of the Coast is no exception. On Monday, Wizards of the Coast announced that it would be updating the content of its collector booster packs in early 2027, with the goal that “every booster you open should feel exciting and meaningful”, alongside adding a chance to pull a god pack.

These new god packs, which have a 1 in 1,000 chance of being pulled from regular boosters and 1 in 300 from collector boosters, is like hitting the motherlode for any Magic player. The standard packs contain 14 rare or mythic rare cards, 2 of which are foil and can include rare alternate versions of cards like those from the recent Japan Showcase, while the collector booster version of these packs contain 8 foil rare or mythic rare cards, and a foil land. Both versions of these booster packs will also contain a commemorative “you did it!” card to celebrate the buyers stroke of incredible luck. 

This does come with a drawback, however. While these new packs increase the total production of rare foil cards, Wizards is removing common and uncommon foil cards from their booster packs and scaling back their presence in collector booster packs. The company cites the reality of card and foil production as a contributing factor in this decision, alongside the fact that the foil versions of these common cards are often overlooked by fans and content creators in booster pack openings.

“How does removing cards make opening Collector Boosters more exciting? We'll be honest here—it doesn't,” Wizards writes in the announcement. “But we do know that most people who open Collector Boosters mostly skip past these cards. Not everyone, but enough of a majority.”

“That's part of the trade-off here. As booster printing becomes more complex, we need to make trade-offs in both complexity and capacity. We wanted to add God Packs and include Japan Showcase cards in Booster Packs, but to do so we needed to find room in our printing capacity to enable that. The items we're removing are both unlikely to be missed and create enough space in our capacity to enable the cool things.”

These new booster packs are set to launch with the Nauctis: The Sunken Realm set in early 2027.

Credit: Mantic Games

Mantic Games Announces Warpath Universe Expansion for Sci-Fi Settings

Mantic Games is making a big swing for the fence this November with its upcoming expansion book, Warpath Universe: Command Protocols, acting as an expansion to not just one, but for all three of its sci-fi setting games.  

Command Protocols is a significant expansion for the games which take place in the Warpath setting, those being Deadzone, Firefight, and Epic Warpath. The expansion book aims to bring the three games together as a way to tell larger stories in the setting, from the close quarters skirmishes of Firefight to the massive army battles of Epic Warpath. 

Among the most prominent additions to the Warpath universe added with the Command Protocols expansion is the inclusion or army lists for Nameless forces in both Firefight and Deadzone. The Nameless, an enigmatic alien race that look like what would happen if you gave a Lovecraftian monster a laser gun, are scheduled to have their long-awaited 28mm army debut alongside the Command Protocols expansion. 

Not to leave Epic Warpath fans in the lurch, while the new expansion book doesn’t bring players a whole new faction to play with, it instead has something for every player. Rather than focusing on just one Epic Warpath faction, the expansion book is giving love to all of them, with the new release including new unit rules for every faction currently in the game.

The new Warpath Universe: Command Protocols expansion is set to go live for pre-order on Mantic’s website on November 2nd. 

Credit: Tabletop Growth Partners

MyMiniFactory Marketing Lead Departs to Launch Her Own Company

It’s easy to leave a company when things are going badly, to run and grab a lifeboat before the whole ship goes down and figure it out later. It’s much harder most of the time to leave when things are going well, and strike out on your own to follow your own path into the unknown. That’s the exact position former MyMiniFactory Marketing and Brand Head Anjuli Smith finds herself in now, as she announced that she’s left the 3D print giant to form her own company, Tabletop Growth Partners.

Smith joined MyMiniFactory after working at Asmodee UK as a purchasing assistant to become the company’s new head of marketing and brand. In this role, Smith focused on expanding MyMiniFacotry’s online presence to potential customers, as well as engaging with existing companies to bring them in to the 3D printing world through a partnership with MyMiniFactory, including big names in the space such as Steamforged Games and Modiphus. 

Despite her success in the role, Smith decided to strike out on her own path at the end of last month, founding Tabletop Growth Partners, a talent agency focused on helping smaller tabletop games and wargaming companies with marketing, digital growth, and business development services. 

“With over 5,000 new board games releasing annually I think it can be pretty daunting for smaller publishers who lack the massive budget of The Op’s Flip 7,” Smith told Boardgamewire in an interview. “Being able to use someone who people already know and trust to open a door for you is a huge win.”

Smith touts the Tabletop Growth Partners “R.P.G Engine” as its blueprint for success, by helping developing small companies with revenue management, pipeline for 3D model production, and growth through representation at b2b trade shows. 

Perhaps a sign of well wishes, Tabletop Growth Partners will work closely with MyMiniFactory, and launched with the help of a grant by Smith’s former employer. Smith also remains a Soulcrafted Ambassador with MyMiniFactory, an anti-AI initiative that rejects AI generated assets in favor of those created by human artists. 

At time of writing, Tabletop Growth Partners is already working with several clients, according to Smith’s interview with Boardgamewire, with more interested parties reaching out for consultations.  

Credit: Nerial Games

Mobile Game Developer Behind Reigns is Shutting Down for Foreseeable Future

Ah, yes, the video game layoffs part of the episode. Had to have one of these, right? It’s like killing Kenny in South Park. It has to happen. Oh my God, you’ve fired even more poor developers! You bastards!

This time the bad news is in a smaller sector of the gaming world, mobile games, as the development studio behind the runaway hit Reigns, Nerial, is shutting down after 13 years.

Nerial hit the big time in the world of gaming with its 2016 release, Reigns, which became such a hit following its release that the company was approached to produce two spinoff titles for Reigns based on some of the biggest IPs on the planet at the time, The Witcher and Game of Thrones. Yet following this breakout success, Nerial struggled to put lighting back into the bottle, which would ultimately lead to the studio’s acquisition by Devolver Digital in 2021. 

This acquisition brought its own slew of problems, as studio head Francois Alliot told Kotaku in an interview.

“Having been bought by Devolver, you become an internal studio,” Allio said. “So that means each of our games needs to be a Devolver game. And Devolver needs to publish all Nerial games, which it did match very well for Reigns, it did match very well for Card Shark, for example. So there is this common ground and we would have found a way. But it’s like, every time we have to think about what the other wants. And there’s a bit of a second guessing.”

Fast forward to 2026, and Alliot and his wife, Tamara, have decided to break away from Devolver and go into what he describes as “hibernation mode” as the two focus on where next to go with their passion for game development. While the couple will keep the studio name, Devolver will keep the rights to all of the titles previously worked on by Nerial. The studio also will lay off the other seven team members it had on staff at the time of its separation with Devolver, though Alliot hopes that he can work with them again one day in the future. 

Despite the uncertain future, Alliot is optimistic, and is eager to have creative control over his games again.

“I don’t know, but at the moment, you feel like you’re in the washing machine and it’s turned on and you’re like, ‘Well, oh my God, this is going so fast,’” he told Kotaku. “I’m not particularly worried facing that as an indie. I still have things that I can say and I can create that will be new and different and bring a new voice. And hopefully people will recognize that.”

Credit: Jagex

RuneScape Development Team Takes Down Trailer After Discovering It Was Made with AI

To close us out this week, we’ve got a weird one for you, as once again a company has outsourced some work to folks who said they won’t use AI, only to immediately step on a rake when it turns out that was a total lie and the contract company is absolutely using AI. This time, the victim of the ol’ artificial intelligence switcheroo is Jagex, the team behind the venerable MMO RuneScape, as a recent trailer shown for a new quest in the game at RuneFest was discovered to contain AI generated images.

First noted by Eurogamer, the now-delisted trailer contains a knight fighting off a dragon to promote the Dragon Slayer II questline coming soon to Runescape, and contains multiple red flags for generative AI use, such as the incorrect number of fingers on the knight and boots that sometimes looked like Air Jordans. The trailer was quickly taken down by Jagex once it came to the attention of the fanbase, as Jagex has made their anti-AI stance clear in statements to the press. 

James Doborowski, Senior VP of Product at Jagex, told Gamesindustry.biz in an interview previously that while the company is open to using AI tools to assist with development tools, the company has “a pretty hard line stance with the team, which is a commitment that no generative AI will ever be present in any asset that a player can touch, hear or feel. There will be no generative AI in the game that they experience.”

Responding to the use of AI in the trailer, Jagex wrote a statement on the RuneScape Subreddit, telling fans “We’re aware of the concerns raised about the Dragon Slayer 2 trailer and have taken it down, while we investigate urgently. This trailer was produced by a trusted external partner who had explicitly confirmed that GenAI would not be used. But, among other things, there’s a sixth finger and, well, that’s pretty hard to explain away. For that we apologise.”

At time of writing, there is no update from Jagex regarding the contracted company used for the trailer, but Jagex has committed to working with the RuneScape fanbase to give the Dragon Slayer II questline a new trailer created and commissioned by human artists from the community.
