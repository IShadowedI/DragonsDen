Battle Kiwi STACK System Review

September 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

As mentioned three years ago when I reviewed Battle Kiwi’s Battle Box, I am still to this day an accessory goblin-like hoarder. So when I found out Oli from Battle Kiwi was back in his design studio working on a new range of storage/gaming organisers for a range of Wargaming systems, I reached out to arrange a temporary pause in the never ending Aussie vs Kiwi debate on the origin of the Pavlova so we could instead focus on me getting my mitts on the latest evolution of his Battle Box, the Strategic Tactical Accessories Command Kit or S.T.A.C.K. for short.

We would like to thank Oli from Battlekiwi for providing us with the STACK - Core Set - 40k, the Game Mission Levels for Age of Sigmar & Kill Team along with the STACK Starter Painter set for review.

What is the Strategic Tactical Accessories Command Kit?

It’s a series of customized 3D printed trays (or layers) designed to stack together allowing you to pack and deploy various tools and accessories suitable for almost any tabletop game you care to mention.

For the Gamer

Starting with the Starter Stack is the Tools of War layer which has room for forty 16mm dice arranged in groups of five alongside the ever important tape measure and slots for all sorts of tokens you may have laying around.

Battle Kiwi S.T.A.C.K.S. - Deployment TrayThe Deployment layer has a lot of handy pieces to mark out deployment zones and useful measurement rulers in the 3”, 6” and 8” dimensions. The 3” ruler can be replaced of course with almost any flavour of 3” irregular shaped gaming pentagon measuring gauge you may have in your collection.

From this point the layers start to focus on different games where they each have a tailored layout designed to keep track whatever your game of choice needs, tracked with neat little dials and swappable tags where applicable.

Loading...

Currently Oli from Battle Kiwi has revealed trays designed for Warhammer in all her glorious forms, Infinity for the five people still playing that, Bolt Action/K47 and they have recently also revealed a tray for Necromunda. There are various trays and caddies with dividers so pretty much every game can be catered to using the STACK system especially with the options to print your own dial tags and labels.

For the Hobbyist

Battle Kiwi S.T.A.C.K.S. - Painters Range

The STACK isn’t just for the gamers, there are also trays for the weirdos that have issues with a sea of grey miniatures (I’m slowly getting better myself in this regards) as Battle Kiwi also has also provided me with their STACK Core Painter trays.

Battle Kiwi S.T.A.C.K.S. - Paint Tray

Every Core Painter set comes with a tray designed to hold assorted brushes and files as well as a removable drybrush insert that also serves as a cover for a small palette. In protecting the brushes when transporting, unless your brush fits snuggly within the slots, you will want to try and find the tiny plastic tube that vanished within seconds of removing it from the brush head… or some blu-tack.

Battle Kiwi S.T.A.C.K.S. - Contrast Tray

There are also several different trays designed to hold a wide selection of paints for all sorts of dropper bottles, enamels and whatever sized pot of citadel paint you might have. With my tray I was able to fit 24 of the Contrast pots. Each slot was nice and tight allowing you to tempt fate and hold the box upside down with the lid off.

Battle Kiwi S.T.A.C.K.S. - Transporter

For all the other tools, Oli has designed deeper storage trays that range from 5cm to 15cm that have slots to various removable buckets and doodads. There are base holders as well so whatever models you’re working on won't slide around too much and damage them. They would also be handy for transporting models for skirmish style games like Necromunda/Infinity/Blood Bowl.

How to get your hands on it.

At the time of this article, the STACK is currently available to back on Kickstarter having smashed its funding goal within hours of launching. It is set to close at the end of September with the stated goal of having the Digital print files in our hot little hands mid October through their shopfront on MyMiniFactory. Those that opt for the physical boxes will have a bit longer to wait with Oli estimating December at this stage.

Pricing at the time of writing varies on what you're after. Those that want all the digital files can pony up $79 NZD, otherwise there are small bundles for $34 NZD. Those that want someone else to do all the messing around with filament can opt to back the Physical level rewards that start out at $55 NZD all the way up to $175 NZD. You'll also need to factor in postage.

Those worried about paying through the nose for postage from Middle Earth, never fear as Oli has partnered with businesses in USA, Canada, the UK and in the land Down Under to print and post the STACKs in those countries. Sadly for the rest of the world, they’ll have to settle for the STL files.

Final Thoughts

Oli and his crew of hobbits and orks at Battle Kiwi have once again delivered yet another cool new gadget for me to play with.

Few things that we ran into in the course of this review. The dials are at first are very stiff to turn but become much smoother to rotate once rotated a few times. We also ran into some minor issues with the larger tray but since Battle Kiwi take great pride on providing excellent customer service, that was resolved very quickly with minimal fuss.

It's with mixed feelings as I ponder which game I play that gets to keep my old faithful Battle Box and I will certainly miss the faux leather lining of the dice tray but overall I will be jumping into the deep end by having backed the Kickstarter personally at the Digital STL Pro STACK - ALL IN! level. I’m keen to print up a bunch more trays for the overwhelming number of tokens, wound counters, Command Decks and so many other doohickeys that come with me as I travel with for games day.
