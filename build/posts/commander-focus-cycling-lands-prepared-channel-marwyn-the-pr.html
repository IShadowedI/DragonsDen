Commander Focus: Cycling Lands, Prepared Channel, & Marwyn, the Preserver

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The two things I love most in Magic: The Gathering are attached at the hips: Taking the most game actions of anyone at the table, and activated abilities. I would much rather be the player doing one seemingly pointless behavior a dozen times than slamming a splashy spell, or oversized threat, because the doing is more important than the result, at its core. Matt Sperling put it best, in saying “The player who performs the most game actions wins the game, and that player is always me”, because even if all you’re doing is exiling one card at a time from a graveyard, or duplicating a pointless counter, there’s something else that synergizes with that fruitless tic to result in victory. I say this all because the Commander I’m talking about today is about as close as Wizards has gotten to being a parody of my predilections: Marwyn, the Preserver. At her core, she’s a Groundskeeper in the Command Zone, albeit more flexible in terms of the ability’s cost and its legal targets. Not only that, but in providing Hexproof to your Lands, she’s insurance against the occasional Strip Mine that crops up at high power. It should be noted that Marwyn being redundancy for Groundskeeper gives her an obvious role, as another buddy of Six with whom she can loop Lion’s Eye Diamond, but I intend to showcase that Marwyn can be even meaner than Six, if you’re willing to cast off a few moral scruples. Marwyn, like our last Focus’ subject Immortus, is a card that only gets better relative to the power level of your podmates. If the idea of a union between Six and Erinis, Gloom Stalker gets you excited, this new Marwyn is for you.

“Return target land card from your graveyard to your hand”

First, let’s get a lay of the land: Marwyn is a very reasonable 2 mana, affords your Lands Hexproof, and can pay {2} at any time to put a Land from your yard into your hand. That cost, unlike Groundskeeper, is 2 generic mana, which does give us substantial freedom in terms of its payment. Our goal is to, bare minimum, enter into the midgame with Marwyn capable of activating 4 times per turn cycle—that’s the floor. Luckily, there are a number of ways to ensure we have spare mana for this endeavor, with some cards we’ve spoken about at-length on Commander Focus making a triumphant return: Victory Chimes, Bender’s Waterskin, & Urban Burgeoning. Each of these causes a mana source to untap during our opponents’ Untap Step, and in the case of Urban Burgeoning, we can enchant any Land, meaning something like a Guildless Commons which taps for 2+ mana entirely pays for Marwyn. This can be doubly exploited via Heartstone (although watch out, as it’s reciprocal) to reduce the input cost of Marwyn’s ability to {1}, or via Burgeoning, which can (hand providing) also result in ~1 extra mana per turn via a Land being put onto the battlefield. Once the engine starts humming, we’ll be activating Marwyn far more than just 4x per turn cycle, but I wanted to demonstrate that goal was attainable across most of your games. Still, what Lands are worth getting back a dozen times per turn cycle, surely there’s nothing that readily exploitable which gets itself back into the graveyard at a moment’s notice? If you silently cursed the name Boseiju, Who Endures, you’ve got the right idea.

Yes, Boseiju, the Green staple that acts as a difficult-to-counter answer to problematic Artifacts, Enchantments, & Nonbasic Lands is itself a Land which discards to activate. With Marwyn onboard, it effectively reads as a {2}{G} ability to pop one of those three permanent types, and replace it with a Basic (or depending on how commonplace proxies are, surveil, shock, or OG duals). While this does bump up our opponents’ mana, as opposed to a similar repeated source of removal like Aura Shards, that too is something we can use to our advantage. As a reminder, Carpet of Flowers is a rather funny Magic card, and if a player at the table has Blue, Boseiju allows you to crack them open like a pinata to reveal plenty of Islands for its effect. While Boseiju is the Land we’re most keen on recurring repeatedly, it’s far from the only one. Tranquil Thicket, Slippery Karst, & Desert of the Indomitable all allow you to go card-positive, and while they’re a bit expensive to get going in the midgame, once you’ve generated infinite mana with the LED combo having any of these available allows you to draw through your Library, and find the capstone to your win. To do so, you need to tutor one Creature, in Six, and a 0-mana Artifact, which brings us to the next touchpoint: Utility Lands. Not every Land we’re playing just wants to be discarded, as there’s more than one way to get a Land into the yard, with many of our strongest tools instead being self-sacrifice effects.

Start Trying to Make Fetch Happen

Every mother has their favorite, you know.

I would contend that, in the grand scheme of Magic’s canon of design, fetchlands are one of the most egregious mistakes. In any format with access to both fetches and any sort of dual or triome, their very existence hews away at the limitations of color identity, and the opportunity cost to splash; that’s less of an issue here, in a Monogreen deck, but I say this instead to highlight their power. Fetches are the reason Marwyn’s ability is never dead, and why we’re able to so aggressively churn through Lands in hand for a card like Ayula’s Influence. Being in Monogreen, we get access to 4 traditional untapped fetches, but we’re less excited about playing tap-fetches like Evolving Wilds. That is, however, with the exception of Hobbit Hole, appropriately appearing in The Hobbit. This is not only a slow-fetch, but also has the capacity to ditch itself to tutor a small suite of Creatures, with our lineup being Delighted Halfling as a standard mana dork, and Erinis, Gloom Stalker as a great blocker that can get back cards like Hobbit Hole directly to the battlefield. This also works wonders for recurring pieces like Lazotep Quarry, which can translate into one of a few very important Creatures, and Inventors’ Fair/Urza’s Saga, each of which serves as limited tutors for things Green otherwise can’t find. Heck, even multiple uses of Urza’s Cave can help us find crucial Lands, albeit at a pricy rate. One final card worth talking about in the context of the hole is Formless Genesis, technically a Hobbit Kindred card, which is a phenomenal way to make use of all the Lands Marwyn recurs; if you need blockers or pressure, it’s extremely accessible during the midgame. It’s worth noting that, in pursuit of a budget version of Marwyn, you can definitely fill some space in your manabase with more slow-fetches and have a pretty good time. For as strong as proper fetchlands are, the real meat of her ability is getting back Boseiju/cycling Lands, and swapping them between the hand and yard as often as possible.

With all of these means of doing the proverbial hokey pokey with our Lands, what’s the reward for shaking it all about? A lot of the best payoffs for gravebreak—the official term for ‘leaves the graveyard’ synergy—either don’t count Lands, or aren’t in Monogreen. What we do have remaining are things like Turntimber Sower (the best of these), and lifegain payoffs like Titania, Voice of Gaea & Skola Grovedancer. There’s also Dredger’s Insight, but that only triggers for lifegain when we recur Dryad Arbor. These payoffs don’t seem impressive, at least not on the surface, but can result in a solid amount of sustain in a deck that intends to play for the long game. In addition, there’s one other reason we intend to have a high life total, and that’s to spend it as a resource; those familiar with my work are likely aware that Harold and Bob, First Numens is my favorite Green deck of all time, and the idea of incorporating the Black idea of ‘life as a resource’ in Green is top of mind, for me. We aren’t doing that with Rad counters here, no, but instead with another synergy package which has been waiting for a home in the colors that finally comes to roost in Marwyn: Yavimaya Bloomsage, and the capacity for a Prepared Channel.

Be Prepared

Man, Ajani sure looks weird in this reality.

Channel’s legality is a thing of beauty, because outside of a fake formal like Brawl (sorry, Arena fans) it’s only available in a Restricted capacity. It’s iconic (see Channel Fireball), powerful, and unavailable—perfect reasons for Wizards to have reprinted it via a Prepare Creature in Secrets of Strixhaven Commander. The Bloomsage is a very safe design, in that it Prepares a Sorcery in the end step, meaning in theory you need to wait a turn rotation before having access to arguably the most powerful Green spell of all time. Of course, traditionally you even need to have a Creature with high enough power to get there, but with a payoff as powerful as this, circumvention is the name of the game; arriving in SOS were Biblioplex Tomekeeper & Skycoach Waypoint, which each cheat the Prepare timing for a functional cost of {4}, if you consider tapping a Land for an effect beyond mana as {1}. This got more redundant in Reality Fracture, with Hexhaven Dueling Arena, but what’s really prompted this package’s inclusion is Marwyn herself, and Carnivorous Cultivator. Between the two, and one of the Prepare Lands, we can chain tutors to get all the Lands we need to pop off.

With Cultivator being an autoinclude, and Bloomsage being a phenomenal way to get an arbitrary amount of Colorless mana for Marwyn activations, it came time to look for other Prepare options worth adding, given we were priced-in for mechanical synergy at this point. As a brief explanation for what I mean by that, in a deck like Marwyn that both asks for a substantial Basic Land inclusion (for fetches, etc) and fetches and Cycling Lands, the number of slots we have for utility Lands which don’t meet those criteria is slim. The opportunity cost to include cards like these which are—barring their combo with Prepare bodies—vanilla Colorless Lands is reasonably high. The other two Prepare Creatures which made it in are Emeritus of Abundance & Vastlands Scavenger, although you could make a case for a couple more, especially in a budget version. These are somewhat mirrors of one another, where Emeritus of Abundance re-Prepares easily and has a cheap way to get nonlands back from the yard, Scavenger is in theory a one-time effect which is reasonably expensive. The latter is far more appealing to re-Prepare, and in fact can even hit our Tomekeeper with its ability, an absolutely adorable interaction that’s come up in testing as part of a mill engine. Even so, though, it’s not a bad thing to get 2+ Regrowths in a single turn with the Emeritus, or turn on her Spell with a board state not conducive to attacking with a 3/4. Bloomsage aside, the Prepare bodies we’re playing are independently powerful cards which just get a slight boost by being re-Prepared, which causes the inclusion of cards like Tomekeeper and the Lands to sting less. Last note on this package: Emergence Zone is an honorary Prepare Land, as if we can hit the power threshold for Bloomsage naturally, her associated Channel can be cast at Flash speed if we’ve popped the Zone. This does mean we need to do the whole combo in our end step, but given we have Flash access and no timing restriction on Marwyn’s ability that’s not too difficult.

Marwynning

Lucky for Doof, there's only one legal non-Changeling Platypus in Commander.

I established fairly early on that Marwyn is essentially just a Legendary version of Groundskeeper in the well-known Six/LED combo, with the added bonus that all your extra mana can be funneled into getting back nonbasic Lands, some of which have cycling, which in turn draws your Library. This is all fairly straightforward, but what are we actually attempting to do? Short answer: Gain infinite life via one of a few lines we’ll talk about, then use Grinding Station and infinitely-recurred Artifacts to mill our opponents out. It’s not as clean as actually killing opponents, as there can be Instant-speed attempts to fight back during upkeep, but for this list it requires the fewest cards you don’t really want to draw. Let’s talk lifegain though, because infinite life in this deck actually can mean the end of the game, unlike in so many; this comes down to Yavimaya Bloomsage being a card we intend to break, and of course Marwyn being a Colorless outlet which can sorta draw. Any of our cards which gain life when a Land shifts around works, or with Dredger’s Insight, solely Arbor Elf. This can be paired with one of a few means of ‘dribbling the ball’, with Ayula’s Influence & Ashnod’s Altar being the easiest. A similar interaction exists with one of my favorite cards of all time, Cryptic Trilobite, and Invigorating Boon, which approaches infinite when paired with some other cost reducer for abilities. Do note that Prepared Spells aren’t abilities, but Cycling is, so Trilobite can pay for Marwyn’s activation and get back ~{C}{C} once per cycle, which goes fully infinite with a card like Fluctuator. On the topic of Fluctuator, it’s obviously pretty crazy here as a means of churning through our Cycling Lands with a cost of {2}, but honestly still feels great when turning your Desert of the Indomitable into a Tranquil Thicket.

Back to win conditions that don’t require a bunch of things to go right, however, and we have Magus of the Candelabra & Maze of Ith; each are phenomenal cards in their own right, but because Maze doesn’t remove the targeted Creature from combat, it can be paired with the Magus and Lands which tap for 2+ more mana than their targeted number (to account for Maze and going +1 on mana) in order to make infinite mana and Land untaps in the declare blockers step. As above, the fact that Marwyn is totally fine with doing her thing at Instant-speed is a huge boon here. If you’re looking to win right then and there, you can certainly play Swell of Growth for Emergence Zone (the only Monogreen means of putting a Land from hand onto the board at Instant-speed while drawing through our deck, I wish I was kidding). Part of the reason I adore building Monocolor is dredging the absolute dregs of Scryfall to find hyperspecific effects like this; with only one color to contend with, oddballs like Swell take the place of what would be more ‘normal’ pieces like Growth Spiral if we had access to Blue.

Example Decklist: No No, the Other Marwyn

Marwyn is a deeply weird Commander to build around because a lot of what she wants isn’t especially available in Green, unless you contort your mind to believe it so. Take for example the fact that we’re on a pretty minimal amount of ramp; Marwyn isn’t looking to go super wide super quickly, and instead aims to grind out value over time. As such, 3 mana rocks like Bender’s Waterskin end up providing substantially greater value than say, Cultivate, over the course of a game. Monument to Endurance is perhaps better here than any other Monogreen deck, doubly so given we can flex the Treasures it produces as fodder for Grinding Station. Emrakul, the Exigent Doom gives us an extra Land which taps for {C}{C} (helping us hit the threshold for Magus of the Candelabra), but that ramp also works at Instant-speed for all of our means of making mana during opponents’ turns, or even via sources like Thran Turbine. Note also that, if you want to insulate your combos, you can always Natural Order or the like into Ashaya, Soul of the Wild, who allows you to spread Marwyn’s Hexproof to your nontoken Creatures. This list is weird, and all the little considerations surrounding its peculiar internal synergy, from Cycling to Prepare, coalesces into something the color just hasn’t seen before. It has shades of my Harold and Bob list (which as mentioned is one of my favorite decks of all time), but its entire flow resembles far more a mid-Bracket 3 list co-opting the fringe cEDH Six list’s tools.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

This list is Bracket 3, and almost has to be, if you intend to get the most out of Marwyn. Her role as a Legendary Groundskeeper is part of the reason to put the card in the Command Zone, and the combo that effect has with Six and LED is cEDH playable. As such, if you lack Game Changers (or the ownership of/capacity to proxy LED), it’s far tougher to justify this Commander. At Bracket 2, you’re far more likely to reverse-engineer Monogreen Cycling, playing Shefet Monitor and similar to fully lean into that gameplan; the issue is that Cycling’s never really been a Green mechanic proper, unlike the Jeskai colors, so it lacks compelling payoffs beyond what we’re already playing. I’m not kidding when I say Invigorating Boon and Cryptic Trilobite is like the only neat thing Monogreen Cycling has going for it. Two of our three GC slots are spoken-for, in LED & Seedborn Muse, and the third comes down to your moral scruples, or lack thereof. You could choose to play Natural Order, and chuck a Muse or Ashaya onto the table early...or you could play an untouchable The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale, and tax Creatures with no recourse. If you did want to do that, be sure to play Urborg/Petrified Hamlet as well, so it still taps for mana. Maybe find some new friends, too. On that note, the question emerges as to whether this can be pushed to Bracket 4, and that gets into discussions around whether Marwyn or Six is better in the Command Zone. I want to stress that Marwyn’s printing has improved Six’s viability demonstrably, but if you’re really getting down to brass tacks, the fact she has a redundant piece in Groundskeeper means Six is our limiting reagent for this little reaction, and as such belongs in the CZ. Marwyn’s an absolute blast...at Bracket 3.

Arboretum for Breakfast

Quintin Gleim manages to make Marwyn feel small amidst the roots, an antithesis to her destructive Echoverse counterpart.

I was going to start this conclusion with a witticism about the peculiar nature of a Cycling+Prepare deck in Monogreen, but I can’t hide my excitement about finding a home for the Bloomsage line. I’ve mentioned that option as something Green can do since the card was initially spoiled, and I’ve been waiting for a Commander in the color(s) with a compelling Colorless mana outlet in the months since Secrets of Strixhaven. Well, she’s here, and her name is Marwyn. The other Marwyn, as my list’s name alludes to, because this is a character with a shocking pedigree in terms of Legendary Creatures; the original Marwyn, the Nurturer is one of the best Elfball Commanders of all time, and in her day saw pretty extensive play in cEDH as the fastest means of comboing off with Wirewood Symbiote. This Marwyn takes things quite a bit more slowly, and thankfully for me doesn’t require a typal gameplan (unless you count Halflingcycling). What does tie everything together though is a pursued sensation, a feeling: You get to drive stick in a format dominated by automatic.

It’s rarely the more efficient option to drive a manual car these days, as outside of high-performance vehicles it’s been slowly phased-out of most models. The thing is though, manual uses all four limbs, it keeps you active, it keeps you awake. Much like Marwyn, or any Commander whose gameplan is about doing small adjustments to card zones and liquid advantage every turn, four turns per cycle, stick keeps you engaged. A lot of the worst habits that arise from Commander do so because players intend to perform their linear gameplan, pass the turn, and enter torpor until the time comes round for them to act once more. If you aren’t thinking about what Land to pull back, what abilities you can flex, etc you can lapse in consciousness and tune out when it isn’t your turn, and uninteractive games like that tend to be dull experiences for all involved; I’m not saying this particular Marwyn list is interaction-dense, but rather that it calls upon you, the human sitting at the table, to interact and handle timings and game actions during other players’ turns. Your strandbeest of mediocre Cycling Lands and repeated Boseijus scurrying across the sands is one propelled by the winds of the turn cycle. If you didn’t have three opponents, you’d do half as much. All that’s to say, I wholly recommend Marwyn, and the only reason she’s not entering my paper lists comes down to my heart being claimed by a different peculiar, Land-oriented Monogreen Commander that paradoxically plays no Landfall. Two nickels, etc etc.

Until next time, Marwyn doesn’t care a whit, Marwyn doesn’t share our devotion to...style.
