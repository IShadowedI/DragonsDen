[40K] 11th Edition Points Review: Overview

June 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

This is it - the moment you’ve been waiting for, when 11th’s secrets are fully unfurled and everyone can start building Army Lists. The points are here, and as always our team have been busily analysing what they mean for the first few months of 11th Edition, and today we can share our thoughts with you, the beloved reader.

As always, we’ve split our review over four parts, covering:

  • Space Marines
  • Imperium Factions
  • Chaos Factions
  • Xenos Factions

If you want to get the scoop for your army of choice/your most troublesome nemesis, then follow the links above. The rest of this article provides a short overview of general trends, and some thoughts on which factions we think will thrive early on.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of the Munitorum Field Manual. Any points we refer to are subject to be changed in the final versions published to the App and Warhammer Community.

It’s also worth saying that Warhammer Community put up an article covering some of the rationale behind the points changes to many types of unit. It’s worth having a look at that, since it (mostly) aligns with what we’ve observed, and we’re not going to repeat everything it says. You can find that here.

Points Structure

Defiler. Credit: Rockfish

One of the big changes for 11th Edition is that the costs for some units can now vary in two ways:

  1. Some units increase in cost for your second or third instance of them (or sometimes 4th+ for Battleline/Transports). These are referred to as points steppers, and are designed to discourage always spamming three of whatever is most efficient right now. The overwhelming majority of these only kick in on the third unit, so more all-rounder armies will not be massively affected, but a small number of particularly potent choices (and many TITANIC units) have a step at 2. We assume that adding steps at 2 will be a commonly used balance leaver going forward.
  2. Some units pay a cost (always a multiple of 5pts) for some wargear items, usually where one choice has proven to be significantly better than others. How well this works varies. For big threats centred around a main weapon, it’s generally pretty good, as these have historically had to be balanced around the best option, so weaker picks never saw any usage. Examples of this are things like Riptides, where the price with the ion stays the same, but the heavy burst cannon build is now significantly cheaper. Where it feels more awkward is for some squad weapons like psycannons in Grey Knights, where each gun probably isn’t 5pts better than the alternatives. Wouldn’t surprise us if the exact way this works gets revisited for this case - rather than 5pts per gun, do 10pts on the unit if you take any psycannons, and balance that higher price around the assumption that it’s all of them.

While the exact implementation may need a few rounds, it’s definitely good to have both of these in the game as levers for future balance.

General Trends

Rainbow Warriors Land Raider Redeemer by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

As mentioned above, Warhammer Community has covered a lot of the common reasons that points have changed, and in most places units have changed in the direction you’d expect based on how the Core Rules affect them. There are a few more things we’ve noticed, however, and an important overall trend to highlight up front.

  • There are more nerfs than buffs: In general, if your army churns a bit, but ends up in about the same place (or you’re one of the few factions that nets out ahead), that’s good compared to the overall field. The reset for 11th has provided a chance to rebalance the points on a lot of top stuff, and the studio have used it. The (slightly worrying) outlier is Marines, which generally get quite a generous treatment, probably too generous.
  • Torrent Weapons get cheaper: …and probably shouldn’t. Yes, technically they now have Ignore Cover built-in, so they’ve lost an ability, but good Overwatch is now much more reliant on Torrent, and it’s a lot easier to use them to shield an objective. The Land Raider Redeemer is the poster child for this one (and kind of the issue above too), going down 20pts and thus immediately ejecting other Land Raiders from consideration again.
  • Upgrades are competitively costed: A lot of the new 1DP Detachments come with fancy Upgrade options that you can take multiple times, and the price is right on a lot of them, making them a real draw to some of the new options.
  • Most things giving out -1 to Hit go up: This stacks with Cover now, so this is sensible, and if you see a big hike on a Leader, this is probably why.
  • Invulnerable Saves working with Cover does seem to be priced-in: the WarCom article mentions Monsters having an easier time reaching melee, but there also seems to be a recognition that your 3+/4++ Monster is doing very well out of the new Cover rules, and fair play on that one.

I’m sure there’s more, but those are some key standouts for us.

The Big Winners

Riptide Battlesuit. Credit: Jack Hunter

Normally for an update we talk about winners and losers, but today we’re just going to cover the five factions we think are looking hottest out of the gate. We do think some factions are looking on the weaker end (and will discuss why in our articles), but the new mission structure provides much more of an angle for people to build to mitigate those weaknesses, so it’s harder to pick out who is actually going to struggle, and who is just waiting for the right list.

At the top it’s a bit easier - we have enough games of 11th under our collective belts that we’ve seen some of the things that work, and these five factions seem ready to hit 11th running (in no particular order).

  • Codex Space Marines: Space Marines massive roster of units gives them tools to cover every base, and their points changes were pretty generous. Quite a few units get a cut to reflect getting weaker in the new edition, and technically they did but the existing price was already super pushed, so as a result they remain excellent. Ironstorm Spearhead at 2DP is also…probably not wise. Expect Ultramarines galore out the gate.
  • T’au: T’au’s high speed shooting with access to Ignore Cover is exceptional on the new maps, and they come through the points changes relatively unscathed despite being a strong faction at the end of 10th. Mont’ka looks vicious, and there are other combos that merit exploration as well.
  • Necrons: Necrons have a deep roster of good options, and the durability to fight for objectives very effectively in 11th. They also get a very strong 1DP Detachment in Hand of the Dynasty, which gives a major boost to their solid suite of tier two detachments.
  • Chaos Daemons: Daemons will see major shakeups in their lists, but they do very well out of Core changes to Cover and Battle-shock, and they have multiple 2DP Detachments that must have been right on the edge of being priced up to three and got away with it. Shadow Legion, in particular, is one of the broadest 2DP options in the entire game.
  • Thousand Sons: Tzeentch’s finest have all the right tools to dominate in 11th Edition: lots of Psychic weapons, lots of Torrent, and Invulnerable Saves everywhere. Many of their deadliest weapons are mid-ranged at most too, so Hidden doesn’t do that much to them. They do pay a few extra points for their privileges, but they’re still going to rock.

Final Thoughts

That’s where we are. Are we right? Are we tragically wrong? Is the truth somewhere in the middle (probably). We’re all going to find out together - now go read the points articles to get the full scoop!
