Tabletop Battles Reviews: Age of Sigmar Khazalite Holds Army Box Miniatures

October 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Khazalite Holds have arrived, and Norman and Bair are taking a look at the army set. Our thanks to Games Workshop for providing Goonhammer with review copies.

A year after the release of the Helsmiths of Hashut, we have some new duardin on the block with the release of the Khazalite Holds. It's somewhat odd these guys are brand new, as they’re the most generic dwarf type army we’ve gotten in the setting and feel a bit like coming home, but that’s not to say its unwelcome. If you’re looking for heavy armor and a habit for grudging, look no further because the Khazalites are for you.

First Impressions

Norman: The box set is much what you’d expect, you get the models (obviously), a limited edition Battletome with some pretty sick cover art, and the faction cards which I personally have still never really had a use for but I’m sure some sickos out there are excited to get. One weird quirk with this box set is that the instructions only let you build the variants for the Spearhead (boy type Gholems and cannon instead of girl type Gholems and mortar). You can find the instructions for those builds on Warhammer Community, but it's a bit odd not to get them.

Bair: I built all of these before WarCom put up the instruction PDF and did not feel like getting creative either. Norman covered the rest already, it's an army box like you've come to expect for a new army release. I like that it's a Spearhead straight out the box giving you something to start playing with immediately too until the rest of the army comes out, pretty smart move there.

The Models

Khazalite Holds Hjulda Drakkblud - credit Bair

Hjulda Drakkblud

Bair: She sure is a named character foot hero model. That's to say that there aren't any options whatsoever in the kit, which is fine really since you're just buying the army box once anyways and being patient for the kits to come out separately. She goes together really well and I was happy that the cape is all one piece instead of the weird splits that can happen in minis sometimes that can cause weird splits in them to glue together. Virtually no mould lines to clean off and a real joy of a mini to paint to boot! I like the Dragon/Magmadroth scale cloak that sits on top of the normal cloth cape on her back, a really nice tie-in detail.

Oathkeeper Cannon

Norman: Unfortunately I only had time to build this kit and not paint it, but I have some thoughts on the build. The build itself is fine, there are some nasty seams along the length of the cannon if you’re not careful but it's nothing that can’t be solved through the use of some sprue goo. Much like its Helsmith counterpart, the base has a little diorama of the crew loading and firing the cannon. While this is neat, it can be a bit daunting that you have to paint three characters along with the cannon itself as just set dressing. They aren’t simple asks either, there’s one guy grabbing a rune from the fire, another firing the cannon, and a woman making ammo out of some rocks (had to look this up in the book). It's far from impossible and a treat to do the first time, but I can see this getting daunting if you end up wanting to run multiples of these.

Khazalite Holds Oathkeeper Cannon

Khazalite Holds Oathkeeper Cannon

Bair: I, too, did not get time to paint it but found the build the be real easy overall. There's a bit of awkwardness with the crew and spacing them all out correctly, the smith apprentice guy feels a little cramped, but that's about it. The only "real" complaint I have is the lack of variation and options in the crew so if you want multiple without looking the same you'll want to do some conversion work with other kits.

Khazalite Holds Gholems of Gazul - credit Bair

Gholems of Gazul

Norman: These kinda sucked to build honestly. For one, none of the pieces you need are near each other, which makes every step mean you have to look across three sprues for what you need. On top of that, there are some weird sub builds on these, like having to build the neck and the legs and feet being multiple pieces. There are a ton of options here though, letting you fully customize your constructs to fit your army. I wish there was a bit more variability with the poses, but at least for big stone guys they’re a bit dynamic.

Bair: I didn't mind the build on these so much actually but they're definitely not intuitive with three parts per foot and parts spread across each sprue instead of being next to each other. I put these together before Warhammer Community put out the PDF and the instructions in the box only have the ones for the Gazul version so that's what I built. Lining up the two handed hammers was easy enough and they joints lock into place to make it nearly idiot proof.

Clanshields

Khazalite Holds Clanshields - Credit Mildnorman

Norman: On the build side these guys are pretty straightforward, barring two interesting quirks. First, you can build your standard bearer and musician off two different models, meaning you can get some nice variance when you inevitably have to build 40 of these. The second is while there are a ton of options here, they aren’t labelled in the best way. The shields are straight forward but the heads are looking in different directions based on where the socket sits and it can make certain poses look off when your dwarf is looking in the opposite direction of the horn he’s posed to blow. Speaking of the heads, I wish the female heads looked a bit better here. The shawls they wear frame their head weirdly, and only one has any hair showing which helps break things up significantly. 

On the painting side, I tried to paint these well but as quickly as I could to see how much I could get away with. These models are mostly armor, so I decided to prime silver. While I don’t regret this course, it was a pain in the ass to paint over. There’s also a lot of leather on these models, which in combination with the beards that are everywhere, you’re gonna probably want a bit of variety in the browns you’re bringing to the table. The details themselves are pretty easy, with nothing on these models being a particularly difficult task. That is until you get to the banner. Unlike the Helsmiths there are no transfers for these guys. The banners are big wide open canvases that will work very odd with nothing on them, so you’re gonna have to get comfortable with freehand. Luckily there’s some good references in the Battletome for the different runes the Khazalites use, all of which are relatively simple geometric shapes. Beyond the banner though, these paint up pretty quickly for a modern GW kit.

Khazalite Holds Clanshields

Bair: Just wanted to add here that you get 20 shields in total and 20 heads in total for the kit of 10 models; you get 10 of the same shield with the mountain symbol and then 10 shields with random runs on them, 15 male heads and 5 female heads in total. I agree with Norman that the female heads could be better, I really like the long braids that Hjulda and the one head in this kit have going on and would have loved to see more in that style!

Final Thoughts

Norman: This is a good set of models! As you can see in our review, a lot of them are useful too which is always a plus. With a named character in the mix, you probably won’t be doubling up on these, but clanshields and the cannon will be in all my lists most likely and I’m sure I’ll find a spot for the Gholems. I’m really looking forward to seeing the rest of the range and building out this force.

Bair: Good dwarfs. I don't care much for the official scheme and think that photos can be a difficult medium to show new minis off in. Are these as good as the golden age of dwarf sculpts (6th Edition WHFB sculpted by Colin Dixon)? No, they're not, those hold a special place in my heart but I do really like these and am very glad to see "regular" dwarfs in Age of Sigmar. Finally, an army for me.
