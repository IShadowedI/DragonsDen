Road Through 2026, Part 48: The 2026 Grand Narrative

November 27th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Dear Reader, to my ongoing blog of hobby and competitive play progress through 2026. Last week I was doing my final hobby progress for the 2026 Grand Narrative and World Championships, painting up the army I'd be taking to the events. This week I'm talking about my experiences at the 2026 Grand Narrative - the games I played, the hangouts shared.

Atlanta isn't the best location in the world but it has a few things going for it. First, there are a lot of flights in and out. Second, said flights aren't long - it's like a 2 hour flight from Houston, which makes it a pretty chill trip, and means we can leave like midday Thursday and get in with plenty of time to grab dinner and meet up with people.

Which was the plan - we got in Thursday night and went through registration. We were a bit too late to do trivia, but we got in with plenty of time to meet up with people and grab dinner and drinks. Most of the Tabletop Battles guys were in battlegroup 5 this year, playing as filthy imperials, but PierreTheMime was in my battlegroup so that was cool. These things are kind of a crap shoot - they're always way more fun when you have a battlegroup full of your friends and not randoms - but sometimes you meet some really cool people playing teammate roulette - my 2022 and 2023 battlegroups were awesome.

If you want a rundown of the event and its format, you can find it in my Tuesday article. I'm not going to cover most of that here, so if you're unfamiliar it'll be a good primer for how the event works and what's going on.

By a narrow vote, we ended up aligning with the Warrant over Voss. It turns out not a single Chaos battlegroup chose Voss, but it was certainly contentious within our group - a 5-4 vote, and I know some of them were fairly sour about it. I was running Red Corsairs at the event and was never interested in siding with Voss - he seemed like a loser bitch doomed for failure out of the gate; I'm here to fuck shit up for Huron. The only downside to this is that the Warrant is also kind of a lamewad, but whatever - the Chaos guys usually die during these events anyways. Halfway during the event they'd open up the option to let players defect, and when they did about thirty players took the option.

It's customary at these things to have some kind of costume going on and in that regard I'm very happy with how mine ended up - I tend to prefer something with good function and comfort over going all-out on something I'll be miserable in, and so my "Chaos Cultist" hits those marks pretty well. It communicates fairly obviously what I'm trying to do and whose side I'm on, and is even in Red Corsairs colors anyways - a clear advantage this year. I might vary it up next year, particularly if I take Emperor's Children, but the stuff I want to add is like, maybe a pair of pauldrons/shoulderpads and a chest plate at most. I'm not trying to win best costume, I'm trying to show the appropriate amount of school spirit while staying comfortable playing Warhammer.

That's always the roughest point for me with the Grand Narrative - I don't particularly care about LARPing as my Warlord; I don't see the game as being about my warlord, and when I play Crusade I think of it as being about the story of a Warband. I like Narrative play and campaigns because they make games meaningful and give you a narrative focus - why we're fighting and what the battle means in the larger scheme is very compelling for me. I like the experience of being a tactician, fighting a larger war moreso than being some random dickhead in the 41st millennium. Warhammer tends to be pretty bad for roleplaying, because the entirety of its defined mechanics are "fighting battles in games of Warhammer." The Crusade rules for Codex: T'au Empire in 9th/10th showed this off pretty well - the T'au are ostensibly diplomats but there's no language in the game for doing diplomacy. The Grand narrative can conceivably solve some of this by adding new mechanics but those have to be built and fleshed out wholesale - it's a big problem to solve and you don't know if/how they'll solve it any given year.

So I'm here with a gorgeous army, I'm in costume, and I'm ready to play, but I'm not so much about doing goofy voices or Sephiroth posting on the Discord.

The single biggest improvement this year was the addition of terrain footprints. Tables had terrain laid out in advance - a bit of a mixed bag, but largely fine - and these were entirely saved by having abstracted lines of sight and terrain footprints, which both ensured an adequate amount of objective markers (though you had to be careful about which deployment map you used), and that there weren't bigass long sightlines across the entire table. Don't get me wrong - these layouts were largely a mix of adequate to bad, but even the bad ones were better than most of the layouts in prior years, where you might get a collection of scattered ruin walls on planet bowling ball.

Games at the Grand Narrative are always a mixed bag. I try to take a bad list and sandbag things - I ran Disruption as my Disposition every game, and I took a list without Huron or Bile, only a 5-man terminator unit, and a unit of 10 bolter legionaries with no attached characters. It's designed to be aggressive but fairly light, using multiple units to contribute to taking down bigger targets, and still using the Cabal of Chaos +1S bonus and the Terminator Sorcerer to drop high volumes of AP-2 bolter fire and melee attacks to take down bigger threats.

The challenge is that while I can play a worse list and a bad disposition, most players at the Grand Narrative haven't learned to not be too aggressive. They push forward too early and expose key units before they should, which plays right into my plan of "rush forward and cripple their army by killing the 2-3 biggest threats immediately, leaving them unable to answer effectively." This was the case most games - four out of six, and the notable exceptions were my first game into Grey Knights (which was played with a bit more caginess), and my round 3 game into Knights, where my opponent got army-wide Advance and Shoot/Charge for his army and went first, throwing an insane amount of firepower and damage my way turn 1 to kill my Land Raider and putting me on the back foot. I made a good comeback in that game but lost 71-79 when I couldn't chase down his last two Armigers.

The problem is that I can't turn off good placement/strategy, even if I'm bringing a worse army. If I just sit back and don't fight, I'm not really playing Warhammer, I'm just playing the role of NPC for my opponent. And that's not fun for me, either. It's a tricky balance to hit, and I probably need to tone my list down a bit more next year or figure out how to sandbag a bit more to drag these games out. That said, I made a lot of my army choices this year with an eye toward what I needed painted for Worlds, and what I thought might help me win best painted again this year. So the army could have been worse, but then we come back to that same problem - how bad does my army need to be to make games fair for weaker players? I genuinely want the person I'm playing to have a good time, but the problem here isn't "running a meta list," it's "understanding unit placement, position, outputs, and trading economy, and those are much less tangible.

A lot of this just boils down to making better use of reserves and transports, keeping your units back and trying to get advantageous trades. Inexperienced players often underestimate how deadly 11th edition is, and so find even large units evaporate quickly when they touch terrain. The best I can do when those games happen is offer to buy my opponent a drink and talk about the hobby. End of the day, we're both Warhammer fans who love the setting and the minis. Not everyone is stupid enough to play a hundred games a year of this dumb game.

The other upside to winning games quickly is more time to spend with friends and family and doing stuff outside the games. This year there were a ton of opportunities to do things between games to influence the story with warlords/vox localum, and while I had those chances, I also didn't really take on most of them - my involvement in the story means I knew some of those details going in and so wasn't super keen on trying to have a big influence on how things went. For reference, we run the Discord for the event and help with some of the setup, so that means I typically have insight into things like the characters and their motivations ahead of the event.

This year's event generally did a better job giving players ways to influence and impact the story, and I think that was a big win. It wasn't the best story they've told, but I very much enjoyed parts of it, and I though the custom secondary missions were a big hit. They need some tweaking, but the idea of customizing your deck with cards you get from the campaign characters is great, and I think it needs to be a bigger part of the story. The Warlord effects I was less a fan of, in part because they were just kind of wildly unbalanced and had some insane drawbacks. Overall though, I think this was the best they've done with the event. I think doing more with the Vox Localum and battlefields, making the setting feel more alive is the next step. This year's location felt just kind of like an afterthought, and I think more work can go into making the setting feel real and alive. There are also some things we want to do with the Discord and making it a bigger part of the event that we're going to pitch next year.

I don't go to these things just to win Best Painted, but it's definitely my primary goal while I'm at them - I'm not going to win best cosplay, and I don't interact in a way that would get me protagonist/antagonist (see above and prior knowledge). So I tend to focus on the most objective measure of success: Having the prettiest army at the event. My Red Corsairs already won best painted at Dallas this year, and I've done a ton of work on them since, painting more models with the new Tone Pro paints. That's a bit of a mixed bag, since it means my army looks awesome but has two different shades of red in it now and that kind of hurts me.

My army has a lot going for it - great freehand, good solid colors and gradients, good weathering, and some stellar conversions on things like my bikes, the Mutilators, and Armmenus Valthex. It's very cohesive and I'm proud of it. My biggest competition was form an army of Chaos Knights painted by CK Studios, which had absolutely stellar freehand work on the armor plating. I set up for Showcase but didn't think I was going to win against them, though there were some things I felt were working in my favor - it seemed like it'd be close, anyways. There was also a lovely Night Lords army at the event I felt was an easy top 3 contender, but not likely to win.

At the end of the event, Voss killed the Warrant then was murdered casually by Arathiel, who reclaimed the soulstone of his lost companion. The awards ceremony was wrapped up this year in the final presentation, and I like how they did that. I put my Warlord's name in a survey like three months ago and had completely forgotten it, so when they called my name for Best Painted it took me a moment to realize I'd actually won.

Oh, by the way I won Best Painted.

That's my second trophy this year and my fourth Best Painted award since I started doing this more seriously. It's the third army I've won with and I'm now looking to next year and my Emperor's Children as a way to get a new army in the mix. That's the part I'm proudest of - not winning the awards, but winning them with multiple armies. And I really believe that each army has been better painted than the one before it.

I was really glad they separated the events this year. If I'd had to choose between the Grand Narrative and the World Championships, I'd have picked the Narrative. It's less stimulating tactically, but it's just a much more fun and chill time for the family - my wife and son have a much better time joining me at the Narrative than at Worlds. Being able to do both is great, and it also meant there were fewer people at this year's Atlanta event overall (there was a GT but it was comparatively small), making for a more comfortable time at the event - though we were still sharing the space this year with the Model UN crew. You can find more on that here.

As is customary, we finished the event with an escape room. We escaped.

Next Week: WCW Prep

That does it for this week's write-up. I'll be back next week on my way to Barcelona with a final hobby update as I hastily finish my army in time to get curbstomped at the world's hardest event. I'll be doing a bit more coverage of the event as well next week, so stay tuned for that as I resolve to spending my time between games blogging about the event. It's the closest you'll be able to get to actually being there, I assure you.

See you next week.
