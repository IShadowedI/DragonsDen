Tabletop Battles Reviews: The New Ork Models

August 22nd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Accompanying their new Codex, there's a wave of new models for the Orks. Thanks to Games Workshop who have kindly provided us with review copies of all the new kits - let's dive in and take a look at them.

Welcome back to another uh... Orkgust, we guess. It's not as catchy as "Orktober," but it's as big a release as we've ever seen for the Orks, and without the weirdness of having certain key kits like Boyz being tied to a limited run boxed set. In this article we're going to look at the new kits and talk about assembling and painting them, providing thoughts on whether they're worth it and providing lessons learned from our experiences.

Nazdreg

Nazdreg - Credit: keewa

Keewa: The biggest and baddest of the Bad Moons finally rolls into the 21st century with a bang and a massive upgrade. It’s appropriate that the warboss of the flashiest, blingiest clan around that Nazdreg’s getup is that of a Meganob on the expensive kind of steroids, armed with a kustom shoota that can only really be described as “all the Ork guns welded into one” and a massive five-fingered power klaw to deal with any fool who dares to mix it up in melee range.

This new and improved Nazdreg retains some fun design elements from his original 1990s miniature, particularly the belly plate, knee pads, and bannerpole adorned with a big grinning moon. I like it when GW does these little call-backs that tie their new versions of characters to their old counterparts; I think it adds a good deal of charm.

This guy is… well, he’s big. He’s not quite Ghazgull-sized, but he’s certainly bigger than any other footslogging character, and since Ghaz is basically just a head in a robo-suit, maybe he’s the biggest organic Ork after all.

Building Nazdreg is a relatively painless experience; the piece count isn’t egregious, and the mould lines are relatively well hidden. All well and good, but there are a few small things I noticed while putting him together. As soon as I saw the back-banner piece, I, Cassandra-like in my perspicacity, said to myself: “Well, that’s going to break for sure.” And… a scant day later, lo and behold, the damn back banner snapped off. Perhaps I am a particularly ham-handed fellow, but it came apart with only a slight knock, so bear that in mind when you’re transporting him.

As far as painting goes, he’s a tricky sort, having multiple layers of “stuff” that overlap and interrupt your access to the deepest parts (the arms and the trousers in particular were a bit of a headache), my general distaste for looping wires and cables is well attested by this point, and Nazdreg has a whole bunch of them draping off his engine and under his arms to fee his gun and claw. The head is also a bit hard to get to with all the stuff in the way; if I were to paint him again, I’d probably leave the head off and paint it separately - I mistakenly thought that once the Jaws-thing is attached over the top, there’d be no way to slot the head in after, but I was wrong. I didn’t use any subassemblies at all to paint Nazdreg, because I am clearly insane.

Nazdreg - Credit: keewa

Liam: Nazdreg is a tremendous model. As keewa says, he’s not as big as Ghaz, but that’s hardly a mark against him - Ghaz is bloody massive. Compared to anything else that isn’t literally the Grand Warlord or mounted on a Squigosaur Naz is very impressive - including his hero rock he’s twice the height of an Ork Boy, and overhangs the 60mm base he comes on too. I loved this model as soon as I saw the first preview, and I like him even more now that I’ve got him in my hands.

I was anticipating him being a longish build, but he’s really not. My recollection is that he’s 26 pieces, and none of those are particularly small or fiddly. There’s no options in here, which helps keep him simple.

The model is absolutely packed with details harkening back to his 90s incarnation, plus adding some new ones (the old T-pose guy lacked the fur cloak, for example). I guess one minor mark against is that there’s a few sculpted Bad Moons on here, so if you were really keen to have a non-Bad Moons version of this model you’d have to scrape those off, but that is surely the pickiest of all possible complaints. The actual worst thing about Nazdreg is that he looks so much better than the Meganobz that are going to accompany him - the contrast will be very stark. Maybe in 12th edition we’ll get a new spin on those.

MasterSlowPoke: The one drawback to Nazdreg is that subassemblies are going to be hard for him. The gun arm is easy enough to keep separate, but the claw arm is essentially load bearing. You can keep part of the fur cloak and the apparatus that goes around his head separate as well. I don’t think it would make painting him too much of a hassle if you refrain from gluing those on until you’re done.

Trukk

Keewa: As the “big model” in this new slate of releases, the Trukk is definitely going to command a lot of attention from the audience in general, and with good reason; it’s a great kit and super upgrade to the older Trukk from the 00s. The new version resembles a kind of funhouse-mirror Opel Blitz with a big block engine (with a turbo air scoop), a very spacious flatbed, and (if you choose) a big cow-catcher blade on the front. The alternate front for the truck includes the pretty neat skull design around the radiator featured on the box. The design of the Trukk manages to upgrade the detail while maintaining the whole Mad Max/Hot Rod vibe so beloved in Ork vehicles. Other options include whether you’d like your mounted weapon to be a power-klaw arcade crane, or a weaponised hedgetrimmer, the presence of a Gretchin hanging rather precariously off the side, swinging a bladed spanner, and what the turret-mounted weaponry should be. 

A Trukk

The Trukk goes together nicely, although it’s not the quickest build ever (Admittedly, I’m not a very fast builder, but it still took me 2-3 hours), and some parts of it had me scratching my head; for example, you can adjust the angle of each of the front wheels independently of the axle. I’ve been thinking about it, and I don’t know why you would want to do this. The build has some cool scale-modelling features; the rear and central axle aren’t glued in, but fit around a central strut, allowing them to rotate, handy if you need to put your Trukk down on some uneven surface without it levitating. 

Liam: What a relief to have a new Trukk at last. The old one has done sterling service, but if you’ve built one recently you’ll know that the kit was showing its age and then some. This version retains much of the same aesthetics, but with the benefit of another couple of decades of improvements in sculpting tech and the onward march of the studio’s vision for what Orks can be. It’s a small irony that this kit comes out as the 2018-era buggies seem to be going away, because aesthetically it fits with those really well.

As keewa says, one thing that hasn’t been saved here is build time - there’s a lot of parts to this thing, and when you’re putting something of around 100 pieces together there’s only so fast you can go if you’re clipping and cleaning properly. That said, once you’ve got everything cleaned and ready to glue, you can fly through it - everything goes together really cleanly. 

There aren’t masses of options in the kit. There’s a couple of optional pieces (a grot hanging on to the sides, a flag pole), and you can choose between the buzzsaw and grabbin’ klaw and the dual big shoota and the rokkit launcha, but apart from a couple of head options for the various Orks riding in the Trukk that’s it. What you can do however is build it either way around - there’s mirrored pieces that let you position the gun on either side of the vehicle - and also build the suspension in different ways so that it sits in the middle or leans to the right or left. The front wheels can be turned independently of each other, while the central axle can move up and down, making the Trukk very posable and quite toylike in a way.

Size-wise, it’s not a lot different to the old one - I think once you account for the klaw/saw on one side and the gun on the other it ends up being slightly wider, and the arm of the klaw is maybe a little taller, but it’s not a huge difference and there should be little issue running both old and new alongside each other. Overall a great kit, and a worthy replacement for the workhorse vehicle of the Orks.

Deathskulls Trukk by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

MasterSlowPoke: Honestly, here I found the build really quick for a modern kit. From breaking the seal on the box to assembling it, it probably took me about 3 hours. I normally spend far longer on a vehicle of this size.

If you want to magnetize the klaw and buzz saw weapons, there’s already a hole that’s almost exactly 1/8th of an inch, or 3mm, and ready for magnets there. I would probably not try to magnetize the big shoota or rokkits, though. The join on the Ork holding the grips is pretty small, and while you can kind of balance the gun option on the railing, it would get annoying fast. I guess you could just magnetize the whole Ork, and have a different gunner holding a different personal weapon instead. Another option would be to pin the gun, which might add enough friction to hold the weapon in place.

Deathskulls Trukk by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

The Trukk's bed is a little cramped, but when you're putting in the claw/saw operator, you have just about enough room to fit in space for a 32mm base model to also stand in the bed, great for showing when the Trukk is occupied. Just grab a spare Boy when you're putting in the melee option and use him to ensure you have room.

Warboss

Keewa: The multiple-option sibling of Armageddon’s one-pose big lad can be decked out with either a big power klaw or an axe, plus a combi-weapon with either an underslung flamer or rocket launcher, two different belt buckles, a few head choices (there’s a nice jaw piece with a big honkin’ cigar), an Ammo-Grot, and the option for an Attack Squig on a chain, like an even uglier and bitier Pit-Bull.

Warboss (and runt)

He’s pretty huge, easily as big as the Armageddon Warboss, though not as big as Nazdreg; the grumpy little, put-upon Ammo Runt provides a nice contrast (he’s so annoyed, it’s great).

He builds nice and easy, for the most part, although the Squig is a bit tricky, having a single point of contact on an otherwise flat, smooth armour panel. 

A note to would-be converters: the toes, heels, and soles of his shoes are part of the hero rock he’s standing on, so swapping out the hero rock for another basing element is trickier than it might otherwise have been. 

MasterSlowPoke: Another wonderful build for me, probably about an hour and a half all together. For as static as the pose here is, you can get a lot of variety out of the various head options and other accoutrements. Every Warboss you own can be pretty unique and have their own character, even if they all fit the same exact silhouette.

If you want to magnetize options here, the right arm seems pretty easy to do. There’s a big cavity you’d need to fill with putty, but the arm already kind of friction fits in, so you don’t need a ton of magnet tooth to hold the arm on. Magnetizing the kombi weapon seems impossible to do well, so just pick one. A Warboss’s gun will rarely turn a defeat into victory anyway.

Liam: I really like this guy. He’s suitably massive, the equal of his Armageddon version and with a full range of stuff to krump and dakka with. There’s a bunch of different heads to work with too, including the Blood Axe one with the general’s cap which is an immediate favourite in our team. The squig chain is maybe a touch fiddly in how it attaches. That’s about it for my critiques of the kit. 

I painted mine up as a Deathskull as I continue to pursue my plan of having a Warboss and Boyz mob for each of the clans. One unsung hero of this release is the new Orks transfer sheet, which is absolutely packed with decals for all six clans - importantly, it has the big ones that fit the open space on a Bannernob’s banner, plus the slightly smaller ones that fit on the Warboss’ back banner. Two interesting changes here are that Snakebite symbols are now brown, rather than yellow, while Deathskulls have a much lighter blue and have changed quite a bit stylistically - the skull no longer has horns, and they’re all half and half blue/white (whereas the old ones were generally a blue skull with white bones, with just one half and half), including some that have white or blue dags on them. The rest of the clans are much the same as they ever were, though there’s a lot more of the Goff glyphs we saw in Armageddon with the white skull backed with red. There’s also designs for Meks, Painboys, and Freebooters, plus a whole bunch of general-purpose Ork ones. There is literally twice as much stuff here as the old one, which ought to add a ton of great looking new details to your Ork army. I applied mine liberally to the back banner on the Warboss, plus another skull on his shoulder.

Deathskulls Warboss. Credit: Corrode

Warbikers

Liam: I personally liked the old Warbikers, but they were a little long in the toof, most notable by the way the Orks didn’t quite fit into their seats. These ones are more evolution than revolution, being a similar size and style, but with updated aesthetics. One immediately noticeable change is that the guns move from the back of the bike to the front, which makes them a little sleeker; the old ones were pretty back-heavy. The Orks have weird hollow backs so that they can actually sit in their seats rather than sort of hovering in front of them.

There aren’t tremendous numbers of options here - an alt build for the Nob to be a regular biker, hands on handlebars or holding a weapon for the other guys, and a few heads - but several parts are swappable between bikes so you can still get some more variety. One thing I am annoyed with myself about is forgetting to check if you can glue Boyz weapons to the bodies of these Orks - it looks like you should be able to, but I haven’t tried.

Evil Sunz Warbikers. Credit: Corrode

The one thing I didn’t like about these is that the guns don’t have much of an attachment point. You just kind of jam them on. If you’re not careful about it you’ll find the barrels going a bit cross-eyed or droopy.

Painting them feels much easier than the old ones for some reason. I think these have poses that are a bit more open, which helps a lot, and also the guns moving to the front (and also lifting up a bit) means they’re not blocking off space in the same way they once did. I haven’t gotten around to the Nob yet, who’s a much busier model than the other two and takes a bit more work, but the regular bikers were pretty simple to paint even with the pressure of a looming deadline.

Putting the old and new ones side by side, I think the main things that will stand out are that the old ones are a bit squatter and blockier - an artifact of their age - and of course the positioning of the guns. Looking at the photo, the other big thing is that you can actually see the face of the new guy.

The old vs. new Warbikers. Credit: Corrode

Boyz

Keewa: Oh dear. I had a bad time building the Boyz. It's entirely possible that I got unlucky and received a slightly duff frame, but the mould lines on these lads were pretty atrocious compared to their stablemates or their Armageddon cousins. Now, I conferred with my honoured colleagues who also received some Boyz, and they didn't report having had any issues, but even so, mine were pretty janky. It felt like each Boy had more parts than strictly necessary too; there's not really any reason why the legs needed to be separate parts, for example.

Boyz

Unlike the strictly push-fit Armageddon Boyz, these lads are customisable, with a variety of weapons, heads, and accessories available. I opted to have one of my Boyz equipped with a flamer, which proved to be a total pain in the arse; the whole flamer assembly is 6 parts (arm, fuel tank, flamer, back plate thing, scuba tanks, hose) and only fits on a certain body (3c), so make sure you’re paying attention! I don’t really like these fiddly cross-body options at the best of times, and this one was a real fiddler alright.

Even with those gripes out of the way, the models have lots of character to them, and the customisation options really help to make them unique - including additional pouches, stickbombs, armour plates, gnarly severed heads, and some base decorations. The Nob is particularly cool, with a choice between a nasty-looking double-handed chain-axe and a power klaw/combi weapon.  

Liam: I’ll disagree here and say I had no problems with the Boyz at all. They do have mold lines, because they’re plastic models, but I didn’t think they were egregious - nothing like so bad as the Deathwatch Kill Team for example, or even the Intercessors in Armageddon. I also don’t think the piece count is a particular problem - except for the special weapons, each Boy is 4 parts for torso and legs, two arms, a head, and then an optional accessory to go on their back. There’s some pouches and severed heads and other such delightful things you can attach if you want, but 7-8 pieces for an infantry model seems fine to me.

The special weapons are a bit more fiddly, and both the rokkit and burna have attachment points for one arm that are not as clear as they could be, but once I figured out the trick to how they’re meant to go together it was fine.

Enough about mold lines. I love this kit. Everything in here looks great, and it’s delightful to build things out of this box. As highlighted on Warhammer Community earlier this week, while you’ll need to match the right bodies to the right weapons for the specials, everything else is completely interchangeable. If you just want to build the “right” guys, the sprue is laid out in a format that will help you there - the Ork you build with body parts 79, 80, 83, and 84 is located conveniently next to weapon arms 81 and 82, for example, and head 80, and back accessories (in this case a pair of choppas) 85. So if you just want to clip those parts out and go, you can. Equally you can get more adventurous with it, and mix and match weapon sets and heads and produce something of your own. There’s loads of heads to pick from and use however you want, with a couple of classic call-backs like the guy with the fur-lined helmet. Give them extra shoulder armour to make them ‘ardboyz. Even without bringing in parts from other kits, you’ve got lots to choose from here, and that’s not even mentioning the little squiglets you can use for basing - I sprinkled these liberally around mine, and also put one on Nazdreg’s base too.

The Nob is necessarily a bit more constrained; you’ve got a good selection of heads and a choice of bosspoles, but most of your choices here are what weapons he’s got, and if you’re giving him a power klaw (as you probably always will be) there’s just the one klaw and it’s in the one pose.

With the variety in this kit plus the Armageddon ones and the old monopose kit, your Green Tide has never looked so good.

Deathskulls Nob. Credit: Corrode

Mek

Keewa: Nice little fellow, he goes together very nicely in only 8 pieces, a quick job if you’re nifty at it. On the other hand, because he’s just fine, it’s hard to find much to say about him (which I find to be the case with the previous Ork Mek model); he just… is a regular-looking guy with tools and a crane on his back (maybe I should get one of those for lifting moving boxes?), he’s a nice, blue-collar Ork who probably owns quite an extensive library of Haynes Manuals. I like that he’s holding a screw in his mouth (we’ve all been there). 

Liam: Yeah, there’s really not tons to this guy. He goes together incredibly quickly and paints up just as fast. It’s a good job he’s got the big crane on his back or he might be at risk of getting lost in a mob of Boyz. That said, the model does the job fine. He looks good, there’s neat details, if you were envisioning an Ork who’s good at doing Mek stuff this is pretty much what you’d picture. I made sure to paint mine up as a Bad Moon to support the primary role I envision him having - attaching to my Flash Gitz.
