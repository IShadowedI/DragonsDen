Why the July Update Is a Great Time to Get Back Into Umamusume

July 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The in game 1.5 year and the actual 1 year anniversary of Umamusume: Pretty Derby’s global release is here, and with it came a huge surprise update that has wildly changed the game in ways no one could have predicted. If you’re a new player curious to give the game a try, a longtime player who got burned out, or someone who has been slugging it out through the Trackblazer era, this July update has fundamentally changed the game, and we have another huge update on the near horizon that will come towards the end of the month. But what, exactly, has changed, and how can you take advantage of it fully? Well, this week, we’ll dive into the various changes and updates and why now is the best time to get back into Umamusume.

Unity Cup and Ura Finale Changes

Let’s start off with the biggest surprises of this update: Unity Cup and URA Finale have been changed to be far more comparable to Trackblazer, and in fact, Unity Cup is now far easier to get superior results in than Trackblazer is! The biggest URA Finale change is that Happy Meek now has her “duel” mechanic, which means that on certain training days, you can see an orange “duel” graphic. Dueling Happy Meek gives you three options for stat trainings, the first being the one you selected originally, and the other two randomly selected, along with an X, triangle, circle, or double circle icon. If you win the duel, Happy Meeks level will increase, and you will either get 4 points uncapped on that stat, 10~25 stat points dependent on Happy Meek’s current level, 30 skill points, 4 max energy, or a skill hint related to the new “Essence of Racing” skills. Defeating a powered up Happy Meek also gives you a new end career event that can give a whopping 20 in EVERY stat and 150 skill points, depending on some factors, and a unique skill called Past My Limits.  URA Finale also has an increased stat cap, with all stats now being able to hit 1400 in URA Finale. This is amazing news for parent runs for early players or for starting your own dynastic runs, since URA Finale remains the fastest and easiest career, and also the best one to view a new trainee’s story events for the first time; with the 1400 cap, you can also get some fairly amazing new trainees out of the deal too, if you manage to unlock solid trainings and stat cap increases off of Happy Meek interactions.

Unity Cup, however, is suddenly and shockingly THE place to train the best possible trainee. Why? Because it has the second highest Speed stat cap AND the highest Wit stat cap in the game currently! The attribute caps for Unity Cup are as follows: 1300 Speed, 1300 Stamina, 1300 Power, 1300 Guts, and 1800 Wit. There are some other good quality of life changes too to Unity Cup to make it far easier and less punishing, such as being able to use alarm clocks on team races, better Spirit Bursts, and even unlocking Elite Teams to face off against that give better rewards. While you can’t hit as high a stat cap as you can from URA Finale, you’re more likely to arrive at better quality and highly competitive trainees from Unity Cup, especially because the 1800 Wit cap is exceptionally important to skill usage and you can expect most highly competitive players to be looking to hit those high numbers. This also makes Guts/Wit builds very viable in Unity Cup, as well as the importance of Riko Kashimoto’s card once again as one of the best cards in the game.

Trackblazer did get some changes, but they’re a little odd: Speed, Power, and Guts are all still 1200, but Stamina can now hit 1900, and Wit 1500. While these are impressive numbers in a bubble, you’re unfortunately going to see far more trainees with higher Speed stats and Wit stats that will greatly eclipse any benefit that a 1900 Stamina Uma will give you. It does at least mean that on average you should be getting similar quality trainees from Trackblazer and Unity Cup, but also that Unity Cup is now your better destination for Ace trainees until the new scenario arrives later this month.

Positional Changes and Updates

There were also some surprise changes to the various positions in the game, giving them all access to new tools and skills that can help them all become or remain highly competitive. There was a fairly long list of changes to skills in general posted on the official website for Umamusume, so I’ll only be focusing on the general changes and things to be aware of for specific trainees. Front Runners now have the ability to be run alone; in previous versions, you often needed to run double Front Runners to make them competitive against Pace Chasers who would surprisingly close the gap. It might still be better to run two, but you can now run a single Front Runner in your PVP teams and find them performing much, much better than they did in the past. For the other positions, Pace Chasers, Late Surgers and End Closers will no longer be penalized as harshly for being too ahead of their respective packs. This was often seen as “rubberbanding” in previous versions, where a trainee who was running far better than their competitors would suddenly be pulled back into the pack and sometimes lose on account of being penalized in terms of velocity for, essentially, being better than the other racers ahead of and behind them.

If you happen to be in the Nasty Nature Crew and love running a Debuffer, you’ll also be happy to know that it is much easier to ruin people’s lives with Debuffers in your team, as the game has been adjusted to no longer check for the Rushed status of ALL racers, and instead now only checks for OPPONENT racers. Essentially, the previous check would look at ALL trainees on the field, meaning that your own trainees being debuffed could trigger or hinder the effectiveness of your debuffs, making them do nothing; now, they will only look to see what opponents are doing, meaning that you are more likely to have your debuffer help your team instead of doing nothing.

Finally, any trainee that has 1200+ Power can now activate “Fully Charged”, a status that will occur in the late race and looks to see if your trainee meets the status threshold. If they do, they’ll receive an acceleration boost that scales off of your trainees power. This sounds great, and it is, but 1200+ Power is actually a bit harder than it initially seems; Power is one of the harder stats to raise to exceptionally high levels and also get what you need out of Speed, but with a Guts build, it is possible to at least meet the minimum for this if you’re looking for it. Overall, Fully Charged is “nice to have” but not essential to a high powered trainee; at best, you should be aware of it for what you might face in the future.

Spark Rerolls Are Finally Here!

If you’ve been playing since launch or for a while, you are probably familiar with the joke about “1 Star Guts” when it comes to sparks. Despite all your hard effort and amazing success, nothing feels worse than getting to the end of the career and getting a totally useless set of sparks that means your strong trainee will maybe be visible in team trails and do nothing else. Well, you can now use TP to reroll for sparks, costing 30 TP to reroll, and you get to choose which set you prefer after the reroll. You won’t lose the sparks you started with, and sparks are also not guaranteed to be better on a reroll, but it is at least a far better quality of life feature to allow you to choose between various options of sparks than to simply grin and bear it when you get nothing you wanted. Considering how quickly we are getting these various changes and updates, it isn’t unlikely to expect that the Spark Selector mechanic from JPN is going to arrive in Global as well (although, frankly, at 100 Carat a use, I think you’re better off just using your TP and trying again).

Independent Training (AFK Auto) Coming Soon!

Although this update has already delivered on far faster auto play and made going through career modes for better trainees far easier and faster, the Umamusume team posted that before the end of the month and the new scenario arrives that Global will be getting Independent Training far earlier than anyone really expected. This is essentially an “afk training” mode that allows the game to run a career mode in the background, even with the app closed, meaning that you can have your daily career going without even having to look at your phone or the screen on your PC. Essentially you get to choose how you want the game to prioritize things for training, and it makes choices and runs through the career for you based on that suggestion. Although it isn’t going to result in the best trainees ever, you’d be surprised at how well optimized this is, and for more casual players, this implementation means that you can now fulfill your daily career run without a care in the world. Select a trainee, turn it on, and check back later to see what happened; no matter what, you’ve completed a career! This is maybe the most surprising part of the overall announcement, and even though it isn’t live yet, it will be in a few weeks and will likely make the game far more relaxing to play.

Brighter Together: Our Grand Concert Is Almost Here!

We also got the news that the new scenario, renamed to “Brighter Together: Our Grand Concert” is almost here! This means that if Trackblazer wasn’t already basically dead as disco thanks to the changes to Unity Cup, it certainly is now; you will have no reason to ever play Trackblazer again while Grand Concert is available to you. We’ll have more about the new scenario as it gets closer, and I’ll be sure to write up a guide similar to our Trackblazer one for you all to help navigate it, but know that going into this new scenario that it is far less taxing and frustrating than Trackblazer was, and also far easier to get high quality trainees out of. Next time, we’ll talk about July’s overall schedule (Hint: Don’t pull anything except Summer Maruzensky’s SSR card), and the upcoming Champion’s Meeting.
