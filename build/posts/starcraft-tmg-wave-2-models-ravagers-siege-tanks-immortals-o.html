[StarCraft TMG] Wave 2 Models: Ravagers, Siege Tanks, Immortals, Oh My

October 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

It’s a pretty good time to be playing the StarCraft TMG. Between a new balance patch, the updated release schedule, and the previews that have gone up for the November releases, we’ve got quite a bit of meat on our plate. Today we’re going to dig into some of that meat and talk about the new units that came out this month.

Ravagers

Added with StarCraft 2: Legacy of the Void expansion, Ravagers quickly became a fan favorite. A quick-moving psuedo-artillery unit that rewarded micromanagement and allowed Zerg to push entrenched positions earlier, Ravagers function in much the same way on the tabletop. Taken individually or in units of two, Ravagers are Speed 4/8, with an armor and evade stat of 5+, and a comfortable 9 hit points. With the Squadron rule improving their coherency to 4”, in the movement phase Ravagers can perform the Deep Tunnel action, which allows them to relocate at the end of the round to a new position.

Their flagship ability, Corrosive Bile, lets you set up one Corrosive Bile token for each model in the unit within 14” of that model. At the end of the Assault Phase, each unit within an inch of that token will immediately take Hits (5). Their assault phase ranged weapon, Plasma Discharge, is a 4 RoA weapon hitting on 3’s and surging against Light. While respectable, the single point of damage it deals means generally it might be better to get away from the enemy as it probably won’t be doing much.

Credit: Blizzard Entertainment/Archon Studios

Besides the Burrow Ambush upgrade most Zerg units have, their other two upgrades both improve the Corrosive Bile token. Bloated Bile Ducts improves the token's effect range to 3", allowing you to hit more enemy units with it or screen a zone out more effectively. Potent Bile will apply Debuff (Armor) 1, improving how much damage goes through on those hits. Zealots and Marines in particular despise this upgrade. Both of these upgrades are 20/40, making them fairly expensive on a unit of 2, but definitely worth considering and trimming upgrades around as they massively improve the utility and damage of the Ravager.

Placing your Corrosive Bile token near ranged units that have already activated is crucial, as you will subsequently force your opponent to choose between Shooting and taking damage, or Running and wasting their chance to shoot. Deep Tunnel can let you stage to a new position for discharging more bile in the subsequent round. Both of these abilities are performed in the Movement Phase, and you can perform them before or after a move, so setting your Corrosive Bile token then running away with your big Speed stat is a great way to harass enemy units without suffering damage from the enemy in return. Combine this Deep Tunnel, and you’ll set yourself up perfectly to relocate somewhere to reset your Bile Token in the next Movement phase, before moving to safety and beginning the process again. Despite their large hit point pool, Ravagers are very frail on the save and evade stat compared to Roaches, and Marauders and Immortals will love to try to hunt these guys down. Hit and run is the name of the game, and Ravagers are very good at playing it.

Zerg Ravager | Credit: Benjamin "Toll" Scott

Ravagers also come with the Cocoon Tactical Card, with two powerful Movement Phase abilities on it. Ravager Morph is a once per game ability that lets you morph a Roach into a Ravager. Keep in mind this new Ravager will be bereft of any upgrades, so plan accordingly if and when you want to use it. Spawn Larva allows you to perform Respawn (2) on any Size 1 unit, like your Zerglings. Bringing back two models, when combined with the Swarmlings' innate ability to regenerate, can bring 4 or 5 Swarmlings each turn can swing things back your way if the tide begins to turn. While strong, both these abilities come at a cost; Cocoon does not provide a Biomass, so you’ll only be using it for its abilities all game.

Siege Tank

For many players, when asked to think of the most iconic StarCraft unit, it will be the Siege Tank. A combined armored platform and mobile artillery unit, the Siege Tank is great at two things: blowing things up, and blowing lots of things up. Always taken as a single unit, the Siege Tank has a base cost of 220 minerals, but considering the Siege Mode upgrade cost 20 minerals, we can safely consider it a 240 base cost unit. With a Speed of 7, and whopping 14HP, it's reasonably mobile and deceptively tough even considering its mediocre 5+ armour. While not in Siege Mode, the unit passively benefits from the Heavy Plating rule, gaining Tough (1) and vastly improving your saving throws. It also has the Large rule, meaning despite being Size 2 while not in Siege Mode it cannot move through gaps smaller than 3”. This rule also allows the model to pass through, and end its move on, Size 0 or 1 terrain pieces, and if it does either of those things, that Terrain piece is removed from the game. Rolling thunder indeed.

Credit: Blizzard Entertainment/Archon Studios

The Siege Mode upgrade lets you engage Siege Mode before or after your movement, and utilize the Shock Cannon. You can also take the Coordinated Strike upgrade, and target a friendly non-engaged unit on the battlefield. When shooting with the Shock Cannon, you can ignore its range limitation and instead target an enemy unit within 8” and visible to a model in the selected friendly unit, effectively utilizing them as a spotter. These actions are both used in the Movement Phase, so plan accordingly.

In the Assault Phase, while not in Siege Mode, the Siege Tank utilizes its Twin Cannon profile. With a Range of 12”, an RoA of 8, and Pierce 2 and Surge against Armored targets, even without the cannon it can devastate armored targets. It also has a Devastating Charge of (4) 3+, if you need to use that for whatever reason. But the real showstopper is of course the Siege Mode-exclusive Shock Cannon. With a base range of 18” and surging against both Light and Armored targets, the Shock Cannon will delete entire units. Dealing damage equal to the size characteristic of the target, and with RoA of BT+4, you want to maximize how many models you’re hitting, as not only does it increase your RoA but you’ll also surge against every model you hit, denying them armor saves. The only downside to this beast is that it hits on 4+, so you could be prone to a whiff on low dice count.

Credit: Blizzard Entertainment/Archon Studios

In the Movement Phase, stage this somewhere defensible, like near or on a home objective. You don't necessarily need to get it where it needs to go Round 1, but by the end of Round 2’s movement phase it should be in a semi-permanent home. After that, it begins to speak for itself. Use things like the new Factory (Tech Lab) to give it Precision (2) and negate some of those potential low rolls on the 4+. Its Shaped Blast upgrade lets you shoot into engagement range once per game. Very useful if you expect to be going into a combat heavy list, as you can potentially remove your own units from Combat by eliminating the engaged enemy unit, freeing up your own unit to Shoot accordingly. (Condit: Though you will need to be careful since the Spillover rule does mean your own units will take damage if they're caught under the template.) Its final upgrade, Smart Shells, gives you a once per game shot at Long Range 24” and with Indirect Fire. This one can be hit or miss (literally) as the Long Range Shock Cannon will be hitting on 5+. Into something like a swarm of Zerglings, where you’ll have up to 22 Surging rolls, and combined with the Precision from the Factory (Tech Lab) it can soften up targets before they can close the gap.

Immortal

Returning to serve on the tabletop, the last mainline unit in this wave is the Immortal. Dragoon pilots willingly relocated into the Immortal chassis, continuing to share their centuries of combat experience with other Protoss warriors. Like in the game, Immortals serve as Armored hunter-killers, surging against these targets while reducing incoming damage of anything more than 1. With a Speed of 7 they are deceptively quick, and their combined 4+ Save, 6 Shields and 8 Hit Points makes them a difficult target to engage at range.  With their Improved Barrier rule, a 0 PE Reaction, they can reduce incoming damage from a shooting attack to 1, which can be life saving against a Siege Tank which otherwise deals 3 damage per success with no armor saves. Combine this with the ability from your new Robotics Facility card to give you Tough (1) and Dodge (1) and your shields are all but guaranteed to hold.

Credit: Blizzard Entertainment/Archon Studios

With the Indomitable rule, Immortals are blessed with the ability to shoot out of combat, but this ability can be a double edged sword. Enemies can shoot back into it, and whatever it shoots will gain an Evasion roll that the Immortal will not benefit from. The Immortal is vulnerable to being swarmed by Zealots or Zerglings, who can potentially lock it into a very unfavorable combat.

The Immortal really shines when providing fire support against those pesky Armored targets. Its stock weapons, Photon Disruptors, are actually two separate attacking profiles. The Right Phase Disruptor has the Sidearm keyword, allowing you to shoot with both weapons in a phase. Both weapons otherwise share identical stats; a range of 12, a RoA of 4, hitting on 3+, surging D3 against armored targets with a damage of 2, and being able to fire at Long Range 16”. On the downside, they also have the Concentrated Fire (1) meaning they can never eliminate more than a single target during an action, so even firing both will mean you never get more than two model kills on them.

Their upgraded weapon, the Phase Disruptor, can be considered a small upgrade veering on sidegrade. Like the base guns, they have two profiles, one of which has side arms. This weapon trades Long Range 16” and the 2 damage for a base damage of 1 but gains the Pierce (3) Armored rule and loses Concentrated Fire, allowing damage to spill over and eliminate more models. Excellent into swarming armored units like Roaches and Ravagers, who likely will die to single round of shooting, but significantly more anemic into Light targets who will be suffering only a single damage point per success now if these bigger targets aren’t available. You can choose to swap out only a Photon Disruptor for a Phase Disruptor if you wish, and this isn’t a terrible choice into something like Zerg which often will give you multiple targets. Since the weapons batch separately, you can optimize accordingly which units you want to shoot with which weapons. (Condit: The separate batching also means each weapon gets its own Surge die, so even if you wind up firing them both at the same Armoured target you're a little better off than you would have been otherwise.)

Credit: Blizzard Entertainment/Archon Studios

Outside of the weapon upgrades, they also come with two additional ones. Fury Unyielding gives their ranged weapons Critical Hit (1) whenever they are within 3” of an objective, providing another hit that bypasses armor saves, and a must have for anybody who just wants to point their Immortal in a direction and have it remove things. For the Ancients gives you Precision (1) whenever you are shooting a target from more than 8” away. With the Photon Disruptors, this synergizes quite nicely and can help offset Long Range shooting, but may be less useful with the Phase Disruptors as their range is only 12”. But consider things like the Twilight Council tactical card, which can boost your ranged weapons by 4”, and this upgrade may be worth the squeeze even there.

Final Thoughts

Three great new units with three very different niches continue to shape the battlefield in exciting new ways. Ravagers will disrupt your opponents careful positioning, allowing Hydralisks to take shots or Zerglings to charge even as you force them backwards, the Siege Tank provides the heavy fire support your Terran armies crave, and the Immortal will hunt down any armored targets and give them a beat down. The StarCraft Tabletop Miniatures Game is shaping up to be a fantastic addition to our retinue of wargames, and these new units and tactical cards will let you pound your enemies into submission like never before.
