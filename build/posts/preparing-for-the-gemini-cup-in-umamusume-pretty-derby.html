Preparing for the Gemini Cup in Umamusume: Pretty Derby

May 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The reruns of Champions Meetings are underway, and our second returning challenge is the Gemini Cup, albeit with quite a few changes from the initial running of the competition. Unlike Taurus Cup, which was functionally the same as it ever was, Gemini Cup’s second turn round the bend changes the distance of the race completely! But don’t worry, if you’re looking to get your trainees up to speed for this upcoming event, we’ve got the tips you’ll want for training a new set of Aces and Supports to see if you can fare better in the second Gemini Cup than the first (or, perhaps, try it for the first time!).

Before we get into that, did any of you happen to catch the Mambo Breaker competition this Memorial Day weekend at Combo Breaker? I wanted to give a shoutout to Sayaduck for putting on an excellent and amazing Umamusume: Pretty Derby competition on a big stage, and shout out to the 67 people that competed! If you didn’t see it our are curious, check the link above for the official vod, and see just how amazing Umamusume competition can be on a big screen in a huge hall with a ton of people.

Gemini Cup Returns With a New Cut



The second running of Gemini Cup changes things up quite a bit, so let’s cover that first. This time, Gemini Cup is a 1600m Mile Distance competition run from the Left during Spring in Sunny Weather on Firm Conditions in Tokyo. While a lot of these keywords are very similar to the usual expectations for Green Skills, the distance is going to be the biggest change from the previous run and also change up who you can run and how. The good news here is that if you are looking to get a new Ace for this competition, Milers are extremely privileged right now due to how well they perform in Trackblazer; you can very easily train up a Miler Ace thanks to the bonuses and stat gains from Trackblazer faster than you may have in the past.

Firm Conditions is still one of the most widely available Green skills in the game, and with the release of Nishino Flower as a trainee/parent option, you have a lot of ways to potentially obtain the skill either through cards or inheritances. Firm Conditions is probably one of the skills you should always shoot for on your trainee when looking at Greens, especially for Front Runners, simply because it is so easily available that you can consider a pretty sure bet. If you’re training a Front Runner, you could even consider trying to snag Firm Course Menace from Narita Top Road’s SSR events.

Tokyo Racecourse on the other hand remains pretty restrictive, only appearing on Vodka’s SR and SSR, as well as inherited from Ines Fujin, Special Week, Eishin Flash, or Winning Ticket. Spring Runner is another oddball, only available from Inari One Power, Sakura Chiyono O Stamina, and innately on Mejiro McQueen, Sakura Chiyono O, Cheerleader King Halo, Ballroom Seiun Sky, Anime Teio and Kitasan Black. These two are a lot harder to obtain if you aren’t specifically going to try inheriting them from your various parents, so if you’re trying for Groundwork you may want to secure your other greens before looking for these. Spring Runner also has a Gold version you can inherit from Cheerleader King Halo and Sakura Chiyono O called Spring Spectacle, by the way.

Left-Handed Is another skill that hasn’t changed much in terms of availability, although it is a bit more widely available than it was the first time Taurus Cup ran. You’re still looking to a specific set of cards, being Agnes Digital Power, Mejiro Ardan Wit, any Silence Suzuka card (including the Team Sirius card), or Zenno Rob Roy Speed. You can also try to inherit it off of a Mejiro Ardan or Silence Suzuka parent/grand-parent, which may be easier as none of those cards are particularly meta (although Team Sirius can be good if you aren’t training any of them).

Standard Distance is another fairly common skill, and primarily comes from Nishino Flower’s cards, as well as Kawakami Princess Power, El Condor Pasa Power, Eishin Flash Speed, Bamboo Memory Power, and Ines Fujin Wit. Sadly the cards that provide this aren’t great, and Nishino Flower Power is probably the best card in this pool. Finally, Sunny Days can be obtained from El Condor Pasa Power, Satono Diamond Stamina, Mejiro Dober Wit, and Hishi Akebono Guts; if you’re trying to get numerous Greens, you could look to run El Condor Pasa and target both Standard Distance and Sunny Days for maximum efficiency.

Trainees to Consider

The bad news is that Milers are a smaller field than Medium/Long competitors, but the good news is that there is a pretty wide variety of trainees you can select for Gemini Cup, from F2P options to deeper rosters.

If you don't run her yourself, Louder! Tracen Cheer! is very important to Late and End trainees.

Free to Play: Daiwa Scarlet remains an excellent choice for Front Runners in the F2P category, and Gold Ship, to the surprise of nobody at this point, remains a great choice for End Closers.

Two Stars: Two star competitors are sadly frozen out of Front Runner, but you can challenge in other categories easily; El Condor Pasa is amazing in Pace Chaser, while Mejiro Ryan and Vodka work for Late. You can also consider Grass Wonder if you are looking for a Debuffer in this race, although Debuffers are (less) common in the history of the 2nd Gemini Cup.

A new contender in Front Runner races, particularly in Mile and Mediums.

Three Stars: Front Runners are dominated by Three Stars, including most of the usual suspects at this point: Summer Maruzensky, Seiun Sky, Valentine Mihono Bourbon are here as usual, joined also by Smart Falcon and Ines Fujin. Wedding Fine Motion can also be an option in Pace, as is Christmas Oguri and Taiki Shuttle. Late and End is generally the domain of Cheer King Halo, Oguri CAp, Agnes Digital and Inari One, as well as Narita Taishin if you can tolerate training her (which is, to be fair, a lot easier in Trackblazer).

If you can't run her, try to inherit Budding Blossom.

A special shoutout here is Nishino Flower, the smallest Uma to ever do it, who makes for an amazing Pace Chaser Ace OR Pace Chaser parent; if you are considering running Pace in Gemini Cup and don’t have Nishino Flower, you may want to consider borrowing one as a Parent to get her unique skill; Gemini Cup is an excellent place to show off the power of the flower, so expect to see a lot of her in the competition, and if not her directly, a lot of people running Budding Blossom.

Stats and Skills

The Stats you’ll want to shoot for in Graded League are probably what you can expect of CMs of late, which is very high and almost approaching stat caps in numerous categories. Most very competitive Aces are going to want 1200 Speed, 1000+ Power and 600~ Stamina, 600 Guts and 900-1000 Wit, a fairly brutal mixture that requires excessively good rolls during Trackblazer runs and very specifically chosen decks (and luck. Lots of luck and patience). If you’re considering trying Open League, you’ll find that the stats are… actually a bit more comparable than you expect, but can be a little forgiving: 1200 Speed, 500+ Stamina, 1000 Power, 600 Guts, and 800 Wit, the last two of which are pretty easily obtainable. The biggest challenge with Open League is riding the line of getting to these numbers and NOT exceeding them, because moving your trainee from A+ to S makes the unable to compete and yet also not good enough for Graded either.

Yes, you still need this on Front Runners. Forever.

Although Miles are shorter than Mediums and Longs, you are still going to want to consider at least some form of recovery in your trainee’s kit; there is a very good chance that Global Players especially will include Debuffers or at least try to debuff, which means that Stamina management is going to be important. This is especially true because the Stamina cap for Graded races is pretty low, since the focus is going to be on Speed and Power; you won’t want to sacrifice either of those for Stamina, so try to pack in at least 1 or 2 recovery skills in your skill suite if possible. Good options are Hydrate, Moxie, Corner Recovery, After-School Stroll, or Pace Strategy, and you don’t really need to splurge on Gold recoveries; just remember that if you are running Christmas Oguri, you need 3 recovery skills for her Unique to activate.

A pretty solid strategy for your Support Card deck is going to be 2 Speed, a Power Card, and 3 Wit Cards; you won’t need to worry about splashing in Riko Kashimoto here for Stamina/Guts growth, and your Sparks will also want to prioritize Speed, Power, or Wit depending on what your trainee’s growth favors. If you’re looking to do a Guts Build, you’ll still want at least 1 Speed Card, 3 Guts, and 2 Wit cards to round things out. Speed wise, Narita Top Road is probably your best Speed option and likely worth borrowing, especially if you are going to utilize your own Kitasan Black Card; if you’re doing Guts, use whichever speed card you have if you can’t fill in with 3 Guts cards and need to borrow one. The SSR Haru Urara Guts card really shines here, and you can also get a lot of mileage out of SR Admire Vega if it is MLB; other good options include Ines Fujin Guts and Bamboo Memory, as well as Yukino Bijin’s Festival Guts SSR. Guts has a lot of build variety, but can be a frustrating run at times, so consider what you want to try and experiment a bit before cementing on one or the other.

You’ll probably want to try inheriting a few very specific uniques: For Pace Chasers, you want Budding Blossom from Nishino Flower (if you are not running her), and Give Mummy a Hug from Halloween Super Creek and you can also consider the always good Triumphant Pulse or Lights of Vaudeville. Front Runners are going to want Angling and Scheming but also consider El Condor Pasa’s Victoria por Plancha, which can help compete with Angling and Scheming from other Front Runners. Late and End are going to look for very similar skills, Triumphant Pulse, Let’s Pump Some Iron, and Cheer King Halo’s Louder! Tracen Cheer is a new option to fish for in your parent and lineages.

Half a Year, Full Year, 2 Year!

The ever changing state of Global Umamusume means that we have a few interesting things coming up in the next few months. We’ve exceeded the half year real time of the Global Launch since last June, as well as much of 2026, and we’re approaching the first actual Year celebration for Global Uma. However, we’re also approaching the 1.5 and 2 year anniversary of the accelerated timeline, meaning that there is a lot of potential for exciting things to come as the game should be speeding up again; Trackblazer is almost a thing of the past! Until then, if you have any questions, feel free to shoot me an email over at marcy@tabletopbattles.com, or drop by the Discord and ask me there!
