Commander Focus: Dwarves, Wheels, & Balin, Loremaster

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The history of Dwarves in Magic is a storied one. Receiving their first big typal push all the way back in Odyssey (funny name for a set in a world without Odysseus, in retrospect), it took all the way until Kaladesh 15 years later for the next evolution of the type. There, they were bound with Vehicles, which lasted until Kaldheim, where they were linked additionally with Equipment; to make a long story short, since Kaldheim, Dwarf typal support in some form has been around every year, ebbing and flowing with worlds in which the stout of body and large of heart were relevant. Middle Earth is of course one of those worlds, perhaps the world, and as we return to it in The Hobbit there’s no shortage of cards to excite Dwarf enjoyers. The thing is though, Dwarves already have a quintessential typal Commander, one that is arguably the best Monocolor Commander in cEDH, Magda, Brazen Outlaw. She isn’t really typal, if you actually look at her, but that doesn’t stop a deck at the format’s highest level of play from unironically jamming cards like Bloodfire Dwarf with intent to see it in the opening grip. Magda is typal, but only insofar as she cares that Dwarves…are, that they exist, and are cheap. That tradition continues with my favorite Dwarf from The Hobbit, Balin, Loremaster. While he’s slower than Magda, in Brackets a step below cEDH he’s arguably stickier, and merges Magda’s play patterns with that of another famous Red-adjacent cEDH darling, Etali, Primal Hunger. Let’s talk about how.

"If you have an enduring story..."

Let’s start with Balin’s condition, because it hinges upon a new mechanic from The Hobbit, the ‘Enduring Story’. Much like Ascend, if you ever control a card that mentions an Enduring Story, and you control 3+ historic permanents, you have that story marked as ‘on’ for the rest of the game. In Commander, given Balin himself is a Legend, and we probably have spare Treasure tokens or Legendary Lands lying around, this is functionally flavor text. It does matter a bit more in Limited, but I have a hunch it will be on for most decks anyways. Onto the meat of his textbox though, and we see that Balin affords you a wheel (for the same number of cards) when he or a Dwarf enters, pinging opponents for cards discarded if you have an Enduring Story. This is already fairly silly with anything that benefits from mass discard, like Surly Badgersaur & Magmakin Artillerist, but once you realize Dwarves have an abundance of exactly 1 mana Creatures you’ll realize this will often trigger 3-4 times per turn if untapped-with. These are essentially mana dorks in Magda, but here they’re Fateful Showdowns that hit each opponent; the floor is obviously a heck of a lot higher with the queen of the Dwarves, but Balin is his own form of insanity, albeit slower. Moreover, Balin works wonderfully with a second suite of cards you often see in Monored, which aren’t typically used in Magda: Temporary clones.

Turns out it was super easy, who knew?

Twinflame, Heat Shimmer, Molten Duplication, and more—these are where Balin actually gets his power. Unlike in Magda, who can make use of these 1-mana Dwarves from the first turn by coming down early, at 5 mana Balin requires a fair bit more setup. Just playing a 1 mana functional-vanilla feels pretty rough until he’s on the battlefield, so the density we play needs to be a tad austere. Clones though, they never go out of style. Of course they combo with Dualcaster Mage, but in Balin, they each represent 2 triggers of his ability on resolution, with the enters effect of the clone, and the original Balin seeing his mirrored copy, before it dies to the Legend Rule. While the damage is of course nice, and stacks up handsomely, the real power of Balin comes down to simply seeing enough cards. While you’re dunking opposing faces and ripping through your Library by way of cheap Dwarves & clones, you’ve probably pitched a considerable number of cards. Whether you intend to get them back with a Bag of Holding, or have cycled them to top each trigger via Library of Leng, Balin gives you the tools to continue his funny little game, so long as you play a necessary density of ways to both trigger him…and retain the necessary cards in hand.

Playing with Advantage

Red is very good at churning out mana, playing through hate, and putting a clock on your opponents. Those traits are not conducive to a large hand size, and Balin certainly feels that if built haphazardly. While you can pop off pretty fast, by way of rituals and means of pitching for mana, by the time Balin comes down it’s not unlikely you’re only wheeling for 3-4 cards, and that isn’t thrilling for the investment. Therefore, part of the challenge that Balin poses is squaring the circle of red card advantage in hand; impulse draw, its main means of seeing cards, won’t cut it here. Red traditionally lacks ways of filling their hand, outside of exactly Wheel of Fortune and its peers. Those cards are good, and in here, but if you’re entirely reliant on them be prepared to be disappointed. The good news is that you can use a hypergeometric calculator to get a rough idea of your likelihood to hit a card that propels Balin forwards, based on the number of cards seen. Essentially, if you plug in every Dwarf & temporary clone being played in your list as intended targets, you can then modify the sample size and pool size to get a feel for how many targets you need to play.

He will never be ballin'. He has always been Balin.

This gets even cooler when you realize a temporary clone provides two wheels (albeit, you’d need to cast at Instant-speed to use cards from the first redraw), so some number of your cards expand the sample when seen. For Flash Dwarves, we’re minimally-invested, with just Emergence Zone & Winding Canyons, but it does still come up. Just remember, unless your Dwarves are cantripping, every time you see a new sample it’s n-1 cards, where n is number of cards played from the hand. If you want your wheels to go positive on advantage, look to Phial of Galadriel, one of my favorite non-combo pieces to look for in the midgame. Whenever we're drawing cards via a wheel, Phial nets us an extra card, as we're emptyhanded before the new set of draws occur. The lifegain isn't just incidental either, because as we'll cover later, giving Balin Lifelink is unironically part of a win condition. If you’re just trying to pop off though, making your {R}-cost Dwarves free is the more important part, even compared to advantage. Birgi, God of Storytelling & the new The Vision and Scarlet Witch do this readily, but if you’re willing to get your hands dirty, a Phyrexian Altar is just as powerful. For whatever reason, storming off by gambling on finding Dwarves and shooting your opponents until you bust out is wildly Dwarven. Slam that comically-oversized keg on the table and roll those bones.

Stop Posting About [Baller]

You know now how Balin operates in a general sense, but what are the actual paths to victory? The most obvious is of course via DCM, Dualcaster Mage, which goes infinite with hasty bodies alongside any of Red’s clone spells. It’s a mainstay of the color identity, especially in cEDH/Bracket 4, and especially so for a list already excited to run those Twinflame-style clones. Why isn’t it in this list, then? DCM is my high water mark for what separates Bracket 3 & 4, being a reasonably-redundant 2-card combo that come down as soon as turn ~3, alongside rituals; it’s not as resilient for Monored as a combo like Underworld Breach+Wheel of Fortune+Lion’s Eye Diamond, but it certainly is faster—too fast, in my eyes, for where I’d want Balin to sit. Besides, we have significantly cooler ways to win here, starting with quite the novel concept for the color, in giving our Commander Lifelink. Balin can repeatedly deal chunks of 10-20 damage across three players, as we cycle through our Dwarves, and one of the best ways to use this excess life is via Treasonous Ogre, who in turn exchanges 3 life for a single {R}. With three opponents, that means a single discard with Lifelink generates one mana, and from there we’ve more than got the capabilities to tear through hands and find Dwarves, as we push towards the endgame. The biggest boon to this line is how accessible it is; Lifelink arrives via Witch’s Clinic, Hidden Hideout, & Shadowspear, with the former two being extremely low opportunity cost Lands we can incidentally find while we’re cycling hands, and the latter being a 1-mana Artifact, available via Urza’s Saga, Goblin Engineer, & Tezzeret, Cruel Captain. The Ogre, meanwhile, has the right power to be accessed via Imperial Recruiter, and can be utilized from the graveyard via Agatha’s Soul Cauldron & Dermotaxi (both of which can likewise be accessed via Engineer). Note that Hideout’s counter requirement can be filled by things like Agatha’s…or Dwarven Armorer. Not as free as other options, but it’s come up! You can put some iteration of this combo together about as frequently as the Breach line in Red, and that’s saying something, because wow is it sick to pull off.

Get to spinning.

One of the best ways to find more Dwarves to cycle Balin’s ability is via pieces of our other combo, Cloudstone Curio, two nonartifact 1-mana Dwarves, and a way to break even on mana. I say nonartifact because we are on Universal Automaton, and ~9 targets besides. There’s a caveat to that caveat, however, as with the inclusion of Fíli and Kíli, Joyous that number rises to 10; costing 3 and producing {R}{R} with haste, these are effectively the same cost as all our janky vanilla-ish Dwarves for combo purposes, albeit with a higher initial cost to kick off. In terms of ways to go mana-neutral, there’s Birgi, God of Storytelling, The Vision and Scarlet Witch, and the previous Treasonous Ogre line. In all these cases, we win via Balin damage. Just don’t forget to stack your triggers such that you discard before the Dwarf is bounced via Curio.

Example Decklist: Balin Out of Control

You’ll notice immediately that we’ve forgone fetchlands here, and that’s a purposeful choice; we’ll be ripping through our Library by way of Balin, and so in the mid-to-late game there’s a very real chance we won’t have the necessary basics to fetch, given the density of Legendary lands. Getting an Enduring Story isn’t difficult, but we never under any circumstances want to have a gamestate where it’s off when Balin comes down. Monored lacks the Landfall synergies, or use for cards in the graveyard, to really make fetches necessary, which is a benefit for those playing with a budget (and/or without proxies). Some Red decks definitely still want them (Tersa Lightshatter & Gau, Feral Youth are great examples I’ve covered before), but in general a deck drawing/milling a ton will get less value over time unless you’re on ~16+ basics.

While we’re not what I’d call a “traditional” typal strategy, there are still worthwhile tools available which hinge on the fact we’re full up on Dwarves. In my eyes, the biggest one is Heirloom Blade, which allows us to ensure there’s a Dwarf to continue Balin’s chain of triggers, so long as the wielder meets an untimely end. That can be achieved via Phyrexian Altar or Lazotep Quarry, though of course it also works as a rattlesnake effect to prevent attacks where we’d be able to block. Though there’s a chance we could in theory run out of Dwarves, the amount of wheeling that occurs makes it worthwhile to include one of the shuffle titans, if only to reset our Dwarf count in Library. Another typal piece of note is Mana Echoes, which produces an inordinate amount of Colorless mana; this is worse than if it were Red, as it prevents Echoes from slotting into our core combos, but the sheer amount of mana produced means the card still ends up being worthwhile.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

As is always the case with Monored, pushing the deck to B4 does mean you get access to Blood Moon, and thanks to Balin, you’re far more likely to see it or Magus of the Moon throughout the course of a given game. The truth of the matter is, however, that Blood Moon these days is only-okay. Rocks are plentiful enough that unless it’s coming down in the first three turns, most players won’t be stalled, and ergo Balin’s capacity to dig for it is moot. I believe Balin solidly sits as a great pick for high B3, with the opportunity to skew low if you lean more into Dwarf typal as the theme, rather than just an enabler.

Mogged by Magda

DZO contributed to one of the most striking showcase frames of Magic history, and Magda's is a fantastic part of that.

Magda is a tough act to follow, for Monored Dwarves, and frankly Monored as a whole. It’s an unglamorous thing to be compared to her, because she’s one of perhaps the best Commanders of all time. She’s in Vintage Cube, appears frequently in top cut, and is the second-most-popular Monored Commander on EDHREC. I remind you of this, dear reader, because absent these comparisons to Magda, Balin would be the coolest Monored Commander released since Final Fantasy, if not further beyond. If he didn’t mention Dwarf, and instead honed in on some modestly-supported type, I guarantee you there’d be ample praise to be had, but instead at 5 mana he’s trapped in the outlaw’s shadow. In spite of this, I’ve had an absolute blast with him as a Commander, and because he plays so differently to Magda—churning through options via wheels rather than tutoring key pieces directly—he suits my preferences & playstyle far more. Nothing gets me excited more, as a Johnny, than wresting control of variance from cruel mathematics, and seeing as many potential options as one can. The fact Balin kills your opponents while you’re busy rifling through your deck is just a bonus, really.

Until next time, forge an enduring story.
