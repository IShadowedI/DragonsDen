Hobby Build Academy: Say Hello to Gunpla, Haro!

July 27th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Summer is coming to an end, and you know what that means: Time to go back to school! But not just any school, because we’re introducing a new column today that promises to take you to class with us and explore the wide world of model hobby building! If you love Gunpla, Lego, and similar styles of building but never really knew where to start or worried that they might be more daunting than they appear, never fear, Professor Marcy and her trusty assistant Elly will take you on a personal tour of getting started with models, reviewing kits, hobby tips and advice, painting, and much more!

Come join us as we talk about building these three little guys!

For our inaugural article, we’ll be introducing ourselves, talking about the recent Gundam Base Pop-Up Shop that helped inspire this column. We hope you’ll join us in this regularly running article as we both work our way through various models, coming from a combination of model enthusiast and relative beginner; to kick off the column, we’ll be reviewing two Gunpla kits this week, 2019’s Gundam Base Haro [Painting Model] Clear & White and 2020’s Hello Kitty x Haro!

Introducing Your Academy Faculty: Marcy and Elly

Marcy: Hello beautiful readers! As always, I’m Marcy, your maestra of advice and horse girl writer, but I’m also a longtime Gunpla and Lego builder that, in some ways, helped lead me to writing here at Tabletop Battles in the first place. Ever since I accidentally bit into a tube of super glue building a model battleship as a kid–

Elly: Wait, what?

Marcy: –Anyway, I’ve loved building models for a long time! I used to get Legos as a reward for doing well in school, and a comic store I used to frequent as a kid often carried Gunpla that I would sometimes buy with spare money I had as some of my first kits. It actually has been a while since I’ve built a proper full kit, though, since shifting to cards and tabletop games! Anyway, let me introduce you all to Elly!

Elly: ¡Hola! 

Haro plushies were all the rage; we actually picked up a Hatsune Miku Haro and a Red Zaku plush.

Marcy: Elly will be my amazing assistant instructor throughout these workshops, and we’ll be working on our own models and talking about how building them felt and what we thought about them. Elly’s my wife, and she’s an amazing, funny, and charming woman who I’m very fond of, if that’s not apparent. She’s an editor and streamer when she’s not playing with our cats or putting up with me.

Elly: Hey–

Marcy: Right! So, let’s start with a few little intro tidbits. How about you, what’s your history with Gunpla?

Elly: My brother and I were very much into anime as kids, and a particular giant robot show that blew our little minds away was none other than Gundam Wing. Growing up in Puerto Rico at a time where getting official anime and more niche hobby goods were hard to come by, so there were a ton of bootleg goods instead to fill the growing demand. Our local shop was filled with bootleg stuff, but back then, we didn't really care because it was the only way to get any sort of merch! It took me a long while, since I usually only had enough to rent their bootleg anime tapes (that I usually copied over to my own blank tape, hehe), but I mustered up my courage to get my first Gunpla: a Deathscythe model kit. I remembered it being difficult to put together, and almost giving up, but I persevered to complete it, and when it was finished, I was so happy and proud! My own Deathscythe, how cool! Sadly, I don't have that model anymore; it got lost when I moved to the US. At that time, my home situation was a bit strained, so I had to keep some hobby stuff a secret from my parents, which meant I couldn't really get stuff I couldn't hide. So I enjoyed watching and helping my friends build their own model kits, while taking countless photos to look at over time. Thankfully, now I'm freely able to indulge in this stuff again, so I'm incredibly happy to be able to build kits and learn a bunch of new stuff while doing so!

We wanted to take this Epyon home, but they apparently never brought it at all to the event except to tease us with it.

Marcy: Is Deathscythe your favorite Gundam?

Elly: Deathscythe will always have a soft spot in my heart, because it was the first Gunpla kit I built. But then I became enamored with Epyon's design and colorway. However, I wasn't able to get that kit as a kid; our local shop was either unable to get it in, or they couldn't keep it in stock by the time I got there. Perhaps this will be remedied soon... If we want to make this a top 3 list, my third favorite Gundam is God Gundam from the very hot-blooded and passionate G Gundam series. That show fascinated me as a kid, and it's still one of my faves. I don’t think I actually know what yours are?

Keep your eye out for Zeong. Just saying, this one might have come home with us.

Marcy: I’m a big fan of some of the stranger designs, which fits when you think about how I am for a minute. The Zeong is absolutely up there for me, just an absolutely peak design that is both powerful, stupid, and a bit scary. A lot of the original Gundam designs rank up there for me because of the somewhat goofy nature of them, and honestly Original Gundam Zeon has to have the all timer roster of designs like the Zaku, Dom, Gouf, Gogg, Acguy, Gyan, and of course Big Zam. I’m a big fan of the GQuuuuuuX, and probably my final top three is also the Epyon, just an AMAZING design and such a slick profile.

The Gundam Base Pop-Up Shop: A Catalyst

This was the first thing we saw, and honestly, it was a great start.

Last weekend, Elly and I were able to attend the Gundam Base Pop-Up Shop in Florida Mall; we’d heard about the event but hadn’t had the time to go visit it, so we managed to take two trips to the event at the end of the week: one to see the Pop-Up Shop itself, and another to get an introduction to The Gundam Card Game (spoiler: I’ll be talking about that soon here on TTB too!). The Pop-Up Shop was surprisingly well stocked even after weeks of running, and the crowd on both visits was far bigger than I think either of us imagined.

Marcy: I think what stood out to me was just how popular Gunpla seemed to be; while there were certainly a lot of tourists who were curious to see what was going on, it was really obvious to me that the crowds on our visits were looking for and excited to pick up just about anything they could get their hands on.

Elly: I really loved how many women and people of color we saw at the event too, it really helped make it obvious that everyone loves Gunpla and that just about anyone can get into it; we saw a lot of kids, older people, and just about everyone in between there.  People were swapping stories and chatting with other fans of all sorts of levels.

The pop up featured a free model kit workshop, and the tables were packed both days we went by people young and old.

Marcy: I really loved the free model building workshop they ran; sitting down and building a free little Gundam honestly really did light a fire in us both, since we’re sitting down to do this now because of it.

For a free model we built in 30 minutes, it was honestly very cool looking!

Elly: Having experienced many unorganized pop-up events, I was so surprised at how organized it was! They effectively and efficiently used their space so everything wasn't just squished together. The shop was way bigger than I thought it would be, and it had soooo many model kits to choose from! There were also some plushies and decorative pillows, along with some blind box and prize figures. It was essentially Gundam paradise for Gundam fans! You could get a free RX-78-2 Gundam model kit to build at their Gunpla Workshop station, which was a fun experience to share in a group. I wanna say that experience re-ignited my desire to build more stuff!

Don't worry, the Zaku statue had a friend.

Marcy: The Card Game table and availability was also great. It has been really frustrating to get our hands on the card game for prices that did not make it unappealing to do so, and so the second day we went back with the express purpose of getting our hands on the starter sets and see how well they taught the game; I tend to judge a game on how easy it is to teach, and I wanted to see how Bandai itself would actually accomplish that.

This thing ruled to see.

Elly: Their card game demo and TCG sales were limited only to the weekends, to keep things more controlled. That's something more companies SHOULD do, but that's commentary for another day. The card game demo had you learning the game with one of two decks that you could take home with you. The staff at that station were very lively and very much into the game with their vast knowledge, and that made it so much easier to get into the game for a smooth experience! We ended up grabbing more TCG decks to experiment with at home. Overall, it was an awesome and memorable event, and I can't wait for their return!

Elly’s Build: Hello Kitty x Gundam Haro

Hello, Haro Hello Kitty!

For our first set of model kit builds and reviews, we decided to start with something fun and simple: two Haro kits. Haro, if you’re somewhat unfamiliar with Gundam, is a robot that became kind of a brand mascot. It really isn’t that hard to see why, considering Haro is round, friend-shaped, and has appeared in just about every Gundam series in various ways. Bandai released a series of models dubbed “Haropla” based around the little guy, including a crossover version with another mascot icon: Hello Kitty.

The interior of the Hello Kitty Haro kit, showing the iconic ears already.

Elly: I actually bought this Haropla kit back when it was released, as well as receiving the Hello Kitty Zaku and Char Zaku kits as gifts, but I’d never quite felt motivated to build it until the Pop-Up Shop helped motivate me. I found the kit to be pretty easy to build, and it only took me around an hour to actually get the model completed, some of which was taken up by the application of the decals.

The Hello Kitty Haro box features the instructions on the inside of the box, which... is a choice.

Elly: Something I found weird about this kit was that the instructions were printed on the inside of the lid of the box, and I ended up having to flatten it out by ripping the corners to make it easier to read and follow. I’m not really sure if this is something they did as a trial to be more ecofriendly or if it is something that they do only on smaller kits like this, but I found it a little annoying! It didn’t stop me from having fun with the kit and it didn’t get in the way of building it, but it certainly made keeping the box impossible.

Elly had to get her own tweezers to help, mostly due to the fine and tiny stickers.

Elly: I do a lot of journaling, so I have my own pair of stationary tweezers, and I ended up having to get them out to work on Haro-kitty; the nose sticker in particular really gave me a hard time, but I think that the final product looked amazing and was worth the effort. I think the biggest surprise to me with the kit was the decals, though, and it kind of bumped up the difficulty of building this model. I think the final result looks really cute, though, so it was worth it.

The full guts of the Hello Kitty box; it was a little more involved than we expected, but turned out great.

Elly: In the end, I really liked the quick build and easy to put together kit that looks super cute! I think its fun that the model has some articulation, since you can move Haro’s ears, arms, and legs, and you can even remove the arms and legs to have Haro be in Ball mode too. Something that was surprising to both of us is that my Haro came with grips attached to its hands, meaning it can likely hold various Gundam sized accessories like beam sabers, shields, or guns.

A job well done!

Marcy’s Build: Gundam Base Haro [Painting Model] Clear & White

Gundam Base Limited Haro: Painting Model Clear & White

Marcy: My kit for this week was a dual build kit of Haros, one with a clear outer plastic shell and the other a white and grey kit that is meant to be painted. We picked this kit up at the Pop-Up Shop, and Elly actually has one of her own, so expect to see this kit potentially return when she works on hers at a later date, and we will be revisiting it to discuss how to paint Gunpla. I decided to go with a simple kit like this as well because I’ve been a little rusty on building models, but I also wanted to show that you can do some really great little customization to your models with very minimal effort.

The Clear and White pieces are basically identical, and that is indeed the whole kit for both models.

Marcy: I first built the solid color plastic Haro, which has a white outer shell and grey arms/internals. Something that surprised me while we were building the two Haros is that Elly’s version, being a year older, had a bit of articulation that mine didn’t: Haro opens up! I also noticed that my Haros didn’t have hands that could hold anything, but that’s okay; we’ll talk about that a bit later. The model went together very well, and I basically had “Haro” complete in about five minutes.

Haro arms

Marcy: The arms and legs use small rubber style ball joints that push into a socket that you can remove, which was nice; the initial instructions were a little unclear about that, making it sound like you had to choose to make EITHER a Ball Haro or Active Haro, but that’s not the case; I tested it a few times and found it simple to remove the arms and legs and replace the covers to transform the model between the two modes. The hands also attach to the balls at the end of the arm pieces, and although my kit doesn’t have hands with peg holes or grips, the instructions note that swapping in Super Deformed (SD) Gundam kit accessories make it possible, so next time I get a chance I will look into picking up some SD accessories to test it out.

Don't let fear of priming due to weather scare you; I primed this up in 100% humidity and it came out fine!

Marcy: The clear Haro is where I decided to get a little complicated. The guide showed the internal piece painted, and it made me inspired to do it myself; on a whim, I grabbed a can of Retributor Armor spray and Mr Surfacer 1500 Black spray, and quickly painted the body piece and limb pieces. My main goal in doing this, other than for myself, was to prove that painting in general is not that hard. It rained earlier on the day we built these models and humidity was at about 100%, yet I still managed to spray all of these pieces without an issue and had the paint job done and ready to dry in about 5 minutes total.

As a test, I wanted to see what it looked like unpainted, and realized then and there I needed to paint it up.

Marcy: Once the painting dried after about 30 minutes, I hit the body piece with some Reikland Fleshade Gloss, making sure not to let it pool; something you’ll have to contend with on Gunpla is the large amounts of flat surfaces and corners that they create, meaning that paint will potentially pool up and dry in unflattering ways if you paint them like a Warhammer miniature. Once I was satisfied that the model had some shading but wouldn’t have dark splotches, I put some Spiritstone Red on the eyes and some Nihilakh Oxide into parts to give it a little bit of a weathered feeling. The arms and joints I simply painted black, and used the Gundam Pour Type Marker: Black to fill in some areas I didn’t get with spray-painting. Honestly, I didn’t even need to bother, since none of those areas were visible once I snapped Haro together.

Look at that shiny little guy!

Marcy: I may go back and try some gloss varnish tricks I’ve read about with the outer shell of the clear Haro; for now, I think it looks great with the golden interior and painted eyes with little details! I found out that some of my GW paints have also dried up, but that tracks, since it has been a few years since I’ve used anything paint related. It was fun to get back into it and that, all told, it took me about an hour to paint up the model interior here, most of which was simply ensuring that the initial prime was dry enough and getting some of my old painting supplies out; someone with a more ready set-up or airbrush could probably do this in less than ten minutes.

Wrap-Up: What We Learned

Overall, the Haropla are a really fun little kit that can be a nice diversion if you’re a seasoned Gunpla builder, or a pretty good starting point for someone getting into the hobby. Although it isn’t quite marked as a beginner kit, there’s nothing that can really be messed up here, aside from the decal applications on the Hello Kitty variant. When completed, they look great and are fun to pose and play around with, and the Haropla line tend to get new releases pretty regularly through Bandai and Gundam Base, while also being very affordable; the two kits we showed off today were only 15 dollars for the Clear/Paint version, and 20 for the Hello Kitty version when it was released a few years ago.

Side by side, they look great and all have their own charm. We can't wait to paint up the white model.

Marcy: I think if you’re interested in Gunpla and like Gundam, a Haropla kit is actually a very solid place to start aside from Bandai’s “Entry Grade” line. They’re cheap when you buy them from legitimate retailers of Gunpla, quick to build, and really don’t have any parts you can mess up or break. If you have a pair of snippers from working on Warhammer Models or just a comparable pair from a hardware store or similar, that was really the only tool you need to work on this; frankly, most of the pieces came right off after snipping a single joint. I only actually filed a single piece on the Clear Haro’s inner body piece, making it very easy for any level of builder to work with.

Haro Hello Kitty had this "hinge" piece, which the ones Marcy built did not, an apparently new part to the line.

Elly: It's not a very big model, so I thought to start with something smaller and build up to bigger kits. A preview for what's to come! I don't know why I thought the designs would be printed on, so when I saw the stickers, I made sure to take it slow while applying them to be juuuust right. The stickers were definitely the most difficult part of the build. The rest of the build was pretty smooth-sailing. The clicks as the parts went into their place was so satisfying! I like that you can take the arms and legs off to display the Haro without them. I like even more that the base doubles as a storage area for the spare parts! This might make me want to make a little army of different Haros, each with their unique colors and designs. I feel like this might be a good starter kit to give to a beginner, so they can get a taste of the hobby.

Hello Kitty Haro, just happy to be here!

Marcy: They also paint up quick and easy, if you want to try it. I loved that the Paint Version kit came with a booklet with some very funny ideas to consider, like a Takoyaki Haro paint job that includes using wood shavings and some manner of epoxy for the sauce, and a metallic dabbed paint version that talks about using plastic wrap to dab the model with for effect. We have some plans to revisit this little guy to paint him up and discuss the painting process, but for this model, I will say that you can very easily spray some primer on or use paint markers and go to town and you’ll likely be very happy with how it turns out.

The interior paint was the right call here; I may go back and try to gloss the clear part to see if it looks even better.

Elly: I probably won’t try painting the Hello Kitty one, simply because the decals give it a lot of character and I don’t know if I’d really get a lot of extra enjoyment out of it.

Just a little guy, waiting for a scheme idea!

Marcy: You did great! They were a ton of fun to build, and I’m already itching for our next projects.

The Takoyaki Haro is an AMAZING idea. I almost wish I bought more of these guys to play with.

This shiny one with the plastic bag trick is something I hadn't thought of either, so I have to say I learned something; the Battle Damaged War Haro also looks really fun.

We hope you enjoyed this first edition of Hobby Build Academy! We’re hoping to help you embark on the enjoyment of building, painting, and customizing models, and we’ll be back soon with another edition of Hobby Build Academy soon.
