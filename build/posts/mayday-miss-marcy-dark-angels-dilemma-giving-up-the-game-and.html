Mayday, Miss Marcy! Dark Angels Dilemma, Giving Up the Game, and Pod Problems

August 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back once again to Mayday, Miss Marcy!, in which I take your reader submitted queries and provide them with the advice vested in me by being alive for a while. You'd think there'd be a better pedigree, but sometimes that's all it takes! If you've never read this column before, we take 3 questions sent in by readers from various places and try to come up with actual (and sometimes entertaining!) answers to them! If you'd like to know how to get your own questions submitted to the column, please read the instructions below!

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

ALSO, we're heading OUT of Summer, so if you have some Back to School, Fall, or similar turn of the seasons questions, let us hear it!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

Last week, Condit joined me to help out, and we had a great comment from Mintopo that I wanted to give a shout out to in regards to the question "The Girl Reading This"; I really agree with the comment and think its great advice! As always, I love getting your questions but also your comments. Now on to this week's questions!

Greg, Line 1, Greg,

Dear Miss Marcy,
As a young collector of Dark Angels i have been having trouble deciding what to add to my collection. I have the Marine half of Armageddon the new combat patrol and the lion as well as a few other kitbashes / cheap ebay finds. I'm not asking for a shopping list but just a way to decide what should be a priority and what I can wait to get.
From a Totally non traitorous Dark Angel
Dear Totally Non Traitorous,
I may be stepping into the domain of Greg here for a moment, but Dark Angels do rule severly hard, and I applaud your choice of Chapter. I assume you are maybe looking to potentially play in 30k and 40k, as you mention The Lion, so you might be on the right path for collecting your army by looking second-hand due to the differences in units between the 2 games. 40k is going to be a lot easier to shop for in general, since you'll be looking to field mostly Primaris Marines and Terminators, all of which are easily available from Games Workshop themselves and without a lot of extra work involved. 30k you might be best suited going the secondhand market, as the older scale models are going to be available in bulk. From there, you'll need to find heraldry and other options to help you do kitbashes, and making a full army is really just going to come down to settling on what units you want and then tracking them down. If I could make some suggestions, aside from heroic units like Lazarus, Belial, Asmodai, Azrael and an Interrogator Chaplain, you will probably also want to look into bikes!
If you want to provide us with more detailed questions, such as what type of game you'll be playing more often, we will happily provide you with answers from our vast array of Dark Angels specialists!
Edit: Thanks to GW's website being very, uh, good at being a website, it did not include The Lion in the 40k section under "Dark Angels" when I looked. The advice is still generally the same.

They Return

Dear Miss Marcy,
I am more of a tabletop hoarder than a gamer unfortunately. I have yet again lost interest in some games I worked on hobbying (building, magnetizing bases, priming, basing), and am not thinking about trading the projects in. This time however the issue is not one that has come up before. In the past it was due to me thinking the game was awesome, but no one played in my area. This time however it is because I really do not gel with a majority of the players in my area. The thought of playing with some of the folks (they could be described as "those guys") makes me not want to engage with the game anymore. Them being smaller games in my area they are going to be my main opponent. In your opinion what should I do? I already have decided to go with the most played games in my area because I do enjoy the hobby, games, and a huge bulk of the player base. Any advice is appreciated.
- The Insulin Dependent "Wargamer"
Dear Insulin Dependent,
Nice to hear from you again! For those who haven't been reading the column for a long time, Insulin is one of our longtime writers, and it's always a pleasure to hear from you. Sorry to hear that the smaller game you've found here is unfortunately mired in "Those Guys" sorts of players, because I absolutely have been there before. even longer time readers may know that my favorite miniature game of all time was Guild Ball, and when that game died I tried to find many other games to fit the same feeling, which never, sadly, materialized for me. Part of that is that the groups I tried to meet with and play with often were filled with "Those Guys" who tended to trade chudd-y jokes, present an unsportsmanlike and unfriendly atmosphere, and often just tended to repel me from the games I wanted to try playing.
The sad truth to this situation is that you should consider either shelving that game for now or offloading it if you need the space and clarity in your life. It sucks to say that your best option is to sell out of the game, but if the only people who are going to play it are people you would rather not socialize with or be associated with, then you are going to really struggle to ever actually play the game. It will also just sit there and remind you of the fact that you've had this problem, and make you unhappy looking at it; I won't name the games themselves on my end, but let's just say that I sold myself out of about 4 games for this very reason over a number of years, and while it made me sad that I never got to experience the games fully, I also realized I would have been miserable and not wanted to play anyway.

Pod Bound

Dear Marcy,

I've been playing with a Magic Pod for a while now, and while it seemed like the dream, it has sort of become very stale. Our Pod does not really like to invest in change in new cards, decks, or anything, and after a while it has become difficult to constantly play against the same decks over and over again. The worse problem is that I do actually like to invest and try new things, which has also gotten some annoying comments from my pod about "upsetting" the balance of things.

I love playing Magic and my Pod is my primary way of doing so, and without them I very likely won't have much option or ability to play the game at all. That being said, it feels like my interest in the game is being drawn away from me anyway, because I would rather just not play than face the same exact decks every week. Considering the decks are 100 cards, it feels like I know what every player is going to play by heart now.
Unenchanted Magic

Dear Unenchanted,

Ah, Magic Pods. They are actually a lifeline to a lot of players, because they provide stability and often a very consistent schedule of players who do not always have the time or interest in more competitive formats like Standard or Modern at the competitive level. They come with the caveat/problem that you've mentioned, though, which is that they can get stale VERY fast if the players are committed to their "routine". I think the best way you can perhaps approach this if you feel like you'd like them to change is try to sell them on some form of group investment or concepts that can help shake things up. I think there's about a 50/50 chance that this doesn't work, though, because sometimes groups of people just want to get together and play the same game over and over again not because of the challenge or the struggle, but mostly because of the socialization and regularity aspect of it.

If your group declines and you're feeling that frustrated, though, I think there is some self-defeat built in to "I won't get to play". You already said that you are not having fun playing, and almost would prefer not to play if you're just doing the same thing over and over again, so what really changes if you stopped going? You also could absolutely find a new pod I am pretty sure, but it would just require putting yourself out there. And that can be just as challenging or difficult as other parts, but do consider that you're opting to sit around doing something you don't enjoy, OR finding a new group or way to spend your free time. Unless this Pod is consisting of your closest friends and loved ones, separating yourself from them might hurt at first, but I think it will be better than being miserable, which you clearly are.
