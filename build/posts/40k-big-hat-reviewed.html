[40k] Big Hat, Reviewed

November 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The hat, she is big. Specifically, I am posting from within the 1:1 scale Mark X helmet (in Dark Angels colors, necessarily) from Joy Toy. I paid my own money for it, and got the one with the stand and the included t-shirt.

The author, pictured. Credit: Greg Chiasson

As an aside, it is not lost on me that the only two articles I’ve written since my son was born have been about 1. sitting in a comfy chair and 2. wearing a dorky hat. I realize how this looks, but I don't generally buy things. I do, obviously, but only when forced. I cycle through two water bottles, and both have my company's logo on them. My main hoodie is also something I got for free. I haven't spent my own money on a computer since 2014, and only last year got a new phone, getting rid of an iPhone that was fully a decade old. So the fact that I've been instantly and furiously slamming the buy button on any Warhammer merch with a Dark Angel on it represents either intense personal growth or whatever the opposite of growth is.

Take a quick peek at this photo, and think about how your head would fit in there. Wrong, it’s bigger. Roomier. Cavernous. This helmet is enormous on a replacement-size human, but comically tiny on an Intercessor model, which really drives home how massive a Space Marine must be. This information may not rise to the level of useful, but it is at a minimum illustrative.

The big hat existed already, in an Ultramarines version. Many of us have purchased it, talked about it, and perhaps even reviewed it. If we did the latter, I haven’t read it, and you shouldn’t either. I will explain why: the Ultras version could not have been better than a 4 out of 5, because this is a 5 out of 5 and if painted as any other chapter it could - objectively - not be rated as highly. Equally, it could not have been worse than a 4 out of 5, because it is, at the end of the day, still a big goofy space helmet you can wear around your home, and categorically that cannot be a bad thing. So that review, if it exists, is not required reading; it either ends the way I already told you it does and your curiosity is now sated, or it doesn’t and is thus so wrongheaded in its conclusion that you can skip it.

I mentioned that the helmet comes with a stand and a Dark Angels shirt. The shirt absolutely whips; great design, is appropriately Dark Angelic, and better quality material than I’d expect, given the source.

The stand looks like nothing so much as an oversized piece of Warhammer terrain, which I mean as a compliment. The Dark Angels logo on the front is a separate piece, which makes sense if they wanted to use the same stand design for other, lesser, chapters.

It does feel like the Warhammer merch people are making up bullshit directly for me, and to them I would say: keep it up, I have poor impulse control. Lest you think that’s a joke, I want to point out that when the Dark Angels chapter banner went up for pre-order, there was a gap of less than two minutes between Rob sending me the link and me replying with a screenshot of my order confirmation. I know what I’m about here.

Much like that banner, I still don’t know where to put this gigantic thing. For now, it lives on top of my printer, and so every time I want to print anything I have to move it out of the way or it causes a paper jam. Admittedly that problem is specific to me, but otherwise: what is it like living with big hat? How practical of a wardrobe or lifestyle choice is it? In terms of the five main senses - I don’t have any proven psychic or psionic abilities, but I assume the helmet wouldn’t interfere with those, and it’s plastic so something like an electric Eel, or a direction-finding organ attuned to the earth’s magnetic field, should be fine - the results are surprisingly positive.

Sight: I watched some of Dune Part 2. The tinted eye lenses turn everything red, which is a problem generally speaking but notably makes the Giedi Prime gladiator sequence look insanely cool. This was all aided by the fact that it’s roomy enough in there that I could keep my glasses on the entire time. That said, the big hat does have a limited field of view, so if/when I go to an IMAX showing of Dune Part 3, I will probably not wear this.

Insanely cool to watch Dune through a Dark Angels hat.

Sound: Phoebe Bridgers’ Lost Weekend is a towering achievement, an absolutely titanic record. I don’t want to be reductive about this, because it’s a very good album that can’t be filed away or derided as sad music about boys - it’s weird and incisive and will probably rewire my brain the same way that Punisher did - but some portion of it is about her relationship with Bo Burnham, and a lot of the rest is about her relationship with her (dead? I should look that up) father. Now, as also the father of a daughter, if my kid grows up and writes an album this good, but where she talks about me like that, I would be very proud of her and also probably want to fake my own death to get away from it.

Anyway I can’t hear things very well through the helmet. The muffling effect is pronounced, and when music isn’t playing, it makes my tinnitus go hog wild.

Touch: You cannot be touched while wearing this, which is bad. However, that is what helmets are for, which is good. So this test fails, but fails by design, and so paradoxically it passes.

For the helmet’s part, where it touches the top of your head there’s an adjustable doodad that keeps it steady and comfortable. No complaints, really.

Taste: There is enough room to sneak a hand up in there and eat a small snack without taking the big hat off your head, but something like a pulled pork or even brisket sandwich would not fit. A popsicle or corn dog shape would probably work. Nothing bigger than that though.

Smell: There’s no nose hole up front, but the neck hole on the bottom is gigantic, so you can position your behelmed head above the Dark Angels scented candle and still get a whiff of whatever they were trying to do there.

I love the big hat, for some of the same reasons that I love the chair. No, this one isn’t nearly as useful, but it still amuses me that it exists, and I enjoy having it in my presence. I am going to go on claiming to be god’s only creation that is immune to propaganda, but I think we can look at the fact that I spent two hundred dollars on something to take up space I don’t have just because it was the right shade of green, and know on some level that I am lying. The trade-off for chaining myself to samsara a bit more tightly is that I can sit in my Dark Angels chair, wearing my Dark Angels shirt and helmet, looking at my Dark Angels art prints and painted models and (soon enough) anime wall-scroll banner, and feel incredibly, unspeakably, Normal. The feeling won’t last, but as long as they keep putting out new Dark Angels merch to distract me from reality I can skip from purchase to purchase like a stone across a pond and never have to confront anything. The stupider the better.
