[40k] Competitive Innovations Editorial: How is 11th Edition Going?

November 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Thanks to the relative drought of events last weekend, I’m in the unusual position of having time to write about something that isn’t just army lists. Glorious. This provides a perfect opportunity to put together an editorial I’ve been meaning to tackle at some point prior to the WCW, so here we go.

11th Edition - how’s it going? The new edition of 40K has had four months to bed in, and we’ve all got plenty of games under our belts, enough to start having some opinions about the changes and trends in the new edition. Inevitably, some of these are very positive, and some less so. In general, I’ve been having a really good time with 11th, and it’s clear there’s plenty of enthusiasm for it around the world, but some elements make me a little nervous about its likely trajectory over the next year or so. That’s plenty of fodder for comparative list article, the classic content creator gambit when there’s a schedule to meet, so let’s go. I've also pulled in several of my esteemed colleagues to help, specifically TheChirurgeon, Corrode, and of course Lowest of Men.

Obviously - my 40K focus is on the competitive game, so will be primarily focusing on things that impact tournament play. I do broadly think that 11th so far has an additional weakness that there should be more narrative focused products, but it’s outside my wheelhouse.

TheChirurgeon: It’s in my wheelhouse, though and I can tell you: It sucks!

If you're looking for your regular Competitive Innovations, Wednesday's column is here.

The Good

Let's start things off on a positive note - what do we like about the current edition? What's working right now?

Terrain and Objective Rules

Sneaky Raven Guard Aggressors. Credit: Dan Boyd

Terrain objectives is arguably the biggest change to the fundamentals of 40K that 11th Edition made - and they rock.

I’ll put my hands up and say I was nervous about this one, particularly in combination with the significant reduction of the physical size of standard terrain pieces, but at this point this is firmly in the category of things that just feel like a natural part of the game now. The terrain rules can make the game more lethal, but the relatively dense sets of pieces on maps, the Hidden mechanic, and the broadened impact of Cover means that it really works. Fighting over terrain pieces creates interesting dynamics (especially with the changes to Heroic Intervention, another positive), and also looks great on the table, creating an experience that works for both competitive and narrative play. I think this is a bit less true on Dawn or War style maps, where the game can be a bit too tight, but other deployments work well.

Success on this front is a relief, because a driving source of my nervousness here was that, because the terrain was tied to physical products (both areas and the terrain itself), if it was bad we were going to be stuck with it. It’s good, so it’s fine that we’re locked in! I’d maybe hope there’s some scope to add a few extra pieces to shake the game up half way into the Edition, but if we stick with what we have for three years that would honestly be fine, especially as many TOs (including us) have now invested in it. Increasing the standardisation of the tournament experience is a major upside too, and it also makes actually running events easier than ever for organisers - you put the right pieces on the table, the players take it away, sorted.

The last thing to call out here is that the use of map changes as part of the rules updates has been really cool, and having that extra lever on missions (or just to change the experience) is great. This is where I’d like to see things go a bit further - a very limited number of missions now have different sizes of central objectives on their different layouts, and others swap the triangle pieces to be home objectives, giving more large blocks to work with in the mid-table. Do more with that!

Lowest of Men: Games look and feel more immersive than they ever have in my time playing. Fighting for control of ruins and power generators feels a thousand times more interesting than unspecified disks of dubious origins. Hidden is an edition defining mechanic and opens up a wealth of tactical plays, and it has allowed Terminators to (finally) shine in a wide range of armies. Positioning needs to be very good in this version of the game- very rarely is a closed off ruin wall going to bail you out. Generally, a massive success.

Liam: I am mostly positive on this change, with the one caveat that I think it contributes to the edition feeling “more lethal” - not so much because stuff hits any harder, but because there are fewer circumstances where a unit is truly safe while holding an objective in the way that they could be in 10th. It’s not necessarily a problem and Hidden, especially with the addition of Gone to Ground, does help, but it is definitely a contributor.

TheChirurgeon: I think terrain as objectives is great for making sure that games don't feel like you're fighting over a bunch of round billboards, but ultimately I think it makes it much more lethal as objectives are now just kind of always in the open. I think it's also an area where I'd have liked to see more variety or a mix - it feels like a decision made to sell me terrain footprints in some ways and tethering objectives to terrain areas feels like the kind of thing that you can and should do most of the time but not all of the time, especially if you want to have non-terrain objectives in narrative play.

List Variety

Bloodletters of Khorne. Credit: Corrode

10th Edition had a big problem with a lack of list diversity. Inevitable imbalances in power between Detachments tended to bring some clear best ones to the fore, and because you only had one detachment, you’d generally see fairly similar selections within armies.

The revamp of detachments at the start of 11th blew this wide open, particularly in combination with the mission pack. Allowing players to bolt a 1DP add-on onto the second tier of detachments in Codexes opened up a massive number of viable strategies, which players have explored with gusto. Early on there were some issues with 3DPs being hit or miss solely based on their disposition, but that’s now been resolved and there are plenty of factions where there’s a genuinely viable selection between a 3DP detachment or various combo setups. Even when players run the same detachments, there’s sometimes viable ways to play multiple Dispositions, creating variety at every level, and access to Upgrades adds another way for players to tinker and tune.

This has made 11th builds super enjoyable to write about, and opening up creativity is hugely positive for player engagement. This is obviously a good thing. I do have to caveat this one, however, that players primarily choosing between 3DP or 2+1DP combinations felt a lot stronger than the all-1DP setups that have been pushed by the Codexes we’ve received so far (mitigated somewhat for Marines by the Chapter detachments). We’ll come back to that later on, but I do slightly worry that this will ebb away as more books are released.

Lowest of Men: Detachment design is really good and has brought back list design tinkering and excitement. Codices simply ARE far stronger in this regard than anyone without a book full of 1DPs as they have more scope to flex and adjust than anyone else - it is almost impossible to consistently tech for the books with a wide roster (Marines and Orks) as their ability to pivot on both disposition and detachments is unrivalled. Let’s see if that pattern continues!

TheChirurgeon: This is an area where I think we have good list diversity but I'm not sure there's that much more than we had in 10th. The top Detachments/builds still float to the top, and I think you get a lot more interesting choices in teams than you had in tenth thanks to the Disposition system. But there are a dozen factors which contribute to list diversity by faction so it's hard for me to judge this holistically. On the whole I think it does feel like most armies have multiple options.

Teams Events

The current 11th Edition mission pack and Team Event Companion create a fantastic teams format, and I’d argue that this is where 40K currently shines the brightest. The Disposition system and the way map choice has been implemented adds some great layers of strategy, and the breadth of list-building options really comes into its own here. I finally got sucked into team events in 10th, having been defiantly singles-only prior to that, and while singles will always be my first love, you owe it to yourself to give teams warhammer a try in 11th, it’s really great.

Top tip too - if you want to try it out, but don’t have a team ready to go, keep an eye out for local team tournaments, and make it known to the organisers that you’d be available as a substitute. It’s very common for some sort of real-life emergency to force a player to drop out of a team, and having a pool of substitutes ready and able to fill the gaps is a huge boon to organisers, and ensures that the most fun is had by the most people.

Lowest of Men: I will add a single caveat here, though I have been loving 11th in teams. Dispositions on top of army choices really hurts certain armies and can make assembling a comp quite stressful. Disruption and Recon especially are often poison chalices and trying to cram certain armies into them can make teams in the harder roles quite stressful and labour intensive. Top players are absolutely loving it though, and cooking up all sorts of interesting things.

Wings: I think I'd say that's part of the fun, for me - restrictions breed creativity, and we see some wonderfully out-of-the-box angles on these such as the Intercessor-heavy Ultramarine Recon list from Talavera we saw on Wednesday.

TheChirurgeon: I think Dispositions on top of army choices works wonderfully in 5mans and is clunky in 8s, where the doubling up is usually a no-brainer.

Honourable Mention - Rules Streamlining

This doesn’t need a deep dive, but some of the changes that looked very strong when we reviewed the edition? Yeah, they’re great in practice. Charging combat movement, wound allocation, changes to the timing on some things, generally working very nicely, with only a few complaints around the rules hygiene on combat movement standing out as a downside here. 11th’s core engine is really good, and the new books coming out within it have generally been very clean rules-wise.

The Bad

With the good out of the way it's time to stop praising Caesar and talk about some of his more ambitious - and thus less revered - traits.

Mission Pack Issues

Credit: PierreTheMime

So I have to preface this by saying that the design of the first mission pack for 11th Edition was very ambitious, and what the studio has accomplished is at minimum a decent delivery against its goals (rising to good for Teams, as above). Asymmetric scoring delivers interesting strategic challenges for four of the five Dispositions, and players being able to tune their list to their objectives improves list-building options.

With four months of experience though, it’s pretty clear that there are some issues with the pack, which I’d broadly put in three buckets:

  1. Disruption is bad.
  2. Scoring is too easy (especially on Secondaries).
  3. Games can be repetitive.

The first one is the elephant in the room. One of the five Dispositions is not like the others. Disruption is bad, attempts to fix it with map changes have not worked, and the solution at this point appears to be to simply avoid forcing anyone to play it, and pretend it’s not there. After the change to 3DP detachments to have multiple Disposition choices, top players simply do not pick Disruption unless they’re at a team event or trying to stunt on people. I have a fun anecdote on this one - the processing script I use as part of preparing Competitive Innovations every week spits out a breakdown of Disposition usage among lists that were X-1 or better, and one week (across a total of ~160 lists) there were more players that had forgotten to set their Disposition in BCP than had picked Disruption.

Is that good?

This is, obviously, not ideal, and it exacerbates other issues with the mission pack. One Disposition functionally not being in the mix reduces the number of scenarios players need to plan for, and also uses up a whole bunch of design space that never actually appears on the table, making games more repetitive. I don’t really have a good suggestion here other than either a ground-up rewrite of Disruption, or just officially killing it and using its design space to broaden the others.

Next up, scoring. It’s too easy. Tense, high-scoring games are one thing, but 100-98VP scorelines being a relatively common sight in 11th is probably not healthy. Both Primary and Secondary scoring patterns contribute to this, but Secondaries feel pretty clearly like the bigger issue. Now that you can hold cards in your hand as long as you like, and you can blow through scoring (usually) three cards in any given turn, a maximum 45VP score becomes a likely outcome of any game where you’re participating meaningfully throughout. You can score no Secondaries until turn 3, and still max out your points. You probably shouldn’t be able to do that! Secondaries also tend to ask less of you - I like some changes like Forward Position having multiple modes, but too many 5VP cards are things you can just make happen incidentally now.

This ease of scoring frees players up to focus on their Primary, which results in very linear plans towards forcing high scores. A lot of matchups give one or more players a “safe” ladder to somewhere between 35-45VP that can be achieved while playing cautiously, and if they can rely on hitting 45VP on Secondaries, that sets a very high score floor. There is some strategic nuance from this - a key skill in high-level play is tracking what your opponent’s probable minimum primary score is from where you’re standing, and whether there is anything you can do to set a hard cap on their points that you can then plan around - do you come out ahead if both players keep doing what they’re doing, or do you need to try and smash them off the table to try and avert disaster? I quite enjoy this kind of dynamic, but at the moment it turns too much on hair-thin margins of points, except for a few missions (primarily the ones with some sort of snowballing points mechanic like Triangulation) that go too far the other way where an early disaster can put such a low ceiling on your score that it’s almost impossible to compete.

I do think some of the primaries lean unhealthy in this regard, but it would matter less if 45VP on Secondaries wasn’t on lock, or even if you had to work a bit more to achieve that (and also, if we had a fifth set of primaries to work with).

Finally, and somewhat downstream of both of these, the missions can be repetitive. I’ve described this mission pack to some colleagues as one that’s fantastic for three months, OK for six, and a disaster for anything more than that, and I’m not yet convinced I’m wrong on that. There’s a tonne going on with the pack, but the design space is spread very thinly, and as a player you’ll only interact with very limited amounts of it with any given list. I’ve already had the experience of playing the same matchup on the same map within the same event multiple times, and it just isn’t a good feeling. That’s only going to get worse the more events I play with the pack.

What do I want to see here? Hopefully some bigger changes to the mission pack come the first normal quarterly update at Christmas. At minimum, something needs to happen on Secondaries, likely a cap on how many cards you can hold, but I’m open to more ambitious options. I think it’s less likely we see big Primary changes, but I’d point to what I mentioned above about getting bolder with map changes - significantly changing the way maps for some missions play would freshen up the experience without having to sully the sanctity of the printed physical product. Disruption probably just has to stay dead until a full mission pack refresh, though I guess you could just throw up your hands and give them one free CP at the start of the game or something.

Credit: Kevin Stillman

Liam: Early on there were a few disposition changes where a detachment that was too good would be made to play Disruption instead of whatever it had before. I feel like if you’re viewing one of your five mission sets as a punishment beating, that suggests you ought to be rethinking its inclusion at all. Now, of course, the advent of triple 1DPs has sidestepped that anyway.

Rob may just jump in to say this himself, but the other thing that is quite weird about this pack is that outside of Priority Assets and some of Recon there’s only two things that require you to ever do an action, and they’re mutually exclusive if you want them to be. Now that actions are a formal part of the rules again after the aberration of 10th edition, there’s quite a lot of stuff out there which has gained rules that interact with it - Ork Boyz in Taktikal Brigade can do an action in any conceivable situation, including while in combat - and most of the time you don’t care at all because there aren’t any actions to do.

Bring one deep striker that can pop up and do a Plunder at some convenient point (preferably one that has uppy-downy, so that you can hold it until late game if needed), maybe have a chaff unit that hits half of Cleanse while locking down your 4VP - hold one objective primary, and you’re good to go. Or just table the guy and worry about scoring afterwards. I will continue to bang the drum for “at the end of your Command phase you should be required to discard down to 3 cards max.”

TheChirurgeon: I was going to say that, before you stole my thunder. But yeah, actions just don't exist in 40k now unless you want them to, and I think that's both bad for internal balance - it makes it harder to create compelling "action doer" units - and external balance, as actions tend to strongly benefit horde armies and armies which can afford lots of cheap units with good OC to throw at them. Tenth edition had to solve action economy problems by allowing knights to shoot and still perform an action, and that kind of dynamic is what I want to see in the game, generally - there need to be more ways for units to interact with each other and the game outside of just killing, or the game turns into who can table the other faster. One of the unheralded reasons 11th feels more deadly is that you just don't have to invest in action units now.

I'm really not a fan of the missions. I think they don't actively deliver on the promise of asymmetrical primaries - there are too many times I feel like the game is still hold one/hold more/kill one or hold one/hold more/action when it boils down to it, and having had to play the Take and Hold mirror three straight rounds on day 1 at the Lone Star Open was just brutally unfun. They also feel massively under-served in narrative play, where the disposition system more or less locks you into playing competitive play missions and needs a lot of additional work (custom dispositions, new missions) to accommodate anything else. I'm also not sold on the notion that the new system feels any more narrative than the old one - Dispositions don't actually feel tied to my unit selections, in part because they've started to use them as balancing levers, using good dispositions to prop up worse detachments, and vice versa.

And that's kind of the rub, right? You expect Dispositions and Detachments to be closely tied - if my Detachment is Disruption, why don't the rules in it favor or interact with the mission at all? And the answer is they can't, because you can change the Disposition at any time for balancing purposes, and the missions pack may change every year, so you don't want to tie the Detachment rules to the Disposition or mission rules. But that just gives the game away - these two things have nothing to do with each other and never did. The World Eaters have a Disruption Detachment because they have to have one of each, and so they just kind of pick the one that vaguely fits that designation the best.

1DP Detachments as the Norm

Flash Gitz. Credit: 40khamslam.

I talked a lot in the Goods section about how much I like the dynamics of 2+1DP list-building. I am colder on the 3x1DP paradigm that the new Codexes have used thus far, and am not thrilled at this apparent direction of travel. I like some of what it enables - I’m a toolbox list enjoyer, of course I do - but when you get to put three detachments in most lists, it’s way too easy for some to become way too prevalent. We’ve seen that already with Assault Brethren and Wreckas (arguably Blitz Brigade too) - the best detachments will feature in a massive proportion of armies. This has a nasty feedback loop with internal unit balance too - if a detachment happens to synergise with a unit that’s too cheap, then great news - it’s in every list now, and they still get to do whatever their plan A was alongside it.

There are some hopeful signs that maybe this is teething issues rather than something we’re going to be dealing with all edition. Part of the reason Custodes is my favourite of the Codexes so far is that it does a better job of accounting for these risks via the broad use of the Shield Host unique tag, and very tightly scoped detachments outside that. The Marines book is the opposite - it has the three very broad Brethren detachments and plenty of others that can scatter buffs on significant chunks of your army, or overlap and compound. I’m a much bigger fan of the 2DP chapter detachments, which are seeing a healthy amount of play, and I hope we’ll see more use of 2DP options and more liberal applications of Unique tags as the edition progresses.

Liam: I think the other thing with Custodes is that even in a 3x1DP framework, you can figure out an identity for the army. This is an Emperor’s Chosen army, or a Shadowkeepers one, and then it has some other stuff bolted on. What is the identity of any Ork army this edition? Are they playing Goffs or Evil Sunz or Bad Moons or is it just “whatever three detachments are best?” Sometimes you can point to something and kind of figure out a ‘main’ detachment, but there are times when I’ve found myself figuring out a list and deciding to take a 1DP to benefit literally one unit. It’s a curious position when an arguable strength of 10th edition detachments was that they could really encourage armies towards a narratively-suitable theme without having to add loads of restrictions to force them into a box.

TheChirurgeon: I straight up do not care for the Detachment and DP system. I think 1 DP detachments feel flimsy and remove a key part of the subfaction flavor you get from 3 DP or even 2 DP Detachments, while turning your construction into more of a min/max decision. In an ideal scenario, I'd prefer if armies were purely built as 2 DP + 1 DP structures, where your 2 DP Detachment is your subfaction flavor and the 1 DP is an add-on for personal taste or to showcase a particular unit or strategy. 3 DP detachments feel like they're underpowered compared to most of the 2+1 options and the 1+1+1 armies feel like they're built on a pile of buffed units and nothing else. I'm not a Warp Talons player, I'm a Night Lords player - I want rules which give me the feeling of playing Night Lords on the table and make something like Disruption a compelling option.

And generally the 1 DP Detachments just do a piss-poor job of feeling flavorful or fluffy. "Tacticus armor" as a theme is maybe the lamest thing you could build around.

Rules QA Issues

Khorne Berzerkers. Credit: Corrode

11th’s launch was marred by the significant number of 10th Edition rules where necessary wording updates had just been missed or made incorrectly (and there are still a few outstanding), and the switch to only updating core rules and new codexes via the App rather than FAQ documents or Faction Packs has had some teething issues, especially around lack of clarity. It’s clear that GW are aware of this - WarCom announced that there will be a changelog in future, and apologised for the sporadic October rollout, so we’re firmly on “wait and see” for this now, but fundamentally if the app is going to be the source of truth for the game rather than simply an alternative to Faction Pack, it really needs to be perfect, or close-to.

To hit that might require a mindset shift in how testing works - you ideally need to be rolling these out as beta releases to a pretty sizeable community to crowdsource the validation, but that creates definite challenges with managing information flow. I wonder if this is something the approved TO program could be leveraged for? The needs feels pretty urgent - even this week, the new FAQ for the WCW has created a few uncertainties while trying to fix others, and most of these have been identified by the community pretty swiftly - there’s really no substitute for having lots of people taking a crack at rules. I’d also say on event-driven rulings in particular that there’s a mild trend towards preserving some of the big brain things you could do with edge cases in 10th, even when they now contradict the tidied-up language of the 11th Edition rules (Surge moves are my giant bugbear here).

Liam: I generally try to be forgiving of the design team because they are always spinning far more plates than anyone ever believes, and get quite a lot of shit in return for producing rules that I think are light years ahead of where we were in then-revolutionary 8th edition. That said, there are times when there are rules issues that I think we could fix in an afternoon session on the TTB Discord that persist for round after round of balance slates, and those can be very frustrating. GW have also burned off a lot of credit in the bank in this particular area with some of the really bone-headed decisions in 11th - the random smattering of things that are app-only, the stealth updates, data errors like “Upgarde” or breaking all of the default weapon options for a bunch of Chaos datasheets when they updated boltguns and the like. Details matter and getting lots of small things wrong at once can annoy people more than one big error.

TheChirurgeon: From the jump I was annoyed that they were putting rules in the app which should have been in the rulebook. I want and like digital rules, but I also want a book I can just sit down and read start to finish to learn the game, and the app is horrible for that. The search feature continues to be a nightmare if not actively crashing and it just serves to highlight that the technology aspect of Games Workshop's offering is not up to the promise of what they want to do with digital rules. Moving to more digital rules is the correct play, but it feels like their entire tech infrastructure is just woefully inadequate when it comes to actually delivering.

Wrap Up

So, those are our thoughts on where things are, and I think it’s important to stress again - 11th is in a vastly better place today than 10th was at the equivalent point, and there’s lots to like, but hopefully us kicking around what we’re concerned about provides some food for thought.
