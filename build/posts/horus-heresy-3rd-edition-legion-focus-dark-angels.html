Horus Heresy 3rd Edition Legion Focus: Dark Angels

July 20th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The Dark Angels are the ur-Legion, the prototype for all who followed. Veterans of the Unification Wars long before their brother Legions had even been sired, they pioneered the tactics of Legion warfare that would go on to form the Principia Belicosa. Renowned for their peerless adaptability and mastery of all spheres of conflict, they served as the Imperium’s ultimate eradication force, wielding proscribed, ancient technologies to purge the galaxy’s most horrific threats. 

This era of grim fatalism was finally tempered by their reunion with Lion El’Jonson. Their Primarch reshaped the Legion’s myriad Hosts into the renowned Hexagrammaton, each of its Wings epitomising a unique type of warfare. His leadership and the influx of new blood from Caliban infused the Legion with a newfound spirit of martial honour, melding their knightly traditions with the grim, original purpose of the 1st Legion. Though not all of his ‘little brothers’ saw these changes with the same pragmatism as the Lion, and within the secretive inner circles of the Legion a canker grew. This is the legacy they would carry through the fires of the Heresy, the Scouring and into the 41st Millennium.

Dark Angels Veteran Tactical Squad. Credit - Max

Why Play Dark Angels?

Do you want to play knightly wardens, guarding the fledging Imperium from its most dire threats using proscribed weaponry? Do you like big swords and plasma flamers? Do you want an excuse to field literally any combination of units and still be lore accurate? And most importantly, do you like painting black? If you answered yes to all the above then Dark Angels are the army for you. And let me just say it’s a slippery slope. 

When approaching an Legion in Horus Heresy it’s good to play with tropes. Iron Warriors Siege Forces, Blood Angels Day of Revelations Jump Pack madness, etc. For the First Legion there’s too much. Their centuries of fighting in the Great Crusade and their deep and esoteric lore provide carte blanche to field almost any archetype you can imagine. Out of the box you’ve got six unique playstyles built with the Hexagrammaton; from the knightly assault of the Deathwing to the war-crime-laden Dreadwing. And that’s before you get into any of the pre-Lion Hosts. The flexibility is honestly incredible. Plus thanks to this edition’s list-building changes, creating a mono-wing or multi-wing force is easier than ever with some of the best Legion specific detachments in the game at your finger tips.

But with this freedom can come analysis paralysis. What exactly does a Dark Angels force look like and what is even good in their list? To figure that out, it’s best to go back to basics and have a look at what everything actually does. Without further ado, let’s dive in.

Legion Special Rules

Legion Tactica - The Angels of Death

Your Leadership can never be modified to be less than 6 and you count all sources of Fear (x) as Fear (1).

Having faced the worst the Galaxy has seen, the Dark Angels are even more stolid than your average Legion force. Now, on paper this might seem a little niche, but it has some pretty powerful implications. 

The vast majority of the time this rule will trigger is during Combat Resolution. Thanks to the FAQ clarifying that Combat Res Panic Checks uses the difference in combat resolution as a Characteristic modifier as opposed to a Target number modifier (god this game is overwritten sometimes), the scions of the 1st can use their Legion Tactica to cap all their leadership tests at 6. This basically means that no matter how bad you lose combat you’ve always got 42% chance of picking your best option, whether that’s Disengaging to potentially open up your opponent’s Deathstar to ranged threats or Holding to keep them locked in pointless combat for a whole additional turn.

Secondly, the Ld 6 cap effectively reduces the efficacy of Panic (3) weapons. Whilst most regular Legion units boast a respectable Ld 8, a Panic (3) check can still pretty reliably apply the Routed condition, disrupting your battleline and preventing scoring. But for the First? Rolling at Ld 6 instead of Ld 5 makes Phosphex slightly less terrifying, especially when your opponent starts trying to stack Fear (x) modifiers on top. Speaking of which:

Dark Angels also downgrade all Fear (2) sources to Fear (1). Whilst these will only appear once in a blue moon, you’ll be glad you had it when they do, because these are some of the scariest threats out there:

  • Transfigured Demon Primarchs (Fulgrim and Angron)
  • Conrad Kurse
  • Sevatar
  • Archmagos Scoria
  • Dolorous Knights 
  • Kytan Demon Engines 
  • Ka’Bandha

If you see any of these on the battlefield, Rejoice! You can finally use the second part of your Legion Tactica.

Horus Heresy Second Sphere Defense Credit: Soggy

Sword of the Order Special Rule

Even prior to their reunion with the Lion and knightly aesthetics of Caliban, the First had a penchant for long blades. To represent this, a lot of their next set of rules synergise with models armed with ‘Swords of the Order’ which is basically any weapon with sword in the name; Paragon Blades, the humble chainsword, and all of your Legion specific swords (more on them later).

Advanced Reaction - Vengeance of the First Legion

Once per battle at the end of any fight on your opponent’s turn (it’s a reaction after all), ignore the combat resolution step and begin a whole new fight phase. Also gain Shred (6+) if they’re wielding a Swords of the Order

An interesting little reaction that has a decent amount of tactical play and has two broad uses. Arguably it’s intended use is immediately swinging again into a tarpit unit that’s charged you, freeing up your deathstar to keep up the pressure on next turn (the extra Shred isn’t too bad either). Inversely I think the best use might actually be forcing a unit that’s barely hanging in there to heroically sacrifice themselves fighting to the death leaving your enemies exposed and vulnerable to your ranged threats at the start of your next turn. 

A scion of the night haunter demonstrates his "Nostraman Courage" against a Dark Angels Praetor. Credit: Soggy

Gambit - Sword of the Order

Representing their mastery with the blade, in a Challenge if your Challenger is armed with a Sword of the Order, they can lose an attack to gain Crit Hit (6+) (or +1 Crit Hit if it’s already got it). Initially I thought this might have been a case of the Design team overvaluing Crit Hits, as losing an attack tends to generally be worse. But once you do the maths, it’s actually pretty decent.

Statistically it maps out like this:

Average Damage Output in Wounds vs equal WS Foe w/ 2+/4+
Normal      w/Sword of the Order      Damage Increase
Praetor w/Paragon Blade   1.39 1.56 12%
Praetor w/Terranic Greatsword   1.25 1.67 33%
Centurion w/Terranic Greatsword   1.22 1.61 32%

 

This Gambit pairs beautifully with Terranic Greatswords, giving them a fairly decent bump in damage output (autowounding on 6s to hit with AP2, D3 can be challenge winning). It also synergises a little bit better with Terranic Greatswords due to other Gambits like Flurry capping your Damage (not great for D2 weapons) and Finishing Blow (which whilst very strong, negates your initiative advantage vs the ubiquitous Thunder Hammer). Definitely one to consider but not necessarily an auto-pick every time.

Legion Detachments

Famed for its wings of the Hexagrammaton, the Dark Angels could quickly and easily deploy specialists across a variety of tactics into the battle. As a Dark Angels player you’re lucky enough to have six unique Auxiliary Detachments representing these. With five of these having inbuilt primes (thus being straight upgrades of the standard Aux Detachments), list building with Dark Angels gives you absurd flexibility (effectively it’s list building on easy mode). Sprinkling Wings throughout your list also feels slightly more true to the lore of how the Dark Angels operate than the Wing specific builds of last edition (though I’ll never not be salty about losing the Eskaton Imperative RoW).

Ironwing Gauntlet

Two Armour, Two Heavy Transport - no primes

An ideal Detachment for an armoured assault force with 1 or 2 Land Raiders and a couple of tanks. And given that most players are only half filling an Armoured Support detachment, it’s giving you two for one value on Detachments, leaving your prime slots on your elite troops open for more fun stuff (rather than Logistical Prime Benefiting in a Transport).

Dreadwing Cadre

Three Support slots - one prime - locked to Interemptors and Rapiers

This is a great little thematic Detachment bringing you easy access to some of the strongest units in the Support slot. Both Rapiers and Interemptors are fun and good and can do a variety of things for your list. The Prime slot is the cherry on top allowing you to easily Logistical Prime Benefit in Dreadnoughts or Apex locked slots easily.

Stormwing Muster

Two Troops (both prime), two transport

I think this one’s okay. Two Prime slots are really solid, but Troops and Transports generally aren’t something you’ll find yourself needing more of. That said this is a really useful Detachment to use to field three 20-man die-hard Line squads with either Combat Veterans or True Believers (for you Fallen fans out there).

Deathwing Conclave

Retinue (Prime), Elite, Heavy Assault

Representing the speartip insertions of the Deathwing, this is really asking you to bring a load of Terminators and Elite combat units. This is a fantastic Detachment giving you easy access to Elite and Retinue slots without needing an Apex (though why not take both for the chance to run two Master Sergeants in your most dangerous units). A very easy pick if you’re planning on running Deathwing Companions and Inner Circle Knights Cenobium (which let’s face it, you probably will be).

Ravenwing Lance

Two Fast Attack (one prime), Two Recon (locked to Outriders)

Effectively just a better First Strike Detachment because of its Prime. I imagine this will see even more play if Outrider bikes ever get a plastic kit.

Firewing Echelon

Two Recon (one Prime), Two Elites (locked to Seekers)

Not only is this just a better Combat Pioneer Detachment (again, it’s got a Prime), this gives you non-Apex access to Seekers (though I’d recommend running them in squads of five, unless you’re absolutely sure you’re not playing to make friends).

The Dark Angels Ironwing rolls out. Credit: Soggy

Armoury of the Dark Angels

Calabanite Warblade

Got a Power Sword? For +5pts you can upgrade it to a at-initiative Power Axe with Sword of the Order. That’s not bad! Frustratingly it’s still locked to Sergeants and up, where it’s competing with Thunder Hammers or Power Fists in the same points bracket. You can also give it to Command and Champions, but for an extra 10 points you could also be giving them a…

Terranic Greatsword

The premier casino-sword in the Dark Angels Armoury, the Terranic Greatsword has a storied tradition of being annoying to fight and fight with. As the anti-elite weapon in your list, it’s great… about a third of the time, relying on Breaching (5+) to get its AP3 through anything with a 2+ save (which will be the primary target anyone wielding these will be going up against). To make up for this it’s got the same solid Damage 2 as a Thunder Hammer, but strikes quicker at I-1 rather than I-2, allowing for a little alpha-strike potential vs Thunder Hammer wielding goons. Whilst this is a little bit of a downgrade from 2.0 (RIP Murderous Strike), I still think this is a decent weapon that you’ll be giving to most of your Command and Elite choices, especially as it’s a Sword of the Order for your Crit Hit (6+) Gambit..

Plasma Burner & Plasma Incinerator

The Dark Angels have always been famous for their plasma, not just in its traditional patterns, but also in more esoteric and dangerous forms lost to most legions or proscribed by the Emperor himself. Chief among these are unique flamer-style plasma weapons, the Plasma Burner and Plasma Incinerator.

These template weapons allow you to combine the benefits of Plasma’s armour penetration with the saturation fire of flamers. However, as with the other changes to Astartes Plasma, the humble Plasma Burner now has two profiles:

  • Sustained Fire: A S5 Ap4 D1 and Breaching (6+) Template
  • Maximal Fire: A +1S and Breaching (5+) Template with Overload (1). 

The Plasma Incinerator is the same, but with Damage 2 on both profiles. Now these previously didn’t Overload, which left a lot of people (me included) pretty salty on first reading. (Aren’t these supposed to be proscribed DAoT plasma tech? Were they proscribed because they were shit?). Regardless they’re still much more reliable than plasma guns when comparing their potential output (once in range), though with the changes to Reposition (units can now move their Initiative in any direction) they’re a bit more easy to dodge, so you’ll have to be careful to maximise your shots against canny opponents. 

You also can’t upgrade any old plasma gun to them anymore, so they’re strictly the purview of Interemptors and Marduk Sedras. The most bewildering thing to me is that these lack Panic (x). (Listen I get that not every template weapon needs a status, but it’s a Plasma. Flamer. What do they think fire is even made of? Even Irrad Cleansers, with their invisible waves of radiation get it. I mean, come on!)

Plasma Repeater [Legacies]

Another DAoT plasma weapon unsealed from the vaults, the Plasma repeater is a slightly weaker, 12” plasma gun with three shots, available as a Legion Special Weapon for 15pts. These lost twin-linked between editions, which really stings, making them much riskier to use with Maximal Fire, which you should be using to double your effectiveness into Marines (Breaching 6+ to 5+). Being 5pts more expensive than a Plasma gun puts them in a tough spot value wise, especially now that Plasma guns can consistently output 2 shots at 24”. Can’t really see the use in these outside of flavour.

Units of the Dark Angels

Paladin of the Hekatonystika (Command)

Deathwing Terminator Praetor. Credit: Jack Hunter

Representing the leaders of the myriad secret orders of the Hekatonystika, Paladins are chosen from the ranks to lead the warriors of the first, using their own particular expertise to gain the edge against their foe. As such your Legion specific Prime Benefit allows you to upgrade your Centurion to a Paladin of the Hekatonystika. This gives them a free Terranic Greatsword, the Orders Exemplar rule (little combat buffs - see below) and +1 WS, giving you a very cheap melee Centurion. Whilst this is directly competing against Paragon of Battle (where its additional +1 Attack can be very strong), the ability to field a decent combat Centurion for only 80pts is very appealing and what I find myself using on every list I write.

Inner Circle Knights Cenobium (Elites)

One of the best unique Terminator units in the game, the Inner Circle Knights Cenobium are your premier elite unit. Majority WS5 with a WS6 Sergeant (which is really screaming to be made a WS7 challenge monster through Master Sergeant PB) all armed with Terranic Greatswords and Plasma Casters (mini plasma burners) these can go toe to toe with the worst your opponent has to throw at them. Not only that but at list creation you can also choose an Orders Exemplar to give them a very specific buff representing the esoteric and highly specialised martial orders of the Hekatonystica; from monster slayers, to warlord hunters to psyker killers. Beyond this the only real option with these Knights is whether to give any of them Thunder Hammers. Despite Terranic Greatswords costing exactly the same as a Thunder Hammer these days, these are an extra 10 points for ICKC. I can only assume someone forgot to change this when they ported this over from last edition. Regardless Thunder Hammers are perennially great, overcoming the natural swingyness of the Terranic Greatsword’s AP but likely striking at the same time or after your opponent. Throwing a couple in a unit seems like a decent choice without compromising on your Sword of Order synergies.

How to use this unit:

Throw these guys at the toughest thing in your opponents list and see what happens. Even better with your favourite combat monster character. Also as ICKC are Heavy (so have a pathetic 1” set-up move) they’re dying for a Transport to get them close enough to guarantee a charge. A 5-man squad in a Land Raider is your bread and butter, but don’t sleep on the 8-man in Storm Eagle/Caestus combo (from Turn 2 you can basically get them to any point on the board… so long as you can make your reserves roll).

Orders Exemplar

Representing the myriad orders of arcane knowledge when you bring either a Paladin of the Hekatonystika or Inner Circle Knights Cenobium you must pick one of the below buffs for them:

N.B. On paper these are technically more flexible now as the game process specifically allows you to ‘write’ your list once you know the mission and opponent (though importantly not their list), letting you to choose the most appropriate order for each game. That’s probably a bit too WAAC for most folks, but check with your EO if you’re so inclined.

Augurs of Weakness

Fighting a Vehicle? Gain Armourbane

Useful if you’re rocking Thunder Hammers and your opponent is running a car park of a list and you’re lacking decent ranged output, but generally speaking ICKC are always going to be better equipped to deal with enemy Elites and Retinues instead.

Icons of Resolve

Get Ld 10 when you’re charged for that assault phase

What even is this? Anything that can take an Order Exemplar already has Ld 9 so at most it’s +1 Ld. And only when charged. Your Tactica already puts a floor on Leadership for combats you’re going to massively lose so it’s very likely that the +1 Ld won’t ever trigger. If this was unmodifiable like the old Stubborn rule I could see you running this, but it’s not, so you’ll never run it, because it’s pointless.

Slayer of Kings

Fighting a unit with WS6+? Set your Attacks to 1 and gain Critical Hit (6+)

Whilst Inner Circle Knights are definitely the unit you’ll want to be sending against WS6+ combat monsters, this is, actually, a case of the design team massively over-valuing the Critical Hit rule. The maths will never work out on this. Statistically it's always better to have three normal attacks than one special one, regardless of whether you’re fighting a Praetor or a Primarch. Yes, even if you buff it with your Advanced Reaction. It’s just bad.

Hunter of Beasts

Fighting a unit with T6+? Increase your D by 1

Allows your ICKC to effectively one-shot Saturnine and two shot Contemptors, so if you think you’ll be facing a Linebreaker Echelon of Saturnine or two Mortifactors and all their sacrophagi-interred mates then this is the Order Exemplar for you.

Reaper of Hosts

If you’re outnumbered, increase your Attacks by 1

Inarguably the default pick, this allows you to pretty consistently punch above your weight. The primary way you’ll be using ICKC will probably be as 5-man squads in Land Raiders so it’s very likely you’ll be able to reliably benefit from this Order in most games.

Breaker of Witches

Fighting a unit with one or more Psyker or Malefic models? Get +1 to hit and +1 wound

Situational, but incredibly strong if you find yourself matched up against Thousand Sons, Word Bearers or anyone bringing Daemons. Just be aware that Breaching will still only proc on the unmodified wound roll, so you’re not suddenly Breaching on 4+ with your TGSs.

Inner Circle Knights Cenobium - Order of the Broken Claws (Elites) [Legacies]

A specialist unit dedicated to guarding the stasis vaults holding relics from the Rangdan Xenocides, this is still a completely separate unit from regular ICKC rather than another order, despite being effectively identical in profile. They also cost +10 points more, so let’s see what you get for that:

Order of the Broken Claw

Fighting Walkers, Automata, Malefic? Gain +1 Toughness and ignore negative modifiers to all your mental stats.

This is a defensively-themed combo of both Breaker and Hunter, allowing you to tarpit massive threats, by taking a beating and staying in the fight longer, regardless of if you win or lose. A very decent choice into Dark Mech lists, but very situational so unless you’re creating a Rangda veteran force, I’d give these a miss over the regular Knights. 

Corswain and his Deathwing Companions take on the perfidious Third Legion. Credit: Soggy

Deathwing Companions (Retinue)

Deathwing Companions are your other premier elite unit. Their role is to escort your Command units from Challenge to Challenge unmolested thanks to their special rule Companions, allowing you to simply ignore Precision wounds from shooting at Command units. Beyond having the standard elite marine combat profile these boys have one more trick up their sleeves. Representing their oaths to protect their charges with their very lives, they have Eternal Warrior (1). Most elite counters (Thunder Hammers, Lascannons etc) all rely on D2 to one shot Elites. Reducing their effectiveness by half suddenly makes this unit incredibly tough to shift.

Companions as standard are equipped with Calibanite Warblades that can be upgraded to Terranic Greatswords or Power Fists for +5pts. Whilst Terranic Greatswords are probably the auto-pick, the Power Fist definitely feels like it has a role in the unit, giving you a slow but reliable AP2 punch. Taking a couple is never a bad idea. 

DWCs also have bolters and bolt pistols as standard which you can swap out for Combi-weapons or Plasma pistols respectively. You can also swap the bolter for a Cytheron Pattern-Aegis. This piece of wargear has unfortunately had a significant downgrade from edition to edition, losing its cool shooting/combat invuln bubble, and its initiative debuff. Instead it’s now just an overcosted Boarding shield; for 10pts per model you get a 5+ invuln and the Shield trait. That said you’ll probably still want to take these (even just a few) to add even more survivability to this unit (though not your Oathbearer who has a 5+ invuln au gratis). 

This unit is insanely points-efficient. A 5-man Praetorian Command Squad armed with Thunder Hammers and Combat Shields at 215pts. 5 Deathwing Companions armed with Terranic Greatswords and Cytheron Pattern Aegises is 240pts - for effectively for 5pts for per model you’re gaining Eternal Warrior (1), the Companions Special rule, and better mental stats - bargain. You’ve also got incredibly easy access to them without an Apex Detachment through Deathwing Conclave (where they’d also be prime!).

How to use this unit:

These can form the backbone of any melee heavy list, acting as a very reliable hammer to smash most dedicated combat units your opponent throws at you. Running a brick of these to escort a Praetor or Champion around the battlefield, normally in some sort of metal box (flying or otherwise) is really the premier choice. 

Tartaros and Cataphractii Deathwing Companions (Retinue) [Legacies]

Your two Terminator flavours of Companions are locked to 5 man squads, have all the same rules, and are as good value as the Companions are. For 20pts more than the equivalent Terminator Command Squad you get their incredible Eternal Warrior (1) and the Companions special rule. This gives you very hardy elite terminators with access to Terranic Greatswords, Lightning Claws, Thunder Hammers and Power Fists. 

Comparing the two units of Terminators vs regular Companions, Tartaros Terminators are roughly equivalent to Deathwing Companions (slightly more expensive for +1 Toughness), whilst Cataphractii seem a much stronger choice (in built 4+ invulns coupled with Eternal Warrior (1) can be absurdly sticky)

How to use this unit: 

As with their artificier armoured counterparts these are the perfect backbone for a thematic Deathwing list, especially if you’re leaning more into the Terminator heavy proto-1st company vibes of 40K Dark Angels. Locked to 5-man squads these are perfect in Land Raiders, accompanied by a character of your choosing to get them wherever you want to go.

The battle for Thramas rages. Credit: Soggy

Interemptors (Support)

Interemptors are your premier war crimes unit, here to incinerate large blocks of infantry. These Legion destroyers are all armed with plasma burners, rad grenades and the Dreadwing’s unique penchant for salting the earth with the irradiated remains of their foes. If you can get these boys in range they can chew through Tacticals or blunt deathstars like nobody's business. 

Template weapons can be a little janky to make work in larger squads due to friendly overlap or potential Repositioning, but have no fear. Now that Rad Grenades are ranged weapons any model out of position can still contribute (Pro Tip: resolve the Grenade firegroup first to ensure that the rest of their shooting can take advantage of Phage (T)). Additionally for every 5 Interemptors you can take one special weapon; either the fine Plasma Incinerator (+1D plasma burner) or the excellent Destroyer Missile Launcher with Rad (2 shot 24” Rad Grenades? Yes please) or Stasis (Blast 3” with Stun (3)?! Hell yeah!). It’s even Assault unlike the Plasma Burners.

Interemptors are no longer good in combat. After bizarrely having 4 attacks every first round last edition, they’ve dropped to 1 attack in 3e. Not that Combat was really where this unit was ever destined to shine (they can’t even take power weapons on their sergeants). Close range is their home and thanks to legacies Phosphex bombs are back on the menu for that extra spicy Panic (3) threat. It’s also odd that these don’t have Vanguard (x) (instead having Support Unit (1)), given their Lore and how they’re best used. Their limited range will force you to keep them moving from target to target anyway.

How to use this unit:

Interemptors are a really useful unit in your toolkit. Reasonably cheap and easy to access due to the Dreadwing detachment, they’re solid anti-infantry punch in any list, though they desperately need a Transport to make them useful. Even if their weapons lack Panic (x) their presence tends to induce real-life panic in your opponents so they’ll normally attract a disproportionate amount of enemy firepower. I’ve found them most effective in one or two bare bones 5-man squads in rhinos, thrown up the board as distraction carnifexes or held behind cover as a mid-board counter punch. A squad of ten in a Drop Pod can also be a very scary threat to keep your opponent second-guessing their positioning all game long. Also they’re still filth in cramped corridors of Zone Mortalis.

Legion Inductii (Troops)

Dark Angels Inductii. Credit: Max

In a simple but brutally efficient example of the Lion’s pragmatism, the Inductii of the Dark Angels were given the Legion’s surplus of weapons of the Old Night. Whilst you never really see Volkite Charger tactical support squads, you'd be tempted to run the them at a 40 points discount with Line (1). With the lowest mental stats of any Marine unit in the game, they’re also well poised to take advantage of the Dark Angels Legion Tactica.

How to use this unit:

Run ‘em like aggressive Tacticals. Pop them in Rhinos and try and hold midfield objectives. They’re also especially good in Zone Mortalis where the limited range of the Volkite Charger isn’t as much of a liability.

Firewing Enigmatus Cabal (Elites) [Legacies] 

Engimatus Cabals represent small elite cells of Firewing operatives. Masters of Assassination and subterfuge, they agilely move behind enemy lines disrupting supply lines, exploiting vulnerabilities and eliminating enemy command staff. Truly they represent the purest distillation of the martial prowess of the Firewing. It’s just very unfortunate that their unit profile is absolute dogshit. 

For 90pts you’re getting an incredibly overcosted 3 man Veteran Assault Squad with “flexible” power swords with either:

  • I+2 AP4 and no Breaching(!?)
  • I-1 Str+1 AP3 and Breaching (6+) (aka a worse power axe)

Every member has Champion but with that weapon they’re all but guaranteed to lose every Challenge they start. They have Vanguard (2) (as opposed to say the Vanguard (3) of a Veteran Assault Squad) with the ability to give an enemy unit Marked For Death (1), allowing you, once per game, to effectively turn on Vanguard (3) against a unit you’ve told you’re opponent you’re targeting (good luck!). They also come with needle pistols (fine), and for an extra 15pts you can give one a cool Stasis Missile launcher (Stun 3!), but it’s infinitely better on something you’ll actually field, like Interemptors. 

This unit requires an Elite slot. That’s not the easiest slot to get access to so you’ll have to work hard to get them into a list. Sadly their profile does not justify that in the slightest. There’s been a lot of changes to Dark Angels from edition to edition and I’ve tried to be restrained and positive in the rest of this article but this is honestly a crime. Whoever wrote this unit entry deserves the title of Eskaton for the complete murder of this unit.

How to use this unit:

Don’t.

Excindio Battle-Automata (War Engine) [Legacies]

The super secret Silica Animus makes a return from the vaults of the Invincible Reason, and is still a repository of absolutely bonkers rules. Representing their infamous status you can only bring one of these in a list and only if your Primary Detachment is Dark Angels.

Firstly these things are still their own bizarre unit type, Artificia. The neutered Abominable Intelligence within these shells ignore the Mechanicum’s facile Cybertheurgic Statuses but as they’re ‘alive’ can still gain all other statuses (yes even Routed). Which is just as well that their Mental stats are all 10. Otherwise these are much more Contemptor equivalent to this edition, though with some notable differences (with WS and BS 5, 8 Wounds and a 3+/5+ save). More wounds but a worse save is probably bad until you factor in that it also has Eternal Warrior (1), which compensates quite a bit to their survivability.

These entities harbor a legendary loathing for all living things, represented fittingly by Hatred (Infantry, Cavalry, Walker, Automata). This malice is barely contained however, represented by the Vengeful Rage rule; once the unit is reduced to 4 wounds or less, their techno-archanic wards fail and they must shoot at the closest visible unit each turn, friend or foe. So be careful where you put them.

For shooting, they start with 2 combi bolters which can be swapped for Grav guns, Plasma guns or Irad-cleansers which are all great and fun. They can also be given one of four unique weapons:

  • Athanax phosphex canister launcher (15pts) - a weird phosphex quad launcher that uses Pinning (3) (rather than Panic (3)) and AP4. Pinning (3) is amazing but without any breaching won’t likely do much beyond that.
  • Tyrhenius nerve induction shredder (15pts) - a 18” 10 shot, Poisoned (2+) with Panic (1) and Assault. Stats wise this probably does a couple of wounds into MEQs with Panic (1) so is seems pretty decent
  • Magaron Atomantic pulse cannon (30pts) - 24” 3 shot S8 AP2 D1 with Assault. For the points I think this is probably the worst of the bunch as it’s not doing much to Vehicles with D1 and lacks the Statusing utility of the rest.
  • Cytheron graviton flux projector (20pts) - A template Grav weapon with S7 AP4 Breaching (5+) with Pinning (3) and D2 and Assault (on a Template!). A personal fav.

In Combat you’ll be relying on its Excindio Claws which act a bit like larger Terranic Greatswords at Str8, AP3, D2, Breaching (5+), Reaping Blow (2). Not the strongest anti-elite profile, especially when compared to Gravis Power Fists, but you will be harder to hit at WS5. Oh and it’s a Champion so it can challenge.

Finally, taking an Excindio also allows any Command model to take an Artificia Kill-Switch for +10pts, allowing you to effectively auto Explode your Excindio if you’re within 12” at the end of any of your turns, which is potentially 8 S8 hits on every unit within 6”. It’ll take quite a bit of set-up but this can really ruin your opponents day.

How to use this unit:

Excindio tend to be best used aggressively, thrown directly into your enemies lines to cause as much carnage as possible. Because they’re Artificia (and not a Walker) they can’t get in Dreadnought Drop Pods, Dreadclaws or Kharybdises which is so disapointing (they'd be the perfect and most loreful delivery method for them). As such you’re best off slogging them up the board on a flank, using their Status weaponry to disrupt enemy infantry before closing and eventually blowing themselves up. Ultimately they’re an incredibly fun unit to both build and play with, which should probably be how you’re building lists anyway.

Characters of the Dark Angels

Marduk Sedras

A true relic of the Great Crusade, Marduk Sedras has a storied history. A veteran of the unification wars he has overseen the edict of Eskaton on countless worlds; a fell order that sentences a planet to utter destruction.

Marduk initially looks like another Praetor in Cataphractii Armour but his wargear is where it’s at, with one of the most Metal swords in the Heresy. The Death of Worlds is a monster. Constructed from fragments of every world he’s put to death, it has a dangerous I-2, S9 AP2 D3 meaning he can slice up Dreadnoughts and Challengers alike with world-ending strength (though notably because he’s Terran Vet, this isn’t a Sword of the Order and so doesn’t synergise with those rules). He’s also armed with an Interemptor’s Plasma Burner and Phosphex Bombs, making him a menace during Volley Fire.  Finally he gives himself and any unit he’s with Hatred (Traitor) representing his role in getting the Scouring started early. Hot stuff.

How to use this unit:

I’ve found it hard to justify running Marduk much this edition. The additional 70pts you pay vs a basic Terranic Greatsword Terminator Praetor feels pretty significant, though the times I’ve gotten the Death of Worlds into Challenges have been incredible, as he can basically one-shot most opponents with the right gambit. Use him similarly to any other Praetor-tier character by zooming him around in a battle bus with his favourite tooled-up pals.

Corswain

One of the GOATs of the Heresy, Corswain was inarguably the finest blade of the 1st Legion, able to go toe to toe with any other First Captain in the Legions. Fittingly, in 3rd Edition, Corswain is built for Challenges. Possessing both WS7 and The Blade, effectively an at initiative Terranic Greatsword with AP2 and Duellist’s Edge (2), he can hit hard and he can hit fast. A gift from the Lion himself, his Armour of the Forest also grants him an effective Eternal Warrior (1) in all Challenges, meaning he can take as much as he puts out.

How to use this unit:

It’s very straightforward. Find your opponent’s biggest, baddest dude and Challenge him to death, whilst your retinue of Deathwing Companions finishes off the rest. Yes, he’s pretty expensive, but how much is winning every Challenge worth to you?

Lion El’Jonson

Credit: Kevin Stillman

The embodiment of the 1st Legion, Lion El’Jonson is an absolute monster as Primarchs go and is probably the best non-demon infused duellist in the game. Mostly this comes from his deadly statline of WS8, Str7, I7 and A7 and a choice of two blades, the Lion Sword for slicing up the toughest customers (Str9, AP2, D3), and the Wolf Blade for butchering mooks (AP3, D1, Breaching & Shred (4+), Reaping blow (2)). He’s also packing the Fusil Actinaeus, a cool 2-shot plasma gun with a single profile (and it doesn’t even get hot?! What is this, Mech?). Along with this solid baseline, he’s also packing a couple of extra tricks.

He’s armed with Stasis grenades, unreproducible relics of Old Night.These grenades freeze potential combatants in place. Once per game, if charged, you reduce the charging units Initiative by -2 (ignored for Challenges). This is a great threat to hold over your opponent, giving you an edge in fights you didn’t choose, or dissuading them entirely in the first place.

The Lion’s unique Gambit is the Lion’s Choler. If he’s ever reduced to precisely 1 or 2 wounds (which happens more than you’d think with Eternal Warrior (2) ), the Lion becomes so grumpy he can choose to ignore all negative modifiers from wounds and gain an extra +2 to Focus. This is potentially a 6 or 7 initiative swing, all but guaranteeing you gaining the first strike nearer the end of a challenge.

Finally, as with all Primarchs, bringing the Lion unlocks a specific Army build (sort of like 2e Rite of War). Sire of the Dark Angels, representing the true force of their combined Wings, rewards you for bringing a diverse list:

Specifically:

  • If you take 4 Troops in your Primary (at least 1 Tactical, 1 Breacher and 1 Assault), they all become Prime
  • Outriders and Sky hunter Squadrons get +1 to reserve rolls (either outflank or Deep-strike)

Needless to say, 3 additional Prime slots on your core Troops is excellent (combat veterans on scoring units is always welcome) and giving +1 reserve rolls to Outriders with plasma guns or Sky Hunters with Meltas can give you some very reliable backfield counter-punch.

Allies of the Dark Angels

Solar Auxilia - Calibanite Jaegers (or Gramarye Castellans)

Whether you're building a Lutherian Defence Force or representing the scores of cohorts the 1st fought alongside during the Great Crusade, you can represent their stolid determination through Calibanite Jaegers Legiones Auxilia Designation. By taking this, your Solar Auxilia allies gain the unique Advanced Reaction Bring it Down!; if any of your units are successfully charged by Walkers, Automata or a list of other things that are on 50mm+ bases, you can spend a Reaction to let every model in the unit make an attack with a Detonation weapon that counts towards combat res. Given that all infantry have Krak grenades, this can be a fun little surprise for opponents looking to bully your Line sections.

Dark Angels Saturnine Terminators stalk the cities of Thramas. Credit: Soggy

Sample Lists

To round us off, I've put together a couple of lists to play with. I don't think these are optimally balanced or anything, but they could definitely act as foundation for you to edit and iterate if you're not sure where to start. First off is a Deathwing ZM list, featuring a couple of your heavy hitter elite units:

Primary Detachment - Zone Mortalis Linebreaker

Praetor - Terranic Greatsword - 135pts

Elites - 5x Inner Circle Knights Cenobium. Prime (Master Sergeant) - 275pts

Elites - 5x Veteran Despoilers w/5x Power Mauls and Nuncio Vox - 155pts

Troops - Tacticals w/Nuncio Vox - 110pts

Troops - Breachers w/ 2x Grav Guns, Nuncio Vox, Breacher Charges. Prime (Combat Vets)  - 180pts

Heavy Assault - Saturnine Terminators w/Plasma Bombards and Particle Shredders - 215pts

Auxiliary Detachment - Deathwing Conclave

6x Deathwing Companions w/Terranic Greatswords, 2x Cytheron Pattern Aegis. Prime (Logistical Prime Benefit)

Logistical Prime Benefit: War Engine - Contemptor w/Gravis Plasma Cannon and Gravis Power Fist, Grav Gun - 175pts

The idea is pretty straightforward with this force. Simply throw your elite units into the fray with your Dreadnought in support whilst protecting your main scoring units (Breachers, Tacticals). The Veteran Despoilers can act as a bit of a flanking force tailored specifically for going after back line power armoued mooks. The plasma bombards on the Saturnine might seem an odd choice, but blast weapons are always handy in the close quarters of ZM (though Interemptors would be much more effective here).

And secondly, here's a more Dreadwing style list:

Primary Detachment - Crusade Primary Detachment

High Command - Saturnine Praetor w/War axe, Teleportation transponder - 260pts

Command - Centurion w/Jump Pack, Terranic Greatsword. Prime (Paladin of the Hekatonystika) - 100pts

Troops - Tacticals w/Nuncio vox, Combi-Grav. Prime (Combat Vets) 120pts

Troops - Tacticals w/Nuncio vox, Combi-Grav. 120pts

Troops - Breachers w/2x Grav Guns, Nuncio Vox - 170pts

Transport - Rhino w/Dozer Blade, Havoc Launcher - 70pts

Transport - Rhino w/Dozer Blade, Havoc Launcher - 70pts

Auxillary Detachment - Armoured Support

Armour - Predator w/Plasma Executioner, Heavy Bolters - 125pts

Armour - Vindicator Seige Tank - 140pts

Armour - Whirlwind Missile Tank w/Pyrax Warheads - 110pts

Dreadwing Cadre

Support - Dreadwing Interemptors. Prime (Logistical Prime Benefit) - 125pts

Support - Dreadwing Interemptors - 125pts

Support - 2x Rapier Laser Destroyers - 130pts

Logistical Prime Benefit: War Engine - Leviathan w/Grav-flux Bombard, Leviathan Siege Drill, Phosphex Discharger 255pts

Deathwing Conclave

Retinue - Saturnine Command Squad x3 w/1x War Axe, 2x Paired Disruption Fists, 3x Particle Shredders. Prime (Master Sergeant)

Elites - Veteran Assault Squad x5 w/Lightning Claws - 170pts

Elites - Destoyer Assault Squad x5 w/Power Maul, 4x Two hand flamers, Destroyer Missile Launcher - 145pts

This list aims to take lots of thematically Dreadwingy units (though admittedly I'm missing any Disintigration weapons which are bang on theme). The rhinos should be used to get the Interemptors into position, but are equally as effective rapidly deploying a tactical squad onto an objective in Turn 1. The Saturnine Command squad and Praetor are an interesting counter play, allowing you to drop a threatening blob into your opponent's backline or any hard-fought objective.
