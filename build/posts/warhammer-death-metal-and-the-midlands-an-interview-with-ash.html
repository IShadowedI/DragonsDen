Warhammer, Death Metal and the Midlands: An Interview with Ash Moore of Mutagenic Host

September 26th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The Lamb’s a nice little local boozer in Surbiton. Ash Moore, vocalist for Mutagenic Host, is in the beer garden setting up a table for Spearhead. Inside the pub a guy is plugging an acoustic guitar into some amps.

It’s sunny. Kids are running around. One of their dads comes up and asks what we’re playing. He tells us he plays Kill Team and a bit of 40K when he gets the chance. Ash enthusiastically tells him about two clubs he is part of nearby.

You should come. It’s kid friendly too.

By the time we sort out the cards, board and terrain for our game, there is already soft music drifting from inside the pub. Ash gestures towards the open window and the singer beyond it.

He’s got a good voice. You into folk?

Yeah. I’ve been listening to a lot of Bulgarian Wedding Music at the moment.

So, you’re eclectic like me. I love folk.

Well, last time I saw you, you were growling into a mic at the Black Heart in Camden. For those who don't know Bob Dylan from their Cannibal Corpse, do you want to just introduce the band for us?

Yeah, so Mutagenic Host is a five piece Death Metal band from London, but we're all from different parts. I'm from Birmingham and I do the vocals, organise the band and do our socials. I also write all the lyrics. 

Credit: Keiffer Passey

Jack Thompson, he's from LA, he's the guitarist and is one of two song writers. Then Dan Bulford, who's from London, also writes and produces a lot of the music. Our bassist is called Eddie Swales, he's from London. And then George Kinella-Pearn, he's the drummer and he's from the Midlands, like me. 

We formed just as Covid ended. I was just searching for a creative output. I just think that the pandemic had such a huge effect on people. It had such a huge effect on me. A lot of people were so angry during that time. A lot of people's loved ones died because of Tory ineptitude and cronyism and I don't think it gets talked about enough.  It's been pushed under the rug. People are so horrified by it that it's almost traumatic. That made me really angry. At the same time, like many others, I got back into Warhammer for the first time since I was a child. I think Covid was a real diving board moment for the hobby; lots of people inside, nothing to do but fear for their mortal coil, so little plastic toy soldiers was the antidote - and death metal. 

Priority? 

We roll off and Ash gets a four to my one. He deploys his Maggotkin of Nurgle: Bleak Host. Today I’ve opted for Flesh Eater Courts Carrion Retainers.

You’ve got a lot of movement in your list. That’s where Nurgle struggles.  We don’t get round the board much.

I begin deploying my force while Ash tells me me how the band got its start.

credit: Ash More Three of us at the time, me, Dan and George were part of the hardcore scene in London. So we would be going to hardcore shows all the time. Dan and I had a band together previously called Downward Spiral, which is more like a crowbar, a depressing melodic-hardcore band.

But I always wanted to really have a proper crack at doing a Death Metal band. 

So, I put it on a Facebook page. Does anybody want to start a band? And I met Jack Thompson. We just became best friends straight away. His song writing credibility is one of the things that's carried this band all the way through. But all the members of this band are multi-talented and just incredible people.

We all are really supportive of one another. All of our family knows each other. Our whole life really revolves around the band.

Ash chooses to go first, clumping most of his units together and moving slowly forward in a big unkillable (but slow) blob of pestilent flesh, but sending one of his Pusgoyle Blightlords to toe an objective.  Being a Nurgle player myself, I know how it goes; move stuff into the middle and wait to see if your opponent is foolish enough to charge.

Not wanting to get bogged down in the middle for the rest of the game, I send my Morbheg Knights to go deal with the lone Blightlord, which mercifully they kill in one round (which is rare), and then send my Varghulf Courtier and my Abhorrant Archregent to toe an objective each, nudging me into the lead at the end of round one.

credit: Saelfe

So we started with the Genotoxic Demo. The first song we wrote was called the March of Pestilence and that was a song about the marching of families whose loved ones had died during COVID, this was due to the failures of the Tories for the most part. I abhor incompetence and posturing. We got on Dry Cough Records, which is the home of underground British Death Metal, and put out a tape. Tapes are synonymous with death metal, for a long time this is how the genre travelled by swapping tapes. It's a rite of passage for any self-respecting death metal band to put out a tape. We had a really good response and that spurred us on to make haste. 

Dry Cough Records sounds like the perfect record label for a Nurgle band!

We loved that label. We've moved away from it now, but we'll always have a place in our heart for it. Mutagenic Host is a Nurgle band. The name alone should conjure up visions of the Grandfathers Blessings. We have songs like ‘Metaphysical Maladies’ which is taken from a spell from the Malignant Spellcaster from Death Guard, or lyrics that include ‘rancid visitations’ which is a spell from Maggotkin in 3rd edition AOS. I own nearly 11k points of Nurgle Warhammer. Im missing Tarmukhan and his horde, Ignatius Grulgor who is the death guard Forge World Daemon Prince, Scabeiathrax the Bloated which is a Forge World Great Unclean one and Cor'bax Utterblight from the Horus Heresy. So I'm not missing a lot at all! If anyone is reading this and wants to part with any of those, let me know and make me the jolliest Nurglite on the planet, that would be kind. 

Tell me about the band name. Mutagenic Host is a pretty memorable name.

It’s all tied to bio-weaponry, eugenics - and Warhammer. A host can be the carrier of the mutagens, or host can also mean an army. Everything that I write has a dual meaning. 

Talking of mutagenic hosts, Ash wins priority into round two and moves his host of rotting fanatics closer to the central objective, not quite having the movement to dominate it, but certainly having enough to get a good number of Plaguebearer feet on it and make me think twice about trying to take it. He has enough battle tactic cards to get a decent score without having to take objectives off me.

You and I played AOS ages ago, maybe even the first time we met, and you told me then that you were in a band. I politely nodded and then forgot all about it. Then a year later I read this article about the revival of Death Metal in the UK, and it mentioned Mutagenic Host. I went and had a listen and messaged you right away saying something like, ‘you never told me you were in a GOOD band!’

We just seem to be getting better and better. It’s a beautiful thing. It’s a creative endeavour that I take incredibly seriously. In a world of throwaway garbage, popular figureheads who do more damage to the earth than good, and insidious overlords that are trying to infiltrate the way you think; to have a vehicle to try and disrupt that is incredibly important. 

We had a really great response early on. Even some of the opening shows that we were playing with all top quality death metal bands. Our first show was with Vacuous who are on Relapse Record, now. An incredible, incredible band. They gave us our first show. We also played with Incantation, who are just absolute legends. Then we played with another big band coming from the US called Undeath. So, you know, the quality of the demo allowed us to develop the band in a way that was professional and that felt that it was making a difference in the music scene. 

Credit: Kieffer Passey

And then you released The Diseased Machine. Which is just such an awesome album. 

We wrote Diseased Machine in about six months, maybe eight. The theme is the biggest talking point about that album, and I think it's why people resonate with it so much. It’s incredibly damning of AI and how the upper echelons of society have been so quick to allow Pandora’s Box to open without impunity; putting yours, mine, and the future of the children of this earth in complete jeopardy just because some play dough complexion nerd who got all his wealth from using children to mine minerals in Africa wants to act like a TEMU Tony Stark. Makes me fucking sick to the stomach.

I digress, the album tells the story of the destruction of humanity by AI. It’s actually the start of a trilogy of storytelling. Much like Warhammer, we create our own lore. It’s a lot of fun, if not taxing to some degree also. After the album we put out the Grotesque Union/ Chimeric Vestige 2026 promo, which is essentially about the last human organism left solitary on earth after the destruction of mankind, at the hands of the   machines that harvest human biomatter (sound familiar?) and their AI overlords. Then the next album we're doing is actually the prelude to all that.

It's science fiction. We're all massive 80s/90s film fans, Terminator, Videodrome, Soylent Green, you know, so all that kind of imagery comes into it. But the real takeaway from it is about the establishment around the world. I’m absolutely disgusted with how human life is so completely transient, left to rot, allowed to fester to meet their means. When you have children that are being bombed by drones in Gaza that utilise AI. And then you’ve got the likes of Palantir trying to take over the NHS, this oligarch of the wrong kind of nerds, is in itself, it’s a disease.

You know what I mean? Like, the complete disregard of human life is always so abhorrent to me and it's always so prevalent in all of this. All the actions of these demagogues who are always only out for their own end. It horrifies me. So it's easier to write an album that's about that than it is to go away and come up with the kind of imagery usually used in Death Metal songs. It's much easier to write about the horrors of the world than it is to actually fantasize about certain tropes in death metal. 

In my half of the dullest 2nd round of Spearhead ever played, I step just enough Cryptguard onto the objective to take control, and started to bring my Morbhegs and Varghulf Courtier back towards the centre for the inevitable bloodbath to come in round three, whilst making sure I score enough primary points and battle tactics to maintain a slim lead. 

Musically speaking, the album really has the feel of a classic 90s Death Metal record. There’s some serious groove to it.

Yeah, we always do groove. We never over produce. 

It was very stressful to record. We were all working our jobs and doing it on our own time. Once it was written we had to record it. That was really arduous. We went to one place, didn't work out, went to another place, didn't work out. We had to go and do vocals elsewhere. Then we ended up doing a lot of it at home where we would do it and then go and have it reamped to the studio. Thankfully there's a bloke called Boulty in Nottingham (Stuck on a Name Studios) Another Warhammer link! And he managed to put it all together.

And we don't give a shit what anybody thinks about us. A lot of bands say that, but we truly mean it. I think it comes off in the music.

We'll never put out music that we think will bore people. I mean, we're all very talented people, but it's never about technicality. It's always about feeling and it's always about groove, and it's always about how it relates back to people. And I think that's so much more important than being great musicians. Someone called us hooligan death metal once!

Ash wins priority and brings down his second Pusgoyle Blightlord and Plaguebearer units, using the Plaguebearers to steal an objective and charging his Blightlord, along with everything else, into my units at the centre of the board, embroiling my Vargulf and Cryptguard in the fray. He decimates the unit of cryptguard, leaving only one alive, and then directs the rest of his attacks into the Varghulf, not wanting to wipe the Cryptguard out, and thus allow them to return elsewhere on the board. The Varghulf retaliates and takes the Blightlord down to one health.

credit: Ash Moore

It’s a pretty visual album, not only the sleeve art, which is incredible by the way, but also how the use of samples and evocative dystopian imagery conjures up this AI-ruled hellscape of a future.

We're very imagery orientated. We got in touch with a guy called Mark Cooper and he was amazing. He came up with this incredible painting for the artwork. I think creating a pink Death Metal album sleeve is a real first. Maybe not a first, but it's definitely interesting. 

I mean, we're all nerds really. The whole band is like proper nerds. Everybody's absolutely obsessed with Lord of the Rings. Everybody's obsessed with gaming, Warhammer, Magic The Gathering, There are actual film samples in that album. There's one from Soylent Green, and there's also another from… I can’t remember now. If you listen, audience, and spot it stick it in the comments. 

And as well as all that, there are a lot of Warhammer references in the album too. You've got a song called Genestealer, and a few other references too. What kind of impact does Warhammer have on the story you’re trying to tell, and on all those themes in the album?

I'm the one who’s obsessed with Warhammer.

I wanted to tie together the lexicon of Nurgle and utilize that as a way of doing storytelling within Death Metal. Death Metal has a solid history with Warhammer. If you go all the way back to 1986 you had Bolt Thrower, who are also from the midlands, they named their band after a war machine from Fantasy Battles, as well as bolters in 40k. Realm of Chaos is one of Bolt Thrower’s seminal albums. That mostly mentions World Eaters actually….

There’s a huge history with Warhammer and death metal. There was even a whole Warhammer record label once. They were doing flexi discs and there were a bunch of different bands putting out Warhammer releases. So, one of my goals when starting this band was to continue that. 

But it’s not really strictly about Warhammer, it was more that I was just applying the imagery and the lexicon as a way of being able to express what I wanted to tell through storytelling. We have a song called Metaphysical Maladies. That was a spell by a Plaguecaster in Warhammer. It was a kind of spiritual disease. So, I applied that phrase to some substance abuse problems I've had in the past. I'm over now. But I was able to use that spell name to bring out what I wanted to say.

And then when we got to the Diseased Machine, I was playing a lot of Genestealer Cults in 40k, so obviously the song Genestealer gets its name from that, but that song's actually about nepotism. 

There's also lyrical elements in the Grotesque Union that talk about apotheosis. If you think about demon princes and you think about chaos essentially when they transcend human form into something that's more. I was utilizing that imagery as a vehicle to talk about real world stuff as well. About how people make changes to themselves, like taking drugs that make you lose weight, or people injecting their lips with filler. All of these transhuman elements, beautification elements as well, and the way people worship all.  The way people worship Love Islands on TV. I just find it immoral and I wanted to just say something about that. 

In terms of Warhammer, the way Genestealer Cults infiltrate the hive cities and politics and start to climb the hierarchies is interesting. Then the Day of Ascension comes, and they're absolutely slaughtered by the Tyranids and turned into biofuel. The way in which AI has happened has been the same. It’s cultish. People have been worshipping machines way too much. We're losing our humanity. Mutagenic Host, believe it or not, it's actually a humanistic band, which is quite rare in death metal.

Kieffer Passey

Now it’s my turn to clap back. I manage to finish off the Blightlord with a spell from the Archhregent and then free my Varghulf to go off and see to the Plaguebearers who had snuck on and stole one of my objectives. Then my Morbhegs charge into the big punch up in the middle to kill off as many Blightkings as possible, and then keep the rest of his forces pinned in the middle. I manage to score myself enough points to keep my slim lead.

People often laugh at me when I say Warhammer lore actually has a lot to tell us about the world. But anyone who reads the books knows it's more than just rolling dice and moving plastic around a table.

I'm a Nurgle fanatic. I know you are too. Really, I just cannot shake it away, you know. I can't be rid of it. I play it all; Spearhead, AOS, 40K and Kill Team, and I play Nurgle across everything.

There's just something that’s so real world about Nurgle at the moment. We talk about geotropic diseases, particularly with COVID you know, and then you talk about a lot of ecological problems in the world. It feels like the age of Nurgle.

Yeah, the thing I love the most about Nurgle is that whilst he’s the god of disease and despair, he also gives his followers this kind of fake happiness so that they can endure it. His followers are all really jolly and they've all got big smiles on their faces even as they're rotting to pieces. That's almost what you see happening today. People are getting used to being treated like shit, having their rights and humanity eroded, but they’re being given this false happiness in the form of Love Island or being able to generate pictures of Putin in a bikini with AI or whatever. So, I can see where you're coming from.

Yeah, I mean his followers also refer to him as the Grandfather. So it’s all pretty patriarchal too. He's devoted to his followers, you know, and they'll do anything for him.

And I just feel like today people's freedoms are just being completely eroded and people are subscribing to ways of living that they shouldn't be. Almost as a spell on society. Degradation and rot to make its way in, and I think the mechanizations of society are so rotted now. 

I'm so sick of it. I guess Mutagenic Host has been a way to reflect on those in. In a positive way.

Credit: Kieffer Passey

Ash gets priority once again going into the final round. There isn’t a huge amount he can do now, other than cough on the Morbhegs, killing all but one of them. As predicted at the start, his movement – or lack of – is his downfall.  With only 4” on his Blightkings, retreating wouldn’t let him get enough distance away to claim any more points for the primary.  But when it comes to my final turn, a retreat with my one remaining Morbheg lets me clip the objective deep in his territory, as well as letting me get a guy within three inches of the long battlefield edge, scoring me enough points to finally win the game.

So, how was getting beaten by me?

Oh, mate, I mean, to be fair, getting beat by another Nurgilite is fine, even if you're using Flesh Eater Courts. But that was really well balanced, wasn't it? I think I made a bad move to start with by going first and putting something out on its own. The Morbehg Knights movement is so good that it all came down to movement in the end, like I said it would.  

But that's the only time I've ever lost both Pusgoyles and everything else that lived, they’re pretty tanky, but I should have protected them more. 

The interesting thing about Spearhead is that sometimes it’s very bloody very quick, because those are the cards you've drawn. But I think our battle tactics earlier on favoured a more cagey approach. I think it was round two before we even started fighting.

I think Spearhead is one of the best skirmish games GW has ever made. On the whole it’s really balanced. Some armies are maybe a little too strong, like Slaves to Darkness. Some are really, really weak, like the second Nurgle one. But mostly it’s a lot of fun. Not like Combat Patrol. That’s garbage. 

Also we were able to bring it to a pub and play it!

Why do you think Warhammer and metal go together so well? You don't exactly hear of many Warhammer K Pop bands.

Well, it's the grim, dark element, isn't it? It's the perpetual war. People seem to think that Death Metal fans are obsessed with killing and violence. But actually, really what they are doing is just reflecting real world horrors. They’re mostly lovely people.

And I think that death metal and Warhammer are so intrinsic because although all the visual elements are really married up, you know, demons, gunfire, death, at the same time, at the core of it, I think it's about people trying to deal with traumatic and horrible events. Music and war gaming both have a cathartic nature. Because even though we’re touching on some dark points, we can have fun while doing it.

I've seen you play live, and it's a lot of fun.  It's not miserable, everything's quite tongue in cheek.

Sure. I mean, you know, like the depiction of the end of the world via the onslaught of AI. It's not so much a joke, but it's almost like a pastiche, like a pictorial nonsense thing. I think that's just a natural response to something so horrific.

You have to be like that. Otherwise what are you going to do?

So what’s next for the band?

So we did our first headline tour at the start of the year and that went really well. We did six dates across the UK and that went really well. We'd only really supported bands until that point. And it's kind of a little bit of a big balls move to do a headline tour when you're still not on a major label. But it went really well. 

In July we played Dortmund Deathfest playing with Deicide and Asphyx. Also with Benediction who are another Birmingham based old school death metal band.  We also played alongside 200 Stab Wounds and Sanguisugabogg, which are two great bands, Jungle Rot played too. It’s like playing with Death Metal royalty. But someone over there loved us so got us over. 

But now we’re mostly working on our new album. It’s going to be our magnus opus as a band. It's going to be our most creative. Even in terms of visuals. I think there's going to be things coming out that we're going to try that maybe people haven't done for a long time. This band doesn't really know its limits.

So it's not gonna be the awkward second album. 

Absolutely not. No way. Like, this band only has ever kept the best of what we are that we can achieve. We will never, ever accept mediocrity

Credit: Ash Moore And what are you working on in terms of Warhammer at the moment?

I've been playing a lot of Spearhead. I own maybe 11k worth of Maggotkin of Nurgle and and Death Guard. And I've been painting that now for five years. I'm part of the First Founding club in Chessington, but probably spend more time at Kingston Warhammer Club because I've been there from the start. 

I’ve painted a good 7K of each, but then the new Maggotkin range came out and I kind of just got a little bit tired of painting green and grime, you know, So I actually started doing a couple of Spearheads. I painted Helsmiths of Hashut. I did them in this really bold, clean, copper scheme with red pigment on the base, which makes a difference from the really messy disgusting work with Nurgle. I've also got Iron Jaws Spearhead, so I’m playing a lot of that at the moment.

I mean, having ADHD and being a hobbyist is a challenge. I've just started taking medication at 36. It's really difficult to do Warhammer, so I have to really focus. But with Spearhead I can get one box, paint it up, and be able to play right away, rather than having these huge projects where you have some painted, some not painted, some half painted. You know, it's quite difficult really. For anybody that has neurodivergency, Spearhead's a really great way of doing Warhammer.

Spearhead's also a great way of getting people involved in the hobby. There are lots of kids at my club and we teach them how to play. There's a painting competition where everybody paints Spearhead. Hopefully that burgeons more AOS players as well. 

But it’s a good time for Warhammer in general. I think the 11th edition 40K is going to be really good, and I’m enjoying the new Generals Handbook for AOS too.

You can catch Mutagenic Host live in the UK in 2027 as part of their Cult of the New Flesh Tour, keep an eye on their socials for more details. Or pick up a copy of Diseased Machine here.
