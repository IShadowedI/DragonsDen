Black Library Readers’ Hall of Fame: The Winners of 2014, and Class of 2015

August 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Black Library Readers’ Hall of Fame is Tabletop Battles’s long-term, reader-participation initiative to recognize those novels that have “cultural, historical, or aesthetic significance” to the Black Library. Through the votes of readers as well as our community panel of community voices, we’ll examine the entire history of the Black Library, induct the latest winners, and vote for the next round’s candidates every other week.

The End Times are upon us, as we continue to see the end of Warhammer Fantasy. Surely more bitter than sweet for most, there nevertheless have been a number of stories well-remembered by the voters as we induct more Fantasy this week than any other setting.

As a reminder, if you're really enjoying this project and want to see it from a different angle, the companion piece, The Hall Monitor, runs over at my Substack, The Warhammer Wordforge. A cross between a director's commentary and behind-the-scenes look, each installment we dive a little deeper into a particular element of the Readers' Hall of Fame project. Hope to see you there!

The Envelope Please

Now, let's get to the stars of the show, the six books inducted for 2014!

Michael, Track of Words: "This is definitely not for those looking for straight-up Space Marine bolter action, although there certainly is plenty of visceral action to be found. It’s much more of a thriller, bound up in mystery and intrigue and slowly unfolding over the course of its three-part structure. As well-written as any Black Library book across the canon, it’s a brilliant follow-up to Exile, French managing to raise his game and produce a truly exceptional book mixing all of the usual Black Library elements with a compelling flair for the mysteries of the warp and this tragic cast of characters."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "Follow-up books in a series have a higher hurdle to clear. Often bereft of the novelty that saw their establishing book draw acclaim and Hall of Fame consideration, it typically takes an author willing to push the boundaries out and try something different to stand on their own. John French delivers that here, graduating from the character-in-hiding portrait we saw with Ahriman: Exile into a kind of heist story. Weaving in the tragedy of the Thousand Sons and the perils of hubris, its treatment of the Chaos legions as multidimensional beings instead of simple villains has set it apart as something truly special on its own."

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "The definitive 'fall to Chaos' story of the setting, Rob Sanders penned the foundational origin story for Fantasy' preeminent villain. Deviating from being a mere caricature of evil, Sanders' tale has drawn acclaim for its more theological approach to Archaeon's gradual corruption. Despite a somewhat formulaic, quest-centered narrative arc, it's the depth of character- of this character in particular- that sees it ensconced in the Hall.

Jay, Tabletop Battles and Warhammer Wordforge: "The End Times was almost certainly the most controversial publishing event of the Black Library in its history, bringing a swift- and often abrupt- ending to a very storied property. Like a diamond formed from pressurized coal, a few exceptional stories managed to emerge from the ruin and wreckage. Chris Wraight's The Fall of Altdorf was one of the very best of the series, filled with the tragedy and despair that came from the ill-fated last stand of the Empire. Historically and aesthetically, this one's a lock."

Michael, Track of Words: "A grand, epic story, even without looking at wider events. As the forces of Chaos move through the Empire we see through the eyes of characters from both sides, some old favourites and some new introductions. In a clear sign of the changing face of Warhammer, this book very much looks at unexpected alliances, changing power structures and events with truly apocalyptic consequences; even the most powerful or well-loved characters are at risk. Wraight is clearly a safe pair of hands when it comes to this kind of story, not just because of his experience writing for the Empire but also his ability to draw strong, nuanced characters, and pull together a wide range of plot strands."

Michael, Track of Words: "The problem of scale notwithstanding, there is plenty to enjoy here. Reynolds sets the story up well before keeping events ticking along at a good pace, rotating chapters for each viewpoint character and gradually bringing (more or less) everything together towards the end for a satisfying finale. This kind of tie-in story runs the risk of being predictable, but while there is a certain sense of inevitability (given its title, and the fact that this is the End Times), there is enough plotting, backstabbing and infighting among the assembled undead to keep things interesting."

AJ, The Paladin Journal: "There are novels in the Horus Heresy series that have absolutely surpassed all expectations for me. This holds especially true for legions which had been out of the limelight by the time that this novel was released, with only a novella to tide us over. By the Emperor, what a novel though. Charting the absolute schism within the legion brought about by Horus’ civil war, it is undoubtedly one of the best in the series and it placed Chris Wraight firmly on the map as a masterful storyteller. He gave the White Scars everything they needed to be an absolute force and rewarded the fans with great characterizations, great legion culture, and a superb heartbreak story that stands out from all the other legion schism stories we’ve already seen."

Michael, Track of Words: "It’s a masterclass in character building as Wraight single-handedly turns the Scars from an unknown quantity into a legion with both a well-developed identity and a fascinating role to play in upcoming events. Their headstrong, elusive nature is what makes them so interesting, setting them apart from the other more clearly-defined legions and giving them a sense of ambiguity previously reserved for the Alpha Legion or Thousand Sons.

"It’s not just a novel-length origin story, mind – this is a beautifully plotted book full of tension and very much aware of its place within the wider series, the various strands and plot arcs all leading up to a single point which will determine the direction of the White Scars and set other events properly in motion. It’s impressive stuff, comfortably in the top tier of Heresy novels."

Aasa, The Warhammer Scholar: "Talon of Horus is the culmination for the work Aaron Dembski-Bowden has put into making previously vague Legion Wars into exciting postbellum epoch. What was once shattered warbands are now close to unifying into the Black Legion we know (and love!) as Horus and his Ezekarion plot their way out of the Warp. This series is fundamentally about Iskandar's journey of finding purpose after the Heresy, and he reaches his zenith as the Legion faces old enemies at the edge of Warp. The Long War has begun, as well as the Long Wait for ADB and Black Library continuing this exciting series."

Michael, Track of Words: "This is quite simply a book which deserves to be relished, to be savoured and enjoyed. Dembski-Bowden fills it with such life and detail, with tantalising links to the past and future of these characters, and the events that play such vital parts in the wider history. In time this will surely be looked back on as a crucial, seminal book for the 40k setting. As a standalone book this is without a doubt up there with the best that Black Library has ever published."

AJ, The Paladin Journal: "The post-Scouring era of the Warhammer 40k timeline is one of the most underutilized settings in the lore. We know precious little about it other than a few large brushstrokes. And so, to get a novel that charts the real origins of the Black Legion, the greatest villain faction in Warhammer 40k, is something truly special. What makes it even better is that Aaron Dembski-Bowden absolutely delivers on that promise.

"This era of his work for Black Library definitely has to be a golden time. The highlights are definitely the introspections by Abaddon and his cohorts, as well as that spectacular climax which took space marine aura-farming to a whole new level and delivers one of the most fascinating duel scenes in all of Warhammer. This novel is “all gas, no brakes” start to finish and sets up a fantastic beginning of a new trilogy."

Congratulations to all the worthy winners, and a very special welcome to first-timer Rob Sanders!
