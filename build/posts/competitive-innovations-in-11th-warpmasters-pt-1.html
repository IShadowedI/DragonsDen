Competitive Innovations in 11th: Warpmasters pt.1

August 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

It's our biggest week of 11th Edition yet, featuring both the Warmasters GT and a GW Open in the UK, plus plenty more besides. It was also the World Team Championships at the weekend, and we're going to have a bonus part covering that next monday (24th August) because there's too much other stuff going on to slot it in during the week.

In terms of the churning metagame, Orks are still on top, but Dark Angels are even closer behind them (really only a hair separates them), and the WTC has unleashed a powerful new force onto the tabletops in the form of Daemons, with Legion of Excess proving to be a serious sleeper that's been fully unleashed as of Warmasters. Also, when Saturday finally rolls around I get to put myself in the newspaper (which I always like to do), but you'll have to wait for that, as the event-sorting algorithm has spoken. Archetype

Allarus and Vendread Wall

Thoughts

The continued rise of Allarus-centric Custodes builds goes all the way to the top of Warmasters here, with the relatively melee-centric field among the best of the best not being able to crack through. Unlike some variants of this list, Folger actually has brought some Dreadnoughts along, rather than Might just providing a Disposition. They're a pretty nifty addition, all things considered - if a foe wants to reliably put one down, they have to come fight up close and personal (otherwise the dread just stands back up), providing the window for the Allarus to get stuck in on their terms. Valerian's squad is also a neat pick for the metagame - they'll stand up very nicely to Orks and some flavour of Daemons, and can thwart Marines relying on Thunder Hammers as well, giving this list the flexibility it needs to tank against everything. Congratulations to Folger on triumphing in what is always one of the two hardest fields of the year (alongside the WCW).

Archetype

Blood Legion Ultra-Pressure

Thoughts

In second place, we have our first Daemons build, a spicy and relatively unique setup that David has cooked up as part of Team New Zealand. This build is designed to absolutely steamroller foes, and pick up really big wins in teams. Blood Legion makes even the act of getting near to this force a high-risk activity, and Fool's Flight makes it extremely hard for foes to get out from under the gun once battle has joined. There's also really good synergy between the Brazenmaw enhancement and From Beyond the Veil, as foes are going to have to worry about both turn 1 (essentially) Rapid Ingress and the threat of a turn 1 Charge from reserves with +2" to the roll, at which point nothing is really safe. Many Purge missions get broken wide open if the opponent is penned into their deployment zone on their second turn, and this build seriously threatens to do that, helping swing for the really big wins that not all Purge builds can manage. It also appears to be extremely good in singles, and it's something foes will be far less ready for than some of the other Daemon setups. Congratulations to David on second place.

Archetype

Slaanesh Board Control

Thoughts

In third place, we have a representative of the much more widely used breakout Daemons build, which is Warptide/Legion of Excess. This list is incredibly good at controlling the table - it has plenty of units, they're very fast, and some of them are hyper-deadly. In particular, Fiends do incredibly well out of the improvements to Legion of Excess in 11th, as it means if you charge with just them you can go full murder mode at no real cost, they're hard to concentrate a counterpunch on thanks to Hidden, you can stack Cover and -1 to Hit on them, and you can shrug off what damage does break through with Thieves of Pain, which is extra supported here with double Epitome. Daemonettes are also one of the premium Priority Assets units out there, as the combination of speed, cheapness, OC2, and a chance to punish foes that try to clear them out (via their Battle-shock effect) is all you can really ask for. This list is rounded out by the Masque, providing an extremely tiny unit for emergency scoring, or another way to tilt the scales mid-board. This list rules, and plays a fantastic range of mind games with foes trying to pin it down, well done Alban!

Archetype

Slaanesh Board Control

Thoughts

More Slaanesh Daemons in fourth place, this time going for a second Keeper in place of some Fiends and a second Epitome. This gives the list another hammer that it can use to go all-in when a go turn is required, and two keepers and some fiends can team up to present the opponent with a whole wall of -1 to hit pieces. A single-model unit (even a large on) also helps the list play Recon, which Liam has selected over Priority Assets. Otherwise, very similar things going on here - this build is just plain vicious and very hard to take down. Well done Liam!

Archetype

Crisis and Broadside Retaliation

Thoughts

After a few weeks in the doldrums, T'au have had a massive surge this week, and it seems to be down to a critical mass of players putting down the Triptide comfort blanket and embracing Crisis thought. Crisis teams are all extremely good at their designated jobs, and so in a metagame where the top builds have very different defensive setups, they're a really solid choice to build around. Retaliation Cadre obviously pushes that even further via granting access to a 6" Ingress and Torchstar, allowing you to either reach out and punish foes who can't maintain a 6" screen, or keep whichever unit is most precious for the matchup alive and kicking deep into the game. Between double Starscythe for hordes and plasma and fusion crisis teams (plus four Railsides) for heavy threats, this build really can blow up anything, something the more uniform output of Riptides sometimes misses on. Actual Honest to God Brood Brothers as I Live and Breathe

Thoughts

Brood Brothers. Brood Brothers actually made a top four. I'm not even mad about having to look up what it does for the first time in nearly three years.

Honestly? The synergy here is genuinely kind of sweet. Xenocult has been seeing plenty of play alongside Broodsurge because it gives you the tools you need to set up a few really nasty Neophyte units to cover off shooting support, but there's actually very good support for them from Brood Brothers as well. Hellhounds let them set up Ignores Cover, +1 to hit is (obviously) good for really making the Neophyte swings properly land. You also get solid protection from enemy reserves plans via A Dark Network (and there's lots of +charge Terminators in the metagame right now). Extra punch-up capability from Kasrkin certainly doesn't go amiss either, and what brings the plan together is that the cheap Militarum transports you want for them combine with general Genestealer shenanigans to really lock in Recon scoring. If there's a better example of how much the 2DP+1DP options have opened up list design, I can't think of it, huge props to John.

Thoughts

Two factions that are somewhat on the ropes both make it to the top table with expert pilots at the helm, so this should be a good one. Both armies are playing Priority Assets, so the goal here is Sabotage - Actions on objectives, with the unusually small central objective also creating pretty nasty killzones. Both lists are well equipped for that - Sisters have their revivable Characters, while the Aeldari can tearfully feed Warlocks out as required, and benefit from them being OC2, thus trumping solo Sisters pieces till murdered.

Both armies also have some assets that are especially helpful. On these maps, Psychic Shield on a big Dark Reaper unit is especially strong, providing a way of projecting force with minimal response, and being very hard to dodge with teleporting as an option. Being able to teleport the Fire Dragons is also very helpful, as it forces the Sisters to be cautious with Vahl or get her unit instantly blown up, and also makes a tank rush a risky move from the Sisters. They can't be everywhere though, and the crunchier setup from the Sisters side could provide an angle to just bully the table, especially as if the Eldar can only kill Vahl once in a turn, she'll get to go on a prolonged rampage.

What helps the Eldar on that front is that this is one of the missions with the most skewable scoring out there, and even if they get pressured for a few turns, methodically eliminating the foe will allow them to score big later on. That's especially true as either Lhykhis or a teleport can put them straight into position to take big 15VP turns as soon as it's safe. The final thing that tips me in their favour is how good Isha's Fury is in this game - it means that the solo Sisters Characters can lose their first life just as a cost of pushing up to take a point from a Warlock that's about to get shot. That throws off their resource planning, and further helps the Aeldari plan for a big late game swing. My guess with the scoreline here is that both armies skewed their scoring - I'd assume Sisters took early points, and then the Aeldari scored explosively from turn three onwards to close out the game.

Thoughts

Matt breaks through the curse that currently weighs heavy on the Aeldari, bringing three ultra-high threat units to smash the foe, and enough support around them to surgically eliminate key threats (via Jain Zar and Lhykhis) and score Priority Assets (Warlocks and Scorpions). Path of the Outcast provides some key support for the Maugan/Reaper squad (probably Aeldari's best ranged hammer right now), as being able to bump detection range up to 21" lets them hang far enough back that Psychic Shield can keep them safe. Warlocks that have ranged out to score PA can also try and pick up some value on their way out of this life by using Presentiment of Dread to try and knock out some enemy scoring. The threat of teleporting the big Fuegan squad is also higher than ever in 11th, because you can drop a number of Dragons within 9" melta range, so goodbye to any defiler that's ranged too far out. Finally, when the army is ready to consolidate its position, the Overwatch threat from Eldrad's unit can be enough to hold the line, especially against hordes. Well done to Farseer Matt for finding a way to thwart the mon-keigh.
