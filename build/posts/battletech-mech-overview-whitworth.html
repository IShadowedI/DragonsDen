BattleTech Mech Overview: Whitworth

September 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Some questions echo down the centuries, posed again and again until their elusive answers pass into legend. How much wood could a woodchuck chuck if a woodchuck could chuck wood? The world may never know! We certainly won't find the answer to that puzzler today. Blake willing, however, we may at least answer a parallel riddle: How much worth would a Whitworth have if a Whitworth it had worth?

That's right, today we're taking a look at the 40-ton vending machine for missiles known as the Whitworth!

Whitworth. credit: porble

It's easy for a 'Mech to feel unappreciated when "Worthless" is one of its in-universe nicknames, but despite a host of flaws both in and out of the BattleTech universe, nevertheless the Whitworth has persisted through the centuries, from the Age of War to the modern era, even inspiring the (potentially-ComStar-funded) establishment of a company dedicated to nothing but spare parts and upgrade kits for the venerable chassis, "Whitworth Specialty Manufacturing"! Let's see how it holds up against the ol' TTB rating system!

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘Mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

Whitworth. Credit: Rockfish

WTH-0

This isn't actually the original Whitworth, but rather the Limited Edition Extra Warcrimes version built for the Amaris Dragoons, but it's nevertheless strongly representative of the short-range members of the Whitworth lineage: Pantherlike 4/6/4 movement profile, 93% armor coverage doing the best it can, SRM 6 in each side torso, energy weapon in each arm in place of a hand, third energy weapon in the head. The only thing that sets this apart from the earlier-in-universe WTH-1S is that the left arm weapon is a (sinister!) flamer rather than a third medium laser.

The tiny engine on this mech has at least one positive knock-on effect, with eight of its 14 single heat sinks visible on the crit chart. Given three sinks padding each side torso and two in the CT, this mech is a lot less likely to explode than you'd expect from a slow, ammo-dependent 40-tonner without CASE. The Whitworth's legs, too, while a liability in canon or when playing with quirks, are padded out by the jump jets, helping insulate a little against mobility crits.

When judged by points cost (a low, low 863 BV) against its own contemporaries (the Trebuchet 5S, Hunchback 4SP, and Javelin 10N), the WTH-0 looks a little better than I expected, but when the timeline opens up, the Howler 6, Javelin 12N, Ion Sparrow B, and the like really do make it into an obsolete joke. It's just paddling uphill pairing this movement profile and armor layout with 3/6/9 weapons.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

WTH-1

The poster-child Whitworth, the WTH-1 is the result of the Star League saying "Hey, our Whitworth pilots keep getting killed trying to get close enough to use their SRMs?" Ten heat sinks (still with two padding each side torso), three Medium Lasers, same everything-else as the -0 or the -1S, but the missile launchers swap for two LRM 10s, still with a ton of ammo each. Neutral firing the LRMs at a run, builds one heat firing the lasers at a run; don't shoot everything at once please. 982 BV.

I've historically thought about the WTH-1 as "the bad Trebuchet 5N," but on further reflection that isn't really fair. Yes, it has noticeably weaker LRMs, and yes, it has lower ground speed, but the JJs genuinely are useful for getting into a good fire support position, the armor outside the CT is actually better, it doesn't have an unprotected ammo bomb like the 5N does, the backup lasers are almost exactly the same, and it's 209 BV cheaper. I'd also say it compares favorably to the average Valkyrie. None of this necessarily makes it good... mechs dependent on unenhanced Inner Sphere LRMs for value are also paddling upstream... but it's cheap enough I'd say it at least achieves "not-bad"!

Lynn's Rating: C.

WTH-1H

Where walks a missile mech, a funny Marian variant covered in rocket launchers will follow.

This is exactly a 1S (the 0, but with three MLs, no flamer), but with the right torso SRM 6 and its accompanying ammo dropped for EIGHT Rocket Launcher 10s. This has a higher BV cost than the SRM, going up to 993, but I'm going to surprise myself by saying that it might be worth it. We're still just under 1k, the rocket launchers provide something approaching "stand-off capability" for a mech otherwise limited to 3/6/9 range, and there are some coherent firing patterns here. The 1H can fire off four of the RL 10s heat-neutrally at a run, and the three lasers plus the SRM build +1 at a run, so firing half the rockets, the other half of the rockets, and then all the close range weapons across three turns while closing distance seems... plausible. You're still not particularly likely to hit shit with the rocket launchers, but if your dice spike in the right direction it would be, as Liberty might say, "mad funny."

Lynn's Rating: C-???

WTH-1S

The actual original Whitworth in-universe, because BattleTech's alphanumeric codes make no sense, the 1S is exactly a WTH-0 except with a third ML it doesn't have the heat capacity to fire elegantly jacking its BV up to 917. By every metric other than availability, I'd rather have the 0.

Lynn's Rating: D.

Whitworth. Credit: Rockfish

WTH-2

A Helm Renaissance refit, the 2 here is one of the least ambitious upgrades I've ever seen, dropping both arm lasers for Artemis IV on both LRMs. A side-grade at best, this increases the damage output of the LRMs while cutting the 2's close-range output by two-thirds. This ends up being something of a "win-more" Whitworth; more valuable when things are going to plan and it can hold back at long range, but nearly useless in the face of shit hitting the fan. At 936 BV it might fit into a few lists the WTH-1 wouldn't, though?

Lynn's Rating: C-.

WTH-2A

This is the first Whitworth to use any form of weight-savings tech! It was introduced in 3068!

Took 'em a minute, eh?

The 2A has basically nothing at all to do with the 2, aside from the same ol' engine, JJs, and armor layout that all the Whitworths up to this point have sported. It swaps to an Endo-Steel frame, mounts up ten double heat sinks, maintains the traditional arm medium lasers but drops the head gun down to a small laser, mounts a quartet of Streak SRM 4s with a single ton of ammo in the CASE'd right torso, and carries a C3S. It builds +1 heat on a running alpha strike if all the Streaks fire. 1,055 BV.

This is still slow, short-ranged, and kinda squishy, but there's a serious amount of damage potential here, and delivering most of this payload accurately at a range of 9 hexes sounds like a pretty funny payoff for a C3 network.

Lynn's Rating: C, maybe C+ in a C3 net.

4th Deneb Light Cavalry Whitworth. Credit: Valk

WTH-2H

A weirdo RecGuide take on the OG Whitworth loadout, the 2H sticks with a Standard Engine but shifts to a higher rating, moving a sprightly-for-a-Whitworth 5/8/4. The arm lasers shift to ERMLs, while the head laser is still a regular ML. The missile launchers shift to lighter MRM 10s with Apollo FCS, with their two tons of ammo in the CASE II-protected left torso, and the armor shifts to Heavy Ferro-Fibrous... so it can maintain the exact same coverage as every other Whitworth up to this point. With ten doubles for heat sinks, this builds 1+ Movement heat on an alpha strike. 1,148 BV.

This is interesting. It would benefit from having a C3S like most of the other Jihad-or-later Whitworths, but the Core Rules MRM changes are good for it... firing Saturation Mode with 10-tube launchers is probably a fool's errand, but just the ability to use them as normal weapons without any special maluses is helpful. Even the mix of ER and regular lasers kinda makes sense here, not just for heat reasons, but in mirroring the MRM's range bands: the ERMLs hit the same 8-hex medium range, while the regular ML has the same 3-hex short range. I kinda wish it didn't have the short jump range, but they're only costing it about 90 BV and are still situationally handy.

At the end of the day I think the WTH-2H's biggest problem is that 5/8/4 is still slow for a 40-tonner, and that feels extremely fixable. While I do appreciate a SFE as a durability add, with an XL engine this same exact mech could go 7/11/0 with full armor and a C3S and the BV cost would only go to 1,199! That mech might excite me; as-is, the 2H seems usable, but perhaps a bigger jump in BV than its actual jump in capability would warrant.

Lynn's Rating: C.

WTH-3

This is the endo-steel skeleton of a 2A, but with ten single heat sinks, four LRM 5s with four tons of ammo, a C3S, and a full load of Improved Jump Jets taking its movement to 4/6/6. At 861 BV it's the BV-cheapest Whitworth.

This mech is badly impacted by the new Core Rules not allowing LRM 5s to deliver minefields or smoke. The four tons of ammo here are begging to be a toolbox, but a 50/50 mix of Narc-enabled and semi-guided is about the best you can manage.

"Cheap, mobile enough, and able to annoy people from range" kinda takes this into Liberty's "Little Bastard" zone, but I'm not particularly impressed, especially given that it's all-but-helpless against anything which won't allow it to stay at range.

Lynn's Rating: C-.

Lyran Commonwealth Whitworth. Credit: SRM

WTH-5S

Finally living up to the promise of "What The Hell," this experimental Lyran (provincial) variant, which never saw regular production, has a lot going on. It buys a good bit of tonnage by swapping to an XL engine without increasing its rating and also moving to Composite Structure (oof), then spends that mounting an MXPL and AES in each arm, Streak SRM 6s in each side torso (with one ton of ammo in a CASE II-protected bin), a Guardian ECM in the CT, and a Supercharger. It builds movement heat on an Alpha if both Streaks lock. It's the most BV-expensive Whitworth at 1,230.

This feels utterly cursed. MXPLs with AES are a good time, and I always like a good Streak launcher, but the Composite Structure is absolutely the wrong way to save weight, and the Supercharger is absolutely the wrong way to increase speed.

No, bad, no fancy tech toys for you, Supervising Technician Eric Stillman of the Spillman Provincial Refit Facility on Acrux!

Lynn's Rating: D--.

WTH-K

Another endo-steel variant like the 2A and the 3, the WTH-K scrapes the edge of greatness (or at least goodness) like a semi trailer scraping under a can-opener bridge. It has ERMLs for the arm lasers. C3S in the head. MML 7s in each side torso; two tons of ammo total don't give much wiggle room, but at least let it carry the basics. It curiously mounts five IJJs for a 4/6/5 movement profile, deeply weird, but it hits a TMM breakpoint, so it's not the worst.

I love MMLs, and even though the K lacks CASE, this seems like a good loadout! There's just one teeeeeeensy little problem.

This builds 18+Movement heat on an Alpha Strike, perfect for base load of Double Heat Sinks!

Naturally, of course, it mounts ten Singles instead!

There are perfectly fine firing patterns here, but they're a shadow of what might have been. Basically, the K is going to fire LRMs until it hits minimum range, then swap to the ERMLs, then swap to the SRMs when it hits three hexes. Sure, entirely rational bracket firing. Except the Whitworth could do better damage than that at SRM range back when it was using IntroTech!

This just makes my heart sad, such wasted potential. It is at least cheap at 958 BV, but I can't even call it best-in-class of C3-enabled 40-tonners with Standard Engines and double MMLs, not while the Battle Cobra BTL-C-2OE exists!

Lynn's Rating: D+.

Hastati Sentinels Whitworth. Credit: Jack Hunter

Final Thoughts

Wow. I really can't say I've seen another Spheroid mech that's been around for as long as the Whitworth which comes this close to being an OmniMech. The armor layout never changes. The engine almost never changes. The inner structure does, but outside the experimental 5S it only comes in Standard and IS Endo-Steel flavors. It never gives up jump jets completely. Very consistent, very mediocre, elevated to usability mainly because it's also very cheap.

I guess that's the final verdict: Whitworth, Worthless? No, Worth Less!
