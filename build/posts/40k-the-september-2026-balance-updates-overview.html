[40K] The September 2026 Balance Updates: Overview

September 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The September Balance Update arrives, bringing us the last of three promised monthly updates, and setting the stage for the next three months of 40k. It includes big changes for various loyalist and traitor Marine factions, and also the regular round of updates for everyone else. This article provides an overview of the update.

Third time's the charm, as the saying goes. It's time for another balance update, this one the last of the three that have been scheduled monthly to ensure 11th Edition gets a healthy start in the world. This means that after this, we're locked in on the current rules (excepting new books) for the next three months, covering many of the biggest event's of the year, and the World Championships. It's a big one, both in impact and importance, so let's take a look.

The Review

As always, we've split this across separate articles for the game's superfactions, which you can check out below:

  • Chaos
  • Imperium
  • Xenos

No article for Loyalist Space Marines this time around, as they aren't changed (as far as we know) from their Codex (which you can see our coverage of here).

For more traitorous or esoteric Marines, however, this update has more to offer, and we have even more articles for you. The biggest change in this update is that all Marine factions get the new T5 and S5 profiles, and this also extends to Custodes bolt weaponry. You can find detailed writeups for these factions below:

  • Chaos Space Marines
  • Death Guard
  • Emperor's Children
  • Thousand Sons
  • World Eaters
  • Grey Knights
  • Adeptus Custodes

The Video

Campbell's done a video version looking at the changes if you prefer your article to be moving, with sound.

The Headlines

What's the hot scoop then?

All Marines Get the New Profiles

Seven of the game's factions get changes reflecting the new normal for Space Marines. This has...mixed results. Some of them do very well, others probably pay a little much for the boosts, and find themselves a little threadbare on the table. We've covered this in detail in the individual pieces above, especially for Chaos where the biggest changes happen.

This obviously does have an impact on the wider game too, and the key thing is that anything that's S/T4 is now meaningfully worse, and S/T5 matters less than it used to. It has the weird side effect that there really aren't any "T4 factions" left in the game now, which throws off all kinds of maths and assumptions about lethality.

3DP Detachments Get Multiple Dispositions

Following what we've seen for the new Marine and Ork Codexes, all Detachments that are 3DP now get to pick between two Dispositions rather than just one. This has widely varying impact - for some it's "huh, neat", some get offered Disruption and wonder if they're a joke to you, and others are rubbing their hands (or in the case of Tyranids, claws) together in glee. In general, this makes 3DP Detachments a better prospect, and it's a good change.

There are also some bigger swings on DP changes this time around, with a few long-time 3DP options going to 2DP, and our first 2DP 3-stratagem Detachments and 1DP 6-strats.

Orks Get Rebalanced Towards Melee

Ork shooting has landed very hot, probably too hot. The biggest offender (the already notorious Big Mek Dakkarig) gets some especially big point hikes, and in general the best shooting toys get toned down a bit.

Happily, it isn't just nerfs for the Orks. Where shooting has overperformed, traditional krumpin' with da personal touch has merely done OK, but not phenomenally, and lots of Ork melee units get a few points shaved off to make them a more appealing prospect.

Rules Changes

Wings: Now that these are in the App we don't get early sight of them. So - there probably are some, and I assume one of my esteemed colleagues will pick up the baton and edit them in here later on.

Overwatch No Longer Scores Critical Hits

TheChirurgeon: Hey it's the first of those changes we didn't get early access to. Now under Snap Shots is a new rider: The 6s you roll to hit on your Snap Shooting no longer count as critical hits, meaning they won't trigger [LETHAL HITS] or [SUSTAINED HITS]. That's a massive change, and leaves us honestly wondering what the hell they think Overwatch is supposed to do now - it seems like it's purely something you'll either hope to get off a single lucky hit with, or only for units with [TORRENT] weapons.

This is a massive blow to factions which rely on a large volume of non-Torrent firepower with Lethal Hits and/or Sustained, and by my reckoning primarily hurts the Astra Militarum (where Combined Arms gives LETHAL HITS to all of your units against certain targets), Chaos Space Marines (who can get either ability whenever they shoot), Emperor's Children Defilers, and Orks (Flash Gitz seem a lot less scary now).

The Impact

Wings: I'll say that this is a bold update to throw out just before the biggest gap we've yet had in 11th, but there's certainly tonnes to work with here. A lot of factions get pretty sensible changes, often just a small but very meaningful tweak, and a lot of these feel very well-considered. I worry a little about Death Guard and AdMech getting some aggressive buffs, since both have been on the upswing in the recent past, but for AdMech at least it's mitigated by Lords of the Forge going on the 2DP naughty step (though don't worry robot fans, Cohort goes down to 1DP so you can still combo with that). I do, however, like the Ork changes a lot - feels like a much better nuanced set of changes than the knee jerks we've sometimes had in the past, so good job there.

I think the only serious concern I have is around Grey Knights and Thousand Sons, who both have a very rough time on it. Both pay a premium for their updates, and neither really had the unit count to absorb it, nor benefits enough to justify the cost. A bit of a shame, and if there are any fine tunings made in the shorter term, I'd guess these will be where (plus anything riotously broken coming out of Codex Marines).

I think the biggest upside here and a good thing to close on is the change to 3DP Detachments having two Disposition options. This is exactly what we want to see before going into a period of stability, because it really broadens what some factions can work with, and rescues a few fan-favourite options like Subterranean Assault from Disruption prison.

TheChirurgeon: There's simultaneously a lot going on here and not enough. They took some big swings with Detachments, finally giving us good Purge the Foe options again and making some surprising drops for a few 3 DP options, but at the same time a lot of these points updates feel either nonsensical or too slight. Moving a unit's cost by 5 points rarely matters unless it's part of a number of changes for very specific factions with small unit counts, i.e. Emperor's Children, and hits to Grey Knights just feel like kicking a faction when it's down. I think the Adeptus Mechanicus make out very well here and the point drops to Death Guard make me believe that they can absolutely make a come back now just on the sheer volume of units they can field. I think there are interesting builds with World Eaters now that they've fixed Berzerkers and changed Berzerker Warband, but I'm less convinced they'll be good after the point increases to Terminators.

The big losers here are Grey Knights and Thousand Sons, who both eat shit this time around with big point increases to key units and little else to show for it. I think the Emperor's Children hits also hurt more than they help, but it's a marginal loss for them and similarly I think Chaos Space Marines armies won't love this update either when they realize that Chosen and Warp Talons swinging at S5 just don't do what they need to against marines. The Ork changes feel less like a kick in the teeth or fiddling around in the margins and more like a measured response, which is good to see.

Make sure to check out the rest of our review of the update from the links at the top, and happy gaming!
