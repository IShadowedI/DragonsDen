Magic: The Gathering Mystery Booster: Commander Edition Review, Part 1 of 2 – Multicolor Cards

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Magic’s depths know no end, and Mystery Booster: Commander Edition is nothing but the deepest of cuts. Whether torn from the annals of history, video games older than multiple Tabletop Battles site authors, or offhand flavor text, this set is one for Magic’s most entrenched players. It introduces new ways to bend the rules of Commander, via the Rulebreaker mechanic, but even those cards without that exact line still do so in spirit; this is a weird set, and we’re all thrilled to dive into it. This week, we’re covering the set’s Multicolored & Colorless options, from a Commander which lets you play a 10,000 card deck, to Magic’s first true non-permanent Commander.

Mystery Booster: Commander Edition officially releases on November 9th via Festival in a Box, but is exclusively available until then at official Magiccons & Commandfests. Actually acquiring these cards isn’t likely to be easy, especially until November, but in theory you could play against them right now at your LGS, hence our coverage.

Multicolor

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Venser, Visionary Traveler

Saffgor: I’ve already covered Venser in a Puzzlebox, but I think he’s more of an aristocrats Commander than a blink one. Obvious loops with Persist, Guardian of the Forgotten, and some cool tech like Reweaveing into Ugin, Eye of the Storms has the makings of a very nifty deck. Folks have written him off as a worse blink Commander than existing options, and they’re right to do so, but peer beyond the veil and you’ve got something wildly unique. The hidden gem of the Multicolor cards here.

BPhillipYork: Given how many things key off power or toughness, or use counters to do random stuff, the built in "passive" ability here is pretty easy to abuse with various creatures. Combined with the the ability to flicker creatures effectively lets you set up combos. The obvious one is Sage of Hours and The Ozolith and resourceful defense. Flickering creatures to move counters onto the sage will let you take infinite turns pretty easily. But there's plenty of other ways to get enormous value out of flickering things.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Whtz, the Bibliophile

Saffgor: A cool card in theory, that’s for sure. In practice, however, a Whtz deck loses focus fast, and descends into becoming a goodstuff 250+ card pile, an Azorius slurry buoyed by having access to Yorion as a companion. For all the people excited to build this and Battle of Wits, I ask…why? The payoff is winning the game with a meandering upkeep trigger, on an expensive card telegraphed by virtue of your deck being stored in a shoebox. Whtz isn’t clever, it isn’t nuanced. It’s goodstuff with a win condition that rewards you for playing goodstuff, in sufficient quantity, and annoys your opponents when you’re forced to shuffle.

BPhillipYork: This is already spawning huge decks, a 200 card deck isn't so bad, and conceivably of course you could just run 100 persistent petitioners and automill people out. The practicalities of a 200 card deck though kind of make me frustrated. It's almost performative, especially if the person has to shuffle a lot, it's just going to be a painful Mr. Bean sort of sketch where you sit and watch the person. And I don't like decks that create that environment, which is basically, hey, watch me jerk off. It's almost implicitly a rule 0 violation, which fundamentally should start with 'let everyone have fun.'

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Weaver King

Saffgor: A bit on the slow side, but it’s cool to see an Afterlife from the Loam as a combat damage trigger. Seems like a blast in Bracket 2, so long as everyone’s battlecruiser-ing it up.

BPhillipYork: Well, slamming into people, force milling them, and then getting those creatures isn't particularly strong given it costs 6 mana. However it's pretty consistent, and if you run a bunch of other mill and then get that to trigger off first so you get to look through a lot of cards to see what you want, it's fairly powerful. If you combine it with some early game stax and control it's probably a pretty powerful deck vs creature heavy archetypes.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Sifa Grent

Saffgor: Blood Artist as a static ability, and the capacity to make your opponents fight? Smells like a fun casual politics Commander, and as an added bonus, quite enjoys Crime triggers. Like all Planeswalker Commanders with access to Red, she can be Reality Scrambled into a different walker of your choice, so be aware of that as an option, when thinking on practical win conditions.

BPhillipYork: This is the kind of planeswalker that has a powerful ability that seems like it's just going to get her killed unless you have a really good way of protecting her.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ashaya’s Enduring Bond

Saffgor: The first non-permanent Commander, cool! I definitely want to cover the Bond in greater detail, but I’d position this card as a Bard Class ‘Secret Commander’, with a ton of {R}{G}-cost Legends that are free under the class, and enable you to cast this Legendary Sorcery. I don’t think this is a powerful Commander, but it absolutely gets the gears turning.

BPhillipYork: I like the random aspect of running some kind of mana power up deck that just casts this over and over. I really quite like this mechanic where paying more commander tax actually nets you something. I'm sad that WotC doesn't seem to return to it often, but building around a sorcery is totally a fun new deckbuilding challenge.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Grizzlegom, Hurloon Hero

Saffgor: For all of the color identity rulebreakers, you need to ask yourself “Would I play this if it were just printed as 5C?”, because in essence that’s what they allow, to a lesser extent. For Grizzlegom, I think the answer is a strong maybe, because for what he lacks in the capacity to come down early, he makes up for in one huge swing. There’s a few ways to make your Lands all basic types, and if you scrape away the text from this card and just let those abilities count Lands, period, attacking with Grizzlegom is akin to casting an Ultimatum. Weird card, fairly obvious, but a Timmy slam dunk.

BPhillipYork: This is okay, but it just sort of reeks of running an insanely expensive mana base if you don't proxy cards, and then just running every dual-typed land you can and then running maybe an ETB deck. Funny that the mountain and swamp abilities are basically the worst of them.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Elda, Conjuror of Spectacle

Saffgor: Bet you didn’t expect her to be seeing cEDH experimentation. Yes, Elda is in discussions as a tournament cEDH option, for her capacity to force draws with Charismatic Conqueror, and spread hatebears across the board like Collector Ouphe. Getting rid of one Drannith Magistrate is easy, but what about three? She gets markedly worse if draws stop counting for points in tournament play, but as it stands she’s essentially taken the spot of GW Selvala as the ‘wait, she does what?’ Selesnya Commander of the format.

BPhillipYork: I'm sure there's some kind of fun and sort of fair way to play this card, though to me it seems like it'll get abused by people basically just handing their friend the win, which I really don't like. There is a lot of synergy with group hug type creature cards and then you'll be drawing a lot. If all your opponents are drawing extra cards which causes you to draw extra cards, and your deck is like, 40% ramp by volume then you're probably the only one who can really take advantage of it. But this feels like it might lead to a lot of unfun games.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Selenia, the Cursed Heart

Saffgor: Very unhinged rattlesnake effect on this card, sheesh. Don’t make the Selenia player angry, or you’ll be booted from the game at Mach 5; I do think she’s a bit costly to be a real threat, but the political chit of doling out a Curse as strong as hers is very compelling.

BPhillipYork: I think the lifegain is pretty gross potentially, there's a couple of these doublers, actually 6 total, so you can get insane life totals, but then what do you do with that. Aside from just lasering people with Aetherflux Reservoir there's not a ton of things, still having this ability in the Command Zone is potentially pretty good, and then having a threat from killing your commander is an interesting effect.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Itazura, Lingering Wick

Saffgor: Room 8 Studio, the folks behind this card’s art, has a history of implementing GenAI in their workflow. Make of that what you will. Itazura is weird and gimmicky, but this is much like the one ghastly Gruul Toph in being a card I just can’t support. There’s been no statement, but if it quacks like a duck…

BPhillipYork: I hate this. It turns every upkeep into a guessing game while people figure out the "meta" of what damage to take, but then there's every incentive to fill your deck with garbage. Wheel of Misfortune takes long enough to resolve, I'd hate having to do it every turn.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Davvol, Evincar of Rath

Saffgor: This isn’t exactly Carnival of Souls, as it only checks your board, but this is plenty neat regardless. There’s a fair few combos available to Davvol, but even from a fair angle he’s akin to a Black Birgi in the Command Zone, and that’s sure to enable some fun pop-off turns. Happy birthday buddy!

BPhillipYork: So this is obviously kind of a combo card. Black and Green gives you access to a fair amount of life gain to overcome this, and just sets you up to go off from the Command Zone with a massive enabler. Right off the bat with something like a Gravecrawler, a sac outlet, and a couple of lifegain triggers you go infinite, so you know, basic black building blocks, but that's largely all the deck can do.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Chief Magistrate of Mercadia

Saffgor: An incredibly cool card to see from a story perspective, but sadly not a strong one. You really want to be flashing this in to ensure the best version of its upkeep trigger, and that’s not the easiest in Boros.

BPhillipYork: Given that just copying creatures as tokens has become so prevalent this has the potentially to be a game winner, but you have to stay the monarch and he doesn't exactly enable that scenario. Feels like a commander that wins off of a Final Fortune, but that has a very laying it all out there feel.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Rikala, Homarid King

Saffgor: Another banger, good lord. Played fairly, Rikala gives you a modest board buff for your attackers, and a turn of High Tide, all thematically great and very much in like with the OG Homarid. Why bother playing fair though? Rikala is a premier Commander for non-combo counter shenanigans, providing potential for huge damage via your evasive creatures, wearing doubled counters well, and even rewarding you for playing a sufficient density of Stifles. No matter how unfair Rikala gets though, even if he’s providing a board +8/+0 and doubling your mana, he’s still…weirdly fair, in the grand scheme of things. Winner of a card, especially for those Simic-curious.

BPhillipYork: Fun to see a call back all the way to Forgotten Empires, which is sadly largely forgotten except the odd Thrull or Saproling creature. This is basically just Tidal Influence, the card, though definitely stronger. It would be pretty neat to see a bunch of Homarids get printed, especially if they all care about how many tide counters your permanents have.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Lyna, Veil of Vengeance

Saffgor: There are shades of Breena, the Demagogue at play here, helping Creatures from various players get in (or not be able to block). Lyna’s power really hinges on how combat-centric a given set of decks are, though, because on its own she’s paying quite the premium to dole out unblockable and draw a few cards. Perhaps she’s best in a stax shell, to make combat matter more? There’s something here, but in passive pods I doubt she gets enough value.

BPhillipYork: Yeah this is a way to make a basically non-interactive deck. Shadow creatures are fine, with access to all of Esper you get about 20 playable ones, slam into your opponents, drawing cards and dealing damage and then anthem-ing for a win. Another option of course is to use this to make creatures that you really don't want to connect just go right through with shadow. With the slow drip of shadow stuff there should be some way to punish shadow or protect yourself from it, and I'm not talking Circle of Protection: Shadow or Aether Web.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Madcap Jester

Saffgor: People have talked about this in a cEDH context, but the fact he himself only exiles on attack might make this jester a bit too funny to actually hit those heights. Still, the fact you can yoink combo pieces from an opponent via Bitter Ordeal and dedicate no room in your own deck to wincons is an upside! The bigger problem is that a Jester played in a pod without combos is going to feel middling at best, and as such this is likely to be a card with a skewed reputation.

BPhillipYork: Sort of a fun way to just have lots of ramp and interaction and then play off your opponents' decks.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Tresserhorn’s Lord, Returned

Saffgor: The Lord is very much like an inverted Beza, the Bounding Spring, asking you to fulfill three very Grixis conditions and play from ahead, as opposed to behind. More important, though, is the fact that you can finally get your friends to consider not playing Korvold. Look, drawing cards when sacrificing is fun and powerful, but Korvold homogenized the entire archetype under his devious talon. The Lord is probably the closest thing we’ve seen to a true competitor.

BPhillipYork: Fun call back to Legends, pretty playable and actually can get pretty gross if you set up the right conditions, though sort of just a Grixis version of the existing Jund Korvold, Fae-Cursed King which is already kind of an all-star commander.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Muckslinger Gang

Saffgor: This level of weirdness is exactly what I want to see from a set like this; at first the utility of the Gang’s abilities seems low, but between Flings, surviving damage-based wipes, fight spells, crewing/stationing, etc there’s a whole host of benefits to being +3/+3 bigger outside of combat. This is a brewer’s dream, although part of me worries it would lead to decks that ended up too Commander-reliant.

BPhillipYork: +3/+3 is nice but not so big really. Fun for like fighting type things and biting or whatever. Also, yeah you could do Fling type effects, but so what, and if your deck is built around that they'll just kill your commander I would assume. Probably the biggest is just surviving -X/-X board clears repeatedly, but as soon as you use one of those sorcery speed spells I think your commander will just bite it unless you've set up hexproof already.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Jandor, Fortuned Traveler

Saffgor: Wolf as a type to mount is a bit weird, but Jandor’s a great little buildaround for all the Mount-matters cards from Thunder Junction & Aetherdrift. For a pod that doesn’t intend to combo, dedicating an extra body to drawing a card, buffing an attacker, and making a Food is a pretty sweet deal, but don’t skimp on existing Mount options either!

BPhillipYork: This is fun, probably not good, throw in small creatures, any creatures that do helpful things when they tap (Magda, Brazen Outlaw is a perfect example and then just, whale away. If you can turn your foods into creatures that would be really funny, if not good. Sadly most of the really abusive things you can do with food are in black, so out of color identity, but there's plenty of "when you gain life" triggers in green and white, so probably just use them that way.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Feroz, Ulgrotha’s Warden

Saffgor: The most interesting thing Feroz does is create his signature Ban, and that’s not a terribly fun card to sit across from. Bant can then duplicate the token, protect the Bans, etc, but Feroz himself is too expensive and likely too slow to make it in B4. He’s a design without a home, in my eyes, and that’s a real shame.

BPhillipYork: There's enough planeswalker commander now that you could certainly have planeswalkers commander games, which maybe is the point, though the meta shifts dramatically if you do that. Suddenly you don't have to worry about your commander being trivially taken out easily, and lots of people may run mass board clears from damage, via spells like Earthquake that will leave the planeswalkers alone. This is a sort of weird, Bant goodstuff commander, though the idea of stacking up Feroz's Bans and shutting down creature decks is kind of amusing, especially with blue to return to hand.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Olag and Miau, New Friends

Saffgor: This is more a changeling deck than it is one for Zombies or Cats, but funny enough due to Amonkhet we have a weirdly high number of existing Zombie Cats (8, including this card). Still, that Throne of the God-Pharaoh effect for your relevant types will stack up in terms of damage pretty quickly, so I could see this working.

BPhillipYork: I don't know, cats and zombies, I guess that's thing, or maybe I am just not following closely enough. I guess you'd just run a bunch of cats and zombies, pretty straightforward.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Massimo, the Magician

Saffgor: Never was there ever a cat so clever as magical Mr. Massimo here. Between his capacity to combo off with The Great Train Heist, duplicate tutors and dig for combo pieces in one go, and come down shockingly early alongside his cheap, evasive peers, Massimo is a premier tempo Commander across pretty much all power levels. You don’t even need to worry about permanently losing options either, with Pull From Eternity!

BPhillipYork: This is fun but pretty good, and will probably get out of control and turn your deck into archenemy all the time. Like, you just run a bunch of utility spells and evasion creatures, loot or rummage into your yard, and then you're casting tons of spells each turn. It's a fun deck concept, but has so much snowball potential that it seems like Massimo will frequently eat a Path to Exile or something as soon as the cat wizard enters.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Homer, the Hermit

Saffgor: Jury’s out on this one until we get Nauctis: The Sunken Realm next year, wherein we’ll probably see the number of cards with the types mentioned double. The payoff itself is solid, and really more of a self-mill tool until you’ve found an infinite Landfall engine with which to mill your opponents out.

BPhillipYork: That's potentially a grotesque amount of mill popping off, Sultai can land ramp very effectively and do graveyard recursion. Your Crabs, Lobsters, Nautiluses, Starfish and Trilobites can be a bit pricey, though to me the obvious solution is just to use things that can clone Homer and not die, which there are a fair amount of now, and suddenly you're getting multiple landfall triggers of twice X multiple times per turn. Gross.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Uugguu, the Omniplasm

Saffgor: Less interesting than Ratadrabrik of Urborg or Princess Yue as a cloning pile, but likely more resilient. The fact that your first clone ends up with 3 copies of Uugguu is solid, and if you get board wiped, those copies see the original and turn into 4 Uugguu post-wipe. The bigger issue is that Oozes aren’t wildly out of line in terms of power, when we’re looking at cards to copy, so beyond being a resilient clone strategy you’d need to weave in Arcane Adaptation-style effects to get appropriate value at high B3/4. Not a huge fan, but there’s potential.

BPhillipYork: Oozes be oozing and more or less reproducing through fragmentation. Since it's Sultai you can be sacrificing and recurring, making hordes of Ooze, if that's your thing. It just all feels a bit obvious.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Tsagan, Raider Warlord

Saffgor: This is a first—an Alchemy card ported into paper, legal for Commander. We’ve had this before, but never with a real stamp, and it sets an exciting precedent. As for Tsagan himself, this is probably the best Speed Commander available, and represents a ton of aggro pressure in an archetype all about aggressive play. I covered Speed in the context of Commander in a Puzzlebox, but the biggest recommendation I have for Tsagan is to play for the pregame actions, and mulligan aggressively. There are many worlds where he comes down and you’re already at Max Speed, or minimum 3.

BPhillipYork: So at max speed your creatures get +1/0 for each creature your control, which is a fairly hefty bump in power, also handing out first strike to a bunch of creatures makes it them difficult to block. To me though this sort of screams unblockable creatures that just slam into your opponents, so like shadow, which tend to be small and cheap, but if each of them is getting +S, then you need something like 7-8 creatures to kill all your opponents in one turn with 2 power shadow creatures. That's not hard to accomplish at all. Of course you can also just make a go-wide token creature deck and then you'll have no trouble hitting that also.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Zagorka, Mother of Sanctum

Saffgor: I’m not a group hug player, so I can’t really get in the correct headspace to evaluate this card. Zagorka looks…bad, to such an extent she’s probably worse than generic Temur options like Partners for the colors. I guess the idea is that you provide opponents Lands, and they fight amongst themselves?

BPhillipYork: I don't know about this. It seems like when they want to attack you they just won't take the tokens. IMO these lands should create a basic War Tax, where the lands have a built in "that player must be pay X for each Sanctum you control to attack the player whose ability created these tokens". That's a mouthful of an ability, but to me this is like, okay right I'm going to take the Sanctums until I'm ready to kill you.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Arzakon

Saffgor: Arzakon exists for one reason only: Cloning Black Lotus. Token copies, animation + Populate, you name it. You can create a board worth more than many houses, if we take the real card’s value as that of the tokens. Is he worse than Garth One-Eye? Perhaps, but given he can come down and create the token without need of haste, that could be the difference maker.

BPhillipYork: I guess, why not just give him the other abilities too. Give him Giant Growth and Healing Salve. This obsession with Black Lotus tokens is dumb to me.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Nephilim Epochal

Saffgor: This is a band-aid on the wound that has existed in Commander since the format's inception: The original 4-color Nephilim aren't Legendary. I don't think this card is especially good, but what it does accomplish is in allowing you to play any (or all) as 'secret Commanders'. It isn't great, but the Epochal does soothe the soul of the format.

BPhillipYork: Well now you can make your legal Nephilim deck. There aren't actually enough Nephilim for this to be anything other than a stunt, but now they will probably make more.

Colorless

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Everforger

Saffgor: I think this is honestly way cooler in the 99 than the Command Zone, so it being Colorless is a huge boon! This is a nifty secondary option for Myr typal, which is where my head goes to immediately, but I could also see it playing well with all the Job Select Equipment from Final Fantasy. Neat card, and I'll see about jamming it in Sanwell, Avenger Ace's 99.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty nice, though why it didn't just allow all colored artifacts is beyond me, it's not like that would be so good.

That wraps up our look at the Multicolor and colorless cards of Mystery Booster: Commander Edition. We’ll be back later this week to round them out with Monocolor.
