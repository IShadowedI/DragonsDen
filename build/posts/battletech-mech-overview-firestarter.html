BattleTech Mech Overview: Firestarter

August 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, MechWarriors! Lynn's here today to talk about a mech which could be deemed a Prodigy of external combustion, a true threat to enemy logistics, more than capable of burning off The Fat of the Land.

That's right, it's the Firestarter. The twisted, 35 ton Firestarter.

Firestarter. Credit: porble

Well, I say the Firestarter is a 35-tonner... The BattleMech is, but this is one of the mechs with an OmniMech sister chassis, and the Omni Firestarter, despite sharing the same model number prefix and being classed as a variant of the same chassis on the (current) Master Unit List, is a full ten tons heavier. I plan to cover both versions today, since the voices won't let me be if I'm not comprehensive in my approach.

Before we get properly (Fire)started, I want to speak a bit about the role of the humble flamer in mech combat. The basic Flamer is Infantry Guy's dream, delivering a whopping 4d6 damage to conventional infantry (better than a heavy machine gun!), but against other targets it is… significantly less impressive. Unlike Inferno SRMs and Plasma weapons, the Flamer has no special enhanced effect on vehicles or battle armor, as far as I'm aware. And against mechs, well…

A traditional Inner Sphere Flamer weighs a full ton, has the range bands of a basic Small Laser, and generates 3 heat for the firing mech. In exchange, Flamers have historically inflicted either 2 (direct energy) damage or 2 points of heat to a target mech, firer’s choice. I’m not aware of any fluff justification for the “dealer’s choice” aspect; I could imagine that the focus of the plasma jet can be dialed between “cutting torch” and “diffuse flame” modes, but TechManual gave no explanation (spending most of the Flamer 's page space simply talking about how it kind of sucks in mech combat).

Fortunately for the Firestarter, the mech most closely tied to the Flamer, the new Core Rules have increased the utility of the Flamer… somewhat. Flamers now deal both their physical and heat damage to the target, placing them on the “Plasma Rifle” side of the heat-inflicting weapons divide (with Inferno SRMs and Plasma Cannons still on the “heat only” side). Perhaps more importantly, Fire/Smoke environmental rules have been massively simplified in Core, AND heat damage from non-SRM sources is more efficient at setting flammable hexes (mostly woods and buildings) on fire than Inferno missiles are, only requiring four points of heat damage, or two regular Flamers, rather than four missiles.

Notably, being inside a hex on fire at the start of the Heat Phase inflicts 5 extra heat on a mech, and a hex targeted by heat damage catches on fire at the end of the Weapons Phase! Combined with hexes being significantly easier to hit than mechs, as immobile targets, this means that two Flamers are now a pretty dang efficient way to inconvenience the infamous Assault Mech On A Hill In Heavy Woods, particularly the barely-heat-neutral crowd like the Hellstar and the Awesome 11H.

It may not surprise you to hear that MOST Firestarters mount 2+ Flamers.

With that in mind, let's see how the Firestarter fares against our grading scale in the brave new world of the 2026 Core Rules!

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘Mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

4th Sword of Light FS9-A Firestarter. Credit: Valk.

FS9-A

The original-model Firestarter, this is a variant that didn't last long, introduced in 2550 and extinct by the Late Succession Wars. The most persistent Firestarter feature it introduced was its movement profile, with all but two models moving at the same 6/9/6 speed, regardless of the tech involved in getting them there. The FS9-A pairs this with an 87% armor load, notably AC/10-proof on all (non-head, natch) front-facing locations, and IS ML-proof across the back. For weapons, its base 10 single heat sinks are taxed by, uh, one front-facing and one rear-facing flamer in the CT (probably the second-most common trait among Firestarters), a medium laser and a flamer in each arm, and a small laser in each side torso.

For those keeping track at home, that means the FS9-A would go to +9 heat on a running alpha strike with its front-facing weapons, so don't do that. Fortunately, it isn't really meant to do that. Against hard targets, all the lasers are heat-neutral at a run, and you can juggle a good spread of reasonable alternate firing patterns depending on the range and your needs at the time: all the Range 1/2/3 weapons, for instance, build 1+movement and threaten 12 damage and 6 heat. Those numbers won't, ah, set the world on fire, but the 9-A is okay as a mid-speed IntroTech 35-tonner. Just try to use terrain to your advantage for screening on the way in, since the longest-range weapons this thing has are bog-standard medium lasers, and with its armor + TMM combo it won't survive significant attention from an enemy.

Still, at 773 BV, cheapness has a quality all its own, and if you want a dork to run around torching woods, this lil guy sure will do that!

Lynn's Rating: C.

FS9-B

Oh. I see how it is.

So, the "iconic" Firestarter, the second variant introduced in the timeline, is the FS9-H, but earlier letters got introduced out of sequence, sure, whatever. I can deal with this.

We've gone from the Star League to the Jihad with this one, because B Is For Blakist. This little weirdo uses an endo steel frame, light ferro-fibrous armor, and a light fusion engine, but doesn't put the weight where you might expect. The armor is thinner than the A (which is a common trend, I'm sad to say), and the weapons are down to the traditional front-rear CT flamers, a flamer in the left arm, and an ER Medium Laser in the right arm. The B is still at a base 10 heat sinks, and yeah, they're still singles, and yeah, it still overheats if it fires all its forward-facing weapons at once.

So what has actually been improved on the FS9-B, rather than just enshittified? Well, the movement profile jumps up to 6/9/8 via Improved Jump Jets, and this lil guy carries C3i. This oddball package will set you back 668 BV.

So, Hear Me Out. This mech is dogwater at any form of conventional combat, but that doesn't mean it isn't useful. This is a very cheap way to get a Jump 8 C3i spotter on the field, and the flamers can be used for fun (setting hexes on fire) and activities (setting people on fire). That's actually a good amount of utility for the Blakist on a budget, so if you want to twirl your cybernetically-enhanced moustache on the cheap, give this option some thought. Just remember that if you're shooting at anything that can effectively shoot back, you aren't villaining right.

Lynn's Rating: C+, as long as you can embrace the bastard within.

FS9-C

C is for... Circinus Federation? Sure, okay. We have Ferro-Fibrous armor this time (82% coverage, lighter than the A but heavier than the B, distributed to be significantly thinner on the limbs than the torso), and a standard-engine-powered 6/9/6 profile. The arms each mount the ol' standard ML + Flamer, but the torso weapons... ahh, the torso weapons...

This is the Obligatory Periphery Rocket Launcher Variant, mounting four 10s and two 15s for a total of 70 tubes. Shockingly for a rocket mech, it only builds movement heat if it presses the "ALL ZE ROCKETS" button.

Honestly, if this wasn't 940 BV I'd say it's kind of cooking, but I don't rate a single unreliable burst of damage that highly. Excellent shitty pirate NPC mech to throw at players in a narrative scenario, though, and when it does manage to punch up it'll be so funny.

Lynn's Rating: D+, but a very fun D+

FS9-H

I was expecting to find out that H was for "Hegemony" or something, but I think the true meaning of this H is "this mech was introduced before FASA established any real pattern to model numbers, and someone just thought it sounded cool"; this base model of the Firestarter was originally built on Skye.

This is an FS9-A but with the side torso small lasers replaced with machine guns. The full ton of machine gun ammo in the right torso necessitates dropping a ton of armor, with the arms, in particular, dropping down to a Ostmech-thin coat of 6 armor pips.

Compared to the FS9-A, the -H model is only better at killing even larger numbers of conventional infantry (or, you know, civilians). The SLs were not the source of the -A's heat issues, and when fighting hard targets the -H puts out less damage, can absorb less damage, and can now explode. The fact that the -H still became manufacturer Argile Technologies' most successful model of Firestarter should tell you something about what most buyers were using Firestarters to do.

At least Infantry Guy's Warcrime Machine only runs 694 BV?

Lynn's Rating: D.

FS9-HB

This one isn't even real! From the world of BattleTech Gothic, the HB is an H which drops the MGs and ammo, swaps the MLs for Light Blazers, and adds one (1) heat sink. This arguably has some utility even outside the world of Gothic (where the Light Blazers' "Dazzle Mode" gives benefits against monstrous Abominations), since the Light Blazers give it a pair of 7 damage hits and only build 1+movement heat if you can keep your fingers off the flamer triggers, but it's up to 754 BV and, again, doesn't exist in canon.

Lynn's Rating: IDEK, y'all.

Battletech Gothic Firestarter. Credit: Jack Hunter

FS9-K

K is for... Kapellan? Something is off here.

Anyway, this is a weirdo Star-League-Era Capellan variant, an H which drops all the regular armaments out of the arms and side torsos in order to mount two SLs in the left arm and a large laser in the right arm. This allows the K to either (fairly gently) poke opponents from medium range or fire all the lasers at movement heat when it gets in close to... deal the same damage as the H's MLs + MGs, in a different arrangement of clusters. The single forward-mounting and single rear-mounting flamer in its CT feel mostly vestigial, but could still hypothetically come in clutch if you need to throw off a TSM mech's math, I suppose.

At least it can't explode?

Lynn's Rating: D-.

FS9-M 'Mirage'

Here it is, the one Firestarter which dared to be different. The "Mirage" is the only mech bearing the Firestarter name to mount zero Flamers. And it was only in production for a single year before the factory was destroyed, and no subsequent manufacturer ever picked it back up. Oh, there are more FS9-Ms, there's even a "Mirage II," but they all mount flamers. Even the OmniMech variant hard-mounts the traditional CT flamers to ensure this would never happen again!

Like the Skye Rangers (who maintained a lance of Mirages with one arm painted black in memoriam after the battle which destroyed the factory), I am still in mourning over this, because this is a pretty damn decent 35-tonner. The Mirage steps up to a full coat of armor, rearranges the jump jets and heat sinks relative to normal Firestarters (and picks up an 11th heat sink it really doesn't need), and pairs the H's side torso machine guns with a medium laser and a small laser in each arm.

This guy is chunky enough to take a hit, can deal a PSR's worth of damage to enemy mechs (a rarity among Firestarters!), and with the MGs it can still kill the perfidious infantryman every now and then, as a treat. What it can't do is set fires, however, which I'll admit does make it look worse in the Core Rules future to come than it did a couple months ago.

Still, 798 BV for a lil guy who can actually scrap with other mechs (albeit at prohibitively short range) isn't so bad.

Lynn's Rating: C.

FS9-M2

Like Tenacious D's classic "Tribute," the FS9-M2 doesn't sound anything like that song the FS9-M. A widespread Late Republic era variant, based on the MechWarrior Dark Age ("ClickyTech") Firestarter, the M2 utilizes endo steel structure and double heat sinks to allow it to pair fairly thick armor (94% coverage) with six Flamers, three in each arm, plus a single head-mounted ER Medium Laser, while only building 1+movement heat on an alpha strike.

The inefficiency of Flamers at heating up enemy mechs means you still can't quite hit the +15 external heat threshold with this mech, but you could multi-target to set three woods hexes on fire in the same turn, which could be both extremely funny and genuinely useful. And this is still south of 800 BV, at 751!

Am I grading Firestarters on a curve? Maybe, but on that curve this thing really isn't bad.

Lynn's Rating: C+.

FS9-M3

Huh. Those are some choices. The M3 is an M2 which drops all the left arm flamers and the eleventh double heat sink in order to max the armor back out (in the same configuration as the original M), add a TAG module to the right torso, add a small laser in the left arm, and pick up MASC, without dropping the JJs, for a BV-inflating 6/9(12)/6 movement profile.

Honestly, I dislike this less than I thought I would when I saw the MASC? Its anti-mech damage is back down to "FS9-H lasers and machine guns" levels, and its BV is up to 866, but the MASC is genuinely helpful for getting the M3 to the right places for its flamers and/or TAG to matter, and the new Core Rules (and, more broadly, missions with objectives) do have a place for pure utility pieces like this when they're cheap enough. Kinda wish it could've packed in an Active Probe, or hands (almost no Firestarters have hands!), if it's going to be living the objective dork life, but... yeah, you could do worse with this BV for an objective dork.

Lynn's Rating: C?

FS9-M4

I'm pretty sure I own the exact MWDA sculpt which gave us this record sheet.

The M4 is an M2 which swaps to an XL engine and drops the ERML, the left arm flamers, and the 11th heat sink in exchange for the full armor coat and a RAC/2 with a single ton of ammo. This is... okay, I guess? The RAC/2 lets it spin to win trying to critseek opponents downrange while it runs around doing objectives and setting hexes on fire, and it can weather a little return fire, though even with new ammo explosion rules that (well-padded, at least) ammo crit slot will kill the mech if hit. It's still really dang cheap at 798 BV, and can do something at ranges few Firestarters can, but it doesn't spark joy in me.

Lynn's Rating: C-.

Found it! Dragon's Fury Veteran Firestarter, from MechWarrior: Dark Age's Fire For Effect Expansion. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

FS9-N 'Mirage II'

Oh god, there's a lot going on here. And this still has nothing to do with the original Mirage! What's with all these weird modern sequels?

A spiritual successor to the troubled experimental FS9-X81 model (we'll get there), this Dark Age variant from the Recognition Guides brings in a buttload of advanced tech. We've got... Inner Sphere Endo-Composite, eleven IS Double Heat Sinks, a full coat of Reflective Armor, a Clan-spec XL engine, and a weapons load like an FS9-A on PCP: A Clan ERML and an IS Flamer in each arm, Clan ERSLs in each side torso, and the ubiquitous rear-facing CT flamer paired with a front-facing Sea-Fox-supplied Plasma Cannon (with one ton of ammo in the right torso). In proper FS9-A tradition, the Mirage II does not want to hit the big red button, but the long(-er) range weapons are heat negative at a run, and all the front-facing weapons except the plasma cannon work out to be neutral at a run.

There's significantly higher damage potential here than most Firestarters, plus higher heat-generation capability than most, plus situationally excellent durability, but you sure do pay for it. At a princely 1,660 BV, higher than all but one other Firestarter (the OR variant of the 45-ton OmniMech, also equipped with Clan tech), this is too rich for my blood. A little birdie has hinted to me that Reflective Armor BV is coming down a bit in the upcoming BV 2.5, to account for the increased vulnerabilities it brings to physical attacks and falls, and it's plausible that the very high BV of plasma cannons could drop at the same time, but that's a whole lot of maybes that would have to become definitelys before I would seriously consider fielding this mech.

Lynn's Rating: D, but an "Awesome But Impractical" D.

FS9-P

A Civil-War-era Periphery variant, the P mysteriously does not use the same chassis as the C, instead pairing an endo steel skeleton with standard armor in a differently thin coat, gaining one pip on the arms relative to the H in exchange for losing pips on the legs, side torsos, and all rear torso locations. The arms each carry a medium laser and a flamer, in the grand tradition of yore, but the P's armament differs from there, with a front-facing flamer, small laser, and SRM 2 in each side torso, with the single ton of SRM 2 ammo nestled in the CT. An alpha strike would build 4+movement heat, hot but plausible in the right circumstances, and this does have excellent damage output for 775 BV, but, uh... sure is a shame about that (lack of) armor. When an Inner Sphere Large Laser goes internal on any limb, and you're still moving 6/9/6, I don't have high hopes for your life expectancy.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

FS9-S

Okay, the S series are the "Doin' Stuff with LosTech" series of Firestarters, with the S and S1 dating from the very-late-Renaissance/very-early-Clan-Invasion period and the S2 and S3 popping up amidst the Jihad.

The S has an endo steel structure, the classic arm weapons and CT flamers, a small laser in the RT, and both an AMS with one ton of ammo and a Beagle Active Probe in the LT. It's still on ten singles for heat sinks, and it manages to find yet another novel arrangement of armor pips, with the same thin coat as the P on its front-facing locations, but noticeably thicker rear armor, IS-MPL-proof across the back.

It's 754 BV. It's cheap but flimsy. The Active Probe is the only real distinguishing feature it has.

Lynn's Rating: D.

FS9-S1

It's an S but with an ECM instead of the Active Probe. That's it, that's the only change, and it's up to 820 BV.

Lynn's Rating: D-.

FS9-S2

Okay, this one is on the same skeleton, with the same armor load, as the -B, but with a standard engine instead of the light, and double heat sinks. Weapons and equipment are almost the same as the -S, but the arm lasers are ERMLs. It's 842 BV.

I do like a Firestarter that can think about alpha striking, but this armor load is not a good layout, and this is less mobile and has more of its utility wrapped up in its actual damage output than the B does.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

FS9-S3

This is the ECM variant of the S2, same as the S1 for the S. 908 BV.

Lynn's Rating: D.

Firestarter. Credit: Rockfish

FS9-X81

The wacky Unique experimental Firestarter which does everything wrong, the X81 pairs the H's armor load with ten double heat sinks and an XXL engine (still at the same 210 rating, still 6/9/6) so that it can mount the traditional CT flamers, a flamer and an Medium X-Pulse Laser in the left arm, and a Plasma Rifle (with one ton of ammo) and an MXPL in the right arm.

A Firestarter with a Plasma Rifle sounds like great fun, but this is not the right way to get there. Terrible armor, even-more-terrible heat management, 1,065 BV... there's good reason why this one is considered a failed prototype in-universe.

Lynn's Rating: F.

Conclusions

The Firestarter certainly brings value to the battlefield, and the Core Rules enhance that value, but that value does not really lie in fighting mechs. The best Firestarters, in my opinion, are the ones that are cheap, reasonably durable, and outfitted for Activities more than direct combat. The A, the M2, the B if you're nasty, the M3 if you've got the points. An EWAR suite would seem in keeping with this purpose, and I wish I liked the S series more, but their armor is bad, y'all, and they can't play keep-away as easily as the B.

Whew! Okay, that's all the Firestarters! Thanks for reading, hope you enjoyed, see you...

...dammit. Right. The voices demand I talk about the 45-ton Omni Firestarter. sigh

Hastati Sentinels Firestarter. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Other Firestarter

One of the earliest and most widespread Inner Sphere OmniMechs, the Omni Firestarter came to be as the result of passive-aggressive pettiness between Lyran manufacturer Coventry Metal Works and Combine manufacturer Luthien Armor Works in the early stages of the shaky technology-sharing agreements forged between their nations in response to the Clan Invasion, as each of them tried to share the least-advantageous specs they could with their rivals. Fortunately for us, from a BV-balanced perspective, I'd say their attempts at sabotage failed; this is a pretty dang solid budget trooper medium mech.

The basic body of the Omni Firestarter is an enlarged 45-ton chassis with an endo steel frame, an XL engine, and a pretty good coat of standard armor, if a little light on the legs: 12 pips on the arms, 16 on the legs and front-facing side torsos, 20 CT, the full 9 head, and 6/7/6 across the back. It gives itself design constraints by forcing some aspects of the Firestarter identity on all configurations: it fixes a full load of 6 jump jets to guarantee the 6/9/6 movement profile, fixes the traditional front and rear facing CT flamers, and fixes two additional double heat sinks (which are not hidden in the engine on the crit chart)... that's seven tons sunk into fixed equipment, and only 13.5 tons of pod space.

The good news for the mech's utility in BV-balanced gaming is that none of that fixed equipment is particularly expensive (the naked frame only costs 646 BV!), and with the critical slot constraints of the endo steel and the XL engine on top of all of that, the FS9-O really doesn't have enough rope to hang itself with overpriced loadouts (mostly). The only real tragedy is that in the Core Rules, the fixed single flamers to the front and rear aren't enough to set woods on fire without assistance, and there is a grand total of one Omni-config which mounts additional flamers.

Yeah, that's right, I'm describing a lack of flamers as a tragedy. What is this strange new world in which we live?

Still, while the Omni Firestarter isn't particularly good at starting fires, it's actually surprisingly good at the whole "mech combat" thing. Let's take a closer look!

FS9-O

The "Prime" configuration here is the one Omnistarter which embraces the notion of "What if Firestarter, but big?" It slaps a large laser in each arm, two additional flamers in the right arm, and a Beagle Active Probe in the RT. The front-facing weapons build 1+ movement heat, and this runs 1,144 BV.

This feels just a hair pricey for what it is, but this is decent damage, decent mobility, good durability, and can torch woods if that's helpful. Not bad!

Lynn's Rating: C.

FS9-OA

LB 10-X. Two tons of ammo. Small laser thanks to that last half ton of pod space. That's it. 976 BV.

This isn't quite the Minimum Viable LB 10 in the game, but it's pretty dang close. I think LB 10-Xs are fantastic weapons, and even though this mech can't force a PSR on its own, it has a very clear role to play as a premiere inhabitant of Liberty's "Little Bastard" zone. That said, the Arctic Fox AF1B is arguably even better value if you just want a cheapass Omni with a Golden BB gun, and lives on some of the same availability lists.

Lynn's Rating: C+.

FS9-OB

Pulse spam on a budget. The OB mounts an LPL, two MPLs, a TAG, a C3S, and a Small Laser, for 1,070 BV. I'm actually going to say the C3S is the secret sauce here. While C3 is a very difficult tool to use well, extending the range at which Inner Sphere MPLs can exercise their pulse bonus is one of its more useful applications, and the new Core Rules guarantee you can get some value from a network even in the face of enemy ECM.

Even outside of a network, a PSR's worth of pulse lasers on a decently mobile, decently durable chassis makes for a pretty good rat-hunter to keep the flimsier striker lights off more valuable mechs' backs.

Lynn's Rating: B.

FS9-OC

This... is one of those mechs that's more useful in scenarios outside the average pickup or tournament game. Mounting two LRM 10s with two tons of ammo, a small laser, and a C3S for 1,006 BV, this is basically a funny-looking Whitworth. It's not entirely Whitworthless, though; 10 is, amusingly, the smallest sized launcher which can deploy a minefield with Core Rules Thunder LRMs, and I suppose it's cheap enough to make for a funny backline pest in a C3 network, particularly since ECM bubbles are six hexes and the short range of LRMs is seven; a spotter can sit just outside ECM radius and deliver full benefit.

That said, it's still not optimal, particularly given that its two tons of ammunition constrict its access to specialty ammo somewhat.

Lynn's Rating: D+, maaaaybe up to a C- in a C3 network.

FS9-OD

*squints* Is that a... Vindicator? Huh.

ERPPC, LRM 5 with one ton of ammo, medium laser, C3S, Guardian ECM, 1,322 BV.

That's alright? It's a little expensive for its output, and you can either get actual Vindicators to be Vindicators for you for less BV (albeit at a slower speed), or, if you're Capellan, you could pay just a little more for a Vindy 7L and get a headcapping CERPPC and the enhanced durability of Reinforced Structure, but the ECM is handy (and only found on more-expensive stealth Vindicators). If you want a 6/9/6 Vindicator with an ECM suite, knock yourself out, I guess?

Lynn's Rating: C-.

FS9-OE

Oh hello there, Draconis Combine! The OE mounts up a Coordinator-approved weapons load of an MRM 20, MPL, SPL, and a sword for 998 BV. Sadly, they forgot the C3S on this one; the MRM could really benefit from a network, even in the new Core Rules. That said, this is a surprisingly good loadout? A six damage sword is unexciting, but with the new rules' mild debuff to kicking, you might actually use it sometimes, the shooting output is actually pretty good with the MRM buffs, and unlike some MRM mechs, this has absolutely no heat problems. You're not bad, OE.

Lynn's Rating: C+, though when BV 2.5 readdresses MRM BV this will probably drop to a plain C.

FS9-OF

I guess it makes sense that the "OF" is the hottest of the Omnistarters. That said, Mekbay's "Total Heat (Dissipation)" read of 42 (26) is misleading; this is actually a perfectly cohesive bracket-firing mech, if not the most exciting one. We have two ERLLs, two ERMLs, an ERSL, and one extra heat sink here. This means that running and firing both ERLLs is neutral, and running and firing one ERLL alongside the short-range lasers is neutral. That's okay damage, though it doesn't crest the "can inflict a PSR" threshold until the ERSL comes into play, and the range is significantly better than most Firestarters. It's 1,446 BV, though, and you can do better for that price.

Lynn's Rating: C-.

FS9-OG

The real OG is both boring and kind of at odds with itself, I'm afraid. It mounts an LB 5-X with two tons of ammo for long-range plinking and aircraft-bothering, paired with a trio of standard medium lasers. It has the heat sinking for ERMLs, but it doesn't use them, introducing the problem that an LB 5 enters its minimum range at three hexes, exactly when the MLs enter short, causing issues with accuracy and reliability for this mech's damage no matter where it stands. 1,063 BV is cheap, but I can't really imagine ever reaching for this thing when there's so many cooler toys on the shelf.

Lynn's Rating: D.

FS9-OH

...wow, there's a lot going on here. The OH utilizes every single open critical slot on the FS9-O frame! Let me slow down and unpack this. We've got an MPL, SPL, and two MGs in the left arm; an ERML in the LT; an AMS in the head; a half ton of MG ammo, single ton of AMS ammo, and CASE in the RT (and that CASE actually means something outside of campaign play now, with the new ammo explosion rules!); and an MPL, SPL, Beagle Active Probe, and Guardian ECM in the right arm. 1,119 BV.

I actually like this a lot? It builds movement heat on an alpha strike (or movement +1 if the AMS triggers twice), but that would include the rear flamer, so effectively it's usually heat neutral-to-negative. It has Firestarter-typical range issues which the lone ERML does nothing to really mitigate, but when it does get close it dumps damage in a way very few Firestarters can, making it a true striker mech, and the EWAR suite is useful in the new missions.

I think this isn't "good for a Firestarter," this is just a good mech. Huh.

Lynn's Rating: B+.

FS9-OR

Hey boss, we looted these Clan OmniPods, what should we do with them?

As with most-if-not-all "R" variants, this is "the MixTech one." Here the podspace is filled by two CERLLs and a CLRM 10 with Artemis IV and two tons of ammo. That is a significant long-range punch, in theory, but the heat curve isn't great here. The ERLLs alone build movement heat, and the LRM would add another 4 on top of that... You could hypothetically rotate between "all the long-range weapons" and "one ERLL and the LRM" on alternating turns; you'd never get to the to-hit penalties from heat, but you would slow to 5/8/6 during your cool-down turns. For 1,713 BV? That ain't worth the hassle.

Lynn's Rating: D.

FS9-OU

Oh boy! The "Unusual Environments" U generally either means a uselessly-niche underwater variant or a very funny space boarding mech, and we landed on "very funny space boarding mech" this time! Five MPLs. Sword. Liquid Storage Tank for fluff reasons (reaction mass for the jump jets in space). 1,120 BV.

MPL-spamming isn't quite as effective on Inner Sphere mechs as it is on Clan mechs, due to range issues, but the heat here is manageable, and while the close-up damage isn't Locust IIC tier for this BV range, it's spitting out of a much tougher body. My only real complaint is that you can only get two of the MPLs behind you, but back-scratching isn't generally a Firestarter's biggest worry.

Lynn's Rating: B.

FS9-OX

Wait, what's the introduction date on this thing? 3059?!? I genuinely didn't know any of the experimental tech packed into this configuration was being experimented-upon that early. A Streak SRM 2 with one ton of ammo is the only "mundane" equipment in its loadout, paired with a testing loadout of a Large X-Pulse Laser, an Angel ECM Suite, and a Bloodhound Active Probe. 1,190 BV.

I feel like I'm on the cusp of hyperbole here, but this might be one of the biggest beneficiaries of the "Experimental" rules level being retired. (Yes, Battle Cobra X, you're still the greatest, but we're not talking about you right now.) Its damage potential is poor for the BV, but there are only 15 mechs which combine both Angel and Bloodhound, this one has the absolute earliest availability date, it's the fifth-cheapest option out of the list, and it brings the usual Omnistarter mix of decent mobility and decent durability.

The Black Hawk-KU OG is a better option if you can afford it and it exists in your time-frame, since it can actually throw out significant damage and it has thicker armor, but the FS9-OX is literally a game-changer in terms of opening up EWAR tech availability.

Lynn's Rating: B-.

Final Thoughts

There's a reason every single Firestarter I have seen on the table has been playing the role of an Omni Firestarter. This is a significantly better mech than its predecessor in every way other than actually setting things on fire. Technically its art doesn't resemble the BattleMech all that closely, and, as mentioned, it's ten tons heavier, but I promise you that no one will care if you use the regular Firestarter mini. If you absolutely have to differentiate, the Gothic Firestarter sculpt is larger! (The metal Omnistarter sculpt also doesn't look terrible, but it's not their best work, either, and IWM prices recently went up.)

Anyway, it turns out that things like "having armor" and "mounting weapons that can reach further than three hexes" are actually helpful for Firestarters, go figure! Kinda wish more of the 35-ton model's variants had figured that out. (Not like you, Mirage II!)

Until next time, MechWarriors, try to lay off on those self-inflicted mind detonators, eh?
