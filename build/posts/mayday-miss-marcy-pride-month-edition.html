Mayday, Miss Marcy! Pride Month Edition

June 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello everyone and Happy Pride! Last week your authoress took off the week in order to help get coverage of the game festivities, but this week we are back with a set of questions that are all very much in the theme of June, and of course as usual, I provide you all with answers to your questions, just slightly gayer than usual. If you're new to this article, well, you picked a really funny one to be your first, but basically here at Mayday, Miss Marcy! I take reader submitted questions and provide the best possible answers/advice for them that I can. If you're curious how to have your questions answered in this very column, please take a look at the following instructions!

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

ALSO, we're heading into Summer, so feel free to push any travel, vacation, party, or similar considerations our way for the coming heatwave, as well as a brand new 40k Edition!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!'

Last time, we talked snacks, meta, and Star Wars, so if you didn't get a chance to read it, go take a look before we dive in to a new batch of questions.

Whatever the Opposite of Pinkwashing Is

Dear Miss Marcy,

I work at Your Friendly Neighborhood LGS, the usual sort of spot for all sorts of games and comics nerds. although there aren't that many Pride comics this year, we still tend to put out the usuals and some small decorations. a longtime problem that we've had is that games feel a lot harder to promote. i suppose it is a little late to maybe do much about it now with distribution, but is there any way to better offer that our shop promotes lgbtq+ friendly gaming?

Caring Clerk

Dear Caring,

I actually will give some transparency here that I got two different questions that are basically this question, so I've combined the two of them together in a sort of amalgam; I will admit that the advice is functionally the same, but I did think it somewhat amusing that I received two almost identical questions that were like "hey, it is mid June, but..." about displaying/selling/promoting queer games.

I think the impetus behind the questions is well meaning, but it does kind of boil down to a harder part of games publishing than Pride itself can contend with: most TTRPGs/Board Games/TCGs don't really have a lot of "face" recognition. Although some designers like Reiner Knizia become famous for their games, most consumers don't know anything about the people who make games, and so they often react more to theme than they do anything else. You aren't really that off the mark in that it can be difficult to promote games by queer creators since it isn't like most games come with a big sticker that says "Hi, I'm So and So, and I'm ____, here is my game!" in any capacity, let alone queer. There are some easy hits, like Thirsty Sword Lesbians, but in general it would probably require doing a good amount of homework into the game designers you're carrying.

However, the main reason I found these questions bemusing is that you are already promoting LGBTQ+ Friendly Gaming by even considering the concept at all; it sounds like your shops care about their patrons and want their queer players to know that their location is safe and welcoming, and even if sometimes the Pinkwashing from Marvel or DC can feel a little eyerolling, even just that your stores carry and display things like that or want to have a section for queer made games goes a long way to doing that already. So I'd say just keep up the good work, enjoy the fact that you run a safe and welcoming space, and if you get a chance, do some research into smaller publishers and see what you find, but don't feel bad that you don't have a "list" of queer games you need to be carrying (although any readers are welcome to namedrop some in the comments for our clerks here).

U-Haul It, U-Buy It

Dear Miss Marcy,

I hate to be a walking stereotype, but I am a U-Haul Lesbian. In fact, there's a good chance you are going to be answering this question some time in which I'll be driving said stereotype to pick up my fiance and move her to my place. The question I have is that we both read your site, or at least, she reads it and tells me about her hobby. You see, I'm really not into gaming myself, but I love that she's invested in this and that she spends a lot of time on her models and gaming. My actual question is that I'm looking for advice on what type of furniture or similar spaces that you could recommend we consider so that we can get her set up with a hobbying space when she gets here, as she won't be moving any furniture and I don't really have that sort of set up.

Stereotypical Sapphic

Dear Stereotypical,

The amount of fear I had at the first sentence of your question was, thankfully, dissipated by the wholesomeness of the rest of the message. I do have a few suggestions, actually. We have a few articles that go over some of these very topics and show off some spaces of our team, and I suggest you take a look at them to see if anything specifically fits your situation; There's stuff there for big and small spaces alike!

If your fiance uses an airbrush, that is likely going to be the biggest hurdle you'll need to navigate; they're going to need a ventilation set-up of some sort, and that really is going to depend more on your available outdoor space, weather, or the location of your windows at your place. If we assume this isn't a problem, you're going to have a far easier time, but if it is a problem, solve it first.

As far as hobby tables or similar go, I think that's something you two should work on at your own pace when you are settled. I suggest places like the Container Store or IKEA as great starting points, because you can get some variable storage, table, or seating options between stores like this that specialize in space/organization and often affordability. Don't overlook stuff like Facebook Marketplace or similar for anyone getting rid of gaming or hobby tables. And as you're the one doing the U-haul driving and similar, excuse my guess that you may be a bit more of a handywoman than your fiance, but we butches tend to know one another fairly well, but let me teach you an old, easy desk trick I've used for decades: Buy a simple flush door. Varnish the door, if it isn't already. Put it on top of 2 standard height office filing cabinets. Enjoy your desk complete with a built in wire/cord controlling opening (or drill one yourself).

Pride PSA on PDA

Miss Marcy,

My partner and I are a gaming couple, who also happen to be gay. We actually met through the hobby and we are regulars at our local LGS gaming scene, and have been for a while now. However, every June my partner gets a little... aware, I guess, and tends to be more reluctant to be affectionate with me when we're in public. I don't mean anything obscene, but stuff like holding hands, hugging, or being close tends to get a little dicey with them when June rolls around. Our gaming store is not at all shy about supporting the LGBTQIA2S+ communities and we are a fairly "average" PNW style gaming community in that regard. But whenever Pride comes around, it tends to make my partner less open than normal. I don't really want to make it into a huge problem but it does bum me out a little bit for obvious reasons, namely that I'm getting less affection from my partner, but also that I think this feels like a bigger issue and they sometimes shrug it off when I bring attention to it. Any ideas? We both go to therapy, before you say that, LOL.

Puzzled by Pride

Dear Puzzled,

Damn, here I thought I could just say "this could be a talk for a therapist", because I think you've more or less circled that this behavior is something more deep than a quirk; your e-mail notes that this isn't the first time or even the second time this has happened, but if anything you have just become aware of the behavior. Does he do this at any other public place you two go during Pride, or is it JUST at the LGS? I'm not going to pretend that I can diagnose the problem through the internet here, but it seems like the added scrutiny or awareness of Pride makes your partner self-conscious, and because of that, they get uncomfortable when you look for affirmation or affection from them in public. I think that without actually getting them to talk about or confront the behavior, you're not going to make any headway on it. I suppose the only real advice or suggestion I can offer is that you try to get them to talk to someone about it, but also, try to investigate or think about whether there IS something more going on that is locational AND behavioral. You say your store is a pretty "average" PNW shop, but dig a bit deeper: did something happen, are there other people there that your partner knows, when did they come out, etc? Your issue is a bit puzzling but I don't think it's something I can solve, but I hope for the best as it sounds like there's some deep-seated self-loathing or something going on there.
