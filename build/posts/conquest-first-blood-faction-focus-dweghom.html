Conquest First Blood Faction Focus: Dweghom

June 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Today, we’re taking a look at everyone’s favorite vertically-challenged warmongers, the Don’t-call-them-Dwarves of Conquest: The Dweghom! How do they function in First Blood? Let’s dive in! To start off, the Dweghom are Conquest’s third-oldest army, and that brings with it some things to consider, both good and bad. Their models are really old in parts, but they have had many releases and therefore have a fairly deep roster. This is especially notable in First Blood, where officers and alternative sculpts translate to extra characters and heroes. So, how do they play?

Faction Overview

Dweghom Hold Warrior Banner Bearer - Credit Beanith

Leaving the finer points of Conquest lore aside, functionally the Dweghom are Conquest’s Dwarf faction and with that come certain expectations. Namely, that they are a slow and steady, bread and butter type of army that lives on good unit stats and likes to hunker down and grind people down with elite melee troops and strong fire support.

These expectations are fairly deeply ingrained with wargamers at this point, and the Dweghom army rules for Conquest’s skirmish game First Blood play into them while also adhering to the deeper lore in places.

Army Rules

Ewan's Dweghom

To start, the Dweghom are fierce melee fighters. When a Dweghom Warrior (for those joining us from The Last Argument of Kings, Warrior is a rules term that refers to a single model, as in First Blood, models move and act independently from each other) would be Driven Back, they are only moved until there is 1” between them and the Warrior that drove them back. This lets them stay in the fight and forces opponents who want to disengage from the angry beards that assail them to waste an action on withdrawing, rather than getting a "free" disengage when breaking a model.

And to top it off, while a Dweghom Warrior is Engaged with an enemy, they automatically pass any Combat Rally Action. This means that Dweghom armies never miss out on the opportunity to hit back, even when their Warriors have to recover from being Broken.

Taken at face value, this does not seem as exciting as other armies’ rules, but in combination with the generally good to great stats of Dweghom Warriors, it makes a marked difference over the course of a game and adds to the consistency and grindability of the army.

Last but certainly not least, similar to every other army, the Dweghom army roster is divided into three distinct subfactions that get a shoutout in the army rules. They are the Clan Creed, the Ardent Creed and the Tempered Creed – your generic dwarves, your angry naked dwarf berserkers and your wizard-y fire-and-brimstone shooty dwarves respectively.

Characters

Hold Raegh

A Raegh is the de-facto military leader of his hold – from what I understand of the lore, which, granted, isn’t any more than you’re likely to get browsing Para Bellum’s Webstore. Given that the Dweghom can lay claim to the incredibly metal title of being THE FIRSTBORN CHILDREN OF WAR, you can guess that someone who rises to become a military leader in that kind of competitive work environment has some major combat chops. And the model doesn’t disappoint.

The Hold Raegh boasts 3 Attacks at Clash 4 and Cleave 3, 5 Wounds as well as Defense and Resolve 4. This makes him incredibly tough to take down while providing massive damage potential for an Infantry Character – on par with most Brutes in the game. You have to be careful with using him though, as his bloodlust makes him a little difficult to handle.

The Dauntless special rule means that he can never be broken, but he can also never decline a duel or leave an enemy Warrior’s engagement range. So if he gets bogged down fighting chaff, you’re wasting all of his potential. And with March 4 he isn’t exactly quick to move somewhere else once he’s done chewing through your opponent’s screens.

A bit of nice tech he has access to is being able to challenge opposing monsters to a Duel action, even if they don’t have the character keyword. I’d question how useful that is, given that his admittedly good statline isn’t quite on-par with most monsters in the game, but it’s nice to have the option.

Furthermore, he has two command abilities, both deal with Outnumbering the enemy. One that gives Clans Infantry Warriors the Iron Discipline rule, making them never count as Outnumbered and one that gives all Infantry Warriors the Phalanx special rule, making unengaged Warriors within 1” of friendly models that perform a Clash action assist in Outnumbering enemies.

All in all, the Hold Raegh is a great generalist that especially excels in dishing out damage. He rewards careful positioning and planning ahead, since he has the potential to be outmaneuvered if you’re not careful with him.

On the listbuilding side, the Hold Raegh unlocks Hold Thanes as Mainstay units. We’ll get to them in detail further down in the unit overview, but for now let’s just say they’re in the middle on the eliteness-scale of melee fighters in the Dweghom roster. If you want to run a more elite list, it’s a nice option to have, since the role of Mainstay units generally is to unlock more elite options in listbuilding, but the Hold Thanes are cheap enough to just about eke out a spot as a Mainstay unit while still letting you splurge a bit on the really expensive stuff. But be careful: If you’re double dipping on elites, you can easily end up skewing too far toward the lower end in model count, which, paired with the Dweghom’s generally lower movement, usually leads to you being outmaneuvered in objective play.

Ardent Kerawegh

The Ardent Kerawegh is a priest, but since the Dweghom religion boils down to literally worshipping War itself, he actually isn’t a wimp in combat – though he doesn’t quite hold a candle to the dedicated beatsticks in the army.

He comes in at 4 Attacks at Clash 3, with Cleave 2, 4 Wounds, with a somewhat low Defense of 2 but a Resolve of 4 that notably doesn’t decrease through suffering excess Wounds, thanks to the Indomitable special rule. With a bit of luck, he can persevere through being focussed down, but he ideally doesn’t want to take the brunt of an attack but sit on the sidelines and support with his spells while keeping the option open to charge in and finish a vulnerable unit off himself.

On the spellcasting side, his somewhat average Spellcaster 3 is off-set by a special rule that nets him an essence point whenever a friendly Warrior within his 6” Command Range charges. He has access to three spells that cost 1, 2 and 3 Essence respectively and scale in power accordingly.

  • „Embolden“ (1 Essence) provides an aura within Command Range that allows friendly Warriors that become Broken to immediately rally and not be Driven Back.
  • „Fury“ (2 Essence) gives friendly Warriors within Command Range the Relentless Blows special rule (each Attack result of 1 rolls an additional attack)
  • „Dismay“ (3 Essence) is the big one, since it flat out breaks an enemy model, generally forcing them to rally. For Infantry, this usually isn’t worth the opportunity cost of not casting either other spell, but it really hurts Monster and Brute models, effectively making them skip an action during their next activation if you can get it off.

To top it off, the Ardent Kerawegh unlocks Flame Berserkers as a Mainstay unit. While also leaning elite, they are a little cheaper and faster, which helps offset the inherent greediness of running them alongside other restricted choices. It’s a pretty thematic package and would probably be my starting point for an Ardent themed list.

Exemplar

Born from an alternative Hold Raegh sculpt, the Exemplar is now sort of half-retconned to always having been the personal champion of a Raegh’s host, leading the army’s vanguard into battle. It’s a bit of a bummer that his statline doesn’t quite measure up to the Raegh’s, but it’s understandable given the new lore surrounding this sculpt.

Offensively, he provides decent damage with 3 Attacks at Clash 3 and Cleave 1, while still boasting solid defensive stats with Defense 3 and the Shield special rule, Resolve 3 and 4 Wounds. He can never decline enemy Duels but benefits from the very powerful Blessed special rule while dueling, and he straight up ignores enemy Evasion during Clashes, which is interesting but probably not always going to come up with a Cleave value of only 1.

His unique Command Ability Unbreakable provides friendly Infantry within his average Command Range of 5” with the Bastion special rule, giving them the option to not be Moved when Driven Back and denying single enemy Warriors engaging them the bonus from Charging.

Sadly, I don’t find the Exemplar that exciting as a Warlord, though he does have the option of joining any army as a Hero, more on that below. He does unlock Dragonslayers as a Mainstay unit in listbuilding, but given that they are incredibly expensive, I don’t see much point in that outside of fun skew lists. You’re better off running a different Warlord and a somewhat cheap Mainstay to unlock your Dragonslayers and going from there. But if you really don’t want to do that and just want to run 12 Dragonslayers and a Monster or something, it’s nice that you have the option.

Lost Ancestor

Ancestors are incredibly powerful Dweghom who have returned from War’s prison deep beneath the earth, imbued with special magic powers. This explains their incredibly sweet model and the somewhat weird hybrid rules that they have in the game, where they are a combination between a melee beatstick, a combat spellcaster and a potential walking nuke.

Coming in at 5 Attacks with Clash 3, Cleave 1 and Flurry (which lets him re-roll hit rolls of 6), the Lost Ancestor can dish out damage against weaker enemy units fairly reliable and doesn’t have to be afraid of the blowback as long as he doesn’t become the target of any higher tier units. His defensive profile mainly relies on his 6 Wounds, but Defense 3 and Resolve 4 isn’t anything to sneeze at either.

The Spellcasting side is where he gets truly funky: He only has Spellcaster 3 but gains 1 Essence whenever a friendly Warrior in Command Range is slain – and he has a Lethal Demise Value equal to his current Essence count. So when he does end up at Essence 3 or above, every somewhat defensively weak enemy Warrior Engaged with the Ancestor has to fear just straight up getting blasted for their trouble if they manage to take the Ancestor down.

His spell suit is pretty interesting as well, leading to a fun push and pull: Do you use his spells early in the turn or try to bank the Essence to discourage your opponent from trying to pop him?

  • „Inner Fire“ (1 Essence) gives friendly Warriors in Command Range (5”) the Burnout special rule – +1 Attack and +1 Clash at the cost of suffering a single Wound at the end of the Warriors activation, but still allowing them to test Resolve.
  • „Vengeance“ (1 Essence) gives all friendly Warriors in Command Range that are slain the Lethal Demise +1 Special rule
  • „Avatar of War“ (2 Essence) gives all friendly Warriors in Command Range the Flurry special rule

As you can tell, the Ancestor sort of leans toward the playstyle of charging into enemy units and either taking them out straight up or taking them down through mutually assured destruction. To cater further to that playstyle, the Ancestor unlocks Lost as a Mainstay unit. I’m not too thrilled with them at the moment, since they are a bit lacking in Defense for a unit that also wants to sit smack down in the middle of the enemy army, but it is very thematic and fun, if most definitely not optimal.

Tempered Sorcerer

The Tempered Sorcerer – as the name might lead you to believe – is the main spellcaster of the Tempered Creed and your typical damage-focussed caster, akin to his Last Argument counterpart.

His stats are alright, notably, he’s somewhat tough for a caster at Defense 2, Resolve 3 and 3 Wounds, but his spells are the main attraction. He has a Spellcaster value of 3, but gains additional Essence on Attunement Rolls of 1, which he uses for a variety of spells.

Namely, the Tempered Sorcerer always has access to the two spells seen below, which he can augment with a third spell from one of three schools, also seen below.

  • „Primordial Awakening“ (1 Essence) gives friendly Warriors activating within 6” the Aura of Death +1 special rule.
  • „Eruption“ (2 Essence) lets the Tempered Sorcerer perform a unique volley with the Barrage 2 (10”) and Armor Piercing (3) special rule.
  • „Scorch“ (3 Essence) is available if the Sorcerer chooses the Fire School and lets all enemies in command range suffer 1 hit.
  • „Molten Invocation“ (3 Essence) is the dedicated spell of the Magma School and is more of a support spell. Each friendly Warrior within 6” gains the Cleave +1 special rule.
  • „Upheaval“ (3 Essence) is available through the Earth School and limits enemy mobility. Each enemy Warrior in Command Range treats the Battlefield as if it was Hindering and Broken Ground Terrain.

Personally, I believe all his special School spells are a bit of a dud, but the Fire and Magma spells seem more usable than the Earth spell in a pinch. In general though, the Sorcerer is a fairly straightforward character: Hang back in a safe position, ideally in a higher elevation and with some kind of screen unit, and cast Eruption over and over.

To aid this playstyle, the Tempered Sorcerer makes Fireforged available as a Mainstay unit, and Fireforged are a decent short ranged threat while also providing a lot of staying power, as we will see below. I have had decent success with a Sorcerer and a brick of Fireforged to bog down the midboard while flanking around with Inferno Automata or Flame Berserkers – or just blasting the opposition with a Hellbringer Drake. But we’re getting ahead of ourselves.

Tempered Steelshaper

The pinnacle of the sorcerous arts of Dweghom society, the Tempered Steelshaper has a correspondingly baller profile. Defense 3 and Resolve 4 with 4 Wounds means he can take a fair bit of punishment before folding, especially since he ignores Dread, Fearsome and Terrifying.

His offensive output is also nothing to sneeze at: 3 Attacks at Clash 3, but with a special rule that always matches his Cleave (X) value to the opponent’s defense, making him a very efficient can-opener.

His spells offer a more supportive slant, making him a decent choice to support a large, dedicated Infantry threat that can help him avoid getting focussed down.

  • „Hone Blades“ (1 Essence) gives friendly Warriors that perform a Clash or Volley Action within 6” the Deadly Blades and Deadly Shot special rules.
  • „Temper Plate“ (3 Essence) gives friendly Warriors within 6” the Hardened special rule.
  • „Unmake Armor“ (2 Essence) lets a target enemy model in 6” suffer Hits equal to their Defense characteristic.

Notably, the Tempered Steelshaper has a higher Spellcaster value of 4 and gets to gain additional Essence by personally slaying enemy Warriors – 1 per. This is a pretty exciting package, but you do pay for it, since the Steelshaper on his own clocks in at 140 points, and that is without a more elite unit to babysit him.

He does unlock Magmaforged as a Mainstay, which is probably where the designers want you to put him. But personally, I have found Magmaforged to be a slight bit underwhelming for their cost. Your mileage may vary, of course.

Heroes

Exemplar

In the new edition of First Blood, there usually is one character that can be played either as a Warlord or a Hero, and the Exemplar is the Dweghom’s pick for the slot. It’s a fairly good pick as well, since you probably don’t want to run him as your Warlord: The only thing you’d be getting out of the deal is unlocking Dragonslayers as a Mainstay, and they’re too costly to run on top of another restricted choice usually.

This way, you can run an Exemplar in your list alongside another Warlord that unlocks a more attractive Retinue unit. He is the most expensive Hero choice at 110 points, but sitting him in a unit of Initiates or Hold Warriors to act as a buffer for him is a pretty tight package that still leaves some room for other threats.

Flamecaster

This guy absolutely slaps if you’re playing a Tempered list. There is no dancing around the issue that at a mere 60 points, the Flamecaster is probably your best bet for a Hero if you run any ranged units that aren’t monsters.

His range is quite short at 8”, so you probably want to run him with Fireforged, but his profile is not bad at all. Defense 2, Resolve 3 and 3 Wounds let him shrug off minor threats, and 2 Attacks at Clash 2 are okay if you’re bound up in melee. And if you don’t want to be, he has access to the unique Clash action „Pyromaniac“, which has all enemy Warriors engaged with him suffer 1 Hit and get Driven Back.

Barrage 3 (8”) with Armor Piercing 1 and Torrential Fire has a bit more damage potential than you would assume at first glance. The real spice comes from his Command Abilities however. Ignite lets him give friendly Infantry and Brute Warriors within his admittedly tiny Command Range of 4” the Deft special rule, letting him and his pals re-roll Hit Rolls of 6.

Slap him in a unit of Fireforged and watch your opponent either charge them and get bogged down for ages or ignore them and get peppered by pretty good short range shooting. It’s one of my favorite packages in the army, though I admit that I have a strong bias for the Tempered Creed.

Herald of Fire

Full disclosure: It took me an embarrassing amount of time for the different flavors of Herald to not blur together in my head anymore. They’re all variations on a theme: Spellcaster 2 with a special rule to gain 1 Essence as a Draw Event if any Warriors in their somewhat narrow 5” Command Range are engaged. Though as written they count themselves, so you’re somewhat likely to make this happen. So in case you, too, get them confused, I’ve prefaced each Heral with my handy mental shortcut to keep them separated.

The Herald of Fire is your damage dealing Herald. He wants to get up in your opponent’s grill, do some damage and then take a bunch of models with him, when he dies. To aid in that, he boasts 3 Attacks at Clash 2, Flurry and Lethal Demise 2, which is a pretty good statline to chew through chaff units.

For spells, he has two options to choose between:

  • „Fireball“ (2 Essence) does just about what you’d expect it to. It lets the Herald perform a unique Volley Action with the Barrage 2 (8”) and Rapid Volley special rules – which means additional Hit Rolls for each result of 1 on the original roll.
  • „Gift of Fire“ (1 Essence) just flat out gives each friendly Infantry Warrior within Command Range +1 Attack. This is pretty good, especially considering that the Herald of Fire wants to be in the thick of a big melee in the midboard, so you’ll probably tag a bunch of your melee fighters with this.

Herald of Magma

The Herald of Magma is your grind-y hybrid. He wants to outlast the enemy while dealing damage over time. His defensive statline isn’t amazing for that purpose, with only a Defense of 2 and a Resolve of 3 – just like his Fire counterpart – so you need some kind of bodyguard unit to protect him, like a squad of Hold Thanes.

If you keep him protected, he can burn through weaker units quite reliably with his Aura of Death (1) while having some flex to focus down more elite problems. For that purpose, he has 2 Attacks with a Clash of 3, Deadly Blades and Cleave 1, which is a tasty combo.

He also has two spells for you to choose between:

  • „Volcanic Retribution“ (2 Essence) gives friendly Warriors in Command Range the Counter-Attack special rule, which lets them Drive Back enemy Warriors after defending a Clash action and suffering no wounds. Hot tip: Pair this fellow with Fireforged and watch your opponent tear out their hair trying to get past your big ranged brick without getting shot to pieces as they are quite tanky.
  • „Gift of Magma“ (1 Essence) gives friendly Warriors in Command Range Overwhelm, which forces opposing Warriors to re-roll block results of 1. Simple and efficient but very good in practice, especially paired with units that have high Cleave values or Flawless Strikes.

Herald of Stone

The Stoner is your defensive specialist and there isn’t as much to say about him. He has the Bastion special rule, Defense 3 and a Shield, which makes a big difference in survivability in First Blood 2.0, but a slightly lower offensive potential with just 2 Attacks at Clash 2 with Cleave 1.

His spells are spicy though:

  • „Avalanche“ (2 Essence) is straight up 3 Hits with Cleave 2 and Smite against a single Enemy engaging the Herald. Important to note: Smite operates differently in First Blood now. It means that every Enemy hit by this unique Clash Action become Broken and are Driven Back, even if they defend all the hits.
  • „Gift of Stone“ (1 Essence) gives every friendly Warrior in Command Range a flat +1 Defense. This is deceptively powerful, as it turns even the lowly Hold Warrior into a sizeable obstacle to overcome.

For a final review of the Heralds, I have to say that – even though I like them all – the Herald of Stone is probably the most impressive one. He is a fairly large force multiplier to the cheapest available infantry. Even just a flat 90 points of Hold Warriors with a Herald of Stone offeres a very strong presence in the midboard or on a side objective that your opponent likely has to overcommit to remove in a timely manner. For pointers on his siblings: The Herald of Fire probably pairs best with a mid-sized unit of Flame Berserkers, while the Herald of Magma wants to run around with Magmaforged or Hold Thanes.

Mnemancer Apprentice

The very fun to say Mnmemancer Apprentice is a bit of an oddball just on his statline alone. He has just 1 Attack at Clash 2 with no further offensive special rules, Defense 1, Resolve 4 and 3 Wounds. It is incredibly swingy to roll for defense on this guy. Sometimes he just refuses to die, especially given that he always Rallies as a Draw Event, while at other times he just taps out immediately.

Though usually of course, you take him for his abilities. First up, he has a unique Action („Written in Stone“) that lets all friendly Broken Warriors in Command Range immediately Rally. HIs two Command Abilities are as follows:

„Aghm Above All“ let’s a friendly Infantry Warrior immediately perform the Duel Command Ability, even if they are not a Character. This is pretty spicy as it potentially lets one of your melee grunts lock down a big scary character in combat – and if your opponent declines, the fact that the character becomes Broken as a result means that you always get to waste some of your opponent’s potential.

„In the Presence of the Mnmemancers“ gives all friendly Infantry Warriors in Command Range gain the Indomitable special rule, meaning they suffer no Resolve penalty from excess Wounds. This is just the default option for if you don’t have a Character to challenge to a Duel, just straightforward staying power for your dudes and always nice to have.

The real kicker is that all this utility comes at a measly 50 points, which is almost worth it just for the extra activation and drop in deployment alone. I almost always take this guy with a unit of Hold Warriors just to fill out a list, as the Dweghom tend to skew fairly elite with low model count.

Mainstay Units

These four units make up the Mainstay options available to every Dweghom list. And as I have pointed out a number of times so far, you’re likely to run at least one of these given how points-heavy many Dweghom units are, even if you have access to better Mainstay units through your Warlord.

Hold Ballistae

The humble Hold Ballistae are actually fairly expensive for a basic unit. 135 points nets you four of these, with 35 points for every additional model. They are worth it though, with Defense 2 and Resolve 3, and the Pavise special rule, which provides re-rolls on defense rolls of 6 against shooting attacks.

Offensively, they bring fairly potent shooting at medium range, with Barrage 1 (12”) and Armor Piercing (2) and a somewhat piddly 2 Attacks at a Clash of 1 in melee. Don’t underestimate them though. Like most Dweghom units, their above average Resolve (for an Infantry unit) gives them a lot of staying power. In large enough numbers, they can get lucky often enough to do some chip damage and bog down a mid-tier enemy unit.

Hold Warriors

These are the glue of just about every Dweghom list. 90 points for four with 20 for every additional dwarf is a fantastic starting point even before you consider their Defense 2, Resolve 3 statline with a Shield. Even their offense is decent, coming in at 2 Attacks with Clash 2.

Their only drawback is their somewhat low movement. March 4 means you are heavily incentivized to pay for a leader or Hero to give them access to the Press Forward Command Ability for some added mobility. It’s easy to then give in and also give them a banner and some kind of upgrade to their leader for some extra oomph. But I believe it’s best to just stick with the basic unit + a leader to keep your list efficient.

Initiates

Initiates have always been weird in First Blood, since they are modeled as two distinct variations of bearded short dude: one with a spear and one with a shield. In the previous edition of First Blood, they were two distinct profiles, while now you have to buy them in pairs.

110 points gives you four Initiates, two with spears and two with shields, while every additional 55 points nets you another power pair. Within that distinction, the spear guys are counterintuitively also named Initiates, while the fellows with the big shields are intuitively called Bulwarks.

Initiates have a very basic and somewhat lacklustre profile of Defense 2, Resolve 2, 1 Attack and Clash 2, but they have access to the Support special rule, which lets them attack using the Engagement Range of a friendly Warrior within 1”.

Bulwarks have Defense 3, Bastion, Shield and Pavise, which makes them very durable against mid-tier units and chip damage shooting. Their weak spot is their fairly low Resolve of 2.

All things considered, Initiates (the unit that is) very reliant on good positioning and planning. If your opponent can flank your wall, the spears in the back can quickly fall, leaving a unit of stubby lads with shields that your opponent can safely ignore.

With good positioning and planning, though, you can really get a lot more value out of these guys than you pay for them, even if they can get expensive quickly. 6-8 models is the sweet spot in my opinion, with a leader to get access to Press Forward for some extra mobility.

Wardens

Wardens are comparatively straight forward and a step up from Hold Warriors in eliteness. They are also the midpoint between Warriors and Thanes in terms of cost at 120 points for 4 with 30 for each additional model. And they offer decent survivability at Defense 2, Resolve 3 with Iron Discipline to offer some extra staying power through flat-out ignoring enemy Warriors outnumbering them.

Their weak point, conversely, is more elite units that do not rely on the extra Attacks from outnumbering their foes. But they positively blend through weaker units with 2 Attacks at Clash 2 with Flawless Strikes. If you’re looking to just play good stuff and want to forego decked out characters and monsters, a unit of Warriors and a unit of Warriors are a good start, giving you plenty of room in your list for two more elite picks to round out your force.

Restricted Units

Dragonslayers

Dragonslayers are the best of the best, the absolute chad-tier dwarves, and it shows. They come in at a positively eye-watering 200 points for just 4 models, with a whopping 65 for each additional model.

In turn though, they offer an absolutely insane statline for an Infantry unit. 2 Wounds (!), Defense 3, Resolve 4 on defense and 2 Attacks, Clash 3, Fiend Hunter and Cleave 3 on offense. They ignore Fearless, Dread and Terrifying and gain Dauntless when engaged with an enemy Monster.

If your meta runs a lot of monsters or Brutes and just mashes them forward, running a cheeky four or six-dwarf squad of Dragonslayers can put a damper on that real quick. If not, their somewhat low March of 4 and their incredibly steep cost usually means you’re better off running another unit in their place. With this kind of investment, you really want to get every point of value out of them or they can end up too inefficient to justify.

Fireforged

I have sung the praises of the Fireforged before, when we went over the Tempered Sorcerer and the Flamecaster, and I will shamelessly do so again here. They look unimpressive at first glance, with March 4 and Barrage 2, Volley 2 at 8” with Armor Piercing (1).

But what differentiates them is their toughness. Defense 3 and Resolve 3 means that they don’t just evaporate when they are engaged in melee. This reveals their true purpose: A hybrid ranged anvil.

Plonk them down somewhere in the middle where they can flex their short-ish range to the flanks or hold an objective with another unit waiting in the wings to counter-charge if the opponent engages them. And if not, they get to soften any unit in close proximity up with pretty decent shooting.

I really love these guys, though they are expensive at 145 points for four, with 45 for every additional model. If you account for my bias of all things Tempered, the Fireforged are probably still a good option for most lists that want such a specialized unit, but their low movement can make them cumbersome – especially in scenarios that don’t have some kind of centralized layout.

Flame Berserkers

Flame Berserkers feel a bit like a Mainstay unit in disguise. They are fairly cheap-ish at 120 points for four, with 35 for each additional dwarf. They have a respectable 2 Attacks at Clash 2, but what puts them over the top is Flurry and Lethal Demise 1.

They can output a surprising amount of damage against Infantry with Defense 2 and below, while remaining cheap enough for you to justify trading them, especially once you consider their defensive statline of Defense 2, Resolve 3 and Oblivious, which lets them test Resolve even when Broken.

Put them in your list to mow down enemy chaff and then break against the enemy backline elites, softening them up for your stronger units to grind down later.

Found

Dweghom Found painted by Josh Gagel

As funky as a gamepiece as their model would have you believe, Found are walking artillery platforms. At range, they provide decent fire support with Barrage 3 (14”) and Armor Piercing 2 per model, and their special rule „Primordial Cannonade“ leaves every enemy unit hit by their Volleys Broken.

In melee, they don’t last long against powerful units with Defense 2 and Resolve 3, but in turn gain +1 Resolve when engaged with multiple units, letting them hold out a bit longer against weaker opponents looking to gang up on them.

Their unique Juggernaut Action lets them Drive Back all engaged enemies with a single action, leaving them open for a point blank artillery blast that also usually leaves them Broken. Pair that with 3 Attacks at Clash 3 and you have a very tempting unit – that in practice often fails to make the cut due to sheer cost.

125 points for one model with 135 for each additional model just takes up a bit too much space for the amount of damage and staying power they provide. Though that won’t stop me from playing them: They’re a fun option once you adjust your expectations and plan the rest of your list accordingly.

Hold Thanes

Do you want a unit that can do it all? Well, Hold Thanes are as close as you can get to a swiss army dwarf. 2 Attacks, Clash 2, Cleave 1. Defense 3, Resolve 3, Shield and Bravery. 140 points, 40 per additional Thane. This kind of statline is like the golden ratio for a First Blood unit. If they had a March of 5, I honestly believe they might just be the exact median for an Infantry unit.

You can never really go wrong with Hold Thanes, though maybe you want a more specialized option in your list. They lean ever so slightly toward being something like a character boat in The Last Argument of Kings, considering their Bodyguard special rule. If you have a squishy character like a Sorcerer you’d like to protect or an absolute monster like the Raegh to provide ablative Wounds for, the Thanes are a slam dunk.

For every other list, they are a fine addition. Just think real hard about what you want them to do – you may find that there is a unit that leans more toward your intended role, or does it cheaper.

Inferno Automata

Clocking in at 80 points with 90 points per additional model, you might mistake the Inferno Automata as chaff units, but they are far from it. The hint is in their movement characteristic: March 7 is way beyond every other non-monster Dweghom unit, making these lovely old models the dwarven equivalent of cavalry.

With that shift in perspective, everything else falls into place: 3 Attacks and Clash 2 with Relentless Blows don’t seem like much. But if you run them into units that are preoccupied with shielded short kings that are stubbornly refusing to die, you can freely take advantage of the Burnout Draw Event to pump them up to a much more respectable 4 Attacks at Clash 3 – and all you lose for it is one Wound per model, of which each Automaton has 4 to spare.

Given that, 270 points for a full unit of these weird hunks of metal really isn’t asking much, and I’ve had great success using their mobility to wipe out cheap scouting units and smash grindy stalemates wherever I end up needing them. Just make sure to never let them get charged or have them fight fair and they will do great things for you – if you don’t, their Defense 3, Resolve 2 statline means that they will quickly fold.

Lost

Dweghom Lost painted by Josh Gagel

Lost really, really want to just stand in as many enemies as possible. Their problem is that at Defense 2, Resolve 3, they very quickly die if they do so. Even with 5 Wounds and a +1 Resolve Bonus for being engaged by multiple enemies, the melee cousins of the Found are just too squishy to end up being worth it.

On paper, they offer a lot: 4 Attacks at Clash 3, Brutal (which lowers enemy Defense by 1 on a turn the Lost charged them) and +1 Attack while being engaged by multiple enemies – and Aura of Death 2. They can burn through chaff like butter, but even chaff can put out enough weight of dice on them to chew through their measly defense if you get unlucky and lose the Supremacy roll.

The only use case I can see for them is as a buff piece, as they do confer their attack bonus against multiple opponents to other models, but that package is probably not worth 115 points to start off and definitely isn’t worth an additional 125 for each additional Lost.

If they ever get up to Defense 3, I’d take them in a hearbeat. They might honestly be too god at that point, come to think of it. But as-is, it’s just too easy for even basic units to roll well – or for you to roll poorly enough – that your Lost get lost before you get any value out of them. And that is before we even take enemy shooting into consideration.

Magmaforged

Much like their corresponding herald, the Magmaforged are a weird grindy hybrid unit that is very hard to evaluate. 1 Attack and Clash 2 with Cleave 2 is pretty decent for offense while Defense 2 and Resolve 3 are okay defensively against mid-tier units.

Aura of Death 1, Deadly Blades, and a special rule that means Hit Rolls of 1 from their Aura of Death cannot be Blocked are supposed to put them over the top. But I find the whole package just a little lacklustre on paper, given their price of 130 points with 35 points for each additional model.

Give them a try with the Tempered Sorcerer to boost their Aura of Death output or the Tempered Steelshaper to give them extra staying power through Hardened and see if you end up liking them more than me. Sick models though!

Steelforged

The Steelforged are the Tempered Creed’s equivalent of Hold Thanes: An incredibly value package wrapped up in a sweet little murderous steel egg with gorilla arms. Science! March 6 is a great starting point for an army as ponderous as the Dweghom, and the rest of their profile – 4 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 2, Defense 4 with 4 Wounds and Resolve 3, Iron Discipline and Unstoppable – leaves just about nothing to complain about.

To top it off, they get to choose between one of three bonuses as a Draw Event:

  • Impact +2
  • Overwhelm (Enemies Re-Roll Block results of 1 during Clash Actions)
  • Overrun (A free halved move after destroying an enemy model in engagement range)

They’re not cheap at a 135 points to start and 150 model for each additional model, but if you build a list with that cost in mind, foregoing monsters and other elite options, they can and will do just about everything you want them to.

Monsters

The Dweghom have access to three monsters, each of which can also be used as a mount for a specific character. I won’t mention that option in-depth, as it basically just staples their statline to the character, makes that character more expensive and loses them their retinue option.

In general, monster characters aren’t in a great spot right now. You probably want to just run the character and the monster separately for greater flexibility and ease of listbuilding, but keep in mind that the option is there.

Hellbringer Drake

The Hellbringer Drake – the mount option for the Tempered Sorcerer – is the cheapest Dweghom Monster at 265 points and you get a lot out of him for that cost. March 7 is, once again, great for an army that struggles so much with mobility, even though the large monster base can make navigating terrain a bit of a struggle.

Offensively, the Drake shines as a ranged weapons platform: Barrage 6 (12”) and Torrential Fire is the base Volley statline in his profile, but in practice you will probably only use this if your opponent has brought a monster or a threatening Brute unit you need to focus down.

Usually, you will instead make use of his unique Volley Action, Immolate. This lets you target an Enemy within 12” and every Warrior – including friendly ones – suffer 2 hits. This is great for breaking up big Infantry blobs and with some finesse can let you absolutely decimate grinding midboard stalemates without hurting your own guys too much.

To top it all off, the Drake can use his unique Take Aim Action, Overcharge to gain Armor Piercing (2) on both the big cannon shot and the Immolate Volley Action. But each Hit Roll of 6 has the Drake suffers one Wound in return.

With 6 Wounds before becoming Wounded and Defense 3 and Resolve 3, you probably don’t want to really tussle in melee anyway, but know that the option technically exists, and the Drake is no slouch in close combat either.

5 Attacks at Clash 4 and Cleave 2 lets him absolutely decimate lighter units that try to flank him, but be warned: More elite units will chew through his 11 Wounds much more quickly than you might expect.

In short, the Drake is the center of your castle. Protect him and let him shoot from the back with the option to later charge in and claim a point from some poor battered Infantry unit and you (and the Drake) will be eating good.

Ironclad Drake

A mere 15 extra points nets you the melee equivalent, the fearsome Ironclad Drake. He is the mount of choice for your Hold Raegh and trades in ranged firepower for devastating charges. He is a tad slower at March 6, which can make it difficult to get a good Charge and Clash combo going, but if you do so, he will absolutely slap the beans out of all but the most elite units.

6 Attacks at Clash 5, Cleave 2, Impact 2 with Brutal (-1 Defense for enemies being Charged by him) and Terrifying basically means that the Ironclad Drake straight-up deletes just about everything he gets into.

And as if that isn’t enough, a bunch of very flavorful special rules sweeten the deal. To start, the Ironclad Drake counts as a Standard Bearer (letting Infantry Warriors within his 7” Command Range Re-Roll Resolve Rolls of 6) and gives them Fearless to boot.

Last but not least, he has the option of driving any engaged Enemies back with the Juggernaut action or choosing to Break and Drive Back every Enemy Warrior he Charges with the „Draconic Rampage“ special rule. Just keep in mind that if you do choose the latter option, you won’t get to hit them afterwards, as you won’t be engaged anymore.

Taken as a whole, the Ironclad Drake is kind of like the Hold Thanes mentioned above. He just gives you a bunch of options alongside a very good statline. Just make sure that you support him and send him after the right targets (enemy Infantry) – after 6 Wounds he counts as Wounded, and after 11 Wounds he counts as dead. And that can happen quicker than you’d like against opposing Monsters and Brutes with Defense 4 and Resolve 3.

Stoneforged

Conquest Dweghom Stoneforged credit: Bair

And finally, we have the Stoneforged. My favorite model in the Dweghom range and the mount option for the Tempered Steelshaper. He is – as the model suggests visually – a bit ponderous, but very tough to deal with once he is in position.

Defense 5 and Resolve 3 makes him a very tough nut to crack, while 5 Attacks, Clash 4, Cleave 3, Smite and extra attacks against Warriors of smaller size (Infantry and Brutes, usually) make him a menace in close combat – just getting there with March 5 can be a hurdle against opponents that know to run circles around him.

Gameplay-wise, I personally prefer the more mobile and flexible Drakes, but there is something to be said for a big, blunt beatstick of a problem for your opponent to solve. An interesting thing to note here and that both you and your opponent will forget is that the Stoneforged buffs friendly Tempered Character’s Spellcaster value within Command Range while subtracting 1 from enemy Spellcasters.

What you definitely won’t forget is the cinematic and appropriately named rule „Seismic Demise“. When the Stoneforged falls, all Warriors – friendly and enemy – within 1” of his big pie plate base immediately become Broken. A very fun model to play with, and a joy to paint up as well!

Sample Army List

=== First Blood ===

Sample Army List [1000/1000]

Dweghom

* Tempered Sorcerer (1) [135]: Tempered Goad, Earth

* Flamecaster (1) [60]:

* Fireforged (7) [280]:

* Hellbringer Drake (1) [265]:

* Inferno Automata (3) [260]:

This has been my go-to Tempered list for a while now, but it’s a bit more specialized. Basically, the Flamecaster and the Fireforged take up space in the middle of the board while the Hellbringer Drake shells the opposing units that march up the board from the back.

The Tempered Sorcerer crucially gets deployed near the Inferno Automata and Hellbringer Drake to give them extra movement with the Tempered Goad upgrade and get them where they need to be.

=== First Blood ===

Sample Army List [1000/1000]

Dweghom

* Hold Raegh (1) [130]: Warborn

* Mnemancer Apprentice (1) [50]:

* Herald of Stone (1) [85]:

* Hold Warriors (6) [130]:

* Hold Thanes (6) [220]:

* Dragonslayers (6) [385]: Leader, Standard Bearer

If you’re more interested in the Clan Creed, this is a fun and thematic list but probably not optimal, given the large point sink of a 6-man squad of Dragonslayers. Rest assured though that this will nevertheless provide a very dwarfy play experience.

Pair the Mnemancer with the Hold Warriors and the Raegh and Herald with the Hold Thanes and march forward to war!

Alternatively, you can swap out the Dragonslayers for an Ironclad Drake and beef up your Infantry Squads a bit – though personally I find the Dragonslayers more exciting than the Drake. But I am completely biased.

Sample Army List [1000/1000]

Dweghom

* Ardent Kerawegh (1) [100]:

* Herald of Fire (1) [85]:

* Exemplar (1) [110]:

* Flame Berserkers (7) [240]: Standard Bearer

* Wardens (7) [225]: Standard Bearer

* Lost (2) [240]:

And finally, here is my take on an Ardent Creed list. Take this one with a grain of salt or two, since I am not too familiar with the Ardent Creed as a whole, but you can’t really go wrong with Flame Berserkers and Wardens. Pair each of them with a leader (I’d pair the Herald with the Flame Berserkers for extra Attacks and the Exemplar with the Wardens for some additional punch) and a Lost to take some of the heat off of them.

This is another fairly blunt list, but …

Conclusion

… the Dweghom are a very blunt faction. Much like in the larger rank and file cousin game The Last Argument of Kings, in First Blood the Dweghom rely on good stats to balance out their below average mobility. They are fairly easy to pick up and forgive small mistakes, but also reward good fundamentals. They might not have the highest skill ceilings in the game, but a competent Dweghom player will still find ways to make the faction sing.
