Mayday, Miss Marcy! 11th Edition, Holiday Hobby, Storage Wars

November 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello everyone and welcome back to Mayday, Miss Marcy!, in which I take your reader submitted questions and provide them with the best advice I can come up with in response, which is generally helpful (I like to think, at least). If you're new to the column, think of the likes of Dear Abby or any Agony Aunt style work. The weather sure is cooling down in most places above the Equator, and with that comes the ever encroaching holiday season. Surprisingly, this week I do NOT have any questions about gift giving, but I'm sure there'll be more! If you'd like to have your own questions submitted to the column, please read the blurb below.

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

As I mentioned, we've been having a collection of gift related questions, and if you haven't had the chance to read last week's column, go take a look! This week, I have a collection of three new questions, so let's get right into them!

New Edition, Old Problem

Hey again Marcy!

Since sending my original question my organizational skills have improved and what was quite a cumbersome collection has been compacted quite a bit! But today is a 40k question.
I'm the local TO for my town, we run small RTTs and other events, but i've came into a bit of a problem; I think the game sort of stinks right now. That is not to say playing 40k is bad, but I feel like matched play is in a woefully boring state, and will not be getting better any time soon. This is a fixable issue, but it may be a couple years before that even happens (the primary mission/dispositions being the bugbear).
So as a TO, where do I go with this? I see two main routes ahead. I could thug it out and keep providing matched play for an admittedly dwindling audience who VERY vocally prefer it, or I can scrap it and go all in on narrative and campaigns, which may alienate some players but save my sanity.
Which direction should I go, or is there a secret third way i'm not seeing?
Regards,
Cluttered Canuck
Dear Canuck,
Thanks for writing in again! Glad to hear from you. I unfortunately think that you're seeing what a lot of people are feeling, and something that even we've seen in the Discord and on various places; while there is some really cool stuff in 11th and obviously a lot of things we haven't seen yet, it does seem like a sense of fatigue has set in over the game already. I'm sure more competent players than I could expand upon why that is, but the reality is that the reason doesn't matter as much as other aspects do, because while GW may be the one to solve the problem, that also means those of us playing the game until then have to figure out how to do so.
I think that your solution is the latter; you should start trying to run different events and seeing if that helps stir the pot on interest levels. If you're having a problem with attendance already, continuing forward is unlikely to make that number go back up, and until something more concrete shows up that gets your locals interested again, trying something new is probably the best way to solve the problem in the short term. Even if you don't run the campaigns forever, spicing up the routine is going to probably at least drag some people back in who have otherwise found issues with wanting to be motivated in the first place.
I think that the lack of narrative and crusade in particular has likely created some issues also; although the gameplay itself is good, if people aren't having fun and don't feel invested, it becomes harder to convince them to keep playing. If you can invent some narrative missions and concepts yourself, it might help to then spur on more play and interest. Although, even though people seem to believe "narrative" = "casual", I think we all know that isn't true, but it DOES tend to bring people to the table. I think you've got the right idea, and just have to work on making it until something else takes the weight off of you; perhaps we'll get some update to 11th that helps bring people back, but you've still got to run your tables! Good luck out there and let us know how it goes, cause I think many people are asking the same questions.

Holiday Hustle

Dear Miss Marcy,

During the holidays, things tend to be busy around our household. In the last year, I realized that I haven't had much time to myself during them, which also meant that my hobby progress basically ground to a complete halt. I'm not thrilled by this, even if it sounds selfish. My hobby is the thing I do for myself and my stress relief / reward for putting up with work, and while I love the holidays and doing things with my family, I absolutely realized that last year I had a few moments where the lack of "me time" was really spreading me thin.

This year I've decided that I want to avoid this from happening, but I'm kind of struggling with how to go about it. Do I just tell my partner "hey, I'm going to go paint some miniatures now" or something while relatives are around or plans are being made? I feel like there's a wrong answer and a right answer; do I just have to accept that my hobby time isn't really "mine" during a busy season like the holidays?

Hobbyist Hangups

Dear Hobbyist,

I... think you know the answer to this, don't you?

If I can be honest, your question does come across as selfish, but for a few reasons that are different than you might expect: You talk a lot about yourself and how the holidays encroach on your "me" time, but you don't really illustrate something. Does your partner get their me time? What about your family in general? Are you the only person doing literally everything while others rest, or are you just looking for your own peace of mind time for things?

To be clear I don't think there's anything wrong with you wanting me time or decompressing, I just think it might be worth considering: Are you the only one being asked to give up this time during the month of December, or is it equally shared? Because if it is equally shared, I think you should both approach the issue in a way that is about how you two can protect your boundaries and mental health during it; if this is just about you and you aren't thinking of how your partner can get some alone time, you should do that too!

Anyway, I will say that I think the best thing you really can do is TALK to your partner about this. "Hey, I really want some time to myself, I need to decompress and last year was stressful". I don't think there's anything wrong with that, and it is really a problem people don't think about; planning out a lot of holiday events and things but not "you" time; yes, holidays can be hectic and busy and force things into your routine you don't expect, but you also still have to live your life.

Coming Out of the Closet (Literally)

Dear Miss Marcy,

I'm having some serious clutter problems with my hobby. I had a pretty small space in my closet that was devoted to holding boxes and storing things that I was working on, as well as holding my completed models, but the more I get into the hobby, the more space things are taking up, and the closet space I originally had designated is no longer working out. I've considering slimming down and already have some models and stuff I've realized are just never going to happen or work in my army, but honestly, what should I do for storage that isn't just boxes flooding out of my closet?

Storage Wars

Dear Storage,

Ah, yes. A hobby often starts out with one or two items, and suddenly, you've got a tower of things that you have to store, all in various sizes and shapes. Odd how models are a lot easier to store before you build them, huh? Well, then you just have a horde of unbuilt plastic, so then of course you have to build them, but then you have a lot of BUILT plastic, and...

Well, you get the idea.

I think your idea of planning out space was good, but like most hobbyists, we never really seem to plan out the exact RIGHT amount of space we really need. And buying temporary solutions creates long problems, because you'll suddenly find that the thing you did ended up only working for a short period. Your honest best bet is to carve out a serious, specific, and as large as logically possible space that you can. Then, start looking at what types of furniture and storage solutions you can afford in that space. Will it fit a book case? How tall? Wide? Will it allow you to store both unbuilt and built models comfortably? while IKEA is often a very "easy" solution here, it isn't always the best bet. That said, Billy bookcases really do a lot of work for their size, price, and general shape.

The other thing to consider, though, is space, in the empty sense. Something that tends to happen when you make a dedicated space for a hobby is that when you have excess, empty space, your brain tends to start thinking about how you COULD fill it. And, well, that's how we end up with excess models we're suddenly looking to sell. You seem to already be aware of this issue, so just consider that whatever you do end up planning, don't think you need to FILL your space; let it be empty! Trust me!
