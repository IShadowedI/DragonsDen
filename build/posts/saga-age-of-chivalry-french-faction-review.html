SAGA Age of Chivalry: French Faction Review

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

SAGA has easily the most factions of any game I’ve played or seen. Even if you’ve settled on a particular Age, you might struggle to pick from a dozen or more of them; and if you’d like to leap into the amazing time-warping craziness of full Age cross-compatibility, you’re faced with over sixty! Allow me to make the decision for you. If you’re just starting SAGA, you should pick up Age of Chivalry and play the French.

The French have a solid case for being the best starter faction in the entire game. With a lot of unit variety but a straightforward play style, you can pick them up and play them well with no knowledge of any kind of meta at all. Where the Age of Vikings’ Vikings are glass cannons with a tricky self-sacrifice mechanic, and the Age of Crusades’ Crusaders alter their battle board abilities depending on whether they have pilgrims on the board, the Age of Chivalry’s poster boys do two things and they do them well: attack and defense.

SAGA Warband. Credit: Michael O "mugginns"

From a collecting standpoint, the French are the rare faction with a Hearthguard and Heroes focus, meaning they need fewer models than nearly any other faction. You can quite feasibly play the French with 25 models on the board. From there, the similar dress and equipment of soldiers across the HYW means you can easily expand your collection to cover other Chivalry factions - add some bowmen to play as the Burgundians, pikemen for Scots, serjeants for the more Warrior-focused Germans and so on. Studio Tomahawk gave all Chivalry factions the same faction dice with this in mind!

Who are the French?

The Hundred Years War was a pivotal era in the development of the French nation. A chaotic feudal mess in the early 14th century, France suffered disastrous defeats against much smaller English forces in the first part of the war, such as the battles of Crécy, Poitiers and Agincourt. Great swathes of French territory were repeatedly conquered, military and political leaders were decimated, and thanks in part to the Black Death the French population at the end of the war was half what it had been before. Many French nobles sided with the English, the Houses of Orléans and Burgundy fought each other, and the long-reigning French king Charles the Mad spent crucial portions of the war convinced he was made of glass. Still, somehow, the French won - turning the tide at the Siege of Orléans and recapturing almost all of the country by 1453. National heroes such as Joan of Arc arise from this conflict, and France emerged as a centralised nation and the most powerful in Western Europe.

SAGA Warband. Credit: Michael O "mugginns"

French military development over the HYW is in many ways the development of the modern army. From the feudal levies and knightly charges that bloodily spent themselves at Crécy, to the professional standing army and field artillery that won the Battle of Castillon, the French forces totally transformed over the period - and the SAGA faction has the options to reflect this!

Why Play the French?

Pros:

  • Powerful attack and defense
  • Simple to play and understand
  • Low model count makes them quick and cheap to field

Cons:

  • Limited movement and fatigue removal options
  • Counterable with fatigue, terrain and pikes
  • Elite Hearthguard focus leads to swingy defense

Warband options:

The French have a choice of two warband compositions: Feudal and Retinue Companies, and Compagnie d’Ordonnance. The first represents the feudal levies and later indentured companies of volunteers that made up French forces for the bulk of the war, the second the permanent army created by Charles VII in 1445.

SAGA Warband. Credit: Michael O "mugginns"

In the Feudal and Retinue Companies, your Warlord, Hearthguard and Warriors can choose to be on foot or mounted. One unit of Warriors on foot can take pavises. Your Levies can choose between taking crossbows or bows. The big choice here is how heavily you want to lean into mounted troops - since you can choose a unit’s equipment at deployment, you may want to deploy your Warriors and Hearthguard on foot if you’re facing a heavily ranged force and on horseback otherwise, just as their historical counterparts would often dismount before an attack against archers. I prefer crossbows to bows on French Levy, since you’re unlikely to want to shoot multiple times a turn in such a melee-focused faction and hitting the enemy’s own mounted Warriors and Hearthguard on 2s and 3s is devastating.

In the Compagnie d’Ordonnance, your warband represents the first professional national army in Europe since antiquity. Your Warlord and Hearthguard can again choose between being on foot or mounted, or mounted with barding - a later innovation that shortens your equipped cavalry’s movement back to M while keeping their charges at L, but keeps their Armour value the same against shooting. Your Warriors can be on foot or mounted (no barding for the poors!) or on foot with bows, the French having abandoned the crossbow in favour of English-style longbows at this point. Fittingly for a professional army, the Compagnie d’Ordonnance has no Levies. A force of on-foot or mounted, unbarded Hearthguard and Warriors fits into either subfaction, so other than historical preference your reason for choosing is whether you want access to barded Hearthguard and bow Warriors. Both are flexible choices, with barding being more forgiving if you get shot at, and ranged Warriors being able to fight much better in melee than ranged Levy, letting them switch between roles in a pinch.

Either way, you’ll want plenty of Hearthguard, as several of your SAGA abilities work best or exclusively on them.

The Battle Board:

Nearly every French SAGA ability revolves around melee, with your only movement boosts and fatigue manipulation either getting you into combat or rewarding you for being there. The Battle Board is nice and easy to understand for a newcomer: trigger its effects to hit hard or tank hits. Very simple.

Flying Wedge ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Flying Wedge

This is it. The knightly ideal. The glorious moment in each French commander’s battleplan. Few other SAGA abilities let you apply so much pressure across so many units, forcing your opponent (and you!) to choose which of these three combats they’re going to spend their ability bonuses on. Trigger this one every game.

Montjoie! Saint Denis ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Montjoie! Saint Denis!

Absolutely daring, this is what a Warlord should be for. Use it to massively boost his threat range with multiple moves before a charge, or best of all to heal him up after a melee before throwing him straight back into another one. Obviously it risks overextending him, so perhaps save this for the late game. Or combine it with:

Pledge of Battle ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Pledge of Battle

Extremely useful on the aforementioned Warlord, to whom the fatigue after a melee is equivalent to an automatic hit taken. Hearthguard also love this due to their vulnerability to Armour penalties.

Armed and Armoured ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Armed and Armoured

No notes, this is one of the strongest abilities on your board and wildly stacks a melee in your favour. I’d usually take the extra defense dice to take advantage of your opponent’s loss of attacking power.

Full Harness ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Full Harness

Ah, now this is tricky. Especially if you're bringing a cavalry list, Full Harness can spare you the historically accurate experience of being brutally humiliated at range. It essentially changes your army's entire dynamic while active, swapping audacious charges for steady advance, and is the main reason for the breadth of possible French play styles. Be warned, Resilience (2) may look very tempting, but it comes with a price as the fatigues pile up. At Agincourt, the French men-at-arms trudged through arrows and mud for so long that when they finally reached the English line they were hopelessly exhausted, and if you're not careful this too will be your fate. For this reason, keep Full Harness up for as short a time as possible while you get into position for:

Prowess ability. Credit: Studio Tomahawk

Prowess

A great way to catch out an incautious opponent (warn them). Note that this works on barded mounts too, letting a unit that has hitherto moved like infantry pull off a suddenly enormous charge. It still has to be in a straight line however. Sadly, Prowess is specified to be useless when charging through terrain, canny use of which is very effective against the French.

Ways to Play

Massed Cavalry Charge and You

You know it, you love it. The French loved it too, which is why they persisted in doing it for decades after it should have become clear that hurling your best-trained and highest-ranking officers headfirst into a shower of arrows was a Bad Idea. But never mind all that - this is the Age of Chivalry, and there is nothing more chivalrous than mounting up most or all of your warband and stampeding forwards to glory or death.

A Saga Game in full swing. Credit: Lenoon

This is how I like to play French, and it feels amazing. If both sides deploy aggressively and the terrain is open, Prowess can guarantee first-turn charges for your Hearthguard, and combining it with Flying Wedge can slam most of your army into melee with shocking speed. You’re gambling that your opponent hasn’t set anything up yet, and that no plan on earth can survive this punch to the face. No tricks. No fatigues. Just attack dice.

Of course, if your opponent has set something up, or even if you roll badly, all your brave and beautiful knights can evaporate on contact. Losing half your army in your own first turn is absolutely not a recoverable error (as I’ve learned), so you’d better get this right. Check which defensive abilities your opponent has set up and whether they allow things like reactive movement that can scupper an early charge. Concentrate on improving your own defense as you come in with Tenacity, or Armed and Armoured if you’ve got the SAGA dice - your priority should be keeping your chargers alive to fight again over heaping on further offensive benefits.

Pikes are the bane of this strategy, giving their wielders a +1 bonus to their attack dice against mounted targets as long as the pike-equipped unit has no fatigues. I like to bring a couple of points of mounted Warriors along to address this. If you can, charge your Warriors into the pikemen first, probably without any ability support at all. They’ll do badly, but they’re less susceptible than Hearthguard to being wiped by a few freak rolls. Then, once they’ve bounced back off and the pikemen have taken a fatigue from the melee, follow up with a charge from your Hearthguard and clean up.

Barded Mounted Warlord credit: Bair

If the Flying Wedge of Prowess goes well, the opposing army will be devastated, and still have a few units of angry knights right up in their deployment zone. Extra points for catching out exposed missile units, or doing this as the second player in a scenario where the first player has limited SAGA dice available in their first turn, hampering their reactiveness. You’re effectively shifting the burden of nuanced thought onto your opponent as they struggle to deal with your mobile cavalry and hard-hitting Battle Board, and if you’re like me, you love that. The best thing of all about all-horse French gameplay, however, is that it’s quick. One way or another, by Turn 3 bright pennants will be fluttering over a battlefield of corpses. I’m usually a slow player and end up having to rush through the last couple of turns of my tournament games to keep to time. But whether they deliver you victory or not, the mounted French will certainly deliver you half an hour of relaxed strolling around the other tables, sipping a cool drink as the Germans and Flemish frantically wrangle massive infantry blocks, weeping over their attritional tactics and poor life choices.

Variations of this army theme include taking barded mounts in the Compagnie d’Ordonnance, leaving you less exposed if you need a little more movement before you can make the big charge; and taking at least one ranged unit to counter opposing cavalry, pepper units hiding in terrain, and camp on midfield objectives while their mounted betters keep the foe pinned back.

Minimal Models

24 Hearthguard and a Warlord, all on foot. SAGA as a one-box wargame.

The French are probably the only faction that can make such a small force work. Here you’ll play as nearly the opposite of the glorious flying charge, and grind inexorably up the table, armoured feet stamping a synchronous rhythm of doom.

Fatigue management is more key to Hearthguard than any other SAGA unit type. Reliant on their high Armour value to keep themselves alive, your elite Men-at-Arms cannot afford to pick up excess fatigue markers. If you do, your army will reach combat with the resilience of a couple of points of bow levy.

Foot Knight Hearthguard credit: Bair

The French have no way to clear fatigue on their Battle Board, so avoid double-activating or plowing through dangerous terrain if there’s a risk you’ll be charged before you can rest. Where most factions can play effective SAGA by focusing their dice through just four or five units over the course of the game, here you’ll try to have your entire army advance at once, each unit ideally making one move per turn. This can give your opponent a headstart on seizing objectives and is especially punishing if Full Harness is keeping you at the speed of the snails your soldiers had for breakfast, but in scenarios scored by massacre or survival points is much less of an issue.

So long as you can avoid fatigue being used to drop your armour, this list can absolutely abuse the French defensive abilities. Armed and Armoured is your bread and butter here, letting you effortlessly fend off melee assaults, while Tenacity and Full Harness can make you nearly invulnerable to shooting (and Tenacity rewards you with an extra SAGA dice roll for being so). It’s tempting to form your Hearthguard into a large unit to get the maximum benefit out of their specific Panoply ability, although remember that the initial attack pool limit is 16 dice. This makes eight Hearthguard probably the largest you can go to if you want to be sensible, which is of course a very optional approach. However, multiple smaller units increase your total fatigue capacity, while generating more SAGA dice that you’ll need to keep your army rested and advancing together. Finally, use Pledge of Battle to keep from picking up fatigue in melee, stopping your opponent’s units from coordinating to take you down.

Example List:

Feudal and Retinue Companies

  • Mounted Warlord
  • 3pts of mounted Hearthguard (in 3 units of 4)
  • 2pts of mounted Warriors (in 2 units of 8)
  • 1pt of Levy with crossbows (in 1 unit of 12)

This is an aggressive charging list that makes some concessions to defense and range. The more expendable Warriors ride out in front, screening the Hearthguard and Warlord while they line up the perfect charge - make sure those Warriors don’t block them! The crossbow Levy work in support, holding objectives and softening up targets when they get the opportunity.

Specialists

Age of Chivalry's version of Mercenaries. The French have several good options here.

I'm particularly excited by Lombard Lancers, extra tanky barded Warriors that are almost a straight upgrade on the screening Warriors you probably weren't going to spend any SAGA abilities on anyway. With the ability to force your opponent to select them as the target of shooting attacks, placing them centrally and rolling them up the field can spare you from having to use Full Harness at all, in an ancient French tactic known as the Distraction Carniféques.

Cannon credit: Bair

A Knight is a thematic choice, particularly mounted variants that can benefit from Prowess (but not Knighthood, ironically). As in modern times, the French are fond of their Bidets, light scouting Warriors that can ‘turn off’ uneven terrain. With no easy answers to dealing with terrain of their own, this is an excellent way for a French cavalry player to pave the way for their sweeping charges and counter tricksy terrain-hogging factions like the Free Companies. As well as a fun modelling opportunity, a large tarpit of 16 Ribauds can also bolster the survivability of a Hearthguard-heavy warband.

Second best to removing your fatigues is inflicting them on your opponent and making them cancel out, so if you’re struggling to do this, Handgunners or a Cannon can help you, re-enacting the 300 guns the French fielded during the Battle of Castillon!

Collecting the French

Aesthetics are probably what sparks many players’ interest in the Age of Chivalry - a bewildering variety of armour types and bold heraldry, and another reason why the French are such a good starter faction is the variety of styles they can present! If you like chainmail and tabards, the Feudal and Retinue Companies are a good choice for that 1350s chic. If you prefer sleeker, encasing plate, the Compagnie d’Ordonnance has got you covered. Wherever you pick up your knightly minis, they’ll slot into the French faction just fine. And, of course, you have full freedom to add as many clippy-cloppy horsies as your heart desires. They can wear plate too.

Eastern Princes cavalry slam into a Pagan Peoples battleline. Credit - Goremaw and James T, London SAGA Mead Hall

The ongoing war between Victrix and Wargames Atlantic [click for our affiliate links!] has recently blessed us with two fine ranges of plastic knights in chainmail, on foot and mounted. These will work well as Hearthguard for the earliest years of the HYW, and can pull a Home/Away double duty for the Age of Crusades while you’re at it. If you're going for the foot route, a bag or box of these will get you 32 or 24 knights respectively. Especially if you pick up a separate Warlord from the infinite ‘cool guy in armour’ choices available, that’s your whole warband right there. Any left over can be built a little less well-equipped to represent Warriors, although both Victrix and Wargames Atlantic have kits for serjeants and missile troops too! Wargames Atlantic’s Renaissance Heavy Cavalry box can also be built to represent mounted Hearthguard with barding in the Compagnie d’Ordonnance.

Although slightly older, Perry Miniatures also has a range of plastic kits specifically designed for the Hundred Years War and is currently still the broadest available array for covering the middle and end of the conflict. If you prefer metal minis like Gripping Beast’s, the French’s elite composition helps mitigate their higher cost. You might have to wait for the dust to settle on the Gripping Beast range though.

Painting the French

The Age of Chivalry is both extremely easy to paint for at minimum and extremely challenging at maximum. If you’re looking for speed, a man in unadorned plate puts Necron Warriors to shame - spray ‘em steel, paint in the leather details, base, wash and done. If you’re a freehand sicko, splendid heraldry beckons, but even if you’re not the shields and surcoats of French knights are a great place to apply all those cool spare Space Marine transfers you’ve got (or you can get specific transfer sets, for instance from Victrix). The HYW featured earlier, simpler heraldic designs; and the French were never as facetious about its rules as the English, so paint what you like! Arguments rage over how common painted plate was, but the answer seems to be ‘at least some of the time’, so you have freedom here too.

Go Play French

This comprehensive review should have prepared you well to enter SAGA with Studio Tomahawk’s own home nation and crush the foes of France under the thunder of hooves. Cheap to pick up and quick to paint, simple yet powerful in their abilities, and with a depth and flexibility to their gameplay to reward a veteran commander, the French are the Space Marines of the SAGA universe and I hope I’ve sold them to you as a starter faction you can’t go wrong with! En avant, hardiment, and win glory for the everlasting line of the Kings of France!
