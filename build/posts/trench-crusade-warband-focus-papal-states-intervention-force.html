Trench Crusade Warband Focus: Papal States Intervention Force

August 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome everyone to another Trench Crusade Warband focus! This time we’re looking into the Papal States Intervention Force. When the Supreme Pontiff of Rome wants something done, they send in this squad. Let’s take a look at why this force is one to be reckoned with. 

The Lore

Image: Beezer

Operating under the command of the Supreme Pontiff of Rome, these warriors are sent to the front lines on vital missions, from eliminating key leaders to recovering important artifacts of particular spiritual significance. 

The force is small but includes some of the most elite soldiers available, led by warrior monks and priests selected by the Pope themself. Backing up these soldiers is an arsenal of specialized equipment that no one else has access to.

The Variant

Before we get into things, let’s quickly break down the Variant’s rules: 

  • Far from Home: You cannot include Trench Moles.
  • Lector: You do not have to include a Lieutenant in your warband, but you must include a Trench Cleric, and this Cleric gains the Leader keyword, as well as the Arise and be Healed! ability: 
  • Arise and be Healed!: After a successful Risky Roll, the Cleric may pick another unit within 3” (including themselves). That unit stands up if Downed and can remove up to D3 Blood Markers and/or Infection Markers. 
  • Specialist Force: This rule is what makes this Variant so unique. Essentially, when you start a campaign, or even a one-off, you start off with 200 fewer Ducats than usual (so 500 Ducats for a first campaign game). However, you start off with 11 Glory (adjusting for one-offs or late campaign joining) to compensate.
  • Supreme Blessing: You must give the Supreme Pontiff’s Crucifix to one model at warband creation, at no extra cost. If you lose it, you gotta buy another. 
  • Swiss Guard: Your Lieutenant and up to 4 other models gain the Negate Fear Keyword for no additional cost (modeling these models with cool hats is optional, but recommended)

Whilst the amount of changes may be smaller than most variants, the impact of each individual change is far-reaching. The massive change to your warband construction is how you spend your Glory. Do you focus on Glory Items or Mercenaries? Glory Items can stay around and provide longer-term value when on your Elites, but Mercenaries pad out your unit count. Each has their pros and cons, and you’ll need experience to get a feel for this new way to build lists. Even when it comes to playing a game, this change significantly affects how you approach the scenario. Do you want to go for more Glory? How do you want to protect your Mercenaries, as the loss of a Merc is a big dent in your Glory economy?

Unique Battlekit

Supreme Pontiff’s Crucifix - 3 Glory - Limit: 1

A very simple but effective piece of Equipment, where the bearer’s activation does not immediately end if they fail a Risky Success Roll. This is such a great piece of Equipment for your Lector, who has two abilities that require a Risky Success Roll, on top of their Dash Action. Throw a Med-Kit on them too, or anything else that requires a Risky Success Roll, and you have a unit that can consistently contribute without fear of needing to map out their activation.

Key Units

Lector

Marco Veneziano. Image: Beezer

The Lector (aka, a Trench Cleric) will be your key piece in your warband, not only because it could potentially replace your Lieutenant, but also because their reliability can skyrocket, as they will most likely be the bearer of the Supreme Pontiff’s Crucifix. Not having to worry about the consequences of failing Dashes or using Arise and Be Healed! will ensure that the Lector gets to at least try and succeed at every ability they have (let’s not forget, God Is with Us also requires a Risky Success Roll) 

Recommend Starting Loadout: 

  • Automatic Pistol / Automatic Rifle / Submachine Gun
  • Supreme Pontiff’s Crucifix

Witchburner

Witchburner General. Credit: SRM

Now I know that you’re wondering why a Mercenary is a key unit for this Warband, but remember that Papal States gets easy access to Mercenaries thanks to a large amount of Glory upfront, and you need to bump up your unit count in the early game. 

With that in mind, the Witchburner is a great unit to take early. As they have the Elite keyword, you can start to generate Experience and gain skills for them early, really helping them contribute beyond their initial skillset. 

The Witchburner is slow to get up the board, but their worth comes from their Divine Judgment ability, which ignores Line of Sight and allowing you to dish out Blood Markers to the units that you want to. With a healthy 24” range that is not hindered by Cover or Long-Range penalties, it ensures the Witchburner contributes despite them needing to slowly swagger up the board. 

Communicant Anti-Tank Hunter

Communicant Anti-Tank Hunter. Credit: SRM

A staple in almost every list at some point, the Communicant Anti-Tank Hunter gives you amazing early game pressure, where your enemies' key long-range units need to be extra careful. Usually, an enemy Artillery Witch has relative freedom in early campaign games, as long-range weaponry is a little more sparse. However, with a Papal States force with a Communicant Anti-Tank Hunter, the Artillery Witch has to be very careful with their positioning; otherwise it goes bye-bye. 

Other Faithful factions can only dream of having such early game Armour Ignoring pressure. 

The List

Supreme Pwntiff

Faction: Papal States Intervention Force

Rating: 500 Ducats | 14 Glory

Patron: None

## Elites ##

Lector - 110D | 3G

Battlekit: Automatic Pistol, Medi-Kit, Supreme Pontiff ’s Crucifix, Standard Armour, Trench Shield

Sniper Priest - 76D

Battlekit: Automatic Pistol, Pistol

Witchburner - 5G

Battlekit: Gavel of Justice, Combat Helmet, Reinforced Armour

## Troops ##

Yeoman - 52D

Battlekit: Musical Instrument, Frag Grenades

Mechanized Heavy Infantry - 120D

Battlekit: Heavy Shotgun, Reinforced Armour, Satchel Charge

Combat Engineer - 105D

Battlekit: Shotgun, Shovel, Engineer Body Armour, Satchel Charge

Yeoman - 37D

Battlekit: Frag Grenades

## Mercenaries ##

Communicant Anti-Tank Hunter - 6G

Battlekit: Anti-Materiel Rifle, Trench Club, Trench Club, Combat Helmet

So, with this list, the most difficult aspect was keeping my Ducat cost down by including only the most vital aspects. With a 500 Ducat limit, you really have to stick to just the core aspects of New Antioch: a MHI and Combat Engineer with Satchel Charges, a cheap Yeoman (who will get Flamethrowers asap), and a cheap but effective Sniper Priest. Pistols and Automatic Pistols help ensure your models contribute in range and melee without pinching the purse too much. Grenades are on the cheap Yeoman to not only hold objectives, but put on Blood Markers for cheap. 

The Lector, however, is packed with more equipment. They, of course, take the Crucifix, and I added on a med-kit, so they can heal units twice without worrying about failing a Risky roll. 

The big downside here is the complete lack of a Tough model, so if things go well, it’s good to grab a Lieutenant to ensure you at least have some kind of Tough in the list and can be more aggressive. 

Final Thoughts

The Papal States play a completely different, but very fun game when it comes to campaign play. The Ducat change really forces you to think about what you want to include and to boil down your list to only the most vital units and pieces of Battlekit. There is no frivolity allowed, even more so than in a typical New Antioch list. The Glory changes allowing you to bring Mercenaries mean you can fill in gaps as you see fit, but the downside is that losing them hurts a lot.

If you want to try a unique way of playing New Antioch, give the Papal States a try, and bring the might of the Supreme Pontiff straight to the front lines!
