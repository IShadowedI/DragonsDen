[40k] 11th Edition Force Disposition Review: Disruption

June 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome to Tabletop Battles’ coverage of Disruption missions! In this article, we’ll look at each of the five possible missions that a Disruption army will be confronted with. We’ll explain how to score primaries for each objective in the missions, break down how your opponent will score, and then give tips on offense, defense, and list building (if applicable). Finally, we'll rate each mission on a scale of 1 to 5 to give an idea of the relative strength of the mission compared to their opponent’s mission. At the end, we’ll average out those results to give the whole Force Disposition a general strength rating.

There’s a lot to digest with the new set of rules, and foremost amongst them are the 25 (!) brand new primary missions! For this article, we’re looking at Disruption! Disruption missions have a lot of early scoring opportunities, a lot of mid-board control, and 0 opportunities to score your home objective. It rewards aggressive, reactive, and generally sneaky play, and if that’s the way you like to lay 40k, then you’re in luck. Let’s get into it!

In brackets at the end of each objective description you’ll see information detailing which battle rounds the objective is scored in and when in the turn they are scored. As always, Primary objectives are capped at 45 VP, and you can score a maximum of 15 VP per turn.

To evaluate matchups, we are scoring the matchup on a scale of 1-5 with the following criteria:

1 - Terrible. This is a very bad matchup. The player has a big deficit in the ease of scoring primaries to overcome.

2 - Disadvantaged. This is a bad matchup. The player has a slightly harder time scoring maximum primary points.

3 - Even. This is an even matchup. Either both players have the same mission (mirror match), or are roughly equal with regard to ease of scoring primaries.

4 - Advantaged. This is a good matchup. The player has a slightly easier time scoring max primaries.

5 - Great. This is a very good matchup. The player has an extremely favorable set of objectives relative to their opponent.

Finally, as we go through the mission descriptions, take note of the number of objectives attached to each one. It will be either five or six, and what those numbers really tell you is if there is a big central terrain area objective or not. If it’s five, then there will be a big central objective, and if it’s six, then that objective will be broken up into two smaller, triangular central objectives. Having one big objective in the middle of the table really impacts pace, flow, and scoring during a game, so keep an eye out.

But before we do that, we’d like to thank Games Workshop for sending us a preview copy of these rules for Review Purposes.

First up: The mirror match!

Outmanoeuvre (vs. Disruption) [5 Objectives]

Disruption vs. Disruption

This mission pits Disruption players against each other in what is essentially a battle to control the other's home objective. Your own home doesn't matter in the sense that it won't score you anything, but you don't want your opponent to take it. Otherwise, this one's pure objective control, and all three layouts are 5-objective maps.

How You Score

This mission has four ways to score points, though they’re staggered based on what battle round it is, so it’s really only two ways to score simultaneously:

1: Score 10 VP if you control your opponent’s home objective. [Any battle round, end of your turn]

2: Score 4 VP for each objective you control, excluding home. [1st battle round, end of your turn]

3: Score 5 VP for each objective you control, excluding home [2nd and 3rd battle rounds, end of your command phase]

4: Score 6 VP for each objective you control, excluding home. [4th battle round onwards, end of your turn]

So we’re standing on objectives! Easy peasy. The thing to remember here, though, is that not only does the amount you score change, when you score the points during the turn changes, too. Let’s take a look at how scoring should fold out:

  • Turn 1 - 12 VP (control 3 at end of turn)

  • Turn 2 - 5 to 10 VP (hold 1 or 2 objectives)

  • Turn 3 - 5 to 10 VP

  • Turn 4 - 12-15 VP

  • Turn 5 - 12-15 VP

It’s clear here that turns 1, 4, and 5 are going to be your big scoring turns. If you’re going 1st, you can pretty easily start the game with 12 VP, fall back a little during turns 2 and 3, and then make a big push on 4 and 5 to take advantage of that end-of-turn scoring. It shouldn’t be too hard to get to 45 in this mission, even if you end up going 2nd.

How Your Opponent Scores

It’s a mirror match, so your opponent is playing the exact same mission!

Tips for Offense

There’s no reason not to go for all 3 NML objectives on T1 with end-of-turn scoring, unless your army is low model count. And if that’s the case, maybe don’t play Disruption! Anyways, if you can score at least 8 on T1, and hold on to 1 objective on turns 2 and 3, then you’re set up to make a nice, big push on 4 and 5 to max out the score. And who knows, maybe you’ll back-cap your opponent for a nice extra chunk of VP. Though, you’ll probably want to go for it on T2 or T3, which is weird timing.

Tips for Defense

Well, you’re probably going to want to stop yourself from getting back-capped! Other than that, attempting to disable or nullify your opponent’s late-game push will be crucial. Packing objectives with high OC, getting some critical unit kills in, or move-blocking the objectives are all good strategies to stop the big push.

Tips for Listbuilding

Lots of MSUs with 2-3 infiltrating units will help with spreading out to score both early and late.

Matchup Evaluation

It’s a mirror match! This one’s easy.


Evaluation Score: 3

 

Death Trap (vs. Take and Hold) [5 Objectives]

Take and Hold vs Disruption

All three of these layouts are five-objective maps with symmetrical layouts. The Take and Hold player scores for holding objectives while the Disruption player has a special action they can do once on each terrain feature, and they're looking to do that action and then kill enemy units on that terrain feature.

How You Score

There are three ways for the Disruption player to score:

1: Score 2VP per terrain area trapped this turn (+3 pts if the terrain area is also an objective). [Any battle round, end of your turn]

Action - Booby Trap: 

  • Starts: Your shooting phase.

  • Eligible units: One unit within range of one objective that is not your home objective OR within one terrain area that is not within your deployment zone that is not yet trapped.

  • Use limit: Unlimited. Each unit must be within a different terrain area.

  • Completes: Immediately.

  • Effect: The terrain area is trapped. Place an operation marker within that terrain area.

An important thing to note here: You do NOT have to control an objective to trap it, and you can trap a terrain area with enemy units on it. Your unit just has to touch the target terrain area, and only that terrain area, to trap it.

The trapped condition does not expire or get removed regardless of how many of your opponent’s units are within the trapped terrain area.

2: Score 3VP if 1 or more enemy units were destroyed while within a trapped terrain area this turn. [Any battle round, end of your turn]

3: Score 4VP if you control 1 or more objectives, excluding their home objective [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of their turn in battle round 5]

Here’s what scoring will look like without counting points for killing units:

  • Turn 1 - 12 VP (trap the far expansion, the center, and the far NML ruins. Could be 14 if they want to trap the close NML ruins)

  • Turn 2 - 11 VP (trap the close expansion, the close NML ruins, score 4 for the close expansion)

  • Turn 3 - 4 VP (controlling close expansion)

  • Turn 4 - 4 VP

  • Turn 5 - 4 VP

Before killing any enemy units, the Disruption player can net 35 VP in primary points. You’ll need a bunch of infiltrators or extremely fast units to do it, but that isn’t really a problem for most armies. So, to max out primary, you’ll need to kill 4 of their units standing in trapped terrain areas and/or sneak into their backfield and trap stuff in their territory. Not particularly difficult to do, especially if you bring deep strikers and fast infantry.

How Your Opponent Scores (Determined Acquisition)

There are two ways to score in this mission:

1: Score 2 VP for each objective you control that they did not control at the start of the turn, excluding home objective. [Any battle round, end of their turn]

2: Score 3 VP for each objective they control (+3 VP if that objective is within your opponent’s territory). [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]

It is important to note that the big, central objective is within the Disruption player’s territory, so holding on to it is worth 6 VP per turn!

Here’s what scoring will probably look like:

  • Turn 1 - 4 VP (2 each for the close expansion and central objectives)

  • Turn 2 - 8 VP if they score home, close expansion, and have central at end of turn, but it jumps up to 14 VP if they could have held on to the central from last turn.

  • Turn 3 - 8 to 14 VP

  • Turn 4 - 8 to 14 VP

  • Turn 5 - 14 to 15 VP

Without getting the bonus points for controlling objectives in enemy territory all game and only re-capping the central objective every turn, they’re going to net 36 VP. Which means that they only need to hold on to an objective within the enemy territory 3 times during the game to max primary. And one of those instances can be done on T5, when it’s at the end of the turn! It might not be automatic to max primary, but it’s pretty dang close.

Tips for Offense

Getting out there to score a bunch of primary VP on T1 is absolutely necessary. You’ll need units that can infiltrate or jump infantry to do it, so plan accordingly! After the glut of scoring on T1 and 2, you’ll need to fort up on an expansion objective and blast the enemy off of the central objective or another trapped terrain area every turn. Finally, if you can sneak into their backfield late game, trapping their home objective will net you a cool 5 VP, so look for opportunities to do it!

Tips for Defense

You’ve got to bottle the Take and Hold player up in their own table half. If they have the temerity to try to score an objective in your board half, stop what you’re doing and focus fire until their units on it are dead! Most likely this will be the big, central objective, so get ready to battle it out over the middle. But after you’ve trapped it, you can stay off of it, so don’t overreach!

Tips for Listbuilding

2-3 units of infiltrating infantry are necessary for the T1 scoring bonanza. Just make sure to back them up with some scary damage dealers so your opponent can’t just move up and take them out with impunity if they go first!

Matchup Evaluation

This one’s pretty even! The Take and Hold player has a slight lead in low-effort primary VP, but on 2 out of 3 layouts, you'll have the edge in objective placement and the opportunity to back-cap.


Rob: I'd actually put this one at a 2 for the Disruption player. My experience on this mission is that the Disruption player tends to score 10+ VP on each of the first two turns as they come out and trap every terrain feature they can reach, then they just get murdered trying to cross mid table by the Take and Hold player, who doesn't have to worry about actions and can focus on killing the Disruption player.

Evaluation Score: 2.5

 

Delaying Action (vs Purge the Foe) [5 objectives]

Purge the Foe vs Disruption

All three of the layouts for this one are 5-objective maps, which tend to be favorable for the Purge player as they make the board a much deadlier place when there's one big ruin in the middle they can toe in to see through.

How You Score

Three ways to score in this mission:

1: Score 2 VP for each enemy unit destroyed this turn [Any battle round, end of your turn]

2: Score 4 VP if you control 1 or more objectives, excluding home [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in battle round 5]

3: Score 3 VP if you control 1 or more central and 1 or more expansion objectives [2nd battle round onwards, end of your turn]

Let’s see how ideal scoring will look without adding points for killing units:

  • Turn 1 - 0

  • Turn 2 - 7 (hold one, control 1 expansion and central at end of turn)

  • Turn 3 - 7

  • Turn 4 - 7

  • Turn 5 - 7

So, before getting credit for killing stuff, you’re looking at an extremely anemic 28 VP. And that’s if you can hold on to the center pretty much the whole game, which is not likely! To max your score here, you’re looking at killing 9 enemy units. Seems like it might be a bit of an uphill climb!

How Your Opponent Scores (Punishment)

Four ways to score for the Purge player:

1: Score 5 VP if 1 or more condemned units left the battlefield this turn. [Any battle round, end of any turn]

At the start of the Purge player’s turn, they can select up to three enemy units on the battlefield that are either within range of an objective or have destroyed one or more of the Purge player’s units in the previous turn. Until the start of their next turn, those units are condemned.

It is important to note that this objective can be scored in either player’s turn, though there is still a cap of 15 VP per battle round for primary scoring.

2: Score 4 VP if they control one or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of turn in battle round 5]

3: Score 5 VP if they control more objectives than you. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of turn in battle round 5]

4: Score 8 VO if they control their opponent’s home objective. [End of battle]

Hoo boy. This is one of the most aggressive and rewarding set of objectives I’ve ever seen! Let’s see how it scores without counting points for kills:

  • Turn 1 - 0

  • Turn 2 - 9

  • Turn 3 - 9

  • Turn 4 - 9

  • Turn 5 - 9-15

If the Purge player solely plays objectives, they can easily net 36 VP, or 42 if they take your home objective. That’s without killing a single condemned unit! That’s crazy! On top of that, any units you have on objectives can get condemned and killed, netting the Purge player 5 VP. And if a condemned unit dies in melee on your turn? Then the Purge player will get 5 VP towards that round’s cap of 15 VP. Furthermore, the kill stuff trade is bad from the disruption player’s standpoint. To keep up on kill points, you’ve got to kill 3 enemy units for each condemned unit killed. Not a great exchange rate! Some (me) would call this “pushed”.


Rob: The best part is that on round 1 the Purge player can use be aggressive and tricky with the Condemned factor and use it to "freeze" a unit on the table - if that unit goes back into reserves or hops into a transport, they'll score points for it leaving the battlefield.

Tips for Offense

Well, you’ve got to fort up on the close expansion, and then try to keep sneaking units onto the center at the end of every turn. The thing is, those units will get condemned and likely killed. And then you’re in the hole because of the aforementioned kill point exchange rate. The only way out is through, I guess, and you’ve got to be ready to aggressively capitalize on kill points.

Tips for Defense

First off, condemned units can’t uppy-downy or embark in a Transport. That’s just free points. Second, you have to be careful that you’re not giving up condemned kills in your turn. And finally, if you can knock units off of objectives to keep them from scoring the extra 5 for holding more, then you’ve got to.

Matchup Evaluation

On Wednesday, Rob and Shane rated this matchup a 3.5 out of 5 for the Purge player. I disagree! I think this matchup heavily favors the Purge player, allowing them to dig a very deep hole that the Disruption player can really only get out of by tabling or nearly tabling their opponent. I’m inclined to say that this is going to be a pretty gnarly uphill climb for team Disruption.


Evaluation Score: 1.5

 

Locate and Deny (vs Priority Assets) [5 Objectives]

Priority Assets vs. Disruption

Ooh! The rare smaller central objective! How novel! Strap in, because this mission is a LOT. First, at the start of the battle, select 5 terrain areas not within your DZ and place an operation marker in them. There are 5 NML terrain areas for this layout, so I’d suggest you place your markers in those instead of your opponent’s DZ.

How You Score

There are four ways to score for this mission:

1: Score 4 VP if 1 or more enemy units that started the turn within range of an objective are destroyed. [Any battle round, end of your turn]

2: Score 4 VP if only one operation marker is on the battlefield and you have one or more friendly units within that terrain area and there are no enemy units within that terrain area. [Any battle round, end of your turn]

Each player can sensor sweep once per turn which allows them to remove an operation marker. Sensor sweep is an action that you can do when a friendly unit is within range of a central objective and completes at the end of your turn if you control that objective. Both players can do this, identically. For the sake of brevity, the remaining operation marker will be called “the relic”.

3: Score 4 VP if you control 1 or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of your turn in battle round 5]

4: Score 5 VP at end of battle if you control the relic and no enemy units are within it. [End of battle]

Yeah, so this one’s kind of a shitshow. Let’s look at idealized scoring without kill points:

  • Turn 1 - 0 VP

  • Turn 2 - 4 VP (from holding 1 objective)

  • Turn 3 - 8 VP (holding 1, holding the relic)

  • Turn 4 - 8 VP

  • Turn 5 - 13 VP (holding 1, scoring the relic, holding the relic at end of battle)

That’s 33 VP, meaning that you only need to score 12 VP from the kill points objective to max out primary. But here’s the thing: it might not look like this at all. The PA player wants to do actions because it will get them VP. And you want to be able to dictate where the relic ends up, so you have to be judicious as to whether or not you’re going to interact with this process at all. Some of the TTB brain trust think that the disruption player should just ignore that part of the mission completely and focus on other ways to score and secondaries. Others think that you have to time it right by trying to perform the 2nd sensor sweep to try to choose the right spot for the relic. I’m not sure which way is better, and it’s going to take repetition for people to figure it out.

How Your Opponent Scores (Extract Relic)

Oh wow, five different ways to score here:

1: Score 4 VP for performing a sensor sweep [Any battle round, end of their turn]

2: Score 3 VP if 1 or more enemy units on an objective were destroyed [Any battle round, end of their turn]

3: After the relic is revealed, score 4 VP if they control it and no enemy units are within it [Any battle round, end of their turn]

4: Score 4 VP if they control 1 or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of their turn in battle round 5]

5: Score 5 VP at end of battle if they control the relic and no enemy units are within it. [End of battle]

Alright, let’s take a look at scoring, but remember: this might not be all that helpful. Again, this is without scoring for kill points:

  • Turn 1 - 4 VP

  • Turn 2 - 8 VP

  • Turn 3 - 8 VP

  • Turn 4 - 8 VP

  • Turn 5 - 13 VP

That’s 37, which is a slight edge for the PA player. They’d need to kill 3 of your units on objectives to max out.

Tips for Offense

Other than shrugging and saying in a thick Australian accent, “Good farkin’ luck, mate,” I’d say you need to capitalize on destroying enemy units on the central objective, as the PA player will have to repeatedly send bozos over there to do the sensor sweep. Other than that, you’ve got to fort up on the close expansion and try to pounce on the relic when it is revealed.

Tips for Defense

I’m not sure you spend any time doing sensor sweeps. If they kill the unit what done it, which is likely as they have to stand on the central objective, that’s kill points for them. So maybe you just focus on stopping the relic from even happening at all. I’m not entirely sure. After people spend some time playing this mission, I’d love to hear what everyone thinks.

Matchup Evaluation

Honestly no clue, this one’s a bit of a head-scratcher. Even though I’m not sure how canny players will game this one, the passive scoring scores suggest a slight advantage to the Priority Assets player.


Evaluation Score: 2

 

Smoke and Mirrors (vs Reconnaissance) [6 Objectives]

Recon vs. Disruption

Six objectives and an action! You love to see it!

How You Score

Three ways to score:

1: Score 2 VP for each objective that is decoyed (+2 if the objective is within the opponent’s territory). [Any battle round, end of your turn]

Decoy is your standard “do this on a non-home objective” that completes at end of turn. It has unlimited uses, though, so the Disruption player can kinda go hog wild early in the battle. Markers on decoyed objectives are immediately removed if an enemy unit ends a move within range of the objective, so objectives can be scored multiple times per game.

2: Score 4 VP if you control 1 or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of your turn in battle round 5]

3: Score 10 VP if four or more objectives are decoyed [End of battle]

Scoring is hard to predict on this one, but there’s nothing stopping the Disruption player from going all out on T1 to snag a bunch of points:

  • Turn 1 - 8 to 12 VP

  • Turn 2 - 4 to 8 VP

  • Turn 3 - 4 to 8 VP

  • Turn 4 - 4 to 8 VP

  • Turn 5 - 15 VP

Scoring 15 on T5 is probably a lot harder if you went hog wild on T1, but decoying both of the close objectives and the far central on T1 is completely doable. If you want to spend an infiltrating unit to try and get the far expansion on T1, you can, but if you don’t go first, that unit is almost certainly dead as hell. So on the low end, you’re looking at 35 VP, which includes a T5 push to decoy 4 or more objectives.

How Your Opponent Scores (Surveil the Foe)

Four ways to score here:

1: Score 4 VP if 1 or more enemy units were surveilled this turn unless each of the surveilled units are within range of a decoyed objective. [Any battle round, end of turn]

Surveilling a unit is as easy as being within 18” with visibility. Surveilled goes away at the end of the turn. It completes immediately.

2: Score 4 VP if they control 1 or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of their turn in battle round 5]

3: Score 4 VP if they control more objectives than their opponent. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of their turn in battle round 5]

4: Score 5 VP if no objectives are decoyed. [2nd battle round onwards, end of turn]

Ok, let’s look at how scoring will break down:

  • Turn 1 - 4 VP (surveillance)

  • Turn 2 - 8 VP (surveillance and control 1)

  • Turn 3 - 8 VP

  • Turn 4 - 8 VP

  • Turn 5 - 15 VP

That’s 43 without having to control more objectives all game. It’s pretty trivial for the Recon player to nab 15 on T5 with all the end of turn scoring, so, yeah. This one looks pretty bad for the Disruption player.

Tips for Offense

With a large deficit in easy primary scoring, it might be necessary to go for the 4-decoy turn on T1 and make a huge push for a big turn on T5. However, if you’re going second, the 4-decoy turn might be out of reach. And that’s going to be a big hole to dig out of. The Recon player doesn’t have to, and shouldn’t, spend any time getting rid of your operation markers until they can get rid of all of them. Which they don’t have to do until T5 or so. But if they mess up and pull decoys off objectives that you could easily re-cap, you’ll need to move quickly to re-capture them, as you’re gonna need the points.

Tips for Defense

Don’t get seen! Hide as much as your army as possible, and stick to decoyed objectives if you can to deny the foe their 4 surveillance points.

Tips for Listbuilding

Once again, you’re going to want a bevy of infiltrators to help you get out ahead quickly.

Matchup Evaluation

Seems like this one’s pretty easy for the Recon player. It’s an uphill climb for sure.


Evaluation Score: 2

 

Final Thoughts

Evaluation Average: 2.2

Sheesh, I’m not a big fan of that average. 2.2 is the lowest we’ve seen so far, without any favorable matchups in the bunch. Disruption missions are among the most diverse missions sets around, and that’s not a good thing. A lot of the good 40k armies excel at doing one thing, while Disruption missions necessitate flexibility. Not ideal.

However, if you’re looking for a challenge, and you like flexible, interesting, and diverse matchups, then Disruption might be perfect for you. But, if what we expect is true, that Purge the Foe is among the most popular Dispositions, you might have a bad, bad time at an event with Disruption. Either way, thanks for reading!
