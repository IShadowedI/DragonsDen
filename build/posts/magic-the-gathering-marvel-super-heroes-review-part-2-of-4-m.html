Magic: The Gathering Marvel Super Heroes Review, Part 2 of 4: Multicolor Cards

June 18th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This week we tune in to watch all of your favorite Marvel heroes enter the realm of Magic, presumably with a mandatory Excelsior! In this episode, Magic players are being introduced to a swath of new cards as their favorite Heroes and Villains duke it out with the power of friendship and dastardly plots. In this article we’ll talk about the multicolor and colorless cards from the set, and offer some thoughts on what they mean for Commander and other formats.

Marvel Super Heroes will release to Magic: the Gathering Online and Arena on June 23rd, and to the tabletop on June 26th. Commander: Marvel Super Heroes will release on the same date.

Multicolor

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Thanos, the Mad Titan

Saffgor: The funny thing is that I don't actually think Thanos is especially good as a wipe. Sure, a take on Extinction Event with a body is cool, but some things persist, and the arguably better part from a 60-card midrange perspective is being a 4/4 Deathtouch Lifelink for 3.

FromTheShire: Thanos seems like he represents the version from the MCU quite well, in that this plan also doesn't really solve things. As just a body he's not actively terrible or anything in Standard but he's not a massive threat even when he is actually attacking and when he isn't he's not advancing your game plan in a very fast current meta. I can't see your mana base being able to support reliably activating him on curve, especially with needing a colorless source. Much more likely he is getting hit with a Boomerang Basics or a sideboard Sear and you're dying. In Commander he's again flavorful but not particularly powerful.

BPhillipYork: This is okay for a 5-color commander. The built in sort of board clear is nice, hitting it from the Command Zone for 6 mana is interesting, 3 colored mana for a 4/4 with deathtouch and lifelink is itself solid. And this is a decent way of showing the Thanos version from the movies, so its fine if not particularly exciting. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Absorbing Man

Saffgor: The fact this says enchantment & land means some very silly things can occur, but the fact he says 'first main phase' means it's probably too slow for onboard combos to work. Worse than you'd think.

FromTheShire: Yeah I'm sure there's some wild combos you can pull off here but we have plenty of copy and clone effects already so it's not doing anything particularly exciting for me.

BPhillipYork: Copying weird things is fun, and there's some interesting things you can do with man lands and other things that are already 4/4 but mostly I can't think of anything all that useful to do with what is essentially a doppelganger + in the Command Zone. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Moon Girl and Devil Dinosaur

Saffgor: A 'fair' value engine like this really doesn't do enough in the modern day, though as a Voltron list at Bracket 2, I could see her.

BPhillipYork: This is potentially a lot of card draw with off-turn Treasure triggers. I wouldn't want to run this as a commander really, but it could be just powerful card draw if you have ways to trigger it every turn.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Astonishing Ant-Man

Saffgor: I think he gets out of hand fast, and while he is a tad slow to get going, any form of doubler is going to make this cool at low power. I expect to see him, but I wouldn't build him.

FromTheShire: A classic Simic counters and tokens card, this basically does exactly what you would expect. Quite powerful if your doubler affects both at once.

BPhillipYork: Well this is fun, if not really great per se. Simic is always great at drawing tons of cards and then you can just dump out a horde of ants which is, I dunno, a horde of ants. In red or black this could be nasty but in blue green it's not so amazing. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Worlds Within Worlds

Saffgor: It's like Living Death...but from the hand! At 7 it's probably too expensive, but it's surprising this design feels so fresh. Cool take on a classic.

FromTheShire: Well if you have 7 mana and a grip full of cards this should certainly win you the game if you're teched for it. That was already likely the case in that scenario however. Pretty neat though!

BPhillipYork: This is an okay way to bust out a huge combo win, or some nasty combination of creatures and also clear the board of creature based stax pieces. I think there's some really nasty things you could do as the active player, like put out a Containment Priest. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Super Hero Civil War

Saffgor: What, were they saving the name 'Civil War' for the eventual U.S. History UB set? Mouthful aside, this is pretty fun to blink if you have a sacrifice outlet.

BPhillipYork: Pretty expensive for that effect, this feels like a card they just had to do because story.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Coming of Galactus

Saffgor: Fittingly spooky, this feels very much akin to Dark Depths, but with counter increasing like Proliferate, rather than counter removal.

FromTheShire: A Golgari midrange deck does exist in Standard already, and this would be a pretty fun include. While the deck isn't tier 1 it does have the usual Llanowar Elves and Badgermole Cub package to ramp into this ahead of curve, and this certainly has inevitably packed in.

BPhillipYork: Yeah I really kind of like this, its not amazingly fast or good unless you generate read ahead, but every stage does useful things and if you have ways to recur it it gets nasty. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Serpent Society

Saffgor: That Ward cost is hiliarioussssss, but I doubt this is actually fun to play in the Command Zone. I've built Savra, Queen of the Golgari, and it's the kind of design you disassemble once everyone groans upon its arrival at the pod.

FromTheShire: People have been freaking out about this card in some places online and I really don't think it's that deep. In Standard the first ward payment is largely free since without Fynn, the Fangbearer there are almost no poison creatures, and then after that this is a hexproof 3/4 that can still be chumped or traded with that requires significant deck warping to get value off of. In Commander....board wipes exist, and are in fact common. Like yes this deck can do some dangerous stuff with the right draws and I'm sure I'll lose to it on occasion but I'm just not that worried about it.

BPhillipYork: That is an absolutely bonkers ward. Love it. I love deathtouch matters cards too, and they are generally in Golgari, so I like this. Combining this with something like Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons is fairly mean. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Armor Wars

Saffgor: Oh so this gets the snappy 2-word name. Lucky for us, it's also better, being a fantastic gameplan for an Artifact deck, starting you with a new hand, then discounts, and finally a huge chunk of damage. Great design.

FromTheShire: At the right point in the game that's a hell of a lot of card draw.

BPhillipYork: So the first chapter of this Saga is potentially huge, and blue can flicker things, and that's really all that matters. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Speedball, New Warrior

Saffgor: This guy is wacky, and part of me wishes we got a MDFC with Penance on the back. As it stands, he rewards you for spells that target, and has functional Hexproof...but with no Haste or evasion I'm not convinced he gets there as a Commander. Very cool design though, and surprisingly resilient.

FromTheShire: Probably too expensive for the prowess decks, especially with no evasion. There are still a bunch of pump spells around though so maybe this does something unexpected.

BPhillipYork: So I guess you cast like Lightning Bolt on your own guy, and give him +2/+2, and then hit something else, which is pretty fun. But overall this seems like a less good version of decks that have been floating around a while, like Feather, the Redeemed though maybe in stronger colors.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Vision Quest

Saffgor: Whoa, Creature Whir. This is going to find a lot of Scrap Trawlers, and even more Esper Sentinels. I appreciate the name is generic enough to be reprintable, because this feels like a staple.

FromTheShire: Agreed, very powerful effect with a lot of fun targets. Even better that it hits both graveyard and library and can give haste to KO someone out of nowhere.

BPhillipYork: There's definitely strong enablers and cards that are themselves tutors this is good to go get, though usually when tutoring for artifacts it's non-creature ones you are looking for. Nonetheless a useful tutor. Or if you have a huge amount of mana you get Blightsteel Colossus and instagib someone. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Winter Soldier, Icy Assassin

Saffgor: I think this is a new space for Equipment, and allows you to cheat some huge costs, both on Commander Tax & Equip abilities. Really cool with a sacrifice outlet, even cooler with counter removal, and there's a world where this guy is a sleeper hit for Voltron aficionados. You have White for a Faith's Reward after a Worldslayer, you know.

FromTheShire: Definitely cool to get an Equipment matters commander on Orzhov, and having vigilance and menace built in make him even more of a banger. The activated ability also opens up an interesting self mill angle for reanimation and things like Brilliant Restoration or the many one off artifact returners found in white. I could easily see building this guy for fun.

BPhillipYork: Solid enough, especially as a way to cheaply attach Equipment, though I'm not that fond of these "buff for each Equipment" effects. The cost of getting and attaching a ton of Equipment is just too high. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Alien Invasion

Saffgor: Another card that quickly gets out of hand with Proliferate, though it's odd this wasn't a Plan, as it easily could have been.

FromTheShire: We have seen Urabrask's Forge see a bunch of play and be very good not that long ago and Assemble the Legion was a house so this could absolutely be a thing. Admittedly the extra mana and lack of trample on the tokens vs Forge are downsides, but they rapidly scale out of range for damage based removal which is solid.

BPhillipYork: Sort of fun but expensive and doesn't scale that rapidly. If it made an Alien token for each counter on it, then it would be growing into a very rapid threat. As it is it's just kind of whatever. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Storm, Windrider

Saffgor: Reverse Moat in the Command Zone...ish. Not just that, with some clever casting you can force cards into the air, and likely at other players. What a cool take on politics! Gets to use Repartee from Secrets of Strixhaven too, which I'm sure was intended.

FromTheShire: I would absolutely be running both with a bunch of protection and stax pieces because I am a bad person.

BPhillipYork: This is neat, sort of fun to have flying become not an asset suddenly. Also fun that Storm herself can basically only be blocked by creatures with reach, you could use this with Giant Growths and such to generate a nearly unblockable horde pretty fast and just bowl people over. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Wolverine, Fierce Fighter

Saffgor: This is a deeply difficult-to-parse wording that basically means 'cannot die to damage less than 5 in one instance'. Annoying, and not even very good.

FromTheShire: The ability reflects the character well, the card is not good.

BPhillipYork: Neat way of trying to deal with regeneration that isn't just regeneration, and you end up possibly in a funny situation of wanting to ping your own guy to heal him, but it's not like 5 damage is that hard to do at one go. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ares, God of War

Saffgor: This is a pretty cheap version of this effect, although it's of course limited by needing to attack. It can reset things like Port Razer with the mana available, but I wonder if this is too cute.

FromTheShire: This seems like it might be a menace with a bunch of cheap threats in Standard. There isn't really an existing Rakdos shell that this fits in that I'm aware of but maybe one emerges, or a Grixis version.

BPhillipYork: This is potentially really brutal with various ways to keep mana around and dumping out cheap creatures with ETB abilities. Or you have an Impact Tremors, or death triggers, and you sacrifice them after dealing damage or they get blocked and they pop back. Can build around this in Red/Black really easily. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Avengers: Under Siege

Saffgor: This kinda just goes in every 'fair' Villain typal deck that can play it, surely. Hard not to like the card if you're playing fair magic, and typal often is.

BPhillipYork: It's fine, I guess, I suppose there's enough Villains now to build decks around them as a type, but there's probably not enough payoff cards for that yet.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Scientist Supreme of A.I.M.

Saffgor: This enables a host of combos, though the main one is by way of Time Sieve and/or Conjurer's Closet. This just reads as very frightening, so even in spite of being 2 mana I think it eats removal pretty often, and isn't compact enough to make a true impact on cEDH. In short: Poorly positioned, but potentially powerful.

FromTheShire: Agreed, the ability is dangerous enough to require removal, but not good enough if played fairly. Probably pops up doing something wild in 60 card.

BPhillipYork: Really cheap single shot copy source, though you could end up with ways of blinking this. It's even possible to do it with what you are copying and that can probably be built into a loop, though with the 2 life loss you need a life gain trigger in there or it's hardly infinite. I think this guy is probably more threatening than actually effective. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Taskmaster, Mercenary Mimic

Saffgor: Cool spin on reanimator but that timing, lord, this is just another Absorbing Man! Let us have our fun, this is probably just worse than Superior Spider-Man.

BPhillipYork: Another copy thing, I think there's a lot of doppelgangers in Marvel because it's basically just soap operas with super powers.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Captain America, Living Legend

Saffgor: I'll touch on it more in our WUB review, but the uncommons in the set might be the most interesting Commanders. This version of Cap is fascinating, and makes for so many routes to victory. Vehicles? Spacecraft? Untap shenanigans? He's open-ended and gated in power, but not so much so that you can't do some nasty things.

FromTheShire: Even when he's just granting pseudo-vigilance he's pretty good, and then there's a bunch of other fun stuff you can do with the many tap abilities out there.

BPhillipYork: My brain immediately goes to like, mana dorks, but it's only 1 time per turn. Still could effectively generate a lot of mana this way quickly but I think it probably just ends up being a value engine and you run a lot of things that tap to do things.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Mighty Thor, Jane Foster

Saffgor: An interesting way to reset things like For Mirrodin! & Living Weapon, and with Flying she can probably come out early enough to draw at least 1 card per turn. It's an odd niche for Equipment, but...maybe?

FromTheShire: I think if you want to do this blink game plan you just play Brago, King Eternal instead. If you have an existing Voltron deck containing the colors it's not a bad draw engine in the 99 though.

BPhillipYork: Seems like the obvious play here is to keep having Equipment enter over and over, and would play pretty nicely with some of the For Mirrodin! triggers or Phyrexian Germs (though you'd need a way to gain value from 0/0s). Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

King T'Challa//Black Panther, Hope Enduring

Saffgor: 'A player' includes you, if you cantrip, and with Flash this is basically a slightly more flexible (and expensive) Faerie Mastermind. Given Mastermind is among the best Blue creatures in the format, that's great news, but it's a shame his back side is so chumpable.

FromTheShire: Made me think of Drogskol Reaver from days gone by because I am old. This feels very solid in the control decks. Most of the time you just want the 2 extra cards per turn cycle but there will be times where the transform closes out games for you still.

BPhillipYork: 3 mana to draw when opponents draw their second is strong on it's own, and having that in the Command Zone is solid. Flash on top of it is great. Paying 6 more to get a double striker with a Magpie effect (twice) is to me not as exciting, but still a nice back up option. And a with a bit of buffing (+8 power) you can just take people out in one turn, which is always nice. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Monica Rambeau//Photon, Living Light

Saffgor: This design bores me, it's just soup you can't interact with that gets big. Quintessential Bracket 2 bully that fails the moment someone plays anything approaching a combo deck.

BPhillipYork: This seems fine for prowess decks that want to try to snowball someone out, and have a backup plan of transforming her. There's definitely ways you could use red cast triggers to draw through your deck, generating Monks and pumping them up and just killing everyone off, but it's a bit involved.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Tony Stark//The Invincible Iron Man

Saffgor: Tony's good to cast early, and can dig for the mana rocks you need to flip him, that's a nifty bit of design. Once he is on the back side, that combat trigger rocks, and can be spun up into multiple drops by way of Red's access to extra combats, and Blue's access to extra turns. I smell a popular pick.

FromTheShire: On this one the flipped version is for sure what you are building around, and 'free artifact' can place some terrifying things directly into play. Some of those things are even Equipment with big equip costs you'll be happy to cheat as well.

BPhillipYork: This is solid to dig to get artifacts, and eventually transform Iron Man to get it for free. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Jennifer Walters//The Sensational She-Hulk

Saffgor: Oh hell yeah, more Voice of Victory effects? This is the cheapest we've ever seen of this effect in the Command Zone, and I aim to use it solely for nefarious purposes. I'll be covering what she enables at some point soon in a full article (It's Land destruction).

FromTheShire: Keeping the ability on both sides is excellent because in Commander turning off spells during your turn should be a giant flashing warning sign to the table that you are planning to do some heinous shit. Naturally this means your deck should in fact be full of heinous shit to do, and these colors have plenty of things that fit the bill.

BPhillipYork: Okay well, the thing is "opponents can't cast spells during your turn" is a super powerful hatebear ability and kind of crazy to have in the Command Zone. I don't really know what the payoff is though, but combo protection is super nice. The transform here is kind of whatever, but I suppose it can just be a bog standard green/white go wide and pump and kill deck. Though it makes me think people won't let you untap with her on the board, since you could easily drop a Triumph of the Hordes or a big green pumper or whatever. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Bruce Banner//The Incredible Hulk

Saffgor: The visual of Hulk getting so mad he stepped on Caltrops he kills everyone is hilarious, and will be a common sight at your LGS. That said, I don't think he's especially good, even in the context of a ~1.5 card combo, and probably caps out at a low Bracket 4. Just too telegraphed.

FromTheShire: Funny to do once but seems like he would get boring for me at least.

BPhillipYork: This is Temur sort of wrapped up in a package, draw and transform and then various ways to generate infinite combat steps. There's Caltrops, but there's also just pinging him for one, weird stuff like using your own Desert on him, etc. It's all expensive, mana wise, so its fairly balanced.

Lands & Colorless

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Avengers Tower

Saffgor: Hero typal Land. Next.

FromTheShire: Well at least it's less parasitic than these set types frequently are because we know we're for sure getting more Heroes in the future.

BPhillipYork: It's okay, just a 'can cast Heroes' and buff with Heroes card, which is a pretty normal standard utilitarian enabler at this point. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Baxter Building

Saffgor: Get it, it says 'four' a bunch! Next.

FromTheShire: This feels like it maybe sneaks a copy into 4 color control for fixing purposes and a potential mana sink late game.

BPhillipYork: It's not good but I guess if you're into the Fantastic Four (I'm not). Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Castle Doom

Saffgor: This is actually interesting as another Land with the capacity to sacrifice an Artifact, something previously found on Phyrexia's Core, and used to break parity on stax effects. In decks that want it, this probably slots in for low opportunity cost.

FromTheShire: It wants to go in very specific decks but yeah not a bad utility piece. As a side note, why are these not all Legendary? Like they're not good enough to be but these are very much specific named locations.

BPhillipYork: Kind of unusual to have a land that can generate creatures so consistently, which is kind of fun. Solid color fixing for artifact decks that um, care about colors. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Villainous Hideout

Saffgor: Villain typal Land. Guys, really, did we need to?

FromTheShire: I mean it is only fair.

BPhillipYork: Okay this is the opposite land for the Hero one. Much better in my opinion, conniving is stronger than looking at the top 3 and hoping. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Arc Reactor

Saffgor: The biggest thing this does is serve as a (usually) mana-positive rock once you have your deck in your hand, but not infinite mana. Outside of that context, it likely replaces Thran Dynamo in most shells that play it, a rapidly-shrinking niche but one that persists nonetheless. Nice to have access to.

FromTheShire: You probably don't have a ton of non-mana rock artifacts out early for the improvise outside of very specific decks so it's not exactly a new staple. Since it enters tapped I don't even know that I would consider it as a Dynamo replacement actually.

BPhillipYork: This is an actual contender for a good new mana rock of the expensive but reducible variety. If you have lots of Treasures or other kinds of artifacts and a need for tons of colorless mana, this is a solid way to get it.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Captain America's Shield

Saffgor: Toughness-matters and 'butts' decks really like this, and it looks especially good for The Pride of Hull Clade, which doesn't inherently get evasion, and usually runs some number of activated ability-centric mana.

FromTheShire: Kind of weird since toughness matters decks are already usually well above rate once you get them able to attack, giving +8 to one creature doesn't feel like the area where you're going to be falling short and need to shore your plan up.

BPhillipYork: I guess the whole tapping thing is knocking people down or whatever, and then he has his untapping thing, which I guess is like him giving someone a hand, so that's fine. I think this card will be used in "toughness as power" decks more than anything. Which I sort of enjoy the irony of. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Cosmic Cube

Saffgor: This is pretty Chimil, the Inner Sun-core, likely a brand-new sentence. Ultimately, the fact you can whiff probably makes it worse.

FromTheShire: Seems like a big cost and a relatively large hoop to jump through to potentially get nothing, or at least nothing impactful. Breaking timing restrictions does make it at least somewhat interesting though.

BPhillipYork: This is potentially really stupid. Like for Eldrazi. Yes you already have massive amounts of mana, but why not just dump out even more huge juggernaut Eldrazi things. Other than that can just be used if you have a bigly power commander and a deck full of midrange and utility. Since most decks do this by default its a decent value card. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Iron Man Armor

Saffgor: This isn't exciting, but having the ability to retain an attacker/blocker after a board wipe as an Equipment deck is very relevant. I'd toss it in there on spec you'll see removal, and want a suit to suit up.

FromTheShire: Flying is a solid bit of evasion for your Voltron deck and it does come with a stat boost so that's nice as well. Potentially pumping out big damage by equipping whatever other Equipment you have laying around for a turn is a neat trick too.

BPhillipYork: Neat, if not overwhelming. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Super-Adaptoid

Saffgor: The fact this generates counters means it can do deeply funny things with The Ozolith effects and the like. For that reason, I'd love to see it as a 'Secret Commander', but probably not the Command Zone proper.

FromTheShire: Note that it doesn't have to steal the ability from one of your creatures either. A cool card but yeah unlikely to be one you run as your commander since colorless legendary typal isn't a thing.

BPhillipYork: It's fun to get all those keywords onto someone, or just play Atraxa (watch out for falling buildings off-screen though). Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Ten Rings

Saffgor: Weirdly enough this seems like a worse The Endstone, because if you think you're getting to do this more than once you're out of your mind. I prefer just the one ring, thanks.

FromTheShire: You for sure want to be cheating this out with Goblin Welder or flipped Iron Man or something along those lines because 8 is a lot. Even then this is going to become a priority target, or your life total is.

BPhillipYork: OK. Lol. Expensive but whatever, draw a lot. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

The Vision

Saffgor: For decks looking to cast one spell per turn, every turn, meet your new Wavebreak Hippocamp-like. Good redundancy for those strategies.

FromTheShire: Pretty solid set of abilities to pick from. Most likely you are trying to use this as a card draw engine though yeah.

BPhillipYork: Draw a card every turn you cast a noncreature spell is good. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ultron, Artificial Malevolence

Saffgor: Turn a Caltrops into a man, then give it Deathtouch. If I had a nickel for every time Caltrops came up, etc etc. In all seriousness, Ultron is a fantastic option for folks looking to find their Colorless Commander of choice, and Marvel on the whole has bolstered that roster by about a half-dozen! I'm not too high on this set overall, but it's done huge work in making the Colorless identity sing.

FromTheShire: There's a whole bunch of powerful big mana artifacts that you would love to dump onto the battlefield with your many costs reducers and then pay 2 to get a second copy of. The token being a creature is usually a downside you're going to be fine with accepting, and then occasionally an upside that lets you do something gross.

BPhillipYork: This is pretty hilarious to me with the things you can copy, Door to Nothingness and stuff like that. Or Aladdin's Ring. Why you ask? Because you can.
