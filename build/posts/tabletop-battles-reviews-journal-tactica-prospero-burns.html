Tabletop Battles Reviews: Journal Tactica - Prospero Burns

August 29th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The new book for 30k has the snappy title Journal Tactica: Prospero Burns - The Judgement of Valdor. The title is a pretty decent summary of the book’s extensive background section. In addition to that we get a new mission and an unusually large amount of rules content for one of these Journals.

Journal Tactica - Prospero Burns - The Judgement of Valdor. Credit: Warhammer Community

You might imagine that a book on the Burning of Prospero would tell you loads about the Space Wolves and Thousand Sons, who were the principle combatants. But Custodes are the faction with the new models so instead they get a new unit and loads of pictures of their new toys. The majority of the rules are for a new Tzeentch supplement for the Ruinstorm Daemons list. there are are also two new Auxilia Cohort Doctrines; one for each side of the conflict.

Thanks to Games Workshop for sending us a copy of Prospero Burns to review.

Background

The story begins at Nikaea and runs through Magnus’ catastrophic visit to Terra, the assembly of the Censure Host, Horus’ interference in its mission parameters and then a full account of the fighting on Prospero. It’s a story we’ve already had covered in the black books and novels so there isn’t a huge amount of point in me going through it yet again. It’s nicely produced with some maps, colour plates and so on, particularly focused on the Custodes. The Daemons are mentioned and so is a Custodes Adjutarum Sodality.

Legendary Mission: The Fall of the Domus Temebri

Towards the end of the background section we hear about a group of Thousand Sons carrying out a ritual, which the Custodes’ attempt to stop. There ends up being a fight with a load of Daemons, led by a Lord of Change. This mission recreates those events.

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought. Credit - Soggy

The game doesn’t use any objectives and instead each side can win by wiping out the other, or routing any survivors. If neither side is wiped out then the attacker scores points for killing enemy units and the Defender scores for having their units not be wiped out.

This is a 3000 point game intended to be for Custodes vs Thousand Sons. The Defender has to take 500 points or more of Daemons that can only appear via warp rifts, caused by the Ritual that’s happening. The rifts have set locations on the board to be set up.

New Custodes Unit: Adjutarum Sodality

This is the first Retinue unit the Custodes have been given. It comprises three Custodians for 275 points - which is usually what you’d pay for six of them. There are no options of any kind for this unit. Each of the three is unique, bringing their own special wargear and abilities. The members of the unit are:

  • The Vestarios, A Sergeant with a spear and an ability that can remove tactical statuses.
  • The Signifer, with an extra wound, a Sentinel Blade and Imperatoris Vexila.
  • The Blade Champion, a Champion with WS6 and a fancy sword.

The unit gets Bulky (2), Eternal Warrior (1) and Lightning Blows, like the standard Custodes infantry. They have a slightly better invulnerable save of 5+, despite not having shields. They’re Vanguard (1), which is kind of rubbish, preventing them from scoring objectives properly or from getting much reward if they kill an enemy unit. However, the Imperatoris Vexilla allows them to score the full value of an objective once per game, even if they are only contesting it or are locked in combat. The objective’s value then becomes 1 for the rest of the game, potentially resulting in a big change in the final VP total.

A Vestarios can use their Promulgation ability in the Start Phase to make a Cool or Leadership Check. If successful, they can remove a Tactical Status from one of your units that has a Magisterium Vexilla. The option to remove a tactical status is pretty huge but needing a vexilla on the target unit is a problem. It doesn’t work on jetbikes, terminators or Venatarii - or vehicles obviously. I worry a bit that it encourages armies made up of loads of infantry, which will be boring but good. If you did play an army like that you’d probably want these guys hidden out of sight, which would be extra boring.

The Blade Champion has the very powerful Vaultsword. It has IM +1, AM+2, SM S, AP2 and D1 with Duelist’s Edge (1) and Precision (4+). There’s no gun in it. A model with a 5 AP2 attacks at I6 is very useful, either in a challenge or just for wading through normal enemies.

Overall the result is a thematic unit that probably doesn’t offer a huge difference to just taking another unit of Custodes. Having a Sergeant in the unit opens up some new Prime options not previously available to any other units, which might be interesting but probably won’t happen. Most of the time I think you’ll be adding these as a Logistical Benefit anyway, or possibly in a Warlord Detachment with Valdor, meaning you won’t be able to make them Prime.

The book suggests building the unit from Sentinel Guard models with bits from the Shield Captain and Custodian Guard. They’ve got one Custodian with the Captain’s Eternity Blade and a Misericordia drawn for the Champion, one with the Custodian Guard’s Vexilla and a left-handed Sentinel blade for the Signifer and a pretty normal one with a spear for the Vestarios. All have the weird mohawk/crest things, which works quite well. Personally I think I’d try and make them a bit more unique than this as I enjoy kitbashes. If I get round to doing some I'll do an article on them.

Cohort Doctrine: Oblitum Pattern Cohort

This is a new Doctrine for Solar Auxilia - the first of two in this book. It represents people who aren’t really Solar Auxilia at all, but rather direct servants of the Throne. They are subjected to all sorts of weird “psycho-memetic obliteration and neural suppression”, meaning they are able to function when faced with some of the galaxy’s worst horrors. This comes at the cost of sometimes falling into a fugue state at inconvenient moments.

Solar Auxilia vs Night Lords. Credit: Andrew_N

Mechanically, this means their Leadership Characteristic can never be modified negatively, though note their Cool and other stats still can be. The downside of having their heads messed with so much is having to take a Willpower Check if they ever want to score an Objective or make a Reaction. If they fail they get Stunned, meaning they can’t make Reactions or score Objectives - though the Reaction Point isn’t used up. You do have the option of taking a Navigator for Willpower 9, but that might be a bit naughty, and in any case quite a big tax.

The real point of an Oblitum Pattern Cohort is that they get the Talons of the Emperor Trait, meaning that Custodes can take them as allies. However they can’t have a Legiones Auxilia Designation, which makes sense as they’re nothing to do with the Legions Astartes.

There’s an interesting new Detachment that you get as a bonus for every Veletaris Cohort you take. It comprises a Command slot, two troops and one support slot that can only be filled with Discipline Masters, Infantry or Levy Troop units and Fire Support Troops, all from the Militia list. They replace the Imperialis Militia Trait with the Solar Auxila Trait but the Discipline Masters don’t give you any more Detachments.

I don’t really understand the point of this. Why go to all the trouble of messing with these guys’ heads so much only to provide them with no training and awful equipment, then sending them into a fight that they have no chance of surviving. There’s no need to wipe the minds of the dead. In game terms they don’t really do anything that Auxilia don’t do better, for roughly the same price. Maybe there’s a narrative here about your Custodes herding dross ahead of themselves to catch bullets, locate mines and stuff, in which case fine. It’s just a shame that Militia units are grossly over-priced, especially when you see them like this without any upgrades from their base stats and gear.

Other than the Militia part, which in any case is more an issue with the Militia list itself, I like how they’ve implemented these rules. Part of the point of the design for Custodes is that they all have Vanguard instead of Line. If they could just bring a few Auxilia squads alone to hold objectives, that would be busted. You can do that, but they have pretty rubbish Willpower stats, maxing out at 7 for the Legate Marshal, so they wouldn’t usually do the job very well. Access to the Auxilia list and part of the Militia one allows you to represent mortal servants of the throne in all kinds of ways, from well-trained and equipped Veletarii-analogues to meat-grinder assaults by penal militia.

I've actually converted some models to make alternate Veletarii a few years ago. They might work quite well as an Oblitum Pattern Cohort, though I'd probably need to give them their original helmets back.

Solar Auxilia Volkite Squad and "Grox". Credit: NotThatHenryC

The most effective thing might be to bring a bunch of AV14 tanks, perhaps including a Baneblade or something. They will be pretty safe behind a wall of angry golden people and they were never going to hold objectives for you anyway.

The Daemons of Tzeentch Army List

All the previous Daemons we’ve seen in 30k have been either unaligned or else associated with Aetheric Dominions that don’t match perfectly with the Chaos “gods”. This list changes all that, giving us rules for the Daemon units that 40k and AoS players will be familiar with, from Pink and Blue Horrors all the way up to Lords of Change.

Daemons of Tzeentch fight Custodes. Credit: Warhammer Community

Aetheric Dominion: Tzeentch

In game terms this works as a supplement to the Daemons of the Ruinstorm list. Tzeentch Daemons have a new Aetheric Dominion: Tzeentch. All the units here have the Tzeentch Dominion but there are no rules for giving it to Ruinstorm Daemons. Tzeentch and Ruinstorm Daemons can be part of the same Detachments but they don’t necessarily play well together. 

Tzeentch Daemons can be in the same Detachments as Daemons with the Infernal Tempest, Formless Distortion, Encroaching Ruin and Malevolent Artifice Dominions. If there are any other Dominions in the same Army, even if they’re in different Detachments, then all Daemons in your army get a -2 modifier to Leadership, Cool, Willpower and Intelligence. 

We obviously don’t have rules for any of the other Gods’ Daemons but if they eventually appear then presumably those will also be new Aetheric Dominions. Since they are not on the list above, that -2 modifier would apply to an army containing Daemons from two gods. This is actually most impactful to Tzeentch Daemons, who are affected significantly by losing the Willpower stat they need for their Psychic powers. Something like a Bloodthirster wouldn’t mind much at all.

Tzeentch Daemon Psykers also get the Convocators rule. In every Victory Sub-Phase each one can pick a friendly or enemy unit and make a Willpower Check. If they succeed the unit loses Vanguard and gets Line (1), or increases their existing Line stat by 1 to a maximum of 3. If they fail the Psyker takes D3 wounds that invulnerable saves work against but armour doesn’t and the target unit’s Line stat gets reduced by 1. It’s a bit weird that you can do this to enemy units as you’d kind of want to fail the test to nerf their Line stat, but I suppose Tzeentch’s plans are hard to interpret.

Gambit: Master of Fates

This lets you make Hit and Wound tests in a challenge using the WS, Strength and Weapon Profiles of a Lord of Change. It doesn’t actually say that the enemy’s save stat is affected by the LoC’s Staff’s AP of 2, or if they take D2 when wounded, so I believe those stats aren’t applied. Your model’s WS is not altered for calculating what the enemy needs to hit it. Even so, being hit by something even slightly like a Greater Daemon is no fun whatsoever, so this is a very nice gambit to have.

Thousand Sons Auxiliary Detachment: Aetheric Incursion

If Thousand Sons are your Primary Detachment then Esoterists and Prosperine Sorcerors can unlock a Detachment with a Command Slot, two Troops, on Support and a Fast Attack, which you can only fill with Tzeentch Daemons.

Unusually, the Command slot here does seem to potentially let you take other Auxiliary Detachments. I’m not sure if that’s an accidental omission. Otherwise, the main advantage of this Detachment, compared with just taking an Allied Detachment of Daemons, is that you can take Flamers or Screamers without a Herald. Heralds are quite good though, so it’s not a bad thing to take one.

Psychic Discipline: Fateweaving

The new Discipline grants the Fateweaver trait and three Psychic powers, whose rules take up nearly three full pages of the book. I’ll try to briefly summarise what they do below.

There’s a rule that Psykers with Fateweaving can’t have access to any other Disciplines unless it says otherwise. Actually though, all the Psykers in this list can buy Pyromancy for 10 points and Telekinesis for 20 points, so I guess we can just ignore this rule entirely and move on to look at the powers.

Metamorphosise is cast in the Shooting Phase on an enemy non-vehicle unit within 15” and LoS. The target unit then has to make a Resistance check, modified by the difference in its Willpower and your Psyker’s. If they fail you roll a dice and they get a random Status: Pinned on a 1, Stunned on 2-3 and Suppressed on 4-6. You have Psykers with Willpower 9 and the Lord of Change has 11, giving it a really good chance of causing a status.

Phasewarp is also cast in your Shooting Phase. A single piece of terrain doesn’t block LoS until the end of your opponent’s next Shooting Phase. If there are only very large terrain pieces on the board then you can make part of one with a 5” radius not block LoS instead. This isn’t a huge deal with Daemons, as they don’t have all that much shooting, but for allied Legion or Auxilia forces it could be huge. It can be done to terrain anywhere on the board too, so your Psyker can be tucked up safely somewhere.

Warpshield is a Reaction that gives your Psyker’s unit a 5+ shrouding roll until the end of the Sub-Phase if it succeeds on a Willpower test. There’s no effect if it fails. This is fine I guess, though there are quite a lot of ways to negate Shrouding. It doesn’t work well with Horrors, which are the unit you’re most likely to stick a Herald in. Their ability to split into Blue ones is also a Reaction - meaning you can’t do both. It’s nice for an Ascenant Lord of Tzeentch or Lord of Change.

Tzeentch Daemon Units

A Lord of Change and Shield Captain Duel. Credit: Warhammer Community

There are six new units here, covering a similar set of Tzeentch Daemons to the ones available to Thousand Sons armies in 40k. Despite all the references to Fateweaver we don’t get rules for it, or for Magnus Transfigured - which he hadn’t yet done when Prospero burned.

Ascendant Lord of Tzeentch

This High Command choice is basically a Daemon Prince, though it doesn’t quite say so, meaning we aren’t told what model to use or base to put them on. It’s a slightly less beefy version of the Ruinstorm Daemon Sovereign for 50 points less and is a Psyker. Similar to the Sovereign it has to be picked for Challenges unless there’s another model with the same Champion of the Dark Gods rule in the fight, though instead of taking wounds if it gets Disgraced this thing just up and dies, so I guess don’t let that happen.

A slight issue is that Champion of the Dark Gods is not Champion of the Ruinstorm - which is what you need to get a bonus Reaction for a Ruinstorm Daemon army. I don’t know if that’s deliberate but it’s a shame to have a High Command choice that doesn’t grant a bonus Reaction.

Herald of Tzeentch

Your Command choice is a very powerful Horror. It’s almost exactly the same as a Ruinstorm Daemon Harbinger. It gets Bulky (3), Eternal Warrior (1), Fear (1) and the

Harbinger’s Herald of Unreality ability to summon its friends, who can subsequently charge. Unlike the Harbinger it’s a Psyker, which is a pretty big deal as it doesn’t cost any more. It also gets a Staff of Change that hits at IM-3 with SM+3, AP2, D1 and Warpdoom, meaning anything it kills in melee explodes. This could be hilarious if it goes through a unit of troops of some kind, causing a series of explosions. It’s not obvious to me why your Herald would want people it kills to blow up right in its face, but I suppose that’s Tzeentch for you.

Heralds can be upgraded with Pyromancy for 10 points or Telekinesis for 20. These disciplines give them access to various useful ranged attacks, Force Barrier for their unit and the opportunity to explode, just in case you haven’t had enough explosions. 

Flamers of Tzeentch

Flamers of Tzeentch- @henrysouth

90 points gets you 3 Flamers and you can have up to 6 more at 30 points each. They’ve got a 10” move with Move Through Cover, WS and BS2, S and T4, W3, I4 and A1. They have 4+/5++ saves, Bulky (3) and Support Unit (1). You can pay 15 points to make one of them an Exalted Flamer, making it a Sergeant and buffing its stats, though not its gun.

The gun is Changefire. Template, FP1, RS5, AP5, D1. It has Panic (1), Heavy (FP), Immaterial and Assault. If your opponent is careless enough to let these get Volley attacks then each one will shoot out two templates, but most of the time Changefire is just quite a lot like any other kind of fire: hot and makes things run away.

Screamers of Tzeentch

Screamer of Tzeentch- @henrysouth

This is a promising assault unit ruined by being harmless in melee. They’re a Fast Attack choice of 3 Screamers for 130, plus another 6 more if you want at 40 points each. These are Antigrav Cavalry with a 12” move, so they can get around the place. WS4, S5, T4, W3 and I5 are all promising and they have ok 4+/5++ saves. Fear (1) and Outflank are ok. A2 is a problem though, especially when their Razorspines just inflict AP- D1 attacks with Impact (S) and Breaching (6+). I think they’ll just fail to kill things. They might have made a useful objective-grabber but they have Vanguard (2), so even that won’t work. Rubbish.

Horrors of Tzeentch

Credit: Liebot - https://instagram.com/liebot_pics

This is your Troop choice and they’re ok. 140 points for 10 Pink ones. These have a marine-like profile full of 4s with M7, W1 and A2, though only 5+/5++ saves. One can have a Totem of Dominion for 20 points, reducing their casualties by 1 if they’d get a tactical status.

For shooting they get Warpfire with R15”, FP1, RS5, AP5, D1, Deflagrate (3) and Heavy (FP). They’re Immaterial and Assault. This is an ok-ish anti-personnel gun. They have a surprisingly good melee attack, using their base stats but with Rending (6+) and Shred (6+). This means the unit is a credible threat in two phases.

The fluff for Horrors is that a pink one splits into two Blue ones when it dies. Unfortunately this rule is written as a reaction to a shooting attack from a single enemy unit, meaning that it’s needlessly complicated and only occasionally takes effect. If your opponent kills a Pink Horror in melee, in your turn with something like a Return Fire Reaction or if a second unit targets them after the first, they just die. Plus of course you can only use one Reaction for them, so they can’t also Return Fire or do any of the fun Psychic stuff an attached Herald might want to do.

Blue Horrors are a bit worse replacing most of the 4s in the stat line to 3s, though T stays at 4. They have the same gun, though less accurate and only A1 - which is ok as you’ve got twice as many of them.

Lord of Change

Lord of Change. Credit: Corrode

The first Greater Daemon to appear in 30k is a new Warlord choice. As such it’s only available to Ruinstorm Daemon armies. It’s Unique, which is a bit odd as there are quite a lot of them, but you can’t get more than one Warlord anyway so I suppose that doesn’t matter. Despite being in its own Detachment it will still cause everyone that -2 modifier to mental stats if it’s in an army with Daemons it doesn’t approve of.

You will not want to earn the disapproval of a Lord of Change. It has a 10” move and Antigrav, so it’s not super-fast but should be able to get to where it needs to be. All its stats from WS to A are 6, except for W7. It has LD and CL 10, WP 11 and Int 12 with a 2+/4++ save.

Special rules are Bulky (7), Fear ((surprisingly only) 1) and Eternal Warrior (2). Tides of Change sets your Reaction allocation to D3, plus any bonuses for battle size. Primal Terror makes any unit that wants to Charge a Lord of Change, or do a Reaction to something it does, take a Leadership Check. If they fail the Check they get Stunned and can’t do any more Charges or Reactions this turn, though no Reaction Point gets spent.

The Lord of Change’s big stick is a Staff of Tzeentch. It hits with the Daemon’s base stats but with AP2 and D2. It has Reaping Blow (3), Force (S) and Critical Hits 6+, so the Lord of Change can have S12 attacks to kill big tanks if it wants.

You die if a Lord of Change looks at you. Its Baleful Glare inflicts a Template with S8, AP4 and D1. It has Breaching 4+, Force (FP) and Warpblaze, which makes damaging hits do D3 wounds instead of 1. It’s got Assault and Immaterial, 

Telekinesis seems like a good option for a Lord of Change. You’d happily pay the 20 points just for Shrouded (4+) on a thing like this and you can make that 3+ with Force Barrier almost automatically if you want. I don’t know if it needs Pyromancy but for just 10 points you may as well take it.

A Lord of Change is the full package. It’s mobile, tough and a threat in every phase. It would probably struggle against a Primarch or the very best melee characters, due to having “only” WS6, but otherwise it won’t be afraid of much at all. 400 points might be a bargain for one of these. 

Cohort Doctrine: Fateweaver Cohort

This is the other new Doctrine for Solar Auxilia and represents a Tzeentch-corrupted Legion. These models get the Tzeentch Trait, the Traitor Trait and a 6+ invulnerable save against Perils of the Warp and Psychic weapons - none of which really does anything. I’m pretty sure they don’t get the Convocators rule, which is written as part of the Tzeentch Aetheric Dominion and would therefore only affect Daemons… I think.

The fun thing is that any models with the Command Sub-type get the Psyker Trait and the Fateweaving Psychic Discipline - and never any others. This allows them to do some quite fun stuff, though with the unfortunate caveat that their rubbish Willpower makes it far from reliable. You don’t lose much for trying, except perhaps the life of a person Tzeentch doesn’t care very much about. Davinite Lodge Priests, or possibly Navigators, would be an interesting way to represent dedicated Psykers who had a reasonable chance of successfully casting their powers.

Metamorphise, which relies on comparing Willpower with the enemy’s, is especially unlikely to work unless cast by a Priest. Phasewarp is kind of fantastic for an Auxilia army though, as you can “turn off” a piece of terrain that the enemy is staging behind and fire loads of really big guns at them. Or you could fail your Willpower roll and leave all your guns pointed at some trees. Warpshield might help your Psyker and some of his friends survive boring gunfire so that they can die later in a far more entertaining and warp-related way.

There’s nothing stopping Fateweaver Cohorts from being Legiones Auxilia. You can have them be Prosperine Spireguard if you want - or any of the others.

Fateweaver Cohorts can also take Aetheric Incursion Detachments, just like the ones available to Thousand Sons. And in game terms there’s still really no benefit in doing so when Allied Detachments exist. I suppose you’re not using up your Allies allocation for an army with a Fateweaver Cohort as its Primary Detachment, but I don’t think that would be a problem very often.

Overall I think this is a nice way to represent Tzeentch cultists, and especially for the Spireguard fighting alongside the Thousand Sons and/or Daemons.

Final Thoughts

Pallas Grav Attack. Credit - Soggy

This is a really nice Journal. Tzeentch daemons are clearly the part of the book that’s most impactful to the game as a whole, as we might start seeing them on tables.

It’s quite notable that different parts of the book spend their time on very different factions. Daemons have by far the most new units but also only very vague background information. 

The book has been used to show off the Custodes range. It’s full of pictures of people and vehicles in shiny golden armour. 

It’s a bit odd to have a book about Prospero in which the rules don’t cover the Thousand Sons or Space Wolves. We get to hear a fair bit about the Sisters of Silence’s part in the fighting, as well as the Questoris Sinister and Ordo Sinister  but there’s no more content for them either.

Overall this is a nice book with a good amount of rules content for armies it’s relevant to. Daemon players will really want it but Legion players don’t get much out of it in terms of rules. I think it’s a good thing that we don’t need to buy absolutely all of these Journals as they come out. They’re great for people with the relevant armies and fairly easily skipped by everyone else.
