BattleTech Mech Overview: JagerMech and JagerMech III

November 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Today, MechWarriors, we are cancelling the apocalypse!

Or, well, today we're talking about the usually-65-ton JagerMech. Some of its variants might be capable of cancelling a small apocalypse. We've got Abomination creation rules now, we could put this theorem to the test...

Lyran Commonwealth Jagermech. Credit: SRM

One of the multiple Star-League-era efforts to address the flaws of the Rifleman, the JagerMech is Kallon's own second bite at that apple. It's very close to being the same mech, down to utilizing the same Garret D2j targeting and tracking system (with the Anti-Aircraft Targeting Quirk that comes with it), but it's usually five tons heavier (we'll... we'll get there) and usually mitigates the Rifleman's heat issues by leaning further into ammunition-dependent weapons.

On the one hand, this means that it's usually a thinly-armored mech stuffed with explosive crits.

On the other hand, this means that it's usually extremely cheap.

Which hand wins? Well, let's take a look!

Variants

These mechs have all been reviewed based on a standard F through S scale, which you can find described on our landing page here (along with all of our other ‘Mech reviews, the name of the box you can buy to get any of the mechs we have covered, and our general methodology).

JM6-A

BattleTech model numbers are made up and the points don't matter, so the "A" is not the first in-universe variant, but it does date to the Star League. It forms a little sub-clade of its own alongside the JM6-B as "the ones that have LRMs on them," namely an LRM-15 in each arm a la Catapult. The A pairs its missile launchers with an AC/2 in each arm, and one medium laser in each side torso strives valiantly to divert critical hits away from the spicy ammo slots (one ton AC and one LRM in the left torso and a second ton of LRMs in the right torso).

The JM6-A doesn't jump (moving 4/6/0)  and doesn't have anything beyond the base ten single heat sinks, but the autocannons are still heavy enough to stop it two tons short of the Catapult C1's armor coverage. 17/20/17 across the front torsos is passable, if not ideal, but its rear coverage is a dismal 3/5/3, and it only has 16 pips on each leg and 11 on the arms. Heat's okay; 2+ movement firing the long range guns, neutral at a run firing the ACs and the MLs, and -2 at a run firing just the MLs.

1,122 BV is cheap, though, 277 BV cheaper than the aforementioned Catapult C1 (and even a little bit cheaper than a Trebuchet 5N!), and this mech has great range. Within the specific context of "I am way in the backfield providing fire support for a larger force," this isn't bad, but that's not really the BattleTech most of us are playing, and dear lord this mech is allergic to being within six hexes of its opposition.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

JM6-B

Oh no, they made it worse. The -B is exactly the JM6-A except it swaps the AC/2s and their ton of ammo for a pair of Large Lasers and a wholly inadequate +3 heat sinks.

This mech builds 3+ Movement heat firing the Large Lasers alone. 9+ Movement firing all the lasers. If you wanted to fire the missiles and the large lasers together, that's 13+ Movement. I'm not even going to address the possibility of an Alpha Strike.

And this costs 1,248 BV?

Weren't we trying to fix the Rifleman's heat problems here, Kallon?

Shameful.

Lynn's Rating: F.

JM6-DD

Jumping ahead to the Helm Renaissance, the DD is... hm.  I'm going to call it an interesting value proposition, though the JM6-DDa, its Jihad-era refit, overshadows it in that regard. The DD uses an XL engine (still 260-rated for the JagerMech's usual 4/6/0 profile) to buy tonnage to pack an AC/2 and a UAC/5 in each arm, a ton of ammo for each gun, CASE which actually matters now with the Core Rules update, and a medium pulse laser in each side torso. Ten single heat sinks are honestly enough for that as long as you don't take an engine crit, with even a full alpha strike being plausible if a shot somehow lined up for one (though the poor range compatibility of the AC/2s and the MPLs makes that unlikely).

The Double D's Ferro-Fibrous armor coat is thinner than the A and B's armor, but it's been redistributed to keep decent protection on the torso (17/18/17 across the front and an improved 5/5/5 across the back) and full coverage on the head while sacrificing the limbs, with just 12 armor pips on each leg and eight on each arm. Each arm has 18 HP total.

The major value here is cost-effectiveness. A stiff breeze can threaten the DD, but it only costs 965 BV, and what you get for that is the potential for a PSR's worth of damage with good range, added together from a set of guns which looks piddly enough your opponent will likely underestimate them, backed by an okay close-range bite if anything tries to dive it. Make no mistake, this is nowhere close to a world-beater, but it is dirt cheap for what it is, and that's its own value.

Lynn's Rating: C is for cheap.

JM6-DDa

What I just said about the DD's cost-effectiveness?

Yeah, let's double down on that.

The DDa swaps the DD's armament for LAC/2s (two tons of ammo), AC/5s (three tons of ammo), regular Medium Lasers (moved to the CT) and a targeting computer. The ACs are happy flinging AP and Precision both (and you can even feed the AC/5s some flak ammo on top, as a treat, if you need to actually shoot at planes), their range bands line up perfectly (except for the AC/5s having a minimum), the DDa only builds movement heat on an alpha strike even with its 10 SHS, accuracy is improved on all the guns, and the BV?

The BV goes down. This sets you back 911 BV.

This is still a slow mech with terrible armor, but the value proposition it represents is phenomenal, and the Core changes have only made it better. Pressure your opponent with a scary frontline to distract them and you can absolutely make your investment back from this mech.

Lynn's Rating: B is for budget.

1st Federated Suns Armored Cavalry JagerMech. Credit: Valk.

JM6-DG

Remember the Avatar OG? We like the Avatar OG. The "Double Gauss" JagerMech takes the exact same weaponry as the Avatar OG (plus an extra two tons of ammo it won't live long enough to need) and shoves it into the chassis of a JM6-DD. At 1,625 BV, this only saves 150 BV relative to the Avatar OG, and it trades away seventy-six pips of armor to get there.

The JM6-DG wants you to think it's the minimum viable caddy for twin gauss rifles. This is a lie. It is below minimum. Take a Galahad 2D. Take a Sun Cobra. Take the Avatar OG. Take a friggin tank; if you don't need mobility you can get three Gauss Rifles with a targeting computer on the DI Morgan (Gauss) and save four BV over the JagerMech DG.

Just don't take a JagerMech DG. 1,625 BV is too much to pay for one-shot gauss rifles, and it's not likely to live long enough to shoot twice.

Lynn's Rating: F+.

JM6-DGr

Take a JM6-DG. Give it double heat sinks. Swap the medium lasers to ERMLs. That's the DGr. It goes up to 1,661 BV and has gained absolutely no survivability.

These mechs could have carried less ammunition and had more armor. They could have dropped the medium lasers and had more armor. They could've done Galahad cosplay and shrunk the engine and had more armor.

Instead, they decided that valuable weapons with explosive capacitors should be put in arms as well-armored as an Ostsol's.

Shameful display.

Lynn's Rating: F.

JM6-H

Okay, after those DGs I need something to pick my spirits up, I need something...

Bah Gawd, that's the Marian Hegemony's music!

Heck yeah, we've got a funny rocket launcher variant, and more than that, it's a... shockingly competently designed rocket launcher variant?

The JagerMech Haitch mounts an LB 2-X and a Large Laser in each arm, and can sink all their heat at a run with ten Double Heat Sinks. Three Rocket Launcher 15s in each side torso give it a dramatic, if inaccurate, close-in punch, and it still has enough tonnage for the thickest coat of Ferro-Fibrous we've yet seen on a JagerMech, 24/29/24 and 5/7/5 pips of armor on the front and rear side torsos, 16 on the arms, 22 on the legs. As a further benefit, unexpended rocket launchers apparently don't explode when crit, and expended rocket launchers still soak crits, so the side torsos are hilariously well-padded, even without CASE on the LB ammo.

It's not all upside here: The H only carries one ton of LB 2-X ammo, so you need to choose between being able to make flak attacks and being able to consistently hit PSR-threshold damage output, and the range bands are a bit fiddly on the arm guns, with the LLs not hitting short range until 5 hexes, but the LB2s' minimum starting at 4 hexes. The rockets, too, are more of a nice-to-have than anything you can count on, with the double drawbacks of their penalty to hit and the fickle cluster table determining their damage. Still, hell, I think I'd pay 1,348 BV for this, as long as I've got an objective to park it on.

Lynn's Rating: ...C?

JM6-S

Would you believe we've finally hit the original JagerMech, after all this time? The J. Michael 6 Straczynski is The DD But IntroTech (so, worse). Two AC/2s, two AC/5s, two tons of ammo for the 5s, one ton for the 2s (in the CT, naturally), a medium laser in each side torso, and the thinnest armor a JagerMech ever mounts: 13/16/13 and 3/5/3 pips on the front and rear torsos, six pips on the arms, and 11 on the legs.

It's 901 BV, and that's not nothing, but if you have any access to the DDa, it's an improvement over this in every regard.

Lynn's Rating: D+.

Warrior House Imarra JagerMech. Credit: Lynn B.

The JM7 Series

Before we get into the JM7 JagerMechs, I want to note that they move up to 70 tons, are referred to under the moniker "JagerMech II" in-universe, and yet are officially categorized on the Master Unit List as the same mech as the preceding JagerMechs, while the "JagerMech III," which is 65 tons and shares the JM6 model number, is officially a separate mech.

Sometimes I hate it here.

(Although it is pretty neat that apparently the JM7s exist to canonize the mistakenly-overweight JagerMech from the MechCommander games!)

Anyway...

JM7-C3BS

This Jihad-era variant mounts two Light AC/2s and a Snub-Nose PPC in each arm, an emotional support ER Medium Laser in the CT, and the C3 Boosted Slave which gives it its moniker. With 13 DHS, this is neutral at a run firing the ACs and Snubs, or gains 3+ Movement on an Alpha Strike. The extra tonnage is very kind to the armor coat here; our -C3BS carries 24/34/24 and 6/6/6 pips on the front and rear torsos and 22 on each limb. That's thin on the rear and a little light on the legs, but phenomenal by JagerMech standards.

This mech's 1,354 BV doesn't really want to eat the increased C3 surcharge that the ECM-penetrating Boosted system gets stuck with, but it is very cost effective on its own as a mech that can throw around a pair of ten-pointers at a nine-hex range thanks to those snubbies while also spitting Precision mosquito bites at your opponent's fast-movers and/or spinning to win with four chances each turn to crit their big boys with AP ammo. LAC/2s get so much ammo per ton that even with four guns you will have enough of both types if you take a ton of Precision and a ton of AP, it's great.

I can't lie: I, personally, am feeling very bullish on this mech right now. I'll be taking it to Southern Assault, one of the largest Classic BattleTech tournaments in the country, this weekend, in a list which coalesced around the fact that the Capellans salvage this thing from the Feddies in large enough numbers that it shows up on their availability list. Once again, it's not a world-beater, but at less than 1400 BV it doesn't have to be. It's just a solid budget heavy with good reach, and I like the cut of its jib.

Lynn's Rating: This B may be revised in my tournament report.

JM7-D

The original JM7, this has the same armor and heat sinks as the -C3BS, but for weapons it mounts an ER Large Laser and an Ultra AC/5 in each arm and two MPLs in the CT. That's a touch spicy for its sinking - it builds 2+ Movement heat if both of the Ultras double-tap alongside the ERLLs - but it's manageable. This isn't a bad set of guns or a bad chassis, but it runs you 1500 BV, and I'd rather have the -C3BS even if it wasn't a savings over this.

Lynn's Rating: C.

JM7-DD

This Rec Guide variant is more interesting... By swapping to Endo-Composite Structure (not the kind that takes double damage) and an XL Gyro it finds weight to mount a Clan-grade Ultra AC/10 and a LAC/5 in each arm, a Spheroid ERML in each side torso, and CASE II in each side torso for its four tons of UAC/10 ammo and two tons of LAC/5, while maintaining the usual JM7 armor coverage. Double-Ds here does drop down to 11 DHS, meaning that it builds 2+ Movement heat on an alpha strike, but that's entirely manageable.

This is a phenomenal increase in damage output for the modest price bump to 1,690 BV. The XL Gyro is a weak spot, but CT crits are always a bad time regardless; I feel the CASE II is a bigger gain to survivability than the XL Gyro is a loss.

Shame this one's still FedSuns-exclusive!

Lynn's Rating: B+.

JM7-F

I'm kind of shocked it took us this long to get to the first Rotary Autocannon variant! Franklin here mounts a RAC/5 in each arm, four tons of ammo (all in the CASE-protected left torso), the same CT MPLs as the JM7-D, a Guardian ECM suite, and a Targeting Computer. At a slightly wasteful 12 DHS, it's heat-negative even if it rips off a running Alpha Strike with the RACs at full spin. Armor is still the same as the other JM7s.

I don't like RACs even half as much as Liberty does, but this is a good caddy for them, and 1,774 BV is a reasonable cost with the TarComp factored in. Given that this is another JagerMech my Capellans, ah, borrow, I should take it for a spin sometime!

Lynn's Rating: B?

JM7-G

Leaning back into the whole "anti-aircraft mech" thing, the -G here mounts an LB 2-X and a RAC/2 in each arm with two tons of ammunition for each set of guns (split across both side torsos and CASE'd in each location) alongside an ERML in each side torso. At the same 13 DHS as the -C3BS and the -D, it's neutral on a running alpha strike, and its armor is the same as all the other JM7s.

It's kind of funny how close this comes to the output of the Bane/Kraken for 1,278 BV, but unless I'm actually taking pot-shots at planes, I'd rather pay a little extra BV for a JM7 that can deal damage in larger clusters.

Lynn's Rating: C- is for Cheap-minus.

JagerMech. Credit: Rockfish

The JagerMech III

Practically speaking, the distinguishing features of the JagerMech III relative to JagerMech Classic are that it picks up an endo-steel skeleton to save extra weight and that it puts those weight savings into a larger XL engine which grants it 5/8/0 speed. Bigger changes have been made to mechs without them officially becoming a different chassis, but the JagerMech III also received a visual facelift in its art, so here we are.

JM6-D3

The basic JagerMech III is kind of a weird side-grade of the JM7-C3BS. Its arms carry a PPC and an Ultra AC/2 each, and it also has a medium laser in each side torso for a token minimum-range bite. It builds movement heat firing the arm guns and isn't likely to want to alpha strike. Armor is solid, but not at the high end of JagerMechs: 22/26/22 and 6/7/6 pips across the front and rear of the torso, 16 on each arm, and 20 on each leg. At its 5/8 speed, this comes out to 1,537 BV.

This mech seems decent, if vulnerable to enemies getting under its arm guns (which all have a minimum range of three hexes), but it doesn't excite me. I don't know what to tell you, the vibes are just off.

Lynn's Rating: C, I guess?

JM6-D4

...huh. Okay, I like this one better than the other JagerMech III, but I have to ask... is this really a JagerMech? It forgot all the bullets! This is the only member of the whole extended JagerMech family which has zero ammunition-fed weapons! It feels closer kin to the Catapult K2K than to its own lineage!

Anyway, this one is basically a D3 which trades away the Ultra 2s to pick up a pair of Light PPCs in the arms, an extra pair of Medium Lasers in the CT, four more Double Heat Sinks, and full armor coverage. Yes, I said full, so this carries the distinction of being the ONLY JagerMech which doesn't under-armor the legs relative to the torso! Progress is real, y'all.

This has a good idea with its bracket-fire set-up. It's neutral at a run firing the arm guns, with their 3-hex minimums... where things go a bit askew is when it hits Medium Laser range. It feels like it wants to drop the Light PPCs in favor of all the MLs, only building movement heat while firing the PPCs plus all four MLs and moving from 30 to 40 damage output in the process, but the PPCs also hit minimum range at the MLs' short range. This mech feels like it would've enjoyed Snub PPCs over the regular PPCs, though I'm not entirely sure what it would do with an extra two tons... Angel ECM, maybe? Or it could turn two of the MLs into MPLs, I suppose. As-is, you're still going to fire the PPCs + MLs when you're at close range, and you're just going to have to eat the fact that you'll always be at a slightly suboptimal range for something.

Anyway, the Catapult K2K's PPC range advantage is real, but it's pretty impressive how much more tonnage for Stuff this gains in comparison to that mech through its additional weight-savings tech and more modest heat-sinking, and it's only 115 BV more expensive at 1,831.

Lynn's Rating: B-.

Final Thoughts

Only a few months ago I mocked the JagerMech in conversation for being universally bad and was embarrassed by being shown evidence that it actually has several decent variants. Many, perhaps even most, JagerMechs are more properly in The Gargoyle Prime Zone: woefully unsuited for a duel with a peer-by-tonnage, but BV-efficient and possessed of real utility when considered on that merit. Some of its variants do spec it in more "conventionally optimal" directions, like the JM6-D4 and the JM7-DD, and that's fine. Where the JagerMech goes wrong is when it takes one of the bargain-basement iterations of its chassis and then straps real guns to it, making it a priority target, like the DG and DGr do. It just needs to pick a lane and stick with it.

Til next time, MechWarriors, remember that building walls to contain extradimensional kaiju is a temporary solution!
