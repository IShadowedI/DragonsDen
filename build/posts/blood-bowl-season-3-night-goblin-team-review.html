Blood Bowl Season 3: Night Goblin Team Review

November 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back, Sports Fans! Today it’s time to delve underground and see what the Old World’s nastiest, most conniving, and least trustworthy Blood Bowl teams are up to! So get ready for mayhem, violence, and more mayhem as we finally welcome Night Goblins to the pitch!

Before we dive in, we’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of Spike! Magazine 23 for review purposes.

Out of the deep, dark tunnels springs what has to be one of the most violent and innovative teams we’ve seen in a while. From a “fun with Blood Bowl” standpoint, this team truly has everything. Heavy hitters like Cave Squigs, Squig Wranglers, and a Troll; excellent runners and blitzers in Squig Hoppers; a new support player that everyone will immediately target every game; and a bunch of cheap yobbos to get in the way and then die. What more could you want?

While this team is certainly going to be a lot of fun, I want to stress that it is kind of a complicated mess of a Blood Bowl squad, and probably not best for a new coach. Managing Squig Rage (more on that in a bit) and the many other skills, traits, and mutations inherent in this roster is going to be tough even for veteran coaches. That being said, it’s a team of fantastic, characterful, and whimsical models, so if you are a new coach and want this team, read on, and maybe the Blood Bowl boffins can help you crack this particular nut.

In this article we’ll touch on what the players do, how to build a roster, and some general tips for playing them. Read on to get the scoop on these hellacious hooligans!

Credit: Games Workshop

Why Play Night Goblins?

Simple: you want the ability to tear your opponent’s team to shreds, and you think you can roll a bunch of 3+’s. Because, lemme tell you, if you can’t, you might be in for a long game! For some people, myself included, the wacky randomness that is part and parcel of a d6 game is a feature, not a bug. And this team leans into that hard.

Pros

  • Line of Scrimmage domination: It is possible to show up to the game with 2 ST7 Ball and Chain players, a ST5 Troll, and 2 ST4 Cave Squigs. Not even the mightiest of Chaos Renegade teams can stand up to a Night Goblin turn one without a couple of concussions.
  • Surprisingly excellent blitzers and runners: Goblin teams aren’t usually known for their top-tier positionals, Cave Squigs are some of the best natural blitzers in the game. The Cave Squig statline is nuts, and then you combine it with Frenzy, Pogo, and Jump Up and no cage is safe! Furthermore, Squig Hoppers are fast (MA7), agile, and immune to Strip Ball right out of the locker room! All you’ve got to do is manage their Squig Rage, which brings us to…
  • Squig Rage is a “negatrait”…or is it?! We’ll dive deeper into Squig Rage in just a bit, but it’s a better Animal Savagery and you can go ask coaches who use Rat Ogres how much they love that “negatrait”. Conservatively, I think half the time a Night Goblin coach fails their Squig Rage rolls, you’ll hear a derivation of “hell yeah, brother” potentially paired with a fist pump.

Cons

  • It’s never hard to get rid of Ball & Chain players: All the opponent needs is cheap Block or Wrestle players, and away those expensive, B&C players go. Also, they’re crazy unreliable even when they don’t get removed!
  • Your best players will never level: Less of a problem for tournaments, but Cave Squigs are going to be nearly impossible to skill up. Fortunately, they don’t need more skills to be good, which is good, because they’ll never get them.
  • It’s in the Bad Moon’s hands: This team is RNG personified, so you better hope the Bad Moon’s looking down on you with a smile. Is that something the Bad Moon does? I have no idea. Get good at rolling 3+’s or else you might be in for it.

The New Traits

Before we dive into the players, we’ve got to address the new traits: Squig Rage and Squig Restraint. Let’s start with the rage (emphasis in italics is mine):

  • Squig Rage (Passive): Whenever this player is activated, before declaring their action, they must roll a d6. On a 3+, the player may activate as normal. On a 1-2, the player must declare a Block Action targeting an opposition player they are Marking if able (using the Jump Up skill if applicable) - they may do this even if they have the Ball & Chain Trait and couldn’t normally do so. If the player cannot declare a Block Action against an opposition player but are adjacent to at least one team-mate, then they may choose an adjacent team-mate to be immediately Knocked Down (this will not cause a Turnover unless the chosen team-mate was holding the ball); this player may then continue their activation as normal. If this player cannot declare a Block Action against an opposition player, is not adjacent to a team-mate, or chooses not to Knock Down a team-mate, then this player becomes Distracted.

That’s a lot of words! But it boils down to: do what you want on a 3+, or get an extra block/smack your own dude if you don’t. At first glance it seems like it might be a significant limitation for the players that have it, but let us, for a moment, consider the Rat Ogre. Rat Ogres have Animal Savagery, which is generally considered to be one of the best negatraits, as it is can be unfailable simply by sitting a yobbo next to the Rat Ogre before they activate. Then, even if the Rat Ogre’s coach fails the roll, the Rat Ogre punches its friend and goes about their business as normal. So take that and add an extra Block Action to the mix. In some cases, the Night Goblin coach wants to fail the Squig Rage roll to get multiple blocks! Furthermore, this test is made before the player declares an action, meaning you can’t waste your Blitz on a Squig Rager like you can with other negatraits! Further-further-more a Ball & Chain player can make regular Blocks when they fail their Squig Rage roll, which is something they normally cannot do! And, finally, all the players with Squig Rage on the team are capable blockers, so even if you are forced to make Block Actions with them, you ain’t even gonna be mad!

That was a lot of words again. I’m sorry. But it’s important to point this stuff out to make the following point: I’m not even sure Squig Rage is a negatrait! I think a Night Goblin coach wants to be failing this roll probably 50% of the time! It can even make Ball & Chain players less random somehow! I think you can tell that I’m pretty excited about this trait. I think it’s flavorful, fun, and, importantly, very good. I can’t wait to get this team on the pitch!

Ok, with all that said, let’s take a look at Squig Restraint.

  • Squig Restrait (Passive): Team-mates within 3 squares of a Standing player with this trait do not roll for their Squig Rage Trait at the start of their activation and may activate as normal.

This one’s a lot easier to parse, thank Sigmar! If at any point you were worried about making those all-important Squig Rage rolls, you can just ignore them with a well-placed Squig Herder. Simple as. You should note two things, though: it’s a Passive trait meaning that if they are standing and Distracted, it is still in effect (a use case here would be if the Squig Herder got Hypo-Gazed). And lastly, this trait fully overrides Squig Rage. You may not choose to roll the Squig Rage check if the Squig Restraint player is within range, even if you really want to.

The Players

In the tradition of the “regular” goblin team, this team has a big selection of special positions to choose from. This team will lean heavily into the madcap fun of RNG, but has some really good statlines and skill pieces that, in the hands of either very lucky or experienced coaches, could cause some serious problems for their opponents.

Each entry has a bolded portion at the top, and here’s how to decipher it:

  • The 1st part is the name of the player. Example: Night Goblin Lineman. In brackets, the first value is price. Example: 45k = 45,000 gp.
  • Then, we have skill access with primary/secondary. Example: GS/AD means General and Strength skills for primary and Agility and Devious skills for secondary.
  • Skills are abbreviated: A  = Agility, D = Devious, G = General, M = Mutation, P = Passing, S = Strength
  • Next is the number you can take on your team. Example: 0-16 means you can take between 0 and 16 of this type of player.
  • Finally, we have the faction and position of the player. Example: Night Goblin linemen have the faction keyword Goblin, and the position keyword Lineman.
Credit: Games Workshop

Night Goblin Lineman [45k, AD/GPS, 0-16, Goblin, Lineman]: The only differences between these guys and their counterparts on the regular Goblin team are that Night Goblin Line-os come with 2 extra skills: Taunt and Shadowing, and are 5k gp more expensive. My take here is that these guys are pretty good! Taunt is a legit good skill to have on what is basically a speed bump character, as it will allow the Night Gobbo to lock down much more expensive players very easily, especially big blockers who don’t have Block or Tackle. Shadowing will almost never matter, and when it does, you’ll fail the roll anyways. 5k more isn’t my favorite, but I’d say it’s worth it to have your linemen control where the opponent can move their players.

Credit: Games Workshop

Squig Herder [55k, AD/GPS, 0-2, Goblin, Special]: The reason you take these guys is the ever-important Squig Restraint trait, which I’ve written about above. Stat-wise, they’re Goblins with worse Passing. Apart from that, they drop Shadowing, Taunt, and Right Stuff to pick up Fend and Disturbing Presence (along with the aforementioned Squig Restraint). This player is tricky, because you need them to smooth out the variance in your team, but they’re also easily removed and massive targets. And besides giving you access to Squig Restraint, they don’t really do anything else. Everyone is going to need to take at least 1, but then you have to work out how to protect them during the game. Good luck with that!

Squig Hopper [80k, AD/GPS, 0-2, Goblin, Squig, Special]: Now we’re talking! MA7, ST3, AG 3+ with Dodge, Jump Up, Monstrous Mouth, Pogo, and Squig Rage. That’s quite the package! These guys are obviously your best candidates for running the ball, but at ST3, they make a pretty good blitzer, too! AV8+ isn’t good for hanging and banging, but pogo-ing over defenders to sneak a cheeky blitz in and maybe even pick up the ball afterwards isn’t nothing. Monstrous Mouth is funny here because it’s obviously great to negate Strip Ball, but the only time you’d actually want to chomp someone is if they’re ST2 or lower. Because, again, AV8+. Squig Rage here is the only real impediment, but a thoughtfully placed Squig Herder or a sacrificial lineman will mitigate that rule quite nicely. I quite like this player and they’ll obviously be a Night Goblin mainstay.

Squig Wrangler [70k, DS/AG, 0-2, Goblin, Squig, Special]: ST7, Ball & Chain, and no Secret Weapon! Apparently a leashed squig being swung around as a weapon is a legal play in the Blood Bowl rulebook. Or maybe it’s an Air Bud situation where there is no rule expressly prohibiting using a squig as a weapon. Either way, the Squig Wrangler is one of the Night Goblin Team’s biggest draws. You can take 2 of them at team creation and combine them with a Troll for one of the scariest front lines in the game. But just remember: they can absolutely whiff every time they activate and if your friends don’t know how to play around them, trust me, they’ll figure it out. Personally I’m not a huge fan of Ball & Chain players who don’t come with Mighty Blow, so I think the 70k price tag is a lot to ask here. I’m sure a lot of you will windmill slam 2 of these into every roster every time, and for that I salute you. But with random, slow movement and the fact that they’re extremely easy to remove, I wouldn’t bet the game on them.

Cave Squig [85k, -/ADGS, 0-2, Squig, Special]: Here’s a player that I will bet the game on: the Cave Squig! Y’all, these big, round, bitey belligerents are not to be underestimated! With ST4, MA6, Pogo, and Frenzy, no cage is safe! Cave Squigs make fantastic blitzers right out of the box, and every roster will begin by taking 2. They even have AG3+ meaning that they’re not easy to pin down like a lot of ST4 players are! This is another player where you kinda want to fail your Squig Rage checks every now and then, as it will net you extra blocks. The only drawback here is that animalistic Cave Squigs don’t have any primary skills. This means that they’ll take forever to level, but the good news is that these bad biters don’t need any new skills to be great.

Blood Bowl Goblin Troll by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms

Trained Troll [115k, S/AGP, 0-1, Troll, Big Guy]: This is the exact same Troll from the Goblin or Snotling teams, and everyone knows exactly what to expect here. I think they have a special place in this roster as your linemen all come with Right Stuff, and you probably want to be able to take advantage of that with your Throw Team-mate troll. We love a cheap Big Guy.

Starting League Rosters

Let’s start with the 1-box, no-Troll look, as it’s the most accessible way to play this team:

  • 2 Cave Squigs
  • 2 Squig Wranglers
  • 2 Squig Hoppers
  • 2 Squig Herders
  • 4 Night Goblin Linemen
  • 3 Rerolls
  • 970,000 gp

You could drop the 3rd RR for 2 more Night Goblin Linemen if you wanted to, but I think this team is thirsty for RRs and linemen are going to be trivial to pick up during league play. I feel like this team really wants a Troll to anchor the line, but this starting roster is 100% playable, and will probably be a ton of madcap fun.

Ok, let’s get a Troll in here and see what we come up with:

  • 1 Troll
  • 2 Cave Squigs
  • 2 Squig Wranglers
  • 2 Squig Hoppers
  • 1 Squig Herder
  • 4 Linemen
  • 2 Rerolls
  • 960,000 gp

This is your “go big or go home” roster with 2 Wranglers to cause chaos on the line with the Troll. Now, I’m a noted Ball & Chain hater, as they’re slow, random, and easy to remove, but if you’re into that kind of mess, then you’ll love the above roster.

I think the following roster is probably what I’d bring to game 1 of a league:

  • 1 Troll
  • 2 Cave Squigs
  • 1 Squig Wrangler
  • 2 Squig Hoppers
  • 2 Squig Herders
  • 5 Linemen
  • 2 Rerolls
  • 990,000 gp

This list gets you an extra body instead of the 2nd Wrangler, which you’ll probably need because there’s a lot of AV8+ on this team! And a 70k player isn’t exactly a big lift once the league gets going, so you can get that 2nd Wrangler sharpish.

Credit: Games Workshop

League Progression

You’re going to have to balance having enough players on the roster to complete games with not letting your TV get too bloated. It’ll be a tough line to walk, so don’t let your linemen accrue too many upgrades!

  • Night Goblin Linemen: I see these guys as speed bumps and foulers. Speed bumps don’t need (and shouldn’t get!) and upgrades, and foulers just need one or two of the good Devious skills: Dirty Player, Lone Fouler, Quick Fouler, and Sneaky Git. If you get a Lineman up to 6 SPP give them Dirty Player, but if you’re gonna play the random game, I’d fire anyone who didn’t randomly roll up one of those four skills and rehire a new line-o.
  • Squig Herder: These guys don’t really need skills to do what they’re there for, which is to stand around, so I would never select them for MVP or intentionally try and score with them. I probably wouldn’t even level one if they did have enough SPP, honestly. They just don’t need it, and your team doesn’t need the extra TV. But if you are going to level them, I suggest something that will help keep them alive or give them extra mobility. Sprint or Block come to mind.
  • Squig Hopper: Hoppers are your ball carriers, so they want the good ball carrier stuff: Sprint, Block, Sure Hands, Sidestep. You’ll probably end up scoring with these guys quite a bit, so secondary skills are definitely on the menu.
  • Squig Wrangler: Mighty Blow! You want those ST7 hits removing players if at all possible, and ol’, reliable Mighty Blow is there for you. If you manage to get 2 on one of these guys, Brawler is a good bet.
  • Cave Squig: With only secondary skill choices and No Ball, progression is going to be slow with Cave Squigs. But these are your ball-hawks, so treat them like so. They come with Jump Up, so Wrestle is an obvious choice here. After that, Tackle and Strip Ball make a lot of sense. Pro is probably nice here, too, to help mitigate Squig Rage.
  • Troll: In Guard we trust! After that, I like Strong Arm for cheeky Throw Team-mate action. Also, Bullseye is really good for that, too.

Tournament Rosters

For this, we’re going to assume 1.1 million gp for roster creation. However, I don’t know what tier this team is going to end up in. I’d err on the safe side and call them tier 3, but they’re just goblins at the end of the day, so they might end up in tier 4. So, for this exercise, I am assuming a skill pack with 8 skill points and a max of 2 secondary skills available.

  • 4 Night Goblin Linemen
  • 2 Squig Herders
  • 2 Squig Hoppers w/ Sprint
  • 2 Squig Wranglers w/ Mighty Blow
  • 1 Cave Squig w/ Wrestle
  • 1 Cave Squig w/ Tackle
  • 1 Troll
  • 3 RRs
  • 1,085,000 gp
Credit: Games Workshop

General Tactics

The number 1 rule for playing Night Goblins is: Don’t fail your Squig Rage rolls!

But seriously, you’re playing a goblin team, so Fouling every turn is an absolute must. After that, you’ve got to be very careful with positioning your Night Goblins. You’re going to have at least 5 players on your team who want a babysitter or a Squig Herder nearby for every activation. Well-placed Herders will be critical on offense to keep your Hoppers from futzing around. And with 2 Ball & Chain players (probably) you might have fewer than normal mobile dudes running around to help you set cages, so don’t be scared to follow your whirling dervishes down the pitch. Cave Squigs are your absolute best players, but they’re only AV9+, so you can be too reckless with them. At some point you’re going to want to cage dive with them, but you need to Mark the entire cage to avoid getting Frenzy trapped.

This team has a lot of potential to be a problem, but it’s goblins with 3+ gates all around, so play patiently and expect to fail: position your players for maximum impact when you do. Don’t go for the big blocks early in the turn, move those gobbos around first in case you turn it over.

Final Thoughts

I think GW is really on to something with this team. It might not be the most approachable or strongest team out there, but it has a sky-high skill ceiling and looks to be a ton of fun, even in a loss. However, I wouldn’t recommend this team to a new player because it is extremely complicated with interlocking rules and fragile tech pieces. That being said, I am pre-ordering this team immediately and hope to run it early next year in local leagues! Thanks for reading!
