Contemptor Kevin's Combat Patrol Issue 72 Review

November 2nd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Not every issue of Combat Patrol is going to have the most scintillating selection of Hobby Materials. After all, we've had several issues filled with Genestealer Cults Infantry. Unfortunately, Issue #72 is a paint issue and we are only now reviewing it on Halloween 2026. However, while the actual hobby materials this issue comes with are sub-optimal, the contents of this issue do their best to make up for it. So what's going on here? Let's find out! As always, thank you to TabletopBattles and Hachette for the opportunity to review these paints, magazines, and materials.

Hobby Materials

The elephant in the room is that this is a paint issue that has two Warhammer Paint layer paints: White Scar and Yriel Yellow. White Scar is a venerable thinner/less pigmented white paint, while Yriel Yellow is a bright warm yellow, much more vibrant than the subscription's yellow base paint, Warhammer Paint Averland Sunset. I own several of these paints, and one pot of Yriel Yellow recently exploded in a paint bag. Why am I so down on these paints, then?

Because we live in the age of Warhammer Tone Pro, and there is a TonePro equivalent to both paints. While I personally do not own the specific paint, Joseph "CrabstuffedMushrooms" Knoblauch has made it his goal to try and test every TonePro paint that he can. And Joey tells me that Hawkshroud Yellow 2 is a match for Yriel Yellow. As I believe every reader of this website knows now, Blackstone Void 5 rendered every single previous Warhammer Paint white obsolete. So while these paints were perfectly cromulent when the issue came out, they have since been supplanted by better TonePro paints that are easier to use.

Necromunda Fixer painted by CrabstuffedMushrooms, where he took Hawkshroud Yellow 2 and Blackstone Void 5 out for a spin.

And the magazine's Paint Guide is fantastic for how to use Blackstone Void 5 and Hawkshroud Yellow 2 on previously painted miniatures. The article has us use White Scar to highlight and edge highlight the whites and light grey portions of such miniatures like the Terminators' helmets and Crux Terminatuses, use Yriel Yellow on Tyranid eyes and Genestealer Cult mechanics, and Warhammer Paint Administratum Grey to edge highlight various black materials.

Base primed with White Scar Spray. Top half is Yriel Yellow. Bottom Half is Hawkshroud Yellow 2. Main difference is the latter has extremely smooth application. Credit: Kevin Stillman

The issue gives good advice and strategies for painting your minis! The only downside is that there's now better paints that make it easier to edge highlight Warhammer minis.

Narrative Materials

We return to the Silver Templars' Squad Askarton in Bonds of Honour, Part 17. In Part 16, the Space Marines successfully defeated an Ork warboss and a bunch of Orks. As thanks for the assist, the Kin of the Leagues of Votann gave Squad Askarton a lift off-planet. The Kin dropped the Marines off at an Imperial world, to which Squad Askarton hoped to hitchhike their way further through the galaxy. Unfortunately, instead of being dropped off at an operating spaceport, the Kin dropped them off in "some shattered urban wasteland". This didn't bother the Marines overmuch, but Sergeant Askarton still wanted to find his missing Bolt Rifle. So they fired off a vox signal for help. The squad was located by a platoon of the Imperial Guard, who were investigating the appearance of a xenos ship. Unfortunately, the Guardsmen had not actually seen a Space Marine before and were scared. Sergeant Askarton removed his helmet to conduct diplomacy with the Guard, but Sergeant Askarton was an uggo and taking off his helmet scared the Guardsmen even further. One brave Guardswoman decided to step forward and was promptly shot in the head. The rest of the Guard blamed the Space Marines, and unloaded their weapons at the Astartes. The Astartes took cover, and waited for the Guard to exhaust their weapons. Once the Guard finish wasting their ammunition, the real hammer blow falls.

Ankran Mechanised Imperial Guards. Credit: Charlie Brassley

We then get narrative articles about the Guard. The first is about the Combined Arms nature of the Guard. The Imperial Guard usually use combined-arms regiments, which mix artillery, heavy weapons, armored vehicles, and schmucks. They also have special forces detachments, and aircraft in the form of Valkyrie Assault Carriers. The Astra Militarum is the symbol of humanity's resolve, as it is how humanity resists Chaos cults, Chaos Legions, the foulest xenos, and everything else.

Lastly, we get another map of the galaxy with active Astra Militarum warzones. The first two, Thyriss Paterna and Norada VII, are "redacted", while the third is Vigilus. We have several other battlezones, such as Rafnar Sub-4c where insurrectionist mining guides are defeated but now an "unknown foe battles against Imperial troops in the cramped mine workings", Gorentz (Where the Orks are having a happy time), and Orvastan Planus (Tyranids).

Gaming Materials

I've mentioned before that the Hachette Combat Patrol magazine is fairly cynical towards the Imperium of Man in their narrative sections, and nowhere is this more evidence than in the gaming materials of this section. We have two Crusade missions, and the first is Astra Militarum versus Space Marines. As if trying to recreate the battle from Bonds of Honour Part 17, we have the Astartes seeking archaeotech and telling the Guard to keep the Marine mission quiet. Unfortunately, Guard command is a mess and so the Marines just opt to wipe out the Guard in order to leave no survivors.

The second mission is the Guard having to fight the Tyranids, because the Tyranids want to eat everything and the Guard want to dissuade the Tyranids from trying to eat them.

The Final Verdict

The materials in this magazine are, bluntly, a lot of fun. We rarely get unity between the gaming scenario and the narrative scenarios at this point in Combat Patrol, and the gaming scenario reflects the cynicism of the author. I was not expecting this level of fun from the narrative section when writing this while handing out trick-or-treat candy!

The magazine's hobby material section is also quite nice, explaining where to make final touch highlights that give miniatures an bright, extra pop. Where the issue runs into some value trouble, of course, is that the paint issues are usually the weaker bargains from Combat Patrol. On top of that, GW has since introduced a new paint range that specifically obsoletes this issues two paints. So if you are only buying this magazine for the bargains...you can get pots of Blackstone Void 5 and Hawkshroud Yellow 2 for cheaper than this issues' MSRP. Luckily, next week we're back to our regularly scheduled model issues.

Until next time Combat Patrolers!

Originally published on Goonhammer.
