[40K] 11th Edition Points Review: Chaos Factions

June 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
The final piece of the 11th Edition puzzle is revealed today, with the Munitorum Field Manual landing and providing points costs and Force Dispositions for all factions. Our team have dug in to analyse what this means for the early 11th Edition metagame in our massive review. This part focuses on the Space Marine factions.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with early access to these rules. Any details we mention are subject to change until published on Warhammer Community or the Warhammer 40K App.

Welcome to our review of Chaos Faction points for 11th Edition. As it's a whole new world out there, most factions in the game get lots of changes, so we're not going to try and list every single one. For each faction, we're focusing on the changes that we think will have the biggest impact on listbuilding, and covering off where we think the army lands in the new edition.

You can find the rest of our review, covering other factions and a general overview, via the links below:

The Key Question: Is the Defiler Still Good?

Rob: The big constant through all five Chaos factions is that Defilers went up in cost, both in terms of base cost - usually to around 290 to 300 points, and in terms of weapons, with the hades lascannon and heavy reaper autocannon options now costing 10 points per weapon. This means most factions will pay 320 points for their first Defiler, and 350 each for the second and third. That’s an eye-wateringly high increase for a unit that was criminally undercosted.

But here’s the interesting thing: Even at the higher cost, the Defiler is still pretty good. The reality is that most Chaos Space Marines vehicles were created before 8th edition, and carry the kinds of older design sensibilities that don’t see them festooned with guns like a Repulsor, Rogal Dorn, or even a Hekaton Land Fortress. The Defiler just has more powerful guns on it than any other single Chaos Vehicle, and puts those guns on a chassis that’s fast, moves over terrain, and can occasionally scrap in melee.

All of this is to say that, even at the higher cost, it may often be worth bringing a Defiler in your list. I suspect the days of triple Defilers are over - that list just loses a staggering 270 points just on the increases for Defilers - but taking a single Defiler still has a lot of value and I suspect we’ll still see lists attempting to run two of them.

Ultimately whether your army brings a Defiler (or more than one) will depend heavily on the rest of your available datasheets. For Emperor’s Children, for example, there just isn’t anything else in the army which can do what the Defiler does. You’re likely still going to be bringing a Defiler because you need to be able to destroy enemy transports and deal with some units from a distance. But that’s a high cost to pay for an army that’s already pretty elite.

For Chaos Space Marines, the will probably also be that you still take Defilers. The CSM Defiler is just much better than the cult options when you factor in the buffs it can get from Vashtorr and Abaddon, Dark Pacts, and its ability to get devastating wounds once per game. That these are only 10 points more than the EC/DG/TS variants is the kind of tone-deaf points adjusting we’re used to seeing for units shared across factions. One CSM defiler is likely still going to be common, and I suspect some lists will go hard on bringing two with Vashtorr and Abaddon, if only because they already bought and painted three of the things and want to use them. And while you have lots of datasheets to choose from, many of them are just bad.

On the other hand, the other three Cult Legion factions have more options and worse Defilers. A Thousand Sons Defiler with twin lascannons will now set you back 310 points. Meanwhile Overwatch is a bit worse and you can easily get two Forgefiends for less (270 pts). Is a Defiler better than two Thousand Sons Forgefiends? I have strong doubts on that one, and the other point increases make finding space for a Defiler even harder.. Likewise, Death Guard have to similarly ask if they’d rather have a Defiler or a PBC and another bloat-drone. World Eaters may just opt for electroscourge Defilers or go back to running Maulerfiends, but the cheaper cost on their Defilers may make them more attractive.

I think we’ve reached the end of triple Defiler mania but one will still be pretty common and two will show up from time to time in CSM lists. You just don’t have that many good alternatives to build around.

Chaos Daemons

Rob: Chaos Daemons get one of the biggest armywide buffs of any faction this edition, just based entirely on the fact that they now benefit from cover, making every single Daemon unit more durable than it was before. This means that any Daemon that isn’t seeing a points increase is being buffed significantly more than it first appears, especially if they’re a melee threat or a shooting threat with a [PSYCHIC] weapon.

Key Points Changes

Not too many units saw point drops in Daemons, though the units which did really needed them. On the other hand, most of the units seeing increases also gained substantial boosts in power as a result of the way rules work in the new edition.

Buffs

  • Bloodcrushers: –15pts on the 3 model unit, -40 on the 6 model one. A chunky drop for them. This probably reflects how difficult it will be to get all of them into combat on those huge bases, and is welcome. It also hopefully reflects how bad it is to have the MOUNTED keyword in 11th edition. May get some juice out of the Cavalcade of Chaos detachment.
  • Daemon Prince of Chaos: -25pts. I’ll be honest, I kind of forget these are even in the faction, which probably explains why they get a sizeable drop here.
  • Rendmaster on Blood Throne: -15pts. These weren’t seeing tons of play, and suffer a bit from new cover, so down they go.
  • Skullmaster: -15pts. Similarly not getting a lot of play right now.

Nerfs*

  • Be’lakor/Bloodthirster/Great Unclean One/Keeper of Secrets/Lord of Change/Rotigus: +15pts. A bump for most of the big monsters, presumably reflecting that they now benefit from cover in a way they didn’t before, and for the flying ones they gain there too. Slightly odd for Be’lakor, who already had Stealth.
  • Fluxmaster: +20pts. Already seeing a lot of use in Scintillating Legion lists, and its datasheet -1 to hit ability is very strong now that it stacks with cover.
  • Nurglings: +5pts on the 3 model unit, but +20pts on the 6. Bucking a trend of bigger unit sizes getting favourable points drops, the 6-model unit costs twice as much as the 3. That makes perfect sense, given how stupidly durable a 6-model unit can be. Nurglings at +5 are a particular steal, as they can now move through walls as a SWARM unit and a 6-model unit can tie up quite a few things in Engagement Range simultaneously with a 2” range.
  • The Changeling: +15pts. Similarly to the Fluxmaster, the Changeling has a -1 to hit effect (which can also stack with its native Stealth). Its Battle-shock ability is also a bit more powerful now since it means they’ll need to re-test.

Outlook

Winner

Thoughts

Liam: The 10th-edition style Daemonic Incursion lists with 5 Greater Daemons probably don’t survive the points changes and needing to run Disruption. However, Daemons benefit quite a lot from the new rules - cover actually helps them now, and they’ve got some interesting options for 2DP detachments to combine with some of the new 1DP ones. Shadow Legion giving you access to Purge the Foe is very much worth a look as a starting point.

Chaos Knights

Rob: Chaos Knights have been slowly morphing into their own faction over the last three editions much in the way Ryu and Ken had become distinct fighters in the Street Fighter series by the time Third Strike rolled around. Unfortunately, similar to Third Strike, one of those is just way better than the other. Chaos Knights tend to just have worse faction rules than Imperial Knights, and are forced to make up for that with better datasheets. In that regard, the point changes here are not kind.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Cerastus Knight Acheron: -15pts. No one was taking these because frankly it's just a worse Ruinator somehow. Its Battle-shock aura is better now but its primary weapon is still a flamer that can’t overwatch.
  • Cerastus Knight Castigator -15pts. This one saw slightly more love than the Acheron but not by much. Its suppression mechanic is actually way better now since it stacks with cover and with the death of the Despoiler (more on that in a bit) means it might see play.
  • Knight Styrix: -10pts. The Styrix is an Abominant if it had a fist and a useful aura so seeing this come down is nice. Could be a good splash into dog heavy lists looking to leverage Executioners.
  • Knight Ruinator: -15pts. The Ruinator is finally cheaper than when he launched! Does this make him usable? No! We may see him splashed in to help dogs but this is still a dogwater Knight. Notably with the extra unit taxes, you still can’t fit 6 bigs in a list no matter how hard you try.
  • Knight Tyrant: -10pts. Neat but this Knight got way worse with the new cover changes.

Nerfs

  • Knight Despoiler: -10pts but +15 per battle cannon and +25 per gatling. On top of that your second and third come with an additional 10 point tax. This brings the triple gatling Despoiler list up at least 160 points (we haven’t even gotten to karnivores yet). The Despoiler definitely committed the sin of showing up in most lists so it was always going to get hit, but this is a bit excessive.
  • War Dog Karnivore: +5pts. This is a small tap but adds up quickly if you were looking to pivot to dog spam. The good news is executioners become much more attractive in the new meta so you can splash in those to offset this.
  • Knight Lancer: +10pts. This Knight now benefits from cover way more than it did before, making it even harder to kill.

2 DP Detachments

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Norman: The obliteration of the Despoiler both in points and rules is a bit of a hard hit for the faction that was being propped up by them. Even with that crutch, they weren’t exactly setting the world on fire and the new cover rules really hurt pure shooting knights since almost all of those do NOT want to be stuck in melee which they open themselves up to if they want to use plunging fire.

We’ll likely see a pivot to dogs and melee focused bigs (even with the nerf the Lancer is a great pick) but it's likely going to look strictly worse than it did before. The good news is that most anti knight shooting pushed them to their invuln anyway so old cover didn’t do much for them and new cover helps them a lot so we may see high pressure chaos knights lists perform well. On top of that all of their detachments have access to Take and Hold and Purge the Foe which is a great set of primaries for them.

Chaos Space Marines

Rob: The Chaos Space Marines somersaulted to the top of the faction standings at the end of tenth edition on the back of some great Detachments and insane new datasheets, the most busted of which was the new Defiler. Everyone knew coming into this that the Defiler was getting some point hikes, so the question remains as to where else the Heretic Astartes are getting hit.

Key Points Changes

The good news is that this is more of a mixed bag than you’d think. There are some units here which go down in points and feel like they got away with something, and a few which as you’d expect go up. That said, some of these feel like they’re going up because of detachments you might take with them, and that’s kind of lame given you might not take those.

Buffs

  • Chaos Spawn: -10 pts. The worst Chaos Spawn gets a much-needed points drop. You still don’t take this guy at 60 however, because being OC 0 just sucks.
  • Cultist Mob: -10 pts for a unit of 20. This will only ever matter in Chaos Cult and if you’re taking them alongside Chaos Knights, which to be fair, is not a bad thing.90 points for 20 cultists is a solid deal.
  • Fellgor Beastmen: -10 pts. Beastmen are the less-loved children of the Chaos Cult family so it’s nice to see them get some love.
  • Heldrake: -30 pts. Like the other Heldrakes, this one drops to 175. It’s nowhere close to the best version of the Heldrake, but it has its uses. At only 12” of Movement, the unit does suffer, but it’s still a really solid unit for making early charges and taking down flying threats. The CSM one needs to be 150 points, however.
  • Master of Executions: -10 pts. The Master of Executions is now a SUPPORT unit, so can’t run solo.
  • Mutilators: -20pts, -10 for your third unit. Mutilators are a hard unit to justify as a slow-moving melee threat. Dropping a unit of three to 180 certainly helps, but it’s still difficult to justify taking these over a unit of Possessed or Obliterators.
  • Red Corsairs Raiders: -10 pts for a unit of 10. This is neat but I don’t see you ever doing it.
  • Red Corsairs Reave-Captain: -5 pts. This is so small it barely matters but the Reave-Captain needs all the help he can get to be relevant.

Nerfs

  • Abaddon the Despoiler: +15 pts. This feels straight-up like a nerf based on Defilers, but if you’ve already nerfed them via points, why double tap Abaddon, who could have helped other units? It’s a weird decision.
  • Accursed Cultists: +20pts for your 2nd+ unit. Part of a pair of rough nerfs designed to hurt Chaos Cults lists specifically.
  • Defiler: The big bad bogeyman of the end of tenth edition goes up an eye-watering 50 points in its base cost, to 300 for your first unit, and 330 for your 2nd+. On top of that you’ll pay 10 pts per Hades Lascannon and 10 pts per Heavy Reaper Autocannon you add to the unit, for a cost 320 points for your first double las/autocannon Defiler and 350 for each extra one. That’s a massive price hike on a unit that’s very good, but not potentially 350 points good when you can get literally two Forgefiends for the same price. It’s enough to prevent us from seeing triple Defilers moving forward, but two is still a very real possibility.
  • Forgefiend: -10 pts for your first and second units… BUT +5 for each Ectoplasma Cannon. This means a triple ecto Forgefiend is actually +5 for your first two and +15 for your third. This potentially makes the case for the Hades Autocannons but I just don’t see them being taken over the Ectoplasma.
  • Chaos Lord with Jump Pack: +10pts. This one is pretty nonsensical. As best I can tell it’s here because Murdertalon Raiders makes the Jump Lord better, but if you aren’t taking that Detachment, then this just makes a mediocre unit worse.
  • Chaos Vindicator, Venomcrawler, Chaos Predators, Havocs, Obliterators, Raptors, Red Corsairs Raiders (5 models): +10 points for your 3rd+ unit. This is pretty common and a lot of these weren’t likely to be taken as 3-ofs, save Venomcrawlers in Soulforged Warpack lists.
  • Chosen: +10pts for your 2nd+ unit. 3x Chosen lists aren’t super common but armies with two units have become really common as players take Huron + Chosen + Masters and Fabius + Chosen. This added tax pairs with the Masters points hike to make it all hurt quite a bit.
  • Dark Commune: +10 pts for your 2nd+ unit. Similarly, this one hurts Chaos Cult lists where you were likely to take multiples.
  • Masters of the Maelstrom: +20pts. Yeah, these guys were too cheap at 115 points. They’re still worth it at 135, because they are better than five chosen.
  • Possessed: +10 pts for a 10 model unit, +10/20 for your 3rd+ unit. Possessed are rarely run in units of five models, and for good reason. This really hits Creations of Bile lists, where running 30 Possessed was a viable strategy.
  • Traitor Enforcer: +15 pts. This seems unnecessary given the changes to wound allocation, but the Enforcer was a bit cheap given how many wounds you got from him.
  • Vashtorr the Arkifane: +30 pts. Vashtorr was a key cog in Defiler lists, and one of the faction’s best individual units. 205 is a lot for him.
  • Warp Talons: +10 for 10 models, +10/20 for your 3rd+ unit. Warp Talons also rarely/never show up as units of five models, so this hits the one version you’d want to take. It makes sense given how good they are even at 270 with Murdertalon Raiders buffing them, but I struggle to think why you’d take them without Murdertalon now.

Outlook

Loser

Thoughts

Chaos Space Marines are getting slapped hard here, paying for their sins at the end of tenth edition. Multiple armies have been nerfed here, from triple Defiler builds to triple Chosen builds to Chaos Cults. There’s still likely to be some play in this faction owing to how strong some of those datasheets and detachment rules, but players are absolutely going to feel it and top players are likely to jump ship to other factions.

Death Guard

The Death Guard have been a powerful faction since the release of their tenth edition Codex, with a mix of strong datasheets and abilities. As an elite army with good access to Invulnerable Saves, torrent weapons and AP-1/AP-2 shooting, they’re well positioned to benefit from the rules changes of 11th edition and a number of their point changes reflect that.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Daemon Prince of Nurgle with Wings: -10pts. The Winged Daemon Prince never saw much play, in part because he doesn’t have the ability to gain Lone Operative and in part because his abilities suck. Now he’s cheaper and still bad. The drop is likely because Desperate Escape tests are less deadly than they used to be, so his second bad ability is even worse. Hooray!
  • Foul Blightspawn: -10pts. This makes sense given how much worse the Fights First ability is. They could go back to him giving it to his unit as a passive ability and he’d still need a 10 point drop. That said, this guy still has a solid gun and 4 wounds, so 65 isn’t a terrible price for him, but there’s no reason to take him over a Plaguecaster if you have the points now.
  • Helbrute: -5pts. This makes very little difference.
  • Lord of Poxes: -10pts. Being untargetable from more than 18” away just isn’t as good in 11th edition, where you can just hide behind terrain or be hidden in cover at 15”. That said, he also wasn’t getting played much and he’s got a decent melee weapon so this is a welcome change to make him more viable with a unit of Plague Marines.
  • Plagueburst Crawler: -25pts. So we know that INDIRECT FIRE is worse in 11th than it was in 10th, making you only hit on a 6+ against targets you can’t see without a spotter. But PBCs have a key advantage over other indirect vehicles: They’re very good just rumbling forward, shooting things they can see. You’ll be doing less indirect fire - though Poxwalkers make fine spotters - and playing more aggressively with them, and you can afford to do that. If you take more than 1, they’ll cost 210 each.
  • Plague Marines: -5/-5/0pts. Units of 5 and 7 models each went down five points, respectively, and you’ll never pay more for taking more units. This is a nice little bonus for smaller units, where if you’re taking a lot of units with characters, those extra points you’re saving can really add up.
  • Plague Drones: -5/-10pts. Daemons just got better across the board in 11th, owing to the fact that they now actually benefit from cover.

Nerfs

  • Chaos Land Raider: +20pts for your 3rd+ unit. Who exactly was taking three Death Guard Land Raiders and why are we punishing them for losing games?
  • Chaos Predator Annihilator: +10pts for your 3rd+ unit. The Annihilator was better in tenth, where the big lascannon wasn’t significantly worse against targets with an invulnerable save. You weren’t going to take three but if you do, you’ll pay marginally more.
  • Chaos Predator Destructor: +10pts for your 3rd+ unit. Destructors have a lot more value in 11th compared to the Annihilator and if you’re taking one you’re probably taking 2-3. This is a small tax on your third, it’ll be fine.
  • Deathshroud Terminators: +10pts for your 3rd+ unit. Deathshroud Terminators are already nerfed by the new Deep Strike rules, going from 6” charges to 7” when they arrive outside 6”, but on the upside they can arrive outside 6” with Rapid Ingress now. This feels like a straight nerf but also most lists will just run two units instead of three anyways.
  • Defiler: +40pts/+70pts for your 2nd+ unit. We knew Defilers were going to catch point hikes, and here we are. The Heavy Reaper and Lascannon options also have costs now, and each one you take will up the cost by an extra 10 points, so your first defiler will cost 310 with double reapers. I think one Defiler still makes a ton of sense, taking two or more seems much less efficient when you can get a PBC and a fleshmower for less.
  • Foetid Bloat-Drones: +10pts for your 3rd+ drone. These have an easier time going over walls now, making them faster getting around the table.
  • Bloat Drones with Heavy Blight Launcher: +5/+15 for your 3rd+ unit. The HBL Drone is still one of the game’s best units, and potentially a nastier threat when shooting at afflicted targets in cover. You’ll want to pair them with Lords of Virulence more now. As units with FLY can now move over walls much more easily to get their shots off.
  • Mortarion: +20pts. Mortarion goes up, in part because he’s able to just jump over walls now. +20 is a bit steep for that given he still only moves 10”, but he’s going to feel more durable if you can get him cover as well.
  • Noxious Blightbringer: +10pts. This one makes no sense, as the Blightbringer wasn’ getting played at all.
  • Tallyman: +10pts. The Tallyman goes up again, in part because ignoring modifiers on hit rolls is really good now. That said, plague marines don’t actually pack all that much shooting, and when they do, two of those guns tend to be sewage flamers which do not roll to hit. The play here is to build around the Tallyman and Biologus Putrifier, packing lots of Plasma guns or Meltaguns.
  • Daemons of Nurgle: point increases for everything but Plague Drones. Daemons are just more durable now and these monsters are no exception..

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Rob: The Death Guard make out OK in these changes. Being able to give enemy units -1 to saves on top of the changes to cover are really good for some of the faction’s weapons but don’t do anything for the large array of TORRENT weapons which will feel the same as always. The biggest winners in all of this are the Malignant Plaguecaster, whose TORRENT gun did not have [IGNORES COVER], and the Tallyman, who suddenly gets a lot more value out of his ability to ignore modifiers for his unit. There’s very much an interesting plague marine unit you can run that takes a Tallyman and Biologus Putrifier with a plasma gun, two meltaguns, and two Blight launchers for a decent amount of shooting. The rest of the changes are fine, and I think single PBCs are a lot more attractive than they were as a result of this.

The only change here that’s truly baffling is the Land Raider, where it seems some brain genius decided that because Custodes were taking three, everyone needed to pay extra for their third.

Emperor’s Children

Rob: No Chaos army benefited more from the new Defiler Datasheet than the Emperor’s Children, who have very few Datasheets and prior to its release had almost no access to good ranged shooting. This meant they were largely unable to deal with transports, having to kill them in melee, after which you’d be vulnerable to shooting and attacked. The Defiler solves this problem and then some by giving the faction a real ranged shooting threat it just does not have otherwise. That said, aside from the Defiler, the Emperor’s Children have several helpful tools in 11th edition’s rules, and chief among those is Noise Marines, who come with Ignores Cover built in on their weapons.

Key Points Changes

Aside from the Defiler, which sees a big points hike, most of these changes work in the faction’s favor, though some are more of a mixed bag.

Buffs

  • Daemon Prince: -10 pts, +5 for your 2nd+ unit. The foot Daemon Prince has a lot going for him, but lacks the raw power and durability of the winged variant. Taking him down to 170 helps make an OK case for him, though he might not be cheap enough yet.
  • Winged Daemon Prince: -10 pts for your first Winged Daemon prince is a big help for one of the army’s best units. You’ll pay more for models 2+, but at least taking the first is less painful.
  • Flawless Blades: -5/-10 pts. Flawless Blades have started to see some play since their boost in melee damage, and this will help them get over that last remaining hump, especially as other units go up.
  • Heldrake: -20 pts. The Emperor’s Children Heldrake sees a similar drop in costs to the others, and comes with the same drop in movement and removal of AIRCRAFT. The output on a Heldrake just isn’t very high, and making the most of the grenades ability is a lot tougher with 12” movement, so this may not matter.
  • Lucius the Eternal: -20 pts. That’s a big drop on Lucius, and is interesting when you combine it with the drop in cost for Flawless Blades. The downside is that it’s accompanied by the change to Fights First being pretty bad and making him a much less effective leader. His Lone Operative ability is still very relevant however, and Lucius is likely to see more play as a lone op now.
  • Tormentors: -5 pts. Tormentors are a staple of the army, so these end up being 5 to 15 points you can use elsewhere.
  • Fiends and Seekers: -5 pts.

Nerfs

  • Fulgrim: +10pts. His -1 to hit ability that is chosen in their opponents Command phase is quite powerful as it stacks with the Benefit of Cover. Not unexpected, but stings a little for a primarch that had only just started seeing the table in Court of the Phoenician.
  • Winged Daemon Prince: As laid out above, the first one is 10pts cheaper than before, but every prince after that is 20pts more than before. This means that even in 2-prince builds their overall cost went up 10pts and 3-prince builds went up 30pts.
  • Defiler: +40pts (+70pts for Defiler #2 onwards); +10pts for each Heavy reaper autocannon or Hades lascannon. The army that benefited the most from the new Defiler datasheet is going to suffer the most from these. The most common builds ran two Defilers, so this represents a 150 point increase in the pair’s overall cost. Not easy to stomach when EC lists were already tight on points.
  • Lord Exultant: +10pts for your third one. Not a huge change, but definitely hurts the MSU-style Coterie lists that tried to flood the opponent with relatively cheap trading units.
  • Noise Marines: +15pts for your third one. This makes pivoting from Defilers more difficult as the Defilers were previously a replacement for three units of Noise Marines. Before it was 500 points for two Defilers or 435 points for three units of Noise Marines. The difference has definitely grown - those same two Defilers are now 650 points while three units of Noise Marines are now 450 points. Less stuff in your army either way

  • Elegant Brutes - Take and Hold
  • Frenzied Host - Disruption
  • Spectacle of Slaughter - Purge the Foe

Outlook

Loser

Thoughts

Rob: The Emperor’s Children just don’t make out great with this, and are a prime example of the flawed philosophy behind dropping blanket “spam tax” costs on every army and unit. In an army with only two dozen units, how exactly are you supposed to avoid taking three of a unit when so many of your options just aren’t good? The Defiler increase hurts the most here, as Emperor’s Children armies just need Defilers because nothing else in the book can do what they do. This is made worse by the increase to Noise Marines, who are otherwise well situated in 11th with Ignores Cover on their shooting. The change to Fights First makes Lucius worse overall, and we’re not sure making Flawless Blades slightly cheaper makes up for that.

Thousand Sons

The Thousand Sons have been among the game’s better armies since the launch of their tenth edition book, but were largely held under the radar by a summer dominated by knights and Death Guard. They saw a strong re-emergence during the height of C’tan madness, owing to their ability to focus down big targets and throw out mortal wounds. As an army filled with [PSYCHIC] attacks, they stand to gain a lot from the 11th edition rules, though their flamers gain nothing from the change to how cover works. On the other hand, they have other ways of Ignoring Cover that work just fine.

Key Points Changes

Most of the changes here are increases, but they come with a comparative power increase resulting from the change to how PSYCHIC attacks work. It makes the army more elite, which hurts, but not necessarily less powerful.

Buffs

  • Heldrake: -40 pts. I cannot tell you how much I love this change - Heldrakes needed a drop, and while this comes with a big change in how they play, moving them to 12” move is a bummer, it also actually makes them a unit you might consider. Thousand Sons Heldrakes don’t have a ton of damage output, but the ability to strip cover from a unit you pass over in the movement phase is pretty big, especially against big threats you need to hit with a unit of Scarabs.
  • Daemon Prince: -10pts. The aura is weaker, but not horrible on a faction full of invulnerable saves.
  • Exalted Sorcerer on Disc of Tzeentch: -10pts. Maybe the assumption is that Lone Op 18” is less good with Hidden in the game, or that the ignores cover is less good, but this is still a strong unit, and applying move and charge debuffs seems better in 11th, so this is welcome.

Nerfs

  • Chaos Land Raider: +20 pts for your third unit. Again, why are we doing this?
  • Predator Annihilator and Destructor: +10 pts for your third unit. Both of these are really good, cheap picks in Thousand Sons armies and it’s unlikely you’ll ever take that third unit but two could definitely happen in vehicle-heavy builds. The AP-2 Heavy Bolters in particular make for a nasty threat that can compete with the sponson lascannons against a lot more targets than you’d think. This isn’t really much of a nerf.
  • Chaos Vindicator: +10 pts for your third Vindicator. Sure, whatever.
  • Daemon Prince with Wings: +10 pts for your 2nd+ unit. Seeing a second winged Daemon Prince was rare but not unheard of in competitive lists and +10 likely won’t stop you from taking one. The Winged Daemon Prince is particularly solid in Thousand Sons in 11th edition, as he has both a great save profile and a decent [PSYCHIC] weapon with Dark Blessing (and the AP-2 Infernal Cannon is also just very good). His Hunter of Souls ability also just seems great for dropping in, stealing objectives, and getting around hidden for units.
  • Defiler: +40pts for the base model, +10 for each lascannon/reaper autocannon, +70 for your 2nd+ model. You knew this was going to happen. Thousand Sons Defilers, like all of the new Defilers, were just way too good for their cost. That makes some sense, given they just have extra AP on all their weapons, but it does mean that taking more than one just isn’t likely to happen. That’s probably a good thing, but I think if you do take them, it’s probably still with double autocannons - the AP-2 Reaper Heavy Autocannons are just too good in 11th edition compared to the lascannons. But as we've said, we think there are better options for Thousand Sons lists.
  • Exalted Sorcerer: +10 pts. The Exalted Sorcerer is one of many characters who went up a bit, likely because of his PSYCHIC attack getting better. That said, his 4+ invulnerable save for his unit is also stronger than it used to be, owing to the new cover rules making AP-2 weapons much deadlier.
  • Forgefiend: +5 pts, +15 for your 3rd+ unit. Holy shit what a steal. Forgefiends at 135 points are an absolute treasure, and I think you run at least two and probably three in most Thousand Sons lists. AP-2 Hades Autocannons can do real work, and the ability to give key threats -1 to hit, stacking with the benefit of cover to drop them to -2 can absolutely neutralize many key threats.
  • Magnus the Red: +20 pts. Magnus gets a small bump, which is technically a nerf but then again he can also jump over walls more easily and his attacks now ignore cover and other modifiers. Players will take that trade all day.
  • Maulerfiend, Mutalith: +10 pts for your third unit.
  • Scarab Occult Terminators: +10 pts for a 10-model squad, +15/25 for your third unit. Scarabs are a stupidly durable unit in 11th edition and the ability to bring back dead models with Ritual fo Regeneration makes them incredibly nasty to fight through. Their AP-2 bolters are even better in this edition, especially if you can find a way to strip cover from the target. The increase hurts, especially if you wanted to run 30, but they come by it honestly.
  • Rubrics: +10 pts for your 4th+ unit. Rubrics are just all upside this edition and taking four or more units could realistically happen. The Icon of Flame gives your unit [IGNORES COVER], and that makes their inferno bolters even more competitive with the flamers, to the point now where I legitimately love the idea of running Exalted + 10 Rubric units in an army with Ritual of Regeneration where you can bring back 2-4 models per turn.
  • Sorcerer: +5 pts.
  • Sorcerer in Terminator Armour: +10 pts/+20 for your 3rd+ unit. This is an additional tax on Scarabs, because you really need this guy, especially now that cover is -1 BS to the attacking unit. He’s still worth it, but just know that you’ll have to pay both.
  • Tzaangors: +5 for 10 model units, -15 for 20s. Only the 10s matter, and this will sting a little.
  • Lord of Change: +15 pts, +30 for your third. The big bird needed an increase, and got one here too.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Grand Coven - Priority Assets
  • Rubricae Phalanx - Take and Hold
2 DP Detachments
  • Changehost of Deceit - Reconnaissance
  • Hexwarp Thrallband - Take and Hold
  • Warpforged Cabal - Disruption
  • Warpmeld Pact - Unique: MUTANT, Purge the Foe
1 DP Detachments
  • Ritual of Regeneration - Purge the Foe
  • Sekhetar Cohort - Priority Assets
  • Servants of Change - Unique: MUTANT, Reconnaissance

Outlook

Winner

Thoughts

Rob: Can an army get better if most of its units go up in cost? The answer here, I believe, is a resounding “yes.” The Thousand Sons hit all of the mathematical advantages you want in an 11th edition army, with the ability to ignore new cover, AP-1/-2 shooting, and invulnerable saves to mitigate enemy AP. You’ll feel the point increases here, but not as much as your opponents will feel the psychological damage inflicted by your army. Several units here are an outright steal for their cost, and units like Rubrics just got much better without really going up in price.

World Eaters

Rob: The World Eaters have a big challenge in 11th edition, which is that they have bad Ballistic Skill, a tradeoff they made for more shots which was bonkers good in 10th edition but looks worse when they’re hitting on 5s against targets in cover. That hurts an army which had some decent tools for popping transports before arrival, but they skate by a bit on being a big melee army.

Key Points Changes

There’s a lot of small tweaks in here in both directions, so we’ve focused on the changes > 10pts.

Buffs

  • Khorne Berzerkers: -15pts on the 20 model unit. This likely reflects the thinking we already mentioned in our review articles - bigger units are doubly impacted by the 9” coherency bubble, because it limits your ability to reach out and touch lots of different units at once, and the change to a 2” engagement range, because it’s harder to get all your models to fight. For Berzerkers this is mitigated a bit by the Lord on Juggernaut, but dropping them by a few points is still welcome.
  • Heldrake: -25pts. A welcome drop for the dragon, which you could be forgiven for forgetting the existence of previously. With 6 melee attacks, the Heldrake becomes a more interesting threat, especially against Flying targets. The World Eaters version probably takes the flamer over the Autocannon.
  • Bloodcrushers: Like their Chaos Daemons version, -15pts on the 3 model unit and -40 on the 6 models. That’s a big old drop for Bloodcrushers, a unit already seeing use in Khorne Daemonkin lists, and very welcome.

Nerfs

  • Defiler: +20pts base, plus a 10pt cost for each heavy reaper autocannon or Hades lascannon. Look, these things were too cheap, but this does feel a bit like the World Eaters version is being punished for the excesses elsewhere.
  • Bloodthirster: +15pts. Bit of an odd one as it’s now more expensive than Skarbrand, which was actually showing up in lists, but this may well reflect the fact that FLY is now much better than it was.
  • Kharn the Betrayer: +15pts. Yeah he’s in every single list. On the other hand, it’s not like he has much competition, and you probably keep running him anyway, so what was the point?
  • Battle-lust (Enhancement): +10pts. This one is a small change numerically, but it doubles the cost of the enhancement. We assume this is because it now nets out to a +2 to charges with the army rule, which is pretty beefy.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Berzerker Warband - Purge the Foe
2 DP Detachments
  • Cult of Blood - Priority Assets
  • Goretrack Onslaught (Unique: ONSLAUGHT) - Take and Hold
  • Khorne Daemonkin - Reconnaissance
  • Possessed Slaughterband - Purge the Foe
1 DP Detachments
  • Brazen Engines (Unique: ONSLAUGHT) - Purge the Foe
  • Butchers of Khorne - Disruption
  • Vessels of Wrath - Priority Assets

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Liam: There’s not a ton of changes here and they’re mostly pretty tippy-tappy, so the faction stands still. It’ll be interesting to figure out if there’s a good angle on doing Khorne Daemonkin with one of the 1DP detachments to take advantage of that hefty discount on Bloodcrushers.

Rob: The biggest problem World Eaters have is that being BS4+ is fine when you’re hitting on 4s and terrible when you’re constantly getting -1 BS from cover. Their core shooting units all needed some drops and they didn’t get them. Some of this can be mitigated - World Eaters Defilers certainly get more out of the Electroscourge - but Forgefiends only going down 5 points feels rough.

Wrap Up

Thus concludes our look at the Chaos factions, but there's foes and victims aplenty to examine in other parts of the review, so use the links at the top to check those out!
