Hobby Build Academy: Holiday Gift Advice

November 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With less than two weeks till Black Friday and the crush of holiday shopping begins in earnest, your Hobby Build Academy faculty have been talking about how to prepare for holiday gifting, but not in the sense of where to buy or find deals, but more how to help guide those who aren't hobby familiar to getting gifts at both a good cost and for what you actually need; sometimes, getting the right gift from someone can have almost as many hardships as getting the right gift FOR another person. This can be especially true if you're dealing with extended family or friends who aren't that into what you do for a hobby, and while we'll be talking about Gunpla, LEGO and other model kits today, this advice can probably serve just about anyone who gets asked "what would you like for the holidays"?

Marcy: So, you and I are pretty good gift givers, I think; you certainly always manage to surprise people and put a lot of thought into things, and I've always felt like it takes some skill to be a considerate gifter.

Elly: I mean, the idea is that you're giving people something they want, but also something that says "hey, I thought about you", so... it goes to reason that you should probably be putting some decent thought into the things you buy?

Marcy: Right. Well, you've met my family, and I've met yours, so...

Elly: ...Yeah. Let's just leave it at "Not everyone is good at gifts."

Marcy: I also think that sometimes people can easily overlook that when people are not in a community or hobby space of some sort, relying on other people to find gifts for your special interest places a lot of labor on that person to produce something they may not have any idea about, let alone access to, and that taking some time to consider their feelings can be the difference between a thoughtful gift and something that was a last minute hail mary.

Elly: Yeahhhhhh. Relying on Amazon to just solve all of your problems is not the way to go,  but sometimes when people really don't know what else to do and it says the item will arrive tomorrow, it can be an easy go tfo. So, we figured we'd try to make it easier by coming up with some tips for people to think about regarding hobby stuff before people start doing their shopping; a lot of people do almost all of it right at the start of the season, so this is probably right on time!

Make a List

Okay, so the first tip is pretty obvious: make a list for people to work off of. Make the list in order of priority and then be sure to give it to people in a method that actually works for them; if they don't shop on Amazon, don't hand them an Amazon wishlist. If they don't know what a "Google Drive" is, don't send them a Sheets file with your color coded wishlist. Figure out what works best for the people you are giving it to, and organize it based off of what you want, but also how they'll best understand it.

Marcy: My mother would almost always only look at the top five items on a wishlist, no matter how many notes or otherwise I added to it; she just went for whatever she saw first and then clicked down the line before calling it a day.

Elly: Mine barely even looked at lists except to look at the price!

Marcy: And, well, we aren't doing family therapy this week but I think this is pretty universal for people; most people will look at a wishlist or gift list and focus on something that indicates priority, and then price, and work within whatever boundary works best for them. If you know the person is on a budget or may have restrictions, consider even just giving them a list tailored to them.

Elly: Also, making a list isn't really greedy, selfish, or immature; it helps people quite a lot when they know what to get you, and can avoid a lot of unfortunate situations where you are throwing out something a person gave you that cost them real money!

Provide Pictures

Marcy: I think that when you're dealing with hobby items, whether they're gunpla, LEGOs or otherwise, providing a picture of some sort can be a huge help. Gunpla especially can be pretty confusing to people who don't really know what they're looking at, so putting a big string of numbers in front of their face doesn't really help. YOU might know the difference between the two kits, but the person shopping for you doesn't, so if you don't give them a visual cue to work off of, there might be some issues with what you end up getting.

Elly: I do think that this can be something that goes over people's heads; I'll be honest and say it has even gone over mine at times. If the item is specialized, just having the name of the item isn't always all you need. And while sometimes you can just send a link to the item off of a website to buy it from, it can still be helpful to have secondary confirmation that the thing you actually want looks like a specific thing, so that it doesn't cause confusion.

Marcy: I actually remember a time when I was buying a gift for someone and the item had two variants, which were unknown to even the person who asked for it, and the website made no mention of except during check out, meaning that I basically had to guess which of the two they actually wanted. I lucked out, thankfully.

Elly:If you have partners, family, friends, or otherwise that are asking or expecting to exchange gifts with you, just remember that telling them "I want the GQuuuuuuX kit" means basically nothing to them if they have no idea what that is. And for LEGO, I think this can also apply: just saying you want a LEGO set without a specific picture can create some confusion if there are multiple in the line, such as the One Piece or Transformers kits; even the botanicals can be kind of hard if people don't pay a lot of attention and just think "flower".

Marcy: Right. I think the second aspect here is that you're trying to fool proof the process, both in terms of bad communication on your part (IE, not telling them clearly about the specific thing you wanted by providing as much information as you can), and on their part (them not knowing what they're looking for and not being able to ask, while also just assuming that anything they get will be fine.

Consider the Source

Amazon used to be an extremely reliable, if questionably run, online store that could often solve much of the holiday shopping experience. The issue is that not only has Amazon gotten worse, but everything else around them has also gotten worse, which means that you can't rely on them as the be all end all solution to general shopping. If you haven't tried ordering anything from there lately, you might be surprised at how hard it can be to search a specific thing you're looking for without being inundated with "Sponsored" listings, knock-offs, and advertisements for unrelated things. Also, shipping speed, time, and availability has also become pretty variable and unreliable, which is a lot of words to say: you can't rely on Amazon being a solution to your gifting issues as much these days.

Instead, by looking into where people can get the things you want, you can also consider that while YOU may know how to order from those sites and be familiar with them, your loved ones are, again, not you, and thus are going to have a harder time knowing who to trust and how. Try to be clear in making your list, offer alternatives, and try to ensure that the things you're asking for can be found easily.

Elly: Yeah... especially with something like Gunpla, you'd possibly be surprised seeing the price variance on them. A kit that is normally 20 dollars might inexplicably be 50 dollars at Barnes and Noble, for example; finding them in Michaels for nearly twice their MSRP would turn off most experienced hobbyists, but people trying to do holiday shopping might not know that and end up wasting money.

Marcy: And, if you live in the US, you probably need to consider that asking for things that ship from outside of the US is a lot more complicated than it used to be, and that the people ordering things for you, either shipped to them or to you, are going to be less aware of and familiar with that issue than you might be if you're ordering overseas items for your hobby regularly. Try to not only give people options, but try to pick easy to shop with, or easy to use, websites, be informative, and try to provide as much information as you can to the person without being overwhelming.

Hopefully, taking some of this information to heart while working on your holiday wants and needs can help smooth things over with people, while also being something you can apply for yourself; when people start asking for or hinting about gifts, these tips also apply! Don't try to shop based on what you want or know, rely on what they're asking for instead, and you'll likely end up with a far more enjoyable overall holiday season!
