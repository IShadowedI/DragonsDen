[Kill Team] Tactical Focus: Raveners

September 10th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This series of Kill Team articles we take a deep dive into one of the available teams, analyzing each team's strengths and weaknesses, how to set up important plays, and ultimately what to consider to pilot the team to victory. Note that this article assumes you already have knowledge of the basic rules for each team, and will reference abilities and ploys from that team throughout. If you want to learn more about the rules for each team, you can find the most up to date version of the rules on the Warhammer Community Website here. In this article, we’re covering Raveners, the terror from below. 

Kill Team’s first and only bespoke Tyranid team, the Hyperadapted Raveners are the Hive Mind's answer to particularly stubborn defenders of would-be biomass. Genetically modified from the typical Ravener strain encountered by the empires and war bands of the galaxy, these subterranean super predators are equipped with chameleonic skin, steel-melting venom, and the sinister alien intelligence required to operate far from the Synapse creatures of a Tyranid vanguard. These biological adaptations are well reflected in their game rules, and Ravener players have many tools at their disposal to subsume their enemies into piles of biological refuse.

Basic Overview

In keeping with the fine tradition of Raveners menacing unsuspecting ground walkers, their primary rule involves creating a Tunnel that will creep across the Killzone as the game proceeds. Raveners can Burrow into and out of this Tunnel for 1AP, striking from below quickly or avoiding the wrath of enemy operatives by leaving the Killzone. Playing around your Tunnel is also how you get the most out of your Ploys, as almost all of them revolve around it in some capacity. Offensive ploys like Death From Below and Burrowing Strike can provide that extra kick you need to finish off tough enemies in a timely manner, while defensive ploys such as Whipchord Emergence and Writhe Out Of Sight can keep your limited retinue of operatives alive. 

Their other faction rule, Predatory Instincts, allows them to perform two Fight actions per activation, as well as counteract regardless of their order. Raveners can change their order before they counteract, or swap their order as a counteraction. This allows you to change your Engage order to a Conceal, or vice versa, and with clever positioning you can incapacitate enemy operatives on counteractions by swapping to Conceal before Fighting, preventing your enemy from returning fire without further exposing themselves. 

Finally, during their counteraction, Raveners can perform a free Burrow action, as long as they aren’t performing Mission Actions during that counteraction. This can be done before or after their normal action, and in addition to the order swap, allowing you some very effective ways to eliminate the enemy without risking Shoot actions in return. The most important thing to remember here is you cannot do Mission Actions during a counteraction in which you Burrow, so planning ahead is crucial depending on your Crit Op. Missions like Transmission or Loot can leave you at a VP deficit if you aren’t considerate of your limited action economy. 

Team Strengths and Weaknesses

In spite of recent nerfs that have reigned the team’s dominance in a bit, Raveners are still a monstrous menace on the tabletop. Emphasizing hit and run tactics, they are at their best when able to stage multiple threats with both operatives in the Tunnel and the ones above. Unlike almost every other team, Raveners are able to exude an aura of threat around the Tunnel that opponents must be wary of lest their operatives feed the Hive (and your Kill Op.) However, the 5+ save and reduced wounds means the team is still vulnerable to being shot off the board if it overextends, and having a mere five operatives means every Ravener you give up is a point for your opponent. Choosing when to engage your enemy is absolutely paramount to avoid unnecessary chip damage and early losses. 

Your five specialist operatives are all important and superior to the generic Warrior operative, so there is never a reason not to take them. Your leader, the Ravener Prime, provides the Synaptic Link Strategic Gambit, potentially giving you big Command Point bonuses in the first few Turning Points. Besides having the Ravener equivalent of a Chainsword (5A 3+ ⅘) with the Rending weapon rule, it also lowers the APL of nearby enemy operatives contesting objective markers, allowing you snatch markers away from your opponents on a crucial objective. Finally, the Prime also turns off your opponents rerolls within 3”, meaning anyone the Prime fights, or anyone foolish enough to Charge or get close enough Shoot it on conceal, is entirely at the whim of fate for any rolls that they make. 

A Ravener Prime rears up before striking at bloodthirsty heretics. Credit: Laura Marshall

Your only ranged piece, the Venomspitter, is a real game changer. While many players prefer utilizing it as a defensive piece, which it does excel at, the Venomspitter can also be used as a mobile weapons platform. Lacking the Heavy keyword on its dangerous weapon profile means the Venomspitter can easily Burrow into the Killzone, Reposition, and take a key Shoot at a specific operative with the Focused profile, or a clustered enemy group with the Blast profile. If an opponent moves too close to the tunnel, you can Burrow, Distend Dorsal Sac, and take an even more devastating Shoot at would be aggressors. Don’t be afraid to use it in a Fight either, as it still maintains the Ravener Chainsword profile every other operative has. 

Your remaining three operatives all have their use cases as well. The Tremorscythe stays in the Tunnel, deterring anybody from approaching your ever expanding territory lest their activation be interrupted. When it comes time for it to emerge, the ability to Charge on Conceal can save you headaches from being shot later on. Keep in mind this operative doesn’t have to Fight when it interrupts; a well-placed Krak grenade can be more beneficial at times. The Felltalon’s awesome melee profile, boasting Lethal 5+, Shock, and Poison, makes short work of even the toughest combatants, and it has the ability to soften them up with his Toxic Lunge, dealing wounds directly. A 6” Silent Poison shooting weapon gives it some ranged utility during your counteractions as well. Finally, the Wrecker boasts a 4+ save, reduces incoming damage, and the Crush rule on his melee weapon allows for game swinging damage spikes if you roll well. Don’t forget this operative’s 3” Silent ranged tail weapon, which all the Raveners come equipped with, also has the Piercing 1 rule. 

It’s not all sunshine and biomass here though. As mentioned, the 5+ Save most of your operatives have leave them very vulnerable to Shoot actions. With the change to their Wounds stat, the Ravener dreads the melta gun even more than before. Previously, even four hits was survivable with the single saving throw afforded by the melta, but this is no longer the case except on your Ravener Prime. Four hits guarantees death for these beasts, so be mindful of where your opponent is staging their melta Gunner. If you have an opportunity to take it out, do so, but baiting the melta into moving up and taking a shot in a place where you can eliminate him afterward can be a valid, if risky, tactic. While the melta is certainly the deadliest threat to your operatives, all shooting is potentially lethal to you, so be careful which shots you allow your opponent to take. 

Outside of combat, Raveners struggle heavily with their action economy. With just 15 AP across your five activations, and Burrow costing an AP reducing this action economy even further, you may suffer from indecision regarding where to commit your operatives. You’ll find certain Crit Ops, like Loot or Data, to be difficult to play, as you may have to commit an entire operative to playing the Crit Op, and potentially being dead in the water. Every action on every activation matters. Whether or not you’re able to manage these decisions effectively will determine a huge portion of your success.  

Raveners are at their best around the Tunnel, and even more vulnerable away from it. Overextending early to take suboptimal fights can spell doom for you if not done in a calculated manner. If you do have to move far from the Tunnel, try and remember the 7” Rule. The Tunnel moves 5”, and Writhe Out Of Sight is usable on an operative within 2”. Any Ravener within 7” of the previous Tunnel marker is potentially saveable with this combo. A Ravener alive at one or two wounds is a Ravener that isn’t contributing to your opponent's Kill Op score. 

Standout Tactics

If the Ravener stat line is the hammer, the faction rules and Firefight Ploys are the scalpel. With several sources of mortal damage, ways to escape combat or reposition for a more optimal Fight, and the ability to Burrow directly into control range, Raveners are perfectly poised for an ambush predator style of gameplay.

Let’s start with the Tunnel and Burrow. While you should have a general idea of where your tunnel wants to go, always stay flexible with it and be ready to adjust the plan as necessary. Making a pivot in Turning Point 3 or 4 to keep a piece alive can be more important than strictly adhering to whatever plan you might have had for the Tunnel, especially if you lose the initiative roll. Writhe Out Of Sight is easily your most important survival tool and can get you out of many difficult situations. On the opposite side, winning the initiative can let you position your tunnel into an opportunistic angle, where you can possibly Burrow into control range of two or more enemy operatives, hitting them all with the mortal damage from the Burrowing Strike Firefight Ploy, before performing two Fight actions and incapacitating them both. In these situations, be sure to decide which operative is the greater threat in a Fight, and whether or not you want to deal with the weaker one first to deny combat assistance. A clever Charge can also leave you poised to Burrow away after the Fight. Keep in mind you can Burrow both into and out of control range, so if a Fight doesn’t go your way, you have a way to escape.

Another deadly tool in your arsenal is the Slithering Evasion Firefight Ploy. This lets you Fall Back for a single AP, but more importantly, it can let you Charge out of control range. If your opponent overextends an operative in a Turning Point, this can allow you to stage multiple models off your opponents. When you’re ready, one operative can Charge away into two others, incapacitating them while the other stays on the original enemy model, ready to incapacitate him before Charging elsewhere. Keep an eye out for these emergent opportunities as seizing them will be critical.

The Venomspitter can be a key piece in removing enemy models On Guard in Close Quarters Killzones. This model's head tends to overhang its base, meaning it can draw line of sight from some weird angles. Since models On Guard tend to only see in a specific direction, while simultaneously exposing parts of their own model, the Venomspitter can very easily set up and deal with these pesky operatives in a way that doesn’t allow the enemy to return fire.

I see you; you don't see me.

A final nasty trick in your repertoire should be an understanding of how to make counteractions work in your favor. Raveners are currently one of three teams that can perform multiple actions on counteractions, performing a free Burrow and an additional action. Normally during an activation, operatives are limited to performing any given action a single time, but counteractions don’t have this action limit imposed on them. While the other two teams that can do this, Deathwatch and Angels of Death, both specify that during a counteraction their actions must be different, Raveners do not have this stipulation. What this means is an operative can Burrow for free, and then Burrow for 1AP, performing the Burrowing Strike Firefight Ploy between them. While a niche use case, dealing 2-4 mortal wounds to enemy operative or operatives without risking anything in return in terms of damage is an excellent trick to have in your back pocket should the opportunity arise, whether you use it to finish off an enemy or merely weaken one for the future.

Your equipment selection is going to be more flexible than most other teams, as Raveners don’t have much in the way of mandatory considerations. That said, Metamorphic Flesh is something you want to take almost every game as the healing it provides is indispensable. Chromatospore Camouflage is something to consider, but always clarify with your opponent how much access to Saturate they have. If the enemy has a lot of weapons with the rule, it won’t be doing anything for you. Acid Blood is something to consider in the Elite match up, as the extra mortal damage can make or break a fight for you (just remember that you have it, as it can be easy to forget about.) Heightened Senses is probably something you can leave behind, but if you’re struggling to fill out a fourth slot, it can give you an edge in a key initiative roll. Light Barricades are unnecessary thanks to the Tunnel Lurkers strategy ploy providing cover on your Tunnel. Krak Grenades are something to consider in the Elite match up as well, giving your ranged attacks a bit of extra spice.

Tac Ops and Crit Ops

Raveners archetypes of Seek & Destroy and Infiltration presently occupy a weird position in the game. In the Seek & Destroy category, Route involves a risky overextension in Turning Point 2 if you want to maximize your score. Sweep and Clear has the same problem as Route, while further straining your action economy with extraneous mission actions. On the Infiltration side of things, Track Enemy involves a lot of assumption your opponent will be giving valid targets for your operatives early, and that you yourself won’t be for them, which isn’t really what the team wants to be doing. Plant Devices has the same issues as Sweep and Clear, bogging your team down with unnecessary mission actions and risking exposure. Steal Intelligence also requires early exposure, and performing wasteful Pick Up Marker actions when you could be making Fights or playing Crit Op.

By far the best choice for tac op, even with recent changes, remains Dominate. There are no extra actions required, it rewards you doing what you want to be doing anyway (slaughtering your opponents operatives), but perhaps most importantly, it isn’t scoreable until Turning Point 3, which as we’ll discuss is when the Raveners game should truly be starting. Keep in mind you can still Burrow while having Dominate tokens, you just won’t be able to collect them. This can be useful Turning Point 3 to keep an operative alive until Turning Point 4, when it comes time to collect them. 

For Crit Ops, Raveners best ones are things that don’t require them to be wasting valuable AP. Things like Loot and Download where you can set up to get your point early, or force your opponent to do so, expending an operative in the process, are going to be some of their easier missions to play. Transmission, Stake Claim, Reboot, and Data can force you to leave an operative behind if you don’t wish to fall too behind on VP. A defensive tunnel can come in handy here in case operatives need to do rapid shifting around. Orb and Energy Cells are some of the hardest missions for the team to play. You can pick up an Energy Cell early in the game, but the operative who does so now needs to choose between staying safe the remainder of the game, or going towards the enemy and risking bringing their marker towards them. In this case, and especially for Orb, an offensive tunnel where your opponent is forced to play around their own objectives can be very handy. 

Matchup Considerations

With their high movement, wounds, and chainsword equivalents, Raveners enjoy a healthy range of favorable match ups. Most 7W or 8W teams are going to be trying to stay a reasonable distance from your operatives and your Tunnel to avoid being made mincemeat out of, but even this can only delay things for so long. Teams like Death Korp, Brood Brothers, Aquilons, and Spectre Squad are all going to try using their hard hitting melta and general ranged shenanigans to whittle you down. Patience will pay off in these ranged match ups, as eventually these teams have to extend to play the game they want to play. When they do, Raveners get to play the game they want to play.

Elf teams (Corsairs, Blades of Khaine, Hand of the Archon, and Mandrakes) are some of the best match ups you might encounter. With their 8W making them ripe for the picking, Elf teams will struggle heavily into Raveners if you’re playing a careful game. Mandrakes in particular deserve a special shout out, as they are currently the only team in the game that actually improves Raveners Save Stat, as Soul Strike is not an optional rule at the time of this writing. That doesn’t mean you can allow these teams to shoot you at will though. The plan remains the same into these teams, the execution (of your enemy) just becomes a bit easier in Fights.

Deathwatch can present a unique problem. The Deathwatch Gunner Veteran operative is the bane of Raveners, and with Suffer Not The Alien and applying Devastating Wounds to the weapon, the Gunner can easily incapacitate two operatives before you’re able to put him down. Be cautious of this operative, and the team in general. The reduction to 18W means power weapons can potentially kill your operatives with three critical successes before you’re able to kill them. You must be especially patient with Deathwatch, and only make trades when they are favorable to you.

Sanctifiers can also present a problem to the team. While ostensibly a 7W team, there are a few operatives with 8W, and more importantly, their Ministorum Sermon faction rule can make your scything talons feel a bit anemic. Furthermore, many of their weapons have Saturate on them, meaning your Chromatospore Camouflage equipment is not a reliable option here. To top it all off, they also have access to a melta gun themselves. Be extremely cautious in this match up. Try to use the Venomspitter, Felltalon, and Burrowing Strike to deal mortal wounds to ensure you’re killing them in one or two hits. No single operative on this team is going to kill a Ravener in a single fight, but losing out on a critical hit and taking two hits instead of one multiple times can cause you to take more chip damage than is sustainable for you long term.

Piloting the Team to Victory

The Tunnel being such a key part of the team makes us ask the question: just where does it go? Generally speaking, the Tunnel can be used in an offensive or defensive fashion. A defensive Tunnel wants to thread through your home objective marker before making its way towards the center. An offensive Tunnel wants to beeline for the center marker before making its way towards your opponents home objective. What you decide is going to depend on your opponent, the Crit Op, and the Killzone. If your opponent is playing an aggressive team, staging an offensive Tunnel will allow you to punch them much harder than they punch you, as well providing egress when your operatives need to retreat. By the same token, a cagey or defensive team allows you to play a defensive Tunnel yourself, and punish any enemy operative who wanders away from the relative safety of your opponents back line.

A critical thing to your success here is using the right piece for the job. Your Ravener Prime might want to occupy the center point to seize it away from your opponent with its APL manipulation. Sometimes the Felltalon needs to go in to wither down a particularly stubborn enemy operative, like the Deathwatch Demolisher who reduces incoming damage. These kinds of operatives are especially susceptible to both the mortal wounds Toxic Lunge inflicts, as well as the Poison it leaves behind.

Raveners real game begins on Turning Point 3. By this point, the Tunnel should be either already on or reaching the center objective, and enemy operatives will be in position to be charged directly out of the Tunnel. Positioning your third Tunnel marker to charge multiple operatives will be a key factor in your success, and you should be aiming to take away as many activations from your opponent as is possible. Fewer activations means fewer opportunities for something to go wrong for your own operatives, as well as giving your opponent less time and actions to play their own game. Raveners are performing a balancing act with this: the more your total APL can outweigh your opponents total APL, and the faster this can be tipped in your favor, the better.

A Brood Brother realizes just how expendable he is to the Star Gods. Credit: Laura Marshall

Final Thoughts

A team that requires more patience than most, Raveners should always be walking the line between caution and aggression. You’ve likely read the word “opportunity” a lot in this article, and while spotting these things is a key part of playing Kill Team in general, it is even more important with this ambush team. Embrace the Ravener mindset, and think like a predator, always waiting for your opponent to make that mistake that will cost them the game. Strike too soon, your operatives will be lost to volleys of shooting attacks. Wait too long, your opponent will run away on VP before you have a chance. Timing is absolutely key, and knowing when to strike will be the difference between your Tunnel collapsing on itself, or dragging your opponent into the sandy depths.
