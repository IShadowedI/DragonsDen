Tabletop Battles Reviews: Age of Sigmar Khazalite Holds Battletome

October 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer
We’d like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of this Battletome. We have also been provided with a provisional Battle Profile list for the army to use when writing the review, but please note that any points referenced in this review are subject to change until they are published on Warhammer Community.

For a few years now now, rumors have percolated about what exactly was going to be done with Fyreslayers. It’s an army with a very limited model range and seemingly very little enthusiasm to expand it. Much hay has been been made if they would be folded into Kharadron Overlords, or just removed entirely. Turns out there was a secret third option: A return of Dwarven Mountain Holds from Warhammer Fantasy, rolling the Fyreslayers into this larger force that helps expand their theming and grant a larger variety of playstyles.

The lack of a “traditional fantasy dwarf” has been felt pretty strongly in Age of Sigmar so this seems like a very exciting addition to the game. We got our hands on the new Battle Box with the Battletome included, so how do they stack up on the table? We’ve had a dozen or so test games under our belt and are here to report our findings.

Khazalite Holds Clanshields - Credit Mildnorman

Battle Traits

For Khazalite Holds the first thing that the army deals with are Grudge marks, each unit in the army gains a bonus effect for having Grudge marks on them. To gain them there are five ways:

  1. The first one is if your general is slain, you will hand out 1 Grudge Mark to up to 3 units.
  2. If a Grimmdron (aka Fyreslayers) unit charged this turn, did not destroy an enemy unit, and was not destroyed.
  3. If any Khazalite Hold units were slain that turn, hand out 1 Grudge Mark to a unit.
  4. If any damage points were allocated to your general, give one unit that is not your General a Grudge Mark.
  5. If any Krommdrom units are contesting an objective that is being contested by enemy units. Give a Grudge Mark

Next if an opponent gains control of an objective in your territory you can hand out one mark to a unit. Also, if a unit with a Grudge Mark retreats they lose a Grudge Mark. In your Hero Phase you can also have a unit count as having an additional Grudge Mark.

With some exceptions, Grudge Marks are binary, the unit only cares if you have them or you don’t. This kind of makes them read like a second pass at Demonic Power Points from Helsmiths, which isn’t an unwarranted comparison. Demonic Power Points required multiple to get anything out of them (often 3) and generated too slowly. This requires more interactivity to generate marks, but are more impactful out of the gate. It’s basically an army about deciding who needs to be buffed first and who can wait.

On top of the Grudge Marks, the army also has five Ancestral Vows they can activate once per game in any Hero phase. You can only activate one per round, and they last for the round, so it’s basically the successor to the Ur-Gold Runes from Fyreslayers.

  1. You can select an Infantry Hero and Infantry unit who are not in combat and wholly within 6” of the same piece of terrain, and remove them from the battlefield. Set them up wholly within 6” of a terrain feature outside 9” of enemy units.
  2. Pick 3 units and if they have not charged this battle round, reduce rend from combat attacks by 1.
  3. Pick 3 units and add 1 to the wound rolls of combat attacks made by the unit. If they are Grimmdron Infantry they gain First Strike when they charge, as well.
  4. For the rest of the Battle round friendly units have +1 to Casting and Chanting Rolls. While they are Wholly 12” of a Khazalite Wizard or Priest they gain a 4+ ward against spell, prayer, and abilities of manifestations.
  5. Finally, 3 Duardin Khazalite Hold units in combat roll a D3, then heal/return models equal to the roll and do that much damage to an enemy they are in combat with. If the unit is Valayan use a value of 3 instead of D3.

And lastly while your units are missing at least half of the models in the unit, add 1 to the attack characteristics of melee weapons used by the unit. Nice little perk for making sure your units are never truly out of the fight.

Battle Formations

War Callers subtracts 3 from the control scores of enemy units while they are in combat with friendly Khazalite units. This battle formation is great to have your army be able to take control of an objective that your opponent is trying to hold. With the General’s Handbook moving in a direction of Objective Control being the focus, having the ability to flip control is great.

Hearthsworn Defenders: Subtract one from hit rolls for shooting attacks that target friendly Khazalite Holds while they are wholly in friendly territory or while every model in the unit is contesting an objective you control. The ability to have your entire army be able to be minus one to hit in shooting is amazing. Khazalite Holds are an army that wants to fight in the middle and hold the objectives. Being able to be a little more survivable while your army treks up to the objectives is the way to go in my testing.

Unyielding Anvil: While an enemy unit is in combat with a friendly Khazalite unit, subtract 1” from pile in moves to a minimum of 1”. This battle formation is solid, the amount of times that the reduced pile in move actually matters in a game is non-zero, however either one of the previous battle formations I believe are better choices.

Wayfarer Nomads: When you declare a Run or Charge ability for a Khazalite unit and they are more that 6” away from all other friendly units, you can reroll the dice that determine how far it can move. This is probably the weakest of the bunch regarding the battle formations. As an army that has a decent amount of auras that you want to stay within. Forcing your army to social distance weakens the strength of it.

Heroic Traits

Long of Beard or Braid: Each time a visible friendly Khazalite unit wholly within 12” of this unit makes a Rally roll, you may make 3 additional rally rolls of d6. This is a solid choice, being able to rally your units on 9d6 is always good. Especially when a lot of your units are single wound models you can bring back huge chunks of a unit, if not the entire thing. Next there is Sworn to Vengeance which if the bearer’s non-Companion weapons allocates any damage points and the enemy unit was destroyed, you gain a vengeance token, to a maximum of 3. Until your next turn add 1 to the attack characteristics of non-Companion weapons of this unit. Out of the 3 options for heroic traits I think this is the weakest one. It is super situational and the other 2 traits are better options.

Finally Innate Resonance gives a non-priest Wizard(1). If it already has Wizard(1), it gains Wizard(2). In addition enemy manifestations strike last while they are within 12” of this unit. I think this is the one to take, being able to have a second wizard or even having your wizard be a 2 cast is great. With both spell lores being solid, the ability to cast another spell without having to take another model is great.

Artefacts of Power

Hearthflares: Pick an objective within 12” of this unit. Until the start of your next turn add 5 to the control scores of friendly units while they are contesting that objective. This to me is by far the best Artefact for the army. This allows you to overtake objectives very easily and it does a great job of allowing you to keep an objective flipped in your favor to deny your opponent from scoring what they would need to score the 10 points on primary.

Flamescale Rune Cloak subtracts 1 from hit rolls for attacks that target this unit if it has not charged this turn. This one is just fine, I just don’t think the army has a problem with survivability.

Finally, for the Deep-Earth Runic Weapon you select one of the bearer’s non-Companion weapons to be the runic weapon and add one to the rend characteristic. Then you can either get +1 to attack or take a gamble on getting +1 attack and damage on a 5+. Probably not worth it unless you’re a gambler, but fun if you are.

Spell Lore

This book has two spell lores which both are solid and a viable option. First we will cover the Resonance of the Runes. Up first is the Rune of Might casting value of a 6, until the start of your next turn add 1 rend to melee weapons used by friend Khazalite Holds who are within 3” of the caster. After that is Rune of Sanctuary, this spell on a casting value of 6 makes it so your army isn’t visible outside of 12” of enemy units. Nothing flashy in this spell, just a solid tool to allow you to move up the board without fear of being shot. The last spell in this lore goes off on a 6, and it is the Rune of Warding This creates an aura around the caster of 12” of enemy units can’t score critical hits. This is probably the strongest spell in this lore.

The next lore is the Reverberations of the Runes. This spell lore I think is a little weaker than the first one but still worth consideration. The Rune of Dismay, this spell subtracts 1 from the attack characteristics of melee weapons used by enemy units within 6” of the caster. The bubble that this emits is a little small to get a great use out of it, but it is really useful. Next up, Rune of Courage this spell emits an aura of reroll charges within 12” of the caster. This spell is useful, but you can just take the free manifestation of Aetherwrought and get this effect with the Cogs. The final spell in this lore goes off on an 8, and the Rune of the Furnace Will do d3 mortal wounds to all visible enemy units within 12” of the caster.

Prayer Lore

The prayer lore of Ancestor God Recitations is a fine one. However, there are no new models that are priests in the book, so you have to rely on the current crop of Fyreslayer Priests. First up is the Recitation to Valaya, this prayer goes off on a a 4+ and gives a unit -1 to be wounded which is great and the empowered version on a 8+ selects two units. After that we have the Recitation to Gazul which on a 4+ prevents manifestations from being set up within 12” of the caster. Which is nifty but that’s it. Just nifty. The final Prayer is a Recitation to Grimnir which picks an enemy unit within 18” on a 4+ everytime they do a Core ability they take 1 Mortal Wound. If the roll was a 10+ they take D3 instead. 

Endless Spells/Terrain

With the release of this book the army did not gain any terrain or manifestations. I would recommend using either Aetherwrought or Forbidden Power, as those are some of the more generally useful ones.

It is worth noting that the Fyreslayer Terrain and manifestations are still legal until they move to legends in the next year. Rules as written though, they don’t work because they proc off of the “Fyreslayers” keyword, which the book does not use anymore. So either they’ll change the warscrolls to word replace Fyreslayers with “Grimmdron” or just kill them entirely. If they do make them usable, though, the terrain is free so maybe worth it if you bring a priest.

Since Fyreslayers have a bit of weird synergy with each other, we’ll talk about them separately.

Khazalite Holds Hjulda Drakkblud - credit Bair

Heroes

High Kyng Valankrom, the named big boat does so much stuff. To start, this boat is a beater in combat with 6 attacks at rend 2 damage 3 and Crit (Mortals) and then 6 more attacks at rend 1 damage 2. As expected from a big centerpiece model it also comes with a ton of buffs Along with that it grants +2” move to Khazalite Holds wholly within 12” of it. Along with in your hero phase you select one of 3 abilities:
  • Give retreat and charge
  • Make an enemy unit maximum 1 objective control
  • Give your Wizards and Priests +1 to chant, casting, and banishment rolls

It also hands out a grudge mark and gives units Anti-charge (+1 rend). At the start of each battle round it can also pick an enemy hero within 18” and start accusing it of things. If the enemy hero accepts the accusations, your army gains +1 to hit against it. If they deny the accusations, they cannot use any commands until your next turn.

Thrynn on War Throne the unnamed boat variant is still a great combat piece at 12 attacks at rend 1 damage 2. With 6 of them being Crit (Mortals). When this unit charges it does a 3+ that many Mortal wounds to an enemy within 1” of it. Also When this unit moves it can select an Kromdon Infantry unit wholly within 6” and not in combat, remove it from the battlefield and set it back up wholly 6” of it when it finishes the move. Abilities of the boat are sort of  similar to the named one. They both have an ability that you choose one of three: it can grant +2” to move, it can select a friendly unit wholly within 12” and turn off weapon abilities other than Companion for attacks that target it, or it can pick an enemy hero and remove the ability of Guarded Hero from it. Just like the named boat it hands out anti charge +1 rend to units equal to Grudge Marks. However it also has a Charge phase ability to do D6 mortals on a 3+, which can be nice if you roll high. For the most part you probably want to take the named variant, as the effects are just generally better.

Thrynn is an unnamed hero who chain fights with another non-hero infantry Kromdon unit within their combat range. That infantry unit gains +1 to hit rolls. In addition this unit gets +1 attack for each friendly unit wholly within 6” of it to a maximum of 3.

Hjulda Drakkblud picks an enemy to be grudged during deployment and while the enemy unit is within 12” of them, she emits an aura of reroll charges wholly within 12”. If there is no Grudged enemy unit on the battlefield you add 3 to the Control scores of friendly Khazalite Hold units while they are wholly 12” of her. The great part of this ability is you can select a unit in reserves, so you can get the control buff even if its not dead. While she is in combat she also gives a non-Hero infantry unit +1 to hit if they are within her combat range. The last ability she has is a Hero Phase ability where she selects herself and 2 other non-hero infantry and on a 3+ ignore the first wound of each phase. If she has a Grudge Mark, no roll required.

Runewright is the generic foot wizard has +1 to unbind and banishment rolls while it has a Grudge Mark on it. In addition to that, after you successfully cast a spell you can attempt to extend the range of it by 3” by rolling equal to or greater than the cast value again. You can repeat this as often as you want, but if you fail a casting roll, the entire spell fizzles. This is done before your opponent attempts an unbind, so it might be worth if if you either aren’t going to be able to cast the spell without extending the range anyway, or if your roll technically passed the casting value but is low enough that it would be too easy to unbind.

Gotrek, the little murder dwarf is back. With 8 attacks (and +2 attack for each Hero or Monster he’s in combat with) on 2+/2+ rend 2 3dmg Crit(Mortal) he is adequately designed to be a hammer in the army. With being so angry that he now stands up on a 3+ the first time he dies, he is a little more survivable than before. He lost his fight twice, but in exchange can now be teleported. The one downside about Gotrek is that he does not benefit from Grudge Markers or Ancestral Vows (He doesn’t believe in such nonsense). That said he’s pretty resilient and killy on his own, so he might still be worth it.

Infantry

Your basic troop is the Clanshields, a 1 wound 10 man unit with a 4” move, 3+ save, and 2 attacks. Their attacks are 3s/4s(3+ with a grudge token), with anti-infantry. The biggest perk to these frontline units is the fact that if all of them are contesting an objective you control they reduce rend for combat attacks that target them by 1. Pair that with the ancestral vow that reduces rend if you don't charge, you have a seriously survivable unit.

The gunline unit has two different builds: first up is the Clangunners with Shotcannons. This unit has 12” range with 2 shots 3s/3s crit(auto-wound). After they shoot, they can select an enemy unit that had any damage allocated to it and on a 4+ (3+ If they don’t have Fly), subtract 1 from charge dice Alternatively they can be built as Clangunner with Rifles. These are 2 shots at 18” range on 3s/4s Rend 1 Damage 1. After they allocate damage on a 4+ you can halve the units move. You add 1 to the roll if they are a calvary unit, or add 2 if they are a monster. Of these 2 units I prefer the the Shotcannons since these work in either turn, being able to potentially give 2 units one less charge die is worth the 6” less range in my opinion.

The next units we have are the Valayan Guardians, which come with two different loadouts. First of them is the Valayan Guardians w/ Runic Halberds these are a unit of 5 ladies on a 4+ save and a 4+ ward. 2 attacks 3s/4s 2 rend 2 damage. These are the more combat focused version of the kit. Their ability allows them to charge as if they have fly. They have to end within ½ “ of an enemy model. If they pass over any models they inflict D3 mortal wounds to them. If they have a grudge token you can re-roll the charge.

On the defensive side of the kit. Valayan Guardians w/ Rune Axes and Shields. These emit a 6” aura of a 5+ ward to Infantry and non-Gholem War-machines while they have a grudge token. Also if they are in combat with an enemy unit that charged they get to roll a die for each enemy model in combat with them and on a 4+ inflict 1 mortal wound. This is a great unit to support the unit of Clanshields that is guarding your frontline. Them being a 3+ save and keeping the 4+ ward save that their aggressive counterpart has is a solid tech piece to hold those objectives. Having 3 attacks each 3s/4s rend 1, 1 damage. What they lack in combat profiles they make up for in the ability to do some serious mortal wound output.

War Machines

In keeping what you’d expect from a Duardin army, there are your fair share of Warmachines here. Gholems of Gazul are 3+ save and units of 3 with 5 wounds each. They are a serious melee threat at 3 attacks 4s/2s rend 2 3 damage and they reduce Wards by 1 for their swings. If that wasn’t enough, every combat phase they trigger an ability to try and crush the armor of an enemy unit in combat with them. You roll a die, if you roll equal to or higher than their save the enemy unit is -1 save for the rest of the turn. Before you roll, your opponent can choose to sidestep the swing, giving you -1 to the roll but they also get -1 to hit. This unit is great and the ability on their scroll is fun and flavorful.

Gholems of Valaya, the female Gholems, do 1 less damage than their male counter-part but they gain 1 extra attack each, so it balances out the profiles. These Gholems will hand out a Strike-Last on an enemy unit that charges them on a 3+ for the rest of the turn. In addition to that, when this unit dies it will hand out 1 grudge token to every friendly non-Gholem Valayan visible unit on the battlefield. I think these are a solid choice especially when you need some of the Valayan Guardians to be able to hand out a ward save. However, I don’t think there are enough Valayan keyworded units in the book to justify taking these. Oathkeeper Cannon having 3 shots at 24” that inflict D3+2 damage at rend 2, can put a serious dent into a unit. If they have a grudge token the shots gain Crit(2 hits). However the big thing that this cannon does, is in your shooting phase you select an enemy unit that had any damage allocated to it by it’s shooting attacks. On a 3+ you can select one of three options. You can either: 
  • Give Strike Last to the unit,
  • Reduce their rend by 1, or
  • Give your army the ability to score crits against the unit on 5+ while in melee for the turn.

Now, Stonebreaker Mortar this mortar shoots at 30” with 5 shots, however it’s only rend 1 and damage 2. But just like their cannon counterpart the mortar also gains Crit(2 hits) with a Grudge token and it has a select one ability: 

  • Reduce the control score for an enemy unit that took damage from it to 1.
  • Prevent the unit from being able to use Run abilities until your next turn. 
  • Select each enemy unit that is within 3” of the target and roll a bad D3 mortal wounds on them. 

The first two are solid abilities, and can deny your opponent an objective or the ability to complete battle tactics. These are a solid pick for any list, having the utility of their abilities from that far of a range puts a lot of pressure on your opponent on where they can go.

Khazalite Holds Gholems of Gazul - credit Bair

Fyreslayers

Since Fyreslayers (or Grimmdron as the book calls them now) are kind of a weird faction within the army, with their own synergies and support for each other, we decided to cover them separately.

First, the token new foot hero, Ormyr and Ashtongue. This model is a pretty punchy combat piece for the army. If they have a Grudge Mark, they can add +1 to companion attacks for units within 12” (so basically the Big Boats and Magmadroths) and it can give either a Magmadroth’s Mount attacks +1 to hit or a Flameseekers unit ignore negatives to hit, wound and control. A final cute trick is he can make himself totally hidden from all units outside of 6” on a 3+, but its both friend and foe so you may not want to use this every turn. He’s fine, but you really need to be running a Magmadroth heavy list to get much out of him.

Many of the units are quite similar (in some cases, worse) so assume they’re the same unless noted otherwise. Runefather on Magmadroth was tweaked slightly to give +1 attacks to non-Fyreslayers infantry if it has a grudge marker, while the Runefather foot version gets a 4+ Ward from its royal Retinue if it has a grudge marker (sadly, it lost the bonus to control it used to have).

As for his son, the Runeson on Magmadroth lost his nitpicky monster centric abilities and got a new suite of abilities. He can transport a unit of Fyreslayer infantry like the Kharadron boats can, which honestly is pretty welcome for the 4” moving infantry. He also gets a horde clearer in the charge phase, rolling dice equal to the enemy unit size, and dealing mortals on a 5+. The foot version of the Runeson got a slight buff to its charge bonus, giving +1 to any Khazalite infantry or +2 if it has a grudge mark. He also got a twist on the chain fighting, where he doesn’t necessarily need to swing first, he can swing after another infantry unit instead and get the +1 to hit. It’s a lot less clunky in certain situations, and can help if you’re carrying a heroic trait or artefact that you’d rather he get the +1 to hit for.

Next is the Priests, the thing you’re liable to take even if you’re not all that interested in Fyreslayers as a whole, since they’re your only priests. The Auric Runesmiter on Magmadroth is basically the same, with a slight update to let it give its +1 to wound to any Khazalite unit if it’s got a grudge marker. The foot Runesmiter probably took one of the bigger hits, since it lost its deep strike ability. It can give +2” to move to a Fyreslayers infantry unit, and gets +1 to cast with a grudge marker. It’s fine, and since its your only priest its probably the one you’ll take anyway, otherwise you’re taking nothing.

Rounding things off with the miscellaneous Foot Heroes who survived, the Auric Flamekeeper got a bit of an upgrade on his pretty bad ability. It hands out the same buffs as before if you roll less than the number of destroyed units, but it does include all Khazalite units now and a 1 always works, so you might be able to get it on turn 1, even. It also gets -2 to the roll if it has a grudge marker, so it’s way more consistent. Grimnir’s Resolve in the Masterflame Rune is now reroll charge rolls instead of +1, which is a much nicer buff. He also hands out a grudge marker to every Fyreslayer within 6” if he dies, so your opponent has to choose between dealing with the Masterflame Rune or giving all your Fyreslayers a grudge token. Grimhold Exile got a once per battle teleport, Strike First if he’s outside of 6” of all other friendly units and a free D6” movement in the movement phase, even into combat. It’s a cute trick, but his damage profile is still just kinda mediocre which makes him difficult to recommend.

Onto the Infantry, Vulkyn Flameseekers got more anti-Monster tools, which might be useful in this monster heavy season. Drothmasters is now a flat 2 instead of D3, but with a grudge mark they can cause -2 to attacks on Monsters and shut off Rampages. Auric Hearthuard lost their ability to reduce movement characteristics, which will probably ruin them being taken, even if their new ability to cause -1 to hit isn’t bad. Finally, Hearthguard Berzerkers were compressed into one warscroll, mostly to their detriment since it means they didn’t get to keep Crit (Mortals), like the Grimhold Exile it gets a free D6” move in the movement phase, even right into combat. This could make them an interesting tech piece up front, to lock down enemy units before they can charge into you

We feel that, mostly, the Fyreslayers in the book got worse. They’re locked in a bit of a closed environment that makes them struggle to play well with others (the Runesmiter aside) and many lost the things that made them top the charts this past season. If you want to use Fyreslayers, you kinda have to lean heavy on them to get much out of their synergies.

How They’ll Play

When reading through this book, it seems like you want to create a force that sort of pushes up the board in a “death-ball” style. You have a lot of auras that your army wants to stay within. You’re going to push up the board and with all the Objective control manipulation that you can generate in the army. You will overtake the primary and then be survivable enough to prevent your opponent from reclaiming them. Each of these units when played has the potential to give some serious output and be able to out-grind most armies over the course of the game for a victory.

Overall this army has a lot of interesting gameplay, but I think that it’s going to focus more on a defensive style by controlling the primary. With the War Callers subfaction, enemy units face -3 OC when they are in combat with you. Combine this with the Hearthflares artefact that gives your unit +5 OC you have a lot of ways of controlling the objectives. On top of that you bring a cannon with you, now you can give a strike last, you have the option of the Gholems that can give a -1 to save and make your opponent -1 to hit, shock gunners will give -1 charge die. After putting the clan shields on objectives they become a seriously tough front line unit to deal with. After you place some debuffs on your enemy’s army, you really have a force that is going to be able to control the battlefield.

Armies of Renown

You get a whopping three Armies of Renown, and seemingly learning their lesson from Helsmiths, they did retain some way to get Grudge Tokens as part of their Battle Traits, since every unit relies on them to reach their full potential.

Valankrom’s War Throng

The “high concept” of this army is Valankrom’s personal retinue, you can include most of the army, just stopping shy of the Fyreslayer units:

  • High Kyng Valankrom (must be included)
  • Any Gholem unit
  • Any Kromdron unit

Essentially how the army works is Kyng Valankrom gets to pick D3 enemy units he can see in deployment and mark them as wrongdoers and then pick one within 12” each one of your Hero Phases. He also gets to do this at the end of any turn to a unit they inflicted damage on him. Being in combat with a Wrongdoer counts as having a Grudge Token, and its the chief way to (at least count as) having Grudge Tokens, so this shifts the playstyle from a more anvil style of play, where you are handing out Grudge Tokens in the base army, to picking enemy units that are vulnerable and striking out against them.

This unfortunately means ranged units are at a huge disadvantage, so once per battle you can target a unit and shoot them as if they had a grudge marker, but it has to target a wrongdoer. Finally if Valankrom dies, everyone gets +1 to attack for the rest of the battle. This is still not ideal since you need him to label wrongdoers, but if the battle is almost over, this might help you at least close the deal.

It gets a wholly unique spell lore, which is neat. The unlimited spell Rune of Vigour gives +2” of movement, while Rune of Banishment subtracts 1 from casting rolls and lets you banish manifestations on a 3D6 when within 12”, and Rune of Upheaval makes enemy units fail commands on a 6+ within 12”. All 3 encourage moving up the field quicker to make the most use of them.

Overall it’s…an interesting concept but definitely way less powerful than just playing the base army.

Fyrd of Fyrepeak

The first of the more interesting Armies of Renown, this is for people who have an ample Fyreslayer collection to have something to play while they build up their new, more diverse Duardin force. Here you can include any Grimmdron unit and that’s it, that’s the only requirement.

As you’re probably expecting, the battle traits here revolve around Ur-Gold Runes. Going back to the third edition tome (minus the 6+ amplified version part), these are all in either player’s Hero phase again, and last the entire battle round. Like before you only get one per round and they’re only usable once per game each. Putting it in the Hero phase is probably for the best so you don’t forget what you’re doing and can plan your turn ahead, though it can be rough to have to plan for a charge phase or combat phase that ultimately does not pan out like you hoped.

The Runes are all brand new, and include:

Rune of Fortune: Only hit by shooting attacks on 5s or 6s. Good opener, particularly against shooting armies.

Rune of Spellbreaking: Subtract 2 from enemy Casting and Chanting Rolls, while you get +1 to chanting. Another good opener, but you might save it for a key combat turn as well, depending on what spells they have.

Rune of Guarding: Ignore first point of damage each phase. It’s fine.

Rune of Speed: Re-roll run and charge rolls. Probably good round 1 or 2, right before a big mega charge.

Rune of Striking: +1 to hit and wound rolls. Not as flashy as Crit Mortals, but still nasty.

They also get Grudge points for killing a unit, so you're encouraged to push up the board early and start clearing away units. Their Heroic Trait Foeseeker also grants a grudge point to everyone in the army if you kill a marked enemy Hero with the bearer of the trait (or a consolation prize of everyone wholly within 12” if they didn’t do the deed).

Like Valankrom’s War Throng, they have a unique lore, but it’s a prayer lore, naturally.

Grimnir’s Protection gives a 5+ Ward (to 2 units when charged), Grimnir’s Fury is a horde clearer which does mortals on 5+s (4+s if supercharged) which is above average for this type of ability and finally Grimnir’s Geyser gives back the deep strike the Runesmith lost.

Overall I’d classify this as “Cute”. The intent and concept is clear, Fyreslayers players get to keep their Fyreslayers, but it’s probably not going to last long term as the limited ability options and the pared down Fyreslayer unit selection is a real hindrance.

Throngakor Alliance

Easily the most intriguing of the three, instead of restriction choices this expands them. This is the second Army of Renown since Legion of the First Prince to integrate units from another book. While that limited your options to a very select few, this opens up the entire Khazalite Holds book alongside the Kharadron Overlords book.

First, at the start of each battle round you trigger Stone and Sky where you choose a non Hero infantry unit from each army and as long as they are within ½” of each other they are -1 to wound rolls against them and have the Stone and Sky keyword (This does a few things with other abilities). ½” feels a bit harsh, feels like maybe should have been combat range, but it is what it is.

So what does this keyword do? First, Duardin Pride gives all units wholly within 12” of a Stone and Sky unit +10 Control which is a good start. If an enemy unit within 12” of a Stone and Sky unit uses a command, you get to hand out a Grudge point once per round. You can technically give these to Kharadron units, but it won’t do anything.

The keyword is also defined but what it does not do. With Hammer and Anvil you can pick a non-Stone and Sky unit that is in combat, and then pick a unit from the opposite faction (e.g. if a Khazalite unit is in combat, pick a Kharadron Unit) to shoot them and get Crit (2 Hits). Not a bad perk. The Vice also lets you pick a Friendly Khazalite Unit and a Friendly Kharadron unit in combat to give them both Crit Mortals which feels like an insane deal.

A small little perk as well is the straightforwardly named Transport Duardin which simply adds an addendum to let Khazalites be transported by Skyvessels as if they were Kharadrons.

The Spell and Prayer lores all key off of using a mix of units as well. The spells Rune of Spirit and Rune of Defence give +2” of movement -1 to be hit, respectively, within 12” of the caster as long as they have both a Kharadron and Khazalite unit near them. The sole Prayer lore lets you target an enemy unit, giving them -1 to attack when in combat with a unit of both factions.

Overall, neat idea. I’m not sure if it’s worth some of the tradeoffs, however. Grudge generation basically comes to a standstill and while Kharadrons don't care about that, the Khazalites certainly do which will impact their effectiveness and I don’t know that Kharadrons add much to their already pretty solid roster of units, except for maybe transport ships.

Regiments of Renown

Two Regiments of Renown, both are available to any Order army. As a side note worth mentioning, unless they invalidate it, Gotrek does remain as his own Regiment of Renown, just because he’s not here doesn’t mean it is invalidated and the new and improved version of him is probably very likely to see play unless it is just removed outright.

Fjori’s Vengeance

The Fyreslayers regiment, it contains:

  • 1 Grimhold Exile
  • 1 Auric Flamekeeper
  • 1 Heartguard Berzerker unit with 5 models

The regiment ability basically allows the Grimhold Exile to take his other units with him when he teleports, though he works best alone so you’ll want to split him up as soon as possible after landing. One unit then gets a Grudge Mark, you choose so do what you want.

Kinda lackluster, only gets one grudge mark and probably only useful if you really need a teleporting hammer.

Thengin’s Grudgesettlers

A chonker of a regiment, it contains:

  • 1 Runewright
  • 1 Clanshields with 10 models
  • 1 Oathkeeper Cannon
  • 1 Clangunners with Rifles with 10 models

This is a pretty expensive unit to drop in, but what makes it interesting is every unit in the army starts with a Grudge Marker and only loses it once they kill an enemy unit, which makes them an interesting sort of Blitz unit, they’re stronger in the early game before they’ve killed stuff, so you can use them to try and take out key pieces early while the rest of your army marches up. That said, it is pretty expensive and hard to recommend for that reason.

Final Thoughts

New armies are always exciting since they bring a huge dynamic shift to the game. They can be difficult to predict, since we don’t have as much information to go on as an updated version of an older book, but we’re pretty confident that this is a strong book.

It is very technical, with a high skill floor where minor mistakes might cost the game, but under a skilled hand it’ll probably win a lot of games. Some of the attacks we’ve been able to dish out in test games are absolutely eye watering and it’ll be exciting to see what people do with it.

This may not have been the army Fyreslayers players we’re hoping for, but it’s a pretty damn interesting one.
