Tabletop Battles Reviews: XCOM The Miniatures Game Wave 1 Aliens and Operatives

August 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Thanks to Modiphius for providing these models for review.

When Modiphius announced XCOM: The Miniatures Game, I was more than a little bit excited. As no stranger to the video games or Modiphius' previous resin and plastic offerings, I wasted no time in jumping on a call with Chris Birch, Modiphius' CEO, and interviewed them about their new project. Suitably hyped, I got cracking on their first wave of releases. Said wave covers the first Operatives set - consisting of 6 XCOM Operatives, and the first Alien wave, which includes 3 Sectoids, 1 Sectoid Commander, 3 Thin Men, and 1 Outsider. I was sent the resin versions, though plastic versions of the same sculpts are also going to be available.

Details

XCOM Operatives on sprue. Credit: SRM

I started with the Operatives, as they're the folks I've spent hundreds of hours sending to their deaths in the video games. The details are as crisp and clean as I'd expect from Modiphius. The guns even have undercuts at the ends of their barrels so you don't need to drill them out. Cleanup was fairly minor; some flashing around the boots, the odd mold line on the gun, and no huge, obvious mold lines in inopportune places. The Sniper had some light mold slipping along the legs but nothing a knife couldn't fix. The faces are expressive and a little cartoony - honestly, the whole set has a cartoony look very much in line with the modern Firaxis-developed XCOM games. The weapons are chunky, the armor is blocky, and there aren't any overdone details that will make painting a pain. In short, these models are clean and simple, but I mean that in a positive way - excessive detail and sharp detail are not the same thing. The only thing I don't really love are the sculpted eyebrows, which can look a little too cartoony on models this scale.

XCOM Wave 1 Aliens on sprue. Credit: SRM

The aliens might appear simpler at first glance (after all, a Sectoid is just a classic "Roswell grey" alien) but have some sharp detail too. The folds in the Thin Men's suits are crisp, the musculature on the aliens is well-defined with some creepy veins around the Sectoid Commander's brain, while the Outsider's armor and the crew's weapons all have clean, sharp edges to them. These guys involved a bit more cleanup than the Operatives, with more flashing and mold lines to deal with, but nothing too egregious.

If you're used to Modiphius' models, you might have been expecting sculpted scenic bases. These guys have simple plastic 30mm bases, just a smidge shorter than Games Workshop's. I was missing one, so I filled in with a 32mm base from my bits box. Given the variety of locales an average XCOM campaign can take you, I don't mind getting to choose for myself how to base these guys, though it wouldn't shock me if Modiphius made base toppers in the future for this like they have for Fallout.

Assembly

The preview copies we got didn't have instructions, but it wasn't a big deal in this case. The models rarely consist of more than 3 or 4 parts, and each resin sprue is stamped with a number that makes it easy to tell what goes with what. Sprues MXU 005A and MXU 005B are one model, MXU 001A and MXU 001B are another, and so on. There was a little more confusion on the alien side, but when in doubt, just look at the product photos and see how they want to go together. Most pieces fit with a pretty simple peg and divot that has a tiny bit of wiggle room. Being resin, there's often a bit of warping or bending that makes a few fits kind of wonky. Most cases where I had two arms holding a gun, I had to cut at least some of their guiding pegs to better fit the arms together. The joins on the Operatives are very well hidden, largely lining up with armor plates and the like. The Sectoids aren't quite so lucky, being so organic, but the other aliens have well hidden joins too. Even with a few minutes given to base decor and locating an extra base for the Outsider, it took me like an hour and a half to build both sets.

XCOM Operatives WIP. Credit: SRM

My only real complaint here is that there's no variety or options should you end up with more than one of these groups of Operators. You can swap the arms on some of them pretty readily if you wanted to change things up, but aside from the sniper, all of their heads are integrated with their bodies. It would have been nice to have head options, just for variety's sake. I understand that more models will come in the future which will likely have some of the other heads, helmets, and hairstyles we've seen in the games, but I would have liked a couple options in this initial offering. XCOM Operatives are very much "your dudes" in the video games, and I would have liked to see that better reflected here.

XCOM Aliens wave 1 WIP. Credit: SRM

By their nature, the aliens of this wave are much more diverse. The Sectoids and Sectoid Commander are fiddly, with thin arms and small contact points. The one on all fours was especially tricky, and I could never line up the Commander's arms quite right. In a few cases I had to just cut off the guiding pegs to stick the limbs on, and I was even using fresh superglue. The Thin Men have larger pieces which fit together more easily, though I had to brace the long outstretched gun arm. There was some confusion on the crouching pair's weapon arms, but dry fitting quickly made it clear which gun went with which guy. Lastly, the Outsider was chunky enough that he took no effort at all.

Painting

As I've mentioned in my previous Fallout painting articles, I treat painting models from a property like this the same way I'd approach a historical miniature. I gather reference material from places like the XCOM wiki (get your adblockers ready, it's a Fandom page) and then base my schemes on those. Most screenshots out there are low-resolution given the game's age and its more niche fandom, but it's hardly a big ask to open up XCOM: Enemy Unknown and start nabbing screenshots of your own, or crawling around on Steam for recent shots from the community. It was helpful to also have a tab with the studio paint jobs up too, but they differ from the game models in a few ways here and there.

XCOM Wave 1 Aliens. Credit: SRM

The aliens were first, as I figured they'd be easy to knock out given their simpler details and textures. I painted all of the alien weapons in Grey Seer and Thamar Black, with highlights of Ulthuan Grey and Dawnstone respectively. They have kind of a 2010s Apple store aesthetic, and I wanted to distinguish their alien materials from human ones by using non-metallic colors. For the green of the plasma, I'd pick that out in Caliban Green then highlight with Warboss Green and Skarsnik Green.

XCOM Sectoids. Credit: SRM

The Sectoids seemed most straightforward, so I started there. I primed them with Mechanicus Standard Grey, and washed them with thinned Drakenhof Nightshade, plus some thinned Gal Vorbak Red in some deep muscles for color variation. The Sectoid Commander got a Carroburg Crimson wash on his top half as well, and they all got some Carroburg Crimson around the eyes. I built the grey of the skin back up with the base color, and did a two stage highlight, gradually adding more Fenrisian Grey to it to give them their blue-grey skintone.

XCOM Sectoid Commander. Credit: SRM

For the Sectoid Commander, I added a little Emperor's Children to that mix to highlight his veins and brain. I picked out the eyes in Gal Vorbak Red, with a little black mixed in for the pupils. For their sickly yellow glow, I tried a Cassandora Yellow wash then some thinned Yriel Yellow paint, but wasn't crazy with the look. It got the point across, but were I to paint more Sectoids in the future I'd probably try something else.

XCOM Thin Men. Credit: SRM

I primed the Thin Men with Chaos Black primer, since they're just creepy dudes in dark suits. A blend of Thamar Black and Dark Reaper made for the first highlight, and pure Dark Reaper for the second. Their skin was Rakarth Flesh, washed with Seraphim Sepia and then Athonian Camoshade around the visible scales on their necks and wrists. They have kind of a sickly look in the game, and I think that got there. I reestablished the base color, leaving shades in the recesses, and highlighted their chins, cheekbones, eyebrows and noses with Pallid Wych Flesh. I then picked their scales out with Caliban Green and highlighted those with a mix of Caliban Green and Grey Seer, since those colors were still wet on my palette. Any lighter green will probably do. Their ties were just Wazdakka Red with Wild Rider Red highlights, and their buttons and glasses were picked out in Retributor Armour. I highlighted their hair with Eshin Grey and their shoes with Skavenblight Dinge and Stormvermin Fur on their sharpest points, just to give them a sharp, warm look.

XCOM Outsider. Credit: SRM

The Outsider was the biggest challenge to figure out. While the studio paint job is lovely, finding reference photos from the games was much harder. For one, they're less prevalent than the other aliens, only showing up on UFO crashes, so there aren't as many images out there. For two, they're a messy ball of polygons around a crystal, and clean images of them are in short supply. I turned to the starting point of any would-be ROMhacker or 2000s sprite-based webcomic creator, Spriter's Resource, which had a clear model of an Outsider, plus a few other aliens and items from the game. After that, it became pretty clear which panels were brown and which were grey, and I was able to pick them out in Gorthor Brown and Corvus Black, respectively. I washed the whole thing with Agrax Earthshade, reestablished the base colors, and highlighted the brown with Rakarth Flesh and black with Mechanicus Standard Grey. He's got way more edges than anything else in the alien crew, so was a little tedious to paint, but it made figuring out what colors go where pretty simple.

XCOM Outsider. Credit: SRM

The crystal he's built around was just Averland Sunset, washed Cassandora Yellow, and highlighted with Yriel Yellow.

XCOM Operatives. Credit: SRM

For the Operatives, I wanted to paint them like their standard appearance in the game, which is a brown, tan, and green uniform. I started with Hinox Brown primer (the equivalent of Citadel's Rhinox Hide) and worked my way up from there. I put down base coats of Castellan Green for their BDUs, Tallarn Sand for the tan parts of their armor and weapons, Corvus Black for the dark fabric straps and pads on their armor, Evil Sunz Scarlet for the red cloth on the rifle magazines, lenses on scopes, and shotgun shells, and Loren Forest for the grenades, while leaving the boots in their primer brown. I united all of these with a wash of Athonian Camoshade, reestablished their base colors while leaving shades in the recesses, and gave them a quick round of highlights - Loren Forest for the green, Karak Stone for the tan, Mechanicus Standard Grey for the black, Wild Rider Red for the reds, and Gorthor Brown for the dark brown boots. I gave them a diverse set of skintones using (dark to light) Catachan Flesh, Bloodreaver Flesh, Knight-Questor Flesh, Cadian Fleshtone, and Kislev Flesh. I would base one of those colors, wash with Reikland Fleshshade, establish that base and blend in a little of the next color in line. Eyes were just black with white in the corners. Lastly, I painted the metallics, mostly because I don't like painting metallics. These were just Leadbelcher with Nuln Oil wash, highlighted with Pro Acryl Silver (Stormhost Silver equivalent).

XCOM Sniper Operative. Credit: SRM

Every Operative has a tiny square on their collar where you can paint a flag, representing their home country and the international, collaborative nature of the XCOM project. I'm sure someone out there makes tiny enough decals to pop here, but I just painted a few by hand. My recommendation would be to choose simpler flags, or abstract ones that are more complicated. After all, it's a surface only marginally larger than a grain of rice, so don't try to get all 50 stars if you're painting an American flag or something similarly detailed. I'd also go for more saturated colors here than on your base models just to make them stand out more.

XCOM Support Operatives. Credit: SRM

I united all of the models with a lush forest basing scheme, first for color contrast and second because it can represent so many places in the world. I used a few leftover basing bits from Epic Basing that I'd gotten in one swag bag or another, plus the single Medikit that came on the sprue of one of the Support Operatives, with the same red and white recipes as on the XCOM Operatives and the Alien weapons. The ground was just Stirland Mud texture paint, washed Agrax Earthshade and drybrushed with Steel Legion Drab and Karak Stone. I added tufts from Army Painter and Woodland Scenics clump foliage for some denser grown and called it a day.

Final Thoughts, Parting Shots, and What Have You

XCOM Assault Operatives. Credit: SRM

Working with these models was a generally pleasant experience. Building them could be a bit finnicky, as their cartoony, video game designs were probably never meant to exist in the physical world and likely weren't made with considerations towards that. The sculptors at Modiphius did a great job translating those designs to physical miniatures, and holding these little guys who've only existed on my screen for over a decade was a delight. This is a solid start for the XCOM range, though I don't feel it'll hit its full potential until we start seeing the multi-part plastic kits they've mentioned. This may be a humble beginning, but it's a promising one.
