Commander Focus: Temporary Clones, Legendary Artifacts, & Shuri, Wakandan Inventor

July 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer

At this point, everyone knows that Universes Beyond has led to an explosion of Legendary Creatures in sets, a sharp rise in the named characters which exist at the center of stories, and possess the capacity to serve as Commanders. When I started playing Magic, in Aether Revolt, there were a mere 6 new Commanders in the set—7 now, with the change to Legendary Vehicles. Now, depending upon the set, you could receive as many Commanders in a single booster on any given day, largely because the floodgates are well & truly open for Common/Uncommon options. A ton of these low-rarity legends exist to fill roles in Limited, or check a box that their character appeared in the set, but amid the ~260 new Commanders in Marvel Super Heroes, one particular Uncommon stands out: Shuri, Wakandan Inventor. While she doesn’t read as particularly flashy, that activated ability enables a number of crazy interactions, and the archetype she inhabits appreciates a tacked-on Etherium Sculptor in the Command Zone. Moreover, I have the sneaking suspicion Wizards knows she’s a sleeper hit waiting to be cracked open, given this version of Shuri is receiving a unique promo at—of all things—this year’s D23 expo. With but a single Uncommon printing, there’s little better way to give her some chase potential, for the diehards. With that being said, it’s time to take our tinfoil hats off, and turn them into The Stasis Coffins.

"Becomes a copy of a second target artifact..."

Let’s start with the workhorse portion of Shuri’s textbox: “Artifact spells you cast cost {1} less”. It’s nothing too exciting, but the fact you have a 2 mana Commander ramps up the potential of this line, pun intended. Shuri allows us to avoid playing any 2-mana rocks, because we have a better one in our opening hand, every game; instead, we’ve shifted towards a critical mass of 4-mana rocks, like Stonespeaker Crystal & Sceptre of Eternal Glory, which not only provide additional utility, but rocket us forwards along the mana curve. Shuri certainly can play more expensive Artifacts that you’d appreciate in multiples, like Mindslaver, but I’ve chosen to keep her curve fairly low. This plays into her capacity to enable flexible clones you can actually keep around, rather than gamebreaking copied Artifacts. That bring us to Shuri’s standout ability, to launder one Artifact into another for the turn, at Sorcery-speed.

Candidate for the coffin dance, perhaps.

There’s not really another Commander which does exactly what Shuri does, lifting the name & text in its entirety from one Artifact, and putting it on another, returning to the original not with a trigger but merely during cleanup. The fact the Legendary status isn’t preserved is another wrinkle, and something we’ll absolutely be abusing, because the way Shuri does this is via my favorite word in Magic: “Except”. We’ve covered copiable values a bunch of times here on Commander Focus (to the point it’s practically my gimmick), but to remind you, ‘except’ denotes that the following text is a copiable value, meaning it’s part of the card that gets transferred when you—shocker—make a copy. If we use Shuri to turn a Clue token into The One Ring, that former Clue isn’t Legendary, and if we were to copy it with something like Phyrexian Metamorph, that Metamorphed One Ring would also not be Legendary. Of course, just having a second (or more) copy of many of our best Artifacts is already powerful, and while the ceiling on Shuri is in the stratosphere, the floor remains exceptionally high. At worst, she’s a discount in the Command Zone for 2 mana, that provides you with a second copy of whatever your best Artifact is, during your turns.

Wakanda: Double Tap

Now for the fun part: What Artifacts are we doubling up on? I've chosen to lean into the strength afforded by Shuri to double up on exactly Legendary Artifacts, specifically those which exile themselves. Prior to Shuri, the best way to do this was via Doctor Who's Idris, Soul of the Tardis, so as you can surmise Shuri's a fair bit more efficient. First of all, the big one: At nearly all levels of play, The Stasis Coffin is among Shuri's finest tools. This can replicate the protection afforded by The One Ring (also great here, you just dodge the life loss) every turn. You ask a question a lot of more casual lists can't readily answer, and boy does it feel good. Next up is a bit of modest hate, with Stone of Erech & Hylda's Crown of Winter—the former is just a bit of one-use gravehate we can keep around, and the latter scales with itself, tapping down boards for a huge amount of refill. My favorite might be Gerrard's Hourglass Pendant, however, which can perform some soft loops alongside Amulet of Vigor, and a sacrifice outlet like KCI. By getting rid of your board with two Pendants around, you can use the one laundered by Shuri to get all your Artifacts back, and with Amulet, untapping them...including the real Pendant. It's just a great way to potentially go very mana-positive, and avoid removal that puts cards in the yard.

One of the worst parts of a very poor run.

Let's get a bit silly though, by way of the Liquimetal cards. These can turn any permanent into an Artifact, and you probably see where this is going. Shuri can turn something else into the thing you've just made an Artifact, and if you potentially clone it, congrats, you keep a non-Legendary copy around. I did this with Rhystic Study in testing, and it was hilarious (for me, anyway). This is also relevant with Shuri herself though, as making things into additional non-Legendary copies not only gets you a discount, but if the relevant permanent didn't enter, you can just use the new Shuri normally. More discounts are nothing to slouch at! There's also a few weird little subthemes in the deck, being Initiative & 'tap matters'. You've seen the former with Hylda's crown, but there's also Ring of the Lucii & False Floor, the former being one of the many 4 mana rocks we're playing, and the latter a potentially-repeatable, mostly one-sided board wipe. Initiative meanwhile stems not only from another competent 4 mana rock—here Sarevok's Tome—but also the phenomenal Dungeoneer's Pack, which is a stand-out for repeatable uses of Shuri in terms of raw value. The goal here is to accomplish something silly, by virtue of sheer number of instances of certain Artifacts. One Dungeoneer's Pack isn't very good, but if you're getting one every turn, perhaps more, suddenly it becomes an aggressively solid value engine you can tack onto normal play.

Laundering Ethically

We'll talk about it a bit more after the decklist, but suffice to say Shuri is capable of some truly heinous play patterns, both in the early game via stax (and breaking parity therein) and in finding combo wins. Shuri of course goes readily infinite with Gonti's Aether Heart, as by far her easiest 1+1 line, although some enabling is needed to get the necessary energy every turn. Not only that though, but Shuri is a phenomenal user of tax effects and lock pieces. You can turn off a Mindlock Orb or Omen Machine on your turn, and have it up for your opponents, or cheat a Trinisphere while your opponents struggle to play through it. The fact her effect ends is, in these cases, an upside, and way to 'pause' the worst of our stax while we play. This is in contrast to Urza, who revels in using the Orbs and cards which turn off while tapped; Shuri breaks parity for a different suite, and therefore has a bit of her own identity in the stax/lock space. Highlighting the fact that having such an identity is a recipe for social malaise, it becomes increasingly necessary to not do these things with Shuri, outside of Bracket 4. So, her obvious turn loops are out, her lock/stax is largely out—what's in? For this list's win condition, we have two debatably-fair options, with the capacity to pretty easily slot in a third.

Complete with the NM piss filter.

First, the one we aren't playing: Dramatic Scepter. This is a now-deprecated combo of Dramatic Reversal & Isochron Scepter, which allows you to untap things infinitely so long as you have rocks producing 2, ideally 3+ mana. Given our suite of rocks, we'll definitely have that going for us. The issue is primarily that, while Shuri is good with infinite untaps, she isn't actually an outlet, and we'd need some additional means of drawing our deck. Instead, given she's a cost reducer in the Command Zone, we're playing the Artifact-only line of Sensei's Divining Top, and a means to cast it off the top (Mystic Forge, Crystal Skull, Isu Spyglass, etc). This draws our deck, at which point we can pivot to a different win condition, usually via Basalt Monolith+Forensic Gadgeteer. The other win condition is a fair bit more honest, and it's...killing our opponents the old fashioned way, via a comical amount of Karnstructs. This is achieved via Simulacrum Synthesizer, a bane of Standard which comes to roost here amid ample 4 mana rocks and a way to double up. Beyond that, I'd like to highlight a fun toolbox available to Shuri in this list, being all the 1-mana Artifacts. Via Tezzeret, Cruel Captain or Artificer's Intuition, we can find everything from gravehate, to removal (Aether Spellbomb), to haste (Lavaspur Boots), and even untaps (Sewer-veillance Cam). Beyond that, as you might surmise, we can just find Sensei's.

Example Decklist: Ah, Put the Shuri in the Sarcophagus

Let’s talk about an alternate build path for Shuri we’re not pursuing: Charge counters. This hinges on the fact that counters are retained, even when we xerox one Artifact onto another, so you can do things like take a 20+ counter Vexing Puzzlebox and turn it into a Darksteel Reactor for a quick win. In Bracket 4 and above, Magistrate's Scepter becomes available, and that's another tremendous use case for Shuri. This is likely to get even better with additional Spacecraft support in Star Trek, but the reason I’m less keen on it for Shuri now is because we have Donatello, Mutant Mechanic that does it better in the Command Zone. Shuri’s a bit faster, sure, but Donatello being able to create bodies with which to Station Spacecraft (and be live immediately, with his triggered ability not requiring haste like Shuri) makes for a better Commander. Shuri is still phenomenal in the 99 of that list though, and I felt it worth bringing up to showcase the diversity of builds she can enable. If I had a nickel for every 2026 Monoblue Commander from a UB product that can flex Spacecraft counter abuse as a wincon, I’d have two nickels. How this happened twice is beyond me.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

One thing worth remembering when thinking about your Bracket is the fact that Shuri is excellent at turning off stax pieces for your turn; while Sorcery-speed on her activation means hard stax like Winter Orb isn’t avoidable, she’s a premier Commander for jamming cards like Vexing Bauble, Torpor Orb, etc. This arrives with the intent to change them into something else for a turn, avoiding the worst of their downsides, and can even be used with some of the lock pieces mentioned earlier in this piece (Omen Machine gets gross). In that way, Shuri is quite forgiving, allowing a player to run free spells & Enters effects with no fear of screwing yourself over. This is likely more at-home in Bracket 3-4, but for the beautiful devils in the audience, yes you should jam Trinisphere/Thorn of Amethyst with a discount Commander. It shouldn’t work, but it sure does. Shuri scales very well no matter what bracket you use her at, because the conceit of the deck is essentially 'what if I did a thing I was only meant to do once...multiple times', and whether that's locks, controlling players, or comboing off, it's rarely underpowered. She's basically a cooler Urza, and that alone should get you excited.

A Lot of Rough for a Diamond

Wayne Wu's blocky rendering certainly works well with Wakandan garb!

Shuri is an absolute blast. There’s no denying it, she has fun things to do at essentially every power level, whether as a funny value piece, teaching tool for copiable values, or means of breaking stax parity that isn’t as obvious as Urza. I’d rate her as the best Monoblue Commander of the set, and frankly, she’s likely a sleeper pick for the top 5 overall new options for the Command Zone in Marvel Super Heroes. Not only that, but she’s a stand-out card for the 99s of any deck already playing Etherium Sculptor, which has a ~6% inclusion across all legal decks in which it can appear. Suffice to say, she’s going to be popular somewhere. Despite this, the buzz around all the new Commanders from Marvel has already started to dwindle, especially compared to discussions surrounding recent sets like Avatar or Tarkir; anecdotally, I’ve seen far less interest in Commanders from the set in my local scene than basically any set prior, outside of maybe Spider-Man & Aetherdrift. To couch that, the precons have been everywhere, which is no surprise, but there’s a fog hanging low over the main set, with not a ton of deckbuilders I speak with being especially fond of it. Whether that’s a lingering effect from the poison pill that was Spider-Man—or product fatigue on the whole—is hard to say. Sure, Wizard has released statements that it’s the fastest-selling set of all time, but they said something similar about the popularity of Spider-Man, and your local LGS likely can’t get rid of those boxes now with any real margin. Given we’re expecting ~4 more Marvel sets, that’s not a great sign, especially if there’s no change of course in the way these sets are designed; while having an absolute truckload of new Commanders can lead to diamonds in the rough like Shuri existing at Uncommon, it’s probably doing more harm than good, and leading to a host of cards with single-digit decks logged on EDHREC. This Commander won't be one of them, but I expect a huge portion of Marvel's milquetoast options to rot, unloved.

Until next time, pray the D23 promo won't cost a kidney.
