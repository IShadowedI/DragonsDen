Tabletop Battles Reviews: Necromunda Skirmish - The Trading Post

August 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer
As mentioned in the preview articles, fighters are no longer restricted to certain types of weapons, and this extends to equipment too, no more prestige items for Leaders/Champs only. Accordingly, terms like pistol, basic weapon and special weapon are now out of use. Weapons are instead organised into thematic types, like las weapons or power weapons, while armour and personal equipment have their own sections. Weapon Traits have also been overhauled, and we will go through them in detail in this article. Thank you to Games Workshop for providing an advanced copy of Necromunda: Skirmish for review.

Trading Points (TP)

A frequent criticism of Rarity/Illegality in N18 was that it didn’t sufficiently restrict access to the more esoteric weapons or equipment. In response, the new Trading Points have throttled back the ability to buy any equipment, beyond what used to be termed common items, en masse. Every item in the Trading Post has a TP value. A stub gun is TP0, and a plasma gun is TP3, for example. Gangs need to use their Leader and/or Champions to perform the Visit Trading Post Post-Cycle action to buy anything from the Trading Post, even TP0 items. A Leader visiting provides 2TP, a Champion provides 1TP. Players do ‘spend’ those TP on the items they select to by (in addition to paying the credit cost). So if you send 1 Leader and 1 Champion, you have 3TP, and if you buy that plasma gun, that’s it, no more nice stuff can be bought during that visit. Unused TP are discarded at the end of the Post-Cycle Phase, they can’t be banked up. 

This system will make players think far more carefully about what Trading Post equipment they aim to procure for their gang, because equipping them en masse with high-quality equipment is going to be a very slow process (unless it’s on their own Equipment List, see below). If we think about the usual starting gang including 2 Champions, together with their Leader, you could access a single 4TP item, like a meltagun - if you have the creds. But any Recovery results on those important Fighters from Lasting Injuries will hold you back. Those same models are the only ones who can take critically injured fighters to the doc, and even if no gang members are in need of immediate medical attention, there’s an opportunity cost. You can use up to 5 fighters in a gang to generate income via the Work Territory action, and anyone can Train for 2XP. Post-Cycle Actions are a real resource. Even when you definitely want to target some piece of equipment for purchase, we foresee Cycles where you have to put it off, because the Leader/Champions aren’t available to provide the TP. In such cases, do you stash the creds, or find something you can buy immediately?

On that note, gangs’ Equipment Lists are the unknown quantity to us. These will be available in the Gangs of the Underhive/Outlands books, all we know is that ‘weapons and wargear may always be purchased from a gang’s Equipment List’ without recourse to the Trading Post or any actions. If these are anything like as permissive as in N18, it’s fair to say that gangs won’t be any less heavily armed in the future (campaign income, which is reduced, permitting). They will just be more inclined to wield their house-favoured equipment than going shopping for the perfect vanity piece. 

Necromunda Traders. Credit: Dylon

Item Costs and Balancing

There has clearly been a general sweep over the credit cost of weapons in the game. As with their profiles, everything has changed, so we don’t yet have a really firm grasp of what’s good and bad. So in this initial article we demur to give solid ratings to weapons - we’re just not that confident. Apart from anything else, we don’t know what other weapons, exclusive to this or that House, exist or how they compare.

Our general impression, however, shows a few trends. A lot of melee weapons seem to have been reduced in effectiveness, losing Strength or AP. The same applies to most of the ranged weapons. There does appear to be more rationalisation of weapon costs, as if things were seriously compared. We don’t really know if it’s all sensible yet, but there aren't any complete head-scratchers of why something is so cheap or so expensive, as there were in N18. We do have to note, though, that personal equipment still seems overpriced compared with weaponry. 

The most esoteric equipment, things like Frenzon Collars and Falsehoods, have been removed, and we must wait and see if they ever re-appear. Probably for the best; things are simpler, but also less prone to abuse. This cull also extends to Chems - no great loss, since hardly anyone ever used them - and to a lot of the really useless equipment, like Skinblades. 

Weapons

Important Weapon Changes

With the change to weapons’ statlines, Lethality is perhaps less important than N18 Damage was, but it is still a very important stat for weapons to have - and many weapons, like shotguns, that were D2 in N18 are now L1. The loss of to-hit modifiers has created some losers and winners, as one might expect. But the changes to Cover, in particular, have changed how we analyse weapons. Nowadays, the actual distances of Short Range is more important. Weapons with high Short Range, like the Long Rifle, functionally have better AP than others in most situations. Sufficiently high AP weapons effectively ignore cover, at least against models that weren’t heavily armoured already - they will be deadlier than before.

While it may seem that many ranged weapons are more restrained, we need to remember that simplifying cover (no more Partial and Full Cover) and moving it to Save modifiers has made a lot of shooting easier, because N18's Full Cover was terribly punishing, particularly to models with average-low BS. Shooting targets in the open will often be a little harder, but even at Long Range, a BS4+ weapon, S3 against a T3 target, is more effective against a target in cover than it would have been against a Full Cover target in N18, and similarly effective to shooting at a target in Partial Cover.

Traits

Everything about Ammo

Ammunition-related Traits in general have been reworked. We now have to consider the interaction of several relevant Traits. Only weapons with Ammo(X+), or Rapid Fire(X) have a chance to go Out of Ammo. The Reload (Single) action is normally an automatic success but plenty of punchy weapons also have Scarce(X+), which means they need to roll X+ for the Reload to work. 

  • With Rapid Fire, if any Firepower dice shows the bullet hole/bullet symbol, the weapon is Out of Ammo. This means such weapons are more likely to run dry than in N18, because they don't have any immediate roll to avoid the Out of Ammo condition. If multiple Firepower dice (if the weapon is RF(2-3)) show that ammo symbol, the weapon is jammed and cannot be used again in that battle.
  • With Ammo(X+), the player must roll X+ after every time the weapon fires, whether or not the Firepower dice showed an ammo symbol. If they fail the X+ roll, the weapon is Out of Ammo. If they rolled an ammo symbol on the firepower dice and failed the X+ roll, the weapon is jammed and cannot be used again in that battle. 
    • This means that while weapons with Rapid Fire, or with Ammo(X+) will be Out of Ammo more frequently. But, they can now reliably be Reloaded. While that might seem like a simple trade-off, we also have to call out the incidence of permanent (well, battle-long) jams, on weapons with both Traits, and/or with RF(2-3). A boltgun or bolt pistol (Rapid Fire(1) and Ammo(3+)), have a 1/18 chance to permanently jam each time they fire. A heavy stubber, with Rapid Fire(2), is only expected to jam 1/36 times, although it will run out of ammo about ⅓ of the time. A Heavy Bolter, with RF(2) and Ammo(3+), if our napkin math is correct, has a more than half chance (5/9) to run Out of Ammo each time it fires, and about a 1/9 chance to jam permanently. Harsh!
    • Some weapons, most notably Grenades, are Limited, meaning they can never be Reloaded, or Single Shot, meaning they can only be used once per battle. The N18 Limited rule, which some special ammunition had, which meant you deleted it from your gang roster on failing an ammo check, doesn’t seem to have any equivalent. We are glad of that, since it was hard to balance costs around the potential loss of the player’s investment, and losing the ammunition was always a feels-bad moment.
    • How does this actually impact the game when we look at weapon profiles? Lots of the more powerful weapons have RF(X) and/or Ammo(X+). If a weapon just has RF(1), things are slightly more inconvenient than N18. It’s more likely to run dry, since you can’t pass an immediate Ammo Roll (in N18 parlance) to keep it working. But on the other hand, you know you’ll be able to reload. Having RF(1) and Scarce(X+) is more like the N18 system, where you might run out of ammo and Reloading depends on the dice. Ammo(X+) is just making the initial run-out of ammo very common. When it exists in isolation, it means you can expect to stop and Reload regularly between shots. But it also introduces that chance of losing the weapon for the rest of the battle – if it’s Ammo(6+), as with flamer weapons, that chance is significant (5/36).

To try and simplify the difference from N18:

      • Most weapons, lacking Rapid Fire(X) or Ammo(X+), simply won’t run out of ammo, which is a big boost to reliability of shooting.
      • Between the automatic, immediate effect of rolling the ammo symbol on the Firepower dice, and the prevalence of difficult Ammo(X+) on powerful weapons, the weapons which still do risk running out are more likely to do so on average.
      • Reloading is fully reliable for most weapons, and fewer weapons have Limited (N18 Scarce), so running out of ammunition is a much more even experience. It happens, but the model can usually get back into the fight afterwards.
      • The chance to run out of ammo for the battle is shifted onto combinations of multiple firepower dice and/or an Ammo(X+) roll. Any weapon with either of those traits could jam at any time. This interaction is the equivalent of N18 Scarce - sooner or later that weapon is going to jam for good.

Other Traits

This isn’t going to be an exhaustive list - some traits are self explanatory, like Melee, and some are functionally unchanged, like Twin-Linked.

  • Light weapons can be fired in close combat, using WS, just as Sidearms were in N5. They don’t get Rapid Fire, and as before they are limited to 1 attack only. A model can also fire two Light weapons with one Shoot action, if - and only if - they have the Gunfighter Skill. 
  • Heavy ranged weapons are only fired using the Braced Shot (Double) action. This means they don’t play well with various Skills and weapon attachments, but most importantly the user needs to see their target at the start of their Activation to get a shot off, and they can’t fire at all if Suppressed. This is super punishing, especially since N18’s Suspensors appear to be gone altogether. The Bulging Biceps Skill lets models move a bit before firing, but doesn’t get around the problem of being Suppressed. For melee weapons, Heavy means the model can’t pick a Secondary weapon when activating in close combat, which of course is still negative, but often made up for by the raw power of the weapon’s stats.
  • Arc(X) is a trait for weapons on Vehicles. As in N18, it just means the weapon can fire in that Arc (or those Arcs). In addition to the 4 directions, there is a new Arc, Centreline, which means a column, the same width as the vehicle, extending forwards. 
  • Blaze (X+). Now another hit on the target if the Wound roll is X+. This is powerful, but not as insanely good as in N18 at neutralising enemy models regardless of how tough, armoured or powerful they were. I think we can all agree that is a good change for balancing the game. Importantly, for analysing individual weapons, it also means that Blaze scales in value with the weapon’s profile, it’s not just something you want to access as cheaply as possible. Note that for Templates/Blasts, the extra hit can proc on the closest target to the firer/blast centre only, not for all models hit. 
  • Blast weapons must centre on a primary target. That primary target’s Save bonus from being in cover is dependent on whether it is in cover from the firing model’s Line of Sight, but if the blast scatters (rules similar to N18) then cover is measured from the central hole of the blast marker, as it always is for secondary targets. Blast weapons can no longer target a point on the battlefield. 
  • This is probably a good simplification as it removes edge cases and opportunities for precise ‘blast sniping’ round corners etc. Which was fun for the firer (less so for the opponent) and made us feel clever, but probably wasn’t appropriate to represent the capabilities of untrained gangers with explosives. It does punish grenades, with their short ranges, relatively harshly, but that is carried through from the 2023 rules.
  • Blasts which scatter 1” and roll the hit symbol on the scatter die now automatically hit the firer – previously, it was more likely to be a simple dud. Exciting! But obviously a slight knock to Blast users. It’s still only 1/36 of all misses that cause those mishaps.
  • Shred(X+), Rending(X+), Breaching(X+) and Concussive(X+). These are all effects which apply if the Wound roll is X+. Shred gives extra Lethality, Rending extra AP, and Breaching ignores Saves altogether. These are clearly all pretty good to have, their exact value depends on the weapon and target profiles. Concussive reduces the target’s Initiative by 1, which sounds far more minor, but will sometimes mean all the difference in close combat. Shock (X+) causes automatic Wounds when rolling X+ on the Hit roll, and importantly, it also counts as a 6 on the Wound roll. So weapons with Shock(X+) have extra value when combined with any of those other traits. 
  • Knockback (X+) is now an effect applied on the Wound roll, and as before it moves the target back, potentially falling off things. Unfortunately, it no longer has any alternative effect if the target is pushed into terrain or another fighter, that simply stops the move. A big hit to the rule, since the Damage boost it could give in N18 was often the best use for it! Additionally, X+ is 5+ or 6+ on the weapons we have seen so far, and that’s a lower chance than it had against most targets in N18. 
  • Gas weapons now simply ignore armour saves (Respirators grant a 5+ invulnerable save against them). They now cause Suppressed like any other weapon. Good times for Escher chemical weapons enthusiasts.
  • Toxin(X+) is simply a fixed Wound roll of X+, such weapons don’t have a conventional Strength characteristic. They only Wound Vehicles on a 6+. This is a change which simplifies the N18 interactions. Toxin weapons no longer bypass the target’s Wounds, they have conventional Lethality stats. This tidies things up – no more tracking models which have rolled Injury dice, but haven’t lost any Wounds – and it opens the design space for future Toxin weapons to have Lethality 2 or more.
  • Unstable is still a very risky trait, but that risk now scales with the power of the weapon. On a natural 1 to hit, the user takes the hit from the weapon, not the target. Clearly, in most cases this is going to be less dangerous than N18’s 50% chance to go OOA. It also means that a multi-wound model like a champion can fire certain Unstable weapons and know they are not going down at the first shot. Since Unstable is no longer dependent on the Firepower Dice result, a model can proc multiple hits from Rapid Fire and resolve them all against itself, which is appropriately terrifying. We don’t think an Unstable hit causes the model to become Suppressed, but that’s something we would like to see clarified in an FAQ.
  • Reckless is now much easier to use. On melee weapons, it still assigns hits randomly to other models in combat, friend or foe, but for ranged weapons, it randomises hits on models within 6” of the target (and within maximum range and the firer’s LoS). That is both less of a catastrophic downside, being much easier to manage, and makes more sense. A storm welder is clearly unsuitable for fine marksmanship, but it was always a bit silly that it made the user want to spin 180 degrees and nail one of their allies standing 50 feet behind them.
  • Paired (X) means that fighters cannot pick a Secondary weapon in combat alongside this weapon; instead they gain +X attacks. This is an elegant change and means that such weapons aren’t wildly more dangerous on the Charge than at other times. Remember that it also won’t come into play unless the model is Activating. 
  • Parry gives +1 to armour saves in combat - no more forcing re-rolls, which was functionally not worth much against elite enemies - and Shield gives +1 to saves against shooting. We’re interested to see the stats of Van Saar or Enforcers’ iconic shields, we suspect they will be melee weapons which have both of these traits.
  • Rad-Phage means that unsaved Wounds from the weapon inflict Rad-Poisoned, ie -1T. Relative to N18, this shifts the effect onto an unsaved Wound, which of course makes it a little worse, because many targets will already be in trouble by the time the Rad-Poisoned kicks in; previously it was a decent chance to hinder targets who'd shrugged off your hit. It also can't be stacked up over multiple turns and/or with the old Flesh Wounds, to wear targets down to OOA. On the other hand, Toughness Advances will be much harder to come by in the future, and it's still quite a nice effect.

Ranged Weapons

First we are going to look at some Light weapons - these might not be grouped together in the list any more, but they are worth looking at comparatively, since they are natural picks for models that might run into close combat. 

  • Stub Gun. This 5-credit standby is the cheapest gun in Necromunda and you get exactly what you pay for, a very basic ranged attack. It loses a bit by comparison with N18, where it had an unusually good to-hit bonus at short range.
  • Laspistol. This is also 5 credits, and its short range goes up to 8”, so objectively it’s a better stub gun, as minor as that gap is.
  • Autopistol. For 10 credits, you gain Rapid Fire over the stub gun/laspistol - no longer a pure upside, and twice as likely to run dry as it was in N18.You also shorten your short range to 4”, so will sometimes find opponents in cover (5-6” away) tougher to put down. We are inclined to think the budget-handgun market is now slanted towards laspistol purveyors, but honestly the differences are very slight.
  • Sawn-off Shotgun. At last, it’s a pistol! (Genghis: I may have to perform some surgery on my vast number of Orlock models) It has two profiles, and the solid shot is S4, with 4”/8” ranges, and Knockback (6+). There’s also a scattershot profile which is S2, but RF(2), so will proc 2-6 hits when it connects. That’s actually a little better than the S4 against T3 targets, and about the same against T4. Yes, it has a high chance to run dry, but in N26, you can fight on normally with the solid shot profile. This appears to be a nice little half-step up from the traditional budget pistols…oh wait, it costs 30 credits and 1TP. (Genghis: [puts clippers away])

Needle weapons are positioned that little bit ahead of the basic guns, and rest a lot on the utility of Toxin(3+). Now that is a great rule, particularly against very Tough [non-Vehicle] models, but of course it's situational, against T3 targets these weapons won't really justify their cost:

  • Needle Pistol. A little more boutique than the budget Light options at 25 credits/1TP, the only thing this boasts over the budget options is Toxin(3+), there are no other advantages and it has poor range.
  • Needle rifle. This is quite expensive at 45/2TP, and while we like the profile alright, with its 9" Short Range and -1AP, again it all comes down to the availability of Tough targets.

Shout out here to Throwing Knives, which are a sort of entry level, Toxin(4+) weapon. At just 10 credits, and Stub Gun ranges, they won't achieve that much but might be worth considering if you're fighting Goliaths and/or Ogryns all the time. The wrinkle is Ammo(3+), presumably to represent the limited supply a fighter will carry.

There aren’t particularly big changes to most of the other budget ranged weapons:

  • Autoguns are still 8”/24” range and get RF(1), but now cost 20 credits. They’re still ok.
  • Lasguns have a slightly worse short range than N18 (16”), and cost 15 credits. Which is better? We don’t really know. Putting cost aside, the autogun is better at 0-8”, or when the target isn’t in cover, but sometimes (1/6 of the time) you’ll have to Reload. Frankly, the difference is so slight that it won’t make gangs which prefer either aesthetic to feel disadvantaged.
  • Shotguns seem to be a big loser in this edition change. Similar to a sawn-off, they are far more expensive than other budget guns, at 35 credits. For that, you get a solid slug S4 profile, but with worse long range than the autogun, and it’s only L1. It has a situationally useful Knockback(5+), but that procs less often than in N18, and lost some of its utility. The scatter shot profile is the same as the sawn-off but S3. That is good when you’re at point blank range, but we’re not loving this option compared to the longer-range budget weapons, or to Light weapons. 
  • Combat shotguns have been interestingly repositioned, at the same cost as a normal shotgun, but 1TP. Their ‘salvo’ ammo is just a worse solid slug, with lower range and Knockback only (6+). As before, it really exists for the template, which is now S3 and RF(1), with Shred(6+). That is honestly probably slightly worse than its N18 profile (the N18 Scattershot rule applied to all models hit, while multiple hits from Rapid Fire, now, are only applied to the nearest target) but there is great value in automatically hitting, and Suppressing, multiple models. The kicker is the template has Ammo(6+), so you will usually only be able to fire it once before reloading, and have a significant chance of it jamming altogether. Yes, you can fight on with your salvo profile, but as mentioned that’s objectively bad for the price. We don’t know what to make of this. Its vastly cheaper than in N18, and can do a similar job, but lost a lot of reliability and flexibility.
Flamers have lost a lot due to Blaze(X+) becoming just another skill that occasionally makes a hit punchier, as opposed to a game-defining tactic for neutralising any enemy, no matter how formidable. Weirdly they all have Blaze(5+), there’s no differentiation there. Functionally, a lot of your successful Wounds are going to proc another hit. They universally have Ammo(6+), so like some other categories, you expect to stop and Reload.  But this was clearly taken into account when balancing the actual profiles:
  • Flamers keep their S4, AP-1 profile from N18, but cut their (infamously overpriced) cost to just 70/1TP. Templates are inherently strong and it’s a decent damage profile, this seems alright.
  • Hand Flamers are still lower strength, and now AP-, but they gained Light, so you can use them in combat - they inflict one automatic hit. With Blaze, that actually makes them decent choices as Secondary melee weapons, or as the weapon to use when your model is Activated against in close combat. It also means that a model with Gunfighter and two Hand Flamers can spray flame in two directions. Metal. For 45 credits, given the immense uses of templates to inflict Suppression, we think these are good, not as oppressive as N18 Blaze, but with wider uses.
  • Heavy Flamers can finally fire on the move. Yes, the Heavy Flamer is not Heavy. Confusing, but welcome news. It gets S5, AP-2, which is great, for a reasonable cost bump over a regular flamer, at 95/2TP. We think this is worth it, especially for late campaign play. 

Web weapons changed dramatically with the new Webbed Condition. It is no longer functionally identical to Seriously Injured, instead it prevents the target from moving, and debuffs them with -1 to hit rolls. The Web Gun and Web Pistol are still templates, with S5 & S4 respectively, and still ignore armour saves (Invulnerable saves explicitly can be taken) and thus cover. The problem is the nonlethal effects. Stopping enemies moving is a powerful thing, and of course the power of a template to affect multiple models is still there. But many enemies will just happily shoot back at you, even with -1 to hit. After all, if that engagement continues, without outside help, sooner or later your webslinger is going to get taken out, their opponent will shake it off and walk away. These weapons seem like specialised equipment for keeping opponents back from Objectives in Scenarios, or holding off enemy melee specialists - after all, Nerves of Steel and Spring Up can’t help enemies avoid this effect. But it’s only temporary, Webbed targets take a Strength test to remove the Condition at the end of their Activation. So we don’t immediately see the value of Web weapons. They’re certainly not the terrors they were in N18. To balance this, their credit cost has been slashed, but they still cost 3-4TP, probably for fluff reasons. They seem like a secondary weapon Fighters might want to procure later in campaigns.

Looking at the shotguns, flamers and web weapons above, there seems to be a deliberate design decision that Template weapons should have Ammo(6+), presumably to stop players reliably laying down auto-hits each round and keeping their opponents in a Suppressed state without any risk. This is the kind of thing that intrigues us as to how our games will actually play out in the future. 

Bolt weapons all share the Ammo(3+) rule, and Rapid Fire. That means you can’t depend on them firing every Round, they will run dry more than a third of the time, and they have a chance (1/18 for the gun/pistol, and 1/9 for the heavy bolter) to jam completely each time they fire. Ouch. On the plus side, when they run dry and don’t jam, they can now reliably be Reloaded.
  • Boltguns retain their excellent profile from N18, but with the loss of to-hit bonuses, and L2 not being quite as strong as D2 was, they may not be quite as dominant. They’re also into the category of weapons it’s not practical to spam unless they are in your own equipment list, at 55 credits and 2TP.
  • Bolt Pistols offset some of the relative downgrades to bolt weapons by gaining Rapid Fire(1). That makes this a pretty cool weapon for a Gunfighter model, but they’re not cheap at 45/1TP.
  • The Heavy Bolter is dramatically cheaper than it was at 100 credits/3TP, a trend common to Heavy ranged weapons. Even at that reduced price, and the advantages of range in minimising your enemies’ benefits from cover, we don’t think this is necessarily worthwhile. Your opponents simply need to Suppress the carrier and it’s useless. When it does fire, it is more likely than not to run dry (5/9 chance) due to the interaction of Ammo(3+) and RF(2). When that happens, even if you’re not Suppressed, you won’t be able to fire next Round. If you do want to use this weapon, think heavily about support from another fighter with the Munitioneer Skill.
Plasma weapons were the boogeyman of N18, and while still very strong, we are glad to say they are no longer all-purpose, uncomplicated solutions. All plasma weapons are Unstable, there is no safe firing mode. They also all have high Strength, AP-2, L2 and Damage(2), so there are very few models in the game that can fire them without a real risk of catastrophe. They are also all Ammo(6+), so you expect to have to take an Action to Reload between shots. We need to experiment more with how to mitigate and play around that (Munitioneer will be absolutely key), but clearly it will play havoc with your action economy.
  • Plasma Guns retained RF(1) in addition to the other huge strengths of their profile, so they are truly terrifying, leaving boltguns in their dust. If there’s a weapon it’s worth risking Unstable on, it’s this one. The cost actually dropped to 85 credits, although 3TP means it’s not spammable for just anyone. We’re glad, because a player crazy enough to risk Unstable could put down a lot of hurt by leaning into these.
  • Plasma Pistols are nearly as expensive as the guns, at 70/3TP, for half the range and no RF. Clearly, being usable in melee, and guns akimbo with the right Skill, is a big deal, but this seems like an indication the gun is too cheap. A plasma pistol is a real investment now, but is still probably worth it.
  • Plasma Cannons, on the other hand, suffer from the Heavy curse. They’ve a longer range and greater Strength than the gun, and Blast(3”), but we don’t think that is nearly as good as RF(1) on a more mobile platform. Remember that Blasts must always be centred on a target model now. So unless you luck into a scatter roll, you only expect to hit one enemy at a time - your opponent would have to be really incompetent to group their models up in the face of this gun.
Melta weapons are the apex predator, being S8, AP-4, L3 and Damage(3). The actual melta trait from N18 may be no more, but the effects are usually similar: if a Fighter gets hit with this, it isn’t pretty. Effectively the meltagun is 12” range and the Multi-melta is 24”, because they don’t care if most targets are within their short range, or indeed in cover at all. What are the downsides? Well, both are Ammo(6+), so like plasma, you will need to stop and Reload. The designers are also aware of the monster they’ve created, because they are both 4TP, and 140 and 150 credits respectively - the most expensive weapons in the book. Despite that, these seem like weapons many gangs will aspire to. We must again call out the overwhelming downsides of Heavy. To unlock the extra 12” of range on the multi-melta, a Fighter basically has to take Spring Up and/or Nerves of Steel to have a chance (neither is foolproof) of avoiding Suppression, and still wants Bulging Biceps to get effective shots off. A meltagun-armed fighter can just take two Skills that improve their meltagun shooting!

Grav weapons are very odd, being Blasts and having the Graviton Pulse Trait. Instead of rolling to Wound, models hit have to make their own Strength tests. Those that fail take Wounds with no saves allowed, and they're L2. Further, the blast template stays in place and acts as Difficult Terrain, with a chance to dissipate at the end of every Round. They're also Ammo(5+), although not terribly expensive. These seem like particularly great tech against armoured targets. They will usually be on the equivalent of 4+ or 5+ to Wound, but Blasts and ignoring Saves is tasty.

The Grenade launcher is something of a victim of how range affects cover nowadays. With just 6" of Short Range, although 24" Long, it could be hard to reduce cover. Frag, Krak and Smoke grenades all got Ammo(4+), and we're not sure that with that level of ammo insecurity we can justify the cost (80/1TP for just Frag and Krak). Smoke and Photon Flash Grenades, while both working very similarly to N18, are both cut down to 3" templates.

The Long Rifle is now quite expensive at 55/1TP - more than a boltgun - and its Knockback(6+) isn't as useful as before. But it retains S4 and AP-1, and its enormous range (24"/48") is now useful even on smaller maps, because it's essentially reducing the enemy benefits from cover. We'd like to say the same of the Longlas, but that lacks AP (and Knockback) and is just 18"/36" - in our opinion, although it's only 40/1TP, the Long Rifle looks better. Just to note, the Long Rifle might be the strongest individual ranged weapon in the game that won't ever run Out of Ammo. 

The humble Heavy Stubber, once iconic of Necromunda in the 1990s, and neglected in N18, now looks better...compared to other Heavy weapons. At an unchanged S4, AP-1, and 20"/40" range, with RF(2), it can lay the hurt down, and it can be Reloaded when it does run dry (a 1/3 chance each time if fires). But as mentioned, that Heavy trait is a killer. When you do need to Reload, or when you get Suppressed, kiss your next Round of shooting goodbye. It's much cheaper at 70/2TP, but we just don't know.

Rad Weapons have that Rad-Phage Trait to reduce targets' Toughness...after they've taken an unsaved Wound. Apart from that they're S3, AP-1, not too impressive. The Rad Gun is a Template weapon, so gets Ammo(5+), and it's at least quite comparable to a flamer. We think the incendiary version is better, but it's not that big a gap. We're less impressed by the Rad Cannon, because that's Heavy, which for a Blast(3"), on that anaemic Strength and AP, doesn't spark joy. These aren't too expensive in credits, but are 3-4TP, so realistically will only be used by Van Saar.

The Harpoon Launcher is a great beneficiary of the changed cover rules, because it's AP-3. This makes its 6" short range less of a downside, it will usually be happy to shoot to its max range of 18" whether or not the target is in cover. Against that, it's Ammo(5+), so will usually stop and Reload between shots, and just L1. While it kept Drag, which is pretty much the same and only situationally useful, for dragging fools off of ledges, the Impale trait is gone from the game.

  • Genghis: as much as I fancied the idea of shish-kebobing multiple enemies with a single harpoon shot, I never once had the opportunity come up in a game, so I can't really summon a tear for this loss.

The mighty Mining Laser is still one of the punchiest weapons in the game, but it's only 10"/14" range, and it's Heavy. We can see the internal logic of making this weapon 125/3TP, but realistically, anyone wanting the effect is going to stretch to a very few more credits and take a meltagun, which is even scarier for a marginally shorter range. And of course the meltagun can benefit from a host of Skills (Hip Shooting!) and Attachments which the Mining Laser can't. Sad. 

The Las cutter was one of the most confusing weapons in N18, since it was a melee weapon with an Ammo Roll, a dissonance which we don't think was ever satisfactorily answered by FAQ. Now, it's a Light weapon with a tiny 2"/4" range, so mostly useful as a Secondary weapon in close combat. In that role it does excel, being S9, AP-2, L2 and Damage(2). It hits like a power fist. But it's Single Shot. Better hit with that one WS roll! Together with the high cost, that probably makes this a less serious choice. Fewer N26 close combats will be decided in one Activation, and this won't be any help after the first. 

Weapon Accessories

We can't remember if this was even a rule before, but a weapon can only have one of these attached.

  • Focussing Crystals can go on Las weapons, and give -1AP at the cost of Unstable. We probably wouldn't take that for free, let alone pay 25/1TP for the privilege. Hotshot las packs have the same cost and increase Strength by 1. Which is better, but still a bit underwhelming.
  • Infra-sights now only allow firing through Smoke, and extending Visibility by 9". Accordingly they go down to 10 credits. One to use only if you're doing smoke shenanigans, which will require finer judgment now a smoke grenade is only Blast(3").
  • A Las-projector reduces the Save benefit of cover by 1 when in the weapon's Short Range, while a Telescopic Sight does so at Long Range. At 20/1TP, these genuinely seem quite useful. If you put them on weapons you usually find yourself using at a certain range, e.g. a 6"/24" grenade launcher would prefer a Telescopic Sight, they're functionally close to -1AP upgrades.
  • A Mono-Sight means the weapon gets +2 to hit, not +1, when making an Aimed Shot(Double) Action. As we discussed in a similar-effect Skill, the one thing to watch out for here is that it will hardly ever matter if the user has BS3+ or better - you cap out at a 2+ to hit from a normal Aimed Shot, and penalties to hit are very rare.
  • Suspensors are the only upgrade which doesn't necessitate a Ranged weapon per se. They aren't the old, good suspensors from N18, these are only for weapons marked *, which take up 2 of a fighter's 3 weapon slots. The upgrade makes them only use 1 slot. At 40 credits, this is only for the real vanity picks in a late campaign.

Close Combat Weapons

Gorshiv Hammerfist painted by Crab-stuffed Mushrooms (Joey K)

We regret to inform you all that the Versatile trait is no more. RIP. In all seriousness, we can see why this change was made. Using a Versatile weapon always put models into a sort of Schrodinger’s close combat. By removing it, making melee attacks is only done within the Engaged state. Versatile was a lot of fun for the user, and less fun for their opponent. It also made weapons extremely hard to balance, because if played right, the upsides were so incredibly strong. 

More generally, close combat weapons got less punchy. Many were hurt by the loss of a +1 to-hit modifier, and unlike ranged weapons, this wasn’t partially offset by changes to cover, it just means the average effectiveness went down. There also seems to be a relative downturn in Strength and AP. 

Primitive or budget weapons have been rebalanced, but as with the cheapest guns, the differences in effect are so minor, that players really should feel free to use whichever they prefer aesthetically - it won’t make a major difference:
  • The humble Fighting Knife is now the cheapest melee weapon in the game at 5 credits (this is something of a call back to 90s Necromunda, where knives were the universal, basic close combat weapon). For that price, all you get is Backstab, and we suppose the ability to take another weapon, so they can get the bonus attack for Primary+Secondary. This certainly still ensures that stub gun/fighting knife loadouts will remain popular for raw Prospects.
  • The Flail goes from being perhaps the best cheap weapon in N18, to an alternative knife. It has Knockback(6+), which isn’t completely terrible, occasionally the user might Charge, knock their target back, and that target would be unable to swing back at them. But at 10 credits, we don’t see why anyone would take it over the Fighting Knife.
  • An Axe is 15 credits, with no Traits at all, but gives -1AP. This is probably the baseline choice for close combat fighters you want to keep cheap, but have some expectations of. 
  • Mauls are 20 credits and give S+1 and Concussive(6+). Our instinct is this isn’t as good as an axe, but unlike in N18, it’s not a comically poor choice. The real issue is the credit cost is starting to overlap with other close combat weapon categories, which generally cost 1TP, but will be available via Equipment Lists, and are clearly better.
  • The Two-handed axe and Two-handed hammer were both despised in N18 for penalising to-hit rolls. While that is gone, they both have Heavy and Unwieldy. So despite their relatively good stats, especially high Lethality, they will usually swing second, sometimes simultaneous with their opponent if Charging, and you could invest some of their price into a Secondary weapon instead, the extra attack of which would probably give comparable benefits. At 40/1TP, we don’t see the appeal here.

The Chainaxe and Chainsword both cost 20/1TP, and have unimpressive statlines - the chainsword is just an unimproved attack, and the chainaxe gets -1AP. The benefit is in the Traits. Both get Shred(5+), which actually means that most of their successful Wounds will be at L2. This is very attractive, more Injury dice is more better, but bear in mind that beating down multi-wound models is still unlikely. The chainsword also gets Parry. That means, unlike in N18, it’s dealers choice which of these weapons you prefer. Either of them seems like a sensible upgrade relative to the cheaper primitive weapons, and better than the Maul which shares their credit cost. 

Shock weapons all have their signature Trait and are another fairly budget category, but all cost 1TP, restricting their mass use by gangs which don’t have them on Equipment Lists:
  • The Shock whip, at just 10 credits, is now an oddly weak weapon for something so visually striking - it’s just an unimproved attack with Shock/Knockback ensuring it will drive enemies back 1” on a 6 to hit. That means it’s objectively superior to the Flail for the same credit cost, but it’s still a low powered weapon.
  • The Shock baton, for 20 credits, grants Parry, making it similar to the Chainsword. More reliably at Wounding, but with effectively less Lethality.
  • The Shock Stave lacks other Traits but doubles down on Shock(5+), so actually half or more of most models’ successful Hits will be auto-Wounds. That’s mechanically quite strong, but has an anti-synergy with the S+1 the weapon gives. For 25 credits we’re not sure if we like it.

The iconic Servo Claw retains its S+2, and has Lethality 2. However it is now Unwieldy. At 40/1TP, this seems very mid. Since it’s not Heavy, at least, it might be suitable for a brawling-type Fighters who is tough enough to take a hit in return. It’s at least looking a tiny bit better than the Two-handed axe/hammer, but that’s faint praise.

Toxin weapons are TP2, so really quite restrictive for most gangs (Escher will doubtless have equipment list access). The Stiletto knife is an unimproved attack except for Toxin(3+), the Stiletto Sword gets -1AP and Parry. Now at 25 and 45 credits respectively, the value here depends heavily on how much that fixed 3+ Wound roll means to you. If you’re fighting T3 models almost all the time, then competing weapons are probably a mite better. 

Power weapons, in a similar trend to most other close combat tools, mostly lost +1S from their N18 versions. They all retain at least -2AP, and the Breaching(6+) Trait, which will sometimes come in handy against especially well-armoured opponents.
  • A Power Knife is the entry point at 30/1TP, for which you get the AP, Breaching, and Backstab. Seems OK compared to cheaper weapons, provided your opponents are investing in some protective gear.
  • Power axes no longer get any Strength bonus, but are -3AP and Breaching(5+) - they’re can openers. At 40/1TP, this makes them dedicated can openers. They will only really come into their own against heavy armour, and it helps if the user has high Strength on their own.
  • Power Swords, as is traditional, get the Parry Trait, which is pretty great, especially as something that is still every bit as useful when your model is not the one Activating in combat. At 40/2TP, these are meant to be a bit rarer in the underhive, which thematically makes sense. 
  • Power Hammers are the last of the 40-credit (2TP) weapons, and maybe the best offensively, because on top of standard Power weapon things, they have Lethality 2. That’s not what D2 was in N18, but it’s still incredibly important. They also get Concussive(6+), which is fine we guess.
  • The humble Power Maul is now more expensive than most other power weapons, at 45/1TP. For that it gets S+1 and Concussive(6+). Seems fair enough, a reliable way to put Wounds through on most models.
  • Finally we have the mighty Power Fist, for a princely 105/3TP. This thing is S+3, AP-3, L2 & Damage(2), which is just amazing and by far the ceiling of weapons in the core rulebook. However, alongside Breaching(6+) and Concussive(5+) it has Unwieldy. This does mean that its users are going to have to risk enemy attacks in most situations before they can bring it to bear. Our initial impression is that’s a worthwhile risk. In the new Necromunda ecosystem, unwounded Leaders/Champions aren’t likely to go down in one close combat activation…unless you have something like this. Note that this isn’t Heavy, so it can be paired with a suitable Light weapon or close combat weapon for an attack at normal Initiative when you Activate. 

Armour

The armour ecosystem changed dramatically in Necromunda now that models have Saves built into their profile. Those start at 6+ on normal gangers, but some models will have higher, and any model can improve their Save if they roll very well on the 2D6 Advance table. We’d also note that a model’s max profile Save is 3+. RAW, we think that armour (and weapon Traits like Parry/Shield) can improve the Save target above that, with AP modifiers applied afterwards. But, an unmodified roll of 1-2 always fails. So being super heavily armoured is still possible, but requires lucky levelling up, and has a performance ceiling. More testing is definitely needed before we can make solid recommendations.

Whether you prefer the presentational aspects of Saves in profiles, one aspect of this change seems universally positive: N26 has broken the annoying system whereby everyone bought mesh armour, and if they couldn’t access it on fighter lists, they waited to buy it from the Trading Post. Now there are some proper choices. 

Although some of the accumulated protective gear from years of N18 rules has been purged, there are still a selection of armours available, some of which are very interesting.

  • Light Carapace armour is still eye-wateringly expensive at 100/1TP, improving a model’s save by 1 and reducing Initiative by 1. Heavy Carapace armour is even more dear at 140/3TP, shifts those stats by 2 in each direction, and also applies -1 to Agility Tests. Are these worthwhile? We think it depends on the model’s role. Initiative is used for fighting order in close combat, Consolidation distance, and for fighters with Bulging Biceps, moving before a Braced Shot. That seems like a manageable downside, negligible on most shooting models. But even then, 100 credits is a lot for a marginal survivability increase. Heavy Carapace is probably a better value-for-protection, provided you have the TP, but then you also want to be a bit more careful standing near ledges. RAW, we think a Heavy Carapaced model wielding an Unwieldy weapon strikes at Initiative 1 even when Charging. 
  • Mesh Armour is now 40 credits, with no TP cost, and adds +1 Save in close combat. A very interesting upgrade for purely melee-oriented fighters.
  • Hazard Suits are still just 10 credits and let you ignore Blaze and Rad-phage. But that’s not nearly as important in the current rules. We see these being taken only by players who know they will certainly be facing massed flamers and Rad weapons. 
  • Reflec Shrouds are 25/1TP and treat all Las, Plasma and Melta weapons as AP’-’. This seems incredibly strong and something lots of players will want to invest in on their stronger fighters. Plasma/Melta weapons are very scary and will doubtless be popular. Interestingly, we haven’t actually seen any Las weapons with AP yet (no lascannon in this book) but no doubt Van Saar have something up their sleeves.
  • Refractor fields are still field armour, and can explicitly be taken alongside other armour, providing a 5+ Invulnerable Save that burns out (unusable for the battle) on a natural 1. At 50 credits, in the current Save ecosystem, this might be a decent choice to give a survival chance against things like Melta and certain power weapons. 

Personal Equipment

Despite a pass over costs, the essential problem with most Necromunda equipment remains - it's a luxury that you buy after weapons, and usually after armour. But most of the really irrelevant kit has been removed, as well as the most esoteric and overpowered stuff. We're not going to go through these exhaustively, just the important bits:

  • Stimm Slug Stashes are mostly unchanged, and still very useful, but saw one big improvement: at the start of their next Activation, on a 1, the user takes a L1 Wound with no saves allowed. On a 1W fighter, or one already Injured, that's the same as before, you have to roll an Injury Dice. But an untouched Leader or Champion can now stimm up, and know they're not immediately at risk.
  • Grav Chutes, Drop Rigs and Grapnel Launchers are basically the same. Grapnels are clarified to require the move to end higher up than where the model started (as well as being in a straight line). No more using them as Movement enhancers on the flat!
  • Bio-Boosters work in the same way as before, and are down to 25 credits. These might actually be quite handy, especially as the comparable True Grit Skill is gone.
  • Photo-Goggles improve Visibility range by 9" and now correctly make the fighter more vulnerable to Flash weapons, which was a rules typo/mistake previously. Photo-Lumens extend Visibility by 9" but mean the user can always be seen up to any distance, which sounds like a hilariously bad idea.
  • Respirators now give a 5+ Invulnerable save against Gas weapons, which is OK but clearly not worth it unless you know you're facing them en masse.
  • Medicae Kits are down to 20 credits, and still allow an additional dice to be rolled when offering Assistance to an ally's Recovery test. Given the changes to the frequency of those tests, and likely importance of giving Assistance to good tactics, these seem like they might actually be useful.
  • Servo-Harnesses are basically the same, and in a world without good access to Toughness (or Strength) Advances, could be very popular vanity picks for late-campaign gangs.

Gang Equipment

There's precisely one piece of gang equipment in the game, and that's the Trazior Pattern Sentry Gun. These run 75/2TP with grenade launchers, and 110/2TP with heavy stubbers. These are deployed by the using gang, and have Arcs like vehicles. They will fire at models ending their Activations within their Front Arc (and Range and LoS). A friendly Fighter needs to be within 1", or the gun will fire at friendly models as well! These guns do have an overheating mechanic and a poor BS5+, but given they can be taken atop the normal crew rating, they are probably quite good if you have the right map and a fighter who can man the gun without wasting their own potential. To prevent shenanigans, gangs explicitly may not field more than 2 in a game.
