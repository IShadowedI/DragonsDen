Heroes of the Black Library: Commissar Yarrick

May 29th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Across the grim darkness of Warhammer 40,000, the Mortal Realms of Age of Sigmar, and the battlefields of The Old World, a ceaseless conflict rages between steadfast champions of order, terrifying architects of ruin, and everyone caught in between. Heroes of the Black Library is a deep dive into the incredible characters that make these universes so rich, from the tabletop to the bookshelf and beyond. Join us as we explore their bloody, larger-than-life tales, proving that in an eternity of carnage where there is no peace, the line between an inspiring hero and a monstrous villain is almost always forged in the fires of war. Happy reading!

Hello and welcome, folks, to the latest installment of our brand-new series: Heroes of the Black Library! Each will feature one of the heroes (or villains!) that have been brought to life in the pages of the Black Library, and with all eyes fixed firmly on a return to Armageddon what better time than now to look at Old Bale Eye himself, Commissar Sebastian Yarrick?

In a previous installment we tracked the career of Cadian Shock Trooper Minka Lesk as she rose up through the ranks across the novels of Justin D. Hill. With the novels taking place in chronological order that marked a fairly straightforward trajectory. Not so for Yarrick, whose stories from Canadian author David Annandale take place at different parts of the Commissar's legendary career.

First, though, let's take a quick detour through the Wars of Armageddon. Yarrick is very much a creature of his place and time in the Warhammer universe, so in order to fully appreciate his significance it's important to place him within it.

First War of Armageddon (444.M41)

While we tend to think 'Orks' when we hear Armageddon, the First War was actually against Angron (by now a Daemon Primarch) and his World Eaters when they staged an invasion of the planet. They were defeated by the combined forces of the Imperial Guard, Logan Grimnar and the Space Wolves, and a contingent of Grey Knights.

This tale is magnificently told in Aaron Dembski-Bowden's 2012 novel The Emperor's Gift, for those looking for a bit more reading. We've also got a terrific Lore Explainer for you as well!

Second War of Armageddon (941.M41)

Almost five centuries later came a day when an aged Imperial Commissar attached to the planet's Steel Legion was preparing to cap off his long and noteworthy career with a well-deserved retirement. What better timing for the arrival of a massive Orkish invasion, a Waaagh! under the mighty Ghazghkull Thraka? That Commissar, of course, was Sebastian Yarrick.

Yarrick was familiar with the threat the Orks posed, and moved to mobilize all defenses. However, he ran into a roadblock posed by the Planetary Overlord, Herman von Strab, who dismissed Yarrick's concerns as a gross overreaction. Yarrick sidestepped the chain of command, ordering the astropaths to send out a distress signal pleading for reinforcements. When von Strab learned he'd been circumvented, he banished Yarrick to Hades, an industrial manufactorum hive in the path of the Ork onslaught.

von Strab expected Yarrick to die. Instead, he became a legend.

It was during the defense of Hades Hive that Yarrick, who had lost his eye years before in an accident, lost his right arm in battle with an Ork Warboss, Ugulhard. Slaying Ugulhard, Yarrick completed his iconic appearance by appropriating the Warboss's own Klaw.

Meanwhile, Yarrick's signal had been heard. A force of Astartes, including the Blood Angels, Ultramarines, and Salamanders, arrived to help push back the Orks, and Ghazghkull Thraka fled in defeat.

Third War of Armageddon (998.M41)

Fifty-even years later, Ghazghkull Thraka returned to seek his revenge at the head of the largest Ork fleet ever assembled. Commissar Yarrick is pulled out of retirement to head the planetary defense, bolstered by Marshal Helbrecht and the Black Templars, other Astartes Chapters including the Salamanders, and millions of Astra Militarum troops.

Back to Dembski-Bowden for a moment, his Helsreach is a sublime telling of the tale, and I particularly recommend this one in audiobook due to Jonathan Keeble's masterful narration.

As the war ground down into a stalemate, Ghazghkull Thraka decided to leave the attrition game to his subordinates and continue his mission to bring Orkdom under one banner. Yarrick and Helbrecht, realizing that until the Prophet of the Waaagh! was killed there would never be peace, took to the stars in pursuit.

The "Death" of Yarrick

Commissar Yarrick never had an "on-screen" death. Rather, the belief he had perished were due to a number of very convincing bits of circumstantial evidence, which included:
  • His removal from the 9th Edition Codex: Astra Militarum
  • A notation in the Codex that the Bell of Lost Souls had tolled for him on Terra (something done to mark his passing)
  • Nate Crowley's 2022 novel, Ghazghkull Thraka: Prophet of the Waaagh!, showing a distraught Thraka reacting to reports of the reputed death of his rival
As we learned this year, reports of his death were indeed greatly exaggerated. But that story is still being written, so here are the ones that already have.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Chains of Golgotha (2013)

The first of Annandale's major Yarrick works, it actually takes place the latest in the timeline. Chains of Golgotha sees Yarrick pursuing Thraka into the starts during the Third War of Armageddon, where he is ultimately captured by the Ork forces.

Imprisoned in a labor camp on the Ork world of Golgotha, Yarrick organizes a rebellion amongst the human slaves and workers, setting up one of the most iconic Yarrick/Threaka confrontations in the lore.

I swung my legs over the edge of the table and stood. My injuries had blended into a general wash of pain. Nothing was broken, though. I was intact. I could walk. I approached the door.

 

It opened. I stopped. Beyond it, orks lined both sides of the corridor. They had been watching for me. The moment I appeared, they roared their approval. They did not attack. They simply stood, clashed guns against blades, and hooted brute enthusiasm...

 

I walked. It was the most obscene victory march of my life. I moved through corridor, hold and bay, and the massed ranks of the greenskins hailed my passage...

 

Ghazghkull Mag Uruk Thraka awaited me beside the ship’s access ramp. I did not let my confusion or the sense that I had slipped into an endless waking nightmare slow my stride. I did not hesitate as I strode towards the monster. I stopped before him. I met his gaze with all the cold hatred of my soul. He radiated delight. Then he leaned forward, a colossus of armour and bestial strength. Our faces were mere centimetres apart.

 

Thraka spoke to me.

 

Not in orkish. Not even in Low Gothic.

 

In High Gothic.

 

‘A great fight,’ he said. He extended a huge, clawed finger and tapped me once on the chest. ‘My best enemy.’ He stepped aside and gestured to the ramp. ‘Go to Armageddon,’ he said. ‘Make ready for the greatest fight.’

Image credit: Games Wortshop

Imperial Creed (2014)

From the end to the beginning, we move back to learn of Commissar Yarrick's origin story in Imperial Creed. This much younger Yarrick has completed his training and been assigned to his first regiment, the 252nd Armageddon Steel Legion. An uprising on the hive world of Mistral requires suppression, but soon after arrival Yarrick learns that there's something far more sinister behind it.

Faced with heretical Chaos cults, Yarrick must use every skill at his disposal to both fight the heresy before him as well as navigate between the Guard, the planetary political system, and the Adepta Sororitas.
"The truth was this: the Imperial Creed is a faith of many facets. It is a faith of discipline, of fire, of vengeance. Of will. It has nothing to do with hope." -Commissar Yarrick
Image credit: Games Workshop

The Pyres of Armageddon (2015)

Finally, The Pyres of Armageddon puts us squarely in the Orkish siege of Hive Hades, the seminal event for Yarrick in the Second War of Armageddon. This is a Yarrick at the peak of his powers- but not yet quite aware of the role the Orks would play in his destiny and his future.

The book is a grim, grinding siege narrative of attrition, as Yarrick must assume authority over the hive's civilian population as well as its Planetary Defense Force garrison. Here too we see Yarrick's iconic duel with Warboss Ugulhard- the one where he loses his right arm- as he tries to hold out long enough for salvation to come in the form of the Blood Angels.

Three books. Three different Yarricks- but one extraordinary story.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Other Appearances

As one of Warhammer 40,000's most iconic characters, it's little surprise that Yarrick casts a long shadow. In addition to the above stories, the Yarrick Omnibus contains a number of other short stories by Annandale featuring the Commissar. He was featured in an episode of WarhammerTV's Hammer and Bolter animated anthology (Old Bale Eye), which sees an Ork's-eye-view of a run-in with him.

Finally, bits of Yarrick can be found woven throughout 40K's many stories, such as this from Gav Thorpe's Last Chancers:

'Hades Hive,' says Fenn, bowing his head and making the sign of the eagle across his chest.

'There's nothing left,' says Brownie. 'On the contrary, millions of refugees, soldiers, Space Marines and orks are out there,' says the Colonel. 'It is still a constant battle for control of the ruins. Commissar Yarrick declared that Hades would never fall. Blood is spilt to ensure that he does not have to break his word.'

The Author

David Annandale came to the Black Library from an academic background, and as the Senior Instructor in the Department of English, Theatre, Film & Media at the University of Manitoba he had a heavy background in horror. That particular sensibility was evident in some of the body-horror elements in his first novel, The Death of Antagonis- and would later make him a natural fit for the Warhammer Horror line (with The House of Night and Chains a particularly memorable addition). 

Yarrick offered him the opportunity to write something different. In an interview with Jonathan Ball, David Annandale explained how he came to begin telling Yarrick's story.
In the case of of Yarrick, I was approached by Nick [Kyme] to to write the Yarrick books. We had a a skype meeting talking about what directions we might take and what the first one would be, and since that was going to be of novella length it had to be an incident that was relatively contained.
I think in fact it was his suggestion to look at events on Golgotha, but I had been long been fascinated by it. The entry in the rulebook reads as, "in this way Commissar Yarrick and Warlord Ghazghkull finally come to face to face on the battlefields of Golgotha. Yarrick is captured but ultimately released by Ghazghkull...as the warlord is planning to invade Armageddon once more and wants a good fight."
And so I'd always been fascinated by that entry and wondering well what's the story here?
Since these early offerings, Annandale has grown to be one of the Black Library's more prolific talents, writing for 40K (Ephrael Stern: The Heretic Saint, Warden of the Blade), the Horus Heresy (Mortarion: The Pale King, Vulkan: Lord of Drakes), and Age of Sigmar (Callis & Toll, The Dominion of Bones).
His most recent Warhammer novel is Apostle, which we reviewed here. And while there's plenty of Yarrick on the written page, for those whose preferences run to the tabletop there's been no shortage of sightings there as well!

Image credit: Games Workshop

From Paper to Plastic

Those looking to add Old Bale Eye to their tabletop forces or have him standing vigil over their library are in luck- Games Workshop has produced him three times in miniature over the years. What's especially fun about walking the history of a character as storied as Yarrick is that he's sort of a time capsule of how the miniatures line has evolved over time.

1992: Metal

Take, for instance, his first model (shown above on the right). While largely relegated to made-to-order nostalgia now, this was a time when Games Workshop was making its slow transition away from metal miniatures. At that time, while boxes of core troops were typically now plastic, individual units- like Yarrick- were released in metal.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Yarrick wasn't birthed into the world in a vacuum, as 1992 was also a foundational year for the Armageddon setting itself. Designed by Jervis Johnson as part of the Wargame Series, Battle for Armageddon was packed with the Imperium-versus-Ork action the setting is nearly synonymous with from the Second War.

To bring the good Commissar (as well his nemesis, Ghazghkull Thraka) into 40,000 proper, Games Workshop rolled out the official rules profiles for both in White Dwarf #152 (August 1992). Sculpted by Jes Goodwin, the original Yarrick featured the earlier, lobster-esque version of his Power Klaw and from skulls to banner left little doubt as to the nature of the enemy he is fighting.

2000: Metal to Resin

Games Workshop next returned to Armageddon in 2000 with the game's Third Edition, which saw not only the release of Codex: Armageddon, but also saw the hosting of a mass participation event. Players were encouraged to play out the Armageddon scenarios on tabletop, then report their results to a special website. The results, taken over time, would influence the unfolding narrative of the overall story lore1, and if Armageddon looms large in the Warhammer 40,000 of today, the year 2000 is a significant reason why.

Image credit: Games Workshop

Naturally, such an event couldn't go without an updated Commissar Yarrick miniature, and Gary Morley produced an updated sculpt. Morley revised the Klaw to be more crablike and industrial, better representing the Orkish standard of the day, and also made Yarrick's Bale Eye more prominent. If Goodwin's sculpt reflected the transition from the Rogue Trader era into the 90's, Morley injected the good Commissar with an added layer of grimdark aesthetic (as you can see from the picture above, with this version on the left).

Not only did these miniatures serve as representations of the game's shifting aesthetic, but their manufacturing process as well. The Yarrick of 2000 was initially offered in metal, then later brought back as a finecast resin as Games Workshop sought to pivot to the medium in 2011 in the wake of increased metal production costs and shipping.

Image credit: Games Workshop

2026: Plastic

It's a testament, perhaps, to the Morley sculpt that the 2026 update didn't mess with the miniature's essential elements. The dynamic pose, the aura of command, and his characteristic armaments all were refreshed, but otherwise left alone. This most recent miniature is also notable for being offered in plastic, in line with the modern suite of offerings, and we took recently a dive into his tabletop datasheet here.

All three (or four, if you're a completionist) would look terrific as part of your Black Library collection, especially as part of a Yarrick-themed display. Here are some other things to go with it!

Image credit: Games Workshop

Shelf Candy

One thing I haven't talked enough of is the idea of the Black Library planting seeds for future releases. We see this in Magic: the Gathering, where cards will be released that hint at future designs- even if it isn't obvious at the time, but it holds true in Warhammer as well. We might not have known last November that Renegades: Harrowmaster by Mike Brooks was getting a sequel when it was reissued in a Special Edition- but Games Workshop certainly did.

Similarly, in hindsight it seems entirely unsurprising that Commissar Yarrick's canon of work would be getting a special Box Set release as we approach the game's 11th Edition and a return to Armageddon...but the set was released last October. All the same, what better place to start than here?

While the official picture looks a bit flat, in person these books have a much richer appearance thanks to the cover felting- it's a much more tactile black. Originally offered at USD $170, you'll only find these on the aftermarket now.

Image credit: Games Workshop

The recent Armageddon: The Return of Yarrick release was also given a Collector's Edition treatment, visible above on the left. These typically clock in and double the retail sticker of their standard-version releases, but there's certainly a market for them!

Image credit: Merchoid

Merchoid offers a number of officially-licensed Warhammer 40K ingots, and they're surprisingly heavy. You might only piss off an Ork if you stuffed one of these in a sock and hit 'em over the head with it, but you'd probably knock out a grot. At $38 they're much more affordable, and I have one in the Museum.

Image credit: Games Workshop

This was May's collectible coin of the month, which are offered at official Warhammer stores as a free reward for spending over a certain amount (typically $100). With Yarrick currently in the frame and this coin being a recent release, prices on the secondary market are a bit inflated at the moment but in general most of these can be had for under $25 if you're patient.

Image credit: WarhammerArt.com

Finally, if you'd rather Old Bale Eye stare at you from the wall of your study or library, Warhammer Art has some limited-edition prints available starting at around $55.

Being honest, I'm not sure Warhammer Art has quite captured the magic that's allowed Displate to upsell its own art prints (see my review of the premium Sanguinius one here). The print is lovely, of course, but all you get for the "limited edition" is hand-numbering and a certificate of authenticity.

I hope you've enjoyed the latest Heroes of the Black Library feature. For our next installment, we're gonna be sticking around Armageddon just a little bit longer...
