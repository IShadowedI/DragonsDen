Trench Crusade Basics: Using Elite Models in Court of the Seven Headed Serpent

September 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The Court of the Seven Headed Serpent has a bit of a reputation as the most Elite heavy force available in Trench Crusade. And while you do not need to funnel more than half the Ducats of your entire warband into a few models, you certainly have the option! However, when you pour so many resources (and hopes and dreams) into such a small number of models, it can feel absolutely devastating when one or more of them is taken out of action before they can really pull their own weight. So today we will go over all four of the Elite options for the Court and give some Sin-agnostic advice and best practices to try and get the most out of your monsters!

Court Praetor Credit: Darius Minis

Praetor

The Praetor is the largest model that you can field other than the Desecrated Saint. It is a powerhouse with one of the most terrifying stat lines in the entire game. It is also very difficult to keep it alive long enough to really make a difference in your game as unless you are careful to make sure that your opponent doesn't have lots of sight lines to it then it will very often be the target of most incoming firepower. It is tough. It has Tough. It is not tough enough to be thrown an the enemy and expected to survive. While it looks like a boss monster, it is most effectively used as a brutal flanker or assassin of opportunity. While each individual Sin has a few tricks that they can add, most builds of the Praetor are either melee focused or ranged focused. While it absolutely has the stats to be a brutal hybrid threat, that is adding ever more cost to an already ducat hungry model and raises the skill floor to use them properly.

Melee focus is usually a fairly simple loadout, the Malbrance is practically a requirement if you can afford it. Swinging with a free bloodbath every turn is very tasty and helps you slice through damn near anything your Praetor can get into contact with. The real question is, do you double down on melee murder with a Headtaker or other one handed weapon, or do you take a shield to try and increase durability. Personally, I like to live dangerously and always go with the second weapon. You aren't running a tank, you are running the largest flying assassin available. You should not be throwing them at something that will live long enough to hit back. What you should be doing is lurking up a weak flank or near the center with a musical instrument nearby. A 20 inch melee threat bubble (8 inch dash movement and a 12 inch charge) with flying can cover a surprisingly large chunk of the board, and surprise is something you really want to have on your side.

Ranged specialization is a little less free choice. One of Courts biggest weaknesses, for flavor anyway, is that fact that its armory can feel very prescriptive. Every weapon has a “Correct” unit to use it. And for a ranged Praetor the correct weapon is the Serpent Assault Gun. This is a beast of a weapon, giving two shots per activation that can really pour on the Blood with Gas and Automatic 2. It also lives up to its name with Assault, letting you swoop in to melee to finish something or tie up a much weaker unit if you desperatley need to. Several Sins also have Goetic Powers that can be used to increase ranged firepower or augment your mobility. And mobility is the name of the game with a range focused Praetor. You will want to start an activation out of line of sight of your target, dash out 8 inches of flying movement to get into a firing position and then move back out of line of sight or charge chaff as needed. Playing fair is for the Faithful, don't let them shoot you if you can help it.

Hunter of the Left Hand Path Credit: Darius Minis

Hunter of the Left Hand Path

The Hunter may very well be the single best model in the game. Mobility and movement win wargames. The Hunter can teleport once per turn, touch any terrain piece on the board and come out of any other terrain piece. No ranged limit, doesn't end its activation. This is mind bogglingly useful. Get from safety to any sniping spot you need, grab any objective on the board, you name it and you can do it. You will pay a premium of 2 blood markers per turn to do so, but it is always worth it. Speaking of blood, once per turn the Hunter may spend 3 blood to activate its Oracle Beast Cloak. After it has suffered an injury roll, usually an out of action result, you can activate the cloak to change the result to No Effect. This makes the Hunter one of the most durable models around if you can keep up with its blood guzzling spell use. As for equipment, you will always be stuck with its signature ranged weapon, the Bow of Lethe. Frankly you would not want to change it; it has two ammo types that either give bonus injury dice or ignore armor, a decent range and has assault if you want to get your hunter in melee after a shot with it. Speaking of melee, you generally give a Hunter the best armor you can and either add a Headtaker for that one two punch of bow and melee, or give it a shield to make it as durable as possible. It depends on how daring you plan on being with the Hunter, as both are perfectly viable. If you want to get a little unusual with it, I have personally had decent success with giving the Hunter my warbands musical instrument, ensuring they can always dash and that I can teleport the dash bonus to where it is needed.

Sorcerer
Credit: Darius Minis

Sorcerer

Burning Inferno. Without going into specifics of what each Sin can offer you, it's a wizard and every problem looks like it can be answered via an expanding orb of flame and hate. The Court's version of an Artillery Witch, but less likely to end up on merchandise. Now it is not just the inferno, you can fit three magic spells into these bad boys and that leave two slots. Heck, even the other generic spell, Slavemaster, is close to must-take. You spend a blood, and command a nearby yoke fiend with a torture implement to “entertain” a nearby wretched and boom, you now have 2 blood markers to spend! It's free money, really. Now one thing to note, your built in spell, Blessing of the Serpent Moon, means you can spend blood to give a stacking -1 injury modifier to an incoming injury roll. This stacks with any armor worn, meaning you cannot get a total higher than -3, so if you are rocking that Reinforced Armor and Shield combo, you will not bother using this spell unless something is ignoring your armor.

So that gives you one extra spell from your chosen Sin to play around with, but in general you want to keep your sorcerer on the back line and chucking armor ignoring blasts across the field. They do not possess tough, so a single lucky bolt action rifle shot can take them out. So like your Ranged Praetor you wanna keep it hidden when possible.

Hellknights
Credit: Darius minis

Hellknights

Hellknights are the closest you get to a blank slate in the Court as far as elite units go. They are one of the only models that has armor that cannot be ignored, with an unstoppable -2 armor mod built in to their profile. While they do not have much of a ranged stat at all, they are not stuck only with melee. They make a perfect long range harassment unit with the Ophidian Rifle. By inverting the laws of physics, said rifle gains a bonus when it's targets are at long range or in cover instead of a negative. Combine this with a Hunter or Sorcerer and your opponent will have ranged pressure that they cannot easily escape, as they are punished for staying in cover and being out of cover can have brutal consequences from your more traditional ranged threats.

When it comes to melee you have the usual dilemma of all Strong models. Do you take two weapons, using Strong to have a usually two handed in one hand as well as a normal one handed weapon. Or do you take said two hander and a shield? Well, as you can take three Hellknights, I suggest trying both and seeing which matches your particular needs. One thing I suggest for all melee focused Hellknights is to take advantage of their special discount on Unholy Relics, as this gives them Fear. While this is mostly a defensive boost in melee, it also means they ignore fear themselves! Thus making them more useful into the larger and scarier threats that you might want them to deal with.

Final Thoughts

The Court of the Seven Headed Serpent has some of the most dangerous single models in the game, but if you don't pilot them correctly they can be disappointingly fragile. That, however, is just part of Trench Crusade; anything can die to anything else at any time. However, if you use them wisely, you can make all of your Elite models a real pain to deal with. Now, each Sin will be able to tweak and twist all of these ideas as well (other than the Hunter, they are always the same lethal assassin), so consider these to be broad archetypes that you should consider when building your personal forces rather than a specific recipe you need to follow.
