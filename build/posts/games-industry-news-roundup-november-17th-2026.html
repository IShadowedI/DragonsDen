Games Industry News Roundup- November 17th, 2026

November 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Games Workshop

Veteran Warhammer Miniatures Sculptor Aly Morrison Passes Away

We’re opening this week with some sad news. Aly Morrison, one of the original sculptors for Games Workshop, passed away last week.

“Mharaid and I are heartbroken to announce the passing of Aly Morrison, a very much loved father, veteran wargames miniatures designer and friend to many around the tabletop and the world. Aly passed unexpectedly but peacefully this Wednesday, and will be sorely missed.” Trish Carden, Morrison’s ex-wife and former studio partner, wrote on Facebook last week to announce his passing.

Morrison joined Games Workshop in 1983 as a sculptor, and since then has been responsible for the design of a large number of iconic models, including the original dwarf slayers during his tenure at Marauder Miniatures, a spin off model company from Games Workshop that was eventually folded back into the studio. Morrison would remain an active member of the Games Workshop design team until his retirement earlier this year.

Games Workshop released an official statement on his passing to commemorate Morrison, writing “Aly was a pioneering miniatures designer, whose creativity and talent helped shape Warhammer as we know it across five decades. From Dwarfs to Giants, Space Marines to the Red Gobbo – his imagination and craftsmanship have inspired generations of collectors, painters and gamers.”

“Aly’s kindness and generosity as a mentor have left an indelible mark on the Warhammer Studio. Humble, magnanimous and with an incorrigible wit, to sit beside Aly and hear his wisdom and love of Warhammer miniatures design, was to be in the presence of a master. No matter your level of experience, he always had something insightful to add, to help make the creation ‘more Warhammer’.”

Morrison’s cause of death has not been officially stated, and though he did suffer from chronic fibromyalgia, it is unknown if the condition played any part in his death. No matter what though, Morrison’s tenure at Games Workshop helped design a game enjoyed by thousands all over the world, and will be sorely missed by not only the fans, but his family and friends that loved him.

Credit: Future Pastimes/Eon Productions

Jack Kittenridge, Creator of Cosmic Encounter, Dies at 82

Another titan of the tabletop industry passed away last week as well. One of the original co-designers of the popular board games Cosmic Encounter and Dune, Jack Kittenridge, Died in his home on Wednesday at age 82. 

Kittenridge was born in Baltimore in 1944, and in the seventies would become one of the founding members of Future Pastimes (originally Eon Products), the game design collective which included his Cosmic Encounter co-creators Peter Olotka, Bill Eberle and Bill Norton. Cosmic Encounter was released by Future Pastimes in 1977, with their other most popular game, Dune, releasing in 1979, and both were early pioneers in asymmetric gameplay.

Both games would not only go on to become massively popular board games even decades after release, with Cosmic Encounter getting a new edition in 2008 and Dune releasing a new edition in 2019, but have been cited by several as incredibly influential in shaping the games industry as a whole. Most famously, Cosmic Encounter was cited by Richard Garfield, the creator of Magic: The Gathering, as the biggest influence on the card game’s design. Garfield cited that the unique gameplay of each alien race made each game interesting and exciting, even after dozens and dozens of playthroughs, and that this aspect of Cosmic Encounter became a core design tenant of Magic.

Kittenridge would move on from games design full time into organic farming with his wife, Julie, founding the company Many Hands Organic Farm in 1984, though he would still contribute to games design over the years. He is survived by his wife and four children, who will dearly miss him, alongside friends and fans all over the world who have either enjoyed the games he designed or would influence.

Credit: United Wizards of the Coast

UWOTC Responds to Hasbro CEO’s Anti-Union Comments

The ongoing battle for unionization at Wizards of the Coast heated up last week, with the CEO of Hasbro making dismissive comments about the effectiveness of unions days before the Magic and D&D teams would officially vote to unionize.

On the WBUR Breakfast Club radio show, Cocks shared his thoughts on the unionization efforts being made by the D&D and Magic: The Gathering teams at Wizards of the Coast. While he recognized the workers right to unionize, he defended the working conditions at Wizards, as well as citing that their wages at the company were much higher than industry standard, and that the best way to protect their positions was to make the best possible product.

“I just want to make sure we can innovate and deliver for our consumers as best as possible,” Cocks told WBUR. “That's going to be the best way we can not just protect the people who are on the team, but grow the number of people who are on that team.”

The UWOTC responded to this on Bluesky, writing “Ahead of our union vote, we’re here to correct the record on key statements Chris shared about UWOTC-CWA that were both incorrect and clearly anti-union.”

UWOTC noted that while Cocks claims to be union neutral, Hasbro has engaged in anti-unionization tactics by hiring the union avoidance firm, Fisher Phillips, for a second time. Fisher Phillips was originally retained by Hasbro during the MtG Arena’s unionization efforts earlier this year. UWOTC also states that the average wage mentioned by Cox, $140,000, is much higher than the actual average wage at Wizards.

Finally, the UWOTC pushed back on Cocks’s comments on how the team can create the best product they can to protect their job, writing “Unions are how tabletop workers ensure that we are treated with the respect that we deserve. We want to continue making the games we love. When we hold our union election, we believe we will win and take the first steps toward a better Wizards of the Coast.”

Both the Magic and D&D teams held their union election vote last week, though we likely won’t know the official results until early December.

Credit: Awaken Realms

Nemesis: Legacy Breaks Board Game Crowdfunding Record

Some fun new to break things up here, the extremely popular Nemesis series of board games has once again made crowdfunding history with its most recent outing, becoming the most funded board game campaign of all time at over $14 million.

Legacy is the most recent iteration of the Nemesis series, where players take on the role of space colonists and marines trying to survive being hunted by dangerous alien predators. Like other legacy-style games, Nemesis: Legacy intends to tell a story over multiple games, and develop and grow with each game played.

The campaign for Legacy was launched on GameFound by Nemesis publisher Awaken Realms on 10/6/2026 with a goal of €50,000, a goal that the campaign soared past within the first minute of going live on the site. Since then, Nemesis: Legacy has continued to explode in popularity, with its final number of around $14.8 million raised putting it comfortably at the top of the all time board game crowdfunding charts. Legacy is followed by Frosthaven, which held the top spot for many years by raising just under $13 million in 2020.

While Boardgamewire notes the ongoing volatility in the crowdfunding space, particularly in the board game sphere, it’s hard to not be impressed by what Awaken Realms has done here, and hopefully the team can deliver to make Nemesis: Legacy something truly special.

Credit: Chaosium

Chaosium Halts Forbidden Stars Revival After Original Designer Convicted of Child Abuse

The revival of the cult classic board game Forbidden Stars, under the new title Call of Cthulu: Old Ones Origins, has been put on ice by Chaosium after one of the game’s chief designers was convicted last week of multiple counts of child sex crimes and child abuse. 

Chaosium wrote in an official update on the game’s crowdfunding page that “On Friday afternoon (PT) Chaosium was made aware that Samuel William Bailey was convicted in Skagit County. He was convicted that day of multiple counts of 1st degree rape of a minor and child molestation, crimes that carry the possibility of life imprisonment. Until we were contacted on Friday afternoon, this is the first time we had ever heard of this matter. We received official confirmation today and wanted to give an update.”

“These are very serious crimes, and have significant legal implications on the Old Ones Origins board game, beyond the serious moral implications. We have stopped all development on this project.”

Chaosium further notes that it has reached out to Gamefound to cancel any StretchPay payments that may hit backers bank accounts following this decision, though some charges may still go through due to the announcement coming on a Sunday. The company has pledged to work with their IT department on refunding any of these payments, as well as working with the company’s legal counsel on how to move forward with the project following the news.

Bailey was one of the co-designers of the original Forbidden Stars game at Fantasy flight in 2015. The game was based in the universe of Warhammer 40,000, and allowed for players to take on the role of one of four factions battling out for supremacy on the galactic scale. Chaosium would revive Forbidden Stars with much of the original team and move it away from the Warhammer IP, instead rebranding as Call of Cthulhu: Old Ones Origins. Chaosium launched a crowdfunding campaign to support the development of Old Ones Origins, which eventually raised $770 thousand in pledges before its campaign closed.

Charges against Bailey were filed in 2023, and during that time Bailey was actively working on both the development and promotion of Old Ones Origins at conventions, which Bailey had to receive court permission to travel to. Chaosium claims that they had no idea of any of the allegations or charges against Bailey during this time.

A parent of one of the victims, who wished to remain anonymous, told Boardgamewire that they believed that Chaosium was also a victim of Bailey’s lies, which could’ve been prevented by a thorough background check. “I believe Sam deceived them like he deceived most people in his life. He took advantage of his standing in the board game community knowing that most companies would not perform thorough background checks and see he had active charges for child rape and molestation.”

The current status of the Old Ones Origins campaign is currently unknown at time of writing.
