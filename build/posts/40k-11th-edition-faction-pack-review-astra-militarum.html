[40k] 11th Edition Faction Pack Review: Astra Militarum

June 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer
With a new edition of Warhammer 40K comes a whole new bunch of Detachments, and we’re handling them in groups to make it easier to separate the wheat from the chaff, at least for now. We’ll go through the relevant changes to the Datasheets and rules at play, then see how the Detachments themselves stand up.

Thank you again to Games Workshop for providing us with a copy of these rules.

The loyal forces of the Astra Militarum have had a pretty great run in 10th Edition, consistently performing well off the back of a rock solid set of Datasheets in their Codex, augmented by some really good digital Detachments at various points. The Emperor’s most numerous forces are thus riled up and ready to charge forth into 11th, so let’s see what it has in store for them.

Datasheet Changes

  • A gigantic list of tanks and Fortifications gains FRAME.
  • The Cadian Command Squad gets given two different replacement versions of Cadia Stands. One gives them battle-shock re-rolls within range of an objective, while the other gives the unit +1Sv while within range of an objective. I know which one I’d prefer.
  • Death Riders reactive move changes to trigger at 8”.
  • Gaunt’s Ghosts set up distance changes to 8”.
  • The Balance Dataslate change to Krieg Combat Engineers’ Remote Mine becomes Errata.
  • The Balance Dataslate change to make Tempestus Aquilons have a minimum 6” Set Up Distance becomes Errata.

One thing that we’re slightly surprised by is that unlike other Aircraft transports, the Valkyrie stays an Aircraft rather than getting a full re-work. May be a second pass of this coming.

Rule Changes

  • Swift Interception in Mechanised Assault now triggers at 8”.
  • Draw Them Out in Recon Element now triggers at 8”.
  • Tanglefoot Grenades now targets a visible enemy unit within 12” of one of your units, and subtracts 1 from their Charge Rolls. Less impact, but can’t be bypassed by simply not charging the unit that threw the grenade.

Available Detachments

The Militarum have the following Detachments to work with:

3 DP Detachments
  • Combined Arms (Take and Hold)
  • Grizzled Company (Priority Assets)
  • Recon Element (Recon) (duh)
2 DP Detachments
  • Armoured Infantry (Take and Hold)
  • Hammer of the Emperor (Purge the Foe)
  • Mechanised Assault (Purge the Foe)
  • Siege Regiment (Disruption)
  • Steel Hammer (Purge the Foe)
1 DP Detachments
  • Abhuman Auxiliaries (Take and Hold)
  • Bridgehead Strike (Priority Assets)
  • Designation Force (Recon)

No massive surprises in terms of which Guard Detachments get hit with the 3DP cost, and Grizzled in particular is strong enough that people probably just roll on with using it, especially as building for PA should be pretty feasible in the faction. In terms of using the new tools, standout combos would seem to be either Hammer Detachment with Designation Force to add some utility support, or Mechanised Assault combined with Bridgehead Strike for a really lethal Taurox Prime-heavy build. You could also consider adding Abhuman Auxiliaries to a Hammer list to give one big unit of Bullgryn some tarpit power.

Abhuman Auxiliaries - Take and Hold

Credit: Keewa

Unsurprisingly, this is a Detachment focused on enhancing Ogryn, Bullgryn and Ratlings.

Detachment Rule

This Detachment has the ABHUMAN tag, and cannot be taken with another ABHUMAN Detachment.

Bullgryn, Ogryns and Ratlings gain the ABHUMAN keyword.

This is then used for the Detachment’s actual rule - friendly COMISSAR models can issue the Take Aim Order, and can order friendly ABHUMAN units.

This is pretty sweet. As anyone familiar with Grizzled Company’s exploits will know, Ordering Bullgryn is a really potent option to have access to, and this version isn’t even tied to a single Commissar. This version also gives you access to Take Aim, which feels like it’s meant to encourage you to Order Ratlings, but is much more likely to be thrown at Ogryns, because their ripper guns are extremely nasty, and only going to get better in 11th where AP-1 matters more.

The one sad thing here is that only baseline vanilla Commissars actually have the Commissar keyword, so you can’t get extra funky with Graves for Move Move Move.

Enhancements

  • Sharp Eyes, Light Fingers (Upgrade): Upgrades a Ratling unit, and gives enemy models +6” detection range when the Ratlings are shooting. Probably not masses here - Ratlings mostly turn up as a single small and extremely sacrificial unit. I guess if you wanted, you could take a big squad with this to sit on your expansion and snipe to the central objective while using Low Profile, but that seems like a lot of steps.
  • Enhancement Two (Upgrade): Give a COMISSAR model a 4+ Feel No Pain. Sure, if the price is right - you’ll definitely want to keep them alive in this Detachment.

Stratagems

  • Thick-Skulled Obdurance (1CP): Armour of Contempt for Bullgryn/Ogryn units that are on an Objective, giving a volley of incoming attacks -1AP. Pretty useful to have access to, particularly for Ogryn who you do legitimately want to take a 6-model unit of if you can Take Aim them.
  • Low Profile (1CP): Lets a Ratling unit shoot and remain Hidden. Theoretically cute, practically almost never worth a CP.
  • Stirred to Action (1CP): Lets you make a d6” Surge Move with Bullgryn or Ogryn after they have been shot. Definitely puts the fear of Bullgryn properly into the foe.

Is It Good?

It’s okay, but not super exciting. You could certainly take this with six Bullgryn, six Ogryn and a Commissar and get some value out of it, but that’s a reasonably high commitment and the payoff is merely decent.

Bridgehead Strike - Priority Assets

Militarum Tempestus Scions Command Squad. Credit: Rockfish

Bridgehead Strike returns in a new svelte 1DP package, providing boosts for the Militarum Tempestus in particular, and also a few tricks for other units.

Detachment Rule

If you have a Militarum Tempestus Officer as your Warlord, Tempestus Scions gain Battleline and +1OC.

Whenever a Militarum Tempestus unit is selected to shoot, if they were set up this turn, they get +1 to hit. This obviously improves the output of your Scions when you’re either throwing them out of the sky or a Taurox Prime, and is arguably higher value in 11th than it was in 10th, because the +1BS from Take Aim will often be cancelled out by Cover, so this is your route to hit on 2s.

Enhancements

  • Bombast-Class Vox Array: Allows a Militarum Tempestus Officer with a Master Vox in the unit to apply an Order up to three Regiment units at once. Useful if you’re setting up a big go turn with multiple Scion squads at once, or want to support other mobile Infantry.
  • Priority Drop Beacon: Allows a Militarum Tempestus Officer’s unit to make an Ingress Move in your first Movement Phase. A highly valuable threat to hold over foes heads, and particularly good at counterattacking something that’s moved onto the Central Objective turn 1 if you go second, as you can often set up to be pretty well hidden past it.

Stratagems

  • On My Position (1CP): The Militarum classic returns, better than ever. At the end of the opponent’s Charge Phase, select one engaged Regiment unit, and roll a d6 for each enemy unit engaged with them. On a 2+, inflict d6 Mortal Wounds. Afterwards, your unit takes 3d3 Mortal Wounds. Yeah that’s right - that says Charge phase. No more having to survive the enemy’s swing before you trigger this - if your opponent pushes a key asset into a fight and they’re going to sweep your unit anyway, you can simply have your troops exit the situation in the most resource-efficient manner available. That’s thinking like an Imperial commander. You do have to be a little careful to not set up your foe to double Pile In and get something else valuable, but triggering this on a standard sized unit can never fully wipe them out, so just leave one brave model in engagement range to keep the foe pinned. Dark gods help a foe who has multi-charged a single unit.
  • Firing Hot (2CP): Gives a Tempestus or Kasrkin unit +1S/AP with Hot-shot weapons when firing at a target within 12”. Still very nasty, still very 2CP, so still quite rare that you’ll actually pop it, especially as this will (hopefully) be an add-on to a Detachment with some sort of force multiplier Stratagem of its own.
  • Servo-Designators (1CP): After a Militarum Tempestus unit has hit at a target, other Militarum Tempestus units have Ignores Cover when shooting at it. Annoying that you have to use one of your real units to tee this up, but if something has to die, it has to die, and this will see some usage. You can always fire a few guns from one squad at the target your next one is going to focus on.

Is It Good?

Yes. Scions are really good, this makes them better, and On My Position is a wildly strong thing to have access to more widely.

Designation Force - Recon

Credit: BuffaloChicken

The Designation Force is theoretically aimed at sneaky units like Scout Sentinels and Cadian Recon Squads, but ends up being far more broadly useful because it’s extremely generous with some of its Stratagems.

Detachment Rule

This Detachment has the Recon tag and cannot be taken with another Recon Detachment.

Scout Sentinel and Astra Militarum Infantry Smoke units gain the Signal Flares ability, which allows them to pick one visible enemy unit within 12” in your Shooting Phase to be designated. Designated units have +3” detection range.

This is plausibly pretty useful - it helps you squeeze an extra few inches of reach on your big tanks, and can be particularly useful for allowing one to push into Central Objectives and reach out to Expansions, especially as you can compound the effect with Close-Range Detection. In addition, while the vibe of the Detachment makes you think of Cadian Recon Squads, Krieg Combat Engineers have the Smoke keyword, so as a unit of those heads off to go out in a blaze of Mortal Wounds, they can help with this too.

Enhancements

  • Long-Range Scout (Upgrade): Gives a Scout Sentinel unit Infiltrators. Eh. Given Scout is now a non-bo with Infiltrators, this isn’t a slam-dunk, but it’s plausible it ends up being a more useful way to get an Infiltrator in your roster than taking Ratlings or Cadian Recon.
  • Recon Star (Upgrade): Gives a Platoon unit the ability to make an Ingress move in the first Battle Round. This one is fascinating, and potentially very good, especially in the same Detachment that provides Triggered Alerts. You can use this to hang the threat of Big Cadian over the foe right from the start, and can also utilise it to push smaller units straight onto objectives, as a big difference in 11th is that 6” from a board edge reaches most expansion objectives. Also helps with some Secondary cards. Does depend a bit on the price tag, but this very much feels like something you take one of just in case it’s really good without thinking too hard about it.

Stratagems

  • Close-Range Detection (1CP): At the start of your Shooting Phase, pick a Scout Sentinel or Astra Militarum Infantry unit. Visible enemy units within 6” of that unit have +3” Detection Range. Like the Detachment Rule, this can be valuable to help your key toys get shots off, and unlike the Detachment Rule, it doesn’t even need the Smoke keyword, so any big Infantry unit can pop it in a pinch. Also stacks with the Detachment Rule, so if a Krieg unit is going out on a big, heroic adventure, you can squeeze extra value out of them.
  • Triggered Alerts (1CP). Lets an unengaged Astra Militarum Infantry unit make a d3+3” reactive Normal Move when an enemy ends a move within 8”. No Smoke keyword requirement again, which makes this phenomenal - Scions, Bullgryn, Kasrkin - all the great stuff you want in your army, now significantly harder to pin down and actually kill. A+ Stratagem.
  • Sump-Smog Screen (1CP): Pick an Astra Militarum Infantry unit at the start of your Opponent’s Shooting Phase. They get the benefit of cover, as does any other unit not fully visible to a firing unit because of the chosen unit. Essentially gives all your units access to Smokescreen, not just Smoke units, and again you’re looking at Bullgryn with this and licking your lips.

Is It Good?

Yes. You can apply this to most top Astra Militarum lists without changing their composition at all and it provides significant value, particularly from Triggered Alerts. It also amps the utility value of Combat Engineers even higher than it already is, and has some solid upgrades to add flexibility for minimum investment.

Final Thoughts

Guard do okay out of this - both Bridgehead and Designation Force are really good, and giving you flexibility between amping damage or augmenting flexibility. However, the gap in power between their 3DP Detachments and the best 2DP ones is perhaps a bit wider here than for some armies, so there's a lot more tension in deciding which route to go.
