How to Paint Everything: Napoleonic Polish

September 17th, 2026 via Goonhammer

So you've seen the movie and now you know with absolute certainty where Napoleon went wrong. You can, and will, do better. It's time to get into real wargaming. Welcome to Napoleonics.

This article is part of our Getting Started: Napoleonics guide.

Whilst not a major power during the Napoleonic era, the Poles were some of the most dedicated of Napoleon's foreign forces. Seeing the Emperor as their best bet for lasting independence following the Partitions, Polish soldiers served throughout his campaigns.

Our guide covers two of the most recognizable uniforms of the Polish forces of the time - that of the Legion of the Vistula and the Duchy of Warsaw.

The Legion of the Vistula

Formed in 1807, the Legion of the Vistula served the French forces in the Peninsular War, the invasion of Russia, and the War of the Sixth Coalition. It was made up of infantry and cavalry, with the infantry organized into six company battalions as per the French organization put in place in February 1808.

Their most distinct piece of equipment was the czapka, a square-topped variant of the shako, which became a regular part of Polish uniforms of the era (although historians continue to debate how widespread it actually was, since Polish uniforms tended to be a mix of salvaged Prussian, Austrian, and French uniforms).

Art of Polish soldiers wearing the czapka. (Credit - "Polish Uhlans from the Duchy of Warsaw 1807-1815", by J Suchodolski, 1852)

To build my miniatures, I used the Elite Companies, French infantry 1807-1814 box from Perry Miniatures, along with the Vistula Legion heads from Warlord Games (which come in Fusilier, Voltigeur, and Grenadier varieties). I chose this set for its more dynamic poses, as I originally built my models to play Silver Bayonet.

I used the following Warhammer Paints when painting my Legion of the Vistula force:

  • White Scar Spray (this can be replaced with Wraithbone if you wish)
  • White Scar (this can be replaced with Wraithbone if you wish)
  • Apothecary White Contrast
  • Guilliman Flesh Contrast
  • Iyanden Yellow Contrast
  • Talassar Blue Contrast
  • Black Templar Contrast
  • Abaddon Black
  • Mournfang Brown
  • Leadbelcher
  • Retributor Armour
  • Word Bearers Red
  • Zandri Dust
  • Ushabti Bone
  • Nuln Oil

So the first stage was to prime and undercoat my model. I used Jenolite Universal Etch Primer, and then used White Scar spray for the basecoat since the Vistula Legion uses bright colors. After that, I covered the whole model with Apothecary White Contrast - which shaded all the white belts, straps, and trousers, as well as helping me make out the model's details for the following stages. Once that was dry, I applied Guilliman Flesh Contrast (though any similar contrast such as Darkoath Flesh would also work) to the face and hands of the model.

With that done, I moved onto the uniform colors - starting with carefully painting the lapel beneath the crossed straps with Iyanden Yellow. Take care not to spill over onto the straps or coat - but if you do, just wait for it all to dry before applying some White Scar to the spill to cover it up. The next step was the coat - which I did with Talassar Blue contrast. Then I used Black Templar contrast for the hat, shoes, and scabbard.

From here on, I switched to regular paints for the details. First, I used Mournfang Brown to paint the musket, pack, and pouches on the model. Then, I applied Leadbelcher to the upper half of the musket and bayonet, as well as the dagger handle on the back. The other metallic I used was used Retributor Armour, for the front plate of the hat and the dagger guard. The hat pom and dagger tassel were painted with Word Bearers Red, and the musket strap was painted with Zandri Dust.

The next step was basing - which consisted of a basecoat of Mournfang Brown, followed by a drybrush of Zandri Dust, and then a drybrush of Ushabti Bone. Finally, I checked the model for any small spills and mistakes and tidied it up with the paints I had already used as necessary before giving it a wash of Nuln Oil. Once that was dry, I used Abaddon Black to paint the rim of the base, and just like that, it was table ready!

The Duchy of Warsaw

The army of the Duchy of Warsaw saw action across the continent - the 4th & 7th Infantry Regiments marched at Talavera in 1809, whilst the 8th took part in the Austro-Polish War of the same year. For this I used the Perry Miniatures Napoleonic Duchy of Warsaw Infantry Elite Companies 1807-14, and settled on painting up the uniform of the 7th, to use alongside my Vistula Legion in Peninsular games. Interestingly, painting the 4th would be very similar to painting the Vistula Legion, just with red collars, red cuffs, and red edging to the yellow lapel, and a slightly darker blue for the coat!

I used the following Warhammer Paints:

  • White Scar Spray (this can be replaced with Wraithbone if you wish)
  • White Scar (this can be replaced with Wraithbone if you wish)
  • Apothecary White Contrast
  • Darkoath Flesh Contrast
  • Leviadon Blue Contrast
  • Blood Angels Red Contrast
  • Black Templar Contrast
  • Abaddon Black
  • Mournfang Brown
  • Leadbelcher
  • Retributor Armour
  • Word Bearers Red
  • Zandri Dust
  • Ushabti Bone
  • Nuln Oil Wash

The first few stages (priming, undercoating, and painting the flesh) I did exactly the same way as I did in the Vistula Legion guide above. The big difference is in the uniform colors - I used Leviadon Blue for the coat, and Blood Angels Red for the lapel. In case of spillage, I used White Scar to tidy up the white on the trousers and straps again!

Then, again just the same way as for the Vistula Legion, I used Mournfang Brown to paint the musket stock and pack, followed by Black Templar Contrast on the czapka and the shoes, again making use of White Scar to tidy up if necessary. Then it was time for details - I used Leadbelcher on the upper half of the musket and the whole bayonet, and added a dash of Retributor Armour on the hat plate over and under the white cord. The pom was done with Word Bearers Red.

To paint the base, I used Mournfang Brown as a basecoat, and then drybrushed Zandri Dust and then Ushabti Bone over the top in lighter layers. Finally, I washed the whole model with Nuln Oil to darken and shade it. The finish may end up somewhat glossy, but this can be resolved by using a matt acrylic varnish, which will also protect your models (I use Humbrol Acrylic Varnish spray).

Credit: Hydriatus

Of course, this is just the 7th Regiment - depending on the regiment and soldier you're working on (such as grenadiers, fusiliers, or voltigeurs), the poms, cuffs, collar and epaulettes would be colored differently!

But that's it! Variations on these schemes make up the majority of the uniforms of the Polish forces during the Napoleonic era - if you have any particular tips and tricks of your own, why not share them in the comments?
