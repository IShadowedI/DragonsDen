[Warmachine] Tabletop Battles Reviews: Fane of Nyrro - Nymara, the Shadowblade Rules

August 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Last week, Steamforged Games hit Fane of Nyrro players everywhere with a special surprise during the Court of Shadows livestream event: A first look at the apotheosis caster for the faction, Nymara the Shadowblade. Coming in as Fane of Nyrro’s dedicated warbeast caster, Nymara descends onto the battlefield riding atop a giant vampiric beast of her own to direct the flying circus of pain. Today, our Warmachine team here at Tabletop Battles is taking a closer look at Nymara’s rules, what synergies and combos she has in the faction, some list ideas, and our final thoughts on the Shadowblade herself!

Thanks to Steamforged Games for providing us with a preview copy of these rules.

General Overview

Credit: Steamforged Games

Overall Statline and Weapons

Nymara is big, even for an apotheosis caster, with an 80mm base. She’s SPD 7 like Ashmael, and is fairly accurate at MAT and RAT 7. DEF 12 and ARM 18 (20 with her shield bonus) with 24 boxes makes her reasonably tanky against small arms, but still vulnerable to big hits, which may make keeping her safe on her 80mm base tricky. Her ARC of 5 and CTRL of 9 raise alarm bells at first glance, but fortunately she’s got tools in her kit to mitigate both of these. Lastly, she has a broad suite of special rules, with some standouts being Cavalry for some super accurate charges and Dual Attack to make the most out of her offensive potential when she charges in.

Speaking of her offensive potential, Nymara comes armed to the teeth with some dangerous weapons. She has a Tethered Javelin for a ranged attack, which seems unimpressive at RNG 7, RAT 1, and POW 14 until you factor in Skewer, which lets her drag a model she damages with an equal or smaller base directly towards her until they reach her base, another model, or obstacle. Since Nymara is already on an extra-large base, this opens up almost every model in the game as a potential Skewer target, which opens up the extra funny option of potentially dragging one of the super junior cohort models away from their caster and potentially out of control. 

Finally, Nymara packs a melee punch with three initial attacks from two weapon profiles. Firstly, she’s got her mount’s two Talon Blades, which are POW 16 and RNG 2. Nymara herself has her Daybreaker, a POW 15 RNG 2 Weapon with the Shield and Magical weapon advantages. Daybreaker also has the rule Crack the Skye, which lets her spend 5 hunger points to gain an extra damage die once per attack. Combined with her other damage boosting abilities we’re about to get in to, Daybreak can quickly become a very scary weapon. 

Special Rules

We’ve got a large suite of special rules here, which are focused on boosting Nymara’s battlegroup as well as mitigating her low CTRL and ARC stats. The first rule, Wrought in Anguish, lets Nymara spend Hunger points to cast spells, upkeep spells, or heal warbeasts, which is hugely important considering Nymara’s dismal ARC 5. Murder gives Nymara (and her whole battlegroup, as we will see shortly) bonus damage for ganging up on targets already tangled up with her battlegroup, adding +1 to damage rolls for every other model in her battlegroup in melee range of the target of a melee attack. Lastly, Nymara rocks three field marshals, giving her battlegroup Extended Control Range, Tireless for free runs, and Murder.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Hunger- Dark Valkyrie

Nymara gains hunger points equal to the number of Fury points on a warbeast in her battlegroup when said warbeast is destroyed or, very interestingly, removed from play by enemy attacks. You’ll want a big battlegroup with Nymara anyways, and Fane loves to run its beasts hot on Fury, so generating hunger here should be fairly straightforward.

There’s a number of ways Nymara can spend hunger for powerful effects, like any other Nyrro Warlock. The first ability, Feral Onslaught, costs 2 points and triggers as soon as a warbeast in Nymara’s battlegroup fails a threshold check. When you use it, Feral Onslaught lets you immediately move a warbeast 3” and add +3 to its damage rolls until the end of the activation, giving you a chance to put a beast in a better position for Frenzy and boost its damage output. On Dark Wings costs 3 hunger points during Nymara’s activation to move up to 3 warbeasts 2”, though they can only be affected by this once per turn. Finally, We Descend costs 5 hunger and lets you expand the damage roll bonus from Murder to also apply to the attack roll for the turn, which is a huge help for fixing the accuracy of Fane’s warbeasts. 

Feat

As appropriate for the beast focused caster in Fane of Nyrro, Nymara’s feat, From Lowering Skies, creates some nasty attrition potential by bringing one of your non-character, large base of smaller warbeasts back from the dead within her control range, at full boxes. A major downside here compared to Mortenembra’s similar feat is that the warbeast you return to play cannot activate when it returns. The upside though is that the returned beast counts as two models for purposes of Murder for the rest of the turn. Compared to Mortenembra, who can bring a warjack back from the dead to be a ballistic missile, Nymara’s returned cohort model is instead used to set up a nasty alley-oop for one of your other beasts (or Nymara herself!) to swoop in for a hyper dangerous push. 

Spell List

Nymara’s spell list is brief, and is wholly focused on handing out buffs. Deflection is the only spell not confined to battlegroup only and gives +2 DEF against ranged and arcane attacks in Nymara’s control range for one round, a very strong spell balanced out by Nymara’s small control range and the fact Deflection costs 3 to cast. Slingshot also costs 3, but it’s an upkeep spell so it won’t burn through your Fury reserves every turn and gives every model in Nymara’s Battlegroup Repostition [3’]. Her last spell is a spell unique to the Shadowblade, Borne Aloft, which costs 1 and gives a cohort model in her battlegroup +3 ARM and +2 to damage until you either make an attack or the model buffed by Borne Aloft takes damage, and its on the affected model until they lose it. It’s not a hard spell to knock off, but it's so cheap that it’s not hard to spread liberally through your battlegroup, giving them some nice extra toughness and boosting their damage output.  

Combos and Synergy

Credit: Steamforged Games

The mainstay of any Nymara list is absolutely going to be Fane’s non-character warbeasts, as this vampire girlie loves her Vordaks and Strygons and wants to run a whole bunch of them. Between buffs from Murder, Borne Aloft, and Feral Onslaught Nymara can turbo-boost the normally limp wristed damage output of Vordaks and Strygons into the stratosphere, all while giving them the mobility to be exactly where they need to with Slingshot and On Dark Wings. You’ll want a lot of beasts, and you’ll want them carefully positioned on the battlefield to make the most out of their boosted frenzy charges as well as to get the most out of Murder. Plus, Strygons have the extra utility of being a cheap Shield Guard for Nymara, which is important considering her extra-large base makes her difficult to hide from enemy shooting.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Between the two character warbeasts, Horkos stands out as a likely mainstay in Nymara’s lists. Horkos acts as a tough anchor for Nymara’s warbeast rodeo, sporting both the best damage output of Nyrro’s warbeasts as well as being extra tough with his animus, Indomitable, which grants a cool +2 ARM against melee attacks. Coupled with the Strygon’s Shield Guard, keeping Indomitable up on Nymara is critical in keeping her alive, especially if she decides to play aggressive and get stuck in.

While the stars of the show are the Fane of Nyrro warbeasts, there are still a few units and solos that have some great synergy with Nymara. Fane Stalkers are a standout unit choice, providing some great backfield shooting while handing out either Torment to boost the hunger economy or increasing the threat range of Nymaras beasts with Hunters Lure. The Devoted provide economical Shadow Shift targets while being incredibly hard to hit under Deflection, and while The Klyvenesh are a more expensive option at 11 points, they’re a dangerous unit that can deal with a wide variety of problems, plus they don’t mind that Nymara doesn’t have much to support them with. Lesser Eldritch are a standout solo, since they’re cheap at 3 points each as well as providing some much appreciated dice fixing with Puppet Master.

Swiftblade: This list is all beasts, all the time baby, with a whopping eight cohort models in Nymara’s battlegroup. The Strygons are support pieces here, with the flexibility for two of them to be on Shield Guard duty or to bully charge for Murder bonuses. Plus, the Feral conditioning on the third Strygon gives me Provoke, which lets me get some extra frenzy goodness in a pinch. The Vordaks meanwhile fly around and cause problems, with the two subjugated Vordaks making the most of their Conditioned rule to pick and choose their preferred Frenzy target for a gnarly hit from a Lamprey head bite, and the other two Vordaks are kitted out to provide more solid damage outside frenzy, especially against warrior models thanks to the Alpha head. Nymara and Horkos anchor the battlegroup and can break open hard targets when it’s time to engage.

Meanwhile, the Stalkers can provide backfield shooting support, hunger generation, or threat extension depending on what shot they use, and the Prophets make removing them under Deflection a royal pain. Finally, The Klyvanesh stalk the flanks of the battlefield and keep the opponent on their toes, since they become a real problem if left unattended to.

Trish: This is leaning hard into beasts - no shock there. Horkos is there to provide Indomitable to help keep your leader alive, as well as having solid damage output.

I have 4 Stygons for Murder. They all have Muzzled heads for the armor (this might change) and Bladed Armor wings for the added damage. They are shield guards to save points, but crucially one also has the provoke animus to trigger the Vordaks to frenzy as needed. Fun fact - you don’t have to worry about Murder if you frenzy into friendlies.

The Vordaks are designed frenzy and still give you agency. The Vordaks also have a good bit of control on where they end after frenzying - either returning to the leader or choosing a “safe” frenzy target if need be.

In terms of non beast units you have a unit of stalkers to camp objectives and put out Huntsman’s Lure. The two units of Devoted are designed to be up in the mix and act as Shadow Shift targets. One even has a Prophet in order to apply Ashen Veil to all enemies nearby.

Masterball: This is an idea that wants to play just enough of a melee package to lean into Nymara’s strengths while giving you some alternate play making with the help of a Super Jr package. Both Exolon Nostilla and Thaelmara have Dark Seduction and ways to innately boost their casting. This lets you get around the one issue with Murder; not having room to place all your beasts to trigger it, as well as drastically increasing your threat presence.

Aberration is also distinctly different in how your opponent needs to deal with it compared to your beasts. With Shadow Shift your opponent needs to commit just enough to force the teleport but not so much the crack back is a massive trade down. With Aberration it's the opposite, they need to COMMIT which exposes them to even more reprisal. 

There are also just enough units in the list to either contest or control scenario while providing just enough targets for shadow shift that your opponent needs to respect your positioning. Overall it's a list with the bare minimum of each option to broaden the matchup spread at the cost of consistency and redundancy.

TOTAL POINTS 100/100

Blake: The Fane of Nyrro finally get their BATtlegroup caster! If your opponent hasn’t died from that horrible pun, this list might finish the job. Nymara’s kit is all about swarming an opponent with bats and keeping them safe enough on the attack run to get there with Borne Aloft and Deflection. Once she’s up there, she wants nothing more than to juice her bats full of fury and then let them loose. Strygons are her most perfect tool in that sense, being cheap enough to boost Murder, protecting each other on the approach, being able to Frenzy without loss of function, and providing necessary Hunger for Nymara to access her full suite of abilities. She’s fairly fragile for an 80mm and Shadow Shifting her to safety will have to be in a position that can accommodate an 80mm base, so she’s best at having the bats do the heavy lifting until there’s little enough left for her to finish the job using Provoke and Slingshot to maneuver your activated bats to ideal Murder beneficiary positions. Also remember that nearly all of Nymara’s Hunger abilities are during her activation. If you want to hand out accuracy with We Descend or alpha strike with On Dark Wings, she’ll have to build it up first. Between Shield Guarding with Muzzled Strygons, ample Feast triggers, and accumulating Hunger from expendable Strygons, it should build fairly quickly.

The support suite is fairly straightforward, with Jylvana providing a good flag capturer and emergency snack while two units of Fane Stalkers provide ample sources of Huntsman’s Lure. Nymara doesn’t support many non-bat models due to her record-small control area (somewhat offset by her base size), but it makes the Last Watch and Cylena good general purpose bodyguards that also benefit a great deal from Deflection with their high base defensive stats.

Final Thoughts

Credit: Steamforged Games

Masterball: Nymara introduces a new type of battlegroup caster to the game, one that is hyper focused on the game plan. Where many synergy or steroid style casters in the past had a focus on battlegroups, they often had some carve out for units. Not so much with Nymara, since the best you get is a 9” deflection aura to help protect your bare essentials. This is battlegroup bolstering sharpened to a fine point and for many will be the draw for the army. Fane has so far cemented itself as one of the most unique, complicated, and technical armies in recent memory, and Nymara stands in stark contrast. For many this is the selling point, THE REASON to play vampires, oops-all-bats with nothing but violence and speed.

It's my hope that despite the ludicrous threat ranges and angles this caster can present, she is relegated to at best off-list status, as this kind of play could be seen as oppressive and toxic if Nymara can consistently pull off her tricks. Time will tell, of course, but if Fane is typically an army for tinkerers (Johnny’s for the magic players in the audience) then this is the firmest foot forward for the flashy playmakers (Timmy’s) to take to the darkened skies.

Blake: Nymara is a caster of such organized chaos that it’s very unpredictable as to how devastating her lists are going to be, which is a far cry from the heavy control aspects represented by Ashmael and Vorsalys. The trade-off here is running an incredibly fast army whose models are capable of activating profitably multiple times turn-over-turn as well as being capable of swarming down the heaviest targets that other Fane casters are more inclined to control or try to play around. She doesn’t necessarily look easy to play based on some aspects of her personal vulnerability and not every player wanting to get on board with the “embrace chaos” nature of her fire-and-frenzy playstyle. I do think that her combination of speed and hitting power that both outclass every other choice in the army might be worth the difficulty though. Played properly, and with the proper accommodations, she could be an alpha-striking armor-tearing nightmare.

She also just looks awesome and fun.

Swiftblade: I’m a Protectorate player, a Legion player, and, if I’m honest with myself, a Timmy. My heart yearns for big battlegroups. Nymara has answered my forlorn calls, and man she looks like an absolute blast to play.

She’s very different from the other Fane casters we’ve seen so far. Firstly, while the other three have tools that benefit a wide range of the faction’s roster, Nymara is extremely focused on warbeasts, as her whole schtick is to transform the Fane cohorts, who usually lack killing power and instead act as hunger generators, into engines of winged destruction. Secondly, she’s very likely the easiest of the current roster so far to kill, with her 80mm base and limited defensive tech, so she’s not quite as untouchable as her peers. 

In return for her elevated vulnerabilities to getting murked and narrow list-building focus, we get a caster whose kit is laser-focused and an absolute ball to pilot. I’ve been trying to avoid using the term “flying circus” as much as possible throughout this article for fear of turning into a broken record, but in my final thoughts here I can’t think of any better way to describe Nymara. She’s the ringmaster, and all of her warbeasts are her flying circus, going every which way on the table and being extremely hard to pin down with so much access to flying. With her hunger abilities, Borne Aloft, and Murder special rule, Nymara rewards careful planning to get your warbeasts into the right target at the right time, either with big frenzy hits or by ganging up on some poor model, and then flying away to either safety or into even juicier targets.

Nymara won’t be the easiest caster in Fane to play, nor is she likely the most straightforwardly powerful. When it comes to the fun factor though, I think she’s my favorite caster we’ve seen so far, and make no mistake, Nymara is a strong Warlock choice for Fane in the hands of someone willing to learn all of the tricks of her kit.
