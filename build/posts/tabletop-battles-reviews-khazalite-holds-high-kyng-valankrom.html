Tabletop Battles Reviews: Khazalite Holds - High Kyng Valankrom, Valayan Guardians, Clangunners, Thrynn, Runewright

November 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

High Kyng Valankrom/Thrynn on War Throne

Keewa: The granddaddy centrepiece model of this release, and probably of the whole army in general, High Kyng [sic] Valankrom certainly steals the show on his huge, magically mobile War Throne. The throne itself takes clear design inspiration from the venerable Viking longship, with not one, not two, but three dragons’ head prows rising from the front. The crew comprises three wizard-ish dwarfs, named in the battletome as a Stonesmith (probably the one at the front with the anvil helmet), a Grudgebearer (definitely the guy with a book), and a Holdseeker (presumably the other one, with a raven on her shoulder). Presumably, the one at the front with his hand on the prow is steering, another is making the stone waves that move the “ship” forwards, and another is operating the weapon’s system (It seems the aforementioned dragons’ heads spit lightning bolts)

I like that there are eight pedestals arranged around the rear of the barge, each with the face of one of the Dwarf ancestor gods on top (except for Hashut, who’s been scrubbed for being an arsehole, a nice touch). The throne itself goes together relatively easily; a lot of the pieces slot and lock into place to make them easy to glue, but you do need to be careful in places to make sure that the pieces are actually together; I had a small issue with the aft piece not being entirely “clicked” in when I glued it, and as a result it pulled away from the main piece to leave an annoying gap.

High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa

There are a few other places where the prospect of a gap is an annoyance you might not even notice until you come to paint it - the little pedestals with the dwarf gods' heads fit are moulded as part of the prow, but you do need to attach said heads (each with a letter on the inside to make matching them in the right place easier - I really appreciate these little quality-of-life improvements); the heads attach to the pillars from the rear via a plate with a diagonal cut that left an annoying gap I didn’t even notice until it was too late, ugh. Always check before you prime, folks!

High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa
High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa
High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa

The crew and Valankrom himself go together very easily; everything slots into place without complaint and is mostly 3 parts each. Obviously, you are going to need to keep both Kyng and crew separate for painting - don’t even try it any other way - the floor of the “deck” has slots and holes into which the crewdwarfs corresponding pegs fit, so there’s really no danger of putting them on incorrectly. The quality of this kit is stunning and really demonstrates how far the possibilities of plastic injection moulding have come in the past decade or so; details are tack-sharp.

War Throne: Credit -keewa

The kit has an alternate, generic unnamed character version in case you want more than one of these in your army. The generic version swaps Valankrom for a very doughty female Thrynn with a halberd and shield, not seated but instead standing defiantly on the deck - with my critic’s hat on, I think the composition of this setup is considerably more dynamic and interesting. The seated Valankrom is both quite passive and feels as though his small body gets a little lost against the backdrop of the throne’s rear element. On the other hand, if you assemble the throne with Valankrom, you effectively get a Thrynn model for free, and although she’s leaning forward a bit to compensate for the general upward tilt of the throne, you can fix that with some creative basing. My only problem with the female Thrynn is that she doesn’t have any of the big braids I like about female Warhammer dwarfs (this will be a recurring theme).

The kit itself has been a pleasure to paint; there is tons of potential to explore and experiment with painting stone, painting magic effects, painting metals, etc.

High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa
High Kyng Valankrom - Credit: keewa

Valayan Guardians

Valayan Guardians - Credit: keewa

keewa: The elite heavy infantry of the Khazalite faction takes the form of priestesses of the mother goddess Valaya, which is nice; it’s unusual to see an all-woman unit that’s resilient and hardy rather than fast and fragile. The Valayan Guardians kit can be built in two ways, either with runic halberds or with runic axes and shields. I chose the shields because I love that classic Ironbreaker-style tanky silhouette; each loadout has slight visual differences: the halberd guardians have loose, wizardy sleeves, while the axe-and-shield guardians have more conservative, tight sleeves. The loadouts each have a different set of associated heads, either some skull-caps with metallic bunches, or more bascinet-style helmets with a transverse bladed crest on top (which remind me a lot of the Lannister helmets from Game of Thrones), and while the latter is “supposed” to be the axe-and-shield option, I couldn’t resist those silly metallic Fozzy Bear bunches. Each head goes to a specific body, so if you do decide to swap to the other head set, make sure you keep close track of what goes where.

Valayan Guardian Champion - Credit: keewa

The kit goes together quite snugly, with pieces that sort of snap into place, and pieces that are nicely considered; there's not a lot of unnecessary splitting here. Each dwarf has a front torso, back torso, two leg pieces, and whichever head and arms you choose, plus two pauldrons, which apply in the classic space marine fashion. The weapons are mostly already on the arms (the halberds held cross-body are not attached to arms), and the shields fit nicely in place by means of a peg and slot on the wrist. They block quite a lot of the body, so make sure to keep them separate for painting to save yourself a headache.

You only get five models per set; I know they're elites, but even so, it'll be a bit costly if you want more or a set of each loadout in your army.

Valayan Guardian - Credit: keewa
Valayan Guardian - Credit: keewa
Valayan Guardian - Credit: keewa
Valayan Guardian - Credit: keewa

My main complaint with this set is why no braids?? The expression of dwarven womanhood is big, thick, ship’s rope plaits, and this all-female unit has nary a hair on show, since there aren't really any unhelmeted options (except for one champion model, whose hair is not long). All the helmeted heads are wearing a wimple-style cloth underneath, leaving only their face visible. With the exception of Hjulda, the Khazalite dwarf women don't have the same characteristics I fondly ascribe to the old Warhammer Fantasy ones of days gone by, but they do at least have models, which is nice.

The champion also has a hero rock to stand on; I don't really know why, since it makes her a head taller than the rest of the unit, which looks a little strange. If I were to do it again, I would probably leave off the little plinth.

Gholems of Gazul/Valaya

Gholems of Valaya - Credit: keewa

Keewa: Great Gazul! These Gholems were included in the original army box as Gholems of Gazul (GoG for short?), and although the pieces for the Valayan Gholems were on the frame (since rectified by GW putting a pdf on Warhammer Community), here they are in their official double kit, so you can either build your Gholems as giant hammer-wielding Gazoolies, or as doughty dinner-plate shield-wielding Valayans. I went with the Valayans, because I was already doing the guardians and, honestly, because of the Fozzy Bear bunches, this time in stone.

Gholem of Valaya: Credit - keewa

The meat of the kit is assembled in the same way; the trunk and torso are identical. What changes which kind of Gholems they are are the heads, shoulders, and arms. There are three shields, two with rams’ heads in profile and another with a front view of a ram (which I assume is for the “champion”, but since their keyword is War Machine, and they don’t have a champion, I guess it’s just a slightly flashier one for a bit of fun.

Unlike their fleshy cousins, these stone representations of Valaya actually do have braids represented on their neck pieces, which is nice. Although I airbrushed mine, they’ll also paint up really easily with drybrushes and washes to get a solid stone effect, which is very nice.

Gholem of Valaya: Credit - keewa
Gholem of Valaya - Credit: keewa

Much as I love the finished models, and I think they look great, there are quite a few pieces, and building them was a moderate pain in the arse, not least because of GW’s infuriatingly illogical and arbitrary method of numbering pieces on the sprue, necessitating constant rounds of “hunt the piece on the sprue”, meaning that building each model took twice as long as it should and half of that time was spent getting frustrated, because piece number 6 is on sprue 3 nestled between piece 105 and piece 70 - it doesn’t have to be like this, GW! Please, please just number the pieces on the sprue, going left to right, top to bottom, like reading a book page. I would save so much time on building if I knew where piece number 4 was going to be just by having a general idea of where it should be rather than hunting for pieces.

The face of the Mother Goddess on the shoulder pad - Credit: keewa

Now, I know, I know what you’re thinking: “Surely, keewa, this is a skill issue. It isn’t really much of a problem, and you’re just whinging” You’re not wrong; were I more adroit at the building phase (and had the time to build with less haste), it would be less frustrating. Even so, it’s annoying, and this kit proves it once again.

Thrynn

Khazalite Thrynn - credit: Bair

Bair: The (not so) humble Dwarf Lord, basically. Games Workshop made an error in releasing easily the best Dwarf Lord model to ever be sculpted a couple of decades ago, all dwarf fans will know exactly the model that I mean. This one is pretty great though, as you’ll come to expect from modern plastic hero kits: there are no options whatsoever on the sprue. The model comes as it is. The head is a separate piece but has a sort-of odd attachment, again like modern plastic kits often do, so a straight swap out with Hjulda or another model will take some extra cutting and greenstuffing to get done if you had that in mind.

I do really, really love the bear pelt across his shoulders though. Dwarfs and bears are my two favourite things so whenever they’re combined it’s just perfect!

Clangunners

Khazalite Clangunners - credit Bair

Bair: The other infantry kit for Khazalites arrives. We looked at the Clanshields when the army box came out and these just feel…lacking in options by comparison. You are not able to build them all as male or female dwarfs you get five of each instead. The head options you do have instead are to either give each model a helmet or not, and each head is hyper-specific to each body as well. The commander (champion, drummer, banner) are all optional builds and you can make stock guys with guns instead if you wish and can build either version of the guns for each model since there are two distinct warscroll options.

I went for the Thrundrin Shortcannons for one simple reason: they look much better. Overall, they go together with ease, though there are definitely more pronounced mould lines on this kit than on any of the others, which felt kind of odd; the way the helmets attach to the sprue especially requires a bit of extra cleaning to look good.

My only complaint about the heads here is that the men are all sporting beards (though some are oddly short; they must be young). The women have their braids tucked away into cowls/their helmets, and having those shown and being more pronounced would have looked much better; a complaint that I have across the range for the dwarf women, aside from that one Clanshield champion head and Hjulda of course. There are some braids peeking out on the sides of some heads, but it’s a detail I didn’t spot until I was in the middle of painting them! Something more pronounced would be great.

Runewright

Khazalite Runewright - credit Bair

Bair: Just like the Thrynn, there are no options to be seen here at all, and I’d really have liked a female head option on this one. The model goes together well and I like the movement that the sculpt portrays instead of yet another stagnant dwarf hero like the older Runepriest model since that still exists; that model would also work very well as a Runewright, too, if you’re wanting multiple Runewrights in an army and not wanting duplicate models. They scale very well next to each other and won’t look out of place.

Ormr & Ashtongue

Bair: Unfortunately I didn’t get the time to paint these up alongside the rest but I was surprised just how large they are by comparison of the other dwarfs in this range! Ashtongue especially was much larger than I thought from photos. Fyreslayers do just tend to be larger anyways so Ormr fits in well alongside the rest of the slayer range, it just felt a little funny putting him together after the Thrynn and Runewright!

Again, no options here but they’re named heroes so there’s really no need for options either, they go together really well but the head has a very strange connection point so any head swapping you might want to do will take considerable work, not something I’d be rushing to attempt.

Settling Grudges

Bair: Overall these are nice dwarfs/dawi/duardin models and I’m really just so happy that we have a “normal” dwarf army in Age of Sigmar after all this time! Dwarfs were my first love in Warhammer Fantasy and are an army I’ve continued to collect for Old World since so having them in Age of Sigmar is like coming home. I like how they’ve been modernised and made to be unique in a lot of ways, I just really do wish the women dwarf models had braids on all of the models since it’s so important to them in lore the way a man-dwarf’s beard is to him.

keewa: Needs more braids, where are the braids? Give us braids!
