Magic: The Gathering Budget Commander League, The Beginning

October 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This is the start of what should-be a series as my Commander pod ventures (this is a pun, you'll get it soon) through a league starting with budget decks and upgrading them as we lose games. So join us as we see just how terrible some of these beginnings are and how badly some upgrades are going to be needed! You all know me as Bair on here, but my name is also Zach, which is how I'll be referring to myself throughout. 

The League

Val approached the pod with a simple idea: we take a Precon Commander deck each, don't make any changes, and as we lose games we get to upgrade it with a certain value determined by how badly we lost. That sounded like a lot of fun but we all know that Precons are not all created equally so I had a different idea: we spend £18 each on a completely random, budget, Commander deck from a guy online. There are many such listings you can find around various different marketplaces online so I'm not going to list the specific one we ordered from but we did all order from the same listing to keep this as fair as possible.

As we play games we are going to be allowed a certain value to upgrade the decks as we go. The person knocked out of the game first gets £5, second out gets £2, and then £1. The person that won the game clearly doesn't need any more help so they get nothing! No upgrades for them! Invariably that person will (hopefully) then lose the next or a later game. So, what did we get?

The Players

To get the ball rolling I asked each to answer some basic questions. You're joining us after we've already played our first game so the answers are going to be slightly skewed to having a little more understanding. Let's get into it!

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

So you’ve got your deck, what are your initial thoughts? How do you like the Commander? 

Zach: I’m really excited for this deck, it’s almost all Dungeons and Dragons cards and all about delving into the dungeon! Earlier in the year I treated myself to some Adventures Beyond boosters that I found cheap and attempted to put a couple decks together but they’re not really that great, just dual colour ones that work in like a bracket 1 setting. Not that this is probably better so far!

The commander is Sefris who I believe was one of the precon deck commanders since she’s a thicker card stock card. Immediate thought is that the deck has 34 lands (same as most the others) which is a bit low for me ( I trend towards 37) and 30 of those are 10 of each basic land…not a great start. There is a Command Tower at least and an Arcane Signet but I expect mana to be very rough for a while. 

FTS note: Correct, Sefris was the face of the Dungeons of Death precon from Commander: Adventures in the Forgotten Realms.

Rob: When I first received my Vampire deck, I was honestly impressed. I could easily see the synergies between my creatures and how they wanted to generate and use Blood tokens. Anja as the commander feels like a good choice, acting as a token generator, damage trigger, and as a life gain trigger. 

I was not without concern though, as the deck only came with 34 lands and one mana rock. Not surprising for a budget deck, but I could already tell the deck was going to run slow. 

One positive I would also like to mention is how good the artwork is for these cards. Each card depicts the Vampires as true aristocrats, clearly lording over the underlings in our little setting.   

Val: I was excited when I saw Juri. I had recently been looking at Rakdos commanders because my spouse wanted me to build a Rakdos deck, and Juri stood out as a commander that seemed easy to build around with a burn/aristocrats strategy.

When I received the deck, I scanned the cards, looked through them once, and then deliberately stopped thinking about it. I wanted to discover the deck during gameplay. Looking at Juri, I figured the game plan would be pretty straightforward but I was both right and wrong. Juri himself doesn’t require much strategizing; the challenge was figuring out how to execute his core game plan with the cards I had.

Jon: Having no idea what to expect with this deck, and never having played anything from Aetherdrift, I went in blind and hoped for the best. The main mechanic I had no experience with in the Commander is the ‘Start your engines!’ and while the name for this mechanic is questionable, the ability itself was actually quite fun.

I didn’t have a chance prior to playing to go through the list so it was more of a ‘play cards and see what happens’ situation and to be honest I had a blast with it and do actually quite enjoy the Commander itself! No mana rocks and no tap lands did make me worry about mana, but much to my surprise it actually had more consistent mana than most of my own homebrew decks! 

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

We’ve played our first game now, what do you think were the biggest issues you ran into with the deck? What do you plan on doing for the first upgrades/fixes to make it run better/smoother?

Zach: Well I expected mana to be my biggest issue but my opening hand had the Command Tower, a Plains, and an Island so I was able to play cards pretty quick, drawing into my Arcane Signet relatively soon after as well; luck that I only wish I had in normal games. I got enough Scry, card draw, and top-decked lands that the game went pretty smoothly, if not still slowly! That’s the biggest issue here: it’s slow. Fortunately we’re all facing the same issue, at least.

It’ll need more card draw, lands that can tap for multiple colours, and maybe another mana rock or two to really be smooth. Also, I was waiting to draw a card to give me the Initiative so that I could go into the Undercity instead of a regular dungeon, which never happened; after closer inspection the next day I realised there’s only 4 cards in here that let me do that, so getting more in will be the next priority. 

Rob: Zach was my biggest issue! Locking down my commander early in the game with an enchantment that stripped her of all abilities and prevented her from doing anything! It took Val’s help to get her sent back to the command zone, which highlighted the true biggest issue, mana. 

For most of the game I was stuck on 4 lands, which meant that I could typically play just one spell a turn. Eventually I got up to six lands and was able to recast Anja, but by then the game was pretty much over. 

It’s clear what my first upgrades will need to be, which is to increase the land count in the deck by adding in some cheap duel lands. From there I can start upgrading the basic lands with better quality ones, or start squeezing in some mana rocks. 

It will be difficult to decide on what to remove though, as so much of the deck feels worth keeping.

Val: The biggest issue I ran into was mana. I could get started, but once I began casting creatures, theft spells, and trying to actually use Juri’s sacrifice game plan, I ran out of gas pretty quickly. The deck has plenty of ways to sacrifice things, but not enough ramp, card advantage, or repeatable fodder to keep everything moving.

So for the first £5, I’m looking less at flashy upgrades and more at making the engine actually work. I want a little more mana. Things like Arcane Signet, Rakdos Signet, or Talisman of Indulgence along with cheap cards that either give me things to sacrifice or reward me for doing it.

The goal isn’t to completely rebuild the deck after one game. I want to keep the ridiculous steal-and-sacrifice Juri plan intact, just to make sure I actually have enough mana and resources to cause some chaos before sacrificing myself again for another £5.

Jon: While mana wasn’t an issue, I do feel it needs more rocks as well as some tap lands to ensure I have the RIGHT mana. That was where my issues lay, although I had plenty of mana, I never had the right amount of what I actually needed. Similarly, I managed to get to my max speed quite quickly, however I lacked a consistent enough, and cheap enough, sac outlet to trigger it. 

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Post first-game what went well, or better than expected, if anything?

Zach: My mana base as mentioned. The fact that I won? What the hell? I have the only 3 colour deck here and with almost all basic lands, that wasn’t supposed to happen? I’m supposed to be getting upgrades and now I’m stuck with this for at least one more game? Why? How did I end up here? I even completed two whole dungeons! No dragons in sight, however, is of course disappointing so that will need to be amended. 

Rob: The game probably went as expected, at least for me. Slow, but very entertaining. We still got to see some fun interactions with Blood tokens during the game, particularly with Arterial Alchemy in play, turning them into Equipment to help buff up my vamps a little. Falkenrath Noble also proved his worth, pinging spot damage around the board whilst keeping my life topped up. Bloodlord of Vaasgoth provided good value as well, buffing up new Vampires that came into play, but ultimately came too late to swing the game.

Val: Not much. As we played, I realized I didn’t have nearly as many useful sacrifice outlets as I would expect. So, I decided to change my strategy and become the table's first sacrifice. I encouraged everyone to attack me, and eventually, I used Juri to finish myself off. Since he can deal damage to any target, I made myself the target, sacrificed him, and eliminated myself from the game.

Last place secured. £5 upgrade budget secured. Mission accomplished. 

Jon: I'm surprised it actually did the thing it was meant to! I actually got some Zombies out and way quicker than I thought for a cheap deck. My only thing is I need it even quicker. I'm a fan of flooding the board with silly amounts of tokens, so my steps going forward are upgrading to get that sac engine running faster and more efficiently! Loved seeing the others decks though, always fun watching people learn as they play! 

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

What do you think of the other players' decks now that you’ve had a chance to see them in action? Any guesses on who will do the best over the course of the league? 

Zach: The other decks are all pretty cool and despite us all having black in our identities and two of the players having Rakdos decks each one seems to be doing something different. Yes, I did lock down Rob’s commander early, I have Vampire-PTSD in Magic and just didn’t want it doing things, also it was hilarious after he barely managed to finally get enough mana to play it. The Zombie Cats Jon is playing seemed to actually play really well and I’m scared that a couple of decent, even cheap, upgrades will make the deck very good. Val’s deck just didn’t do a lot and then he blew himself up, no idea what that might end up looking like but I’m not scared of it now; maybe that will change next game since he’s getting the most upgrades. 

Rob: I’m loving all the decks we have received, if anything because of the narrative that is being built. It’s like we have a city we are striving for control over, with Aristocrat Vampires preying on the people, a ‘good’ necromancer exploring for secrets beneath the city, a chaotic jester looking to cause all sorts of trouble, and a Zombie Cat with the extreme case of the zoomies. It’s honestly exciting just to see what stories will grow from these games.

As for who will do the best, it’s too early to tell, but I don’t think it will take long before we start seeing some real chaos in the streets.

Val: On first glance, I think Jon could be a real threat considering how hard he ramped during the first game. That damn “zoomies cat” got out of control quickly. Zach’s dungeon mechanic also feels like it could become the league’s version of “will you pay the one?” (Insert sarcastic yay here.)

Rob was dealing with Aristocrat Vampires, so I’m interested to see how that develops. Hopefully there are enough “servants” in the deck for those Vampires to actually do some work.

If I had to guess this early, Jon probably looks the most dangerous based purely on how quickly his deck got moving. But who knows because I don’t think any of these decks will look quite the same by the end.

And while everyone is watching the zoomies cat, tracking dungeons, and feeding the vampires, I’ll just be the jester in the background causing as much chaos as possible.

Jon: I'll be honest I was in and out of searching for HOBBIT cards for my new Hobbit deck that Zach brought me in a big box, however I did love Zach's deck and it felt incredibly on brand for him so I'm excited to see how he further develops that. Val killed himself quite quickly but looking forward to him fleshing it out! 

Onwards and Something or Other

First game in the bag and as Val made it very clear he took himself out early to secure that upgrading power. I say early, it was still a dozen or so turns into the game. This game was slow and took us well over an hour to get through, maybe closer to 90 minutes even? We all felt pretty tired and played a nice, quick, game with actually-decent decks after to even out the night!

So that leaves Val with £5 to upgrade his deck, Jon with £2, Rob with £1, and me with NOTHING. Means I'm stuck with my terrible mana base that is sure to bite me in the next game for at least one more round. I'm going to aim to get updates for this out as we go so stay tuned!
