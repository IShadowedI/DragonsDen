Trench Crusade Carcass Front Campaign Rules Review

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The salt-spewn seas have drawn each of us here. Our patience has paid dividends as we build and paint our warbands in preparation for the call to the front lines. We eagerly gather our friends, bless our sacred dice, and prepare glorious terrain upon which to lay models to rest. It is finally time for us to venture into the Carcass Front!

As a local campaign organizer for my community, I have been so ecstatic since the announcement to see how the creative minds behind Trench Crusade see a narrative campaign unfolding. And I am so happy to say it is just as buck wild and fun as I was hoping. From in-depth and interesting campaign progression, wild and unique missions, and new content to explore and use in all your games; buckle up because the seas are a bit choppy. Let’s swan dive straight into the Carcass Front Campaign!

We would like to thank Factory Fortress Inc for sending us a preview copy of this box for review.

New Patrons

Heretic Captain Credit: Mildnorman

In this campaign we anticipated a faithful and heretical patron aligned with the opposing sides in the region; however the Iron Sultanate tossed their hat in the ring as we have 3 new patrons. The book specifically mentions that all three new Patrons are available not only for Carcass Front, but all campaigns moving forwards. So let's dive headfirst into the deep end with our new leadership.

Blessed Bartolomeo; the Faithful warbands' new patron option. A war prophet that has reached near-sainthood and a vital soul against the heretical efforts on the coast, he offers a multitude of boons that coincide with the theme of pious suffering. With ways to reduce injury rolls, gain blessing markers, and gain flat +Injury Result; Bartolomeo offers all rounded options to warbands looking to survive the cursed war fronts. The Truth of Suffering is a personal stand out choice, offering a flat -1 Dice to injury results targeting the model. The size restraints are minimal, as this model combined with Machine Armour and Martyrdom pills is going to be nearly unkillable.

War Priest Charon; the Heretic warbands' new patron option. We finally get a glimpse at the big bad the Eirie isles has been dealing with, and boy do his visuals sell him well. He offers a surprising amount of movement shenanigans with Wings of Blooded Iron and We are the Storm. Hands down the immediate eye candy though is Master of Death. Automatic 2 to ANY weapon while it is short range with no other limiters is insane. And even if given to a weapon already with automatic, it ups it by 1. Assault Rifles become mobile Machine Guns, Flamethrowers spout even more fire, or double barrel grenade launchers / Anti-Material Rifles wreak mayhem on the field. This ability is going to single handedly push players towards a heretic allegiance.

House of Wisdom; the Iron Sultanate-only new patron option. This was a surprise to see, and a welcome addition to the pool of patrons. If you love the customization that House of Wisdom gives, this patron is going to captivate you wholly. From ignoring keywords, stealing ammo, and mutating our normal lads like they are Tawkin (love me some more mutations); there is so much customization and depth that this Patron offers. The mutation skills are a personal favorite of mine, but the additional keyword ignoring offered by Elemental Savant is going to always be universally good. If it weren’t for Charon, I think House of Wisdom might have stolen the show.

Path to Leviathan

Beyond the map based campaign that is Carcass Front, there is also the narrative campaign Path to Leviathan. This is designed for two opposing warbands, one faithful and one fallen, as they compete for their patrons during the narrative that unfolds. This is not a restriction but a recommendation, as any warbands and their variants can very easily be utilized for this narrative campaign.

The campaign utilizes five scenarios in a set order from the Carcass Front scenario list. They could be played as one-off games with no campaign steps or exploration, or as a campaign following either the Core Rulebook rules or Carcass Fronts version. Regardless of what you choose, the story told will remain the same as two warbands uncover the Path to Leviathan and the treacherous plot at hand.

The Narrative

Sacred Affliction Ecclesiastic Prisoner - bre4d_

In warning to the faithful, the Lord of Hosts sent a vision to Gaius, the Prophet of Mud, of the Procession of the Sacred Affliction. The vision prophesied the expected return of Leviathan, a titanic monster created by the Lord. Gripped with Envy and Desire, the great beast once attempted to take more than their station allowed, and so God trapped the beast in the Abyss, chained and alone. Now those chains begin to break, and so the Lord tasked those of faith to ensure Leviathan never roams the seas or ascends once again. 

The prophecy of Leviathan is no longer something of myth, for Blessed Bartholomeo, a member of the Sacred Affliction, has witnessed the great Serpent itself. They have witnessed the Seven Golden Stars fall, and the Abyss tremor in response. Bartholomeo sees the opportunity to not only defeat the beast, but consume it, and wear its mighty scales as armour, for the glory of God and those of the true faith. 

It is not only the faithful who have become aware of Leviathan’s possible return, for the War Priest Charon aims to ensure the beast’s return and supplant the head of the Serpent of Envy, with their reward in becoming a lord of Hell himself. 

The narrative of the Leviathan campaign unfolds across several scenarios. Players start out searching for the knowledge to summon and bind Leviathan in the Ruins of Nineveh Novus, find the necessary materials to ensure the binding succeeds in the Steel Necropolis, and ultimately decide the fate of Leviathan at their Alter. The important point is that the ending of a campaign depends on the winning side, Faithful or Heretical.

How It Works

As mentioned, the Leviathan campaign can be played as a series of one-off games or as a campaign, increasing your Ducat limit each game, and with all the post-game experiences that come with it. There are 5 scenarios on offer, and players may choose to play those 5, extend the campaign by using regular scenarios from the Core Rule Book, or use the Random Scenario Generator. The only important points are to play the Leviathan Scenarios in order, with the Altar of Leviathan being the last campaign game. 

As with the Carcass Front campaign, players may choose one of the new Patrons released: Blessed Bartholomeo, War-Priest Charon, and the House of Wisdom Patron. You can, of course, pick one of the other Patrons from the Core Rulebook. 

Ultimately, there are 3 possible outcomes in the campaign: a draw, a minor victory for one player, or a major victory for one player. 

  • Draw: This occurs if neither player can summon the great beast
  • Minor Victory: A player manages to summon Leviathan, but is not able to subdue the beast, as they do not control the Railway Cannon from Scenario 4: The Sword of God. 
  • Major Victory: A player manages to summon and subdue the beast, succeeding in achieving their ultimate goal of controlling the beast. 

Carcass Front Campaigns

Lazarist Castigator. Credit: Dandy

Why have we all gathered in the Carcass Front? Some fight for the church after hearing the calls of Bartolomeo requests for aid, while others heed the warchants of Charon. Regardless of how they came to be here, all are here for the same reason; something churns in the ocean deep that can dictate the outcome of the war in this region. Some may be here to stop this effort, spurn it forward, or gather riches while others are distracted thusly.

What makes this campaign unique amongst others? Typically campaigns follow a fairly linear format; players play progressive games and progress accordingly in very similar fashions with the same goals. The Carcass Front campaign pushes this idea beyond that standard however. Due to the implementation of vision cards randomly dictating players goals in the campaign, resources offer multiple ways for scoring Victory Points additionally, and scoring based off the campaign tracker. There are far more ways to score than just the scenarios that take place and battles that occur.

Another important addition is the determination of Aggressor and Battle Zone when playing your games. The Aggressor is the player who has been the Aggressor fewest between the players battling, roll-off for ties, and they will choose the Battle Zone. There are a few rules for determining the battle zone as well; it must be either linked to your entry zone, previously scouted, or linked to a scouted zone.

Each warband’s starting zone on the map is also their war camp! Over time, warbands will have opportunities to grow this camp by adding buildings to them or upgrading previously built buildings. There are four different buildings with three tiers of upgrades for warbands to build: Depot, Garrison, Shrine, and Vault. They can add direct effects such as influencing exploration rolls, gaining resources, or gaining other powerful effects.

Overall, there’s a lot of extra stuff to track here in addition to your standard campaign homework. If you’re a practiced trenchie looking for something new after your fifth campaign, this is a good way to spice things up. If you’re relatively new to the game, maybe hold off on this one unless you wanna dive head first into the deep end.

The Map

The main draw of the campaign is the campaign map. The map adds another dimension to how players can interact with the system. The map is a network of lines from the outer edges that converge on the Altar of Leviathan, with location nodes peppered throughout. Each of these locations are designed to feel special, with none feeling like filler. From the very start of the campaign player choice of direction and exploration matters.

The campaign starts with the determination of Entry Zones which dictates the starting location for players on the map. With four options to choose from, it will be vital for warbands to make this choice carefully, as each has their own pluses and minuses. Each Entry Zone is set on the sides of the map, allowing players to determine what their avenue of entry will be, with each starting zone having two connected zones, none standing above the rest for connectivity. It's worth noting that with only four starting zones, you’ll likely have folks sharing entry zones and that’s fine. This isn’t a “Paint the map” campaign where you’ll be taking control from each other. Instead its more about where you choose to invest and explore to gain resources.

Speaking of resources, each territory you have an outpost in gives you some. Each territory has a series of letters correlating to the Resources; F - Favor, R - Relics, S - Supplies, T - Territories. All four options can score you additional Victory points, but they also offer additional bonuses that make them worth pursuing depending upon your warband's intentions. Glory seekers may prefer favor, whereas those wishing to explore every corner of the map might prefer Territories. Your choices of zone exploration will affect how you interact with the campaign directly.

Vision Cards

Stigmatic Nun. Credit: Dandy

The Vision Deck is a pack of 16 cards which are distributed to each player at the beginning of the campaign. Each player is dealt two cards and they can pick one to commit to for the campaign. The cards themselves are relatively straight forward, asking for objectives like “scout a bunch of territories” or “have a bunch of your guys die.” These cards are dealt in secret and chosen in secret, as they are your warband's true goals guided by your patron. This means that each player will be after differing goals entirely, their actions and choices in the game being altered towards their goals creating unique playthroughs for each participant.

On these cards will be three goals to complete, with the rewards being cumulative for final scoring at the end of the campaign when it is revealed. With only 16 cards in the deck, you’ll run out of cards to deal after eight players. The book recommends adding more vision packs, but you can also take the discarded cards and divvy them out to remaining players. That said, this means players who pick later will have less choice and will be working with cards other players have deemed more difficult to do, so you’ll want to be careful with this.

Campaign Phase

Once you’re all set up you can start playing games. First you’ll select your Battle Zone and your Scenario based on that. After you play your game the steps you’ll take will depend on if you were the Aggressor or not. If you won the game as the Aggressor, you’ll gain two resources from the zone, mark off a win in your tracker, and add the zone you played in to your “Scouted zones” (basically tracking where you’ve played) and add an Outpost to it. If you lost, you only get 1 Resource, but still scout the zone. Regardless you’ll explore, gaining ducats equal to 5x your roll. The non-aggressor meanwhile will always gain a Resource from the zone and gain ducats via an exploration roll (but won’t explore!), but you’ll scout the area and get an outpost if you win.

Note: It’s worth mentioning the book somewhat contradicts itself here. The Post Game Summary block says you’ll always scout as the non-Aggressor and can never build an Outpost, where the rules in those sections reflect the above. This will likely get clarified quickly after release, but for now we recommend going with the rules in the appropriate sections.

This is a pretty major departure from standard campaigns. For one, you’ll only explore in half your games. This is kind of lame, considering exploration is one of the most exciting parts of the post game step, but there are no blank parts of the table in Carcass Front campaigns, so on average you’ll get results just as much and every resource has you explore for free when you get the second resource of a type. Next, you’re only gaining 5x the roll in Ducats, not 10x. You’ll be getting gear and money from Supply related explorations, so if you want to ramp up, that’s the way to do it.

There are two new steps after your games: the Campaign Tracker Step & the Outposts Step. The Campaign Tracker Step has you update the Campaign Tracker (included in the box) based on the results of the game. As these boxes are filled out in the tracker, VP and campaign bonuses will be unlocked and scored over time. These bonuses can be things like rolls on the exploration table, bonus Resources, more Exploration Dice, and exploration skills.

The most impactful bonuses though are the Campaign Buildings you’ll build as you gain as you collect resources. As you gain resources (at 3, 8 and 13 obtained) you’ll unlock a new tier of building. Every first tier gives you the ability to manipulate your exploration rolls on the relevant table by 1; the second tier will always give you an additional dice when rolling on those tables as well as a special bonus, and tier 3 gives you another special bonus. Supplies will build a Depot, which can increase your Threshold Value by a whopping 200 points at tier 2 and the ability to remove a Scar and Trauma from your guys at tier 3. Territories will build a Garrison, which lets you reduce the cost of Glory Items purchased from a merchant by 1 (this is a common result from Territory exploration tables) at tier 2 and give you a free Mercenary at tier 3.  Favour will build the Shrine which gives you a free Advancement for a model with Leader at Tier 2 and Strong and +1 Injury in melee for a model in your warband at tier 3. Lastly, there’s the Vault which is built from relics, which gives you two picks from a list of results on the exploration table instead of 1 at tier 2, and a free Glory item at tier 3.

This means players will likely want to specialize in one or two resources, but dipping into a few gives you a wide range of bonuses. These are also quite thematic which will let you play into the themes of your warband and help fill any gaps. This adds some really neat player expression to the campaign step and gives you some fun milestones to go for.

During the Outpost Step, the winning player will build an outpost in the battle zone that was selected prior to the battle. A warband can only have one outpost per zone, but any number of different warbands can have an outpost in the same zone. These outposts provide a defense bonus of starting units with shovels and cover while battling within their battle zone, a potential scoring bonus at the end of campaign to the player with the most, and there are special zones that grant passive bonuses like half priced armor or weapons if you have an outpost there.

Lastly we have the reworked Exploration Step. The Aggressor will always scout the battle zone post match, but the opposing player will only end up scouting if they won the match. We also have a special Exploration Table to roll on which includes number rangers instead of flat numbers (1-3 vs 2). 

Only the Aggressor will roll on the table however, when the other player rolls their exploration they only get their Ducats from the roll unless they roll 3 matching numbers. If they do, then the agents of Rudolph's Folly (a mercenary air group) will aid them in their next game. This manifests in either a strafing run, which is a multi step attack process where you can shoot a bunch of enemy models, or an aerial bombardment. The bombardment is an odd one as it has you print out a paper circle, hold it in your hand above the battlefield, and then drop it to see where the bomb lands. 

While this is cute and gives the defender something to hope for when rolling their exploration, this is a bit overdone for what it's bringing to the table. There can be a bit of feels bad when showing up to the game knowing your opponent has a free board wide threat and its all a bit fiddly in the actual execution. I’m sure Magic the Gathering old heads are stoked to see the return of Chaos Orb though.

Scenarios

Leviathal Chorus Credit: Mildnorman

First off, those from way back before Trench Crusade 1.0 will remember the original Random Scenario Generator. I am happy to say it has returned in full force and as an even cleaner four page spread. This is immediately getting printed out for one off battle usage at game nights! All our favorite missions and deployments return with some cleanup and polishing.

As far as the actual campaign scenarios, you get five to pick from with one, The Altar of Leviathan, being tied to the endgame of the campaign. The scenarios themselves will be familiar to veterans of the trenches, tasking you with carrying markers, holding key points and doing actions for scoring. The scenarios are symmetrical so you and your opponent will be trying to accomplish the same tasks. 

There’s a double edged aspect to all these scenarios in that they all have bespoke models and terrain. Two have NPC models that will wander the table and attack your guys and the other 3 have terrain pieces that go with them. Smartly, the TC team have included ways to represent them with things you may have lying around and it seems you’ll be able to print most of them either via STL or 2d image on cardboard, but it still feels like just more stuff you’re gonna have to carry to your games. The upside is these make the missions all feel significant and bombastic, like you’re fighting over actual locations with actual personalities instead of “ruined city #5”.

Final Thoughts

Brandon “Zaros” Oelfke: I think there is so much content that for those who have been playing Trench Crusade for a while, this is a treasure trove. I think the true Carcass Front campaign is really complex for new players and definitely the narrative of Path to Leviathan should be the new player focus before throwing them in the deep end of Carcass Front. The new patrons are absolutely wild, and the addition of an Iron Sultanate one was a pleasant surprise. I think this campaign box is going to be an absolute blast for everyone and was definitely worth the wait.

Norman: Echoing Brandon’s point there’s definitely a lot here. I think there’s a ton of smart moves, having the path to leviathan campaign is great for folks looking to get started with this box and and random scenario generator is good for folks who will gravitate towards one offs. I’m not huge on just how much bespoke stuff this campaign asks for, but to their credit they offer alternatives at every turn. The book and background itself are all very cool, with tons of fun lore sections and art to peruse. If I had to pick something to nitpick, its probably the defender exploration/Rudolphs Folly situation; it feels like an overcomplicated solution to a somewhat simple problem. I have no doubt though this will be a blast to go through with friends.

DGMP: This very much feels like something meant for people who already have a campaign or two under their belts and is more complicated to give them more systems to fiddle with and sink their teeth into. And in my opinion it really succeeds with that, adding a lot of new bits and bobs to mess with and see how they work. Plus I have always been a sucker for a good map based campaign. But it is very much not something I would suggest you start out playing. Path to Leviathan is what you should do as a starter, this feels like something you set up for a play group to go through over a month or two to try out a new campaign system. My only real complaints are very small, a few quibbles over exactly how a thing is worded or not having certain information clearly explained or stated where it is located. But overall, I really think this will be a great experience to go through with your local group, if you have at least 4 players.
