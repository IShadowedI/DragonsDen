Mayday, Miss Marcy! Gaming Grandma, Deductive Disruption, Rolling Badly as a Joke

August 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome once again to Mayday, Miss Marcy, as we kick off the month of August with a brand new slate of questions for you all to read, think about, and possibly glean some advice from! It feels like Summer has just arrived, but here we are transitioning towards Fall and the changing of seasons once again, with more than half the year already behind us. And honestly, for this little advice column, that means a lot! We've had a lot of submissions and I've enjoyed answering your questions for well over a year now. I look forward to even more questions and answers to come, and if you'd like to submit your questions to this very column, please read the instructions below!

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

ALSO, we're heading OUT of Summer, so if you have some Back to School, Fall, or similar turn of the seasons questions, let us hear it!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

Last week, we took on a few questions ranging from being worried about not being woke enough while perhaps being too woke, regifting outside of the Holiday season, and "cute" behavior that is anything but. This week, I've got a new crop of questions for you all, so let's get to them!

We Can All Hope to Be This Grandma

Hello,
My grandmother is 92 years old and recovering from a bad fall.  She used to have a lot of hobbies but due to the fall and some nerve damage in her hands, she can't engage with most of them.
A few months before the fall, we got her an iPad and she has some games on there. She tends to use it most when she's alone, and when she does, she has some games loaded on there.I've been talking with her recently and I don't think these games are good. They're the kind that are technically free, but are riddled with advertisements and paid win mechanics.
I want to help her find some better ipad games, but my choices are very limited. The nerve damage means that she can't do anything with precision so anything that requires timing, is out. She is not a person who's played video games much at all for the majority of her life, and she's into person who likes a lot of violent anything that involves combat is out. And I definitely don't want to get her hooked on gacha or gambling.
Do you have any suggestions for tablet games that don't suck? Grandma likes crossword puzzles and solitaire, and she seemed a little interested in Balatro but hasn't wrapped her head around paying for the game, or that its based on Poker without gambling. I was also thinking Slay the Spire, but I dont think she'd pick up on it. Florence is an option as well, but i don't think she's realized that games have stories
Thanks,
Gamer McGamington
Dear Gamer,
I think it's sweet that you are helping your Grandmother find something to do and keep her active, which is pretty admirable; it isn't easy watching people we care for age and have bouts of problems that can be life-changing despite how simple they seem to be. I think I can make a few suggestions, and hope that they'll be helpful to her recovery and entertainment, as well as something the two of you can bond over!
First, you mentioned Balatro, and I think that's probably one of the best "game" recommendations I can think. I think Balatro has some pretty solid hurdles to getting into it, since it is really a roguelike more than a card game, but I do believe that perhaps if you work with her it can be something that can be fun for her to play around with. I think I'd recommend that over Slay the Spire, really, since I think Slay the Spire is a bit more dense than Balatro is, and it might be simpler for her to work within the realm of card game terms and ideas (as she very likely understands poker hands, I assume?).
I did want to touch on a different sort of thing, which is Puzzmo, and it does have an app. Puzzmo has a lot of the "usual" newspaper games, like crosswords and the like, and has some originals that are all simple and fun to play. I also like Puzzmo more because it isn't tied to an aging newspaper that sucks, although you will need to pay a subscription fee for her to be able to play the app. Still, it gives new games and puzzles constantly, so it can be a great relaxer and it has some of those good mental skill strengtheners that she can benefit from.
I know you mentioned gambling and gacha games, so I wanted to take a minute to sort of separate the two: there are a lot of gambling games out there that have actual real money gambling and "fake" gambling that costs money, which I find pretty abhorrent and often very predatory towards kids and older adults. My own elderly parents have had some run-ins with these types of games on their phones, and I'm always shocked at how predatory these are. I applaud you for wanting to avoid them and to keep her away from them. I will say that gacha games are different, but ironically, there are not a lot that may fit what you're looking for; games like Arknights, for example, still requires fairly good reflexes and timing, and even Umamusume requires a LOT of concentration. But another hurdle is that you mention she may not really be aware of or enjoy gaming stories, and a lot of gacha games are narrative heavy; Arknights has chapters that have nearly 80,000 words in them, Reverse 1999 and Limbus Company also tend to be very reading intensive, and so there's a good chance she'd bounce off of them.
Aside from Balatro and Puzzmo, which I do think are some of your best bets, I will send you a question: do you have a Switch? If so, pick up a copy of Clubhouse games and bring it with you. While it might be hard for her to play by herself, the two of you can play games together using it and a single console with two controllers, and that might allow you two to have some easy fun (And before someone says 'just bring the actual board games!', consider that is a lot of picking up and putting down!!). However, you can also get a copy of Picross™: Juufuutei Raden™'s Guide for Pixel Museum for Switch and let her play that too, as it doesn't require a lot of dexterity and is an EXCELLENT puzzle game that she can enjoy at her leisure. I hope some of these suggestions help!

The Culprit is You!

Dear Miss Marcy,
Recently, I had a TTRPG campaign that I was a part of come undone. The DM was running the campaign as a pretty standard adventure, but at one part in the campaign, they wanted to do a bit of a mystery aspect. Our team really enjoyed it, and perhaps we enjoyed it a little too much, because one of our players ended up deducing the mystery pretty much immediately. This... was not ideal. The DM didn't really react very well to getting the entire mystery "solved", and then tried to continually throw up road-blocks to why the characters could not solve the mystery yet, which then started to create a pretty unfun situation.
I'm not really sure if there is even a solution here, but it feels like we were being punished for having figured out the "trick", and the DM didn't really want to roll with it; it basically killed our group, and while I'm not expecting an answer to solve that, I guess I just want to know if we did anything wrong?
Curious Clueless

Dear Curious,

What is interesting is that your question presents a far more interesting mystery than the situation itself probably did. There are a lot of unfortunate aspects to doing interactive "mysteries", which can be anything from the author of the mystery not really logically thinking through all of the ways the answer needs to be "found", to the players not really being able to deduce the mystery itself. Although detective fiction is very popular, not everyone is really equipped with or enjoys that sort of deductive reasoning that fills the world of mystery fiction, so it is already kind of a fraught idea to bring to a D&D table. There's also then all of the D&D aspects that can go wrong: bad rolls, bored players, missed details, players acting against the will of the DM, etc., and of course bad dming, one of the constants.

It sounds like, however, that you aren't giving me all of the details here. You're asking for absolution in that your party figured out the mystery and "ruined" it, but you aren't telling me HOW you ruined it. Because the reality is that YOU figuring out the mystery doesn't matter, your CHARACTERS needed to figure it out. So did you just railroad your character(s) all having divine inspiration and pointing at the culprit immediately, or did you actually roleplay that you still needed to figure out the mystery? I can believe that your DM likely got defensive and hurt or annoyed by what was going on, but I think you aren't giving me enough information to tell you if what you did was right. It sounds very likely that the outcomes are almost equal: from my perspective, your DM being a jerk and taking their ball and going home is about as likely as your party being insufferable and forgetting that you had to play the role of characters, characters who probably were not spun up as a league of detectives. I think you should perhaps just take this as a lesson that roleplaying requires players to play roles, and DMs to guide those roles to fun experiences, and that neither side should be trying to "win".

Dicey Situation

Dear Marcy,
I roll terribly. Like, almost statistically improbably terrible. I don't know anyone who rolls as bad as I do at a table, and I seem to do it no matter what dice I'm using or what is going on.
The problem is I used to write it off as a joke, and play into it in good faith and sportsmanship, but lately it is, frankly, pissing me off. I lose constantly, things always go not my way, and the constant rolling bad feels like I'm just not meant to play miniature games.
I realize I am asking for advice about how to roll dice better, but seriously, what can I do?
So Tired of 1s

Dear So Tired,

I really, really, really, really, really, really, really, really, really, really really really really REALLY REALLY REALLY REALLY want to say "skill issue" but I'm being so respectful right now you have no idea.

So, instead, I will say: Skill issue, but not the type you think. The issue is your mindset. You mention that you "always" roll bad, almost statistically so, but the reality is that I think your mindset is just negatively geared towards seeing those outcomes and excluding others. Because if you are seriously rolling nothing but 1s every time you grab a grip of dice, you should be studied in a lab by mathematicians, since the improbability of that borders on something we should ask about in Hammer of Math.

Instead, I think you've actually just accepted the mantle of "good sport loser", and you don't want to be that. And so to do that, you don't have to roll better, you have to play better. First, stop thinking that you will always roll badly whenever you pick up dice, and second, look to play your armies in fashions that will reduce the amount of times you need to roll dice, especially defensively. If you play more alert and concerned with your board state, you can start mitigating losses by avoiding them entirely. Second, stop being a "good sport" about being made fun of for doing badly; that just sets you up for being abused by the people at your tables, and tells other players that you either allow, accept, or won't push back at that type of talk.

Your issue is not some curse from the gods, it is a mental block that you've started to use to blame for your bad and likely sloppy play. Playing erratically and over aggressively is liable to make you roll more dice, and the more you roll against your own terms, the worse you are likely to do. Study deployments, look at how players are playing your army successfully, look at what counters you and how you can counter that, and stop blaming everything on dice: the only person responsible for having a bad time at the table is you.
