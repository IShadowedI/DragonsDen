What We Know About 11th Edition 40k So Far: Recapping the New Rules Reveals Through May 22nd

May 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer
Welcome back to our weekly recap of the reveals of 11th Edition Warhammer 40k. We're now in the second week of Games Workshop's Faction Focus reveals, complete with newly-discussed Detachments as well as core rules. Getting a glimpse at the systems & philosophy behind 11th Edition 40k via these previews has been a treat, and there's no sign of stopping the excitement until Armageddon drops!

If you missed any of our coverage so far, you can find the older articles here:
  • Adepticon First Look
  • Reveals Through April 11 - Detachments, List Building, Force Dispositions, Cover, Objectives, Towering, Plunging Fire, Terrain
  • Reveals Through May 1 - Engagement Range, Charging, Reserves, Pile-ins and Consolidation, Fights First
  • Reveals Through May 9th - Battle-shock, Stealth, Close Quarters, Heavy, Orks, Marines, Imperial Guard, Tyranids, Chaos Space Marines
  • Reveals Through May 15th - Hazardous, Combat Disembark, Crushing Impact, Cleave, Detachment Tags, Sisters, World Eaters, T'au, Custodes, Death Guard
At the current pace of reveals, a Faction Focus will be released for each faction by early June. The goal with this recap series is to condense & extrapolate using the information that's been officially revealed, and boil it down to exactly what you need to know. With that prelude out of the way, what's new this week?

Knights Pose Problems in 11th

Before we cover the individual Detachments showcased for the forces of both Imperial & Chaos Knights, it's worth covering a few prior reveals to provide a bit of context. First and foremost, Detachments are set to arrive with one of five different Force Dispositions, which alters the mechanism by which an army scores Primary VP; this matters less for well-rounded factions, but Knights are certainly not among that class. Historically, the main means of beating Knights in a list not packing sufficient anti-armor firepower is to outscore them. When the way you scored the majority of your points came down to having enough units stand in the right place, the faction(s) that often puts fewer than a dozen models on the table is disadvantaged—this is the opportunity cost to playing a list that skews so heavy, and sees your models shrug off a huge chunk of opposing attacks by default. The idea of asymmetric scoring was always going to come down to Knights, because of their innate skew, so it's worth bearing in mind when those characteristics of their Detachments are made known.

Credit: Games Workshop.

As for new Detachments, both Chaos & Imperial Knights received mirrored 1DP options, each focusing on the largest non-Horus Heresy chassis (the Dominus/Tyrant), and their smallest, the Armigers.
  • Beginning with those loyal to the Imperium, Dominus Foebreakers offsets the innate downside of firing into units in Terrain Areas for your massive ranged platforms. Historically, the Dominus chassis hasn't been especially competitive, but Knights are likely to be met with more reductions on their accuracy than ever before, so it's hard to say. The ability to remove BLAST helps a ton to avoid getting bogged down in melee, so while the Datasheets themselves don't inspire on their own, this might be the right set of benefits to make you consider the inclusion of a Knight Castellan, doubly so with the changes to HAZARDOUS we covered last week.
  • Throne-bound Outriders similarly offsets the downsides of firing into cover, this time for Armigers. The fact Knights can shrug off the intended buff to tankiness for those sitting in Ruins with a huge chunk of their lineup is not lost on me, and given the relative mobility (and cost) of Armigers it's likely Infantry is no problem to clear for the faction in 11th. Couple that with the capacity to shrug off another theoretical problem for the faction with their Neural Lash Stratagem—being Battle-shock—and it feels like the designers have given Knights all the tools they'll need to succeed.
  • Finally, there's an update provided to Questor Forgepact, the soup Detachment that allows for the use of up to 500 points of select Admech units. The choices remain the same, but notably both flavors of Skitarii here arrive with the ability to take free wargear that provides Ignores Cover, which has more value in 11th. The fact this Detachment is being previewed alongside the above Dominus Foebreakers, and already incentivizes you to take the bigger Knight chassis, is not lost on us. The buffs to Skitarii also change, from being semi-conditional rerolls to hit and wound to +1BS and HEAVY while within 6" of a Knight. This is a sidegrade at best, and does hurt their capacity to spike damage onto tougher targets, but you weren't really taking these tin soldiers for their damage. Ultimately, the playability of this version likely hinges on the point cost of Dominus chassis Knights, and whether they retain the ability to Overwatch via their Thronegeist Fury strat, as they can in 10th. That Conflagration Cannon is no joke!
Before we cover Chaos Knights, it's worth noting is that unlike essentially every other Faction Focus, there was a gesture made that not all these options are 1DP. Reading between the lines, that likely means both Questor Forgepact, and its Chaotic counterpart, are 2DP. Given the relative strength of being able to take objective play pieces, especially in the case of the free-sticky Skitarii Rangers, this is probably for the best.

Knight Tyrant. Credit: Rockfish
  • As a dark mirror to the forces of the Corpse Emperor, the two new Detachments for Chaos Knights also highlight the capacity for their largest & smallest non-Horus Heresy chassis to...ignore negatives to-hit. Joy. Bastions of Tyranny provides a similar +1 to hit, this time against Battle-shocked units as opposed to those in Terrain Areas. Thankfully this solely benefits Tyrants, but does keep them uncontested as Chaos Knights' premier ranged option...in theory, anyway. Still, as a potentially massive buff to mobility there's also a 1CP strat to give these Tyrants Fall-back and Shoot, allowing you to position with greater lenience.
  • Hunting Warpack is meant to help your other Knights take down cowering enemy forces, letting your War Dogs mark a foe within 12" for +6" of Detection Range. The majority of effects we've seen of this sort are either conditional (via a Stratagem for +6", in Death Guard) or only +3" (a number of 1DP Detachments), so this is no doubt a powerful option for Chaos Knight pilots. Given the army's identity has for its majority hinged on taking a ton of War Dog chassis, that's likely to continue, with this effect helping both things like Executioners make use of their Autocannons' range, but also big Knights that don't necessarily want to step up.
  • Iconoclast Fiefdom is the first Detachment that seems a genuine dud, with it affording a spread of available Damned units up to 500pts, and giving them rerolls for their Leadership. The Dark Pact-style effect for your Knights is gone, meaning the majority of the value here is in being able to take chaff, or potentially a spooky Accursed Cultist brick. If this is 2DP, it's likely too high an opportunity cost for the benefit, but at 1DP suddenly you have a ton of flexibility, and likely sees Chaos Knights field radically different-looking lists to their Imperial cousins-in-arms. Perhaps the removal of their Pact-style SUSTAINED/LETHAL is implying that, it's hard to say.
The design of the 1DP Knight Detachments here isn't inspiring confidence; the crux of what they do is make attempts at ignoring the changes made in 11th which might hamper pilots of these armies. That feels a bit...'hand-holdy', from the viewpoint of this author, which is a shame for an edition priding itself on more terrain-forward gameplay.

Upgrades Highlight Granularity

One of the Enhancements for the above Throne-bound Outriders Detachment had an additional keyword, 'Upgrade', which continues the efforts we've seen in 11th to increase the grit within listbuilding. We've heard that some weapon options, such as the main guns for things like Riptide Battlesuits, will have individual point costs as a return to form for Warhammer...but that's also true of the updated Enhancement system. Upgrades can be taken multiple times, helping to kit out key units and build powerful bricks of things like Terminators. This is a fantastic piece of design that can be used to differentiate 1DP Detachments further from their Datasheet, and means players looking to go all-in on specific unit types don't need to pick and choose some of their buffs.

Emperor's Children Pick Their Poison

Emperor's Children Army. Credit: Magos Sockbert

It's no secret that the model range for Emperor's Children (and to a lesser extent, World Eaters) is a bit thin compared to their brethren, and the Detachments highlighted in their Faction Focus do a ton to offset that. Whether you're fielding waves of pink-adorned troops, haughty swordsmen, or bricks of Terminators, you're rewarded for playing them...in excess. Jokes aside, these push you to pilot fairly skewed lists, and whether that's a good thing remains to be seen.
  • Elegant Brutes is your Terminator Detachment, but it's one of the best we've seen yet. On the turn your Terminators arrive, they have +1 to Charge, whether from Deep Strike or a Land Raider. This is helped by their Datasheet rule, receiving free rerolls on their Charge at the same target you shot at; at worst, flexing a 5-man unit this way gives you a ~66% chance to hit a charge out of Deep Strike for no cost but the unit itself, and 1DP. The one issue is that (at time of writing) the maximum amount of Terminators you can take is 3x5. This doesn't stop Elegant Brutes from being great as a potential way to access trade pieces with Deep Strike, but boy it would be nice to take termies in 10s. Imagine for a second, if you wanted to go all-in, taking this alongside Peerless Bladesmen and suiting up 2x10 with Frenzied Ferocity for free SUSTAINED HITS 1. That has appeal, especially with a 1CP strat to pull back to reserves.
  • The throngs of the Frenzied Host gives your Battleline +1S during the turn they Advanced or Fell-back, and that's for both shooting and melee. EC's rank-and-file was already arguably the best part of the army for their points, so getting to wound most MEQ bodies on 3s is nothing to scoff at. There's even an AoC-style strat while on an Objective, which helps them survive til the Fall-back and counterpunch. As a generic 1DP choice, this feels like something you'll see often when not drawn to Terminators or Flawless Blades
  • Speaking of, the last Detachment shown is Spectacle of Slaughter, providing all your aforementioned Flawless Blades with Fights First. This isn't nearly as insane as it once may have been, with the changes to how the sequence is resolved, but what it does mean is that opponents essentially can't charge 2 different Flawless Blades units without risking their push being interrupted. The rest of the Detachment is all about giving these squads freedom of movement, with an Upgrade for +2", and a Stratagem to Charge through units.
On the whole, these are all great options for Emperor's Children to have, and allow players to specialize their force towards a specific sheet(s), given many existing Detachments are somewhat generic. While it depends greatly on points, jamming some cheap Terminators into an opponent's backline 2-out-of-3 times seems like a great way to burn an extra DP, if your force isn't deep on Battleline, so I'm hoping to see them hit the tabletop for those less risk-averse players. The one blind spot is a lack of a Noise Marine-centric option, though that feels like an obvious add-on during the course of 11th Edition, so I wouldn't be too worried.

Flying Makes a Design Pivot

Credit: Games Workshop.

Pun very much intended, because the rules surrounding models with FLY are changing in 11th, to be in line with the changes to Pivoting we saw in 10th. Now, by giving up 2" of your movement, a unit with FLY can 'Take to the Skies', ignoring other units & Terrain Features. This is an amazing change, and gives units like Drukari Scourges and other shoot-and-scoot pieces even more opportunities to position safely. In their majority, Flying units are fairly fast, so giving up 2" isn't a huge ask, and with the beefy amount of midboard terrain now this feels like something you'll see frequently. Critically, you can Take to the Skies while making a Normal, Advance, Fall-back, or Charge move, but not a Surge move. Whether certain out-of-phase movement gets changed to reflect the new Surge rules will impact how important that tidbit is.

Grey Knights Purge with Purpose

Credit: Games Workshop; Colin Ward.

It's an odd thing for the Faction Focus on Grey Knights to be so similar to that of the Emperor's Children, but with both being fairly elite armies with modest ranges, that makes some measure of sense. The three 1DP options shown all benefit a single Datasheet, but in their capacity to be paired with more broad options in the Codex that likely feels okay, even if the faction's age is showing.
  • I want to start with Fires of Purgation, highlighting the faction's capacity for damage at range. This tacks on a Battle-shock roll when an enemy is Pinned via your Purgation Squads, with a -1 modifier, meaning you can potentially lock up a key Infantry threat from moving far, holding objectives, or being targeted by Stratagems. Better still, for 1CP you can provide them with SUSTAINED HITS 1...or DEVASTATING WOUNDS, which gets wild when you realize the squad can pack 4 TORRENT Incinerators or 6-shot Psilencers. These haven't been a mainstay of the army thus far, but I think you're provided some great potential for denying Primary here.
  • Paladins meanwhile get Argent Assault, giving them +1S when attacking a unit with higher T, essentially wounding everything with T11 or less on 4+. That's fine enough, but ideally you aren't bashing your expensive melee hammer into something they won't be crushing underfoot. There's potential for this being used alongside Grey Knights' dedicated Terminator Detachment, Hallowed Conclave, but encouraging you to see every defensive profile as nails when they took away your hammers in 10th feels rough.
  • Finally, Immaterial Interdiction pushes you to field Interceptors, and makes a pretty solid case for doing so. After they shoot, so long as they didn't arrive via Ingress in the same turn, you can make a Surge Move instead of your Normal Move seen on the Datasheet. While we don't know the comprehensive rules on Surge Moves just yet, from our understanding they do allow you to end in combat, and with Ingress not counting deploying via Transports (though this needs clarification), throwing Interceptors up the board in Rhinos seems like a blast. This is also a great way to avoid being Overwatched, meaning you trade the Charge Bonus for defensive flexibility.
These three Detachments call out units which didn't see universal play in the army during 10th, and depending on how Ingress & Surge Moves shake out, there's a chance for all three to potentially see play in the right list. The obvious winner is Purgation Squads, with access to some brutal shooting now, but the movement you get with Immaterial Interdiction might just be good enough.

Drukhari Divide and Conquer

Credit: Games Workshop.

Receiving three different 1DP Detachments in 11th, Drukhari armies are going to look fairly similar to their 10th iterations come the new edition. Each of them highlights a third of the faction (though calling Covens a third following the removal of Grotesques feels cruel), encouraging you to soup them together, or play a generalist Detachment with special focus on one branch. Each has a relevant tag to likely prevent it from being used with their dedicated branch Detachments, e.g. Kabalite Agonysts has the KABAL tag, as might Kabalite Cartel.
  • Speaking of, Kabalite Agonysts gives your BLADES FOR HIRE/KABAL shooting Sustained Hits 1: non-MONSTER/VEHICLE. This is the first time we've seen a specific wording like this, and it's a welcome change for rules that already do something similar. Condensing what would normally be a Datasheet rule, such as in the case of Admech's Hastarii, into one on a weapon leaves room for greater experimentation by the designers. The meat of this Detachment however is Shadows' Reach, keeping your relevant units in this Detachment from losing Hidden for 1CP after shooting. The obvious candidate here is Scourges, but Mandrake shooting is also solid, and greatly appreciates the conditional Sustained from the Detachment rule!
  • As a mirror to the Agonysts, Exhibition of Slaughter provides WYCH CULT units LETHAL HITS: non-MONSTER/VEHICLE in melee, but for 1CP you can gain Lethals period via their Stratagem Planned Strikes. I feel this likely doesn't measure up to the likes of the other two 1DP Detachments, given Drukhari clear Infantry with relative ease already, and if you wanted to play a ton of Wyches, Spectacle of Spite exists and is fantastic for finesse play.
  • Finally, Tools of Torment buffs exclusively the Talos/Cronos, giving them the classic -1S to attacks with a characteristic higher than their T. The relative ubiquity of that rule makes me wonder if it should have been keyworded, ah well. Coupling this with the Detachment's Upgrade, which provides a 5+ Invulnerable Save to the unit, and pairs of Talos can feel a lot beefier in something like Reaper's Wager. Essentially every Drukhari list takes at least 1 Cronos, and most run some number of Talos, so slotting this in feels like a pretty easy pick so long as there's ~5 or so of the relevant MONSTER in your list.
Drukhari are making the best of a modest model line following the post-Codex cull in 10th, and these should help the remaining tools in their arsenal feel just a bit better. If you don't already own your Scourges, now's a good time. The showcase of conditional weapon keywords is very interesting though, and I'd expect that to imply a number of clean-up erratas across the board to condense rules in that manner.

Inconsistencies Already?

Loading...


While the meat of reveals has been in the form of Faction Focus articles, a few of the actual Datasheets from Armageddon have been quietly arriving alongside them. For one, the new Eradicator Squad with Heavy Bolters, they lack the concise wording seen above with Drukhari for their Overlapping Detonations rule. Why wouldn't this be keyworded as BLAST 1: non-MONSTER/VEHICLE? Our answer potentially lies in another of Armageddon's Datasheet reveals, the Wartrakk; when it was first posted, the sheet had the ability to be attached to Warbikers as a SUPPORT. We don't know the exact wording of SUPPORT, but given the Armageddon box lacks bikers, it could be that we're not seeing the actual sheets of these units—what if these are just the rules that arrive in the box itself? To that end, I'd take all of these Datasheets with a grain of salt until we have physical copies, because figuring out if something like this is a genuine mistake or intentional simplification is rather difficult.

Thousand Sons Solidify Themes

Credit: Sky Serpent

The 10th Edition Thousand Sons book was very well-received, and for good reason—it's a great feeling to have some form of Psychic back. That trend continues in 11th, which makes PSYCHIC more than just a strict downside, your attacks with the keyword ignoring negative modifiers to hit, BS, or WS. There's two big winners from their Faction Focus, both of which crystallize build paths that were missing a little something in 10th.
  • To start with the Detachment that kickstarts its own version of the army, Sekhetar Cohort gives your robots' attacks PSYCHIC, and +1WS while within 6" of a PSYKER unit. That's almost always going to be online, given even Rubrics have that keyword, and means those Power Claw attacks could be coming in at an unmodifiable 3+ to hit. The hidden text here is what's missing though: A tag. There's a good chance you're able to take the Cohort alongside Warpforged Cabal, making your Vehicles truly monstrous to deal with, and leading to a mechanized Thousand Sons list being potentially very real.
  • As for the generic option, Ritual of Regeneration provides a unit that manifests a Ritual successfully with D3 Heal. While unconfirmed, it's been suggested that Healing in 11th automatically assumes model overflow, e.g. healing 3 on a Rubric unit could bring back 1.5 Marines; if that's the case, this gets frightening indeed. Another huge win is in the form of Eruption of Vitality, giving one INFANTRY/MOUNTED characters the chance to stand back up on a 2+ with 3 wounds remaining. This has always been a massive boon for positioning, and with the healing available, makes something like a Sorcerer in Terminator Armor very annoying to shift. As if they needed it, there's also a pseudo-Explosives for 1CP in melee, for any of your PSYKER units.
  • Finally, Servants of Change gives your Tzaangors Battleline, and MUTANTS +6" of Detection Range when shooting. In theory, those Bow Enlightened you're taking could pop off damage at a greater distance, but I just don't think this comes together for the Faction. They do have a sick Stratagem to give a relevant unit ASSAULT + Advance/Shoot and Charge...but not for Mutaliths, just your dinky MUTANTS.
Aside from the probable dud in Servants of Change, this is a fantastic Faction Focus for Thousand Sons, and coupled with the changes to PSYCHIC I expect the army to be well-positioned going into 11th. As a fan of niche lists, the idea of a mechanized Thousand Sons pile excites me, and between Sekhetar Cohort and the Defiler's arrival it may very well be worthwhile now.

More Armageddon Previews

As mentioned, we saw a few more Datasheets from Armageddon itself, with the Marines showcasing their Eradicators with Heavy Bolters & Land Speeder, and the Orks showing off their Wartrakk...and every Leader. Eradicators look like a solid choice for clearing Marine-equivalent units, with ample shots at D2, and Land Speeders are a (presumably) cheap way to rocket up the board and trade up with light vehicles. With FLY and the ability to move after shooting, positioning it at the corner of a Terrain Area and scooting back behind it is sure to annoy. The Wartrakk meanwhile showcases the 'Suppressed' keyword, giving a -1 to hit to something it shot, stacking of course with cover's -1BS. As for the greenskins' head honchos, it's about what you'd expect, but with the reminder present that these sheets are likely just for the Armageddon box itself. There would be riots in the streets if your Warboss wasn't able to bring a Power Klaw to the fray.

As a reminder, while SUPPORT hasn't been fully showcased, what we do know is that they can be attached in addition to your standard Leader, meaning a full block of Boyz could take both a, say, Bigboss & Bannernob. The former provides SUSTAINED HITS 1 in melee alongside a +1 to Charge, and the latter inspires with a 5+ Invulnerable and +1T (while the Waaagh! is active, anyway). Another Leader-Support pair is our last of the reveals, with the Weirdboy and Painboy. The Weirdboy has seen a substantial glow-up, now gaining powerful buffs based on the number of models in his attached unit, with +1S & +1D on his ranged attack for every 5, and an appropriate HAZARDOUS keyword if 10+. Da Jump is back, and can be used during the first turn, but curiously the only unit now legally able to field a Weirdboy is Boyz, meaning the funny Breaka Boyz tech is gone. The Painboy is rather similar to his prior incarnation, but does come with a sick new model. On the whole, the idea of a Weirdboy amid a mosh pit of 20 Boyz delights me, and with the changes to HAZARDOUS you're far less likely to die for your efforts.

Final Thoughts

This week saw the debut of some potential problem children for the new Edition, being Grey Knights, Thousand Sons, and the Knights. The reason across the board is the same: Ignoring the rules around cover, in the edition all about Terrain. While the change to PSYCHIC is great to see as a balancing feature for the occasional anti-PSYKER keyword, giving factions what amounts to armywide access to +1 to hit feels dangerous. The fact it can stack with bonuses, and so many armies rely heavily on Stealth and cover to survive, makes the prospect of balancing factions like the Knights & Sons troublesome; for as cool as they are, these feel against the design ethos of 11th from everything else we've seen.

Next week begins with the Faction Focus for Genestealer Cults, said to host more 1DP options for single-character options, but also the debut of the first games in 11th! On May 25th, we'll be watching three games streamed live, headed by six of the community's finest. That'll be Space Marines vs Orks, T'au vs Death Guard, and two factions yet to receive their focus article, Admech vs Aeldari. That's sure to be a treat, and full coverage of the event (and everything else that week) will be arriving from us at Tabletop Battles next Saturday! Everything's falling into place now, and the shape of 11th is looking clearer by the day. We're happy to put it in one place for you.
