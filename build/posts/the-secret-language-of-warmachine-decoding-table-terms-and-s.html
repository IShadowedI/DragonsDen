The Secret Language of Warmachine: Decoding Table Terms and Slang

July 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer

I have been running a journeyman league recently.  As part of that league we have quite a few new or returning players.  During one of those games I referenced camping on my caster, and my opponent was confused what I meant – and they had a right to be.  Nowhere in the rules is the term camp ever used.  Since then I’ve been collecting terms that are common in Warmachine but aren’t ever officially defined.  Mix in a few nicknames that are used, and it is easy for new players to end up lost.  Since this series is focused on new players, I figured this would be a great place to share them all.

Player Actions

Credit: Ryan "Wolfe_the_Cat" Schmidt

Camp – A leader keeping Focus/Fury/Essence on them on the end of their turn with the intention of using it defensively if the need arises.  If a Warlock has 2 Fury on them, they would be described as having 2 Camp.

Cloud Wall – Multiple cloud (such as caustic mist) effects placed by a spell or an ability placed next to each other creating a wall of clouds that blocks line of sight to all models behind it limiting the ability to charger or shoot beyond the clouds.

Cutting – A warlock can take one point of damage to generate a fury in their control phase – this is usually done if some effect causes there to be fewer fury in play than planned.  Officially this is leaching, however, it is colloquially called cutting.  Infernal masters are also able to cut.

Cycling an Upkeep – Some upkeeps allow a player to have an immediate positive effect. A player will recast the upkeep multiple times to leverage its effect on multiple units in a single turn.  The typical sequence is as follows.
  • A player will cast the upkeep on a powerful unit on turn one.
  • Turn 2 upkeep the spell, then activate the unit with the upkeep on it, utilize the power of the upkeep.
  • Active their leader. Cast the upkeep on a second unit.
  • Active the second unit using the power of the upkeep.

Most upkeeps that are cycled cost 2 due to fury/focus management, but not always.  Arcane Propulsion is an upkeep that might be cycled.

Dice off or Dice Plus – Officially a damage roll is 2D6 added to a weapons POW then the ARM of the target is subtracted with the result being the damage taken.  Players frequently shortcut this by subtracting the POW of the Weapon from the ARM of the model and just dealing damage based on the difference.  For example, if a player has hit with a POW 15 attack, against an ARM 18 model, they will subtract 15 from 18, and describe the damage as “Dice minus 3” since the damage result will be the dice result… minus 3.  The opposite is also true in the case where the power of an attack roll is greater than the ARM of the target.

Durdle – Activating a unit and only making minor moves and no attacks.  Functionally the unit is not doing anything and is instead waiting on a change in game state before committing.

Image courtesy Steamforged Games

Hot on Fury – Warlocks can put more fury onto their beasts than they are able to pull in during the control phase.  For example, if a player has 13 fury spread out across on their beasts, with a fury 6 casters, they would be considered hot on fury.  The player will be making threshold check the following turn unless multiple beasts are killed.

Jam – Positioning a unit or models in front on an opponent such that they must neutralize those models to have an effective turn.  Jamming models may have to run to position accordingly and as such will not even be able to attack.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Tilted – Tilted is a poker term and is used to describe a players state of mind after they have been mentally and emotionally shaken.  The cause of the disruption is not the key aspect (it's usually very poor dice rolls), but instead the disruption itself.  Part of the mental and emotional disruption is a loss of focus on aspects of Warmachine a player knows well, resulting in “silly” mistakes.

Trade – Positioning a model far forward and dealing damage with the knowledge that the model will be destroyed on their opponents following turn.

Model Traits

Mini-feat – Many unit attachments provide a once per game effect such as pathfinder or an improved version of tough.  All these abilities, regardless of their effect, are referred to as mini-feats.

Shitty Nuke -  An offensive spell that primarily does damage as an effect, or has a minor low value secondary effect.  These are generally considered a waste of focus unless there is a bonus like an additional damage die, or powerful secondary effect.   Convection on Magnus the Unstoppable is generally considered a Shitty Nuke because its secondary effect is weak.

Stranglehold on Tyrus in House Kallyss is not considered a shitty nuke because the secondary effect is so punishing.

Signs and Portents/Star Crossed – Signs and Portents is a spell that is not in Prime Warmachine.  It read, “While in the spellcaster’s control range, friendly Faction models gain an additional die on attack and damage rolls.  Discard the lowest in each roll.  Signs and Portents lasts for one turn.”  It was the first instance of “add a die, drop the lowest” in the game, and that effect has been referred to as Signs & Portents ever since.   For example, the Chosen in Old Umbrey have Divine Inspiration, an effect that does the same thing.  When a player is describing Divine Inspiration, they may just refer to it as Signs & Portents.

Star Crossed is still a spell in Warmachine and it has the opposite effect.  A dice is added to the pool and the highest dice roll is removed.   Magic resistance such as can be seen on the Khymaera Shadowflame Shard Trikhymaerax could be described as “star crossed for spells”

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Skornergy – Skornergy can be thought of as the opposite of synergy – models rules end up interacting in an un-intuitive way causing a negative effect.  A model simply having a drawback is not Skornergy, it needs to be frustration caused by rules details.  One current example of Skornergy in Warmachine is Kithguard Scrappers.  They have the assault special rule, letting them shoot on a charge, and a ranged weapon that knocks models down.  Unfortunately, assault requires you target a model you are engaged with, knocked down models are never engaged; therefore, if the Scrappers charge a heavy, the first one shoots and knocks the target down, the other two scrappers will be unable to make assault shots.

Stat Check – A model in the game that assess if a player is able to deal with a specific ARM value or number of boxes.  The player will frequently place a model that is a “stat check” in the middle of the board and force an opponent to deal with it.  Currently the most common stat check in Warmachine is Dekathus in Necrofactorium after his feat turn.  Combining all of his abilities he will likely be ARM 25 or more with several focus.  He will be positioned so many models can attack him, but he is likely to survive it.  He is considered a stat check for a list.

Credit: Masterball

Threat/Threat Extender – The total distance a model can move and engage a model.  Ranged units may talked about as having a range threat which will be SPD + Range of the main ranged attack + 2” Place + Base size.  (The last two are usually rounded to 3”)  A models melee threat would be it’s SPD + 3” for a charge + it’s melee weapons RNG + any effects that increase charge range or SPD.

Victim Stats – A model with stats that are low that will typically die when attack.  Typically, this is Def 12 or below and Arm 14 and Arm below 15.  Models with 14 DEF, fairly high, are not typically considered to have Victim Stats.

List Style, Design and Pairing

Arm skew – A style of list that is designed to build the ARM stat of the models in the list as much as possible.  Typically a list is not considered an arm skew until there are 4+ ARM20 models on the board.

Credit: Masterball

Brick – A brick is a combination of overlapping buffs/debuffs that greatly increase a group of models durability.  A classic MKIV brick is Orgoth Sea Raiders, where Horruskh will feat, a Siege Tarask will have Rage Howler/Mortal Fear active, and models like Ulkor Axers will leverage unyielding to effectively be ARM 23.  The biggest challenge with bricks is they quickly fall apart when they are required to spread out for a scenario.

Def skew – A style of list that relies heavily on models DEF stat.  An army is typically considered a Def skew when it fields either multiple DEF 14+ heavies, or a large number of DEF 14+ infantry.

Drop – When a player has a Pair, when they select the leader they will be playing, they may say they are say they are “dropping” that casters.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Dude Spam – A style of list that maximizes the number of infantry on the table.  These models typically are fairly weak, however, many lists will struggle to kill them all, and they can be a challenge to defeat on scenario since they will continually send models off to their deaths to contest.

Jank – A tactic focusing on a niche aspect of an army frequently involving less commonly seen units.  Jank is sometimes powerful… usually it is not.

List chicken – In Warmachine most events players will bring two different lists (see Pair).  When you are preparing for a game a player will review their opponents to lists and secretly select one of their lists to drop.  List chicken is when a player believes one of their lists will have an advantage against one of their opponents list, but a disadvantage against their opponents second list, while that same players second list will have a disadvantage against their opponents first list, and an advantage against their opponents second list.  As a result the list advantage becomes a coin toss.

Pair – In Warmachine most tournaments have players bringing two lists.  A grouping of two lists that a player will bring to an event are know as a pair.

Trash Machine – See Dude Spam.

Wide – A scenario where the elements are on both the far right and the far left side of the board.  This forces a list to spread out to control and fight over those elements.  This term may be used in such a way as “This scenario is wide”.

Win Conditions

While the missions in the Steamroller or Tales from the Frontlines pack differ from one another in exactly how you win, there are three generally accepted ways to come out on top in Warmachine: Assassination, Attrition, or Scenario. We could go pretty in-depth on each of these three strategies, but since this is just a light overview we'll keep it brief.

Image courtesy Steamforged Games

Assassination – Winning a game of Warmachine via killing an opponent’s leader. Generally favors a game plan where extremely dangerous models can find unexpected or hard to defend angles of attack and kill the enemy caster very early in the game.

Attrition – Winning a game of Warmachine by focusing on killing an opponent’s models until either an assassination or a scenario victory becomes trivial. A good attrition list focuses on grinding the opponent down by playing a much wider game, either by being too tough for an opponent to chew up or hitting hard enough that the other player just can't get back into the game.

Scenario – Winning on a game of Warmachine via scoring scenario points. A list that wins via scenario is tricky, focusing on board control strategies that keep your opponent off of the scoring elements entirely so you can win unmolested. A few ways to do this include jamming your opponent in their deployment zone early, or having reliable access to powerful push or placement effects to simply move contesting models off of the scenario elements.

Models/Units

Big Pig – War Boar MMD47, aka the pig that comes with Carver Ultimus.

Credit: Privateer Press/Steamforged Games

Derp Turtle – The Legacy Skorne Siege Animatarax.

Gaspy – Short for any variation of Legacy Cryx Asphyxiolus. His second incarnation, Gaspy2, is infamous for requiring multiple balance passes from the development team to bring in line.

Pigs - Farrow.

Other

Credit: Steamforged Games

CID – Community Integrated Development, is a discontinued open beta that was used to test Warmachine models for a couple of years while Privateer Press ran the game.  It is generally looked back upon negatively due to high volume of changes made in a short period of time and its failure to properly balance models that were tested.

Infernal Ruling – Infernals are the official Warmachine Judges – they had the name for decades before Infernals became a faction.  These judges, or infernals, will answer players rules questions on the official Warmachine Discord.  When there are rules with very unclear results, infernals will make an official rules call.  This rules call is known as an infernal ruling.

Trashcord – A popular unofficial Warmachine Discord.  The logo is a Racoon, aka a trash panda.  The discord is an interesting mix of players, including some of the top ranked players in the world, but also the worst player in my local meta.    Both talk about the same amount.

Final Thoughts

Those are some of the most common terms I’ve seen for Warmachine.  I’m sure I missed quite a few, and the community is always coming up with new and creative nicknames for things.  If there are any key ones you can think of, feel free to throw them into the comments and I’ll try and add them to the list.
