Mayday, Miss Marcy! Pumpkin Spice and Everything Nice for Fall

September 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back everyone to Mayday, Miss Marcy, our (usually) weekly advice column in which I take your reader submitted questions and provide them with advice in the vein of classic newspaper advice bits you'd find next to the funny pages. You may have noticed an absence last week, as I instead took a moment to talk about a game I very much like, as well as helping to kick off our expanding video game coverage which we've started rolling out and you should hope to see more of in the coming weeks! I'm pretty excited, and I hope perhaps you all are as well, since we do it all for you!

Speaking of doing things for you, if you'd like to get your question answered in this very column, please follow the instructions below:

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

A far off two weeks ago, we took some time to talk about pushy players and when is customizing too far? This week, I've selected a few (what felt like) positive focused questions to help shift from the heat of Summer to the cooling Fall!

Pumped Up Pumpkin Carving

Dear Miss Marcy,

Since you noted looking for questions for fall, I actually had a question. My kids want to do Pumpkin carving this year, and while we usually buy pretty standard stencil kits, I was thinking of trying to do something a little more for myself. My oldest has started getting curious about Warhammer, so I was thinking of making some custom stencils for us to use, but I'm afraid that my younger kids might feel left out, or that the stencils will look kind of lame and not be recognizable. I suppose I'm a little afraid of this being seen as "Dad does whatever he wants", but maybe I'm also just overthinking it? I'm trying to get things sorted early so I can start looking for the right sizes and things I can afford.

Puzzled by Pumpkins

Dear Puzzled,

I think you are overthinking it. First of all, there is a very high chance that no one else in the entire world will care about what you or your children carve into your pumpkins, because that relies on you displaying them somewhere and then having people comment. In my own experiences back when more elaborate stencils became popular, this usually just elicited some comments by parents of Trick or Treaters going 'wow, cool pumpkin' because it was anything that wasn't just a jack o' lantern face. I wouldn't worry so much about them looking lame, because you're focusing on the wrong part; the experience of carving them with your kids is what matters, and unless you are an absolute stickler for perfection, the end result won't really matter that much as long as you know what it is supposed to look like.

As to the other part, you didn't specify ages, and I will admit I am not a parent, but I would have to guess that your other kids would probably just... not care? If you and your oldest are doing something different from them, I think you could probably solve that by helping them pick out or making their choice in stencil feel also special; I doubt very much that the average child would get upset that they aren't carving a Space Marine chapter sign or something similar over whatever stencil you gave them. As long as they all feel included, I think that should probably solve the issue. All in all, you sound like a great dad who cares about his kids having fun, as well as the expanding interest of your eldest in the hobby you obviously care a lot about; nurture all of those things, and if you do carve something, let us see it!

Going Outside (To Play Warhammer)

Hi Marcy,

I was recently invited to a sort of Fall getaway towards the end of October. I'm writing mostly because the group has floated the idea of playing Warhammer outdoors while we're there, and I'm having a little anxiety about it. My first concern is that I have to bring my army with me, and the second is the lingering fear that something will happen to them outdoors. The area we're going to is not exactly prone to heavy bouts of rain or inclement weather, and I would be pretty concerned if it snowed in October, but I am kind of concerned all the same. Do you have any advice, or tips, for outdoors playing?

Traveling Trouble

Dear Traveling,

I think playing games outdoors is pretty common, and although I personally haven't (because I live in a place you would not want to be outdoors playing Warhammer for extended periods of time through like, 3/4ths of the year), I think you're exacerbating the outdoor part. You really should check to confirm with your friends, though, that this is something that will be a major part of the trip. If anything, you've oversized "outdoors" with "I have to bring my models", which depending on how far you are traveling, can be a pretty extensive problem to solve (one we've even written about!). I would first want to be sure that the hassle of bringing your army will be worth it, and then figuring out the best possible way to do it.

As for your fears of playing outdoors, I am assuming (and we know what that means) that you are unlikely to be playing in a literal copse of forest and are more likely to be playing on some sort of patio or even grassy area that can support tables, so realistically, you are not playing somewhere that different from a LGS or your friend's garage or game room, except that there are no walls (And, frankly, there might even be walls if this is on an enclosed patio). Your real issue is the travel, not the unlikely destruction of your army by the forces of nature. Go have fun and don't burden yourself with fears of what COULD happen, instead focus on how best you can get your stuff there!

Brunch and Crunch

Hi Miss Marcy!

I'm running an event that will provide food, primarily breakfast foods, to coincide with a painting workshop and crunch session to help my local gaming group finally start putting some paint on models. I came up with the idea after being tired of seeing so many models covered in gray plastic, but I am curious if you have any suggestions for foods that would be easy, as well as accessible? I will say that I am a pretty okay cook, and I'll have a gas burner that I used for camping and Korean BBQ days. I have some ideas but "eggs" isn't always as easy as it sounds for a group of 10~15 people.

Cookin' Up a Camp

Dear Cookin',

I love this idea? This is almost such a good idea I wish I'd thought of it and promoted it myself. Alas, I did not, so you will have to get all the credit. I think you've got a good sense of things: eggs are great but can be kind of difficult unless you're just making a big batch of scrambled eggs and then portioning it out, and I do think there's nothing wrong with that sort of bulk cooking; it certainly pairs easily with things like bacon or sausages and simple things, but I will give you a secondary option: pancakes!

Now, you can go the easy way, which is to use some various boxed mixes; purists will get on you for using a box, but you're cooking for a huge group, and that really can mitigate the idea of cooking from scratch. If you want to cook from scratch, there's a pretty easy recipe I'll toss down below this reply, but I wanted to also offer you some advice: you can easily substitute a lot of things for pancake / waffle mixes if you need to concern yourself with allergies and dietary needs. Although gluten might still obviously be an issue (you can also find boxed mixes for that!), a really common one people overlook is that you can use apple sauce in place of eggs, and water or a non-dairy milk for the liquid component (mileage may vary). You can even get creative with the oil/fat content of most recipes. So, aside from eggs/toast/protein as a simple one, try something like waffles or pancakes; of the two, pancakes are always easiest because you just need a pan to cook them in.

As for the recipe:

Marcy's Pancake Recipe

300g all-purpose flour

66g granulated sugar

2 eggs

1/2 cup buttermilk

1 cup milk

1 tbsp baking powder

1/4 tsp baking soda

1 tsp salt

2 tbsp neutral oil

1 tsp vanilla

That's it! However, if you need to make some substitutions:

Instead of eggs, use 1 cup of unsweetened applesauce. If you do, reduce the overall sugar from 66g to 44g (the average 'general' sweetness of applesauce is around 22~g).

1/2 cup whole fat greek yogurt instead of buttermilk. If you really can't use or store buttermilk because you have no other reason to have it, you can use greek yogurt! Works basically the same (you can add a bit more milk if you find the batter is thicker than you'd like, though, as the yogurt is missing some liquid).

2 tbsp SOFT butter, not melted, instead of oil.

Realistically you can do a lot more than this, but these are some I quickly use! Good luck with your event!
