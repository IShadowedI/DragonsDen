Trench Crusade Warband Focus: High Cathedra of Pride

November 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to another Trench Crusade Warband Focus. This week we're focusing on the self-styled first among all the Sins, Pride.

The Lore

Pride is the first of all Sins, and as such it's seat, the High Cathedra of Pride, is given privilege over the other six Serpent Heads in the incomprehensibly intricate machinations of the Court. The most recent holder of the seat is Eligos, the Grand Duke of Petals. They have not made any overt moves in the war against Creation recently, but they took the largest loan from Mammon in the history of the war in order to finance the construction of the automaton legions that took Rejika in 1872. From this beachhead into European mainland no doubt they will continue to press forward when the time is right.

Unwilling to let any who they march against be unaware of the glory of whom they face, all of the legions of Pride march under resplendent banners that glow with an eerie and unbearable light as a blinding perversion of the Heavenly Hosts they mock.

Defining Traits of the High Cathedra of Pride

Pride does not have particularly harsh restrictions, especially compared to some of the other Sins like Envy. As per usual, you must take either a Praetor or a Sorcerer to lead the warband. In addition you MUST purchase a troop flag, which either an ELITE or Desecrated Saint must carry.

Court Praetor Credit: Darius Minis

While your flag-carrying model is not Down or Out of Action, your entire warband scores critical hits on an 11+ instead of on a 12. This may not sound huge at first, but this will drastically increase the number of critical hits you can expect to land throughout a game. You'll see this the most with high attack bonus models like the Praetor.

It is against the nature of Pride to retreat from any confrontation. As such, if any of your models take a retreat move your opponent may place one Blessing Marker next to any model in their warband. Yoke Fiends within the Court of Pride are simply not allowed to voluntarily retreat without wearing a restraining muzzle.

Sorcerer
Credit: Darius Minis

Pride has very fitting SIN generation. If any of your ELITE models activate in Open terrain without touching any terrain that could give them the benefit of Cover they gain 2 SIN markers. Your Praetor and Sorcerer also have an additional method, if they activate within 18 inches and line of sight of a Wretched (friend or foe) they gain 1 SIN from their inherent sense of superiority. This generally means you will keep an entourage of a few Wretched near your leader, especially with the unique wretched equipment available to Pride. Pride lacks any unique method of SIN generation for its basic troops, but the peons can still gain SIN from the Desecrated Saint which gives one SIN to any model that activates within one inch of them (including themselves) and the classic Torture Instrument. But really, if you are playing Pride then your game plan is most likely all about the Elites. You'll usually start the game with a glut of SIN from safe out of sight activations when Cover isn't needed, but leaving your Elites in the open for SIN generation in later turns is more of a risk that you need to consider as the game goes on.

Battlekit Highlights

All of the Seats get their own special little toys to play with, and Pride has an assortment truly befitting the First Sin.

The Cloak of the Morningstar is a fantastic suit of armor. It is INNATE, so there is no risk of losing it in a campaign to a particularly unlucky Trauma Roll. Aside from being a way of giving one of your ELITE models a -3 INJURY MODIFIER without taking up one of their valuable hands, and as such I like putting it on a Praetor with the Serpent Assault Gun and the Malebranche Sword. It also makes the model completely immune to being made to take a forced move. For three Glory this item is an absolute steal. It can also be given to a Desecrated Saint, if you really want to prevent them from being moved around by tricky opponents.

Glorious Light is a defensive item available to your Hellknights and Desecrated Saint. For a mere Twenty Ducats you inflict -1 DICE to the Success Roll for all attacks that target the model with the Light. Added to a Desecrated Saint with a Polearm this can make them surprisingly difficult to shift.

Desecrated Saint
Credit: Darius Minis

Laurels of the Supreme is extremely dependent on your terrain set up. If you have a board with a lot of vertical play space it can be a fantastic boon. If you play on a more flat set up, it is a Combat Helmet that costs Three Glory. If at any point in the activation of a model with the Laurels it can draw line of sight to an enemy model that is at least 2 inches below it, you can give yourself one Blessing Marker. Up to 6 Blessing Markers a game can be a massive swing in your favor, and you don't need to begin or end your activation in the open to gain it, so a DASH action combined with a Praetors 8 inch FLIGHT movement means it is easier than it might first seem to get multiple uses out of it. It is less of an immediate Glory purchase than the Cloak of the Morningstar, but still a strong item.

The Lightbringer Rifle is an expensive weapon at 40 Ducats, and aside from CRITICAL it lacks any obvious enhanced killing power for its high cost. But it's special abilities are where it shines. Think of this as a reverse Laurels of the Supreme. Where that item revels in being above others, the Lightbringer is the raw venomous spite at any who dare to raise themselves above you given lethal form. You add +3 DICE to any Ranged Attack targeting a model that it as least 2 inches above the wielder. In addition to that, if the attack is a success the target will automatically Fall if it is within 2 inches of any open window, unattended door or ominous ledge. While not always lethal, this is always hilarious.

Eligos's namesake, the Petals of the Blood Roses are given to the most deserving of Wretched to scatter at the feet of their masters. A strange bit of defensive utility against BLAST weapons, the Petals of the Blood Roses let you scatter incoming attacks by 1d6 inches if they have the BLAST Keyword and would hit a friendly DEMONIC model within 3 inches of the petal-bearing Wretched. You must have line of sight from the initial target point to wherever it ends up. For just 5 Ducats this is a cheap utility item for the Wretched that you likely will have following your Praetor and Sorcerer around anyway as you will want to get the benefit of Superiority.

The Power of Pride

Light of Samael

Everyone's favorite SIN powered unholy laser remains our number one spell. You pick an enemy in line of sight and within 24 inches, and you roll Injury for them. If they survive, and are on 40mm or smaller base, you knock them back 1d6 inches. The utility of an attack that automatically hits at 24 inches is not to be underestimated, and now this Goetic Power specifically states that they move even if they are Down. As if we needed more ways to knock people out of windows.

Proud Defiance

Have you ever forgotten that Trench Crusade has a Morale system? Well keep on forgetting it with this power that makes you immune to the entire thing! Honestly not a huge fan of this power, but it can be of some use as Pride tends to be a fairly tall building Sin focused on a smaller number of more powerful models and only a few losses could potentially trigger a Morale check.

Too Proud to Fall

The other Pride Classic, I am less sold on this one however. While not going Down for 2 SIN per instance of it can be useful for your long term survival, especially if your model is in melee, the BLOOD MARKERS from any attacks that would cause the Down result still stack up. It can be a life saver for an attack or two, but don't expect it to keep you alive if you leave a model completely exposed.

Wounded Pride

Our newest ability, if a model without ELITE would place BLOOD MARKERS on the model with this spell, you place the same number of BLOOD MARKERS on the attacking model. Not exactly a rubber and glue scenario, but very it is deliciously spiteful and very tasty on a Hellknight.

Playing the Warband

Pride lists lean tall. Get your ELITE core going strong, with at least two of the Big Three included in every list. Those being Praetor, Sorcerer and Hunter of the Left Hand Path. I then recommend getting a Saint and a few Yokes and Wretched to fill in as needed. But taking a Hellknight for a banner bearer is also an option if you can't afford a Saint and don't want to give it to the Hunter. Remember, you are required to take a Troop Banner.

Hunter of the Left Hand Path Credit: Darius Minis

If you can do so safely, you want to deploy in a group around your Desecrated Saint to maximize SIN generation for your first turn, as you will want a lot of it and likely won't be able to freely get as much in future rounds. Your primary SIN generation relies on your ELITE models starting their activation's not in COVER. This is less safe as the game goes on, but is possible if there is large line of sight blocking terrain to take advantage of. Otherwise you may need to nurse your initial harvest of SIN for several turns.

The Big Three will live up to their name in a Pride list. You will likely not have any significant killing power other than them for a majority of lists so you really need to make each of their activations count. I enjoy really using the multi-kill potential of the Praetor when you can position them well. Taking one or two softer targets out with the Serpent Assault Gun and then charging into a different threat with a Malebranche backed up by the Light of Samael to add an extra hit that can push the target out of melee and generate another swing with that deliciously deadly Malebranche means you can significantly thin out the opposing force if you position them well.

Final Thoughts

Pride does not exactly play differently than most other Court lists do. Your lists will usually act like more of a refinement of the classic Big Three lists, making each of them into the most powerful and dangerous versions of themselves. Your elites can be fantastically lethal and extremely difficult to kill if played correctly and that only gets more true in campaign games where you have access to the best Glory Items of the faction. Your heavy lifting will be done by your Elites, everyone else is there to sit bodies on objectives and support your star players by any means.

Embrace your clear and objective greatness. Let nothing stand before you. Aura Farm. And rest assured that if they impossible happens and you do lose a game, it's all a part of your master plan and you clearly meant for it to occur so you can set up your future victories. For the Duke of Petals.
