[Conquest Last Argument of Kings] Tabletop Battles at the London Grand Tournament 2026!

October 6th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello again! We’re back from the London Grand Tournament (LGT) 2026, and its been an absolute blast as always. Five games over two days has a way of both exhausting you and getting you really hyped for Conquest. Following on from our previous article, we’d each like to talk you about how we found the event and our games.

Before then though, it’s worth noting we had some stunning armies at the event, a few of which have been kind enough to allow us to share below. Please check them out!

Armies on the Table

100 Kingdoms - Credit: WhyAreCarrots

Spires - Credit: Dave Richardson

Dweghom - Credit: CornishDragon

Yoroni - Credit: Vaelant

Sorcerer Kings - Credit: Jay Gallagher

City States - Credit: FirestormFalcon

General Cross

Another excellent event seeing many friends from across the country, and I had five fantastic games with excellent opponents. I have to caveat though – I did make a tiny alteration to my list after the original post, and swapped my third Quatl for another unit of Warbred. I think this was the right choice, increasing my damage output at the cost of another fast but somewhat pillowfisted piece.

W'adrhun - GeneralCross (yes I know I didn't finish painting)

My first game was against Chris, also known as “The People’s Champion” amongst the UK community. Chris was running an Imperial/Feudal 100 Kingdoms army – albeit half the army was taken up with 16 stands of Guilded Legion, backed up by two Water Mages and a smattering of other troops. We played on Maelstrom, with the game dominated by his Unyielding, Dread, Armour of Dominion monstrous unit going 10 stands wide to contest the central and left zone eternally. With absolutely no way to beat this deathstar, I had to stall him by placing multiple units at awkward angles so he couldn’t legally charge my units and seizing the right objective, which worked out in my favour in the end. We discussed whether he would have been better not trying to play for both objectives and just driving the unkillable blob around to wreck havoc instead. Still, a real puzzle for game 1!

My second game was into Josh, one of the two actual Joshs in the podcast “Joshing around in Ea”. He was also playing 100 Kingdoms in an Orders/Feudal cavalry list. We played Seek and Destroy and played super cagey until turn 5, with our ranged units slowly chipping away at eachother. Unfortunately, I would follow up with the most hideously oppressive dice I’ve ever had in Conquest, my units casually shrugging off his attacks whilst melting his heavy Cavalry with my Warbred and Winglord. With such a crushing turn, we called the game in my favour with very little he could do in response. This game could have gone either way, but the combination of me winning more meeting engagements alongside my units completely no-selling his riposte left the outcome clear.

My third game was into Ben playing City Sates with four Chariots, a Talos and Hephaestion and some infantry blocks. I’ve always struggled into Chariots – they get a lot of work done and are surprising difficult to catch and destroy without overextending. Melee also wasn’t ideal, but at least I can get some aggressive scoring on. I took a strong early position, scoring fast on the zones and hoping to trade into his chariots with my Quatls. His counterattack was strong however, and I ended up losing both a Winglord and Quatl without taking out any of his chariots on the left flank. This left him free to punish the rest of my slower melee force on the way in, and though I did get some impressive damage in (a heroic long bomb charge from a Quatl leading into a long fight with Haspists over his 6” zone), I couldn’t get after his mobile firebases and was whittled down to a loss. Ben played his army really well and put me under a lot of pressure. In retrospect I should have been more conservative and build up my army to charge in together a turn later, but I got greedy and thought I could get the work done I needed. Ben would go on to win the event outright, so I have absolutely no disappointment losing to him!

My fourth game was 100 Kingdoms again playing Pete, with a really interesting Orders/Theist list. We played on Flames of Conquest, trading big blows in the centre whilst facing a Mexican standoff on the left side. I got a strong early lead with my fast Mediums, and used Hunting Pack to contest his zone well. But after my Raptors bounced off a big Crusader squad and my right flank was overrun by Household Knights and Ashen Dawn I was on the back foot in a big way. A bold burning of the central objective slowed the 100 Kingdoms catch up, but a lucky break on my left flank let me tear apart the Crimson Tower and Household Guard with my Warbred, Winglord and Hunting Pack. With my Chosen of War holding the centre, I held my position long enough to maintain a close victory. This was a really interesting matchup – and despite a lot of high Cleave access in my list I still struggled to get the damage in where I needed it. Once again, the depth of 100 Kingdoms lists is incredible!

My last game was into our own Deadly Trout, running his monstrous Weaver Courts list (you can read his opinion of our game below!). Worst, this was on Head to Head letting him be even more defensive on his gunline. I feel I had learnt my lesson about building mass then charging together rather than feed him my army piecemeal and despite poor reinforcement rolls (1 out of 5 mediums arrived T2, none arrived T3!). I managed to get a very aggressive turn in. High rolling let my Winglord kill his Draic and I picked up his Skirmishers with Hunting Pack well enough. Despite this I couldn’t get the damage into his Bombing Moths, who with the healing available managed to sweep up my heavily damaged units over the following turns. I tried to crack the centre and take the zone, but I was millimetres out on a flank charge with Warbred into his Treemen, and then a slightly lucky Wyrd Knight charge into my Chosen created landing space for his Gallowglass Wardens to fly over into the flank and finish them off. With that, I had no fight back into the centre and despite getting a Quatl onto his back zone, the outcome was clear. I think I played reasonably, holding my nerve until the right turn to charge in despite the early losses. I think Weavers still held the advantage thanks to our Trout playing excellent, but I certainly gave them a run for their money!

Overall I came 3-2 and placed 4th place, the same as last year! Whilst I was hoping for a 4-1, I don’t think I made any major mistakes in my games and gave it my best shot. I really like the ‘Breadbasket’ list I’ve been playing for a while, its oddly durable and hard hitting which catches people off guard. I’m interested in some of the infantry focused W’adrhun lists at the moment too; time to get experimenting…

Deadly Trout

Tis I, your friendly neighbour-pond trout man, back with another tale of confusion! The theme of this year was moths, and no, I won't be answering any more questions. Despite a slightly smaller scale than previous years the weekend was still an absolute blast featuring some immaculate painting (all bar two armies being fully painted) and some very happy faces. 

Weaver Courts - Credit: DeadlyTrout

The first success I had was seeing I was the only Weaver player (best in faction guaranteed!) but this was quickly hampered when I saw five 100 Kingdoms and three City States armies; the fear of playing only one faction the whole weekend was real. Luckily I avoided every single 100K, but did hit City States twice.

My first game was one of such players. I played into John and his lovely army. Now, I’ve played a lot into CS but was pleasantly surprised to see no chariots in his list, and a rather interesting composition. His double goats with bows trade very well into my, normally, aggressive Kerns so I had to hold back to avoid losing the shooting war which had me feeling a little nervous as I tend to rely on early damage to have the upper hand when the lines meet and my squishy bugs tend to start dropping. Luckily my speed allowed me to hold the objectives early on so I could hold back a little more later and hit with some counter punches. After a scary brawl in the midboard I came out on top holding just enough objectives to see me to a very close 13-11 win. A great game against a lovely opponent!

Into round 2 I faced Paul and his bird heavy Yoroni list. In one of the newer missions that involved us picking “chosen” enemy units, that could score more for them, but also gave up extra kill points. I chose each of his birds to encourage them to be more aggressive, and thus easier to kill. The theory was fine… ish. I did manage to kill 2/3 of them, but they did gain a couple extra points on the scoring too. A very back and forth game with each of us claiming on flank each. An aggressive push saw his warlord get chomped by a dragon, but my trees failed to land a single hit on 18 dice kept the game in the balance. The end game saw 2 of my units face one pair of birds but luckily for me the points were close enough to eek a 2 point win. Another great game that could have gone either way.

My third game was into an abomination heavy spires list run by Dave. Sadly for him the mission favoured my speedy moth force and his reinforcements let him down something hard giving me a healthy lead with no losses by turn 4. A cheeky Wyrd Knight charge through the middle saw them get into the backline and take out the desolation drones giving me the much-needed ranged advantage. Between that and two of our units failing to roll above a 3 on a rerollable charge meant the momentum was just too in my favour. Even some mighty 6” charges from the A-bombs couldn’t bring the score back. A healthy 112-20 point win to the courts. 

Game 4 was against Ben and his quad chariot City States. Another new mission that I hadn’t played with 6 objective zones had me worried from the get go, and I was right to be worried. It started reasonably, some good reinforcement rolls gave me all 3 of my home objectives turn 2 giving an early lead. SOme minor posturing for a couple of turns saw the zones even out. It was looking set to be a great game. Until everything went wrong. A slightly optimistic charge from some kerns failed to do the damage I was hoping for, and the Gallowglass wardens (my MVPs in the tournament so far) failed to do more than 4 wounds over 2 turns of aimed shooting into some Thorakites with over 20 hits. Some great chariot positioning and a great use of the “I’m a polemarch and I go first” trait saw my big moth block disintegrate. The trees, yet again, failed to hit more than twice on 18 dice, and from there I was pinned in and tactically picked off by a skilled player. Not my finest game, many little mistakes that Ben skillfully pounced on, alongside some dice that have since been retired led to my first loss of the event. A well deserved win to the tourney champion.

My last game was an interesting one. I was playing into General Cross (see above for his run down) and, due to the interesting way the event had shaped out, we were looking at a possible 4 way tie for top place, so all to play for! Head to head is a mission I both love and hate. I like scoring the midboard early, but I dislike how it tends to be a big brawl by turn 5. Weavers don’t like big melee fight :( The standard posturing took place with my rushing archers to cover the objective, countered by some tiny angry dinosaurs eating one of them. They were swiftly burned by dragon fire for their hubris. The Gallowglass returned to form and 2 tapped one of the terror geese that I feared. I was feeling very confident at this point, but my dragon going from hero to zero in one goose’s turn had me remembering why I was scared of them. This was amplified when, much like last year when the General goosed me, I managed to have the big bird alive on 1 wound. I am cursed by these pterosaurs. Luckily, a mighty charge from the wyrd knights, popping deadly blades got lucky and managed to kill two stands of his chosen of war, allowing my Gallowglass Warriors to swiftly change flank, really turned the tide of battle. A really well fought game by the General, but alas, nothing can stop the moths. 42-30 to the Fish. 

Getting a 4-1 with my Moths was a really incredible feeling. I’ve loved weavers since they were just a trial model you could get, so to be having some success in bigger tournaments is really amazing. I came in a happy 2nd place, having learned a lot more about my list, and having an amazing time with so many great people (even if I couldn’t get many to join in the pub session). Highly recommended for any budding player from novice to master. The variation of lists from 14 activations, down to 4 (yes really, read Sam’s review) show that this game has so much flex to offer, however you want to play!

DanDaBossman

I’ve heard the Greeks like it long and hard and oh boy was that long and hard. Five games back to back, wired and tired, and being edged like a madman, with every game being incredibly close. I’m nothing if not a masochist when it comes to playing on hard mode! The City States return heroically with 1st place (not me, don’t be silly!) and an even more heroic 3rd place (that is me, yay!). I went in with the mythical goal of 4-1 and somehow pulled it off.

City States - Credit: DandaBossman

Game One was against the tallest man in all the land, and his merry men, Hywel. After all the tournaments I’ve been to this was actually our first game together. I started strong by forgetting all of my rules, having not played in weeks, and gradually built myself up to narrowly taking the win 15-14. Hywel’s list featured blocks of men with big pokey sticks, and a few men in tincans on ponies. They were no match for my big pokey sticks and ponies! I decided to start by having my Thorakites reinforcing each other, which has worked well before, but a unit of Household Guard and Knights put an end to one of them before being reinforced by a Hoplite block with a steel chair. Hephy and Talos wrestled with an Archangel for a bit and Hywel got some sickening long bomb charges into my backfield units. With the right flank secured (eventually), the remaining Thorakites made the long trip to the middle to beat back what was left. With Hywel left with one unit, and a good half of mine still on the board, I just managed to eke out the win there. With Hywel being our, as of game, resident #1 champ I’ll certainly take that!

Game Two was against the man, the myth, the legend and his dynamite stick (don’t ask), Dave and his Spires. Dave brought along three Abominations, Mildred, Jackie and Daryl, my targets for Search and Destroy. I see big thing, I kill big thing. I started the festivities by yeeting my Thorakites into his Lights, which sounded like a great plan, and not needing the Polemarch’s supremacy I thought I’d be fine. Then it happened… “Oh hello, Mr. Polemarch, won’t you join me for a spot of dueling” said the Assassin. I spent a good minute umming and ahhing as Dave dangled his “COWARD!” token in front of me, so naturally I had to accept, I mean how bad could it-oh god he’s got Cleave 2, attacks lots and goes first! You can imagine how that ended but on the plus side I was the only one to challenge Dave in all five games, so I hold the trophy for “Not a COWARD!” and wear it proudly. My Thorakites were toast after that. What can men do against such reckless trolling. As for the rest of the game, I managed to kill a couple of Abominations, have some Thorakites doing the Cha Cha Slide deciding where they want to go, and a massive grind out in the middle with the Talos and some Hoplites. Having not played much Spires before, having HQs that can throw out d6 hits every turn really hurts. Sadly, all of this led to a loss of 42-24 to Dave. Long on points, short on game, we only had one or two units each by the end. Brutal!

Game Three pitted me against Fillipo, my second Hundred Kingdoms player. Another nail biter at 64-59 to me. The mental fatigue was really kicking in at this point as I’d decided to throw all of my intense training sessions with the Deadlytrout into the wind and yolo’d my Thorakites up the board to be annihilated by Fillipo’s Sicarii and Household Knights. There was lots of grinding on the left flank with a Hoplite block taking on a good chunk of Knights, Militia and Crusaders, and Hephy and some Companion Cav posturing on the right, which was a foregone conclusion with the loss of the Thorakites. Twelve wounds from Mounted Squires through water really put a dent in Hephy’s codling pouch! As for the centre, a ten block of Crusaders, kitted to the teeth, faced off against my Aristarch block and a Talos. The insane Resolve and resistance of the Crusaders would have meant a loss if Fillipo hadn’t bravely accepted my duel into the Aristarch with the lightsaber! As always, if I’m going down I’m doing it in style and somehow against the odds the Aristarch pulled through, putting down the Crusader Lord, and removing all the buffs to his unit. At this point a flank of Hoplites and front Talos made quick work of the rest of the unit. This left the Talos to happily sit on the middle objective, and have the Aristarch back up onto the broken left flank and score the left objective just enough to get the win. Remember boys and girls, dueling may seem fun, and it is, do it!

Game Four was against Jay and his wonderfully painted Sorcerer Kings (one day I’ll win that painting competition). Now if you’ve been following the Conquistadors, which I know you haven’t, you’ll know our current group profile is us kicking the “helpless” wizard. It was my duty this tournament to beat down the only Sorcerer Kings player in a new mission, Flames of Conquest. Having learnt my lessons the previous day of not yoloing into things, I decided to take my usual cautious approach and hold the Thorakites back to get a good reinforcement line and posture up the board as much as possible. With the Thorakites on the flanks, supported by a regiment of Hoplites each, and the Talos in the middle, it seemed like a strong defensive position. It started off well with me holding my objectives and racking up points while Jay struggled to get his units up the board. I pushed into his territory with the Thorakites, though one ate dirt from Maharabi Initiatives (shocking). This mission really pushes you to think strategically, so with the right flank falling apart it was a burn and abandon affair. The Talos got wrecked by Ghols, taking fourteen wounds in one clash and Hephy found a fire hotter than himself in the big block of fire genies. My Aristarch challenged Jay’s Maharaja to a duel, a lesson Jay won’t soon forget as after the first duel he declined a second, leaving the Hoplites open to some serious Resolve damage on his Mahut. I’m really getting my duels in this tournament! Overall, due to early scoring and burning objectives strategically, it was 37-12 to me, once again long on points, short on game as my forces were left devastated.

Game Five, the final game, the game that would determine if I achieved my goal of 4-1 this tournament, against Pete and his Hundred Kingdoms. I’m learning a lot about Hundred Kingdom’s this tournament. This game will go down in history as a game that happened. Flash forward to turn six, no stands had been lost by either of us and only two wounds dealt by my chariot. My lesson learnt, I postured until I was blue in the face! My Talos being the legend that he is, managed to get on an objective early, and being worth six stands was easily able to hold it with my Polemarch being able to deny seizing with Unyielding. I just had to stand there and let Pete make the first move. Admittedly he did have pretty rubbish reinforcement rolls; when the dice say no, what can you do? Once battlelines met though it was a bloody affair. In only two turns most of the board had been left devastated. The early points lead meant I held the upper hand and unfortunately unlike all my other games we had to call on turn eight at 11-2 to me. A lesson to you dear readers, is posture, posture, posture! Speaking of which I think my back and neck had given out by this point.

Looking back I don’t know how I managed to leave with 4-1 and come 3rd place. My opponents have all been smashing chaps and having great people and community to play with makes the long trips around the country worthwhile. Special obligatory mention to Elliot the TO for organising the event. I also somehow managed to leave with more models than I came with, technically winning 4/5 raffles I entered (limit of three sadly) and came away with a sizable starting Dweghom force. Guess I’m a dwarf now, so watch yourself or it’s going in the book!

Sam Isaacson (not Josh)

It's always fun playing Conquest at the LGT. Brilliantly painted armies, excellent sportsmanship, people flying around reminding everyone how much they like moths…

This did turn out to be a special year for me. Not because of my results - 2-3 put me firmly in the lower-middle, as is my wont - but because of the amount of attention my list got. Apparently running only four regiments when the trend is to get into double figures is surprising. 

Spires - Credit: Sam Issacson (Not Josh)

My opening game ended up being against Josh's Hundred Kingdoms, which is a bit too quick for me to deal with and he knows my playstyle a bit too well. I didn't expect to win that matchup, and my prediction turned out to be accurate. 

Game two was against Chris's Hundred Kingdoms. He was running a list that would have gotten attention for its massive block of 16 stands of Gilded Legion, if only I hadn't been throwing 25 stands of Force-Grown Drones into it. His unit was always going to win that battle, the question was how long it would take him. And the answer was: Just a bit too long. This scenario favoured me as I was scoring bonus points for my chosen units scoring objective zones, but with my small number of regiments that was always going to happen! 

Game three pitched me against Jay's Sorcerer Kings, with a multi-Court mashup that I'd not seen before. He smashed into my Siegebreaker Behemoth with his Sword Dancers, somehow managing to give them Relentless Blows on 1s and 2s as well as Flawless Strikes, alongside Deadly Blades. I chose that moment to roll six 6s, and away went my beautiful Monster. Unfortunately, due to time we quickly tried to simulate the last few turns and made an assumption that his unit would kill my Incarnate Sentinels, which handed him the win. In hindsight, maybe we should have taken more time and I should have been more stubborn about it, but my massive block survived so I didn't mind too much. 

Game four was always going to be a challenge for me. Not from a pleasure perspective - Hywel’s delightfully painted army and enjoyable character made my demise a wonderful treat. But dealing with six objective zones when I was only running three units that could score was stacking the deck against me. But my Drones did put in a shift, travelling greater distance than most marathon runners would in the same period, thanks to the rules around wheels in a Charge that don't seem to have imagined that someone would create a unit nine stands wide. A (successful!) roll that needed a 5 on a turn 9 Charge made for a dramatic finish, but it was too little too late. Their journey across almost the full 6’ table was all for nought, although it got the attention of the TO…

My final game was much better suited to my list. John's slow-moving City States in a battle that's all really about a single objective zone is the sort of thing I ought to expect to win. The final score of 59-13 felt like a decent reflection of a game in the end, although there was a moment around turn 7 where a couple of different rolls might have ended up telling quite a different story! 

All in all, loads of fun, punctuated by brilliant TO presence and delicious food, even if my bank balance won't thank me for it. Now to start preparing for next year…dare I come back with only 3?

Final Thoughts

We appreciate this is a lot of words, but we hope this helps convey how much we all enjoyed the LGT! Conquest was the only non-Games Workshop system in attendance this year, which is a small point of pride for us underdogs. If any of this interests you, we hope you'll join us next year. You can spot us easily by the outrageous costumes...

DeadlyTrout, GeneralCross and DandaBossman - Some of us are faction loyalists!
