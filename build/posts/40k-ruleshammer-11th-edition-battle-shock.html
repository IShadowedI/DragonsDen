[40k] Ruleshammer 11th Edition: Battle-shock

August 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back to Ruleshammer! In this series we take a detailed look at the rules of key games, but Warhammer 40k in particular. This week I’m covering battle-shock, its causes, and consequences!

The battle-shock rules are spread out throughout the rules for 11th edition, impacting things such as objective control, actions, and even what move types you can do. This guide aims to bring all of those causes and consequences into one handy reference. At the end of this article there’s a helpful info-graphic summary!

Battle-shock Rolls 01.07

The battle-shock roll itself is simple enough: It’s a Leadership Roll. You roll 2d6 and compare it to the LD characteristics of the unit you’re making the roll for. If it’s greater than or equal to one of the LD characteristics of the unit (which may vary for units with attached characters) then the roll "succeeds." In the case of a battle-shock roll, this means the unit isn’t battle-shocked.

Note that passing this roll during the battle-shock step of the command phase is the only way for a unit to naturally end being battle-shocked unless a unit has a specific ability that allows that unit to stop being battle-shocked. Passing a battle-shock roll at any other time does not cause a unit to stop being battle-shocked.

Causes of Battle-shock Tests

There are several triggers for needing to take a battle-shock test.

Being Battle-shocked Already

This is actually a small but meaningful change in 11th edition that could be wrongly dismissed as not doing anything, but remember that there are ways to be battle-shocked that aren’t being at or below half strength. If an enemy unit has used an ability to make a unit battle-shocked, it could still have its full starting strength on the battlefield but have to take a test at this point regardless.

Also note that removing a unit from the battlefield into reserves or embarking a unit does not remove battle-shock:

Reposition Units 20.02

When they are removed from the battlefield, any rules that are affecting such units for a specified duration or under specific circumstances continue to affect them while that duration and/or those circumstances apply.

Example: … but a unit that was battle-shocked when removed from the battlefield would still be battle-shocked if it makes an ingress move in the same turn.

And you still need to make a battle-shock test for units not on the battlefield:

Not on the Battlefield 01.02.04

… the controlling player must make battle-shock tests for units that are not on the battlefield in their Command Phase if they are battle-shocked, or if they are below half strength.

Being at or Below Half Strength

This brings us neatly onto probably the most likely reason for a unit to start being battle-shocked: Being at or below half strength. This concept has had some subtle changes to be aware of in 11th edition. For the most part it’s still based on how many models there were in the unit at the start of the battle; this defines the unit's “starting strength.” However, if the starting strength of the unit was 1, then rather than using its number of models for this check it depends on the model’s remaining wounds.

Starting Strength and Attached Units

The starting strength of an attached unit is just the number of models in the unit, including the attached ones, at the start of the first battle round (not its number of models during mustering your army, so units that can split up during deployment do not start the battle at half strength). This does not change in 11th regardless of if all the models in the bodyguard unit are destroyed.

19.01.01 Some units have rules stating that when the bodyguard unit in an attached unit is destroyed, leader/support units that were attached to them become separate units with their original starting strength.

When the bodyguard unit in an attached unit affected by such a rule is destroyed, all of those leader/support units remain a single unit for all rules purposes.

This rule is essentially saying to ignore any old rules that say they revert to their old starting strength; they carry on as they were as if it was one big happy unit, that is mostly dead now. So your leaders/support models will still need to take battle-shock tests in this situation, as the unit would most definitely be below half strength.

Note that if the battle-shock test is being made during the battle-shock step of the Command Phase, you usually only need to make a single test regardless of if one or both of these conditions apply.

08.03.01 Multiple Battle-shock Rolls

In the battle-shock step (08.03), unless otherwise stated, no more than one battle-shock test roll can be made by each unit. If for any reason a unit must make a battle-shock roll in this step before one it is required to make for being battle-shocked or at or below half-strength, that unit does not also have to make a battle-shock roll for being battle-shocked or at or below half-strength.

So if anything causes a battle-shock test during this step, that’s the only one you have to make. Bear in mind that you can't choose to avoid enemy abilities here, if a test is required by an ability such as the Psychomancer's Nightmare Shroud.

In the Battle-Shock step of your opponent’s Command phase, if an enemy unit that is below its Starting Strength is within 6" of this model, that enemy unit must take a battle-shock test, subtracting 1 from the test when it does so.

Then this is the test you need to make, even if the unit needed to make one for the core rules reasons. You can't avoid the debuff from this aura by choosing the take one of the "normal" battle-shock tests.

That covers the typical causes; from here we begin to venture into the scatter shot of battle-shock causes that are spread through the core rules.

Making a Desperate Escape Fall Back Move 18.04

Falling back has two modes in 11th edition: Ordered Retreat and Desperate Escape. You’ll see Order Retreat come up again later on in this guide, but Desperate Escape really lives up to its name. Not only does this move require hazard rolls for every model, it also causes the unit the need to take a battle-shock test if it’s not already battle-shocked.

Choosing to gain MOBILE for a move with a model that has the SUPER HEAVY WALKER ability

This is one of the odder ones because whilst there are some automatic battle-shock causes that bypass the leadership roll, this cause requires a roll that isn’t a battle-shock test.

SUPER HEAVY WALKER 24.35

Before moving that unit, you can select for all models in that unit to have the MOBILE keyword until that move ends. If you do, when that move ends, roll one D6: on a 1, that unit is battle-shocked.

Just a straight D6 roll with a 1 in 6 chance of causing the model to become battle-shocked.

Unit Abilities

Plenty of units have abilities that can cause or require battle-shock tests to be made. The T'au Piranha, for example, has its Drone Harassment Tactics ability:

At the end of your Movement phase, select one enemy unit within 12" of this unit; that enemy unit must take a battle-shock test.

These tests are made at the specified time in the ability, not during the command phase or during the battle-shock step, so units will have to take subsequent tests in their next command phase for being battle-shocked.

Automatic Battle-shock Causes (No Tests Made)

There are a few instances in the rules where units can become battle-shocked without any test or roll at all. Those include Combat Disembark Moves 18.04 and Emergency Disembark Moves 18.05. If you make either of these moves, your unit just “is battle-shocked,” so be aware of that.

Core Consequences of Battle-shock

Battle-shock has almost as many consequences as it does causes. This section covers all the core consequences, but factions can and do have rules and abilities that are tied to if units are battle-shocked that would be too numerous to cover here.

Objective Control is modified to “-” 01.07

As its name suggests, the Objective control characteristic determines how much a unit contributes to the control of an objective per model. Having it modified to “-” results in the models in that unit not contributing any amount of control when contesting an objective.

Is there a difference between being modified to “-” or “0”?

No. Some older abilities and rules still reference battle-shock modifying an ability to ‘0’ rather than ‘-’. In 11th edition these are considered one and the same state though, and characteristics modified to either can not be modified again by other rules.

Note that rules that modify a characteristic in this step to a new value of ‘0’, ‘-’, or ‘*’ cannot be modified by other rules (in such cases skip over steps 2-5).

I’d expect more rules to use the ‘-’ term going forward, as ‘0’ implies you can still add modifiers to it in a way that ‘-’ does not.

Controlling player can cannot target such units with Stratagems 01.07

Pretty straight forward one, this; you can’t target them with stratagems. One aspect of this to consider, though, is that this includes the Insane Bravery Stratagem.

First of all, you can’t use this stratagem outside of the command phase, so it can only prevent battle-shock for units that are making their checks in the command phase, but it also can’t be used on units that are already battle-shocked. If the unit became battle-shocked during the previous turn due to an enemy ability or because the unit made a specific move type, then this stratagem can’t be used to succeed on the test that unit needs to make in the subsequent command phase.

Unit is not eligible start an action, and any action it has started cannot be completed 16.01

Important for scoring victory points in many different missions, being battle-shocked causes actions to fail and such units can’t start actions whilst battle-shocked. Pay careful attention to the completion timing of your actions to manage your risk of failing an action if it will take more than a single turn to complete.

Cannot make an Ordered Retreat Fall Back Move 09.07

Whilst you can still fall back, you will be forced to make a Desperate Escape Fall Back move, which I covered above. Ordered Retreat Fall Back moves avoid the hazard rolls and the need to make a battle-shock test, but unlike when making a Desperate Escape Fall Back move, models making an Ordered Retreat Fall Back move can not move through enemy models.

Cannot make Surge Moves 21.01

Closing up the core rules consequences of being battle-shocked is the inability to make surge moves. These are special moves usually triggered by something your opponent has done to your unit, such as the Blood Surge ability of Khorne Berzerkers:

Blood Surge: In your opponent’s Shooting phase, when an enemy unit has shot, if a model in this unit was destroyed as a result of those attacks, this unit can make a surge move of up to D6+2".

Be aware that they do not mention this restriction in the ability themselves; the surge move rules in the core rules restrict them.
