Commander Focus: Artifacts, Changing Types, & Sanwell, Avenger Ace

July 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Ah, the Avengers: Earth's mightiest heroes, debuting all the way back in 1963. A collection of heroes of varying renown, which finally received a dedicated Magic set in Marvel Super Heroes, last month. Today's piece is indeed about an Avenger, given the set has had such a riveting impact on all formats, though one I've wanted to cover for some time. To clarify, I'm no rabid hater of Universes Beyond, but a deeply interesting Monocolor Commander can only nag at my brain so long before I'm honorbound to cover it; it's merely a funny coincidence that today's card of interest is Sanwell, Avenger Ace. Vehicles as an archetype have never truly gotten there, with one of the most popular options being appreciated more for the fact it isn't a Creature, in Shorikai, Genesis Engine, and the other aiming to spend as little mana as possible on Vehicles in a given game, being Greasefang, Okiba Boss. Sanwell, having arrived in Brother's War Commander, ties Vehicles in with Artifact Creatures on the whole, and does so via a semi-random mechanism that leads to highly-variable, reactive gameplay. If that sounds up your alley, join me as we cover this entirely-untopical Avenger. Excelsior!

"Whenever Sanwell becomes tapped"

You've probably not heard of Sanwell prior to this article, and frankly that's understandable; clocking in at just shy of ~200 decks on EDHREC a cool 4 years after its printing, this Commander cratered almost immediately. That is, however, in spite of it being a 2 mana advantage engine in Monowhite, and those aspects of the card give it far more teeth than its popularity might suggest. First and foremost, Sanwell can attack without concern so long as it's with an Artifact Creature; we'll talk about it later, but there's a host of ways to ensure Sanwell can swing by his lonesome, or otherwise just become tapped in another way. The real meat of his text is in that second ability though, giving you the opportunity to cast an Artifact Creature or Vehicle from among the top 6 cards of your Library, with no risk of them being lost. If you whiff, you can always try again, but this does allow for Sanwell to alter the casting window on some very powerful cards, when done at Instant-speed. Surprising the opponent with something like an Unlicensed Hearse—ripped off the top like a jackpot in slots—makes you feel alive. Of course, given the semi-random nature of the potential pulls, the specificity of these answers can vary card-to-card. Skyseer's Chariot was originally in the list, but it very often appeared when not especially useful, or was lost in the Library when it would have stopped a combo. You will be able to dig for some serious answers, 6 cards at a time, but don't count on it.

Being at a fairly low mana cost, and in Monowhite, the utility of Sanwell's ability cannot be understated. At minimum, so long as you're hitting a playable card each turn, Sanwell represents a shocking amount of card advantage for a color identity which has historically lacked it, and in the Command Zone at a pittance. At time of writing, the deck plays a total of 31 targets, which gives us just over a ~90% chance to hit at least 1 card we can cast, each time we roll the dice. That's a number I'm quite comfortable with, and so long as you play around 30 options, Sanwell should feel quite consistent. This is the kind of Commander where it pays to familiarize yourself with a hypergeometic calculator (I appreciate Aether Hub), and put a bit of stats knowledge to the test; while you're sitting at ~90% to hit at least 1 option, recall the fact that percentage is affected by every instance of >1 Artifact Creature/Vehicle, as well. It's worth knowing how often you'll have but a single option to slam onto the board (here ~29%), as well as the average cost of cards you might hit, so you can reserve the necessary mana to actually play the card Sanwell offers. In short, if you like fiddling with numbers, ratios, and stats, Sanwell is an absolute treat of a Commander to build around.

Assembling Our Avenger

I still think about RoboCop (2014) on the regular. Bad film, amazing scene.

The endgoal of any well-constructed Sanwell deck is to find ways to tap & untap him as many times as possible, in order to find the necessary tools in the deck to either combo off or create enough board pressure to win. How that actually gets achieved will look different list-to-list, but in this version it begins with ample means of turning Sanwell into an Artifact. This is a fairly similar package to the likes we used when I covered Rebbec, Architect of Ascension on The Puzzlebox, but given we can use Equipment/Auras without it falling off, that toolbox is a fair bit larger. There's of course Ashnod's Transmogrant, a highly underrated card when it comes to doing silly things, but also the immensely tutorable Silverskin Armor, and even Transmogrifying Licid. The thing to notice in talking about these cards is the ways we have of seeing them—Transmogrant is able to be found off of Tezzeret or Urza's Saga, Silverskin from any Equipment tutor, and even the Licid is itself an Artifact Creature...able to be ripped off the top via Sanwell, to your table's confusion. While this does of course allow Sanwell to attack alone safely, the real meat is in allowing you to better-utilize Voltaic Construct & Clock of Omens. The latter card should be ringing alarm bells, for anyone familiar with its use in cEDH Magda, also here it's mostly fair.

If our goal is just to see more cards, Clock essentially reads 'Tap another Artifact, Sanwell triggers'. This is because we can of course tap Sanwell for its cost, while targeting him for its effect, meaning we need only use one other Artifact to complete the sequence. Go figure, Sanwell finds an untapped Artifact when he successfully hits, and you have the mana to cast it. That's why we're playing a host of discounters, which should allow us to sort-of storm off, once we've secured the right pieces. Foundry Inspector, Voyager Quickwelder, & Jhoira's Familiar all do this marvelously, and of course are cards we can find with Sanwell himself. It's a running theme that the best cards Sanwell gets help you accrue further value with our Commander, and it means a fairly-innocuous pick for the Command Zone can quickly snowball out of control in the right circumstances. As far as non-Artifact reliant untappers, we have the pairing of Honor-Worn Shaku & Relic of Legends, and in a similar vein, the tutorable Paradise Mantle. Once Sanwell can tap for mana, things get out of hand rather quickly, compounding with the discounters to start jamming free Artifact Creatures on our opponents' turns. Sting, the Glinting Dagger & Thornbite Staff also do great work here when coupled with other means of tapping, giving us the capacity to react to opposing plays, and wring every drop of value from the fact this is a 4-player game. Don't forget though—Thornbite has a contemporary we can find with Sanwell, Dross Scorpion, which works if our Commander's been turned into a cyborg.

All's Well That's Sans-well

Bad times ahead.

Let's talk about a few of the interesting silver bullets available to Sanwell, because while you could just use him like a White Magda and find combo pieces, don't you want to jam a randomly-rolled Cryptothrall off the top in response to removal? Sanwell uniquely provides you access to an arsenal of options that not even you know are available, and while that can at times be frustrating (as mentioned with the Skyseer's Chariot example in our intro) Sanwell continues to be interesting time and time again, because of the variance. The human brain loves a bit of gambling, and with Sanwell and a tuned 99, you're playing with the house's chips. As far as other protection goes, Paladin Danse, Maverick is a great inclusion here (with Sanwell often being an Artifact, and always being a Human), and Skrelv is a delight. There is a world where you run, of all things, an Infect/Toxic subtheme with Skrelv, Myr Convert/Plague Myr, Virulent Silencer, etc but your mileage may vary—people tend to focus you, once Poison counters enter the discussion.

Back onto fun inclusions, Scaretiller mandates we play fetchlands, and works like a ramp version of Sanwell, and Canoptek Scarab Swarm is a fantastic board-in-a-box, giving us tons of chump blockers or Artifacts to tap with Clock. Vault Guardsman is the only new card from Marvel we're playing, at time of writing, but it's a pretty good one! Artifact Creature with Convoke and removal is basically purpose-built to be good in Sanwell, as we can use its ability to tap bodies for its cost from exile, when seen with our Commander. In short, it's basically always free. I think the most exciting card in the whole bundle, though, is Arcbound Reclaimer: The capacity to put Artifacts back on top of the Library can not only help smooth our draws, and recur key noncreature Artifacts, but also guarantee a Sanwell hit. I cannot describe how many times this card was the stand-out in testing, whether played fairly, or via a Agatha's Soul Cauldron. Finally, if a deck can justify Lodestone Golem, you bet he's going in there. A one-sided tax not only shoves free spells, but also helps us keep pace as a Monowhite deck, it's classic Death & Taxes philosophy at work.

Avenger's Endgame

Great to get some ace rep in the Avengers.

In terms of win conditions, this is where I feel the list has a few issues; what it actually wants to be playing is likely some form of Scrap Trawler combo, or producing infinite mana via Voltaic Construct and a card which taps for enough mana. That said, the moment you shift towards making Sanwell a tool to dig for combo pieces (which indeed, he does admirably), you lose the capacity to play a density of silver bullets that makes the Commander compelling in the first place. I believe Sanwell's best-enjoyed when you're gambling on hitting interaction in the top 6, playing a tempo-y gameplan that hinges around a bit of luck & a lot of discounts. You can absolutely build Sanwell as more combo-centric (or indeed, more board-centric by way of Virulent Silencer), but this list rides along the middle in affording plenty of ideas upon which to base your deck. Think of the list in this article as scaffolding to be built upon, not the end product without edits. In this list, the actual win condition is most often Halo Fountain, a fairly-telegraphed but safe bet once Sanwell's popped off, or combat damage after the dust has settled. If you chose to jam Silencer or Digsite Engineer to make your combat phase far scarier, I wouldn't bat an eye, especially given this is a Bracket 2 list. You could also launder together some form of infinite mana by way of a Pili-Pala pile and Agatha's Soul Cauldron, resetting your Sanwell via removing a single counter at a time with Arcbound Reclaimer, which to me is a far funnier, more Rube-Goldberg-esque win condition that's only come up a single time in testing thus far.

Example Decklist: Twiddle Me This, Sanwell

There's a pretty good balance between jank and competence here on display, seeing Transmogrifying Licid right next to Esper Sentinel, both receiving solid synergy points from Sanwell. It is fairly interaction-light, as we need to keep a necessary density of hits for our Commander, so much of our means of removing or protecting cards has to arrive via that semi-random slot machine ability; in an unforgiving meta, that can be rough, but in Bracket 2 it's been a blast. As a connoisseur of Monowhite Commanders that produce an inordinate amount of card advantage (my favorite Commander of all time is Beza, the Bounding Spring after all) Sanwell gets a stamp of approval from me.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

As mentioned, Sanwell is an interesting case of getting far less...interesting, the higher your power level climbs. That's not to say a B3 or B4 Sanwell is without cool pieces, but more than it quickly starts to look like a Monowhite Magda, and that's a sorry comparison (albeit a high bar). If you're using Sanwell as an early enabler for things like Mox Amber that can dig for combo pieces, by way of Metalworker, Scrap Trawler, etc you can absolutely raise the ceiling, and there's no doubt in my mind a Bracket 4 Sanwell exists and can find your 2-card infinites by way of Artifact Creatures with relative ease. So, if you're the kind of player that wants to start this deck like a project, and slowly tune it up, that's certainly a possibility worth pursuing!

As Beyond, So Within

Josu Hernaiz makes a great case for returning to do more art for Kamigawa: Titanbreach!

As I mentioned, you probably hadn't heard of Sanwell prior to this article, and that's true of essentially every Commander I've had a fondness for since beginning my coverage here on Tabletop Battles. Sure, I love making new takes on popular options, but my real home is with those oddballs that were instantly-forgotten, swept aside by the tides of an ever-increasing pace of set releases. When I think of some of my favorite Commanders—Harold and Bob, First Numens, Gorex, the Tombshell, Gau, Feral Youth—they largely arrive as Monocolor options thrown into a Commander Deck, not with the intent to be played as Commanders in their own right, but to check off a quota of characters mentioned. That's only grown more apparent with Universes Beyond, with Marvel Super Heroes having more Legendary Creatures across its products than were printed in the entire year in which I started playing Magic. While it's becoming more difficult to sift through the set releases to find interesting, underplayed options, the actual volume of those options is increasing at a rapid pace. In spite of that, I felt more excitement to talk about Sanwell than I did another Marvel Commander this week...why is that? I think it largely stems from many new Legendary Creatures really not feeling Legendary, from lacking the necessary unique text to drive deckbuilding. They feel like forgettable Uncommons or Rares with faces you recognize. In spite of that though, I do have a few more actual Avengers I want to touch on, between Commander Focus & Puzzlebox, so forgive me this cheeky little deviation to talk about a hidden gem from an actual Magic plane. No, New York doesn't count.

Until next time, don't mess up that Avengers: Doomsday pile.
