How to Paint Everything: Star Wars Legion Mandalorian Warriors and Initiates

July 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With the new Mandalorian Army coming out for Star Wars Legion, it is only appropriate that we go over how to get your models painted and on the tabletop. Since there are so many units in the new Army Box, we figured it would take more than just one painter. That's where dirtboi and I will come in to provide a guide on painting a unit of Mandalorian Warriors and Mandalorian Initiates.

Thank you to Asmodee for sponsoring a series of articles on Star Wars: Legion.

While there isn't an "official" scheme for either Warriors, Warrior Fire Support, or Initiates, the box art does do a great job of representing the Children of the Watch Clan from The Mandalorian TV show. They have a rag-tag appearance, using whatever weapons and armor they can get their hands on, most forged by The Armorer.

Credit: Atomic Mass Games

While you can follow the box art, pick a clan-specific scheme, or come up with your own, these models have a ton of great detail and look great on the table. We both had different interpretations of this scheme for the units we painted, and I cannot wait to see all of the unique paint schemes the wider community comes up with.

Mandalorian Warriors

Mandalorian Sleuth here, starting with the Mandalorian Warriors. These kits have so many options; it has been a little intimidating trying to select whether I want capes, no capes, or the type of jetpack. Here is a picture of one of the sprues to illustrate the amount of choice.

I decided on a mix of options: some have the capes, some have small jetpacks, large jetpacks, and I also made one of my minis with the GALAAR rifle, purely so I can go over a quick method of making it look accurate to the TV shows it is in. I prime my minis black, so I can "cheat" a little bit with shadows. The "cheat" is to avoid the parts of the model you can't reach and let the black primer act as a natural shadow instead. For instance, if I can't realistically get my brush behind the arm of one of the minis holding a weapon against its chest, I just don't paint it and leave it black, creating a deep and natural-looking shadow.

To begin, I used the back of the box as a reference when deciding what colors to put where. We have a combination of yellow ocherous colors, browns, blues, greens, and even some metallic silver on our minis. I am a sporadic painter, so in some of my pictures you may see parts I leave until later or see a step I did out of order; they are your minis, so paint them as you wish. I just followed a method of base - midtone - wash - highlight on each piece individually. If you follow the steps and trust the process, everything will come out looking great in the end.

I decided as I went about what color I wanted each armor piece. I'll break down how I did each color and include pictures. I like the ragtag design of the Children of the Watch, but I tend to paint with much brighter colors so some parts of my minis will stand out and grab the onlookers' attention.

For my base coats on the armor pieces, I chose multiple colors. Since I wanted these Mandos to feel like they are mismatched and kind of just using what is available, I split my colors up into red, blue, teal, ochre, tan, green, and silver. For each color this is the base coat I used:

Red: Pro Acryl Dark Red

Blue: Pro Acryl Ultramarine

Teal: Pro Acryl Dark Jade

Ochre and Tan: Pro Acryl Warm Brown

Green: Pro Acryl Dark Yellow Green

Silver: Army Painter Fanatic Shining Silver

I picked brown for the base color of many of these, because it's such a useful tone, and can adapt to many situations. You'll see it a lot, and how it can be transformed using midtones.

For the undersuits, I used Pro Acryl Cool Grey, Pro Acryl Warm Brown, and Two Thin Coats Rodent Grey. I didn't give the undersuits a midtone because I didn't want the eye to be drawn to it; I want it to be darker on purpose. This is something you can do, though, if you want the model to pop more.

For all belts and pouches, I used the Pro Acryl Warm Brown, but you'll see how I transformed the color later so it ends up looking different; the capes are a base of either the Warm Brown or Two Thin Coats Cuirass Leather depending on if I felt the mini was too bright or not.

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Midtones are where I do the brunt of my work. For each color I chose a different midtone; the ones I chose are a bit brighter than normal, but I love high contrast in my paint schemes. To do this, I layered my preferred color over the basecoat. This step is pretty important, and you should try to leave a little bit of your darker color on the outside of your layer here, that darker color is going to help add depth without much work at all. This is also a great way to practice brush control. I don't touch up any metallic paint, but you might see hints of what I did to the metallic silver.

Red: Pro Acryl Orange Red

Blue: Two Thin Coats Gigawatt Blue

Teal: Pro Acryl Dark Turquoise

Ochre: Two Thin Coats Manticore Ochre

Tan: Pro Acryl Beige Grey

Green: Two Thin Coats Goblinoid Green

For the pouches and belts, I used a midtone of Pro Acryl Black Red because I feel that this leather has a bit of a more reddish tint to it. You'll see how that dark red on top of our Warm Brown is going to create color separation and depth in a simple way. My red is a little darker, but because of how the color works, it reinforces the leather look.

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

The next step was to apply differently colored washes onto the different parts of the model. This is also where I gave the metallic silver some love.

Green: Two Thin Coats Orc Flesh Wash

Blue and Teal: Two Thin Coats Tempest Blue Wash

Red: Two Thin Coats Hellion Red Wash

Silver, Grey undersuits, smoke: The Army Painter Dark Tone

Tan, Brown, Ochre, any belts, pouches, and the capes: Warhammer Seraphim Sepia

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

The Seraphim Sepia wash is going to bring together a lot of our colors since all of them are different brown tones.

For the highlights, I focused on edge highlighting all the armor pieces and some of the other details, like the tops of the pouches. Here are the paints I used to edge highlight the previous stages:

Red: Two Thin Coats Orange Flare

Blue, Teal: Two Thin Coats Ray Gun Glow

Ochre: Pro Acryl Bismuth Yellow

Tan, Brown, and Metallic Silver: Pro Acryl Dark Ivory

Green: Pro Acryl Bright Green

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

I ended up saving the Jetpacks for last, because I was going to use them as a way to identify the unit on the tabletop, and didn't know which color I wanted. I decided to go with the same metallic silver I used for the armor pieces so it would be uniform and easy for me to identify, but you could mix and match however you would like using the steps outlined above.'

For the weapons, I used a trick I learned from other painting tutorials: a base coat of my silver and went over it with Warhammer Contrast Black Templar. If the contrast paint is applied thinly enough, it actually lets some of the metallic sheen through, and creates a cool effect. I overdid it on some of the minis, so I'd recommend practising this method properly if you're interested in using it. For the SHUKUR, I decided a quick light grey with a Dark Tone wash would suffice - I can highlight later if I want. For the GALAAR, I base-coated in the Cool Grey, washed in Dark Tone, and highlighted with the Ivory that I already used on the armor.

And with that, the unit needs some basing. I went for a more "destroyed Mandalore" / industrial look, so I am using some AK Interactive Wargame Terrain Concrete texture paste. I slather this around to give a grey and dusty/rocky looking appearance, then go back over it with a Dark Tone wash to bring down the brightness.

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

They are now tabletop-ready. If I wanted to, I could go in and glue on some rocks or even make some bricks out of green stuff and scatter them around the bases to get a more "destroyed industrial" look. I could do even more highlights, or put some paint on the parts I left plain black, like the rangefinders, for now, however, they are done.

Credit: Mandalorian Sleuth

Mandalorian Initiates

Hello everyone! Micah (dirtboi) here giving you a rundown of how I painted some Mandalorian Initiates! I also really like the ragtag look from the Children of the Watch, and I wanted to take a stab at that scheme, but with a twist. I limited my armor colors to only 3, plus metallics, and had friends pick out those 3 random colors: Red, Orange, and Green. For the other details like boots, belts, and the undergarments, I stuck to black, brown, and tan, alternating between models.  It was a fun and refreshing project, but I need a break from batch painting before my next one (and to clean my desk!).

First Steps

I began as any hobby project does, with building the models. The Mandalorian Warriors kit has a TON of building options (see Mandalorian Sleuth's section of this article for more), but I chose capes, generic helmets, the non-GALAAR weapons, and no jetpacks (sad). I primed the models in Monument Hobbies Dark Neutral Grey and got to work!

Picture credit: dirtboi

From there, I started with a messy basecoat on the undersuit. I chose Pro Acryl Coal Black, Dark Umber, and Beige Grey from their AMP Colors collab. Once I was done with the Undersuit, I swapped the colors for the boots, belts, and sundries. For example: If the undersuit was Dark Umber, the boots and belts would be either Coal Black or Beige Grey, alternating between the colors.

While I was messy with the basecoat on the undersuit, the more I progressed throughout the project, the more confident I became with brush control. Patience, steady hands, and three points of contact, my friends.

Loading...

From there, I basecoated the armor segments. For the colors my friends chose, I went with Pro Acryl Dark Camo Green and Orange, and Warhammer Khorne Red and the classic Leadbelcher. I picked random segments, choosing random bits, not paying much attention to color composition on some of the models, except for a few split-colored helmets. I also did the other metallic bits at this step: the pistol in the holster, their blaster, and their belt buckle.

Loading...

I also base coated their capes, wanting a darker blue and purple to add to the clown fiesta of these Mandalorians, but not to take away from the focus of the armor. I chose Pro Acryl Dark Purple and Warhammer Kantor Blue. There are two models with two capes, so I alternated the colors on each of them.

Loading...

Basing and Washing

From there, I went back through each model for a cleanup step on the base coats and based with Warhammer Astrogranite. I basecoated the tactical rocks on the jumping gals base with Warhammer Zandri Dust. After the base had a lot of time to dry, I hit the entire model and the bases with a thin wash of Warhammer Agrax Earthshade, using quite a bit of water. This helped with a bit of the flow into those recesses and prevents egregious coffee stains if I allow any to pool.

Loading...

I had a bit of a hiccup as I started the wash on a few models; I had forgotten to add contrast to their blasters! I went back and added Warhammer Black Templar thinned with The Army Painter's Speedpaint Medium and some water for a thin black look that still reflects the metallic from underneath. Then I went back and continued with the wash as mentioned above.

Picture credit: dirtboi

Highlighting

After that wash dried, I drybrushed the 2 tactical rocks with Warhammer Karak Stone, and the base with Warhammer Administratum Grey.

Loading...

Next, the slow layering step, starting with the three armor colors. For the green, red, and orange, I chose Pro Acryl Camo Green, Bold Pyrrole Red, and Orange Yellow. I really thinned these paints down, trying to build smaller layers, focusing the brighter color on the raised details and towards the top of the model. I don't think I achieved my goal on every piece, but I liked the outcome!

Loading...

Following that, I layered up the capes, mixing some paints this time to keep the colors darker while still providing some highlighting. For the blue, it was a 1:1 mix of Warhammer Kantor Blue and Altdorf Guard Blue, and for the purple it was 1:1 mix of Pro Acryl Dark Purple and Warhammer Xereus Purple.

Loading...

I had spent way too much time layering, so it was time to do some edge highlighting! For the undersuit, belts, and boots, I chose more of an edge highlight rather than a layer to preserve the basecoat color. For the brown, black, and beige, I highlighted with Pro Acryl Light Umber, Neutral Grey, and Warhammer Wraithbone, respectively. I also hit the bare armor with a light layer of Warhammer Runefang Steel and scratched at the top of the weapons with the side of my brush.

Loading...

Final Basing

I was done with the model, so I chose to spice up the base a little bit. Burned grass tufts from Gamer's Grass and some Light Slate Grey Pigment powder from Vallejo Pigments, and I had ourselves a little dusty base! I tried to go for a Navarro in Mandalorian season 3 looking base, but I think the pigment powder is a bit too bright for what I was going for. Either way, I love the way it came out!

Loading...

And with a black base rim, I'm calling them done!

Picture credit: dirtboi

Final Thoughts

I had a lot of fun painting these models. They have a ton of sweet detail and take to highlights easily. I do wish I had picked different highlight colors for the green and orange; they weren't the highlight colors I was going for, but I really enjoy the outcome. I'm already thinking of replicating this for my next Initiate squad using different colors, but The Armorer is up next on my painting desk. After I tidy up the mess of paint pots and projects, of course!

See y'all in the next one! - dirtboi

Mandalorian Sleuth here, and I agree with Micah; I had a ton of fun trying out some new paint and hitting all the details on these models. These models have some great focal points I can draw an onlooker's eyes to and will really make them pop on the tabletop. They are so adaptable, I think you could try any scheme, and it would look great. It's now time for me to get Paz and his best frenemy Din painted up and ready for the tabletop.

How do you feel about these models? Are you going with an official scheme or a custom clan of your own? Let us know, and have fun painting!
