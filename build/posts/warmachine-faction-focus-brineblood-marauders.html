Warmachine Faction Focus: Brineblood Marauders

July 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to our Warmachine Faction Focus series here on Tabletop Battles! In these articles, we will be taking a deep dive into each of the Prime factions available in Warmachine, writing about their rules, their leaders and units, how they play, and give some examples of competitive lists. Whether you’re new to the game and looking for advice, or a Warmachine Veteran looking to get more insight into the various armies of the Iron Kingdoms, we’ve got something for you here.

Today, we are looking at the Brineblood Marauders, the looting and plundering arm of the Southern Kriels. These terrors of the high seas are one of the Iron Kingdoms' fastest and hard-hitting forces, striking fear into the hearts of sea dog and land lubber alike.

Credit Steamforged Games

Why Should I Play Brineblood Marauders?

The Brineblood Marauders are a fast, hard-hitting army with access to some of the longest melee threat ranges in the game, as well as some of the most impressive shooting. From the accuracy buffs to their guns in the form of the Pyg Dirge to the melee damage buffs in the Booty Boss, this army has a dearth of efficient solos and command attachments to make their relatively unimpressive-looking warrior models into true bullies on the tabletop. Not only does their army have the most hot air balloons per capita, it has one of the pound-for-pound best colossal warbeasts out there in the form of the Abyssal King. There are fewer more satisfying feelings than sending a colossal 16" down the field and having it turn whatever it touches into a greasy smear.

Strengths and Weaknesses

Strengths

  • Access to both powerful ranged, and powerful melee options
  • Diverse stable of leaders, whether you love going for their leader's throat or grinding them them down slowly, point-for-point
  • An almost ridiculous amount of utility in their selection of solos and command attachments
  • One of the best colossals in the game with lots of ways to make it shine

Weaknesses

  • Unimpressive defensive profiles
  • Dependence on support models to shore up middling base stats
  • Somewhat against heavy shooting or long melee threat ranges
  • Low quality light warbeast options

Army Abilities and Play Style

Brinebloods are typical in that, like other Trollkin armies of the past, many of their warrior models have Tough. On top of that, what Troll army is complete without Regenerating, Snacking warbeasts that literally chew through enemy models to recover from their wounds? All this paints a familiar picture to those who have spent some time in the Iron Kingdoms, but the Brinebloods are anything but your average trolls. Their training at sea and adaptive nature has given nearly every model in the army Amphibious.

The defensive slant of their past incarnations has been cast aside to make way for a brutal, all-in offense that utilizes their trademark hardiness to protect themselves on the approach before bringing their superior speed and firepower to bear. Not only can they move through water unimpeded, they can also hide in it, giving them more ways to remain intact before contact with the enemy. Brinebloods want to get to the front lines before the opponent can react and leave no survivors or room for retaliation. This means deadly close combat prowess as well as devastating shooting.

Leaders

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Admiral Boomhowler

Boomhowler is a consummate commander, with a focus on army-wide support. With her Battle Plans, she can make Brinebloods move and see through each other, give them Set Defense, or Reposition [3"]. Her most signature spell is Rough Seas, which causes enemies making ranged attacks at models in her control area roll an extra die to hit and remove the highest result. While Brineblood defensive stats on their own leave something to be desired, Rough Seas combined with Concealment or Cover makes for very difficult targets even against the best shooting. This plus her feat Rough House, granting Rapid Healing and +2 DEF, ensures that her offense continues uninterrupted. She herself is a dangerous melee threat as well, with her spell Storm Rager giving her (or any other single warrior model in her army) a huge boon to melee accuracy and damage, as well as armor. While she may struggle into the more elite high accuracy armies or those with easy access to Grievous Wounds, Boomhowler has a long history as a great all-rounder for Brinebloods. In her armies, you will often see a lot of Marauder Crew with Tapper attachments.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Braghen Ragemonger

If you want a warlock to get work done himself, look no further than Ragemonger. With nasty personal threat range reaching as high as 25", few are safe from the wrathful gaze of the shark-riding, spear-throwing, fisher of man, machine, and beast. Ragemonger's feat, Jump the Shark is his most defining feature, allowing him to jump 5" from wherever he is standing before granting your entire army a piece of his legendary luck. For one turn, your models' melee attack and damage rolls turn all their 1's into 6's. Delivering this titanic melee threat is easy, as Ragemonger grants all of his warbeasts Shield Guard, and getting stuck in melee is a nightmare thanks to the unholy dice math of Star-Crossed. Even humble DEF 12 Marauder Crew can seem pretty hardy when the dice are in their favor. Ragemonger is one of the best warlocks for Reef Trolls thanks to their suite of self-supporting animi, too. Ragemonger's focus on his personal output means he is not as keen to support his army outside of his feat turn, however, so the importance of that initial engagement cannot be understated.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Captain Firequill

Continuing the theme of tremendous personal output and devastating offensive feats, the good Captain Firequill represents the ranged output of an entire fleet of pirate vessels. With his brace of Magelock pistols, Firequill has the just the tool for any occasion, whether you need to cast a free spell after a kill, kill several models with a spray attack, or slam something away with Momentum. All this comes with the convenience of Black Penny to allow him to shoot into any melee without penalty. Heavy Rain, his feat, provides a central idea to build around when constructing his army: More guns! Boosting every ranged damage roll in his control area and giving his battlegroup True Sight means that there are few excuses for anything to be left standing after his army lets loose. His spell list is focused on utility, excepting High Noon which gives a rare +2 DEF bonus and Dodge. As the game goes long, though, Firequill becomes more deadly, as Fortune Hunter (an additional die on attack and damage rolls against character models) makes him an immediate and unerring threat to leaders or elite models that happen to still be standing. Firequill has been a true terror to behold since his rework in January, and even after a recent balance tweak, he is positioned to be exploding the enemy for a while to come.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Captain Shadowtongue

A Trollkin with a Cryxian edge, all the warrior models included in Shadowtongue's army are granted Undead. He has access to some of the most iconic Cryx spells and a cost reducing feat to let you sling with the best of them. Ghost Walk, Scything Touch, Hex Blast, and Gallows would already make for an all-time great spell list, but he backs it up with Fog War and Revive. Wrapping this whole package together is his ability Dark Rituals, which lets him channel spells through any friendly faction warrior within 16" of him. Hiding from this necromancer is even harder still thanks to his ability Bird's Eye, which allows him and his battlegroup to see through cloud effects, forests, and other models when drawing line of sight. Shadowtongue's arcane talent is offset by a lack of ability to help his army's accuracy, so his forces will depend on a suite of solos and warbeasts that are geared to that end. Be sure to bring Madame Moriary with Shadowtongue for access to free Ghost Walks, free upkeeps, and rerolls when he really needs to land that game-ending nuke.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Commodore Foulblood

With a stiff drink and a sharp blade, Foulblood inspires his troops to new heights of savagery with the help of some liquid courage. Playing Foulblood is straightforward, if a little one-note. His ability Open Bar gives all friendly Brinebloods within 10" Stumbling Drunk, preventing them from being knocked down and thus letting them continue making Tough rolls. His spell list is a list of stellar defensive buffs as well as Fury's Strength, which makes units like Marauder Crew into a major threat to hard targets that are way above their point cost. His feat grants +3 ARM to Stumbling Drunk models and the ability to stumble any direction they like, but this falls flat with the victim stats of nearly all Brineblood warriors. That aside, Blur granting a unit +3 DEF against shooting and the Mercenary Greygore Boomhowler's Shout it Out granting Force Barrier, Foulblood can make shooting his army difficult.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Warchief Bagadibawm

Bagadibawm represents the pinnacle of speed in an already pretty fast army, with a powerful feat that defines his strategy. Spirit World grants friendly faction models +2 SPD and Ghostly when he pops it, cranking the threat range of Marauder Crew (with a Bosun attachment) to 14" that goes through models and terrain (16" if Ol' Scuttlebutt sets up a Beacon). The same goes for an Abyssal King, but with Bagadibawm's spell Iron Flesh and the Abyssal King's animus, it can get up to ARM 24 after crashing through to the center the opposing army. The Warchief is also a close combat monster, capable one-rounding virtually any target from 13" away under the right conditions. The rest of his spell list is somewhat lackluster, but has some utility. Firestarter gives a cohort's melee and ranged weapons Continuous Effect: Fire, so AOE weapons can help clear infantry that much more efficiently. His Field Marshal [Fuel for the Flames] gives the added advantage of giving fire damage rolls that happen within 5" of his warbeasts an extra die of damage, leaving open some synergies with models like Pyg Battle Brigs and Kreuger, Wrath of Blighterghast.

Warjacks/Warbeasts

Non-Character Cohorts

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

The Vorogger is often the first 12 points you see spent in Brinebloods lists, thanks to Paralyzing Gaze. The ability to make the moderate accuracy of Brinebloods into a sure thing without the need to roll dice is invaluable , but stacking this -2 DEF debuff to models within 5" with Pyg Dirge's Mark Target make for hyper-accurate shooting from the otherwise low RAT models available. The army as a whole is reliant on the Vorogger which has modest personal output, but it also threatens a boostable Drag ranged weapon from 18" down the table thanks to its Jump ability.

The Deepborn Dire Troll, by contrast, offers less raw utility in favor of good base stats and some reliable options in its loadout. The purpose-defining feature of the Deepborn is its back slot which is the starting point for creating a "role" in the army list.  The guns on order for Deepborns are not long-ranged but instead offer a choice between a spray that doubles as a melee threat extender (RNG 3 melee attack with Drag) or an AOE with Quake for knocking down your foes. Each warlock will demand different things of a Deepborn, making the chassis one of the more broadly useful ones among MK IV armies. Even more warlock-dependent is the Reef Troll, with a suite of weapons and animi that are often underpowered without significant assistance, all for a payoff that can be mimicked for fewer points. Usually, a Reef Troll's role in a Brinebloods force will be to provide its animus and the use of its Shield Guard head option.

Character Cohorts

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Chum and Great Old One share the role of "tank" type warbeasts, each approaching the job with different rules. Both have high melee damage output, especially Chum with his explosive Warhead melee weapon that clears low armor chaff with extreme efficiency. His Foul Stench and Stumbling Drunk rule pair well with Enliven from Mawga'Bawza to make him hard to pin down. Great Old One (or GOO as some call him) is very resilient to shooting with Carapace, but has a lackluster animus in Crawling Chaos and a short melee threat range. Overall, both are somewhat pricey at 16 points, but often a fine choice in armies that don't have specialized needs from a battlegroup.

Super Heavies

Credit Steamforged Games

Ol' Scuttlebutt is a great tempo piece for warlocks like Bagadibawm and Ragemonger who both want to be in the middle of the table to threaten their potent melee threat ranges. With Roadblock and his animus, Scuttle he can help deliver models, on top of Hold On Lads! dragging models into the fray with him. His presence in a list must be very intentional to compete with space in a Brinebloods force, but he has stellar melee damage, good ranged damage, and provides utility to an army, which a super heavy must do if it's going to be worth its 22 points. At the time of writing, this is a new release, so what innovations there will be to come in list building with Ol' Scuttlebutt have yet to be seen broadly, but one should expect to see him in many lists going forward.

Colossal/Gargantuan

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

The Abyssal King has been a mainstay of competitive Brineblood list building since its release and to this day continues to represent the benchmark for tournament viable colossals. It has a great melee threat range, a lot of ranged weapons on a Fury 5 beast, and access to great defensive buffs. The Triton figurehead is far and away the best choice for the chassis, with a great animus and +1 SPD. The Plasma Nimbus granted by it is also great for eating lighter infantry in its way. More often than not, the Bulbous Chomper head's access to an additional melee attack and Eyeless Sight will win out over the Electro Lure, but the choice of an additional ranged attack (a spray!) is a very reasonable consideration when building your King. Most commonly you will find the King with any warlock that can increase his output or durability. Firequill is an obvious choice thanks to his feat, but Foulblood and Bagadibawm have ways to increase the King's armor past key damage break points. Abyssal King lovers will tell you that the model looks set up to put in work but its massive threat ranges tend to stall the middle of the table, letting you control the pace of engagement. This means that the King mostly does its work towards the end of the game where fewer models are left to bring it down.

Units

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Brineblood Marauders' namesake unit, the Marauder Crew, are the foremost choice for infantry in the army. Their base stats do not tend to impress, but combined with the ample support available and their modular command attachments, they can fit several key roles. Their Quartermaster attachment gives them some firepower, increasing the damage and range of their guns once per game, while the Bosun Crew extends their threat range with No Quarter and Vengeance. If you're feeling economical or want some synergy with either Boomhowler's or Foulblood's feats, you can use the Keg Tapper to grant the unit Stumbling Drunk for only 1 point.

The Lochabash Brothers are the smaller, more elite character cousins to the Marauder Crew. They have longer range guns with Snap Fire to pick off infantry, Brutal Charge, RNG 2 melee range for getting in close, and the very potent rule Unbreakable Wall to grant them cumulative ARM bonuses when standing next to each other. They share a health pool thanks to Sanguine Bond, so they take an outsized amount of effort to bring down much of the time. They hold buffs well, especially defensive buffs like Iron Flesh and Blur, and are at home in lists with every warlock in the army.

The last of the mainstay units of the army are the Pyg Boarding Party. Their utility is obvious and simple. They have Ambush and above average DEF. That's really it! They're cheap, too, so splashing them into a list isn't normally a huge ask. With how wide Steamroller 2026 objectives can be set apart from each other, being able to stand next to a far away enemy objective and deny them control points is worth the 5 points you spend on them. Their output is not impressive, but that's not why they're here.

The rest of the Brineblood units go from specialized to lackluster, the latter applying most to both the Pyg Shockers and the Fire Spitters. Both are ranged units which do benefit from the accuracy bump of the Pyg Dirge, but their low ranged damage and competition with the very efficient Marauder Crew make them rarely seen on the tabletop.

Pyg Galley Crew are great for Fury-hungry lists, especially those with Abyssal Kings, as their Condition ability can help manage your Fury without having to risk making your warlock vulnerable. Spirit Shamans provide utility to some armies as well with their ability to hand out Magical Weapon and create shallow water for your Amphibious models. They even have impressively high melee damage stats and Blessed weapons, with their only shortcoming being their low MAT scores.

Solos

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Brinebloods are very reliant on their solos for support, and the Booty Boss is the most obvious representative of this need. A generic melee damage buff for warriors, Set Defense, and Sneak provide so much broad utility that much of the time you will be taking two of them to cover your units. Stacking Stir the Blood with other damage buffs like Foulblood's Fury's Strength or Shadowtongue's Scything Touch let Marauder Crew punch way above their weight class. Consider the Booty Boss an unofficial attachment to any units your army takes.

Speaking of supporting Marauder Crew, First Mate Horgar Stormjaw provides a flat +1 to their attack rolls while they're within 10". He hits like a truck, especially with support, making him a linchpin in lists that play two or more units of Marauder Crew. Set him up with Boomhowler's Storm Rager to make him extra menacing.

The Pyg Dirge is tied with the Booty Boss for most important support piece in the Brinebloods roster. This title is earned by its Mark Target ability which grants a massive +2 to ranged attack rolls against enemy models within 5". Taking the average RAT stats up to elite numbers on top of a debuff like that from the Vorogger means your guns will be hitting even elite DEF stats without needing extra dice. The Dirge needs to commit to close range to provide its support and its ranged weapon isn't especially great, so make sure you kill whatever target it marks or you will quickly lose it. Dirges are at home especially with Firequill for help with all his shooting and with Shadowtongue as improvised channelers for his selection of offensive spells.

Mawga'Bawza is a warbeast support solo who loves to see high ARM heavies that she can target with Enliven or dole out extra attacks with Ancillary Attack. Ol' Scuttlebutt is a fantastic pairing, with the movement from Enliven triggering Hold on Lads! even on your opponent's turn.

For games you expect to go longer, the Surgeon brings some recursion with Grim Returns and the occasional trade with Shark Bait. It is also one of the army's only instances of Grievous Wounds! Surgeons go well with Shadowtongue to supplement his Revive spell.

Fire Guardians are the army's only innate channelers, but their lack of movement makes them best suited for handing out buffs to places your warlock can't or doesn't want to stand. Foulblood loves to be able to spread Fury's Strength or swap one of his many upkeep spells, so one is a great add to his force. Boomhowler takes advantage of their other main draw, however. While near a Fire Guardian, her spell Perdition becomes a much more considerable vector at RNG 12.

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Coming to some of the solos that see less play in competitive scenes, we have the Fugue Walker. While it does have some interesting utility with Foulblood, thanks to its ability to give itself Stumbling Drunk, its relatively low MAT and unimpressive damage output make it a niche choice at best.

Mr. Bogs on paper seems like quite the little output monster with his efficient SP 10, RAT 7 gun. Iron Sentinel even protects your modest investment of 3 points. Sadly, the army is starved for support, and options at his point level are often higher priority. That said, with Firequill his gun becomes terrifying into light and medium infantry during his feat.

Also suffering from lots of competition for its point, the Pyg Cannon Crew boasts a long range, medium accuracy gun that provides knockdown on demand. With support, it can often setup some inconvenient slams for enemy models or knock down a key target before other models go in to clean up. This is another model that pairs well with Firequill or even Foulblood thanks to his access to Fortune.

Sully has very little in the way of output, and his Annoyance and Albatross abilities are very easy to play around with correct order of activation. He is a great fit with Shadowtongue, however, as his high speed and flight allow him to be in perfect position to channel spells through with Dark Rituals. On top of channeling, though, is Fiery Blast which you can intentionally trigger with the damage that he takes when you channel through him! Sometimes the bird has to take one for the team.

Mercenaries

Credit @jtgardnerpaints

Magnus, Nostilla, and Carver are all great models on their own, but they do not synergize extremely well with the Brineblood Marauders' support-reliant structure. Since they rarely benefit from that support, it makes the most sense for the bulk of your points to be spent on faction models. The same is true for the two Mercenary battle engines, the Sky Bomber and Sky Raider. Such models are best used in armies that have faction-agnostic methods of support.

Madame Moriarty provides support of her own, her best spots often being in lists where she can maximize the use of Spell Slave. Shadowtongue and Foulblood both have spells that they want to cast each turn but often want to spend their Fury doing other things as well, and Moriarty is a worthy choice in their lists

After recent changes to model accuracy across the board, boostable attacks outside of battlegroups went drastically up in value. Krueger, Wrath of Blighterghast is an expensive but completely independent gun platform with great synergy with Bagadibawm's Fuel for the Flames. In any list he represents a very focused anti-infantry piece that is great at clearing jammed parts of the table.

Greygore Boomhowler, brother to the Brineblood warlock Thorga Boomhowler, is a great support piece in an army with pretty good support pieces. Shout it Out lets the paper thin ARM of Brineblood infantry stand up to blasts and maybe even dodge some guns, and he is hard to bring down with Inhuman Resolve. Packing a good suite of melee and ranged attacks on top of that, he is well worth his points in armies that want to flex some extra defense against shooting. His sister Thorga especially loves Force Barrier paired with her Rough Seas.

The mercenary with perhaps the least to offer the army is Nissak, Totem Huntress Champion. While she is a fine enough melee combat solo, she competes with the slightly more efficient Pyg Boarding Party as an ambusher.

Listbuilding with Brineblood Marauders

Once you've chosen a leader with Brinebloods and understood that leader's role in your list pair, building for Brinebloods is relatively straightforward. Often, it makes the most sense to think of your army in distinct, bespoke modules composed of the various synergies in the army. Often this boils down to "gun plus Pyg Dirges" and choosing the type of Marauder Crew command attachment that makes sense with your warlock and how many of each. The army revolves heavily around your Marauder Crew in many cases, so a pair of Booty Bosses is a common sight, alongside Stormjaw for some higher accuracy and occasionally Greygore Boomhowler for lists that would like some defense against blast weapons and medium accuracy direct ranged fire. An easy place to start when making these decisions is to look at what kinds of armies you xpect to face most and plan around them.

Other notable combinations include Pyg Galley Crew and either Ol' Scuttlebutt or the Abyssal King (or both!) for some fury efficiency and emergency frenzy management, as well as Mawga'Bawza for more attacks and the ability to keep your Scuttlebutt out of danger with Enliven. Enliven movement triggers Hold on Lads! on your opponent's turn, too, leaving the opportunity for some cheeky movement shenanigans.

While Stealth is sometimes not a backbreaking rule to have, Brinebloods have a surprising amount of access to it. Since the whole roster is Amphibious, standing in water and using Sneak from the Booty Boss gives a unit stealth on demand. The army has several ways to generate shallow water in the form of Spirit Shamans' Black Waters and the Deep Dweller head animus on the Deepborn Dire Troll. With Shadowtongue, triggering Prowl is even easier with Fog of War.

What to Watch Out For

Brinebloods suffer greatly from a lack of list-building space thanks to their reliance on support from models like Vorogger and various incidental support solos that are required to make their armies hit accurately and with meaningful damage. This can make lists that carve out this support a big challenge for them. Additionally, in the case of the Vorogger, committing a 12 point model to a dangerous position just for the ability to lower the enemy's DEF can often mean throwing away 12 points in the midgame. Knowing when to put your pieces into position and when to reserve them for a perfect turn is pain point on the Brinebloods learning curve.

All Trollkin armies hate to see Grievous Wounds on the other side of the table, but Brinebloods especially loathe it. The low durability of the average marauder means that their only line of defense against direct hits is Tough. Without it, the army's infantry blows away with the wind. Losing the ability to Regenerate can be occasionally inconvenient to our battlegroups, but Boomhowler's entire feat is shut down by Grievous Wounds.

Strong shooting can be a danger if you are caught without the right tools as well, causing many Brineblood list pairs to have a dedicated list just for combating high volumes of ranged attacks.

Lastly, being prepared for armies that have longer melee threat ranges than yours is essential. Brinebloods rely heavily on getting the first hit in to win, so any time you are forced to react to the enemy strategy, you will be fighting an uphill battle.

Sample Lists

To help understand what a competitive list pair looks like for Brinebloods, I reached out to Alex Kosma from the Musing Panda podcast. Alex is a regular on the World Team Championship circuit and a thought leader in Brinebloods list building. Here's his current pair:

This list is set up well in other strong scenario jam armies, as well as armies like Tyrus that rely on a high saturation of threats. This is largely thanks to Precision Strike allowing your models to move through one another and operate from the rear of your force forward to clear models that have bogged yours down in melee. Rough Seas and Greygore's Shout it Out also makes the list good into generalized shooting. Lastly, Ol' Scuttlebutt is an all-star with bonus movement from Perdition and Avenging Force able to project him exactly where it is least convenient for the opponent.

Alex describes this list as "wildly exotic" but a great generalist list. Going fast is always strong! He says this force is good into most thing but especially Old Umbrey and Grymkin. It has hugely outsized threat ranges and is pretty resilient to shooting thanks to its high ARM centerpieces and Flying High on the Battle Brigs. The Warchief's Iron Flesh spell combined with Unyielding from the Abyssal King animus makes the big guy an impressive ARM 24 against melee damage backed up by a 16" melee threat between your leader's feat and Ol' Scuttlebutt's beacon. This army also demonstrates the efficacy of Brineblood buff stacking, with Paralyzing Gaze, Mark Target, and Shooting Gallery putting your Brigs to a functional RAT 11 (or 13 against something Scuttlebutt hits with Vice Grip)!

Final Thoughts

The Brinebloods are an army with a deep stable of tournament-viable leaders and a solid, alpha strike game plan. While they suffer from poor defensive stats, they make up for it by getting there first and keeping the opponent on their back foot while the rest of your army scores scenario objectives. Whether you want to shoot or get in close, you can't go wrong.
