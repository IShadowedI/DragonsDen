Tabletop Battles Reviews: Commissar Ciaphas Cain, Hero of the Imperium (and Jurgen)

July 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
After a hefty delay thanks to unexpected warp turbulence (the best efforts of the postal service), Rich and Blake have got the new Ciaphas Cain miniature in hand. Our thanks to Games Workshop for providing advanced preview copies of this kit.

What's In The Box

Why it's Ciaphas Cain, the Hero of the Imperium! This limited-release set contains plastic miniatures for Cain and his long-suffering sidekick, Ferik Jurgen, mounted on 25mm bases or, optionally, a 40mm diorama base (more on this later).

Ciaphas Cain & Jurgen. Credit: Games Workshop/Warhammer Community

It feels like almost every new Warhammer kit these days is a candidate for "the sharpest detail I've ever seen" and this is true again here. Neither of these are complex models in terms of posing or equipment, but the faces in particular are incredibly finely detailed, to the point that I imagine hobbyists will need to be careful with their primer in order to avoid erasing some of it. Those wizards at the Citadel Miniatures factory keep on working their magic.

Ciaphas Cain sprue/box contents. Credit: Rich Nutter

Of note to fans of the Ciaphas Cain novels, I think it's fair to say that this depiction of the illustrious Commissar is slightly more down to earth than some of the more intentionally propaganda-styled pieces of Black Library artwork. There are no torn uniforms stretched over impossibly large pectorals here, with Cain instead depicted as solid, rugged, arguably handsome from certain angles, but most definitely the size and shape of a normal man.

Assembly

Ciaphas Cain & Jurgen on individual bases. Credit: Rich Nutter

Both minis go together really nicely, and on Cain especially most of the joins are thoughtfully covered by later pieces. The initial joining of the back of his greatcoat is a little fiddly, as the back piece only really slots into the arms one way, but it's secure and solid once it's there. Cain's head and chainsword could easily be kept separate for ease of painting if desired, as could Jurgen's head and meltagun, but in both cases the poses are really open enough to build them fully without worrying, if that's your preference.

Ciaphas Cain & Jurgen on supplied display base. Credit: Rich Nutter

Back to that 40mm display base. This is a really neat addition, allowing collectors who are not worried about putting these minis to work in their armies an opportunity to instead assemble them on a small scenic layout for show. It's a nice idea, and the base is well made, but I do have one real issue with it, which is that Jurgen is point almost fully backwards on it. It's fair to say that in context this is probably valid, the trusty sidekick covering the hero as he strikes a dashing pose. Indeed, with the opportunity to rotate the model, the setup works quite well, but the reality of it is that sat static in a display case the viewer is only ever going to see Jurgen's back, and while there is some lovely detail on his pack it's a bit of a strange choice.

Ciaphas Cain & Jurgen, alternative base arrangement. Credit: Rich Nutter

As an alternative, I tried setting up the models on a flat 40mm base using Cain's hero rock from the 25mm base option, and in my opinion this is a much better option for display. It's still possible to have Jurgen set back and offset, clearly second fiddle to the Cain, whilst being able to see more of both minis. I'll be moving forward with this option and mounting the models to a plinth for display.

Painting

Given the post office's reluctance to deliver there hasn't been much time to put paint on Cain and Jurgen yet, but keep your eyes peeled in the next few weeks for a How To Paint Everything article covering how to apply pigment to your favourite legend of the Imperium. Blake has made a start, though!

Blake: My plan for this paintjob was to make the cloth on both Cain and Jurgen a ruddy brown, then work in some varying shades of green to give a camo look to it.  I got the model set up to prime, put the base coat on, then packed it up for traveling for the week (where I planned to finish the brush work).  The model did not make the pack slip between point A and B and he was left on my work desk for a week without me.  Needless to say, I have a base coat to show off today!  It is a scheme I have been working to develop for models I want mostly ruddy brown, so I definitely want to take time to talk about what I am working on.

I started this scheme with Citadel Chaos Black primer.  This is my go to for almost every scheme I do, as it gives very consistent priming and doesn't worry about overspray too much.  My first layer was Army Painter Fanatic Oak Brown.  This was applied with airbrush, doing 10 percent sprays, angling mostly from the bottom of the model.  Anywhere I am going to have a shadow, I am focusing this color.  I put the model at a 45 degree angle, held upside down, and applied this color lightly that way too.  This gives an even gradient and takes the black up a bit to accept the next color.  My second layer was Army Painter Fanatic Fur Brown.  For this layer, I positioned my model at 45 degrees above the model.  I applied the color evenly across the entire model at this angle.  This allow anything angled below or hidden by gear to remain mostly Oak Brown.  The next layer was what I consider the highlight on this paint job.  I took AK Interactive Cork and applied it from the light source.  In my case, this was over the left shoulder.  I try to put this on in light sprays a little at a time.  Anywhere light would be bright, I applied a small amount; the main focus was over the actual left shoulder and left side of head.  I did some experimenting on this one, and added some inks in.  For a shadow, I put in AK Interactive Mahogani Ink into the right side and underside of the entire model.  I then took AK Interactive Night Purple Ink and pushed the shadow a bit more in the most extreme recesses.  My final touch was to add AK Interactive Intense Sienna Ink over the most extreme highlights.  I did this very lightly.  The ink is translucent, so it acts more like a glaze.  If you go too heavy here, it will cover up what you already did.

Wrap Up

A hard plastic Ciaphas Cain has been a long time coming, and (basing decisions aside) this version doesn't disappoint. He's oozing character, and his sidekick Jurgen looks suitably beleaguered. It's worth noting that Cain is currently a "while stocks last" limited release, so do be sure to get hold of him sooner rather than later if you want to honour the Hero of the Imperium by adding him to your force.
