Tabletop Battles Reviews: Warhammer 40K Boltgun 2

October 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Three years ago, Auroch Digital dropped Boltgun, and all was good. Then last week Boltgun 2 was dropped, and all was even better. I purposefully hadn’t followed development for this game outside of what I saw from Warhammer Skulls. I prefer to go into things totally blind for my own sake, but deep down I knew I was going to enjoy this game no matter what.

I’m a sucker for Boomer Shooters, a slave to the genre really, the crazier the better, and Boltgun slots in on the tamer end of the spectrum where games like ULTRAKILL sit on the far other end.

Boltgun 2 is a lot more of the same formulae, and I mean that in a mostly positive way, except that it’s bigger, more refined, and just has a lot more stuff in it. It’s the same fun, you’re sprinting, jumping, charging, shooting, bashing, chopping through really cool arenas while the music team use your weapon noises as background percussion for their screeching guitar riffs.

Where the first game was jumping, chainsword charging and strafing, Boltgun 2 is all about more frantic, twitchy, high-speed carnage where you’re slide cancelling into a slo-mo superjump that lets you empty a Storm Bolter into some bozo in power armour and end his 10,000 year survival streak.

Departing from the first game, Boltgun gives you two characters to play with. I always thought they’d add more characters to Boltgun 1, it seemed set up for it, but it never happened. I don’t know if that was a scrapped plan or not, but they’ve made up for it here. Boltgun 2 pitches two badass heroes of the Imperium for you to play, the returning Sternguard Veteran Malum Caedo, (still voiced by Rahul Kohlui) and the Sister Celestian Nyra Veyrath. (Voiced by Alanah Pearce, who you'd recognise as Malevola from Dispatch!)

Promotional image courtesy of Auroch Digital.

You have different loadout options available to each character, with some crossovers, a different ability each that does the same thing (charging into enemies and making them explode), and a different health/armour pool. The Sister of Battle, Nyra, is generally faster and can jump higher, while Malum can tank more damage overall and has access to way quicker horizontal movement with his charge.

I don’t think there’s so much of a difference between the two that you feel like you’re missing out if you only play once, your mileage will really depend on how cool you find the weapons. But I really don’t think you’re going to play just once though.

The campaign for both characters is the same, but there’s a branching path system so there is a chance to do different levels as either character with the same convergence points through it all. It’s a nice way to make a playthrough of each character feel different, and there’s no weak points along the way either. All the levels kick some serious ass, and it makes hunting down Vashtorr as much fun the second time as it was the first.

The improvements they have made to the gameplay from the first game are small but impactful, like improving the controls to include a weapon wheel instead of relying on hotkeys, and your movement options being expanded to the point that Malum Caedo clearly missed his calling by choosing to be a Sternguard instead of a Vanguard Veteran. (seriously, he can leap across the map with his chainsword. and that's without a jump pack.)

If you’re speedrunning inclined, there is absolutely some heinous tech you can do to start beating levels in stupid quick time. Your level of freedom in leaping around, especially with the enhanced mobility in this game, means you’ll have plenty of fun trying to find skips. This isn’t im-sim levels of shenanigans by any means, it’s still a linear game, but I feel they found various ways players could skip parts of the level in development and just shrugged their shoulders at it.

In addition to Josh's review, Rob and Norman have been playing Boltgun 2 over on the Tabletop Battles YouTube channel:

Not that you want to skip past enemies because the new weapons added to the game are sick. Flamers in Boomer Shooters tend to be underwhelming, but Nyra's version of the flamer acts less like a traditional flamer and more like a pressure-washer of death. It was the most fun weapon to use in the game by far. The Meltagun as well has been revamped from a glorified super shotgun into my new favourite representation of a meltagun to date. It’s a solid beam that deals heavy pulses of damage that totally slag anything in front of you, and it only gets better when you get the multi-melta.

All the art they’ve made for the game is gorgeous, and there’s so much more of it this time around. Boltgun 1 had a limited enemy roster of Tzeentch and Nurgle Daemons, cultists, and Chaos Space Marines & Terminators. Boltgun 2 has the same, and then sprinkles on every Cult Legion for flavour.

You’ll be fighting Berserkers, Scarab Terminators, Noise Marines, Blightlord Terminators, and daemons you didn’t see in the previous game too, like Daemonettes and Bloodcrushers. It’s a lot. You won’t be doing it alone either, as a regiment from the Death Korp of Krieg pops up sometimes to help you out (by soaking up boltrounds, mainly).

Boltgun 2 has a much larger scope to its campaign. Boltgun 1 was set on Graia, a single planet, and there were a lot of fun environments to it but there had to be a unifying motif between levels. Boltgun 2 has you visiting multiple planets, different areas on those planets, voidships both Imperial and Chaos, and it let the level designers run absolutely wild.

There are some ridiculously cool environments in Boltgun 2, and it stands out as one of the things I loved most about the game. You need two playthroughs to appreciate them all, as there’s a branching mission path to accommodate two playthroughs with two different characters, but it’s so worth it. There’s some absolutely badass stand outs, like a Khornate Ziggurat with gore pits, industrial blood pumps, and so many piles of skulls that sit nicely against more traditional Imperium grimdark industrial, like an underwater lab facility or an underhive.

I don’t want to seem like all the new sprite work for enemies and allies didn’t excite me, it did, but the level design was just so fantastic I had to dedicate a big chunk of this review to call it out. Explore those levels. It’s worth it. (And they’re littered with secrets.)

Despite how cool it was to see all the legions in the game, I found the variety worked against the game. Boltgun 2 is a brisk experience; I got it done twice in about 12 hours on Hard mode. There’s a good chance you’ll only encounter a type of enemy for two campaign missions at most, and that sucks.

I think the game could really have benefited from some kind of Codex that stored enemies you encountered so you could read about their mechanics and check out their sprites up close. It’s a small gripe because it feels like a ‘my lobster too buttery, my steak too juicy’ type of complaint but I just liked some enemies more than others, and wanted to keep fighting them more.

This game is well worth a buy if you’re a fan of this genre, and a great first try if you’re Boomer Shooter curious. It’s a short, sweet, and gentle introduction to the craziness of this type of game. Obviously if you’re a Warhammer fan this game will speak to you on a lot of levels; the art, the story, the vibes are all immaculately Warhammer but if you’re just someone who wants a shooter, it’s totally sick. Is it a great intro to the setting? Not particularly, it doesn’t explain much, but it doesn’t need to.

I think it’s a shame it took three years to come out but hopefully they open a pipeline to get more content into the game, some DLCs, or some workshop support would be great. Games like this can live forever with a level editor, just look at what people are still doing with GZDoom.

For the price tag, I’d find it hard to regret picking it up. If you’re into replaying games, there’s a lot to do between trying harder difficulties, 100% secrets on both characters, and speedruning especially I think can be a massive draw for this game in particular. There are some great tools available to do skips and I’m excited to see people find them out.

If you’re someone who is one and done, I think you’ll be satisfied too. Getting to that final boss fight and hearing Mick Gordon make a guitar growl and snarl at you before you unleash all the weapons in your arsenal is more than satisfying one time over.
