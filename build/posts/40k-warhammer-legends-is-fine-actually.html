[40k] Warhammer Legends Is Fine, Actually

September 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer

You’ve probably seen posts like these. A lot of posts like these. You might even have made posts like these. Attached to these you may have also heard the term Legends used like a four letter word, and not one of the fun four letter words, but like, the really bad ones. A lot of this vitriol comes from a fundamental misunderstanding of what Legends is and why units get Legends rules. My goal here today is to clear up those misunderstandings and maybe, just maybe, tone down the salt levels a little bit.

The Video Version

If you'd rather listen to my dulcet tones and watch some cool models spin around, check out the video version of this piece instead over on our YouTube channel:

What Is Warhammer Legends?

Let’s just define what Legends is and isn’t before we dig in too much. While Legends sort of existed for old Warhammer Fantasy armies after the transition to Age of Sigmar, Legends as we know it was introduced back in 2019 for 40k, in a post on Warhammer Community:

 “As codexes are revised and editions (and eons) pass, classic models become unavailable, no longer finding their way into current publications. Rather than consign these battleworn relics to oblivion at the back of your shelves, however, we are happy to present them as Warhammer Legends.”

“...these profiles will not be curated as part of the annual points review, and as such aren’t really intended for use in competitive play (unless, you know, you want to organise an event where Legends are welcome).”

So that seems pretty cut and dry, right? They’re rules for old models that GW doesn’t make anymore, they won’t get regular points reviews, and they aren’t meant for competitive play unless you want your event to include them. That post is archived and seven years old at this point, but the goal still remains the same: Providing points and rules for units that GW isn’t supporting anymore. That is not the same as removing a unit from the game or adding it to a ban list, just keeping it out of tournament play specifically.

Now there’s a second, newer thing introduced in a recent Warcom post called Legendary Proxies. That’s basically just taking old models and running them as a modern equivalent, with some pretty similar stipulations: 

“As with Legends rules, Legendary Proxies can be used at home, in stores, clubs, and campaigns, but if attending a competitive Warhammer event, these units should only be used with the permission of the event organiser.”

I’ve got a little exercise for you. Now I’m going to need you to look into your mind palace and picture what I’m about to tell you. Picture a squad of Space Marines. Each of them is holding a massive bolt pistol in one hand and a chainsword in the other. On their backs are huge jump packs so they can leap into combat. Were you picturing these?

Black Templars Assault Marines. Credit: SRM

Or were you picturing these?

Rainbow Warriors Jump Pack Intercessors by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Either way, you’re right. Some people get mad about these but really, how angry can you really be that your rules for a Marine with a boltgun are now the rules for a Marine with a boltgun? The names have changed and maybe some equipment is different, but that’s the only material difference here, and you’re not being asked to do anything else. I’ll even let you keep calling them Tactical Marines if you’d like. I still say Imperial Guard and Eldar; it’s fine, nobody cares. 

Why Do Units Go to Legends?

The first question most people ask when a unit goes to Legends is “why”. Sometimes these are newer models, like the Hammerfall Bunker, or models that started as Legends units to begin with, like most tie-in models from Black Library books. There’s a few reasons, mostly boiling down to kinda boring business reasons and game design considerations.

The simple, mundane reason units go to Legends is because they just stop making the kit. Molds for plastic kits wear out and are expensive to replace, and unless it’s a kit that will obviously sell enough to justify making another mold, like the ubiquitous Rhino, then it might go to Legends. Sometimes it’ll get a new kit instead, like that new, slightly different Intercessor kit coming out, but this is one of the main reasons they stop supporting a unit with new rules - they just aren’t selling it anymore. 

Another consideration is game design and balance. There are hundreds of Datasheets, with more being added all the time. Considering how often the game gets balance updates and new Codexes, that’s a lot of datasheets to maintain. If you have a boring database job you probably understand how tedious maintaining that many assets is, and how that scope can make for a snowballing amount of work every time they want to update something. You might say they shouldn’t update the game as often as they do, and you’re allowed to think that, I just remember people who had to wait 12 years for new a Dark Eldar codex.

Boomdakka Snazzwagon. Credit: Rockfish

The last is redundancy. Sometimes units get straight up replaced with something that does more or less the same job, or a unit gets an updated model with different equipment options. In the new Ork Codex, a bunch of the vehicles from Speed Freeks got condensed into a generic warbuggies sheet, but can you really tell me the material difference between a Kustom Boosta Blasta and a Boomdakka Snazzwagon? Would you even know the difference if they were on the table next to each other? They both go fast and shoot stuff, it’s fine. Games Workshop is always going to put out new models - it’s what they’ve been doing for over 40 years - and that’s going to mean some stuff gets pushed to the side to make way for the new stuff. 

Why Does Legends Make People Big Mad?

From the way you hear people talking about it, you’d think Legends was a program where Games Workshop thugs personally came to your house and smashed your old models with hammers. While Legends isn’t as drastic as deleting something from the game forever, it does mean those units aren’t usable in tournaments anymore. I’ve got a bombshell for you though, so make sure you’re sitting down: Not being allowed for tournament play probably doesn’t affect you. Before you scroll to the bottom of the article and fill that comment section with descriptions of specific objects and where I should shove them, let me go over some data. Now there isn’t perfect reporting on what the whole community is like, but we have run reader surveys here, and respondents are going to be by their nature pretty engaged members of the wargaming community. After all, these are going to be the kind of people who keep up with our daily coverage of the wide world of wargaming.

In those surveys, we’ve found that more than half of our readers play less than one game a month, and less than a third of our readers had been to a tournament that year. Even then, most of those who do go to events only go to maybe one or two a year. This isn't meant as any sort of insult to people who don't go to tournaments or play more often, but more of a reality check. If Legends just prohibits units from being used in tournaments, this doesn’t affect those people at all. Statistics say: this probably shouldn’t matter to you either.

Now if this does affect you because you’re a heavy tournament player: Hey, me too. I’m one of our resident competitive sickos, and most of my games are either at tournaments or prepping for tournaments. I’m the exact kind of person this affects the most, but also, as a tournament player, I’m used to changing up my list to stay on top of the meta already. If this describes you too, my heart goes out to you - when the main format you play doesn’t allow you to use your models, that sucks. There’s no sugarcoating that; it’s just another meta you need to keep up with.

Oldhammer Meets 8th Edition. Credit: SRM and Dylan Gould

Some people also are going to be upset that they can’t buy those old units anymore, and I don’t have much good news for them either. The secondary market has only gotten more expensive, and as someone who used to collect a lot of old out of production stuff, I know that game. Some people have armies with a specific look that relies on a certain kit that is on its way out, and yeah, that’s gonna be tough. Models go out of production every so often, no matter the game or even the manufacturer.

The reason I imagine most people get upset at a unit being sent to Legends is because they feel it devalues the work they put into their own units. You put all this time and effort into a Tactical Squad and their Razorback, of course you still want rules to play with them. It’s also something that’s unusual in the miniatures gaming space. While trading card games like Magic the Gathering or Pokémon regularly cycle out old sets, you’re not spending 20 hours converting and painting your trading cards, and it's just part of the game that most people understand. Miniatures games have largely been exempt from that sort of churn. Warmachine did something similar, but outside of that, people are pretty used to using their old models all they like. After all, at no point did Napoleon introduce a Primaris brigade and send his Voltiguers to Legends, and I can still use the BattleTech minis my dad painted in the 80s, even if handling them is going to give me lead poisoning. 

Watching Out for Legends

The minute Primaris Marines were introduced back in 2017, I knew that the days of short king Marines were numbered. You don’t start releasing parallel, more advanced versions of existing units without a long term plan to phase out the old ones. Even though Vindicators were pretty hot in tournament play for a while, I wasn’t painting any because I had the inkling that since they were a firstborn Marine kit from 2008, they were gonna get sent to Legends before too long, and since I play in a lot of tournaments, I didn’t want to feel like my efforts would be wasted. I’m not sharing these anecdotes to toot my own horn or because I want an award for being the smartest boy - I’ve been barking up that tree for years and my therapist still hasn’t given me one - but because I want to educate folks who maybe don’t recognize these patterns to think on them a little bit. If Legends is something you really, genuinely want to avoid, having some understanding of what gets models sent there might prevent some heartache down the road. 

Kitbashed Black Templars Marshal. Credit: SRM

The first one you’ll want to watch out for is models that require any sort of kitbashing to come together. Games Workshop isn’t in the business of making rules for models they don’t produce, unlike the old days where you might get a codex page with a bunch of wargear you had to convert up or kitbash. That doesn’t mean you shouldn’t kitbash your own models, it’s a ton of fun, but if something doesn’t have a model or you’re banking on some gear that doesn’t come in the stock kit, it’s something you should probably avoid.

The next is pretty obvious but requires some research, and that’s old models. I’m genuinely shocked that the Land Raider didn’t get sent to Legends since that kit is 26 years old. Most of the kits that get relegated to Legends are ones that are older, usually because after a certain point GW stops making them, like the more obscure old tanks from Forgeworld. Based on how recent Necromunda and Specialist Games releases have been, I’d also probably avoid anything resin, since it seems like they’re really trying to pivot completely to plastic. Again, great looking models, but unless you have an easy proxy or genuinely don’t care about Legends, I’d probably avoid picking up a new $500 resin child.

Ciaphas Cain & Jurgen. Credit: Games Workshop/Warhammer Community

Another common factor for Legends units is when they’re explicitly based on Black Library books. Ciaphas Cain and Ferren Areios started as Legends models, while others like Ufthak Blackhawk had rules usable in tournaments before ending up in Legends too. This has been a bit more recent of a change, but I think the best way to think of these is as collector’s models you get just because they’re neat, and maybe you can use them as something else. Pick up Hell’s Last because you want a cool command squad or you like the Minka Lesk books, not because you’re expecting them to have explicit rules for tournament play.

What to Do with Your Legends Units

Credit: Kevin Stillman

Uriel Ventris got sent to Legends? Oh no! Now how am I going to use the man with the most chopped haircut on Macragge? If only there was some way you could take a Space Marine Captain with a power sword and a boltgun! The point here is that ol’ squaretoes here has an obvious analogue, and so do a lot of other Legends units, usually because they’re older or parallel versions of something else already there. Other times they might be characters with wargear that isn’t supported by the new kits, in which case, there’s no rules requiring you to model your exact wargear, so your old metal Autarch can just be armed with whatever you want. It ain’t perfect but it’ll let you keep using your Finecast blorbo for years to come.

Legendary Proxies are also pretty easy. Honestly, Suppressors becoming proxies for Inceptors is the best thing to happen to them in years. The game designers already did the work for you, so keep on using the units you like if they’ve got a ready proxy. And if not, materially, does it really matter that you have to use the Cadian Guardsmen rules for your Mordians or Valhallans? It’s a guy in flak armor with a lasgun, if Cadia can stand, so can Vostroya. 

Rainbow Warriors Chaplain on Bike by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Another thing you might have noticed is that a lot of old Legends units tend to come back. The biker Chaplain peeled out with that initial Legends announcement 2019 and came back in 2020 with a bigger bike. Rough Riders didn't take too much longer. Huge chunks of the Stormcast and Skaven ranges for Age of Sigmar went to Legends in April 2024, only to come right back in June when their ranges got updated. If one of your units goes to Legends and you can’t or don’t want to play with Legends units, you can just hold onto them and wait until later when they probably return. Sometimes that might mean rebasing them to match whatever the newer version of their model is. As far as hobby projects go that’s not a particularly fun one, but it’s not exactly a hard one either. If an old vehicle gets a new version, you can also make a base that matches the new footprint too.

Ultramarines vs. Emperor's Children at the Tacoma Open 2026. Credit: SRM

See that 2nd edition Rhino over there on the right side of the board? It’s on a base the same size as a modern Rhino, and I played against it at the Tacoma Open, a Games Workshop-operated Grand Tournament. Nobody had any problems, and everyone was cool with it. If you ever have questions about that sort of thing, you can follow the advice in the original posts that Games Workshop made and just contact the event organizers. Whether that’s a GW event or an independent one, they’re hobbyists just like you and are generally gonna be pretty permissive.

The last thing to do with Legends units is: Use them! Just use them! They’re legal outside of tournaments, and unless you’re playing in a tournament, bust out your weird old models and go to town. If you’re not sure, ask your opponent beforehand, and they’ll probably say yes. Most Legends units aren’t even that good on account of not being regularly balanced, so it’s not like you’re trying to powergame here, and if you’re not in a tournament you probably don’t care about running the most optimal units anyway. Maybe that’s the invitation your opponent needs to break out their own old Forgeworld model that hasn’t seen the table in the last decade. If you’re so inclined or you’re a narrative player, you can even homebrew your own rules or adapt an old datasheet to the new edition, just again, run that by your opponent. Narrative events also usually allow Legends. Games Workshop’s own Grand Narratives and the Narratives at the Warhammer Open events all allow Legends, no questions asked. In case you’re worried that we don’t put our money where our mouths are either, when we run Narratives at the Tabletop Battles Open, we allow Legends units there too. Outside of tournaments, the only person stopping you from using these models is you. Take your little lead pals out of their cases, break out those obscure tie-in models, and go play some Warhammer.
