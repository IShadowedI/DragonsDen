Mayday, Miss Marcy! The Marine isn't Always Greener

October 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back everyone to yet another edition of Mayday, Miss Marcy! As Fall sets in and October gets underway, perhaps your temperatures are falling and the season is getting into full swing, or perhaps you're feeling much the same as usual (I know I am!), but whatever the season or temperature, there will always be questions. And as long as I'm around to answer them, there will always be Mayday, Miss Marcy! Last week, we tackled some pretty heavy questions, and I appreciate both the senders and you all, dear readers, who gave me some great feedback regarding how the questions were handled and answered. Hopefully all of those people are doing better, and if I get any updates, I'll be happy to let you all know if they wish. This week, I can promise a bit more brevity in our weekly advice column, and if you'd like YOUR question to end up here, please read the blurb below!

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

This week, we have some questions regarding paints and painting, the timeless struggle all miniature painters seem to face at one point or another.

FPOMO

Dear Miss Marcy,

The new GW paints look super good but I have spent a lot of money on paints already. I have a pretty full and stable supply of GW paints and even a good batch of contrasts. I've also tried a few other brands, but the results people have shown off for the various painting demos, yours included, make me feel like these are pretty amazing and that I shouldn't skip them. But at some point, I can only buy, use, and store so much paint, so... what the heck do I even do?

Painted into a Corner

Dear Painted,

I think you're feeling something a lot of people can relate to, as well as something many have actually felt; frankly, even I've been tempted to try the new paints out because they look very cool, and I really don't do a lot of painting anymore. The thing is that this is sort of a version of FOMO, or the fear of missing out, because it is a new, shiny (well, not literally, the paints are kind of matte) object and being touted as the Next Great Thing in Painting. Like many painting inventions, there will still be a use for what you already have, and in most cases, you should really match your expectations against what you already do and what you need. Rob even noted in his working with them that his current army is already a specific shade, which would be hard to match with the new paints, and that's probably the primary thing you should consider.

Also, your older paints are still going to have use; aside from any schemes you already have, they're great for highlighting, detail, or other aspects, and there are some colors that are just not going to be available in the new paints that the older ones have. Contrast paints also have a similar sort of use and home, but the main thing you really need to ask yourself is do you actually NEED the new paints. And I don't mean that philosophically, I mean do you have something you can use them for? Are you suddenly starting a new army, do you have a new project and new paints would mix well with it as a motivator to get it finished? Because otherwise, you're going to do what a lot of hobbyists do and hate to hear: you're going to waste money on something you won't use and have it sitting in your house.

I don't fault anyone for being excited for a new thing, but the issue always tends to be the idea that THESE new paints, whatever they are, will solve all your painting problems, and they won't. The painting problem that most people actually have is time, dedication, and satisfaction with the end result. If you already don't have the time to paint how you like, a new spread of paints is not going to change or alter that.

Green for an Amazing Reason

Dear Ms Marcy,

Recently I put a great deal of time and energy into a small army of marines malevolent. The color scheme for these guys is black torsos and backpacks, with the rest being the ever-infamous-to-paint yellow. My method for the yellow was a white primer, yellow basecoat, standard dark wash and an orange underbrush, with the hope being that they would turn out a sort of orangish-yellow. I thought they turned out pretty good while I was painting. However, when I finally debuted the fruits of my labor, I was met with one consistent question: "why are they green?". And having compared them to the color yellow and and the color green... yeah, they look pretty green, and they definitely do not look even remotely orange. I attached a photo where they look pretty decent, but even then I think the 𝔠𝔲𝔯𝔰𝔢 𝔬𝔣 𝔤𝔯𝔢𝔢𝔫 is evident.

Anyways, having done more research into the esoteric world of painting yellow, I now know that I'm supposed to do an elaborate process involving four shades of paint and a blood sacrifice if I want them to look good, all of which starts with the priming. However, having already finished these models, including a not insignificant amount of freehanding that I do NOT want to redo, how do I "save" them without completely stripping them and restarting from scratch? Is there some kind of esoteric highlighting or shading method that can turn greenish yellow into orangish yellow? Or am I forever cursed to be 𝔤𝔯𝔢𝔢𝔫?

Please help,

Green for an Amazing Reason

Included here, said "Yellow" Marines:

Dear Green,

No, your eyes are not deceiving you, those are absolutely green. I think I have a few questions, and you're welcome to answer me in e-mail (and I can always post the responses for any curious readers), but: when you say WHITE primer, did you actually use a WHITE primer, or did you use something like Grey Seer? Because the funny thing about some White primers is that they are actually BLUE or BLUE GREY. And you know what happens when you mix BLUE and YELLOW?

Yeah.

The reason I'm curious about this is because your Green marines are not, well, entirely green either. You can still see specs of and patches of yellow in the paint; for example, the marine on the far right has a mostly yellow shoulder pauldron that almost looks like you did a yellow highlight on a green paint job, and you can sort of see it on other various raised edge highlight style areas, such as the center marine's wrist, and on his leg. This makes me think that your "white" Primer was not exactly, or entirely, white. The other culprit, since we can't see the stages of Primer > Base > Wash > Underbrush, is what the various stages looked like and at what point they started becoming green. If the white primer was indeed 100% white, then it could have been the wash you used creating a similar effect. I see a spray can in the back that I can't recognize, which depending on brand and make, could also be contributing to it; paint brands and mixes are going to look and cause different reactions.

I know this was not your question, but your painting also looks very thick; I think you might have to own up to the fact that stripping your models would be one of the better options overall, so you could go for a smoother coat and then also get the color you want. As far as yellow is concerned, it is hard; even Rob mentioned back in 2021 that his mix ended up "Greener" than he wanted, much like yours. If you really don't want to strip them, you're going to need to paint over them with something that will over-write the green/yellow, and in a lot of cases, that might have to be a color darker on the spectrum than yellow. There's a pretty well known trick about painting a miniature pink an then yellow on top of it, but I think you'd need to consider stripping it for that to work out.

If any other painters have opinions, sound off in the comments!

Gold with Envy

Dear Marcy,

My friends and I often talk about painting models, sometimes while painting them, or playing. It probably comes off as one would expect as part of the hobby, but recently I created a bit of a scruff in my group: I said I didn't really care for a Golden Demon painted model that the rest of them were harping on about. The thing is that I tend to find those models... impractical? I suppose I can say I respect them at a technical level but they don't appeal to me because I can't see any reason I'd ever want to try and paint like that myself. I'm not going to say I'm a good painter, but I get my models all painted up and table ready and they look pretty good, and that's what matters to me. My group thinks I'm just jealous that I can't win a painting competition, which I felt a little insulted by. Was I wrong?

Demonized by Opinion

Dear Demonized,

Okay, so I'm going to first say that I think I'm only getting YOUR side of things here. This sounds like a "Mom they were mean to me" without including all of the context, and you're going to have to accept that is how it seems to me while reading. You are presenting a pretty reasonable argument here in your question, but my gut tells me you did NOT present such a reasoned response or opinion at the time, which may have been why people were annoyed. Also, let's assume it did unfold as you said, it would be kind of rude to insult your models in response to your opinion, so it does make me think that there is perhaps a little missing here.

That being said, I do not think you are "wrong", because, well, it is your opinion. Opinions really can't be "wrong", but they can be uninformed, and it also depends on the topic we're talking about. In this case, you can't have a "wrong" opinion because you are talking about personal taste: You do not like Golden Demon style painting. That is fine, but your friends opinions are ALSO valid, because their taste is that they like, or admire, that style of painting. You could say that you do not find it appealing because you don't feel like you're able to learn and utilize it in your own models, but I do think it sounds a little strange to say that Golden Demon winners can't teach you ANYTHING painting wise. I'm not agreeing with them that you're sour grapes-ing, but perhaps my answer here is that you should consider the way you word your responses and the feelings of the people around you; if they're all sharing something they like, it can be pretty off-putting to volunteer that YOU hate that thing, specifically, especially when it isn't really something that harms anyone; if you want, consider the conversation in another medium; if they all were talking about a particular food, and you offered "well, *I* HATE that food," does the conversation really have anywhere to go?
