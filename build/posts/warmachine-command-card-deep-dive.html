Warmachine: Command Card Deep Dive

June 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello, and welcome to another exciting episode of… wait… shoot, wrong script.

With the Warmachine mid-year update, we’ve seen a lot of exciting changes to many different armies and leaders. I, for one, think the more important updates come in the form of the revised command cards, and how Defenses will work. Like it or hate it, much like the removal of the rack, command cards were bound to change.

Some things have stayed the same post-update, at least. Faction-specific command cards remain unchanged (Breathe easy Gravedigger and Grymkin players) and Steamforged Games have said they’re open to giving armies more specific command cards. The way you play command cards is also unchanged; unless you're playing Grymkin, you are limited to two command cards a turn, and cannot play more than one on the same model/unit each turn.

Credit: Steamforged Games

For the rest of us we are in a brave new world of choices. The bad here is simple. Some of the Command cards have gone away, and others have doubled up, meaning you can no longer use both effects in one game. Gone are the days of having Lucky penny and Blessing of the Gods and being able to use both.

However the good far outweighs the bad. For one, armies that didn't have access to things like Careful Reconnaissance (Shadowflame shard) or Duck and Cover (Old Umbrey) now have access to both. This opens up some exciting possibilities for those armies in list construction, like making options like Magnus and Invictus even better, or to keep your squishier ambush units like Liegemen Hunters alive the turn they show up. The changes also take a level of potential error out of the equation, you can't take the ‘wrong’ card anymore. A nice little benefit in terms of list building, means you're not punished for taking a more niche option that just didn't come up as often. If you ever added sapper to a list, and none of your opponents brought defenses, you know the feeling. While you can’t use both halves of the cards in one game, you always have access to the half you need. Finally, another little benefit is you always know what tools you have, and the tools your opponent has access to is (largely) the same as you.

Let's go through the specific cards and see what the deal is with each one.

Bite and Hold

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

A new addition to the Command card line up, Bite and Hold is really the only truly “new” card we get this time around. It can be played anytime during a model/units activation to give one of the following effects.

  • The scenario element that model/unit is currently securing remains secured until the end of the turn unless it is contested by an enemy model.
  • For one round while that model/unit is within area terrain or 3” of another scenario element it gains Sturdy (Cannot be pushed).

This effectively gives a few options to you as the player. If you use the first effect, whatever a model/unit is securing (scoring) will remain in your control until the end of the turn. This forces your opponent to have to contest an element, whether that be a piece of scenario terrain or a 40/50 mm objective, or give you the points for it. This is going to be great on scenarios like Trench Warfare, where your opponent doesn’t gain anything for securing their scenario terrain, but you can grab a cool 2 points if they don’t send something over to contest it, rather than just destroy the models and not have to give up board space. A fun little ability for sure, but the limitations are simple. This doesn't help you to contest. If you move a Warjack or Warbeast near a 40mm objective, this doesn’t let you keep contesting, and you only score if the model/unit could score the objective.

Credit: Steamforged Games

The other option for this one allows you to make a model/unit sturdy. This will help a model/unit really stick on an objective, and gives some fun play into Scyrafel (Dusk House Kallyss caster) or Lylyth's (Shadowflame Shard caster) feat turn, which would normally push models away. The downside here is because Sturdy only stops Pushes, things like Slams and Throws will still get around it and render it effectively useless. Keep an eye out for rules or critical abilities like Momentum, Smite, Grab and Smash, Pitch, Influence, Befuddle or place effects like  Winged Nuisance, Banish, or Dark Banishment, because Bite and Hold will not stop them. I expect you will see this one far more for its first mode over its second, but the times that the second effect comes in handy will be great to have.

Blessing of the Gods

Credit: Steamforged Games

An oldie but a goody, Blessing of the Gods stuck around and merged with Lucky Penny, but comes with a caveat it didn’t have before. New Blessing of the Gods now has to be played at the start of a model's activation, which puts it in line with how Lucky Penny worked, but is a change for Blessing of the Gods. This new version of Blessing of the Gods can be used at the start of a model’s activation for one of two effects.

  • That models weapons to gain Blessed as well as damage type: Magical this activation.
  • That model can gain one essence, focus, or fury point, or one corpse, rage or soul token.

The big change here is with the first effect having to be played at the start of the activation. This means models with random Rate of Fire can no longer roll to see how many shots they get before deciding if they want to use this ability. Additionally, if you forget to use it before making a move or charge, you’re out of luck. This one is going to be really easy to get wrong, so be careful and plan out your turn and what you need before committing, otherwise you may find yourself in a tough spot.

The second half of the card is as good as it ever has been. Giving a model that wants a Corpse or Soul token the ability to have one whenever you want is great, or even just giving your leader an extra Focus/Fury/Essence to spend, can sometimes be make or break for getting scenario cleared to score, or finishing out that very personal caster kill.

Careful Reconnaissance

Careful Recon got merged with Hit and Run, giving us a fun combination of movement abilities. Like before, this can be played anytime during the activation of one of your models and does one of the following for the remainder of the activation.

  • That model/unit gains Pathfinder this activation.
  • That model (Note: Model not Model/Unit. It will only affect one model) gains Reposition 3”.

For things like Magnus and Invictus, Carver Ultimus and War Boar, or Exulon Nostilla and Aberration this first ability is a godsend. It gives those Friendly but not Faction models Pathfinder, which they often lack. It’s also really helpful to give to a unit or warbeast/jack that needs to ignore terrain for an advance or charge in a key moment if they don’t already have Pathfinder natively (unless you’re a cool kid who plays armies that have Pathfinder base).

Credit: Steamforged Games

The second ability is also amazing. 3" Reposition doesn’t seem like a lot, but the ability to walk, shoot, and Reposition to contest or get back to safety can’t be underestimated. Additionally, for a model/unit with Ambush, it allows you to come on to the table, walk and contest even farther from the edge or have a model like Goran Lezarenko (better known as THE JACKAL) come on, take a shot, and reposition to still be able to contest/score a scenario element. This ability is especially relevant because it’s effectively on demand, meaning you as a player don’t have to ‘show your hand’ so to speak on when you’ll use it for a big play until you decide it’s time.

Duck and Cover!

Duck and Cover received a bit of a glow up with these changes, as well as a slight wording change. This card can be played anytime during the activation of one of your non-leader warrior models, and gives one of the following effects.

    • For one round while that model/unit is within area Scenario Terrain or 3” of another Scenario Element (40mm/50mm/Cache/Scenario terrain you can’t enter) it gains Dig In. (While Dug In , a model gains cover, Resistance: Blast, and does not block Line of Sight. The model remains dug in until it moves, is placed or is engaged).
    • For one round while that model/unit is within area Scenario Terrain or 3” of another Scenario element it gains Set Defense ( Models suffer -2 on charge and slam power attack rolls against a model with Set Defense).
Image courtesy Steamforged Games

This card has already generated some discussion on exactly how it works, as the changes are simple from the last iteration but important to note. The first effect now gives Dig In while (and only while) you are within 3” of a Scenario Element or in Scenario Terrain. This means you can’t use it and then move into scenario terrain, you can’t use it and run, and you can’t reposition after using it. If you do any of these, you lose the benefit of Dig In, making it effectively worthless. Additionally, where as pre-update the card didn’t give the rule Dig In, just the effects, which meant being engaged didn't help people hit you versus now where it does. If your model unit gets engaged by an enemy, placed, pushed, or slammed, then it is no longer dug in and loses the benefits. Feats like Scyrafels or Lylyths, for example, can just turn this off with a push, so be aware of tricks your opponent might have. Additionally if your model/unit has an ability like Dodge or Evasive, know that if you use  it, you will lose the benefits of Dig In.

The second effect gives a model/unit Set Defense. Again this has the same criteria of the first effect as far as where your model(s) need to be, but doesn't have as many ways you can accidentally lose the rule. Basically if an opponent charges or slam’s your model/unit, it’s -2 to hit on that attack. Simple, but can often swing the math nicely in your favor (especially after the January update, where everything’s ability to hit dropped). Remember this is only on the charge attack, so If a model has 2 attacks, or an ability like Dual Attack that allows them to make melee and ranged attacks in the same activation, the second attack will not have the penalty to hit.

Put the Fires Out

Azdharak, Herald of Immolation. Credit: Steamforged Games

This one is the same as the beginning of the year and merged with Old Faithful. Play it at the start of your maintenance phase on one model and it gains one of the following effects.

  • Shakable and Continuous Effects on that model/unit expire.
  • Remove d3+1 damage from that model.

Some minor changes and clarifications to remember on this card. First off, turn order is Maintenance -> Control -> Activation, so you have to use this card at the start of your turn, and get to use it before Focus is allocated, Fury is leached and Continuous Effects are rolled for/damage applied. This means if you have a unit that is suffering from Knockdown, you get to stand them all up right at the start of your turn. If that same unit is suffering from the Fire: Continuous effect, then you can make it go away before having to roll and see if it goes out or your opponent gets a damage roll.

Similarly, if you have a Warjack with its cortex out, you can play this to heal it for d3+1 damage before allocating focus. A great change here is the damage removal used to just be limited to cohort models, but now it's any model. If you have an important solo you want to try and keep alive a little longer, or your Leader just survived an assassination attempt, you can use it on them to stay in the game a little longer.

Defenses

Credit: Steamforged Games

The final change on this list is the change to defenses. They’re no longer a command card at all, and their counter card, Sapper, has also been removed. Now they are just a thing you can pay points to have, up to 3 of any combination in your army. Barriers are 2 points, and Fire Pit, Spike Trap and Powder Keg all 1 point.

Their effects have not changed except for Powder Keg, which in addition to the Demolition action being able to remove another Defense with 10”, can also now remove a Trapdoor marker (eat it, Kithguard). With Sapper gone, your defenses can now only be removed by coming in contact with an 80mm or 120mm base model (yours or your opponents), or with the Demolition special action from the Powder Keg. Ideally, this should mean your Defenses are on the table longer, protecting what you want to protect or giving you the effects you brought them for. However, because they cost points you could otherwise be spending on models to play the game, you need to be very sure you want to take them. The 1 point options will likely make it into lists that are at 99 and you don’t really want to change for whatever reason, just to use that extra point. Barriers however are a bit more of a hard sell, being 2 points a piece. The days of Caine, Baranova or Kythos having 2-3 Barriers to help keep them safe all game are probably gone, and honestly that's probably a good thing.

And that’s all we have for this article on the new Command Card and Defenses changes! It’s an exciting time to be a Warmachine player right now, as everyone is working out how best to use these new abilities in a post-update world. Hopefully, this piece here helped you figure out some new Command Card tech in this brave new world, and use it to win some tight games.
