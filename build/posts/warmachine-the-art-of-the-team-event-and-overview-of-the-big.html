[Warmachine] The Art of the Team Event, and Overview of the Biggest One of All

October 1st, 2026 via Goonhammer

In only a matter of days as of this writing, the equivalent of the Warmachine Olympics will begin: The World Team Championship!  The WTC, as it is commonly referred to and will remain so hereafter, is the biggest Warmachine team event in the world.  48 teams of five players from 19 different countries getting together to celebrate the hobby we love... by proving whose country is the best!  WTC lists and results, having the rarity of many of the best players in the world in the same room, get plenty of dissection and this will be discussed, but to zoom out for a moment: How does Warmachine play as a team game?  The answer is: Quite well!  In this article, we will be discussing the general format, process and advantages of running a team event and why it's one of the best ways to play competitive Warmachine even outside of the incomparable community aspect before taking a quick dip into what the world thought was the best in Warmachine this year and why.

The Team Format

Credit: Travis Marg

Team events are typically events of three or five people per team using any combination of armies.  The only composition restriction is that each leader can only be selected once per team, even if the same army appears multiple times within the same team.  Despite the name, the games are all still one-on-one, with the team format used to select team matchups and tables.  This isn't a dual-caster or Unbound event.  Those can be fun, but not what we are discussing here.  Instead, the competitive team format is a matter of opponent and table selection.

After the team captains determine who goes first, one of the teams (Team B) selects one of their players to be "offered" as the initial player.  The other team (Team A) secretly confers and then chooses two of their players to be matched up against the Team B player.  The Team B player then chooses which of the two Team A players they will play against, with the Team A player getting to choose the table the two will play on of those available.

The remaining Team A player, the one that was not picked, will remain and Team B will choose two of their remaining players and offer them to the Team A player, who will then pick their opponent and let Team B choose from the remaining tables.  This process repeats until there is only one player left on each side, who play each other on the final unchosen table.  Once the games begin, the team with more games won wins the round.  In such events, individual team member wins are usually the first tiebreaker, so every game is important even if you know your team has already won or lost the round!

Credit: Steve Rabideau, created for the Bokur Brawl Team Tournament, the largest Warmachine team event in the USA

There can be a lot of calculation and thinking ahead at the high level of matchup selection, but the actual process is fairly simple and it becomes quicker as more players are matched up and fewer remain.

Advantages of the Team Format

Credit: Tried & True

The process of team and table matchup selection may seem complex, but there is a method to the madness.  The specific intricacies of the team event actually lead to some of the best opportunities to play fun and competitive games of Warmachine compared to individual events because of several key factors.

Better Matchups

With the team format, both teams have the opportunity to significantly impact the matchups that are played by offering different armies and players at the right time.  Although this rarely results in a particular team getting to choose a highly favorable matchup, at least without significant consequences for the rest of the team, it does enable most team members to avoid a particular "bad" matchup by not being paired when their bad matchup is offered or simply selecting the other player with the choice of two.  Rather than being fully subject to the vagaries of chance and win rates, this format leads to an overall increase of fair or closer-to-fair matchups throughout the whole event, at least assuming there hasn't been a grave miscalculation!

Greater Army Diversity

Credit: Tried & True

Thanks to the unique leader composition requirements, most teams will have five different armies represented to give the team members unrestricted choices in their army pairings.  This not only encourages lots of different armies at a team event, but the matchup process means that some lesser-used armies that have poor matchups into a particular force have a chance to shine when they can avoid playing against that force entirely.  The broad field also encourages "dark horse" choices in teams where an older or less popular army might be successful thanks to opponent unfamiliarity or bringing a unique mechanic that more popular armies may be unprepared for.  In a particularly large team event like WTC or Bokur Brawl, you have to be prepared for anything!

Terrain That Matters

Credit: Tried & True

Any team event worth its reputation is going to have different tables that emphasize different terrain types, configurations, and otherwise make the "table selection" portion of the team format process meaningful.  Not only does this more closely guarantee relevant table terrain for matches, but also adds another layer to the selection and matchup process.  A player that's matched up against an army of big models may have an easier time dealing with them on a dense table packed with buildings, while a shooty army may be looking for more of a killing field.  The ability to choose tables and actually make them a significant factor in the team selection process means that event organizers can have more freedom to put together tables than a standard singles event.  Many larger team events will have distinct themes for the tables available and many of them are just a joy to look at.

Taking One for the Team (aka The Bus)

For all the control players have, sometimes a player just has to get thrown under the bus.  Sometimes selecting a deliberately bad matchup for one of your players is a necessary strategy to try to ensure that your other matches are as favorable as possible.  Every individual game matters, but ultimately rounds are won and lost as a team and the burden is never solely with an individual.  If a team member got bussed into a bad matchup, made a silly mistake, or simply had a sustained spot of bad dice luck, that's okay.  Even a bad loss can help contribute to a team victory and encouragement and support are built into the format as teams cheer a close loss or laugh off a bad game.  They can be highly competitive, but there's also a sense of shared joy that makes the whole thing more fun, win or lose.

WTC 2026

So now that we know the team format and why team events are so great, let's get into a brief overview of some of the stats for this event.

Popular Armies and Casters

Credit: Steamforged Games

Jumping straight into the deep end here, the single most popular army at WTC is Khador's Old Umbrey at 28 appearances, being the only army to appear on more than half the the team rosters.  Grymkin Wicked Harvest (24), Dusk House Kallyss (23) and Khymaera Shadowflame Shard (20) and Dusk Fane of Nyrro (17) round out the top five.  Fane of Nyrro deserves particular attention as the WTC model cutoff date means that most of the models from Harasheth's "The Gathering Darkness" box are not legal for this event, speaking to Fane's power and popularity even lacking some of their models.  Although these armies are fairly diverse, they highlight an important set of shared qualities.  Namely, all of these armies are melee-focused forces that are capable of either performing fast melee alpha strikes (Shadowflame, Fane, House Kallyss) or tanking up and delivering deadly counterattacks (Grymkin) while simultaneously have powerful defensive effects against shooting.

Old Umbrey can excel at both aspects and it's not surprising that Umbrey's Kazimir Morozov is the most popular leader at WTC at 25 appearances, meaning that only 3 of the 28 Old Umbrey players are not including him in their pairing.  Grymkin's Zevanna Agha and Shadowflame's Lylyth, Raven Vengeance round out the top 3, but honorable mention goes to Kapitan Baranova at 16 appearances within Khador's Winter Korps, whose popularity is clearly intended to stop enemy melee armies in their tracks and who leads one of the two more popular shooting forces (Winter Korps and Gravediggers) appearing on the popular end of the army breakdown.

Low End and Dark Horses

Credit: Steamforged Games

Of the armies that were created since the inception of Mk4, Sea Raiders and Necrofactorium are the lowest at just 10 and 7 appearances, respectively.  Both of these armies have competitive builds, but lack the options and certain quality of life aspects to truly make them popular in the field, or else are being pushed out by the popularity of certain armies that counter them.  Of the print-and-play armies, while Grymkin rule that particular subcategory, a handful of Dark Operations (7), Crucible Guard (6) and Convergence of Cyriss (6) armies are around, each sporting unique mechanics that teams more focused on fighting the mainstream popular choices may not be ready for.  The last print-and-play options are represented by the single solitary Infernals and Thornfall Alliance players, likely speaking to that the Infernals summoning mechanic or Thornfall's new and updated tools are not being highly rated by the current field.

Armies of Legend (armies that are not available to print or buy except via the secondary market) are always interesting to observe.  Soldiers of Fortune and Army of the Western Reaches make 3 appearances each, with the powerful feats and cheap models of Soldiers looking to carry the day while Army of the Western Reaches leverages their powerful beasts and Makeda's Stay Death as an option that not every army can easily answer.  Shadows of the Retribution and Final Interdiction both make 2 appearances as extreme factions that can do quite well in a team event, with Kaelyssa of Shadows asking significantly difficult questions to melee armies while Kreoss's Final Interdiction can be the ultimate answer to questions of defense.  4 other Armies of Legend make single appearances as well, showing that, despite being out of production for at least 5 years now, it's hard to keep a good army down.

Final Thoughts

Credit: Tried & True

The team event is one of the finest ways to play Warmachine that exists and I hope that this article has made a case for why that is.  Even if you don't have much experience, just getting together with a group of people and playing through the day with them is a blast and an experience I'd recommend to anyone who plays the game.  Teams are usually pretty easy to form or even join up with an existing one as a "mercenary" in many events by folks who are happy to shepherd a new team player through the event.

As for WTC, it and the Iron Gauntlet (the game's premier solo event) are considered the annual pinnacles of competitive Warmachine, and for good reason.  Nearly a full year's worth of practice, balance changes, new armies, and events have all led to this, and the analyses of teams, team compositions and individual lists can go into the fine details.  Ironically though, the actual results of these events may not be good predictors of what is strong in the future.  Between the upcoming Covenant of the Flame and Dread Armada armies, the impact of Fane of Nyrro and Thornfall Alliance remaining releases, and the lurking specter of the annual balance update that never fails to shake things up, there's never a time to rest on your laurels.
