[Warmachine] Competitive Innovations in the Iron Kingdoms: WTC, Lock & Load, and the End of 2026 Meta

November 12th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The end of the Warmachine event year is defined by two events:

  • Warmachine WTC (World Teams Championship)
  • Lock & Load US (Premier Steamforged Games run end of year Iron Gauntlet)

While drastically different approaches are required for both formats, they equally encourage the best of the best to hone what has emerged as the top of the meta. The sea of releases this year has certainly been a challenge for top tournament players to navigate but some clear winners did emerge.

Credit: Steamforged Games

World Team Championship 2026

First was WTC, the global showcase of skill and prowess from countries across continents with several countries bringing multiple teams. Going into the event the obvious meta front runners were Grymkin, Old Umbrey, and the new meta powerhouse Fane of Nyrro. As such it was assumed they would dominate the overall meta representation, which was remarkably not the case.

Outside of Thornfall Alliance and other Armies of Legend whose results are statistically insignificant, the most popular armies at WTC, Old Umbrey and Wicked Harvest, demonstrated why they were taken so often as they put up solid win rates despite being known quantities that define the metagame, although their win rates were not out of bounds. The other big winners were Brineblood Marauders, Orgoth Sea Raiders and Dark Operations, all of which certainly outperformed the win rates of several more popular armies. These armies were probably very well positioned to fight into the established metagame and carve success out of a crowded field where the more popular armies could be easily predicted. These are also older armies often piloted by dedicated players, so there can easily be something of an experience difference compared to the newer, more popular armies, particularly in cases where the pilots of the newer armies were not as well-practiced into the likes of Dark Operations or Sea Raiders, allowing them to submarine into impressive performances despite their small caster stables.

The biggest losers, win rate-wise, were House Kallyss, Gravediggers and Winter Korps. Gravediggers and Winter Korps have a very gun-focused army plan in a meta dominated by Morosov and Old Witch and thus very hostile into shooting. They also currently demonstrate limited caster variety based on how much Baranov and Cyn were in the field, which made them easier to plan for than armies with a wider stable of viable casters. The popularity of House Kallyss was probably the biggest surprise of the WTC statistics, but those were mostly dominated by Tyrus/Hellyth pairings. Although the pairing was strong and can be successful (as discussed later), it ended up being another known quantity that had questionable success into the major bogeymen armies of the event.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Fane of Nyrro was probably the biggest surprise in terms of expectations vs reality of their popularity and success rate, as many predicted that they would be the most popular and dominating faction of the event, but there are a couple of asterisks to this point. The first being that Fane did not have all of their in-app tools available. Harasheth was a legal model due to his Warmachine 3D version, but the rest of his box was not, and getting access to powerful models like the Riven and Styrge Hounds would have certainly changed the game. The other asterisk is that Nymara, as a caster, had an absurdly strong 67% win rate, with the dismal performance of the other Fane casters bringing the overall stats into the realm of reasonable. Nymara was a popular and powerful choice throughout both of the year’s capstone events and is absolutely a caster to watch.

The winning team, Australia Charging Cassowaries, had an eclectic mix of armies, being one of the rare teams to double up on their factions with two House Kallyss players as well as Grymkin, Old Umbrey and Winter Korps. The off-meta choices continue into the individual list choices as the Grymkin pairing does not contain Old Witch and although Morosov and Lissya were common sights at the event, both those lists have unique spins with Lissya’s extra-heavy cohort build and Morosov’s shifters heavily favoring werewolves over the more common werebears.

The pairing process and performance of the winners indicates that the Cassowaries were well aware of the meta and made their choices deliberately to counter the expected meta. Perhaps the most pronounced examples of this are the double Dusk lists. Hazaroth seems to have explicitly been the Fane of Nyrro drop as Hazaroth was played into it 3 times and going 2-1, while Dan Tacken’s Hellyth list came loaded for bear with an impressive 4 drops into Old Umbrey, going 3-1 in that matchup. The wolf-heavy Morosov list appears to have been built for Grymkin, dropping into Old Witch twice. Finally, Robin de Oliveira’s Heretic/King of Nothing pairing must have presented significant pairing challenges for all of the Grymkin drops that were mostly prepared for Old Witch, going 5-0 and taking the coveted number 2 spot on the individual players list. Although the list has no bearing on the results and shouldn’t be used as a pure indication of player skill given the WTC matchup process, it’s still very impressive for a list with 240 names on it.

Lock and Load US

Lock & Load US is characterized by the now legendary Iron Gauntlet which people have been qualifying for throughout the year at their local aptly named Iron Gauntlet Qualifiers. The format is an iron man event, which is to say players are separated into pods and those pods feed their top players into a final single elimination event where winner takes all.

Out of the 32 qualified players who decided to attend we see a fairly warped trend of stats. Players of this skill level made a true mix of understandable and baffling army choices. Was it just their own personal comfort pick? A back pocket favorite to have fun with? The deliberate attempt to attack this brutal gauntlet with a dark horse choice? Let's dig in and find out!

Dread Armada were obviously the breakout stars, with four players (including the ultimate winner) getting an army printed out in the scant week between the files going live and Lock and Load happening. Wicked Harvest, Gravediggers and especially Fane of Nyrro, now in possession of all of their tools except those announced at the event itself, made up fully half of the top 32, with the others split fairly evenly between most of the other factions and a few Armies of Legend to keep things interesting. Notable at this level is the complete absence of Sea Raiders or Shadowflame Shard as representatives of the wholly new armies released since the start of Mk4. Although none of the Gravediggers players advanced, which was predictable based on their poor performance at the WTC, the Wicked Harvest going 0 for 4 into the top 16 was a bit surprising, but there is a reason for this.

Credit: Steamforged Games

Interestingly, the meta that so defined the WTC team formation and results appears largely absent in the top 16 of the Iron Gauntlet. By contrast, several of the armies on display are either very new (Dread Armada, Fane including new post-WTC models such as the Styrge Hounds and Styrge Abhorreth) or are older armies of legend that are bringing their classic tools to bear in the current game (Devourer’s Host, Ravens of War). It appears that, taking some lessons from WTC about the risks of playing into a predictable meta, several of the players instead opted to try and ambush opponents with options that opponents may not have had the opportunity or time to practice against, leading to a lot of improvisational decision making. When expecting a single elimination format, forcing your opponent to think even more about what you have brought is a valuable edge.

While the broader meta is split between newer and much older armies, the top end of the of the meta seems to be defined by front-line personal work casters, with Terminus, Nymara and Dekathus all dominating the top end and Vorsalys being a choice to try to keep those melee monsters in check. By contrast, more back-line casters like Shadowtongue, Vorgoroth, Vayl and Harasheth did not find corresponding success, all being defeated by the front-line powerhouses. It appears that Wicked Harvest was also fairly vulnerable to these powerful front-liners, whose aggressive play and innate resilience appeared to push the little nightmares out of the top cut. Combined with Old Witch being slated for a power reduction and the most successful Grymkin list at WTC being Witch-less, it will be interesting to see how Grymkin builds will adjust into 2027.

The final match was a battle of the new stuff, with the all new Dread Armada army led by Terminus and piloted by Dan Riker finding success against Peyton Preece’s Vorsalys, who was packing his own brand new toys in two Styrge Abhorreths (soon eager to try the even newer models revealed at the event itself to be sure). The combination of Entropic Shroud and Aegis in the Terminus list reducing most enemy spell ranges to effectively zero appears to be a big part of allowing Terminus to retain his threatening agency against a field where powerful control effects were common.

Image Credit: Steamforged Games

Warmachine in 2026 was one of the most tumultuous competitively. Between the official release of four full armies, the re-release of three more, and a rare mid-year update the overall meta felt like it was changing weekly. The armies and archetypes of gunlines and aggressive anti-gunlines were systematically replaced with aggressive mid board bullies that could play wide in sub-packages and silos. Spheres of control and combat around very widely spread out objectives. While exciting to learn about as new rules were revealed the added complications of new mechanics was exciting to many and suffocating to others. While Lock and load had some distinct surprises and the podium of WTC deviated from American expectations, many hope the 2027 meta is more stable. More armies that feel in line with the new SFG style of mechanics not simply propped up by highly skilled faction experts. Warmachine has been coming back with a vengeance, and as far as army updates/additions were concerned, 2026 was an explosion of accessibility, so we hope things even out with the new update so everyone can play this awesome game their way.
