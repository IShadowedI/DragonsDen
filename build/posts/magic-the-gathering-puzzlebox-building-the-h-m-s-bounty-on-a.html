Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Building the H.M.S. Bounty on a Budget

November 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

If I had a nickel for every Monoblue Commander that saw essentially zero hype during spoiler season that I intended to break and elevate in the months following, I'd have...what, three nickels now? Four, I suppose, but Eluge was always destined to succeed. Following in the footsteps of Princess Yue, Immortus, and the gar himself, my recent fascination has been with the H.M.S. Bounty, Unlikely Timeship, from the communuity-maligned Star Trek set. Excitement for this card was low on release, including my own skepticism, but after having played over a dozen online games, and two FNMs' worth with the ship, I must confess that this is the coolest Commander I've gotten to play in some time. While the Bounty looks janky, the pool of Whales is shockingly deep, and the combos it enables are backed up with one of the best enablers for a tutor Commander of all time, Dreamtide Whale. There's one problem—if you wanted to buy the list I covered in a cool ~4k words without proxying (which I highly encourage), it would run you around $2,500. For playgroups without proxies, or for those otherwise preferring genuine cardboard, that's untenable; for this reason, I made it my goal to shave down the price of this fantastic Commander and make it accessible to everyone, proxies or not. You can build a compelling, competent, and frankly sweet version of the Bounty for $60, and still sail the stars with your cetacean companions pretty darn well. Let's dive into it.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

Whale-Watching on a Budget

Before we get into the weeds, it's worth familiarizing ourselves with the Bounty's core gameplan, which I covered in the primer here. The deck has a three-pronged gameplan, where we aim to find Steelfin Whale and a Knack effect (the fastest win, but requires that it stick or have Haste), Orvar & Great Whale alongside Mind Games (slower, and requires Lands that tap for 5+, but doesn't need Haste), or Luxior, Giada's Gift with a Commander stationed to 18 (functionally 21 Commander Damage). Slight problem there—Great Whale spiked to ~$180 after it was revealed Whales would be included in 'Sea Monster' bucket post-Nauctis, and as a Reserve List card its supply is limited. So, that takes out Plan B, leaving us to be more reliant on Steelfin lines for combo wins, and more reliant on Luxior for fair wins. Another problem that crops up, however, is that Steelfin lines require mana rocks that go mana-positive as a target to bounce and recast, which are a huge reason as to why the budgetless list is so expensive. Things like Moxen and even Grim Monolith are hugely important for your endgame, and those cost a pretty penny. On a budget, the only mana rock that goes positive is Sol Ring, but thankfully it's fairly findable. Once you pare away the combo pieces & mana-positive rocks, what more does the Bounty need? Well, blink helps us protect our pieces and re-tutor, and utility Creatures help us gain advantage over time and station up our Commander alongside Dreamtide Whale. We can take that schema of wincons, blink, and value Creatures, and transfer it to the budget version pretty cleanly!

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

There are some pretty easy swaps, such as Faerie Mastermind being exchanged for Ghostly Pilferer, or Reshape being taken out for Scour for Scrap, but where things get interesting is in the expansion of aspects of our gameplan which aren't fast enough for the budgetless version. Take for example Moxite Refinery, which is sort of a second Luxior, able to turn Charge counters into +1/+1 counters, similarly resulting in combat damage wins. It's a bit more awkward to use than Luxior, but it's also dirt cheap, and does a well enough job when it comes to abusing the fact that our Commander is a Spacecraft. In much the same way, Leyline of Transformation makes everything into Whales, so our Commander becomes a strict Creature tutor in the Command Zone, which helps us play like more of a toolbox and even find Knack effects by chaining through Micromancer. We're even playing Kaho, Minamo Historian alongside Hidden Strings, which can in theory find the entire Steelfin win on its own if we have sufficient mana, by untapping Kaho twice via the Cipher and an unblocked Creature (such as our Flying Commander). Finally, this list is uniquely on a Speed package, with highlights like the Raceways, Vnwxt, Verbose Host, and Racer's Scoreboard. This is in service of having another Haste enabler—Amonkhet Raceway—but the fact our Commander gives us an Extra Turn means that we can hit max speed faster than most decks, which lets us get a ton out of the mechanic! Again, this is assisted by the Start Your Engines! cards being pennies on the dollar.

Dream Big, Dreamtide

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

At time of writing, this list comes in at a cool $59.56, which is ~$15 less than the Borg precon that's come out from the same set, and certainly more powerful. I think there's something telling about the fact that the two most expensive cards in the list are Dreamtide, your first tutor target 99% of the time, and Luxior, a wincon. This is a list running on pretty slim margins, but the strength of a tutor in the Command Zone and what is essentially a guaranteed Extra Turn once per game really does earn its keep in spite of that; when there's a limiting factor on a deck, be it because of its status as a precon, budget, or some other theme, concentrating the power in the one card you know you have access to is a pretty safe bet. This list is currently built as Bracket 2, where the budgetless thrives at the high end of Bracket 3, but you could honestly toss a couple moxen in here and pitch it as the fabled 'Game Changer-less 3', aka Bracket 2.5. The upgrade paths are pretty clear, which does a lot to help this serve as a deck you can keep in its budget state, or slowly build towards something more optimized.

Decklists are kept updated, and may change with set releases.

I think it is worth interrogating why the Bounty has me so smitten, because frankly I'm surprised that it's already become the Bracket 3 deck I'll reach for when that's the power the pod asks for, so much so that I've unironically considered keeping the budget version with me as well just to get more Bounty games in. There's a few objective truths of the card: Having a limited tutor means you get a toolbox, but nothing too broad or obvious, and taking an Extra Turn is strong enough that chaining them is verboten by writ of the Bracket system. It manages to do two of the most powerful things in Commander—tutoring and turns—but neither in such a way that you get out too far ahead, or break the social code of the format. You brush against it, yes, but those lines go uncrossed, and as a Johnny/Spike that has a certain appeal. More than that though is the fact the Whales which exist in Magic are such that you have everything from value plays to combos at your disposal, and moreover, multiple combos enabled by, and using outlets via, Whales. It's such an absurd statement to say that this type which has existed since Narwhal in 1995's Homelands, having received no support up to this point, randomly has the exact tools it needs to both produce infinite mana readily, and expend it just the same.

Subjectively, that's the coolest facet of the card, right alongside the fact you need to work a bit to actually turn it on (via Stationing), but the first thing you tutor can in theory get you to 8 Charge counters the turn after. Whales form this esoteric sort-of archetype, and coming from a Yugioh background, the searching and semi-linear play pattern really appeal to my sensibilities. I would wager they'll appeal to you too, dear reader, and for a cool $60 you can get your fins on the coolest deck I've built since Yue. I think my only real concern is that Whales receive enough support in Nauctis that this becomes too known, or well-supported, and it loses the contrarian appeal I am cursed to bear. Suffering from success. I suppose. For anyone without that particular brainworm, however, this deck has much to look forwards to in the coming months, and I suspect building it now while the Whales not on the Reserve List are inexpensive is likely a wise choice.
