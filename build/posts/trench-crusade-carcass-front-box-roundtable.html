Trench Crusade Carcass Front Box Roundtable

August 25th, 2026 via Goonhammer

It's been a long time coming but the two-player box set for Trench Crusade is finally here. The box includes two new warbands (mostly, we’ll get to that in that section), and two campaigns featuring new scenarios, patrons, and exploration tables, not to mention a ton of cool lore and art that Trench crusade has become famous for. We’ll have articles diving deep into each part of the box, but this is meant to be a roundtable looking at the box as a whole and what we think of it.

Before we dive in, we’d like to thank Factory Fortress Inc for providing us with a preview copy of the rules for review purposes.

New Warbands

Naval Raider Warband Credit: Mildnorman

We have two new warbands: Naval Raiders and Procession of the Sacred Affliction. Both of these warbands were once variant warbands for Heretic Legion and Trench Pilgrims respectively, but are now standalone warbands with their own variants.

Norman: I’m torn on this approach; on one hand, I think expanding on the ideas that were already present in the core factions is a good idea for the game. It means folks will be able to get familiar with like profiles and it gives the designers a good starting point to expound upon. On the other hand, it can feel a bit copy/paste to the point that unless you’re running the War Wolf, there’s not a very compelling reason to go back to Heretic Legion if you’re looking at Naval Raiders. FFI has done a lot of effort to make these Warbands feel distinct but ultimately you can’t unsee their roots. I think a lot of folks are patiently and excitedly (well, maybe not patiently) waiting to see the wacky and weird stuff that hasn’t seen the table yet like Church of Metamorphosis and Path of the Beast, and seeing two factions that we’ve already had a good amount of exposure to might be a bit deflating. That all said, aesthetically, these two warbands are absolutely their own things and have a pretty unique identity so I’m not too beat up about all that.

Dandy: I agree with Norman on the mechanical and rules aspects of the new factions. There’s little to compel me to play a vanilla Trench list or something like Cavalcade, outside of the necessity of a second Punt Gun. Methodius? Sure, that’s distinct enough to get the extra Shrine, but I think that Sacred Affliction offers so much that it kind of overshadows the other Trench Pilgrim warbands. Agreeing with Norman again, the aesthetics are just top-notch. I personally never jived with HNR, but seeing these models and the amazing artwork in the Carcass Front book for the other models just provides such a flavourful take that the narrative and models finally combine to create something really compelling. 

Bre4d: The aesthetics of the factions in the box mesh very nicely together as the “grungy seaside” warbands for the setting of Carcass Front. Not to circle the same point, but these warband variants becoming their own factions do potentially present the problem of making the original versions of them seem bland and not even worth considering when going to play Trench Crusade. On the other hand, as someone who loves to use list building as a way to express myself, it’s great to have more sets of rules within these factions to play around with and try out on the table. 

DGMP: For the most part I agree with the rest of the roundtable about the concern for the new subfactions. Without losing something of significance in choosing to specialize as one of the warband variants, it makes choosing the default option seem either painfully boring or intentionally kneecapping yourself. Why yes I could be normal, or I could take 3 anti-tank hammers on nuns who also get an extra +1 dice to injure downed targets or get +1 Dice to dashing for my entire crew. I love the war wolf, but not that much, I am going +1 Dash and SMG’s for days every time. But simply put, the new warbands are so good it makes me question why you would ever not play as them, unless the vibes for them are just antithetical to your playstyle. However I have faith that in time, Factory Fortress can raise the rest of the rosters up to be just as good, flavorful and fun as these two new ones.

Zaros: I love the idea of fleshing out variants and giving more options, but there is a thin line to walk as everyone has put out. I think for some existing variants, this could be a huge boon for the future but if every variant simply makes the original “variant” overshadowed it doesn’t quite hit the point of intention I believe. Regardless, I will be screaming alongside my triple choristers and grinding my friends to a halt with mass debuffs.

Naval Raiders

You can find our faction focus for Naval Raiders here and the model review here.

Heavy Anointed Naval Raider - @MawQueenMinis

Norman: I love these guys. Are they just Heretic Legion but without the War Wolf and some extra gear? Yes. Does that mean I love them less? Absolutely not. Stuff like the Wrecker’s Torch and Hull Drill will go a long way to making this faction feel a bit more distinct and the lore representation for them (along with the kick ass art) very much makes them feel like they’re their own guys. I do wish they got a Big Guy but I understand why they don’t have one. I do kinda wish that Trench Ghosts had gotten the standalone treatment instead since they have a more distinct roster, but you can’t argue with how good of a fit these guys are for the Carcass Front.

Bre4d: As a Trench Pilgrim simp, I have to say the winners in purely the looks department for Carcass Front would be the Naval Raiders. The Heavy Anointed hit my Bioshock soft spot, and the Chorister with the diving helmet is simply awesome. 

DGMP: Naval Raiders were the faction I played in my first IRL campaign. I am unabashedly biased towards them and I will continue to be so. They are fantastic and I have no plans on dashing less or running less SMG’s in the future with these fantastic models. I am however very excited to try out their new specialties! Leviathan Shoal in particular calls to me, because diving suits are just that awesome. We just need those drill arms now.

Zaros: I touched on it before, but I will scream it out loud again. I LOVE debuff auras. I also love horde focus playstyles (smiles in grunger). The chorister variant offering the best of both is music to my ears, in a very simplified package compared to the infection equation that grunger is. Shoal is going to be insane to see as well, as the ability to give a unit a tough mid match is an insane ability that can swing fights. But my love lies with the choir boys themselves, I shall be chanting to the abyss alongside them…

Sacred Affliction

You can find our faction focus for Sacred Affliction here and the model review here.

Credit: Dandy

Dandy: I was a Sacred Affliction fan when they were in Trench Pilgrims, and that only continues now that they are their own developed faction. From a mechanics perspective, things have stayed the same, with some additions to the Battlekit that further push their offensive power and mobility. I will be interested to see how these additions affect a faction already very strong, as the possibility of wider lists, without sacrificing reliability in mobility/Dashing, kinda fixes one of the few weaknesses the faction had. 

For models, they’re just amazing. Easy to put together, with not just 2 options but also the ability to mix and match them. So it is not just a strict “this or that”, but a chance to make something unique to you. I was initially a little skeptical about the extent to which they could differentiate these models from the vanilla Trench Pilgrims, but they’ve done it! It’s mostly done with bandages. Like, a LOT of bandages, but it's also the hodgepodge nature of the equipment, the religious iconography, and, of course, Millstones. 

Bre4d: Going off of what Dandy mentioned, the ability to mix and match these models is done very well. The fact that FFI was able to do this while the models are as dynamic as they are is super impressive. Although I mentioned that the Naval Raiders eked out a bit better in terms of what I find cool, my favorite shoeless bing bongs were absolutely way easier to build. I still wish we had more Ecclesiastic Prisoner variations, but I guess (literally) beggars can’t be choosers. The aesthetic differences between the original Trench Pilgrims and these sculpts from Carcass Front are a great change, while also being able to fit both kits into the same warband and not have any of them look out of place, which is a huge upside. 

DGMP: The local lepers are sculpted absolutely amazingly. You can practically smell the sickness coming off of every lovingly rendered bandage and boil. And the dad-bod Wrath of God pilgrim! Flawless, no notes. I do share a bit of Dandy’s concern that they really do not seem to have much of a weakness at the moment. Taking Sacred Affliction seems to really be a statement of intent, that intent being that you are going to rush across the board as fast as possible and beat someone's face into the mud like God Himself told you to. Which to be fair is pretty lore accurate. They also have a leg up on the Naval Raiders in the model customization area. The bits for all the various units are generally much easier to swap around and make your own than the Raiders are.

Zaros: I’ll say it one time for everyone in the back; we all LOVE stigmatic nuns. An amazing model with an amazing set of skills and abilities to accompany. The ability to take more of them alongside dope knights with healing capabilities? Sign me up immediately. I still have yet to build a faithful warband yet, and this is my planned first one. I am going to have a blast kitbashing up some leper knights to scour the battlefields!

Campaign Rules

You can find our review of the campaign rules here.

Path to Leviathan

The path to leviathan is a two-player campaign meant to walk new players through the new scenarios. If you’re buying the box as an intro to the game, start here. This guide through the missions is exactly what new players will need and with just 5 games in the campaign. 

Norman: This is a really smart addition from the folks over at FFI. I think a lot of folks are gonna buy this box as a starter set for themselves and maybe a friend and without this would dive into the Carcass Front stuff headfirst and bounce straight off. With this you get a smooth onramp that you can layer as much or as little onto where you can get your bearings.

Dandy: It’s nice to have a structured and easy campaign for beginners to enjoy. Sure, you can start off with simple 700 Ducat games and then move on to one-offs before jumping into a campaign, but getting into a narrative right off the bat is going to be a nice experience for new players. Being able to play this short, scenario-based, narrative campaign as a series of one-offs using the starting warbands lets players jump right into the fun of Trench Crusade. 

DGMP: A perfect weekend campaign to play with whoever you split the box with to cut your teeth on the game system. It’s not only a great introduction to various styles of mission, but it never lets up on the theme and story. It maintains its atmosphere of grungy desperation all the way from scavenging forbidden knowledge to summoning an ancient monstrosity (Almost like a good TTRPG campaign, really) and after those 5 opening games anyone should have a pretty strong grasp of the game and be ready to jump into a new campaign with even more players.

Zaros: Without a doubt, THIS is where new players should start. Diving directly into a proper Carcass Front campaign for a brand new player is going to be extremely overwhelming. This lets them dip their toes properly and prepare themselves for the full plunge when they are properly acclimated and prepared. I think this will create a great transition point for new players; Single Match Play, Path to Leviathan, then a big campaign like Carcass Front.

Carcass Front Campaigns

Sacred Affliction War Priest - bre4d_

The titular Carcass Front Campaign is a broad map campaign that adds node control, resource management, and secret missions to the mix.

Norman: I’m well documented as a Big Fan of narrative game systems. With that in mind, while I like a lot of the things going on here, there’s a lot of stuff. The book does its darndest to make it all digestible, but there’s just a lot of things to keep in your head here between the new exploration tables, the vision cards, and the map itself. I think the ideas here are all good, but you gotta make sure everyone's on board with the extra work going on on top of all the homework associated with post game Trench Crusade (not that that’s too heavy a lift) before you commit to a Carcass Front campaign. I said it above, but thank god for the Path to Leviathan, because without that I think a lot of folks wouldn’t interact with a lot of the systems in here if they tried it right after building their first warband. 

DGMP: I love me some crunchy, complicated rules. And boy, does the Carcass Front deliver lots of those. This is a beefy campaign with a good chunk of new things to keep track of. Secret Missions, a bit of base building, resource gathering, special bonuses conferred by key locations. It’s frankly fantastic but not something you should just spring on a play group without discussing it with them. This will have a bit more of a cognitive load than just rolling up a random mission every week for a few months if that is your current style of campaign. However if your group isn’t averse to adding even more depth to the post game step of Trench Crusade, I think you can get an absolute ton out of this system.

Zaros: As everyone has stated, this is CRUNCHY. There's a lot going on, but I think it is done masterfully for the most part. I love the usage of the map and a new take on exploration. I think that it gives a lot of ideas and inspiration to organizers to really push the limit on campaigns in the future and to not be afraid of diving deep into the nitty gritty. Carcass Front sets a strong precedent of going big, this shows that they are willing and excited to swing wide with interesting and wild concepts that might not hit with everyone. But their presentation and delivery is what sets it up for success. They offer alternatives, designed systems that work independently of one another, and guide organizers and players alike to tailor the campaign experience to what each group is looking for.

Scenarios

Norman: The new scenarios are an interesting one. They’re no doubt cool and thematic, and they avoid the general balance traps you may see when a team goes hard in the paint to tell a story for a scenario. That said, each and every one has either NPC models or bespoke pieces of terrain. These are mitigated through suggestions in the book for replacements and the STLs that are coming if you backed the project, but the main issue is a logistical one. Even if I have all my Mad Monks painted, my Train Gun printed, my piles of scrap drybrushed and my Altar of Leviathan built, it's still an extra thing I’ll need to have on hand if my opponent and I decide to play that scenario. I would’ve liked some more “vanilla” missions but I suppose the random generator accomplishes that goal.

DGMP: I love the visceral feel of these new scenarios. They all have a desperate vibe about them, scavenging things forgotten or thought ruined to try and release an ancient monstrosity. But they all fall into what I consider the “Armored Train” issue. Is the scenario sick? Hell yeah! Does it require its own bespoke special terrain not only to set up but to interact with? Unfortunately also yeah. It is somewhat a cost of having a scenario more interesting and complicated than “Capture the Flag” or “Team Deathmatch” but it is still a barrier to just setting up and playing that I feel needs to be mentioned. As an attempt to smooth this out there are some portions of the rulebook with either paper cutouts you can scan or suggestions it offers to use instead, but the requirement for More Stuff is still there regardless. The new random mission generator is a much simpler addition with no real drawbacks, roll up and set up and you can just get going without any new bespoke shiny things.

Bre4d: The constant struggle of narrative games and the requirement for specific terrain is quite brutal, but once players can get through that barrier of entry these scenarios look like a blast. Not to sound like an online class message board, but I agree with DGMP across the board with how much is required to simply play a game of Trench Crusade, and now Carcass Front has a multitude of additional requirements to get the most out of the game.

Zaros: Adding the need for anything always raises the quote unquote “barrier of entry”, but we also have seen time and time again that the community will always find a way. Kitbashing is at the heart of alternative wargaming, and people have been making their own terrain and models well before Trench Crusade entered the scene. Where there's a want, players will definitely design and print models/terrain to fit the scenarios accordingly aplenty, and I’m certain that it will do the campaign justice. I personally love neutral models that add chaos to the match, and the neutral model rules are excellent! I look forward to the mayhem that will ensue.

Overall Thoughts

Sacred Affliction Ecclesiastic Prisoner - bre4d_

Dandy: All in all, what a box! So two Warbands, some small terrain, as well as tokens, sheets, a brand new narrative book with lore and rules, that can be played with in a dead simple intro game for brand new players, a simple campaign scenario, and then a large map-based campaign mode that introduces mechanics not yet seen? This box has basically something for everyone, regardless of your experience with the game. I’m certainly impressed by how much is crammed in when you stop and think about it.

I think this box is just what the game needed, and those on the fence about jumping in have found the excuse they needed to try it out. As has been pointed out in the campaign section, there’s going to be a lot in the Carcass Front campaign, and it borders on being perhaps a little too much management, but I’m very optimistic about future campaign ideas and what the team can come up with. 

Norman: This is a pretty sick box! There’s a lot to love here, between two new plastic kits (both with amazing sculpts), rules for new warbands, a new campaign system, and a ton of new background lore, there’s some great stuff in here. I’ve got gripes with some things here and there, but for a first big physical release, this is kicking stuff off with a bang. At the end of the day if you’re looking to get into TC, further your TC experience, or just wanna get some cool models, this box is a great buy. I’m really looking forward to seeing what's next from FFI.

DGMP: It is a hell of a box. A solid 8/10 starter box, which I can easily recommend to anyone interested in trying the game who doesn’t want resin minis or Prussians. The new sculpts are fantastic, a perfect blend of being detailed enough to look good without drowning in little greebles just for having as much detail per cubic inch as possible on a model. The token/measuring widget sprue is an amazing little bonus as well, having downed and Tough Removed markers makes things much easier to parse at a glance on the board. Really though I have no complaints, it is a fantastic box for the price point. Sink deeper with the Carcass Front campaign, get started with the Path to Leviathan, it has everything you could want. 

Bre4d: This Carcass Front starter box is a fantastic place for both those who are starting to play Trench Crusade, as well as those who have been neck deep in the game space since the beginning of the TC’s inception. The inclusion of reference materials and ‘how to start’ guides is wonderfully juxtaposed by the gritty and crunchy Carcass Front additions to the base game.  

Zaros: Astounding plastic models and a phenomenal campaign to match, this boxset really hits a homerun and sets a strong baseline of what we expect from Factory Fortress in the future. My excitement for my personal choir knows no bounds and my unending servitude to Charon is cemented (praise be leviathan Master of Death is an insane ability). My local community has been excited for months leading up to the release and our organization of a large scale campaign! I’m excited to see what my talented community comes up with for terrain and model design as well as the rest of the globe and hope the excitement continues to whatever we receive next from the trenches!
