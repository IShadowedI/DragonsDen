Trench Crusade Scenery: Building Trench Blocks

August 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer

With Trench Crusade steadily growing in popularity, I decided to sit down and create some terrain on the cheap and quick to use for introductory games and on my home table.

Curiously, the current rules edition of Trench Crusade doesn't support entire trench boards (classifying trenches as area terrain), but that hasn't stopped the majority of the community on making very impressive and evocative boards to that theme.

Archon Games Trench Crusade demo board. (Credit - Jackie Daytona)

As a long time hobbyist, I happened to have a lot of boxes left over from various projects over the years, which I realized were ideal for creating a trench board with through using negative space - by placing some characterful blocks on a gaming mat, you can easily create a veritable maze relatively quickly (well, about a weeks worth of evenings, if you're working with PVA as I did).

Plus, blocks are easy to store and stack when not in use!

How to Build Trench Blocks

Step One: Materials and Tools

So to make a trench block, you'll need the following:

  • A box (any miniatures box is ideal for this, and they come in a number of sizes no matter what game system you play!).
  • Clippers (but a craft knife can do just as well!).
  • Pen (or pencil) for marking out measurements.
  • Coffee Stirrer Sticks.
  • PVA glue.
  • A pot of water and an old brush.
All the materials you need to make a trench block. (Credit- hydriatus)

Step Two: Assembly

With all the materials and tools gathered, we can begin the actual crafting process!

First, make sure the box is sealed - so all the flaps and openings are glued shut.

Whilst waiting for the glue to dry, you can roughly measure out how long your stirrers need to be to cover an edge. Use your pen to mark where to cut them down to size.

Marking out the strips. (Credit - hydriatus)

Once that is done, grab your clippers and cut the stirrers. Make sure to hold onto the off-cuts - they can be used to add further details to either the walls or the surface of the block.

Wait for the glue sealing the box to dry (if using PVA, this should take about a day). Then, glue the trimmed stirrers to the corresponding side - just put some PVA on the side of the box and spread it out with an old brush or a spare stirrer.

Then arrange the cut stirrers horizontally on the side, and put a weight on to hold them down while the glue dries overnight (I've had luck using the PVA bottle itself - but anything small and heavy will work, like metal minis, or a small mug, or maybe even rubber bands, though those could get stuck to the stirrers if you're not careful and sparing with the glue).

Attaching the stirrers. (Credit - hydriatus)

After one side is done, repeat the process with the others (working on several blocks at once is recommended, since you will be waiting a day for each side to fully dry!). If your stirrers are not long enough for a side, just add another to reach the end - you can cover the join in the next step.

Once all the sides have stirrers attached, it is time to add some details. For simplicity, I just went with some bracing planks, though you can add anything you can imagine - small shrines, doorways into bunkers or tunnel networks, or firing steps.

I glued two stirrers vertically at either end of one side first, then added more in between making sure to space them apart as evenly as possible.

Make sure to have these vertical stirrers extend up past the side by at least one stirrer width. This way, you can add one more stirrer to make a border around the top surface which will make texturing it easier and neater.

Detailing the sides. (Credit - hydriatus)

Simply repeat for the other sides - the vertical stirrers can be used to hide any inconsistencies or joins or rough edges from previous steps. You can also angle them if you wish for a more haphazard appearance.

Step Three: Texturing

With all the sides done, you can now get to work texturing the top of the block to match your gaming mat and miniature bases. Personally, I first used the various off-cuts from the stirrers to make some planking for variety, though you could also use slate, gravel, or any spare miniature bits to add whatever character you want to the block - though I would advise you focus on keeping the majority of the surface somewhat flat so you can put other terrain pieces on top.

Here's where you can use most of the off-cuts from previous steps. (Credit - hydriatus)

Then, I layered PVA over the rest of the surface and applied modelling sand. If you prefer, you can instead just apply texture paste for a more muddy look - but I had none at hand, so good old sand it was!

Ready for painting! (Credit - hydriatus)

Step Four: Painting

With your block assembled and textured, you can now paint it however you want to match your scenery collection and miniatures. But for the sake of completion, I've included my method as well, using the following:

  • Grey Jenolite Etch Primer
  • White Scar Citadel Spray
  • Snakebite Leather Contrast
  • Mournfang Brown
  • Zandri Dust
  • Ushabti Bone
  • Nuln Oil Wash
  • Humbrol Acrylic Varnish

First, a spray of primer all over the block seals the terrain and the surface texture. After that, I sprayed the whole block white, and used Snakebite Leather Contrast to paint the planks on the sides and top for a light wooden look.

After allowing that to dry, I base-coated the sand with Mournfang Brown, followed by a drybrush of Zandri Dust, and then a lighter drybrush of Ushabti Bone. This creates a neutral badlands look, which I washed with Nuln Oil to darken to match the darker palette used on my Trench Crusade miniatures.

To finish off, use an acrylic varnish spray to seal everything and you're done!

A finished block. (Credit - hydriatus)

From here, you can experiment with gluing various boxes together or using other kinds of boxes to make more irregular shapes as you wish for your board! You can even try using box lids if you want to sculpt some sort of terrain feature with "depth" into a block (like exposed tunnels or a particularly deep crater).

Arrange several of these on a gaming mat and you'll have an instant trench board, easy to setup, takedown, and most importantly (to me at least) - store.

Of course, this is just the start. Trench Crusade features far more scenery rules than just trenches - ruins, cursed ritual sites, bunkers, and armored trains all feature in various scenarios... but those are best covered in future articles.
