Gundam Slingers: Building a Better Clan Deck

August 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Last week, we took a moment to talk about how the meta in the Gundam Card Game had been shaping up with the recent release of Freedom Ascension, and more importantly, a wave of bans and restrictions on various cards and deck building strategies. The Gundam Card Game is still barely a year old, and so meta changes and swings are going to happen more often than not; still, there are a few cases in which things that seemed strong or perhaps never got a great chance to shine can always make a comeback as things swap around. One of those possibilities is the Clan archetype, which primarily focuses around Gundam GQuuuuuuX units and pilots; Clan was one of the second waves of deck types released with the starter decks, and although it had some momentum, the archetype never really dominated like other aggro builds did. With some changes and shake-ups to the types of Units that can be built, though, let’s take a look this week at how to build up from the Clan Unity Starter, and consider how the upcoming September releases might help Clan really take off in the game.

Clan Unity

Clan is best described as a mid-range aggro deck that focuses on card unity and cohesion, and very much wants your deck to be built with the Keyword in mind. Clan is a Red-Green deck, but more importantly, it is a GQuuuuuuX deck; there are quite literally no other Gundam series used in Clan decks, which makes it one of the more flavorful styles of play (assuming you like GQX, which tends to have a mixed opinion). The reasoning for this is primarily due to how most Clan cards call for, dig up, or otherwise interact with other Clan cards; if you’re familiar with Tribal style decks in Magic the Gathering, Clan is very similar to that in how it wants your deck to be almost entirely filled with the Keyword. There is one exception, but we’ll get to her in a bit; this also makes this deck a pretty easy one to start construction on, and overall one of the cheaper decks to build in the Gundam Card Game because of that.

The recent ban/restricted wave also totally misses Clan; the archetype is basically unaffected by any of the changes to deckbuilding that other decks faced, as it doesn’t run the LVL 2 1 Cost Units that many other decks flooded the board with, nor did it use Amuro or Mikazuchi; this is another fun way to consider building into a meta as the deck can take advantage of other players scrambling to rebuild their decks, but it also leads to some weaknesses in the sense that Clan has not had any new cards since the Clan Unity deck and sets released, although that does seem to be changing with some of the upcoming teasers Bandai has released. If anything, building a Clan deck now might also help make your gameplan ready to expand once the newer cards are available, and very likely swap in the needed new cards to replace older ones.

Building Your Clan

Right now, the best overall Clan Deck is the one run by Treminho in May, taking 1st Place at a regional tournament by going 9-0 in the competition. That may seem like bad news in that there haven’t been many Clan placements since then, but frankly the deck didn’t dominate the meta and was mostly an outsider contender, and the recent meta shift has seen people looking to establish what is “the meta” versus experimentation with outsider decks, so I don’t expect to see a lot of Clan decks for a little while longer. That said, I have seen quite a few Red-Green decks splash GQuuuuuuX Omega Psychommu and Nyaan, so the cards are certainly seeing play, just not in their specific “deck” type.

Let’s start with the Units first:

2x Ortega’s Rick Dom (LVL 3 Cost 2)

4x Sugai’s Gelgoog (LVL 3 Cost 2)

3x GQuuuuuu Omega Psychommu (LVL 4 Cost 3)

2x Red Gundam (LVL 4 Cost 3)

4x Red Gundam (Green LVL 4 Cost 2)

4x Red Gundam (Red LVL 4 Cost 2)

3x GQuuuuuu Omega Psycommu (LVL 5 Cost 3) 

4x GFreD (LVL 6 Cost 4)

2x GQuuuuuuX Omega Psycommu (LVL 7 Cost 5) 

4x GQuuuuuuX Omega Psycommu (LVL 8 cost 7)

As you can see, this deck runs a LOT of the same Units, specifically the main bulk of your deck is consisting of Red Gundam, GQuuuuuuX, and GFreD, which primarily nails down the point that this is quite literally the GQX deck more than almost any other deck currently played hews to a specific entity in the Gundam universe.

Ortega’s Rick Dom is a great example of what we’re looking to do with Clan, though, as the card reads: “Deploy: Choose 1 of your other Clan Units. During this turn, it may choose an active enemy Unit with 3 or less AP as its attack target”. The effect itself isn’t amazing or world altering, but it shows that the majority of cards in this deck are going to call for or look for the Clan tag in order to operate. The LVL 4 GQuuuuuuX deals 1 damage to an enemy unit on Deply if you have other Clan units in play, while LVL 4 Red Gundam allows you to deal 2. The synergies in this deck are one of the reasons I enjoy playing it so much (aside from that I like GQX), and I think it makes the game easy to understand and has a very flavorful but fulfilling curve to the deck. If you’ve managed to keep your pressure on and get to the point where LVL 8 GQX hits the field, it can do a massive amount of damage to your enemy’s shields, as well as their units, and will often help swing the game very likely in your favor.

A unit you may have noticed from this list does, however, lack the Clan keyword: GFreD. GFreD and Nyaan both have “Zeon” instead of “Clan”, but there’s very good utility reasons to that. GFreD allows you to deal damage by thinning your trash, and since this deck lacks any way to get cards back from there, it makes for a great way to take advantage of an otherwise unused resource. GFreD is also just a pretty solid beater, and can bully other units fairly effectively in combat to help maintain a clear board.

For Pilots, your deck is going to want to include the 3 main characters from GQX:

4x Amate Yuzuriha (Machu) (LVL 4 Cost 1)

4x Shuji Ito (LVL 4 Cost 1)

4x Nyaan (LVL 4 Cost 1)

All three of these pilots have the Newytype keyword, although at the moment Clan really doesn’t interact with that; and as mentioned above, Nyaan is the one who lacks Clan in her own keyword, but her card does interact with Clan itself by allowing you to remove cards from your Deck to deal 1 damage to an enemy unit. Since your deck is literally nothing but Clan and Zeon, this effect will always activate when you Link Nyaan, making her one of the best cards in your deck despite the fact that she doesn’t inherently use the deck keyword.

Machu allows you to deck fix when linked, letting you see the top card of your deck and either reveal it and place it in your hand, or put it on the bottom of your deck. Since this deck lacks any real draw engine, Machu’s ability is a great way to start fishing for what you want to draw next in your upcoming turns. Shuji has a similar ability, although his activates both when you link AND when you attack, making his ability slightly more valuable than Machu’s, but both will help you cycle your deck try and make getting what you need easier. Gundam really tends to lack heavy draw effects, and almost never anything that shuffles, so having the ability to thin your deck with your pilots is very effective at digging for the thing you really need mid-game.

The final cards in this deck are your Bases, running no Command cards:

2x Clan Battle (LVL 3 Cost 1)

4x Shuji’s Hideout (LVL 3 Cost 1)

Your bases are pretty standard, and perhaps the weakest part of the current deck. Where some of the more meta decks have far more impactful bases, Clan has either Clan Battle, which can give AP+2, or Shuji’s Hideout, which does damage when destroyed (on the caveat that the enemy has to be LVL 4 or lower).

Planning Ahead

Following the release of Freedom Ascension, Bandai already began teasing new upcoming Starter Decks that would come with the next set, specifically Raging Onslaught and Silent Barrage, both of which seem to hint at some new color cards for Red and Green. We’ll have to wait and see if we actually get new Clan cards in these, but some new Red/Green toys for this deck to tinker with would be greatly appreciated, and perhaps the next set will also dangle some newer cards for us.

In the event that Clan is more or less left adrift, that’s not exactly terrible either; if the deck itself won’t work in the meta, there are a lot of other decks that can potentially be built into out of our deck; as mentioned, both of the new coming Starter Decks use either one or both of the colors in this deck, and there’s already quite a few decks that are trying to use Nyaan and Omega Psycommu in various decks, meaning that these cards do absolutely have some life in the current state of the game. Perhaps as we inch closer to September, we’ll start getting more decklists and ideas from these new starter decks, but until then, I urge you to consider giving Clan a try! If you want a solid mid-range color combo or just want to try a new deck, I think Clan is a fun and competitive build that doesn’t require quite as much investment as some of the stronger decks in the meta.
