[Warmachine] Tabletop Battles Reviews: Fane of Nyrro Court of Shadows Rules

July 28th, 2026 via Goonhammer

The vampiric menace of the Fane of Nyrro have been making big waves this summer in the world of Warmachine. The first two releases for the new faction, the Executioner's Toll and Death's Whisper boxes, introduced us to two of the highest members of the Fane of Nyrro’s court: Their executioner, Hysene, and their spymaster, Ashmael. Every royal court needs its head honcho, its kingpin, its big boss. Enter Auricant Voralys, the oldest Eldritch in the Nyrro Cult, its head priest, and a very dangerous magic user. He leads the newest box of Nyrro’s dreadful warriors and beasts, the Court of Shadows, and today we’re excited to take a look at the contents of this box and break them down for your own undead conquests.

Before we begin, we would like to thank the team at Steamforged Games for giving us early access to these rules for this review. 

Warlock

Credit: Steamforged Games

Auricant Vorsalys

Trish: Vorsalys looks pretty potent.  There are a wide variety of spells in Fane of Nyrro that will benefit from Arcane Winds (looks at Thaelmara).  Being able to Blinding Light a colossal from 12 inches away, then Repo 3”, leaves your caster safe while crippling your opponent.  Blinding light us SUCH an amazing spell (I miss you Issyria).   Death’s Dominion combined with the new low ARM models being released will be potent as well, blast resist is a big deal when you are ARM 13.  I’m not sure how often Humble will be used, likely it will be cast with Hunger more due to its cost.  

Vorsalys feels like he will be very Hunger … errr um… hungry.  With Ashmael I didn’t find myself using his 6 cost ability often, and Hysene’s 7 cost ability is too risky for me. On the other hand Vorsaly’s 3 cost abilities seem amazing.  (Not using Ashmael’s and Hysene’s High Cost abilities may just be a reflection on me being cheap, who knows.)  Supreme Will letting Vorsaly’s ignore Spell Ward/Sacred ward will be really interesting - and if you don’t know about it before the game starts will likely lead to some salty feelings.  Based on the current wording, it doesn’t look like Supreme Will will bypass Arcane Negation on the Trike, Tyrant, or Cage Rager.  

Masterball: If Hysene is the Aggressive melee caster and Ashmael is the tricky mobile caster then Vorsalys is the CONTROL caster. All your warlocks have their own bonus Hunger generation to help power their bonkers abilities and for the lord of these eldritch forces, it's PLAYING WARMACHINE. The other two have generation issues turn 1 due to needing to engage the enemy (the difficulty of which is thankfully by design) but here you just need to contest objectives with one or more models and contesting/securing can happen any time during the game even before scoring turns on. This helps smooth things out early to power all the spells he wants. Like Hysene, he's Unstoppable, making it hard to pin him down with the added bonus of native Steady eliminating a massive amount of potential tools to take him out. At ARC 8 he will often be able to ensure his most important spells land by having the room to both boost and still camp well before Illimitable Power helps him get further spells off. Speaking of Illimitable Power, it's Vorsalys who casts the spell, so while point of origin is determined by the initiating model, it still follows similar rules and quirks to Channeling or Arc Node where the caster’s capabilities are taken into account. If the user of the Hunger ability is behind a cloud then they need True/Eyeless sight to see through it, but Vorsalys only needs True/Eyeless sight to land his nasty collection of debuffs against stealth and concealment.

Blake: Vorsalys is definitely the offensive control playstyle turned up to 11.  I recall early spoilers of Blinding Light, but Humble and Wither ensures melee armies don’t get to have fun either.  His high spell accuracy and Illimitable Power means that seeing 3-4 of these devastating debuffs on the board each turn will not be an infrequent occurrence.  I honestly feel his most annoying spells are Perdition, seeing how fast bats can go already between Lure, Huntsman’s Lure, Snatch and Drag, Provoke and the new Blood Shift (see the Lesser Eldritch), and Death’s Dominion, which is a massive boon for keeping things like Blood Sirens and the Devoted on the board longer, not to mention any future undead mercs Fane might see.  Never forget that a caster can Perdition itself, as Vorsalys is as potent in melee as you’d expect of any Fane caster.  I’d be remiss not to mention his Hunger condition of Dominion, which is probably the most reliable Hunger generation we’ve seen on a caster at the risk of putting your Eldritch in danger.  Remember that the unit attachments are easy ways to add Eldritch models to non-Eldritch units for purposes of spellcasting and hunger generation.

For all his power, Vorsalys does have some limitations.  Unlike Hysene or Ashmael, he has ZERO ways of increasing the damage of his army.  He’s going to have to have a plan for heavy armor, though his heavy focus on offensive control might make him the best place in the army to include a True Mercenary.  He’s also completely dependent on Hunger to actually channel spells, since he’s otherwise restricted to casting within 12” of him, and that’s after a pricey Arcane Winds tax.  He’s certainly not afraid to stand on the front line like any Fane caster, especially with Blind Obeisance giving him a turn of near-invulnerability, but Shadow Shift with him might be a less frequent proposition as every hunger you save for Shadow Shift is Hunger not used for his devastating spellcasting.  Like all the Fane casters so far, I predict that he’s going to feel overwhelming to opponents at first blush.  I’m looking forward to seeing how he evolves Fane lists and where he appears in a pair now that the Hysene/Ashmael power couple isn’t your only choice.

Swiftblade: Oh, Sevvy2, you’re as beautiful as the day I lost you. And weirdly more Undead.

Vorsalys seems to round out the caster cast nicely in Fane, focusing on arcane might versus Hysene’s martial prowess and Ashmael’s scenario trickery. It’s that spellcasting strength that most excites me about his kit, as he can use his hunger abilities to both boost the power of his spells and even turn off a target’s ability to ignore spells. He lacks the raw hitting power of Hysene, or even Ashmael when things line up for him, but he has such a wide kit that I think he will excel a huge number of matchups. Whether it’s weird angle spell assassinations or just turning off a whole ton of scoring ability on his feat turn, the Auricant will Auri-can do some major work.  

Warbeasts

Credit: Steamforged Games

Horkos

Trish: Defy the Void letting you shadow shift without losing a model feels amazing. It will in turn trigger outrage, bringing the powerful Bite up to POW 17. Combine that with Powerful Attack on the Powerful Bite, Ashmael's Stolen Fate (bonus dice for crits) and you now have a beast that will consistently hit and consistently get 4 dice damage.  Horkos is a little lower def than we have seen on beasts in the faction, but with Indomitable and 30 boxes he will not be considered squishy.  

Masterball: Filling a void in the line up, this fits the role of tanky bruiser cohort nicely. Unyielding brings him up to premium ARM with Outrage making him deceptively tanky at ARM 22 in melee that cannot be ignored by blessed and more importantly he brings that Unyielding to your Caster making aggressive front line options like Hysene even harder to shift and chip. Will be exceptional when grinding into heavy infantry and light targets but doesn’t want to get into Heavy cohort on Heavy cohort violence without support. 

Blake: Hey, that looks like a real beast!  Horkos is easily the toughest bat in the belfry, but it’s balanced out by being pretty pricey and absolutely sluggish by bat standards (SPD 6 with a 1” melee is slow on this army).  Outrage and Deny The Void is a cute little combo that gets you POW 17 attacks on the crackback (Outrage also makes him a solid Misdirection target with Ashmael) and the combination of Powerful Attack and Critical Brutal Damage on top of this gives you an opportunity to crank damage comparable only to casters and the Strygon Rider, but it is still restricted by MAT 6 and only being Fury 4 unlike its smaller brothers. Still, it’s definitely a stronger “hold the line” model than we’ve seen previously out of Fane and thus feels unique to their toolkit.

Swiftblade: I love Horkos, he feels like the closest thing to an old fashioned Heavy Consort beatstick we’ve got in the faction so far, and an outstanding hunger generator thanks to Defy the Void and Feast [2]. He can sit just behind the frontlines, let some shadow shifts hit him for chip, and then leap in to do extra damage once Outrage gives him some extra heft. Plus, Unyielding is a fantastic animus in a faction that wants to play forward with its casters. Despite being the most expensive Warbeast in Nyrro at 15 points, I imagine this bat-winged menace will make his way into many a list. 

Strygon

Trish: We’ve seen the Strygon.  It’s an interesting beast, and having put it on the table, I’m still trying to master it.  It's strange, because with some prudent planning you can mitigate its frenzy most turns,  (few Fane models in the first wave were afraid of a POW 12 hit), and it activates normally after frenzying.  I’ve been playing them with the Muzled head for the ARM and extra hunger, and experimenting with the Feral Conditioning, which provides Provoke, letting a friendly beast immediately frenzy.  Mix it with Vordak’s abnormal frenzy behaviors and you can create some rather strange board states.  I don’t know if it’s good yet, but it is certainly interesting.  I think I just need more games to really figure it out.  The Strygon in this box only comes with a limited loadout, but it can still be magnetized.  

Swiftblade: Like Trish mentioned, the Strygon here comes with a fixed loadout of Muzzled Heads and Claw Wings, making him a dirt cheap 5 points to field. This does leave you some flexibility with the conditioning, and personally I love using the combo of Blind Obedience and Muzzled to turn the Strygon into a pretty effective shield guard that also helps keep the hunger train going.

Units

Credit: Steamforged Games

The Devoted

Trish: First thoughts - a cheap shadowshift target!  Amazing!  The card could stop there and they would be taken.  Dark Consecration, letting the unit get even more powerful, and threaten 14” is terrifying.  I fully expect this will be the first unit added to most lists.  

Masterball: Fast, Def 14, and hard to snipe mean that these are often going to be used as a dedicated Shadow Shift squad and ideal melee punishment for aggressive opponents trying to poke at your caster. They work as a good deterrent, but without any shooting, it's 7 points doing very little outside of bodyguarding, especially since their vengeance is from Shadow Shift expressly. For some lists that will be more than enough, though.

Blake: To shift or not to shift?  That is the question as to whether this unit is good.  Inexpensive DEF 14 models aren’t bad and they are very fast, especially when allowed to use Dark Consecration.  If they’re not being a shift target though, they’re little more than fast stealthy contesters.  If there’s a need for a dedicated unit for Shadow Shift, these guys are it.  If you prefer your units to perform a bit less conditionally, maybe look at the next unit down.

The Merciless

Credit: Steamforged Games

Trish: Ambush, DEF 14, and CMA, what more do you want?  The unit is MAT 6 base, so if you do a bunch of combined two-man CMAs, you should get counts on her fingers, carry the cleave, carry the other cleave, carry the other other cleave four two-man CMAs if need be, making them very reliable at killing other high def/flank styles of units.  

The Merciless are also FA 4, and DEF 14.  I’ve played FA 4 DEF 14 units before - they are a royal pain for a lot of people to deal with.  Combine that with Death’s Dominion on Vorsalys to give them blast resist, and maybe mix in a Prophet, and you are going to have some really tough to deal with units.  Compare them to Quickfang Stalkers, a unit that is generally considered to be top tier - the Merciless are faster (due to RNG 2 on the weapons), hit basically as hard, have the same DEF, same MAT, 1 better ARM and you have a unit that will see play.  If you really want to push them to 11, add Prophet so they are effectively DEF 16, and Death’s Dominion for blast resist, and you will be asking some really tough questions.

Swiftblade: The Merciless seem a little underwhelming at first glance, especially since they’re one of the only one box units in the faction so far, with the other one being the Devoted who have immense Shadow Shift utility. At first, I admit this led me to dismiss them, which is a shame considering their big swords are absolutely my jam. I expected these guys to be Knights Exemplar, and they’re pretty bad at that, but when I stepped back and re-evaluated, I realized these guys aren’t can-opener glass cannon infantry, they’re Mechanithralls: They’re doing their best work when they’re getting in the way. And with their high mobility plus ambush, plus a great defensive profile, they do a great job at jamming up the works, with just enough extra melee punch that your opponent has to respect them at least a little. 

Masterball: All the DEF and cost of the Devoted with a little more upfront oomph in melee; this marks the first Ambush unit in the faction which they were sorely lacking for backboard disruption. If being a DEF 14 ambush unit is good enough for Pygs in Brinebloods it's good enough here and they are FAST to get into the good disruption spots out of ambush. With Vorsalys specifically you'll be able to get weird angles from spells behind enemy lines or at the very least threaten them.

Blake: If you were waiting for the dedicated melee unit that was going to give Hysene her melee frontline… these guys aren’t it.  Instead, these guys are melee skirmishers in the same line as Quickfang Stalkers or Reaver Skirmishers.  They only have 1 base attack each, but Cleave and Combined Melee Attack give them some flexibility in their targets.  What really impresses me is how fast they are with SPD 7, 2” melee, Pathfinder and this on top of all the other movement shenanigans Fane can pull off.  Add a Sythiss Prophet and Death’s Dominion and these guys can be the jamming disruption unit that the Fane Knights haven’t quite been fast enough to be yet.

Solos

Credit: Steamforged Games

Lesser Eldritch 

Trish: I’m not sure where the Lesser Eldritch will sit in final list construction.  Visions of Torment (an enemy within 10” suffers Torment) the Repo 3” will disrupt models that need healing, and will help maintain the hunger economy.  Puppet Strings is always a potent spell, especially for models that need a kill to trigger sprint or another similar ability.   I don’t know if it will be ambushing more, or played near your main force.  

Masterball: The other new Ambush option but now with Puppet Master and some nasty tricks. This is the first of two solos in this box without actual combat tools, so you really need to weigh their use in a world where they can't lock anything in melee nor threaten damage themselves. Blood Shift helps to both enable your army for offensive plays but also disrupt enemy objective scoring. Remember, if you can force one model in a unit outside of the 3”, the whole unit stops scoring that Scenario Element. This guy will be tricky to manage but the ceiling on what he can enable is sky high.

Blake: Puppet Master on a 3 point solo is kind of baseline fair for that ability, so looking at the other stuff is more interesting.  Blood Shift is just the latest in the growing number of “move you around” tools Fane is getting to be known for and is probably one of the biggest beneficiaries of Vorsalys’ Arcane Winds.  Visions of Torment is one more bit of Torment from a solid distance.  Ambush is quite interesting to me since with walk and Repo 3, you can contest quite far from off the side, and as an Eldritch model, contesting is exactly what you need to build Hunger off of Vorsalys’ Dominion where other models couldn’t necessarily reach.  Puppet Master alone secures its spot in many lists, but Vorsalys may be looking at multiples.

Swiftblade: It’s very interesting to me that this box doesn’t give you one or two, but three Lesser Eldritch in total, which as it stands is also the max FA for the solo. Vorsalys’s toolkit probably means that you’ll get a whole bunch of use out of all three, but I imagine the other two casters will still want to bring at least one or two, since all three of their magical abilities here are extremely potent. I’m expecting to see a whole bunch of Lesser Eldritch around. 

Thaelmara, Mistress of the Damned

Credit: Steamforged Games

Trish: Lorekeeper’s Hex feels very potent.  Taking over high powered models and using some of their most potent attacks, feels terrifying cries in Shadow Seraph. Mix in the ability to boost on that attack if needed with Words of Mourning, and you have some really scary setups.   If the boost is not important using Lorekeeper's Hex twice on different models, or Dark Seduction twice on different models (using Danse Macabre) and you can get some ugly situations.  Options like moving a model with Dark Seduction to allow for a powerful spray, then using Lorekeeper's Hex for that model to spray a second time, will be terrifying.  Additionally, some of the most potent abilities in the game can now become liabilities.  Imagine walking up to a Dusk Spectre, using Lorekeeper's Hex on it, using Words of Mourning to boost it, then turn around to unleash a spray with Crit Brain Damage on Moryane.  

As someone who has played with Dyssiss a lot, a quick rules reminder.  A model does not lose incorporeal with making a magic attack, only a melee or ranged attack, so against certain armies Thaelmara will be a tough nut to crack.  Sorry Brinebloods (I’m not really sorry and you smell like fish).

Masterball: The second model without combat in the box, but by far the higher upside of the two (which unfortunately comes with a deservedly higher cost). The pros and cons of her two spells could fill a novel and the built-in ways to make meaningful choices are very interesting to ponder, but where does she fit? I think the best most recent comparison in overall utility is the Guard Tower in Kithguard. Both 5 pts, both have insane utility, but both don't have a lot of meaningful damage so they rely on their “thing” to do all the heavy lifting for the points. I'd be shocked if she wasn't a competitive staple in the list pair but I don't think she's automatic in each list due to costs and the fail state into spell resistant/immune armies.

Blake: This is easily the most dangerous model in the box outside of Vorsalys.  Dark Seduction on a stick is already extremely strong, as anyone who’s seen the Blood Parliament can attest, but Lorekeeper’s Hex can create some scary situations for most 80mm models (Gallant is thankfully immune, as are all colossals/gargantuans) pointing their most potent guns or biggest melee weapons at anyone nearby.  Accursed Lore gives her lots of settings for her powers and those are going to vary widely (do you fish for a critical hit off Lorekeeper’s Hex or not?), but just abusing Passage of Shadows to keep your moderately good defense incorporeal model away from magic melee weapons is very good.  Thaelmara is the ultimate example of turning an opponent’s best weapons against them. Opponents hate that.

Overall Thoughts

Credit: Steamforged Games

Trish: Everything in this wave feels strong.  Like really really strong.  Fane have been doing well in events with half their units, and adding these units to the mix I’m starting to get anxious. Currently Fane have the highest win rate in longshanks, so getting access to an even more potent toolkit could spell trouble.  

Also - random thing I just learned that I am shoving in here if you happened to read this - YOU CAN ADD THE HUNGER CARD AT THE TOP TO YOUR ARMY IN THE APP.  Nobody told me, and I never would have thought you could, but then you have easy access to it to share rules with other players.  

Masterball: My views on this box vary based on what it's for. As an open and play 50pt army it's even rougher to learn on than the already-rough to learn on army boxes that preceded it. As a companion to a previous box to move someone from ~30 pts from the command starter to ~75 pt games, it is great at adding an absolute treasure trove of utility and additional options. As an addition to the current Fane line up it undoubtedly unlocks a whole new world of possibilities in list building. With these additions Fane feels much closer to a fully fledged army. Nothing feels drastically undercosted and nothing feels overwhelmingly oppressive (besides Vorsalys, but that's his whole vibe) and the new solos are distinctly (but still very impactful) utility tax. I'm excited to see how the game responds to all of these options and look forward to future releases that dare to explore concepts as boldly as this one does.

Blake: This is kind of a boutique unit box with some baseline strong solos, a unique beast, and a HIGHLY unique caster.  The box as a whole is clearly meant to work together, with internal synergies between Vosalys and the arcane attacking solos as well as Horkos helping make up for Vorsalys’ low army-wide hitting power.  The units and Horkos have specific purposes, but I think are less baseline than the likes of Fane Stalkers or the Klyvenesh in terms of just plugging them into lists and expecting them to do well.  The solos are the opposite of that, with both of them providing strong utility that can slot well into any Fane list.  Finally, Vorsalys is the ultimate personification of an offensive control caster, combining several of the most powerful manifestations of that we’ve seen in this game with a few new ones wholly unique to him.  Whether his reliance on Hunger and lack of hitting power enhancement proves to be a enough of a counterbalance remains to be seen, to say nothing of his ability to threaten surprise scenario wins with Blind Obeisance, the game has not seen his like before.  It will be interesting to see where he fits into it.

Swiftblade: Man, Fane of Nyrro continues to get some really exciting releases with every expansion. Steamforged really let themselves run wild with their rules design here, and there hasn’t been a unit yet that just feels dead on arrival. Everything feels very thought out and fleshed out, with clear roles and unique interactions. It’s hard to have a batting average this high with rules writing, bravo.

As for what's in the box, I’m chomping at the bit here to play Vorsalys and Horkos in particular, who both appeal to my kind of brainworms. Big spell slinger casters are a ton of fun to play, and Vorsalys looks like he’s taken that archetype up to eleven, as he brings to the table both incredibly potent battlefield control abilities and a pretty strong spell assasination threat at odd angles. And Horkos is just about the most straightforward beast we’ve got in the faction, as he wants to get in there, take some hits, and cause big problems once he gets mad. Plus, Unyielding is just incredible on Hysene, who is my special vampire gal. I’m very excited to get my hands on this box and see what kind of havoc I can cause!

       Bite and Hold

       Blessings of the Gods 

       Careful Reconnaissance

       Duck And Cover!

       Put The Fires Out

TOTAL POINTS  100/100

Blake: Movement manipulation and preserving your hard hitters is the name of the game here.  Sybaris and your solos allow you to bring in prey to tear apart with the rest of your forces while Vorsalys keeps the rest of the opponent's army off balance.  All the most powerful pieces here have ways to move around after attacking, rendering counterattack models afflicted by Blinding Light and Humble unable to strike back.  The Fane Knights provide your Eldritch backbone for casting spells and gaining Hunger while the Devoted serve as your safety net and counterattack for any enemies that do slip past you.

Final Thoughts

It’s a very exciting time to be a Warmachine player, especially a Fane of Nyrro player. The Court of Shadows gives us some excellent tools to add to any Fane list, from nimble skirmishers with The Merciless, powerful board control pieces like Thaelmara, and tough bruisers like Horkos. It helps that the bossman of the Fane of Nyrro, Auricant Vorsalys, is no slouch either, continuing the trend of Fane casters being extra dangerous and packing some of the best spellcasting ability in the game right now. As a Fane player, this is an awesome release, and for non-Fane players, I’d study up on the rules for the contents of this box; Vorsalys and his courtly gang are very strong, and you’re absolutely going to see them on the tabletop.
