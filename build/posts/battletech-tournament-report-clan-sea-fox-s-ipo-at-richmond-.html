BattleTech Tournament Report: Clan Sea Fox's IPO at Richmond Open 2026

May 30th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Client-Centric Solutions (The Packet)

The 2026 Richmond Open's Classic BattleTech tournament (also known as "Eastern Assault," one of the other "directional" tournaments Fortress Miniatures and Games sponsors alongside their flagship "Southern Assault" event) took place over five rounds spread across two days, Saturday, May 2nd and Sunday, May 3rd, in (the approximate vicinity of) Richmond, Virginia. The Richmond Open is something of a legacy event for the East Coast TTB BT crew: It's where Liberty's monster tournament-winning streak got started, and it was the subject of the first BT tournament report on this site which wasn't written by me. This year I had the good fortune to secure a plane ticket before Current Geopolitical Events spiraled to the point they've reached now, so I was able to join Peri, Liberty, and our non-writing friends Nem and Tentacle for RO!

This tournament's packet was equal parts refreshing and perplexing to build for. On the one hand, its TO, Thiel, provides his missions in advance, making prep a lot simpler, and his list-building guidelines are more permissive than a lot of what we encounter, offering a great deal of list-building freedom. On the other hand, his response to the Precision AC/20 and VSP spam my esteemed colleagues brought to his previous tournaments led to an unusual set of constraints his packet called the "Rule of Two."

In short, while there weren't any numerical caps on pulse damage or jump MP like there were at Brawl in the Bluegrass or many of the other tournaments we've attended, players were limited to a maximum of two units (mechs or battle armor, no vees at this tourney) with the capacity to gain a -2 improvement to accuracy through any means other than kicking (pulse, precision ammo, stacking Artemis V and AES, whatever), and a maximum of two units with the capacity to generate a TMM of 4. An accuracy benefit of -3 (VSPs, Pulse with a Targeting Computer, etc) or the ability to reach a TMM of 5 (jumping 10 or running 18) would consume both your accuracy or TMM slots.

I like the concept behind that constraint, but a lot of strays get caught in the net: The unreliable speed of a 5/8[10] mech counts just as much as a predictable 8/12/8 for the TMM constraint, and a mech with a random Inner Sphere pulse laser like the single ISMPL on the standard Perseus gets tarred with the same brush as something truly abominable like the Dire Wolf E. Its attempt to limit the abuse of edge-case mechs perversely encourages concentrating on exactly those kind of edge case machines, while pushing out much milder machines.

Richmond Open also had a 7k BV limit, which is around the point where you really have to start making compromises somewhere in your list. You'll... you'll see how that went for me.

It's a Paradigm Shift! (The List)

My own Richmond Open journey started with witnessing the power of a fully-armed-and-operational Gunnery 2 King Crab 001 while testing Brawl in the Bluegrass lists with Liberty. Long-time readers will recall that the TTB brain-trust has historically advised against paying BV for skills, but there's a powerful allure to the idea of shortcutting the Game of Better Numbers by simply starting with a statistical advantage. Friend-of-TTB Nem pointed the Sun Cobra out to me, a 55-ton Clan mech which vies with the JagerMech DG and the Avatar OG for the title of "minimum viable double-gauss caddy," but with the significant advantage of a targeting computer on its side. A Sun Cobra at 3/5 skills is only 2,012 BV, and its gauss rifles are just as accurate as those on Liberty's monster King Crab.

Thus was born my initial (serious) Richmond Open list concept, an "accuracy skew" list which I dubbed "Have You Considered That I'm Hitting You?"



Through targeting computers, AES+Pulse, and the stacking benefits of Charging with good Piloting skill (too good, in fact; I'd missed that the packet capped all skills at 3!), I felt confident that I could brute-force my way through winning trades on the power of my list's base accuracy. At the time of my trip to Brawl in the Bluegrass, I was bullish enough about that incarnation of the list that I went ahead and bought all the metal minis I'd need to run it while I was visiting Iron Wind Metals.

Unfortunately for my hobby budget and my plans, it was proven to me shortly after Brawl that "Have You Considered That I'm Hitting You?" simply didn't have enough raw damage output to compete, particularly in the context of a tournament mission which asked us to pop three CF 150 buildings - easy to hit, but tough.

I flailed around in a few different directions, trying to cling to the "accuracy skew" concept as best I could, until Liberty pointed out to me the beauty, the horror that is the Tiburon 2. The Tiburon 2, if you've never taken a look at it before, is a 97%-armored, 7/11-moving 35 tonner which is able to perfectly sink the heat from its four targeting-computer-assisted Improved Heavy Medium Lasers at a run, and carries some low-heat backup weapons it can throw in for added damage or cycle into its firing pattern if it needs to bleed off previously-built heat. It costs 1,691 BV, but it's one of the few light mechs which fully earns that kind of BV.

As the Tiburon is one of the few mechs Clan Sea Fox produces and doesn't sell to anyone else, this led me to experiment with their availability, and I quickly started drifting away from the "accuracy skew" concept. Occasional contributor Strobe's preference for fast striker mechs has been lurking in the back of my head ever since he defeated me in a MegaMek game a while back, and a list built around the Tiburon 2 seemed like the appropriate time to try the high-speed, low-drag lifestyle.

Ultimately, alongside the Tiburon, I brought a Gargoyle G as a cheap-for-its-damage-potential anchor for the list, the frightening output of a Mad Cat III 2, a Phantom E for objective play and mild backstabbing, and a squad of Salamander BA at 4/3 to threaten heat/swarming/kneecapping, whichever might seem scariest to an opposing mech.

I also pushed myself harder than I'd ever pushed before on my paint scheme, doing the Hard Mode version of Spina Khanate! Credit: Lynn B.

You'll note that while that list has significant speed, with the 5/8 Gargoyle being the slowest mech on the roster, it can't take much of a hit. A Gargoyle looks like a soft target even compared to some heavy mechs, particularly in the legs, and really pales in comparison to armor-forward mechs at higher tonnages. After it, the next biggest thing in the list is the 55-ton Mad Cat III. This Star also very much wants to be in your face, with everything it's got having an optimal range of 3 hexes or less. Still, it performed well in my test games, with truly fearsome damage output, and I looked forward to running it at the tournament!

We'll Succeed in Achieving Globalization (Travel + the Event)

I didn't get nearly enough sleep in the last couple nights leading up to my flight, but my actual travel went smoothly enough, despite the best efforts of Norfolk, Virginia's Division of Public Works. I'm accustomed to driving to events, and despite the hassle of going through security, schlepping around terminals, etc., it was refreshing to be able to read my way through a relatively short trip rather than having my eyes glued to the road for hours. I also got to check out Liberty and Peri's home FLGS, which was a fun experience!

Unfortunately, I violated the sacred ritual of the pre-tournament Waffle House by internalizing the "this is the time we need to leave by in order to make it to the venue" time rather than the "this is the time we need to leave by in order to have time for breakfast" time, leaving us underfed and almost late going into Day One of the tournament. In fairness to myself, I'd burned serious midnight oil getting my force painted for the event ahead of my flight, and I did actually need the sleep... it was just less-than-ideal timing all 'round.

The Richmond Open itself is a tabletop gaming convention, with plenty going on besides BattleTech: vendors, demos, other tournaments, painting classes, etc.. Aside from the painting classes, though, everything is in one big, rustic event hall, though - the kind of place which seems much more likely to host 4- H kids than nerdy adults. That led to pretty high background noise throughout the event... not enough to seriously interfere with communicating with your opponent in individual games, but enough to thoroughly ruin the TO's attempts to address the group as a whole and to leave me feeling a bit dazed by the end of the longer, louder first day of the tourney.

As a brief note, one of the things that distinguishes the Richmond Open from other BattleTech tournaments I've attended is that players have access to a single point of "Edge," BattleTech's resource for forcing rerolls of catastrophic results (e.g., a hit location roll which results in a headshot), to use across the whole of the tournament (in other words, not one per round, one per tourney [or, as it turned out, one per day, since the TO let folks who came back for Sunday refresh their Edge if it had been burned]). This is probably the last time I'll mention this fact in this article, since I never thought to actually use mine, and I think only one of my opponents ever used their point against me. In theory, having that safety net can help prevent Bad Feels, but in practice it didn't seem particularly impactful.

In any event, let's get to the games!

We'll Set A Brand Trajectory (Round One)

The first-round mission, "Stand Up Fight," was a straightforward King of the Hill mission on short-edge deployment, with the wrinkle that you only controlled the "hill" (an obnoxiously Level-2-tall building) and scored the full 20 points available if there were no active enemy units within 2 hexes of the objective. Otherwise, point control was considered contested and each player would score 10. This made it harder to win the mission purely on objective scoring (especially when/if battle armor got dropped on the point), putting greater weight on Liberty's favorite thing: Killin' Folks. Good thing I brought an extra-violent list, right? Right?

For this one I was matched into Sigma, a TTB reader, whose list I'd actually seen posted to our Discord (where you, too, could post if you supported TTB on Patreon!) before the tournament: the ATM-laden E variants of the Nova Cat and Linebacker, a Fire Moth H hauling around a four-man Elemental squad, and a salvaged-in Flashman 9M. His force had a really unique theme, with Raven Alliance Gamma Galaxy equipment weathered all to hell and back... since they were secretly Dark Caste pirates!

We pulled the canyon-laden "Sand Drifts" Battlemat for our game: In combination with the objective itself counting as a Level 2 building, the terrain divided the objective-control area into basically two tiny battlefields separated by a dividing wall.

This game was pretty well decided by one fatal assumption on my part: I entrenched the Gargoyle G right on the objective, protected by its cover, ready to blast him whenever he pushed forward onto the hill...

Any turn now... Photo Credit: Lynn B.

...whenever he pushed forward onto the hill...

Oh, come on! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

As you might be able to deduce from the photos, Sigma realized that since his Elementals alone were enough to contest the objective, he had absolutely no need to fight me on the ground I'd chosen to try to control the objective. I rashly thought I could force matters by pushing everything except the Gargoyle forward, but that just led to my lighter elements losing a lopsided shooting war with his full force while the Gargoyle was either shut out of the action completely or wasting its talents on killing his Elementals.

Games at the Richmond Open were fairly short, limited to eight rounds maximum and also ending immediately if either player had scored over 100 points by the end of any turn, but this was my one game at the tournament which hit pacing issues. With time expiring in the round and me clearly losing on kill points, I made a desperate bid to split fire between his Linebacker and Nova Cat, wagering that I could only win if I was able to at least cripple both of them. As it happened, I crippled... neither of them, and lost 77-64.

You Tried. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

I was harsh on myself during the lunch break, blaming my loss on my decision to split fire rather than trying to outright kill the more-expensive Nova Cat on that last round of shooting, but I calmed down and went back to actually run the math later, and... I'm pretty certain I was going to lose regardless of my actions at that point; I was just too deep in the hole. Spending too much time holding the Gargoyle G back out of the action was my real problem, not any last-minute fire allocation.

That was a rough start to an event, but I hoped to turn things around in round two...

...And Leverage Our Core Competencies (Round Two)

This was definitely a game decided in deployment. We were on the "Base Assault" mission, which asked players to try to pop their opponent's three CF-150 objective buildings (which would then explode in a 20-damage AoE affecting adjacent hexes), while protecting their own buildings. We deployed from the long edges of the map, and the neoprene we were on had enough cover that all three of my opponent's buildings were in hard cover on his side of the map, and mine, while a bit less protected, still forced certain shooting angles.

Christopher, my opponent, was running a fairly fluffy second-line Hell's Horses force: Spartan C, Thunderbolt C, Royal Sentinel, Jenner IIC (Standard), and two points of Buraq fast Battle Armor (the horsies). We more or less mirrored each other's deployment, with the bulk of our forces weighted to one end of the map and a token force elsewhere (for him, the Sentinel and battle armor facing the bulk of my forces in the "west"; for me, my Phantom and Battle Armor pushing straight up the middle).

The problem, for him, was simply that my Star was significantly faster overall. My Tiburon stuck to his Sentinel until it died, and even with it threatening the gauss I was able to rush my Gargoyle and Mad Cat III past it to start putting damage into his "westernmost" objective building. Meanwhile, the Phantom immediately dropped my Salamanders on his center objective building to start putting damage into it and then shifted focus to interdicting his Jenner IIC.

Two forces on almost entirely different ends of the map! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

With my forces concentrated in the "west," his Spartan and Thunderbolt ran up the far "eastern" edge to put fire into my "easternmost" objective building. They succeeded in blowing it by Round 3, but once they had, they were overheated and out of position to affect the rest of the fight. My Gargoyle finished his "western" objective the same round, with the Mad Cat III already moving on to the center objective, which I finished on Round 4. By Round 5 I was overheating the Mad Cat III to pour damage as quickly as possible into his eastern objective while my Tiburon finished off his wounded Jenner IIC and his heavier assets largely failed to reposition in any particularly useful way.

He was still in the wrong place, and I'd made it the wrong time. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Now, we did both miss a vital component of the mission rules: Although each objective building was ostensibly worth 30 VP, scoring from the Primary Objective was limited to 70 VP total (as, in fact, it was in every mission). We mistakenly gave me the full 30 for the third building I destroyed, taking me over 100 points overall and ending the game, when we should've gone on to play a sixth turn, with me slightly short of the points cap. Despite that significant (20 point!) mistake, I feel comfortable saying the result would've been much the same either way. My Gargoyle was somewhat beaten up, in no small part due to TACs which had more-or-less rendered its AP Gauss arm useless (but at least hadn't chain-exploded into killing the pilot, ha-ha, wouldn't it be funny if that happened), but the rest of my force had taken minimal damage, and I absolutely still had enough juice to take one of his heavier mechs and earn the rest of what I needed on kill score. In any event, it went into the record books as a 116-30 win for me. Things were looking up!

 ...By Awareness of Functionality (Round Three)

Unfortunately for me, something strange had happened on Table 10 during round two. At the risk of stealing their own battle reports' thunder, Peri had edged out a win over Liberty. That meant that with me coming off a lopsided victory and both of us on a 1-1 record going into Round 3... I got paired against Liberty.

Now, there were problems here on top of the "Oh god, I have to try to beat the monster Peri created in a lab to be the best at BattleTech" of it all.

The first was a product of the structure of the tournament. Eastern Assault was announced with a second-day cut to a top four, but at the event we were informed that since the field was a little larger than originally planned, there would be a top six cut instead (which unfortunately rather defeated the purpose of a five-round Swiss tournament, since there could still be two undefeated players at the end of the last two rounds), with the highest-scoring 2-1 player making it in the top six alongside the 3-0 crowd. Unfortunately for me and Liberty, my score in my first game was low enough that I had no hope of making the top bracket even if I should notch a commanding victory against him, while his scores were absolutely high enough to be in contention if he should beat me, so my best-case scenario for this game was basically just crab-bucket-dragging him down into the losers' bracket with me.

Perhaps the bigger problem was the pairing of mission and map. We were playing a revised version of the popular Karnov Down mission, running in off the short edges of the map to grab some downed VIPs and evac them to our board edge, neither of us had jump capability outside our battle armor, and we were playing on this map:

Oh no. We're in hell. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

If my second round was decided in deployment, it seemed like this one might be decided by the roll-off for deployment edge. And I won that roll (well, won by losing, as I'm sure Liberty will point out, because for some reason this tournament packet let the loser of the initial roll-off pick their deployment edge (as the "defender"] in [poorly-balanced] exchange for the winner automatically winning Initiative the first turn).

The Dasher II he'd brought as his objective dork was just a touch faster than my Phantom, and it was backed by an upgunned Phantom of his own (the formidable C variant), a Point of Salamanders identical to mine, and two of the meanest ATM OmniMechs in the game: the Nova Cat E and Hel C. The terrain on his side of the map was so rough, however, that I was able to get to my Karnov a turn faster than he was, and sufficiently mountainous between our Karnovs that we initially had no shots to take at each other.

Scintillating, action-packed short-edge gameplay. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

This tournament's version of Karnov Down created somewhat-unusual incentives: The two VIPs from each Karnov had to be picked up on separate turns, control of the Karnov itself became worth points on the turn after its second VIP was claimed (and only for that turn, or whichever turn past that point one player managed to assert control), and any unit carrying one or more VIPs back to your deployment edge had to actually leave with them to score. With the terrain such as it was, I was able to max out my own primary scoring with zero interference from Liberty over the course of the first three turns (at the cost of my Phantom leaving the map), and I was able to make it verrrry difficult for him to even the score by pressing my Tiburon and Mad Cat III forward and mobility-killing his Dasher II and Phantom.

Granted, my Tiburon didn't fare much better! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Unfortunately for me, these successes came at a cost. My rapid push up to try to deny Liberty primary by crippling his fast-movers meant that I basically drip-fed him my entire list over the course of a few rounds: First the Tiburon, which crippled his Dasher but got mauled in response, then the Mad Cat III, which crippled his Phantom... but got mauled in response. After that I moved my Gargoyle up in a bid to pop the ammo of the Nova Cat, which had defaulted to being Liberty's VIP transport after his faster boys got pasted, but the ensuing ATM onslaught, uh, chewed through the armor on the Garg's AP Gauss arm, set off a crit cascade, and turned Star Manager Dave's brain to soup. RIP.

After that I threw my Salamanders at him out of spite (arriving at the fight late due to having been dropped in a bad position, again, as I so frequently do with BA) and managed to get them crippled, too!

When all was said and done, within the eight-round limit of play, Liberty had exactly the right amount of MP to make it back across his board edge with his Nova Cat, tying my primary scoring and winning via kill-point differential with literally the last movement point expended on the last move of the last turn of the game. 121-88, Liberty.

Liberty McKenna cheered the Nova Cat pilot on from his Omen as she reached the finish line. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

He moved on to the top cut, and I ended the day with a losing record.

Liberty: This was a hellish game and an astounding showing from Lynn. Help from the map or not the only thing she did wrong was the drip feed, and even then if that Garg hadn't suffered the death of the APGR then I would quite likely've lost without some intense attempts at body blocking. I was genuinely an inch from getting drug down into the losers bracket here but I did yet still prevail and move on into the final bracket.

Tune in next time to see how that went. Hint: God I love ATMs.

The Fundamentals Have Changed (Intermission)

From the event packet, I was under the impression that the last two rounds of play on Sunday were only for the top cut, and the tournament would just be over at that point for everyone else... an impression which was not dispelled by the fact that a full table's worth of prizes were awarded at the end of Saturday based on everyone's placement in the rankings at that time. I'd secured myself a seat in a session of mecha TTRPG "Lancer" for Sunday morning....... but then I found out that everyone was welcome to play the last two rounds of BattleTech on Sunday, with the incentive for players outside the top cut being raffle tickets for a final prize giveaway.

In that moment, put on the spot and asked to decide whether or not I was going to continue in the BattleTech tournament the next day, I thought of you, reader. I thought of writing this article, and the bitterness of cutting it short, leaving potential unrealized. I thought about the fact that I could still leave Richmond with a winning record if I could just smash out two more wins on Day Two.

I cancelled my (free) Lancer ticket, and braced for two more rounds of BattleTech.

Liberty was already ready for a steakhouse dinner, despite the fact that the tournament wasn't over yet, and one of our tournament buddies was staying locally in Richmond rather than commuting in, so we ended up detouring to a steakhouse which was more equitably inconvenient for everyone. The ambiance was nice, and everyone else seemed to have a good time, but my overpriced meatloaf was more "loaf" than it was "meat," to a disappointing degree, and I'll confess I wasn't the happiest with another night of poor / abbreviated sleep.

We did make it to Waffle House in the morning, at least! Breakfast: Achieved!

It's Mission-Critical To Remain Incentivized (Round Four)

This round's mission, Communication Suppression, saw us back on long-edge deployment, with a goal of standing on three objective hexes we placed along the centerline of the map. Sam, my opponent, had a straightforward Inner Sphere lance with battle armor support: Banshee 3S, a Warlord BLR-2G (the one which can swap between two heat-neutral Heavy PPCs and ten heat-neutral medium lasers) at 4/4 skills, the Uziel 8S, and the Omni-Firestarter OB, plus one unit each of IS Standard Battle Armor [Magnetic] and Cavalier II Battle Armor [Flamer], each at 4/3 skills to threaten significant anti-mech attacks. Overall, his force was definitely slower than my force, but also much more durable.

This was another game of two halves. I was able to occupy all three of the objective points on the first turn of movement (thanks in no small part to my opponent miscounting and putting one of the objectives he himself placed one hex outside the Uziel's jump range), and I maxed out my primary score over the first three rounds, after which I tried to shift my focus to killing his stuff. That was... easier said than done.

I started off with great momentum! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Under attack from two significantly tougher Assault mechs, my Gargoyle scored a through-armor-crit on its own leg in the process of falling down on Turn 2, got kicked to the point of crippling damage that same turn, and was finished off on turn 3. On the other side of the battlefield, my Mad Cat III ate a TAC to the foot and fell over while dueling his medium mechs and was forced to play cagey in its remaining turns of existence... it did manage to kill the Firestarter through repeated head hits, but the Uziel found its right torso ammo SRM ammo bin and soon after scored the third engine crit to kill it.

Hold on, where's that momentum? Photo Credit: Lynn B.

I did take down the Banshee through concentrated fire from my Tiburon... or, rather, it took itself down, falling onto an arm I'd shot off and popping one of its own ammo bins, but the Warlord and Uziel stood resolute... and his battle armor was quietly evening our primary score the entire time our mechs fought.

He just outlasted me. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

When all was said and done, my early lead on objective points was again unmade by the hard counter of "getting my guys killed a bunch." Despite the fact that I scored 99 points by the end of Turn 5, Sam clinched the game that turn with 109!

Can You Visualize A Value-Added Experience? (Round Five)

Heading into Round Five, I was honestly feeling pretty bummed. The mission gave me hope, however: "Objective Raid" was a long-edge mission which challenged us to grab as many as we could out of three objective crates and then play keep-away with them. Our mechs would score every turn they could keep hold of an objective, but would drop it if they took a critical hit or fell for any reason. Speed, I wagered, was my bailiwick, and I ought to be able to do well.

Oh, what fragile optimism!

I drew into another Raven Alliance list: Lee's Charger C, Merlin C, Griffin IIC 3, and five-suit Points of Sylph Battle Armor (Enhanced) and Elemental III BA (Micro Pulse).

I had the great misfortune to win Initiative, and missed that since both his BA units were footslogging it, he had one more activation until my Phantom dropped its passengers, and since front-loaded Initiative in play for the tournament... he was able to move his Charger C and Griffin IIC 3 onto two objectives of his choice before I got any chance to respond.

It was at that point that I made observations which led me to a very bad decision. I knew from hard experience that the Charger C would be a problem, even with 4/5 skills (as Lee hadn't had enough BV to upgraded it). I further noted that its armor was really only reasonable on its rear, and that out of my force, only the Phantom E was fast enough to backstab the Charger on its first turn. Thus, I made the decision to grab the final objective crate with my Mad Cat III and sic the Phantom on the Charger alongside the Gargoyle. Meanwhile, my Tiburon would go after the objective-holding Griffin IIC.

Off to entirely the wrong start... Photo Credit: Lynn B.

My objective-holding Mad Cat III ran off to hide in hard cover, crucially taking its substantial firepower out of the game, and I went on an Intiative-losing streak, leaving my Phantom with nearly nothing to do for multiple turns (outside halfheartedly harrassing battle armor), as a river cut it off from the Tiburon's fight, and its range was far too short to effectively position to shoot the Charger when I never knew exactly where the Charger would be at the end of movement. I misread his Merlin's positioning as setup for turrettech in the backfield and tried to send my Salamanders after it, only to be wrong-footed when it jumped across the river to threaten my Tiburon instead. My Gargoyle, meanwhile, was inevitably losing to the Charger, between its thinner armor and its lower accuracy (since the Charger C has a targeting computer).

By this point both my Phantom and my Battle Armor were just swinging in the breeze... Photo Credit: Lynn B.

He did get cocky with his Griffin IIC and lost it pretty quickly to the Tiburon, but I barely had a chance to pick up its objective before the Merlin's humble Streak LRM 5 blew off its right leg via critical hit on Turn 4. My Gargoyle also got legged by a Charger kick, and when it struggled its way back to its feet anyway via Careful Stand, I accidentally gave the Charger (which was repositioning towards my ailing Tiburon) a back shot on it, which went internal on its left torso, and Lee rolled double-boxcars on the resultant critical confirmations and crit everything but one of the ERMLs out of that torso.

Pictures taken moments before... Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Ouch! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

By the end of turn 5, Lee had done enough damage to me to hit the 100-point scoring cap and end the game, while I was on 75 points, handing me yet another loss.

One-and-four win/loss record. My worst performance in a BattleTech tournament ever, including the one I participated in within a month of picking up the game for the first time. Ouch!

With Strong Commitment To Quality (Results)

I'll... I'll let Liberty and Peri tell you how they did (much better than me!), but I definitely landed near the very bottom of the pack. I scored more cumulative VPs than some of the 3-2 and even one of the 4-1 players, but I was joined by only two others in the 1-4-or-worse club in terms of game results (with the one player I beat being, uh, the only 0-5 player in the pack [but kudos to him for sticking it out all the way rather than dropping the tournament the second day, like a lot of the bottom-of-the-pack players did!]). That said, Eastern Assault's prize support was pretty nearly as generous as Southern Assault and Brawl in the Bluegrass's, and I was still able to pick up a whole vehicle force pack I didn't have yet(!), along with some cute resin biker infantry in BT scale.

I'd hoped to be in contention for paint honors, but there were a lot of really strong entries, and the field there was topped by the freehand cherry blossoms of Ranger Matthew's awesome Ghost Regiment squad!

The Lich was proxying for a Tarantula. Mechs painted by Ranger Matthew, Photo taken by Lynn B.

Also, shout-out to friend-of-TTB Tentacle for a striking take on Stone's Lament!

*cough* I pointed out this color scheme to him! Models painted by Tentacle, Photo taken by Lynn B.

We grabbed some pretty good Chipotle burritos "tube food" on our way back to (other writers') home, and my flight back to Atlanta the next day was pleasantly uneventful. My pride was certainly wounded, but it was definitely a fun tournament regardless!

If You Can't Think Outside The Box You'll Be Downsized (Post-Mortem)

"What went wrong?" is a natural question to ask after a tournament performance like that, and fortunately, as a proud member of Clan Sea Fox, I have an easy answer...

Eternal rivalry. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

IT'S THE BLOODY RAVENS' FAULT! Three out of my five games, three out of my four losses, were all against bloody SNOW RAVENS! Did you know they were the ones who genetically engineered the Diamond Shark to eat Sea Foxes? They were! They've had it in for us for generations! Clearly, my losses have nothing to do with me and everything to do with SCHEMING BIRDS!

--

Liberty: Heh heh, now I'm a scheming bird! :)

Caw-Caw, motherfucker.

--

*cough-cough*

*ahem*

Okay, seriously now... I played a bunch of tight games with high-scoring losses, and the actual problem in evidence was that I didn't have enough staying power. I think my play style requires at least one solid brick my opponents will have to chew on for a while, and the 24 armor on a Gargoyle's legs just doesn't cut it.

Primary scoring that caps out as quickly as Eastern Assault's could (with me hitting the max of 70 on turn 3 in both Round 3 and Round 4!) also perversely disincentivizes running up the score early... in both those games my opponent was able to take their time killing me and then make up the difference in primary scoring after my force was too dead to respond.

In retrospect I also note that the game I did best in is the game in which I kept my forces most concentrated; in every loss I divided my Star and let it get picked apart piecemeal.

I've chosen to take my performance at Eastern Assault as motivation to keep learning and growing as a player, and I hope this article might also demonstrate that there's still fun to be had, memorable moments to create, and lessons to learn no matter where you fall in a tournament's rankings!

As I write this, I've actually already played in the next tournament I had on my docket after Eastern Assault... I hope to see you soon with an account of my experience at WarZone Atlanta 2026!
