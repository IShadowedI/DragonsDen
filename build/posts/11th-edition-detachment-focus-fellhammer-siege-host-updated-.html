11th Edition Detachment Focus: Fellhammer Siege-Host (Updated October 15, 2026)

October 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
In this series of articles we take a deep dive into a specific detachment for a faction, covering the faction’s rules and upgrades and talking about how to build around that faction for competitive play. In this article, we’re covering the Fellhammer Siege-Host Detachment for the Chaos Space Marines.

The 10th edition release of Codex: Chaos Space Marines gave the faction access to a whopping eight Detachments, each with their own strengths and weaknesses. These can dramatically change how the army plays, as each one tends to push players to build in a different way. With the dawn of 11th edition, we're taking a look at how the Fellhammer Siege-Host plays in the new meta.

Changelog

  • October 15, 2026 (Current): Updated for 11th edition, and with a new list
  • December 19, 2025: Updated with a new list
  • January 9, 2025: Updated with errata, dataslate changes, and a new list
  • May 11, 2024: Initial Publication
Detachment Cost: 2 DP

Disposition: Take and Hold

It’s pretty clear from the art and the rule names that the Fellhammer Siege-Host is intended to represent the Iron Warriors on the tabletop, with their siege-and-trench warfare style of combat and their philosophy of “Iron Within, Iron Without.” As a result the Siege-Host offers some decent durability buffs over other Chaos Space Marines Detachments, though it’s not as vehicle-focused as you might expect. Instead the Stratagem focus here is on heavier infantry, with support for short-ranged shooting and skirmishing that benefits the likes of Terminators and cult troops.

The Fellhammer Siege-Host Detachment has had an up and down ride since its introduction. It has some solid rules associated with it, but tends to struggle when placed next to more offensively-minded CSM Detachment options. Chaos Space Marines tend to be more about offensive output than durability - something their Army Rule encourages - and so durability buffs can often feel like they aren't doing enough. That said, Fellhammer has had both a storied career in teams events, and has received a new look with the September 2026 balance updates, which made Chaos Terminators tougher and more dangerous.

Detachment Rule: Iron Fortitude

Each time a ranged attack targets a non-DAMNED HERETIC ASTARTES unit from your army, if the Strength of that attack is greater than the unit’s Toughness, subtract 1 from the Wound roll.

This is pretty solid. It’s a solid defensive buff that’s both good on your vehicles for ensuring that really big guns like Lascannons and Railguns only wound them on a 4+, and it’s great for making sure your Infantry aren’t going down easy to the likes of plasma guns and mortars. T6 Terminators having -1 to be wounded by weapons that are S7+ is a really great deal: Boltguns may have gone up to S5 but very few S5 weapons went up to S6 and now even S10 weapons will wound you on a 4+.

The big downside here is that it does nothing for you in melee, suggesting that Fellhammer Siege-Host armies want to scrap at a distance with opponents, or at the very least if they engage in melee they want it to be on their terms. That's a problem given the Stratagems in the Detachment, and the fact that Chaos Space Marines are a better melee army than a shooting one most of the time. But it's a problem you can work with, especially if you build around throwing out assloads of AP-2 bolter shots with Lethal Hits. 

The units which benefit most from this Detachment Rule are your more durable infantry: Chosen, Terminators, and Obliterators in particular can make quite a bit out of the extra durability here, and Havocs don't mind it, either. Getting it on vehicles is also pretty great.

Iron Warriors Chaos Lord and Space Marines. Credit: SRM

Enhancements

There are a few interesting options here to consider. None are generically good; they’re more options you have to build around a bit. The good news is that they’re all pretty inexpensive. Iron Artifice is great for turning a Chaos Lord into an anti-vehicle monster, while the Bastion Plate can give you a solid durability buff on a unit of Chosen or Terminators. The missing piece here is something to throw on Terminator Sorcerers. Bastion Plate would be absolutely amazing if you could take it on one.

  • Bastion Plate (10 points) – Goes on a non-JUMP PACK Chaos Lord. Once per battle round, when you fail a save for the bearer’s unit, you can change the damage characteristic of that attack to 0. This is solid. You want this on a Terminator Chaos Lord, where it can combine with Iron Fortitude to create a really tough unit that can weather a bit more damage. This is also the good version of the rule, letting you wait for a failed save to use it, rather than wound allocation.
  • Warp Tracer (20 points) – In your Shooting phase, after the bearer shoots, pick an enemy unit hit by one or more of those attacks. Until the end of the phase they can’t have the Benefit of Cover. At 20 points this feels overcosted, but avoiding the -1 BS from cover for the rest of your army can be a big deal. The only problem is wasting an entire unit's shooting to get this. I like this on a Lord Discordant as a fast, durable unit who can pack a flamer (to automatically let the Tracer go off), and clear the way for Terminators to do their thing.
  • Ironbound Enmity (15 points) – Each time the bearer makes an attack while within range of an objective marker, add 1 to the Wound roll. This is okay. It's going to do its best work on one of the big characters, and it’s a good way to “have your cake and eat it too” with a Daemon Prince, where you can give them this and the Mark of Nurgle for +1 Toughness, or double up on this and the Mark of Khorne for S10 attacks with +1 to wound, letting you more effectively hunt vehicles. 
  • Iron Artifice (10 points) – Infantry model only. The bearer’s weapons have [ANTI-VEHICLE 4+] and [ANTI-FORTIFICATION 4+]. Ignore the fortifications part. The best place for this is on a Daemon Hammer Chaos Lord or a Master of Executions, who can throw out dev wounds against vehicle targets on a 4+. It's also solid on a Terminator Sorcerer, who has his own Dev Wounds psychic attack to throw out.
Credit: Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones

Stratagems

The Fellhammer Siege-Host Stratagems aren’t amazing but they’re pretty useful, and they offer a mix of defensive boosts, spiteful retaliation, and a couple of solid damage buffs which rely on either your unit or the target being below half-strength. The big challenge here is that these tend to mostly be reactive.

  • Persistent Assailants (Battle Tactic, 1 CP) – Pick a unit in your army that was picked as the target of an attack in the Fight phase, but hasn’t fought yet. For the rest of the phase, that unit can re-roll hit rolls, and if your unit is below half-strength, they can re-roll wound rolls instead. This is a decent retaliatory move, and a solid consolation prize for eating some melee attacks before you strike back. You don't generally want to be getting hit first, but if you're going to get hit first this can be a pretty big deal. It's situational but re-rolling all wound rolls is something Terminators don't normally get. Of course getting re-rolls to hit is trivial for them, so this really only has value if you're below half strength. Good on Chosen, though.
  • Brutal Attrition (Epic Deed, 1 CP) – Used in the Fight phase, after an enemy unit picks its targets. Pick one non-DAMNED Infantry unit in your army that was picked as a target; after the attacking unit finishes making its attacks, roll one D6 for each attack, to a maximum of 6D6 per attacking unit. For each 4+ you roll, the attacking unit takes 1 mortal wound. Getting free grenades back on an enemy unit each time they swing at you - and most of the time you’ll take six or more attacks - and the ability to punish opponents for trying to charge you with multiple units is pretty solid. This is really great for putting extra mortals on vehicles, where something like a Rogal Dorn or Land Raider will throw out 6 attacks that do nothing, but then eat mortals in response. It's also good into big threats with 4+ invulnerable saves, like other Terminators or Primarchs. Less useful on Custodes, but still worth it.
  • Pitiless Cannonade (Battle Tactic, 1 CP) – Used in your Shooting phase. Gives a unit critical hits on a 5+ against units that are below Half-Strength. Situational, but very solid since you’ll know full well when this can be used. Remember that this causes your Dark Pacts to trigger on a 5+ and if you have a Helbrute around you’ll snag both effects. Good for finishing off that unit that might otherwise have lived with a single wound left and best with combined with Sustained or Lethal Hits and re-rolls. Which means it's great on Terminators.
  • Point-Blank Destruction (Battle Tactic, 1 CP) – Used in the Shooting phase on a unit that’s within Engagement Range of an enemy unit. Until the end of the phase, your unit’s ranged weapons (except for the blast weapons) gain the PISTOL ability. This can be yet another solid way to make an opponent regret trying to tie you up, though it won’t get you out of combat in time to move. I particularly like it on Obliterators, who otherwise don’t have a way to shoot out of combat since they lack Big Guns Never Tire, and it's absolute fire on Rubrics, Noise Marines, and Plague Marines armed with torrent weapons. This is amazing on a big unit of Terminators now that they have S5 AP-1 combi-bolters. Who needs to fall back when you can literally just shoot your way out of combat?
  • Steadfast Determination (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used in your opponent’s Shooting phase to give a non-DAMNED unit a 5+ Feel No Pain until the end of the phase. This is pretty good, and it’s incredible on 3-wound infantry like Terminators where it can help them power through attacks with Devastating Wounds. This is perfect for the kind of take-and-hold lifestyle you want to live.
  • Siegecraft (Strategic Ploy, 1 CP) – Used at the start of your opponent’s Charge phase. Pick a unit in your army and until the end of the phase, if an enemy unit picks your unit as a charge target, they get -2 to the Charge rolls (this isn’t cumulative with other negative modifiers). This one has somehow survived the changes which hit every other similar Stratagem, and it's the better for it - dropping -2 on a single unit can be back-breaking when you're trying to win the deep strike game against other terminator-style threats, and it plays very well on single big units of Terminators which may eat charges from multiple units while trying to own the middle of the table. 
Warpsmith. Credit: Rockfish

Playing This Detachment

Fellhammer is an odd Detachment. It's defensively-focused and highly reactive. Almost all of its stratagems require putting the game firmly in your opponent’s hands and that’s really not what you want to be doing as a Chaos Space Marines player. Doing mortal wounds or getting re-rolls after you’re being hit isn’t nearly as satisfying or useful as killing things before they get to hit you in combat. That said, the defensive options work very well for something like Teams, where you can be choosier about your opponents and take advantage of a potential mis-match between your units (lots of harder-to-wound 3W infantry units against anti-vehicle or anti-horde options). The format also plays a bit better in 11th edition, where being able to take the Take and Hold Disposition with a 20 terminators can play really well.

And that's really the rub here - your big play is to take 20 or even 30 Terminators here, clog up the middle of the table, and kill everything using your suddenly amazing S5 AP-1 combi-bolters with full re-rolls to hit and [LETHAL HITS]. You attach a Terminator Sorcerer to the unit to give them full re-rolls to Advance and charge and to give you +1 AP against a single target. Dropping 40 Terminators' worth of S5 AP-2 shots into a unit is more or less what Scarabs have been doing, and you trade off guaranteed AP-2 for guaranteed re-rolls to hit. You can of course, opt for 20 Terminators instead, in which case I'd recommend filling the void of the third unit with Fabius Bile and the Masters of the Maelstrom joined to a unit of Chosen - this will give you a unit that's potentially even nastier in melee (S6 Accursed Weapons) and also gives you access to extra Command Points. You can also take Huron as one of your leader options - getting Terminators to OC 2 is a big deal as at OC 1 they can be pretty bad at actually holding midtable objectives.

In either case, your supplementary Detachment Pick is going to be Cabal of Chaos, which supercharges the shooting attacks on your PSYKER units - aka the Terminators led by a Chaos Terminator Sorcerer - to S6, while also giving you access to Infernal Vigour and Wreathed in Warpflame to either ignore cover or regenerate dead models. As a combo, this is very nasty, though potentially not quite as strong as Warpstrike Champions. That said, it's doing different things and very well suited to the Take and Hold Disposition play style.

Plague Marines, Noise Marines, and Rubrics also do well in these lists if you want to bring some cult units along, as they tend to really enjoy being able to suddenly shoot their way out of combat with the Point-Blank Destruction Stratagem. They also really enjoy the added durability of the Detachment rule. The only downside is that these guys can't perform Dark Pacts, so get nothing out of Cabal of Chaos.

Strengths

  • Durability. This Detachment offers a solid durability buff against incoming shooting, and it's great for keeping key units around and works on infantry and vehicles
  • Terminators. There are some good buffs here for Terminators, who really want the Detachment durability buff.
  • Cult Units. The buffs here are great on Rubrics, Noise Marines, and Plague Marines.

Weaknesses

  • Melee. That defensive buff doesn't work in melee, making you vulnerable to being hacked to pieces by melee armies. The good news is you'll shoot your way out.
  • Melee Buffs. The lack of melee buffs pushes you away from some of the faction's best units and makes you even more vulnerable when getting charged.
Forgefiend. Credit: Rockfish

A Sample List

The Fellhammer Siege-Host has a lot more play now with Cabal of Chaos and if you like running Terminators and Chosen this is a list you're going to really enjoy. There are a lot of cool ways to tweak this list based on your preferences.

Jesus Caos' List

This is a teams list - not a shock for Fellhammer - but runs something I'd expect to see in most successful lists, i.e. 20 Terminators + Chosen. The decision here to run Huron instead of a second Terminator Sorcerer attached to the unit is interesting; I think he's a cheaper option than Abaddon and his built-in reactive move is interesting, and his +1 OC ability can be a huge bonus when trying to hold midtable objectives with Terminators who are otherwise OC 1.

Bile, the Chosen, and the Masters go in a Rhino. One unit of Terminators goes into Deep Strike. The Cultists hold objectives at home and do actions, the Bikes range out and hold expansions and do actions. Kravek and his Mutilators go into Deep Strike. You can't put all three in - they total 1,265 points - so you put one unit (Huron's) on the table in Deployment and walk them up to the middle of the board. Huron's unit having the redeploy makes them a fine candidate and you want to have the Mutilators able to arrive via Rapid Ingress so they can actually move and charge something of value.

Final Thoughts

The Fellhammer Siege-Host is a strange Detachment. It has some solid defensive buffs, but it feels like a set of rules more suited to Death Guard than the current Chaos Space Marines army, whose army rule pushes them more toward aggressive, offensive play and whose datasheets push toward fast melee engagement. That said, with the right units and environment, you can build some very annoying if not super powerful lists here and we've certainly seen players go the distance with a Fellhammer before. It remains an amazing teams play and a decent singles play, if potentially a bit worse than the Warpstrike Champions list.
