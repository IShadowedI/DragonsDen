Archetype

Extreme Devastation

Thoughts

Marshall continues the Necron theme he's been exploring this year, which is how best to use the high-volume shooting to obliterate foes. This build is, somehow, even more all-in on that plan than his previous attempts, bringing both terrifying turbo-buffed gauss volleys from Immortals and three different sources of massed Devastating Wounds to combo with Synergistic Empowerment out of Cryptek Conclave. That will annihilate pretty much any Infantry target you care to point them at (and hey, there's a lot of those right now), while the stacked output of all the Immortals can flense enemy vehicles to death. On top of that, there's a legitimately huge number of bodies here, and it can be very hard for foes to put down fast enough to avoid the grind, especially with Fall Back/Shoot on all the Immortals from the Wardens. As a last thing, the build also plays Priority Assets super well, having cheap tools for early scoring and a lot of ways to put plenty of OC on a point to get the final actions over the line. Very unique, very cool, congratulations Marshall!

Archetype

Cursed Herohammer

Thoughts

More Necrons in second place, this time on the popular Cursed/Skyshroud plan. Jacob's build leans especially hard to wards the herohammer angle, doubling up on C'tan, and only taking one unit of Lokhusts, spending lots of points on Skorpekh with Lords instead. I've definitely found the Skorpekh/Lord units to be a very neat little package, and love a bit of C'tanhammer, so this obviously appeals to me very much. Also intersting is the use of a Destroyer overlord just with 10 Warriors - not quite as punchy as Lychguard, but a lot cheaper, and great for maximising the value of the resurrection orb, and flipping points late game. This remains one of my very favourite archetypes to play, so I'm delighted to see it thriving in the wild, great stuff Jacob.

Archetype

Blightlord Vectorium

Thoughts

...and we immediately segue into more Death Guard, also on Vectorium but this time going for a big unit each of Blightlords and Plague Marines. Both of these were already pretty good, and boosting the baseline output of plague knives means you can fully build Plague Marines for shooting, but still get serious melee output from them when required. Both units also work very well with Mortarion's buffs, and for a faction whose vibe is slow and ominous, this build has a surprising number of ways to thwart the enemy's plans. We were bullish on Death Guard coming out of the updates, and thus far that's playing out. Congratulations Kass!

There's honestly just a massive amount of stuff here too, a lot for foes to try and push through, and the re-rolls on the Marshall units help it engage on multiple fronts better than many AdMech lists manage. Thoughts

Shooty and Meganobz Orks face off against maximum Wyches for today's showdown, playing a mission combo that rewards skirmishing in the centre, and tabling the foe if at all possible. Looking at the lists, if I'm honest, this ends up feeling a little mis-matched. The Orks have build a bit wider than normal, which can absolutely help in some games, but absolutely does not here. Smaller pieces like the buggies are perfect for the Scourges to pick off, earning points into the bargain, and the chunkier stuff like the Dakkarigs risk suffering the same fate if they're not careful. Sure they're good at killing elves, but can they actually find a high-value target before they're blown up in turn?

Shooty Ork pieces do, also, have something of a vulnerabiltiy to getting tagged in combat, and that's bad news because this is an extremely "tag you in combat" sort of Drukhari build thanks to its speed and access to A Challenge Met. Because of the way scoring in this mission works, if one side has an angle on cautiously picking the foe apart, there's very little reason not to go for it, and I think the Drukhari have the perfect angle here. The Orks best counterplay is probably to be pushy with the Flash Gitz - they can't really be shot at without taking a whole unit with them, and have enough wounds that they'll soak a swing from most of the Drukhari unit (even Lelith won't take the whole squad out), and then can plausibly interupt and wipe whatever has multi-charged them. Time that alongside an Ingress from the big Meganobz unit (whose Armour of Contempt is very good here) and maybe that provides some momentum for the Orks, but it's tough going. As is, the Drukhari won in pretty confident style.

Thoughts

Double Wych Cult gets its first winning showing, as seen in the Showdown. This setup provides such a selection of delightful Wych Cult tricks to deploy that it's very hard for foes to keep up, and unlocks a very favourable disposition to boot. Tapping in both Malys and Drazhar helps broaden the targets it can engage, which is helpful, and both those units are great into new Marines, so valuable in the new metagame. Cool evil elf stuff, congratulations Cameron!

The Best of the Rest

There were 3 more players on 4-1 records. They were:

  • 2nd - Lewis Archer - Orks (Wreckas/Bully Boyz/Dread Mob) - Priority Assets: A relatively go-wide dakka/mechanised Ork build, with a bit of counter-punch from Meganobz and a melee Deff Dread.
  • 3rd - David Leniewski - Grey Knights (Warpbane Task Force) - Purge the Foe: Big Paladins, big Purifiers and triple Dreadknight.
  • 4th - Craig Pendlington - Adeptus Custodes (Lions of the Emperor) - Take and Hold: Max Allarus with some Pallas and Draxus Custodians supporting.
