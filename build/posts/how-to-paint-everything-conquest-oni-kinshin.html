How to Paint Everything: Conquest Oni Kinshin

November 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

The Oni Kinshin - or, as they are more commonly known, the BIG HORSES - feel like exactly what the Yoroni range needed visually. Previously, it was a two-size faction - large and huge - which somehow made the 'normal'-sized brutes feel small in comparison to the towering monsters. Each brute was the size of a mounted knight, but you didn't truly feel that looking at the army internally until you put them up alongside their version of the mounted knight.

Enter the BIG HORSES. These guys are massive - in sheer mass, they're almost like two Warhammer Rhino tanks stacked on top of each other. Despite the size, the kit is as simple as all Conquest kits are - the designers are absolutely committed to each arm and leg being a single distinct piece, with none of the complex trickery you'll see in a modern 40K kit where one piece might be a chunk of arm, leg and backpack. For that, there are minimal options or wasted plastic here - you've got four rider heads, two helmeted and two unhelmeted, four horse heads, a choice of sword or spear, and optional back banners. The massive horse body goes together in just two pieces.

There's nothing that can be done to make the double base situation elegant. Just run a line of plastic glue between them and smother the bump with texture paint - or go for something abstract as I have here.

Basing

This effect is simple and looks very unique. Step one is to get some cubes. You can find sacks of plastic cubes of varying sizes on your dropshipping site of choice for cheap; get a bunch of different sizes and glue them down in varying patterns.

My original intention with this faction was to do sharp edge highlights for a Tron-style digital reality theme, as you see in the prototype model above, but I got sick of this process practically immediately and went for something simpler and more fun.

See, the whole litany we are taught about thinning your paints? Forget that. To get this effect, grab a huge dollop of paint straight out of the pot, slap it down on the base, and then draw your brush along while spinning it a little; this'll give it a nice jagged effect at the edges. Then grab another colour and slap it down in the middle of the still-wet thick paint and drag it along as well. They'll mix (but only a bit) and settle into a cool layered effect.

The specific paints you see here are Pro Acryl Blue, Turquoise and Magenta. You can do things like slam on big thick puddles and then turn the miniature on its side to let gravity smear the paints for you. Sky's the limit!

Here's the same effect on some Warhammer models, done with a different colour selection but the same idea. While you're doing this, you're free to get wild and experimental with it - add jagged straight lines for lightning currents, huge thick pools for footprints, or even do a normal-looking texture paint/drybrush/shade around the edges to make it look like this chaotic energy is really melting out of reality. Add some snow texture, add some flowers, anything you've got spare or any paints you've accidentally got two pots of. This one's really not about the specific paints or the recipe; it's about doing everything 'wrong' because the unreality of the effect is the selling point.

One piece of advice, though - something you can do to make the colours cohere is to keep them all the same level of saturation (Saturation: How 'intense' the colour is; mixing paint with white or black will change its brightness and its saturation as the pigment is diluted; mixing a paint with its colour wheel opposite will decrease its saturation and bring it towards a neutral grey). All of the colours in the Warhammer miniatures bases are all pastels; they look like they fit together despite the hue being all over the place because they're all similarly saturated.

Painting

The BIG HORSES are drybrushing models. I don't think there's any way around that, unless you're actively insane. The huge, thick clouds of rippling hair scream out for drybrushing the fur texture into place. As a result, I found these went up shockingly fast; there are no fiddly details or painting speedbumps.

For the drybrushing, for each layer my rule of thumb is to halve the total area that you drybrushed last time - so the first layer is 50%, then 25%, then 12.5%. The exception to this is the area of key focus - almost always, the face. The horses' faces are down low, but I made sure to drybrush them up to the maximum so that they'll draw the eye.

Pink Horses

  • Pro Acryl Magenta
  • Overall wash with Magos Purple
  • Drybrush Pro Acryl Magenta
  • Drybrush Army Painter Fanatic Diviner Light - the Pro Acryl line lacks a good, strong pink; the GW Screamer Pink/Pink Horror/Emperor's Children progression works for this too.
  • Drybrush Pro Acryl Fluro Pink. This is the secret sauce; fluro paints are real sometimes foods, but adding a supernatural pop to pinks like this is exactly that use case.

Blue Horses

  • Pro Acryl Blue
  • Overall wash with Magos Purple
  • Drybrush Pro Acryl Blue
  • Drybrush Pro Acryl Turquoise
  • Drybrush Pro Acryl Blue White

The crest into white is more severe than the pink horses, so don't go nuts with it, but we need it because there aren't fluro blues.

Riders

The same process as above but I stop the drybrushing one level early; if you look at the contrast between the pink rider and the horses that's all the fluro paint.

Bronze Metals

  • Runelord Brass
  • Shade Agrax Earthshade
  • Highlight with Vallejo Imperial Gold
  • Teal details are Vallejo Beetle Green. Since the details are so small you don't actually need to do anything else with this, just let the solid colour stand.

Blacks

Your usual Black/Dark Reaper/Fenrisian Grey triad, there's no improving on perfection!
