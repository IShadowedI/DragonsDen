Tabletop Battles Reviews: Horus Heresy Legions Astartes Primary Detachments

November 14th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Behold, Games Workshop has delivered unto the Legions Astartes a PDF containing new Primary Detachments. You can download it right now on the Warhammer Community site and I recommend you do. There are also new Core Missions for all Heresy players to use, which we will cover separately.

As you have access to the rules for free we can discuss them in full here. You can go ahead and use them straight away… or at least as soon as you can bring the required models to a table.

We also have a new Core Mission Pack and we’ll be looking at that separately. That affects all factions whereas these Detachments are only for the Legions.

We get five new Primary Detachments, all based heavily on what used to be called Rites of War in previous editions. The new Detachments are:

  • Pride of the Legion - Loads of Veterans and Terminators. 
  • Fury of the Ancients - Dreadnoughts.
  • Sky-hunter Phalanx - Jetbikes, speeders and planes. 
  • Armoured Spearhead - Tanks.
  • Orbital Drop Assault - Drop pods, with a caveat that you should agree ahead of the game before using it.

How to Use the New Detachments

You build your whole army around your Primary Detachment. One of these new ones would entirely replace your usual Crusade Primary Detachment. You’ll have a new Detachment with some troops and a variety of other stuff but you don’t get those usual Troop and transport slots that you’re used to and you may not be able to get them at all. All let you bring a High Command and two Command choices, one of which is Prime, except the Armoured Spearhead. That has three Command slots but no Prime slots of any kind.

Each of these new Primary Detachments severely limits the type of Additional Detachments you can take. They each tend to list a few things that are allowed but that leaves out an awful lot of stuff. Expect to find maybe one Apex and a few Auxiliary Detachments on the list for each.

None of these new Primary Detachments allow Allied Detachments, the Detachments that you get from your Legion’s Rite of War or the ones that characters like the Champion or Esoterist can unlock. They also don’t allow any Prime Advantage to add units to a Detachment in your army, meaning you can’t take Logistical Benefits or the extra units that people like Alpha Legion or Shattered Legions can unlock. No Assassins, either.

So now that we’ve got that out of the way, let’s look at what fun new things these Detachments do now allow.

Pride of the Legion

You get three Retinue, five Heavy Assault and five Elites slots. One of each of the Heavy Assault and Elites slots is Prime, which is great as Prime Heavy Assault slots are pretty rare. Additionally, Any unit with Vanguard (X) can swap it out for Line (1) during army selection. If a Traitor army wants to deploy a massive unit of True Believers Cataphractii with Line they now can.

Word Bearers Cataphractii Terminator Squad - Credit: RichyP

You also get to take a Heavy Transport for every Retinue, Heavy Assault or Elites unit you bring. However you can’t take any other Detachments other than Officer Cadre, Shock Assault, Heavy Support, Warlord or a Lord of War that has to have a transport capacity - which rules out most of them. You won’t be able to bring any tanks, planes (Except maybe a Caestus) or even Rhinos. You might also miss characters like Apothecaries and Techmarines, though you could potentially bring Commanders like a Master of the Forge to fix your Land Raiders.

The good news is that you probably don’t need any fancy stuff. There are a ton of options between Retinues, Heavy Assault and Elites, covering a lot of possibilities. Land Raiders and Spartans can also do a lot of work. You may well be able to get a 3,000 point army without any extra Detachments so you can save on any character taxes.

The army has to bring a Master of the Legion. It’s a little bit annoying to have to bring a Praetor, as you almost certainly don’t need to unlock an Officer Cadre, which is the only Apex Detachment you’re allowed. You could just have an Auxiliary Detachment with your Praetor. You could also have a Primarch as your Warlord and bring no other characters at all if you wanted.

Finally, your opponent gets a VP for every Elites or Heavy Assault unit they destroy. Not for Retinues, for some reason. This is another reason to have all your people tucked up safely inside Land Raiders.

The main strength of this list is probably the access to Line on Terminators and maybe things like Veteran Breachers. Line is a bit less essential in the new mission pack but this army can do any of the new missions perfectly well.

Fury of the Ancients

This gives you slots for your characters, a Retinue and ten Dreadnoughts, two of which can be Prime. There’s a new Prime Advantage Ancient of War, giving a Dreadnought Eternal Warrior (1), which is excellent.

Salamanders Dreadnoughts Credit: McWhat

Again you aren’t allowed much else. You can only have Command and Specialist units if they’re Battlesmiths. Your only other Detachment options are Techmarine Covenants, War Engines and a Lord of War with a transport capacity. You can add extra units with Prime Advantages, but only War-engines. And again, every Dreadnought that dies gives your opponent a VP.

You need a character to be your Warlord so you can’t only take Dreadnoughts. It probably makes sense to bring a Retinue so they have somewhere safe-ish to be, though the unit can’t have a transport other than a Lord of War. A Deep-striking Saturnine Retinue might be a way to keep your Warlord alive. They’ll attract basically every small arms weapon your opponent has, as they’re basically useless against Dreadnoughts. so they’d better be tough..

I know the community wanted this Detachment and it’s thematically very cool. But for actual gameplay I think it could be a problem. Someone can put down ten or twelve Dreadnoughts and not much else, which leaves a lot of units on the opposite side with no real way of participating in the game. It’s not fun to fire at something needing a 6 to wound and hoping the target then rolls a 1 for its save.

Sky-hunter Phalanx

You get the usual characters, two Retinues and ten Fast Attack slots, two of which are Prime, gaining access to a new Skilled Riders Prime Advantage, which gives them Shrouded (6+). I think it’s a slight shame this isn’t available for the retinue slots, as you’d think those guys would be good on their jetbikes.

White Scars Golden Keshig. Credit - Soggy

The restrictions here are especially heavy. You can only add First Strike, Combat Pioneer or Lord of War choices. Every unit has to either have the Antigrav sub-type, be a Flyer itself or begin the game in a flyer. There isn’t much you can do with the Recon slots from the Combat Pioneer Detachment, beyond sticking some recon guys in a storm eagle, I suppose. Your retinues can be the legacy guys on jetbikes, use jump packs or arrive in a plane.

White Scars get a couple more options. They can swap Retinue slots for Elites and take Golden Keshig units or swap Fast Attack Slots for Support Slots to get Kyzagan Assault Speeders.

I think this will create armies that are basically fine. I don’t think this lets you spam anything all that problematic other than the retinues, which are nasty but which any other army has access to anyway. Jetbikes and speeders are perfectly ok but every army will have some tools to deal with them. Spamming a lot of flyers could be nasty but eventually they’ll run out of fuel/ammo and stop turning up.

The army kind of relies on using the new mission pack. In the core rules Cavalry can’t score objectives, which would be awkward for an all-cavalry army. That restriction is now gone, which is very nice for jetbikes and speeders. You’ll need to be a bit careful about the enemy scoring Vanguard too easily if you put something like 3 bikes or a speeder on an objective though.

Armoured Spearhead

This really is an army of tanks and almost nothing else. You get a High Command and three Command slots, a Support slot and ten Armour slots. There are no Prime slots. You can add Armoured Support, Combat Pioneer, First Strike, Techmarine Covenant, Armoured Fist and Lord of War Detachments. However, you can only have units that are either Vehicles or have Battlesmith (X), meaning you are limited to having a few Techmarines here and there, who won’t have any units to join.

White Scars Fellblade and friends. Credit - Soggy

So what do you do with your High Command and Character slots? Well you stick Decurions in them. Tanks with Decurions can fill Command slots and you can have a unit with a Decurion Magna fill the High Command slot. I don’t think this actually changes those units’ battlefield roles, however, so a Lord of War with a Decurion Magna filling a High Command slot would still count against your 25% maximum to spend on Lords of War.

The final special rule is Squadron Tactics. Vehicles within 6” of other vehicles get +2 to their repair rolls. That’s really handy, obviously. You may not need to take many Techmarines to fix your tanks thanks to this, though it would probably be useful to have at least a couple here and there.

I think this detachment could be a bit of a problem if people decide to max out AV14. There’s nothing to stop you taking an army of nine or ten Kratos here, which would be rubbish to play against. The previous version had to be based more around Predator and/or Sicarans, which was a bit more interactive. I hope that people don’t treat this Detachment as a license to misbehave at their opponents’ cost.

Some of the issues here are with the rules for Vehicles themselves. In particular, the fact that smaller tanks like Predators want to sit still to maximise the power of their Heavy guns but big ones like the Kratos can cruise around and fire at full effect. You wouldn’t want to create a game in which tanks just sat still and shot at each other. Unfortunately that tends to push the Armoured Company player to field more Kratos and Super-heavies, though they are some of the least interactive units for opponents to play against.

Orbital Drop Assault

The Drop Pod Detachment is really odd and the rules don’t quite work unless I’m misunderstanding them, which is certainly possible. The PDF tells us that these are essentially a beta version of rules that will be published later at some point. I hope to see significant revision when that happens to fix some mechanical and balance issues as much as possible.

Credit: JD Reynolds

The Detachment is very varied. You get your usual characters, a Retinue, two each of Troops, Elites and Heavy Assault, one each of which is Prime, plus two Support slots. Every non-vehicle unit you take lets you bring a Transport or Heavy Transport.

There aren’t any restrictions on what you can put in all these slots. However, every unit with Deep Strike or Orbital Assault Vehicle has to start the game in Reserves and flyers have to start in Aerial Reserves. Everything else has to be inside a transport in either type of Reserves or else it’s immediately destroyed at the start of the game. So are any empty pods.

Essentially the deal is that all (or most of, see the table) your Drop Pods come down during Deployment rather than during the game. The Drop Pods’ opponent gets to deploy first and then you Deep Strike all your pods. You first drop in any Deathstorm Pods, shoot their guns and then bring in your other pods. Your other reserves arrive from turn 2 onwards, though any number of units can Deep Strike on any given turn, not just one per turn.

Whenever you Deep Strike a unit you roll on the Orbital Drop Table, which determines whether your unit arrives at all and how much it scatters. You get +1 to these rolls during Deployment, which is good as a roll of a 1 means the unit doesn’t arrive and no others can either. A roll of a 2 still means the unit doesn’t arrive and will have to come in from turn 2 onwards when normal reserves rolls happen.

The rules for the Impact Reactive Doors on Drop Pods say that passengers have to get out during the following move sub-phase. But the Orbital Drop Assault player is going second, meaning that during their opponent’s first turn their army hasn’t had a move sub-phase yet and everyone is still inside the pods. I don’t know if it’s intended that everyone stays in their pods through the first enemy turn, but that seems to be the case for now.

I think that by current RAW you can make disordered charges from the pods in your turn 1. All the restrictions on charging after Deep Strike talk about rules for during the turn in which the pod came down. The pod didn’t come down in a turn at all so that can’t apply. They’d be disordered charges though, due to disembarking.

It’s rough that the other army gets the first turn. You’ll probably have a lot of slow-ish foot infantry scattered around the place and your opponent will have a combined force that may be able to outmanoeuvre you. There’s a serious risk of having all your recently-deployed infantry killed as they clamber out of burning pods. Your best option is probably setting up close-ish to the enemy in positions from which you can take objectives and advance, rather than right in front of the enemy guns. Traitor players may wish to avoid dropping in too close to Custodes and everyone should be aware of the threats they’re deploying close to.

You can keep back some jump pack units if you want and try to do a mass deep strike on Turn 2. But the more of these units you bring the less you'll be dropping at the start and the weaker you'll be in those early turns. Deep Strike is still not great for Assault units as they can't charge when they arrive, so they'll never be in melee before turn 3 - after your opponent has had three full turns already.

I don’t know if the designers have thought through all the implications of all this. Deploying pods in the deployment phase is fine in theory but it causes a bunch of problems. For example, you can’t intercept pods that come down during deployment, because it isn’t your opponent’s turn so you’re not the Reactive player and can’t do Reactions. That might not matter though, as they'll do huge damage to your army as it sits helplessly inside drop pods.

So for now this is a slight mess that needs a bit more thought and clarification. That’s fine for what are essentially playtesting rules. Personally I wouldn’t go out and buy a load of drop pods thanks to this by the way, as these rules are going to be reviewed, hopefully soon. If you have a bunch of pods in a cupboard then by all means give this a go, then send GW your feedback on the game.

Final Thoughts

These new FOCs do a good job of bringing back the lists you could make with the old Rites of War. If anything, they’re more restrictive than those used to be and allow for even more skew. There are good and bad outcomes from all this.

Thousand Sons Legion Saturnine Dreadnought - credit: Colin Ward

Starting with the good, you can make very thematic lists now. New ways of building armies will breathe life into the game and give all of us different ways to play. I’m keen to see what I can make as a Pride of the Legion list for my Imperial Fists for example, perhaps featuring Dorn and Sigismund.

The problem is that skew lists don’t tend to result in good games. The majority of these lists are virtually immune to small arms fire from units like tactical squads. They aren’t necessarily more powerful than an army made using the Crusade Primary Detachment but they introduce an element of rock/paper/scissors to games that not everyone enjoys. Personally I like a game where I don’t know who’s won till quite late on and I’m not sure these lists provide that.

Much of this really comes down to players. If you choose to field a wall of AV14 or Plasma Contemptors you can’t expect to get many games, as people won’t like playing against you. Unfortunately this places the responsibility for deciding what’s fair on us as players rather than on GW, to whom we pay loads of money to write rules.

It’s interesting that they’ve given us these unfinished rules for Drop Pod armies. It’s an interesting insight into the design process I suppose. It’s also a good example of how the Heresy team can write an awful lot of words without quite managing to explain a set of rules. To be honest I’m not sure a drop assault can ever be a balanced rule for standard missions. I don’t think past versions were much fun either. We’ll see where they end up with this.
