The 29 most expensive Yugioh cards and prices

June 5th, 2026 via Wargamer

What are the most expensive Yugioh cards? Since the card game launched in 1999, collectors of rare YuGiOh cards have paid millions of dollars to get their hands on historic, storied, and unique cards. This guide profiles the 29 most expensive Yugioh cards ever, with the highest price they've sold for - and we've verified them all.

The most valuable Yugioh cards are a varied bunch. Some are powerful enough to have earned a place on the Yugioh banlist, while others are prized for their rarity or popularity. They all have one thing in common, though - they're far too precious to include when you build a Yugioh deck. If you are looking for playable copies, we've included eBay links to more reasonably priced versions of these cards.

The most expensive Yugioh card of all time is Tyler the Great Warrior, a one-of-a-kind card designed by a 14-year-old, which sold for over $300k.

The card, created by Tyler Gressle in 2005 as part of a Make-a-Wish request, is the rarest Yugioh card in existence - there is only one copy in the world. Gressle designed the card in 2005, when he was 14, and battling against a rare form of liver cancer.

18 years later, he decided to sell the card. The final auction bid racked up a whopping $311,211 (£249,793.51), meaning yes, this is officially the rarest and most expensive Yugioh card ever, period.

This valuable card is a wonderful memento of the Make-a-Wish Foundation's work, as well as Tyler's own journey. We don't judge Tyler for selling the card - medical bills are very real, and perhaps he wanted to move on from that stage of his life - but we can't imagine owning it. It feels a bit strange to know that such a sentimental card is stowed away in a stranger's private collection.

Back in 1999, Gemini Elf was the best level-four normal monster around thanks to its 1900 attack value. So though it's unplayable today, it was a landmark card in early Yugioh. That's likely why the 2001 Asian Championships featured the Alternate Artwork Gemini Elf T3-04 as its fourth-place prize card.

The Asian Championships tournament was held in Japan and only open to players in the Asian nations that had access to the OCG - remember, this was a year before the Trading Card Game was released in North America and the rest of the world. All five prize cards featured alternate artwork, a first for the game.

This version of Gemini Elf is rare and has plenty of historical value. These prize cards were presented to players by Kazuki Takahashi, the creator of Yugioh himself.

On May 8, 2024, trading card seller Vintage Card Japan announced via Twitter that it had sold a copy of the card privately for 40 million yen, roughly $254,791 by the exchange rate at the time. It's still only a Gemini Elf, but it's the most storied Gemini Elf ever printed.

Masahiro the Dark Clown is a quirky card with a strange history. It's one of three prize cards from Japan's 2001 Second National Tournament. First to third place winners were all awarded with a custom card featuring their literal face. Hence, this strange anime monstrosity was born.

The Second National Tournament Prize cards have a murky history. In a forum post from 2010, Konami's US head of development Kevin Tewart claimed the three cards were fake. However, the card was on the Restricted List for the OCG's 2010 World Championship Series, hinting at its authenticity.

Further authenticating the card is PSA, who seems to have graded exactly one copy. Presumably, it's this one that sold on eBay for almost $174,000 in September 2025.

Blue-Eyes White Dragon might be the most popular Yugioh card of all time, even if it isn't the rarest. A heavyweight both in the early days of the trading card game and the original anime series, it's still sought after today, especially if it's an ultra-rare mint-condition copy, like the Blue-Eyes White Dragon LOB 1st Edition.

Released in the game's first expansion set, Legend of Blue-Eyes White Dragon (LOB), this first-edition PSA 10 Gem Mint condition card sold at auction in October 2020 for an astounding $85,000 (£65,000).

Such rare copies don't always reach that lofty height. In September 2023, another Gem Mint 10 copy sold on auction house Goldins for $28,000, plus $5,600 buyer's premium - a comparative bargain.

This isn't the only valuable Blue-Eyes printing. Back in June 2021, a Chinese court suspended an auction after bidding spiraled up to a wild $13.4 million / £10.1 million, as people clambered to get their hands on an ultra-rare Blue-Eyes White Dragon with a 20th-anniversary card frame.

As the name implies, the Crush Card Virus Shonen Jump Championship 2007 was a prize card from a tournament that pre-dated (and was later replaced by) the Yugioh Championship Series. Originally, 40 copies of this rare Yugioh card were produced, before a further two were made for the SJC 2008 tournaments.

It's the scarcity of this card that makes it so valuable to collectors, more so than its functionality or popularity. In June 2020, a PSA 10 gem mint copy sold for $49,999 (£40,058.70) on eBay. Not bad for a card that spent most of its life sitting on the Yugioh banlist, before Konami rewrote its rules text.

Back when the GX anime first aired on American TV, Konami partnered up with toy maker Mattel to release a range of action figures. Available only from Sears stores, each toy was a popular Yugioh summon, and the packs came with a limited edition version of that monster's card. However, midway through the third series of toys, the accompanying 'MF03' series of cards was pulled from the product.

This meant that cards for monsters released towards the end of the run had particularly short supply, none more so than MF03-009 Cyber Dragon. For many years this promo card was practically a myth: although it was listed in official guides to published cards, there were no photos of it in the wild.

That was until 2020, when redditor loserchris (real name Kevin, no surname given) shared the news on the r/yugioh subreddit that he had found several MF03 series cards in his closet, saying "I don't even remember where I got them". The community was flabbergasted that an MF03-009 was tucked among them.

Kevin sold the Cyber Dragon via an auction on the YGOrganization Discord, and confirmed to Wargamer that he received a tidy $30,500 for the card.

The Seal of Orichalcos has appeared in several Yugioh sets, but that wasn't always the case. When it debuted in season four of the Yugioh anime in 2005, it had no paper counterpart. Upper Deck Entertainment decided to make their own custom version of the card.

This rare Yugioh card appeared only in UDE-organised "Duel the Master" side-events at tournaments. These were challenge games played against a master player, usually a judge, who wielded a stacked deck full of powerful cards.

The Seal of Orichalcos isn't really a promo, since it wasn't intended to make its way into the hands of players, and was never legal in any form of tournament play. This version of the card has an ominous final clause - if you lose against its owner, your soul is forfeit! Legend has it that only fifteen copies of the card were ever printed, suggesting it is one of the rarest cards in any TCG.

In 2017, Yu-Gi-Oh! collector Anthony Nguyen released a YouTube video revealing he'd acquired a copy of the legendary card. Once it became apparent that it was possible for collectors to get their hands on it, there were a spate of sales - with a 7-graded copy selling via Heritage Auctions for $26,400.

Often, when a trading card gets misprinted, its recipient's dismay turns to glee when they realize their misshapen playing piece is now a rare collector's item. But in the case of Dark Paladin, it's the 'correction' to that misprint that now holds immense value.

The first edition English printing of Dark Paladin from the 2003 Yugioh set Magician's Force used the incorrect artwork. Upper Deck Entertainment, the distributor for English-language Yugioh cards in the 2000s, corrected the mistake for later editions of the card. But it also offered players with the misprinted card a chance to swap their 1st edition Dark Paladins for 1st edition Dark Paladins with the corrected art.

The offer was advertised on Upper Deck's website, and in various magazines. But it seems like many fans either didn't hear about it, didn't bother to send off for the card, or thought the misprint would be more valuable in the long run. Very few copies were put into circulation - and now the corrected art version of the Dark Paladin is much rarer and more expensive.

The highest recent price we could find was a PSA-graded gem mint condition copy that sold for $15,000 on Fanatics Collect on May 11, 2021. Props go to the Reddit user AzulRyu who documented this bit of TCG history in exhaustive detail.

Number 89: Diablosis the Mindhacker was an exclusive card given to Yugioh Championship Series winners in 2017. The average duellist then had to wait four years for it to get a regular release in the 2021 set Brothers of Legend.

Most valuable Yugioh cards are rare but not powerful, but Diablosis is both. Its ability to banish cards from the opponent's extra deck, graveyard, and the top of their deck made it a disrupting terror in recent times, forcing many players to warp their deck-building process in an effort to counter it.

Fortunately, the aggrieved fans' prayers were answered when, in June 2023, the card was stuck onto the Yugioh banlist. It found its way to the Yugioh Master Duel banlist soon afterwards, in October.

Getting plenty of play will probably have boosted its market value at least a little, but ultimately it's the low supply, inherent to any prize card, that has made Diablosis so expensive. According to Heritage Auctions, which oversaw the sale of a copy for $10,500 in September 2022, only nine PSA 10 copies are known to exist.

Terrifying in appearance, that is; with no abilities and mediocre stats, this warrior wouldn't stand a chance in the modern TCG meta.

You might think being one of the two rarest cards in the earliest Yugioh set would be enough to explain Gaia's high price, with a copy selling on Heritage Auctions for $9,9000 in 2021. However, the other Secret Rare card from the set, Tri-Horned Dragon, commands a pitiful price in comparison, of just a few hundred dollars.

Just like other more common and yet more expensive Legend of Blue Eyes cards such as Dark Magician and Blue-Eyes itself, it seems it is prestige that accounts for the difference. Gaia the Dragon Champion is a far more popular card than Tri-Horned Dragon, appearing in the anime's fifth episode, getting more reprints and appearing in more video games than its less appealing horny lizard friend.

Blue-Eyes Shining Dragon is one of nine Secret Rares from Retro Pack 2, with a bit of extra prestige thanks to the Blue-Eyes connection. The original limited edition printing of this card was given to kids who saw the 2004 movie, Yugioh: Pyramid of Light, in theaters. But it's actually this unlimited reprint from a few years later (2009) that's most expensive.

That's because, even though it's 'just' a reprint set, Retro Pack 2 had an extremely low print run and was very hard to get hold of in America. Boxes were released only in Europe, and now easily sell for over $5,000.

Less perfect copies of this exact card can be found online for $1000 - $2000, but the most staggering sale we found was a PSA 10 Gem Mint specimen that sold via Fanatics Collect in February 2021 for $9,000.

There's a funny bit of trivia about this card. You have to tribute Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon to summon Blue-Eyes Shining Dragon - but the Ultimate Dragon wasn't released until almost two years after the Shining Dragons's first printing, meaning this iconic dragon was completely unusable! D'oh!

When Magic Formula was printed in the 2007 set Gladiator's Assault, it became a key part of Dark Magician decks, offering power boosts to either Dark Magician or Dark Magician Girl, and a handy life boost should it go to the graveyard. The problem for duellists was getting their hands on it.

Gladiator's Assault was stuffed with secret rare cards and had a legendarily bad ratio of rare cards to commons and uncommons. Magic Formula was incredibly scarce, and copies from that first printing are still highly sought after: a gem mint Gladiator's Assault Magic Formula sold for $8,700 on PWCC marketplace in July 2022.

The notorious Exodia is just as prized on the collector's market as it is by duelists in the Yugioh anime. This copy of Exodia is particularly prized for being a first-edition version that was printed in the game's original set from 2002, Legend of Blue-Eyes White Dragon. It sold for $8,000 (£6,050), thanks no doubt to being graded at PSA 10 Mint Gem condition.

Believed to have been awarded in Yugioh's first-ever national tournament in 1999, this curious card might be the most expensive Yugioh card of all time. Made of stainless steel, the unique one-of-a-kind Black Luster Soldier monster works differently to every other version of the card - since it's a Normal Monster, not a Ritual Monster.

According to Kotaku, the card was listed for sale for around $10 million back in 2013, but there's no indication it sold for that value. Elsewhere, a figure of $2 million is widely circulated, but this does not seem to have a verifiable source attached.

The above image is not the metal monster, but a paper duplicate of the card. 3,000 copies were released in June 2023 as a special promotion in Japan, to mark the 25th anniversary of the game.

How we check Yugioh card prices

Our guide is based on the highest prices each rare Yugioh card has fetched in sales we can verify. Every month, we review the most recent verified sales at various major online auction houses for collectibles, such as Goldin Auctions, Fanatics Collect, and Heritage Auctions. If a new sale beats a record, it makes the list. We also trawl eBay listings, but these sales are much more difficult to verify, and we'll never list a card's sale value unless we're satisfied the details are completely accurate.

There are cards that have been offered at higher prices, like the Blue Eyes Ultimate Dragon from the 2001 Asian Championships, but these don't have verified sales. The one exception on our list is the Stainless Steel Black Luster Soldier: this has never been sold, but it is such a renowned card that if it ever went to auction it would certainly become one of the most expensive Yugioh cards of all time.
