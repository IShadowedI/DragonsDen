Warhammer 40k detachments guide - updated for 11th edition

June 23rd, 2026 via Wargamer

11th edition builds on the Warhammer 40k detachments system from 10th edition, with some key changes: armies aren't limited to a single detachment each, and your choice of detachments shapes the missions your army faces in organised play games. Read on for a breakdown of how the new system works.

For anyone new to Warhammer 40k, the detachment system is actually pretty simple. Each Warhammer 40k faction has access to several detachments. These provides you with three kinds of bonus:

  • An army-wide special rule.
  • A set of Stratagems you can activate with command points.
  • A choice of Enhancements which you can purchase during list building.

In 10th edition you were limited to just one detachment for your list, and all detachments had a similar structure, with six stratagems and three to four enhancements. That's changed for 11th edition!

Warhammer 40k 11th edition detachments

There are two key differences for detachments in 11th edition 40k. First, you're allowed to take more than one detachment. Detachments each have a cost in Detachment Points (DP), ranging from one point for a detachment with narrow unit focuses, to three points for those that affect your whole army with significant boosts.

You have two detachment points in a 1,000 point Incursion game, and three points in a 2,000 point Strike Force game. Rules as written, this means certain detachments simply can't be used in smaller games. However, Games Workshop has stated on the Warhammer Community website that it intends to update the army building rules so "you can always choose to pick any one, lone detachment, regardless of the mission size, even if it costs more Detachment Points than that game might normally allow" - at time of writing, this hasn't been added to errata or to the Warhammer 40k app.

In addition, many new detachments are labeled with 'tags' - you can't have more than one detachment with a given tag in an army.

Enhancements and Upgrades in 11th edition

With more detachments in play, players will access to more Enhancements - but you are limited to bringing a maximum of two Enhancements in a 1,000 point game, and four in a 2,000 point game.

Some Enhancements are marked as Upgrades. These are available to non-hero units, and you can take up to three of them while only using one slot from your Enhancement limit. You still have to pay the points cost for each unit that gets an Upgrade.

The number of Enhancements and Stratagems is not consistent across Detachments. For example the Space Marines' Librarius Conclave has five Enhancements and no Stratagems.

Force Disposition

The second big change is the force disposition for your army. Each detachment has one of five force dispositions, which reflect its purpose on the battlefield. If your army has more than one detachment, you'll select one disposition for your army - this will be when you submit your army list if you're playing in a tournament, or before you start the game for a casual match.

In Chapter Approved missions packs, your primary mission is determined by comparing your force disposition against your opponent's force disposition. Unless you happen to have the same force disposition, you'll both have different primary missions.

Legal detachments

The main source of detachments for each army is its Codex army book, but GW has also released additional free detachments. You can find the supplemental detachments for each army in its Faction Pack, which is available to download from the Warhammer Community website.
