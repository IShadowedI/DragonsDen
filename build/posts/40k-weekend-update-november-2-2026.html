[40k] Weekend Update: November 2, 2026

November 2nd, 2026 via Goonhammer

In our Weekend Update series, we cover the big events and stories that are happening each week in wargames, so you can stay updated and informed every week. The 40k side will be mostly covered by me, Isaac Terada, while Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson covers the non-40k side of event coverage and news. 

Talavera Caps Off Weekend Big for Teams, Light for Singles

October ended with a surprising weekend that held only a single qualifying event in our sample - we typically only look at events with 5+ rounds and 28 players when we define our GT+ events - with only a single qualifying singles event for the weekend. Instead competitive 40k was dominated by teams events, headlined by the Talavera GT, a massive, 804-player teams event held in Talavera de la Reina, Spain, which marked the largest teams event of the year. This skew's the week's stats a little bit when it comes to understanding shifts in the meta, but you can rest assured that bikes and land speeders were still major factors globally.

Speaking of teams events...

Wargames Adictos Wins Year's Largest Teams Event

It was a particularly big weekend for six-man teams events, with Talavera and Super 6 both offering opportunities for teams of 6 to square off. The largest of these - and the year's biggest teams event (and also in contention for the year's biggest event) - was in Talavera, with 804 players split among 134 teams of six. The event itself was six rounds, leading to four undefeated teams at the end of the weekend, though ultimately Team Wargames Adictos won on points with a least featuring World Eaters (Recon), Imperial Knights (Purge), Necrons (Priority), Dark Angels (T&H), Orks (Disruption), and Thousand Sons (Priority).

Congratulations to:

  •  Gonzalo "Raven" de La Moneda
  •  Juanma Joker

for winning the event, with an additional acknowledgement for Team Euskadi, 3 Arpía, and Cónclave for finishing with undefeated records.

Team England Takes October Super 6’s Crown

The weekend's next largest teams event featured only a measly 144 players duking it out over five rounds with six-player teams. Team England won the event with a 5-0 record, edging out Team Morocco for the top spot.

The final large teams event of the weekend was the South China Open, another 6-man teams event with four rounds and 84 total players. Team 黑石要塞 (which I believe translates to “Blackstone Fortress”) won the event with a 3-1-1 record.

Dylan Cowan Wins Weekend's Largest Singles 40k Event

The lone qualifying singles event this past weekend was the Calling the Banners 2026 event in Coburg North, Australia. This 67-player, 5-round event was conquered by Dylan Cowan, who won the event piloting a Dark Angels Take and Hold List running Assault Brethren, Darkflight Pursuit, Stormlance Task Force, and a ton of Outriders and Land Speeders.

Funny Thing About Statistics…

Actually I think Deathwatch might be bad; no one even played them this weekend. Necrons also had a record low 26% win rate. Well, this might be because we tracked a single GT event this weekend of just 67 players. Tracking win rates in teams tends to throw off data since the pairings process can lead to some factions having unnaturally high or low win rates. Tune in next week where we will hopefully have much more relevant results to study.

The ITC Is Dead! Long Live the WGR!

The ITC has been an integral part of the 40K scene for a very long time, much longer then my personal 40K career. Players who have been following the scene will have noticed that the ITC changed hands a few years ago, moving from its previous owners at Frontline Gaming to Games Workshop as part of the new World Championships program. Earlier this year Games Workshop finally chose to scuttle the ITC, renaming the ranking system to the Warhammer Global Rankings. Over the weekend Best Coast Pairings put out a social post on Facebook alluding to new scoring in the new season, starting after this year's World Championships, though no changes appear to have been announced or planned at this time.

I am sure many people can remember a time where Games Workshop did not care about competitive 40K; those days are certainly gone and I am excited to see where they continue to grow in how they mange the 40K competitive scene.

Photo Credit: Mani Cheema

Player Focus: Mani Cheema

As the 2026 World Championships rapidly approach, we'll look at the top players on the WGR leaderboards to see who can have a chance to take home the crown. We already covered spots #1 and #3 over the past weeks so let’s look to spot #2. Mani Cheema is a veteran player well known in the UK scene and currently second in the ITC, with a top teams finish this past weekend at the Super 6s event. Mani previously won the World Championships in 2023 and I'd be shocked if he didn't make a deep run at the crown again this year. Mani is a jack-of-all trades player: You’ll never know what he might be on, though he's often seen running powered-armoured Chaos warriors, such as with this past weekend's Death Guard list.

Alongside our coverage of the what's-what of 40k each week, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson joins us to provide some quick updates on the movers and shakers in the competitive scene of some of the other games we cover here at Tabletop Battles!

Khazalite Holds Release over the Weekend; Wave Two Up for Preorder

It’s been a dwarf-heavy episode over in the Mortal realms, as the new Khazalite Holds faction has been front and center for Age of Sigmar since its announcement at the big autumn preview. For excited fans who are eager to bring the classic dwarf look and feel to AoS, this weekend marks two notable occasions. First is the wide release of the new army box set for the Khazalite holds, which offers not only a wide array of Khazalite models but also give eager fans their first chance to get their hands on the new Battletome.

Normally, we see a bit of a gap between an army box release and the wide army release, but in the case of Khazalite Holds the second wave of releases is hot on the army box’s heels, as preorders will go live on November 7th. There is an impressive number of models releasing this weekend as well, with the entire range of the faction shown off during the preview all included with the big release wave, rather than coming in a more staggered release schedule that we’ve often seen in the past.

If you’re interested in the newest Duardin face in the Mortal Realms, check out our review of the new Khazalite Holds Battletome to learn more!

Rules Updates for Age of Sigmar and Kill Team

While Warhammer 40,000’s rules update release last week was awkwardly released in little bits over several days, fans of Kill Team and Age of Sigmar lucked out and had their rules updates all drop at once last week, providing much-needed clarifications and errata for both game systems.

In the Mortal Realms, the bulk of the changes focused on the Fyreslayers and the Sons of Behemat. For the Fyreslayers, the faction keywords were updated across the board to change out the faction keyword to Khazalite, since the fiery-haired Duardin faction will be rolled into the upcoming Khazalite Holds faction, as well as updating how grudge token work in their regiment of renown. Regiments are also the main focus of the Behemat update, which introduces the new option for their Regiments of Renown to be led by Mancrusher Gargants to better accommodate 2000 point lists without dipping into Auxiliary options.

In the world of Kill Team, the highlights of the update were the changes made to TOmb World and additional errata for the Exodite Dragon Masters. For the Exodites, the new errata and rules commentary finally clears up some of the biggest lingering rules questions for the team, most importantly by making it clear just how exactly gaining cover works. As for Tomb World, multiple teams were updated so that breaching was brought in line with operate hatch for purposes of action restrictions or triggering ploys, and the pillars for Tomb World were updated to now count as cover, which opens up some very interesting new cover possibilities on Tomb World and could be a problem for shooting teams.

For more of our thoughts on the Kill Team update, check out our hot take on the changes!

Mike Stewart Wins Telford Grand Tournament with Sylvaneth in Quiet Weekend for Events

Things were relatively quiet over the weekend for both the Age of Sigmar and Kill Team tournament scenes globally. But just because it was quiet doesn’t mean there’s nothing to cover.

While not as expansive as one of Games Workshop's marquee Open events, the new National Tournament series in the UK drew some big competition this weekend in Telford. 24 players in total battled out over five rounds, with Mike Stewart taking home the gold with an undefeated Sylvaneth list that leaned on the big heroes of the faction, including Alarielle, Drycha, and Belthanos. Mike’s win here continues a trend of very strong Sylvaneth performances in the current metagame, particularly with the big tree heroes list archetype like the one run by Mike.

As for some of the other large tournaments this weekend we have two singles events with over thirty players to look at, one in Oslo and one in Mexico. While both featured at least one Sylvaneth 4-1 player, the tree elves didn’t take home the gold at either event, with Kuba Kostrubiec’s Lumineth taking top prize at 2D6 Carnage in Oslo and Matt Nguyen winning Warhammex GT 2026 with his Slaves to Darkness. Congratulations to Mike, Matt, and Kuba for taking home the gold at their respective events!

Warmachine: Protectorate of Menoth Covenant of the Flame - Scourge of the Unbeliever

The Protectorate of Menoth Return to Warmachine

One of the most notable absences from Warmachine MK IV, the crusading zealots of the Protectorate of Menoth, finally made their triumphant return to the game last week with the release of the defenders of the flame box set, alongside standalone releases for the Revenger Light Warjack and the Crusader Heavy Warjack.

The new Protectorate of Menoth faction, the Covenant of the Flame, is led by the ambitious firebrand Feora and her closest cronies, and on the table the play much like the Protectorate of old with new twists. Like their original incarnation, Covenant of the Flame loves controlling the battlefield and setting things on fire, with the Four Gifts of Menoth army rule handing out one of four denial abilities each turn within your leaders control range to force your opponent to adjust their plans, and each leader having a unique way their army can consume fire tokens to boost their own performance and potential.

For more on the return of the Protectorate and how they were designed for the current incarnation of Warmachine, we sat down with Warmachine’s lead designer Jamie Perkins for an interview where he provided some very interesting thoughts on game design, Warmachine's appeal, and his own history with the Protectorate.

The Girls Are Fighting (You) at the MCP Fall Into the Multiverse GT

Halloween has always been one of the biggest weekends of the year for super heroes. After all, when else will you see so many Spider-Men running around the streets, on the prowl for candy?

Well this year it looks like Crisis Protocol is loathe to get left out of the fun, and hosted one of the bigger events of the weekend over at Element Games in Manchester, the Fall Into the Multiverse Official GT. This 6 round event hosted 37 players over the course of the weekend, with all corners of the Marvel universe showing up to fight for the grand prize, befitting an event named Fall Into the Multiverse.

Once the dust settled, Jim Gradwell took the top spot with his undefeated Avengers Affiliation crew that featured exclusively some of the most dangerous women in Marvel. Jim’s list, which he named “Smashing 4th walls and sometimes Matt Murdoch,” leaned exclusively on the ladies of the Avengers to draw from for his games, including She-Hulk, Captain Marvel, Wasp, Valkyrie, and Nebula, to bring home the top prize at the weekend event. Congratulations to Jim for the win!
