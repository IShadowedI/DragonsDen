[AoS] Tabletop Battles Reviews: Age of Sigmar Fall 2026 Battlescroll

September 24th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Just a week before LVO, GW drops a surprise Battlescroll, just in time for list submissions. The state of the meta has improved a lot since Aqshy began, with a lot of armies falling within that desired 45-55% range. There are some pretty extreme outliers though, such Cities of Sigmar and Fyreslayers on the higher end and Ossiarch Bonereapers, Skaven and Stormcast on the lower end. So will these changes be enough to impact the tournament right before one of the biggest events of the year? We're going to go through what's hot and what's not below.

If you want to follow along, you can find the Battlescroll on the Games Workshop Downloads page.

Core Rule Changes

Not many here. Due to the absolute deluge of generic foot heroes the game is full of right now, and because list building as it stands can make using them difficult, a lot of heroes had a rider added that allows them to be a "sub-commander" for a lot of bigger heroes. There's too many to list here, but basically every generic foot and cavalry hero got this treatment, which is going to help condense a lot of listbuilding. Most importantly, it means that bringing a Wizard or Priest may not cost you an entire new regiment now.

Similarly, many Warcry warbands had their "cannot be reinforced" rule pulled. This isn't quite universal, but it's close. Again, we'll note any interesting ones in the individual army entries.

Order

Cities of Sigmar

Kicking off with some pretty big changes here. War Hydras have gained a lot of traction in the past few weeks, when people noticed that killing them is a gigantic pain in the ass. Previously, you got to roll a fistful of dice whenever it died and if you got any 5s, it didn't die. Very cheap for that, and could keep the enemy clogged up for quite some time for something not even that many points. In exchange now, it just has Heal (6) at end of turn, which probably means it's dead now. Being a legends model, GW probably prefers it that way.

On the points front, as expected Cavaliers took a massive hit. +30 for 5. The unit was far too cheap for what it did and given most people ran 2 reinforced units of 10, that's 120 points and probably will need to be sussed out what else can be done with the army. Along those lines, the Cavalier Marshal went up 10 and Steelhelms up 20, which is a lot for a unit designed to take hits and then die. Taken together these are huge blows to the current Cities of Sigmar meta lists and will likely radically change the makeup of lists going forward.

There are some point drops, as a consolation prize. One of the Cogforts, Zenestra, and Gallants getting some point drops, to name a few.

Verdict: Loser

Daughters of Khaine

Daughters are currently in the "Acceptable" winrate, but they are really only barely at 55%, so time for points hikes on the expected stuff. Morathi, Blood Sisters and Scourge of Aqshy Melusai Ironscale went up and that's going to hurt big time. You're looking at another 60-80 points on your average list (assuming 1 or 2 reinforced Blood Sisters) which is probably going to force cutting down on the chaff even further. Removing Morathi entirely may enter the discussion as her cost gets extremely high, but give how much Aqshy rules love her tail attack (3 Damage 6 attacks!) it's a hard argument to make.

Verdict: Loser

Fyreslayers

Fyreslayers have been suffering through most of fourth edition; as one of the last armies to get a Battletome they have had to struggle through a pretty barren list of warscroll options, only to get the shot in the arm they needed with Ghyran giving them a better Son on Magmadroth. Then punished for their hubris, the points have dropped down on basically everything ever since, to a point that combined with the Scourge of Aqshy buffs, the army has gotten a bit out of control.

A bit of tech that's become popular is the new Blazing Impetus prayer in Scourge of Aqshy. A free movement in the Hero phase, it's mostly fine except for when a unit drops in from deep strike thanks to the Runesmiter, and then gets to move 4", making a 5" charge a lot more reliable.

The point changes were pretty fair all things considered. Bump to Hearthguard Berserkers, Flameseekers (both kinds) and the Runesmiter. Foot Heroes can now join up with Runefathers and Sons as sub-commanders.

The Elephant in the room is, of course, the army is on borrowed time so they likely weren't interested in making too many changes, just enough to get through until the new Khazalite book is out and legal.

Verdict: Loser

Idoneth Deepkin

Real boring. Just some price drops on heroes nobody was using, and likely will continue to not use. There was a rules change to the Scourge of Aqshy trait Abyssal Dweller which used to subtract the battle round from the movement characteristic, likely changed because it was absolutely overbearing to slower armies like Nurgle and Helsmiths.

The army is in an OK spot, could be better but could be a lot worse too.

Verdict: Loser

Kharadron Overlords

No rules changes, 20 point drop on both kinds of big boat is nice, and Endrinriggers getting a slight drop is cute, might see more of them around as a result. Otherwise nothing too exciting here. The army is in the 45% win rate category but it's barely hanging on and might need help later.

Verdict: Winner

Lumineth Realm-lords

After a really strong start in 4th, and a pretty solid book, Lumineth have struggled a bit as the points kept going up. So now they're letting off the gas a bit, mostly focusing on bringing the points on Alarith stone units down, with cuts to Stoneguard, Stone Mage and Spirit of the Mountain. 

Ellania and Ellathor going up another 20 points is probably not unexpected, but  does hurt the most common list building. Thankfully, not much else in those lists is heavily impacted.

Yrdrilan Riverblades losing their "cannot be reinforced" rule is probably one of the bigger beneficiaries of it. It was a popular unit and being able to make them stronger is probably a good thing for trying to assassinate tougher heroes.

Verdict: Neutral

Seraphon

Seraphon have been eating pretty good since their Asterisms were updated, and also have a book coming soon, so it's likely they didn't want to make too many changes. Being able to put Skinks as sub-commanders under Slaan is nice, and a few point drops on Oldbloods both on foot and mounted might help their use a bit. It's fine, if boring.

Verdict: Slight Winner

Stormcast Eternals

I hate having to make judgement calls for Stormcast. Every quarterly update they fiddle with the numbers and drop the points on some unit I've never heard of and next week it's the new meta.

In this case it's nothing but point drops. Knight-Draconis and Stormdrake Guard going down is nice for people who miss running dragons, as they haven't seen play in a bit. This probably gets them a bit closer. Tornus the Redeemed sees limited play so hopefully a point drop will help him see more play. Gryphounds down to 80 makes them good chaff pieces, probably good play there. Knight Arcanum might be able to slip into more lists now that it can be a subcommander is cheaper.

The only thing that stings is Questor did not get the "cannot reinforce is gone now" bonus most Warcry warbands got, which probably dashed a lot of hopes at hearing that. Sucks for them, but that probably was going too far.

Verdict: Winner

Sylvaneth

Points hikes on some of the staples. Warsong Revenant went up 20 points but you probably still take him; as the army's lone priest you kinda gotta. Twistweald isn't required but was quite popular for its control ability and it did also go up 20 points. You can reinforce it, but you likely won't wanna do that anyway.

Being able to put the Branchwych in as a subcommander is a small bit of comfort,  since often people would have to make a detachment solely dedicated to it, so being able to go from 4 to 3 drops might make a difference in deployment.

Verdict: Loser

Chaos

Blades of Khorne

Pretty mild overall. After suffering from the Bloodbound Army of Renown being too damn good, and then nerfed, GW seems to have considered it safe to bring Deathbringer  and Skullgrinder out of probation, just a bit with a -10 to each. Otherwise just a small drop to the cannon and the Scourge of Aqshy Bloodletters.

Verdict: Winner

Disciples of Tzeentch

Some technical changes, Smoke and Mirrors had a minor change to wording to clarify "Set up" means "set up on the battlefield" to make it in accordance with usual legal ruling. The actual important change here is that Fate Points are now capped at 9. Aside from being a fluffy option, it prevents abuse around the lore spell Infernal Gateway which let you vomit dice at your opponent. Nine is still pretty good, but now you might as well start using em once you hit that number.

Small change to Flamers as well, their persistent burning ability didn't work when in reserves. Since it works when the unit was destroyed, it likely was intended from the start it'd work from reserve as well.

Over to points changes, leaning toward more hikes given the army's decent status. Points hike to the Ogroid Thaumaturge and both hero and base version of Chariot. Scourge of Aqshy Screamers were also way too cheap, so a slight points hike was fair. The Silver Summoner trait to summon replacement units costs points now, which is fair since it created a lot of extra value.  The Gaunt Summoners did go down, at least, and Tzaangors of all stripes remain untouched.

Verdict: Slight Loser

Hedonites of Slaanesh

I think we all saw this coming. No rules changes, but as one of the strongest books to come out in the past few months, there had to be hits on some of the stronger units. Lord of Hysteria and The Masque both went up 20 points and Thricefold Discord and Painbringers went up 10. For the Painbringers this is especially rough, since it was common to run 3 reinforced units of them. That said, there were a few cuts; Daemonettes went down 10 (and I think they're better than they're given credit for). The twins, Keeper of Secrets and Lord of Pain went down in points, so overall it may open up some new play options.

Surprisingly, Shalaxi was left untouched, which is nice.

Verdict: Loser

Helsmiths of Hashut

Helsmiths got a huge overhaul. The biggest issue with the Helsmiths right now is basically that everyone is using an Army of Renown and not the actual army. So all four battle traits in the main army were rewritten. The general gist is that all the previous battle traits required 3 Demonic Power Points on a unit to activate their ability. This is a huge investment that won't be useful until probably the second round of the game (And that's committing basically all your resources to one unit).

The new versions scale better. Amassed Legions now only requires 1 DPP and the benefit doesn't apply to just infantry, as long as the infantry unit has the DPP, anyone within 3" can benefit, very solid. Arcane Dominance gives +1 to cast/chant/unbind to all Wizards and Priests within 12" of another Wizard or Priest with a single DPP. A bit convoluted to set up but if two of them have a DPP, then every caster in your army can get the buff. Bulls of the Ziggurat gives +1" of movement for each DPP now instead of 2" for 3 for only specific units. Probably the best, especially now that it affects everyone. Castigation Battery gives Crit (2 Hits) to ranged weapons with 2 DPP. Probably the least exciting, and possibly worse than before unless you spike well.

Are these worth it? Eh, hard to say. I like them, for the most part. The issue is the army take so long to rev up that by the time you have enough DPP to do anything, games over. Tarr's Grand Forgehost gives stronger buffs off the bat, without needing to fiddle with currency.

On top of that, though, you also get some nice point drops across the board. Daemonsmith on Infernal Taurus and both Dominator Engines got a drop, both very usable units. Foot casters are, as expected, able to be included as sub commanders. So pretty solid all around.

Verdict: Winner

Maggotkin of Nurgle

Just some cuts on stuff people weren't really using. Point drops on Morbidex and Orghotts is potentially nice but the best changes are the Rotbringer Sorcerer being a subcommander for many popular heroes, and the Rotmire Creed is now reinforceable, making them a bit more scary.

Verdict: Winner

Skaven

Pretty solid gains here. Rat Ogors have been feeling the hurt since the Scourge of Ghyran Brood Horror left them behind, so they got a nice little drop to make up for it, alongside the vanilla Brood Horror. Huge 30 point drops on the Verminlord Deceiver and Screech the Vermin King also feel pretty nice as these are arguably the best Verminlord builds for the kit.

The most exciting change is for Krittok Foulblade who no longer has to pay a CP for his ability to use Three Clawsteps Ahead on two units. Maybe not an auto include, but that's a big buff if you have any important Verminus units. Warlock Galvaneer also no longer requires a die roll, since a 2+ on a Once Per Battle ability can hurt pretty badly.

Verdict: Winner

Slaves to Darkness

Brand of the Unaligned can no longer be used on a unit with an Ensorcelled Banner, to prevent weirdness with being able to change mark but keep the banner. While I don't think this was excessively broken, I understand how it went against the spirit of the rules.

In exchange, a fair number of points drops. Abraxia, Archaeon and Varanguard drops will likely be appreciated, along with a scattering of a few less-used units like the Vortex Beast/Slaughterbrute, Furies and Chaos Lord on Karkadrak.

Chaos Sorcerer Lord being a subcommander is pretty sweet now, as is the fact that Chaos Warriors and Chosen remained untouched.

Verdict: Winner

Death

Flesh-eater Courts

A fair number of drops, mostly on unused heroes. Crypt Horrors and Crypt Ghouls dropping is pretty nice. GW seems to want to push the infantry side a lot harder now that they sufficiently nerfed Terrorgheists.

Royal Beastflayers did go up, but they can be reinforced now, and I think they're one of the units you would possibly look into doing that for, so that's nice.

Verdict: Winner

Nighthaunt

Nighthaunt were beneficiaries of some big warscroll changes this updated. Death Stalkers, a not often used subfaction has gone from Retreat and Shoot/Charge (an effect they can access quite easily otherwise) to -1 Rend against a unit of your choice. Once you kill that unit, you get to pick a new one, which is nice. Nighthaunt lack real strong offensive options so this is a nice little perk for them. It's also surprising to see the change be so radical, as it is unrecognizable from the old version.

The Shadowy Aura went from a -1 to be hit when Redeploying (A very niche situation) to a 9" hidden aura for Infantry and Cav. That is a huge buff and maybe not as good as Ruler of the Spectral Hosts (which is free now, by the way), still pretty damn good.

For the Scriptor Mortis, the Sentenced to Eternal Torment ability is now for the rest of the game instead of the end of turn. Less clumsy sure, but I think I would have preferred to remove the 3+ (Or give a way to get around it).

Speaking of ways around a 3+, the Lord Vitriolic's Spectral Alchemy also got shaken up. Instead of on a 3+ for picking one effect or rolling two dice at random, you either get one of your choice on a 3+ or if you did any damage with the ranged attack, get it for free. While not getting two seems like a downside, a way to circumvent the 3+ is huge and you almost never took the roll for 2 ability. Was it fun? Sure, but so many of the effects were extremely niche and you risked rolling something like shut off wards on a thing without wards. It's for the best.

GW hit the emergency "Faction is in trouble" button here. They removed the cost of all the upgrades, and gave some points drops to unused unit like the Scriptor Mortis above. Pyregheists also can now be reinforced, which seems like a pretty good play if you still run these guys. Didn't even go up in points either.

Verdict: Significant Winner

Ossiarch Bonereapers

Huge win here. Ossiarchs are currently at the very bottom and so Games Workshop has hit the emergency "reduce the cost of everything" button. I mean just about everything was dropped with Necropolis Stalkers, Immortis Guard and Morghast (both kinds) being some of the big winners. Discounts on some of the bigger models like Katakros, Arkhan and Nagash as well.

There's a pretty big rules change, too. Relentless Discipline used to scale up, with reinforced units costing 2 RDP instead of 1. This also applies to  Katakros's warscroll relentless discipline as well. While it probably doesn't salvage his Warscroll ability, since the cost came from using high wound units (where you'd want to use that ability anyway) it's huge for the rest of the army. Bringing large hammer units like Morghast or reinforced Stalkers was a huge liability as they'd eat through your RDP very quickly. This may move the meter back toward more high damage unit instead of the Deathrider charge and retreat strat that the army has come to rely on.

As a small perk, all the functionally similar wizards being able to be a sub-commander is nice and may help them see a bit more play.

Verdict: Winner

Soulblight Gravelords

The Aqshy Vengorian Lordwas a bit overtuned, able to move D3+Fury Level Inches in the combat phase. The D3 has been removed and the ability is in the charge phase instead. This likely exists to prevent combat phase abilities that target an enemy unit to then effectively fizzle out when the Vengorian Lord moves right after, but the reduction in inches is the big change. Still powerful, probably still worth taking.

Some pretty big changes for points, both up and down. Askurgan Trueblades did go up by 10, but they can be reinforced now. They're probably the biggest beneficiaries of this change across the whole game, especially if you want to run Prince Vhordrai, who did come down in points. As did Blood Knights and Vampire Lord on Steed so a true Vampire list may be workable again.

The Aqshy exclusive buff The Never-dead was given a 10 point hike to 20 pts. A popular take on reinforced Deadwalkers, giving them a 5+ Invul save since those didn't go up, you'll probably still see this as it's a powerful combo.

Overall, pretty good changes, didn't hit anything crucial too bad and it's quite workable if you want to keep building to the previous meta

Verdict: Winner

Destruction

Gloomspite Gitz

Kind of a baffling set of changes here; Sunsteala Wheelaz and the War Wheelz heroes went down. Snarlpack Cavalry is probably the best decrease here, since we haven't seen those in a minute. Then the Scourge of Aqshy stuff went down.

Just...nothing exciting here. No new sub-commanders, not even a Warcry Warband to talk about.

Verdict: Yawn

Ironjawz

Gordrakk and both Megabosses went down. So cheaper cabbages, but the catch is you're paying +10 more for Brutez. GW seems to want to encourage you to use anything else, which I don't think is a hard sell. With Warchanter and Weirdnob Shaman available as sub commanders now, you can condense the list down a bit and make up for it.

Verdict: Winner

Kruleboyz

Shockingly, Kruleboyz have been mostly left alone. They tend to get swatted down once they start doing good, but GW seems to have taken a softer hand here. Some points reductions to the Killaboss with Stab Grot and Hobgrot Slittaboss. Monsta-Killas went up, but again, can be reinforced now which you might actually want to do.

Being able to put the Swampcalla Shaman in a regiment with one of the bigger boys is possibly useful, since it's basically your requisite wizard if you're not running Gobsprakk.

Verdict: Winner

Ogor Mawtribes

Ogors haven't been out that long with their new book, and everyone agrees they do not like Redd the Maw and Gluttons. Redd gives Gluttons their 6+ Ward, and the Gluttons themselves were far too cheap for how many wounds they get to have. Their save isn't even bad, able to rock a 4+ save. So they had to go up for sure. Hunters on Sabrefangs, the other popular unit in the few lists we've seen also went up.

A few random other units went down, probably to compensate and recommend more diverse listbuilding. These sorts of small tweaks aren't unusual in a new book as they both want to let things settle a bit but also be proactive with things that are wildly powerful.

Verdict: Loser

Sons of Behemat

Since the book is coming out very soon, they're not gonna make major changes. The changes here are largely maintenance. Due to the massive overhaul to the fundamental design of the models, a lot of currently existing content that is technically legal but clashes with the rules of the new book needed to be updated and now was the best time to do it.

Most of it is boring bookkeeping stuff, Mega-Gargant is word-replaced with Big, and Gargant is Word-Replaced with Little where relevant. Probably the biggest question was what was going to happen to the Aqshy Gatebreaker since it uses the old profile for a Gatebreaker. Naturally the answer was just to rewrite it to match the new one. 25 wounds, 10 control, and the new combat profile.

King Brodd's Stomp Army of Renown will remain legal, but it was updated to match the new book. As mentioned above, Mega-Gargants are now Bigs, and Gargants are now Littles. Rampages are "Destructive Impulse" and any removed abilities like Almightier Stomp are removed. It's difficult to say if it'll still be worth it with the new book, but it's at least still usable.

The last change is kinda surprising, the Grotmas Stumblefoot Gargant Regiment of Renown was removed, a first for the game. On the other hand, the new Tome has its own Regiment of Renown that's a single little Gargant, so there's not much reason for it to exist. So in the bin it goes, better with updated language.

Points didn't change at all, since they just got dropped so kinda pointless to change right now.

Verdict: Bookkeeping

Wrap Up

Honestly, a bigger change than I expected. The last change was massive, which was expected with the dawn of a new season. This of course wasn't going to quite live up to that, but it's still a lot of changes.

Games Workshop seems a lot more willing to directly tackle warscrolls and battle traits and do top to bottom rewrites if they need to. This is big for the state of the game, especially since many early Battletomes for the edition are a bit lackluster or to put it simply, boring.

I think these changes are going to be positive for the flow of the game, especially the armies that are struggling more. It's a harder read on the armies that are doing very well and needed a nerf, but it's a lot better to raise up the floor than lower the ceiling, unless the circumstances are very extreme.
