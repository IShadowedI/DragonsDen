Mayday, Miss Marcy! Gifted With Yet More Gift Questions

November 5th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Happy November, and if you happen to live in the United States, Happy Bluenami! November really has just started but wow did it start with quite an energetic start to things. We're back with another installment of advice that you can use, hopefully, as Mayday, Miss Marcy! enters into the final stretch of 2026. Funny to be saying that, as it feels like things just got started! Ah, the prism of time. Anyway! If you're new to this column, we take reader submitted questions and produce the best possible answers we can, and this week, we have... yet more questions about gifts! I guess I have to admit that I'm not surprised as the time for gift giving approaches, and maybe I have to say that I'm proud of people for trying to do it in advance! Always better to be safe than sorry.

If you'd like your questions answered here, please read the information below:

Have a question you want answered on just about anything you’d need advice on in this grand hobby space of ours? Board games, 40k, AOS, TTRPGs, MTG, anything and everything that we cover, we can help with!

For some examples, take a look at previous editions of our column at this link with a handy tag to see all previous questions and answers!

If you want to submit questions, we have a few ways you can do it. Patrons can use the Discord bot command to ask questions, which will be received anonymously!

If you’re not a Patron, or just don’t want extra steps, you can comment on this article, or e-mail me directly at marcy@tabletopbattles.com!

And now without further ado, let us move on to the questions themselves!

Gifted Student

Dear Marcy,

I'm writing today because I have a sort of odd situation at least by my own estimation. I am approaching graduation for my first degree this coming Spring, and this fall is very likely my last semester to work with a particular professor as far as I can tell. They've been one of my biggest supporters and also a semi-advisor, so I suspect I'll still try to talk to them but we won't really have any time to work together. I was wanting to get them a gift before I do, but I'm wondering if a painted miniature is... a lame gift for someone who doesn't play or engage with a hobby? But I have talked about it before and they've known me for a few years now, so I thought it felt more personal than a gift card or something.

Thanks,

Student of the Arts

Dear Student,

I don't think there's anything wrong at all with your idea! If anything, I personally find it pretty touching and personal. You are right, it is different than a gift card, and while most educators would be happy with some extra cash in their pockets (even in gift card form), it sounds to me like you want this professor to remember you. A painted miniature that you give them to remind them of the impact they had on you would probably make their day, since it also becomes something they could display in their office (either at home or at the school), it would serve as a pretty nice reminder that they had an impact and don't just sit around grading assignments all day for faceless people who don't think about them at all. Obviously, there's some limits or considerations here; you probably want to paint something that they might actually like, or at least, something that might appeal to them, rather than just handing them a painted Space Marine and going 'I made this for you'. So think out of the box a bit!

A Spouse's Dream

Dear Miss Marcy,

My partner does not enjoy Warhammer, and I've never really managed to get them to try the game or anything. They sometimes express interest in what I'm painting or the more detailed models that seem intricate, but even then they don't seem like they actually want to interact with it. However, I have been long wondering if perhaps I could get them into the game somehow. I feel like this is a type of question or pipe dream for a lot of people, and while I am being vague, I'm going to just say that we aren't the traditional couple someone might envision here, but at least in this aspect it is a one sided sort of hobby interest. I don't even really know if I want them to play/paint/participate, but it does feel like it would be nice.

Puzzled Partner

Dear Puzzled,

I love when questions are extremely vague and almost making it into a puzzle for me to decipher, but since this is all I have, I will hold off making assumptions; to be honest they don't matter that much anyway, but I can tell by the one comment you made that it is unlikely this is a cis-het man/wife sort of partnership, so if anything that is helpful if anything to avoid stereotyping. That being said, I think you are correct; over the years of doing this column we've had a few questions about spouse participation and disinterest in the hobby, and I think my advice remains mostly the same: you can't make someone like your hobby the way you like your hobby. They might like you, they might appreciate that you have a hobby, but they do not like the hobby itself, and you are unlikely to make them like it. When your spouse makes comments about the painting you're doing, there's a level to it that is perhaps simply signaling visibility: they recognize the time and effort you're making and want to be supportive or interested.

Don't worry about making them get into it; if they wanted to, they'd let you know more clearly. If you tend to do your hobbying in a room all by yourself or as a "you time" thing, they may also simply be respecting that because that's the vibe you've given off. If that isn't the intent, or you have the ability to, consider doing some of your hobbying (like building or painting) with your spouse nearby; perhaps talk to them and chat while you both do something different. And if that isn't what they want, then don't stress; you didn't say they are dismissive or hate what you do, just that they don't seem to want to do it themselves!

Tone Pro Policed

Dear Miss Marcy,

Am I weird for not finding the new tone pro paints interesting? I feel like people were buzzing about them a bit but I have not seen much, and I don't really seem to have any reason to use them myself. The armies I play don't really seem to use the colors people keep saying are must uses, and I really like the paints I already have. But when people talk about them and I say I'm not interested, it seems like I'm the weird one; is it just the idea of FOMO talking, or is not being interested in a new release like this just normal?

Painted

Dear Painted,

We had a similar question a few weeks ago, to which I offered the same comment: HOW are you saying you are not interested? Are you saying "I don't think they're for me" (fine) or are you saying "They look lame as shit lmfao" (not fine). Because one of these, as noted by the parenthetical comments, is a way of explaining that you do not feel interested in something for yourself, but that others may like it, while the other is more akin to the dismissive and generally abrasive tone people use online.

But assuming it isn't the latter, my answer is... sure, why wouldn't it be normal? You aren't required to be excited about things the way everyone else is, and when it comes to something like painting, it really just depends on what you already do and how you like to do it. If you've got a particular style and workflow set up, then you really aren't strange for not finding a new item of interest to you. You say that you don't think the paints would help you much, so that also feels normal; and considering the price of paint, there's really nothing weird about not wanting to speculate about spending money on a thing you don't want or may not use solely to say that you did use and didn't like it. Just don't be an annoying hater about things and you won't really have much of a problem; the biggest issue is not being aware of the fact that the things you say to other people carry more than just what it says on the package, so to speak; if you're being overly dismissive and rude, people are going to be weirded out by it since what you are doing is telling them that their opinions on something completely personal (a type of paint) is somehow not worth giving a normal response to.
