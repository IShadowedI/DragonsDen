How to Paint Everything: StarCraft Tabletop Miniatures Game Terran Medics

June 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Medics are the key support units for Terran armies in the StarCraft Tabletop Miniatures Game. These ladies have next to no offensive capabilities, but they excel at keeping your other units healthy, providing some light enemy harassment in the form of Optical Flares, and they manage your Marines' Stim Pack habit.

The Medic was added in StarCraft's Brood War expansion (whose plot we recently covered). The United Earth Directorate evidently didn't get up to a lot of research in the centuries between when the original colony/prisoner ships set off and when the UED eventually showed up in the Koprulu Sector, this and the Valkyrie were the only tech they really introduced. At the very least, they came to try to get little more female representation in, even if the sultry Medic isn't really an admirable example of that.

How Were They Portrayed in the Video Game?

"Need medical attention?" - StarCraft OG Terran Medics

Befitting their status as battlefield medical staff, the Medic is clad in a nearly white version of the Marine's power armor. They had the team colors on their shoulder pads, across the tops of their backpacks, the whole medical cross on their shields, and the bottoms of their greaves. This wasn't really changed in StarCraft: Remastered, though everything naturally has a little more definition.

"Did someone page me?" - StarCraft: Remastered Terran Medics

When they were developing StarCraft II, Medics were considered to be one note units. The designers wanted units to have multiple roles and viabilities throughout matches, so they decided to combine them with the other one note Terran unit, the Dropship, and the Medivac was born. The StarCraft TMG having a largely consistent model scale, unlike the video game, means we're probably unlikely to see MediVacs on the tabletop, but they will probably be introduced as a tactics card with a future biological unit.

"The doctor is in." - StarCraft II Terran Medics

The Medic models were available to use in the campaign mode, though, and this is the version that is used as the basis for the StarCraft TMG models. They are extremely similar to the Brood War version, but the backpack team color is now split into being at the top and bottom of two smaller reactors, the pauldron team color is now only on a little inset part, and they now have bright green medical goo shooters.

Painting Guide

For my Medics, I went with a combination of how both the modern StarCraft 2 unit and the Brood War applied their colors. I left the shields off for painting, as they're a real pain in the neck to paint around.

White Armor

This is the majority of the model's color (well, lack of in this case), so this will probably be the longest step. White is a notoriously hard color to paint consistently, owing to the relatively coarse nature of white pigments. It's best to start with off whites, and use pure white sparingly.

Here, I started by priming the model with a light gray primer, and extra attention must be given here to have a solid coat down. With other colors, it's easy enough to compensate for a little bit of the bright metallic blue sprue color shining through in the deepest parts of the model, but even the tiniest amount will stick out like a sore thumb.

After that, I airbrushed on Warhammer Celestra Grey all over the model. Regular brush would be fine here too, for those of you without access to an airbrush, but if you want a fast, flat and even coat the airbrush is great. This is the left model in the above photo.

I then thinned down some Warhammer Gryph-Charger Grey roughly 50:50 with Contrast Medium and did an all over coat with this. I wanted it strong enough to lightly tint the Celestra Grey, and enough to pool in the recesses, but not strong enough to overpower. After that dried, I did a round of edge highlighting with Warhammer Ulthuan Grey, followed by very light extreme highlights on the sharpest points with Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White.

Gunmetal Portions

To break up all of that white armor, I founds spots all arond the model that I felt should be contrasted with a dark metal. These were the undersuit beneath the breastplate, the leg and arm joints, the area behind the knee armor, the trip on the kneecaps, the round bits and pistons on the feet, the medical goo gun, and the corrugated portion of the pauldrons. For the shields, I did the trim around the window and the handles on the sides and the one the medic is holding.

I basecoated these areas with a 50:50 mix of the Vallejo TMMs Obsidian Black Base and Sterling Silver Base. I thought pure Obsidian would be too dark, and just Sterling Silver too light. I then washed all of that with thinned down Pro Acryl Black Wash, as it isn't quite as harsh as the shades in the TMM range. I did all over edge highlights with Vallejo TMM Sterling Silver Base, and then mixed in about a 50:50 ratio with the TMM Light version for the sharpest highlghts.

Team Color

Figuring out where exactly I wanted the team colors to go was probably the hardest part I found with these models. I decided on the rest of the pauldrons, the little shield badge on the breastplate, the little hoops above the toes on the shoes, the top donuts on the backpack reactors, the little cross on the goo sprayer, the outer trim on the shields, and the outer portion of the cross on the shields. If you're a Terran Dominion player, be sure to not paint the whole cross red, or you'll have the Koprulu Sector's version of the Geneva convention on your ass faster than 6-Pool Zerglings. Then again, they probably have bigger warcrimes to worry about, so paint it however you like.

For my aquamarine team color, I basecoated with Vallejo TMM Hydra Turquoise Base washed with that TMM Shade, and highlighted with a 50:50 mix of the Base and Light versions, with sparing amounts of the light used alone. See my Marine panting article for some examples for other colors you could use.

If you're doing the white of the Alpha Squadron, you're probably going to want to do something a little different. I could see using more gunmetal portions here, or painting them like the rest of the white armor, but tinting with Warhammer Black Templar or maybe something with a slight bit of purple or green tones to help offset it from the rest of the armor. I'd also probably highlight the team color portions a little stronger to further help them stand out.

Brass Metals

The model is looking rather monochromatic at this point, so to add some interest, I added a few bits of a brass style metal to the models. This also helps tie them into the rest of my Terran forces. The bits I picked out are the circle bowling ball looking parts of the calves, the semicircle bits on the tops of the thighs, the circle on the top of and the bar on the back of the backpack reactors, the straps on the goo gun, and the handles on the shields and the breastplate.

I'm pretty set on my brass recipe at this point, and I really like how it looks, especially at the table length. I basecoat with Warhammer Runelord Brass, wash with Warhammer Agrax Earthshade, and do some edge and volumetric highlights with Wahammer Canoptek Alloy.

Exposed Skin and Visors

The wide variety of Medic faces in the StarCraft video games.

Befitting an enclosed power space suit, there's not a lot of exposed skin on these models. It's just the face, and only if you leave the visor up.

Despite what the portraits above suggest, there's more medics out there than one type of girl with a beauty mark near her right eye. We have many different ways of painting skin cataloged here on Tabletop Battles, so pick a variety for your medics to make them individuals.

For the fairer skinned model below, I basecoated with Warhammer Cadian Fleshtone. The white undercoat really helped here. I then washed the face with thinned Warhammer Reikland Fleshshade. I mixed a little Warhammer Pink Horror into the fleshtone to tint the prominent lips on the model, and then did some volumetric highlights with Warhammer Kislev Flesh and Flayed Ones Flesh. I tried like a dozen or so times to do the eyes here, but a combination of being tired, old, and the tiny eyes on this model, I could only do them on one of the three medics. I'll come back with better magnification later.

The clear visor doesn't necessarily need to be painted, but there's a small portion of it visible even if it is raised. I basecoated them with Warhammer Warpstone Green, then glazed up with increasing amounts of Warhammer Moot Green in the direction the model is looking. I then mixed in increasing amounts of Warhammer Dorn Yellow to establish the shiniest part of the visor.

Depending on your team color, I could see any color of visor working here. I'd pick one that complements either your team color or the green firing effect. If you want to leave the visors see through, I'd paint the face with harsher highlights than you normally would, so the shape of the face is still visible behind it.

Chest Lamps and Firing Effects

This is the final stretch! There's two lights on either side of the chestplate, and I painted them with a dead simple recipe. I basecoated the insides and the edge around the lamps with Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White, and then coated it with Warhammer Aethermatic Blue contrast, with the lamp itself getting a second application of white and a tiny bit mroe contrast to show it is brighter.

For the firing effects, I basecoated the areas with the same white we used for the armor, Warhammer Celestra Grey. I then did some chunky highlights with  Pro Acryl Bold Titanium White. I want them to be very stark. I then coated the whole area with some thinned Warhammer Mantis Warriors Green contrast paint. Wait for that to be completely dry, then do some more targeted painting of that contrast into the recesses of the effect, and then do some edge highlights with the same Warhammers Moot Green and Dorn Yellow mix that we used on the visor. The little plus signs should get some extra attention from this.

After this, base the model however you'd like and you're done!

Prepped and Ready!

Terran Medics by Craig "MasterSlowPoke" Sniffen

Though far from the most exciting unit available at the StarCraft TMG's launch, the Medic's pop of white armor adds a nice bit of visual variety to Terran armies. That is, unless I guess, you're playing with an Alpha Squadron scheme, in which case I'd recommend going gunmetal on the main body instead to add that army wide contrast.

Despite being a largely white color scheme, these medics paint up relatively quickly, and their open model design doesn't present any difficulties for painting, especially if you leave the shields separate.
