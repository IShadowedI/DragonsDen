[40k] 11th Edition Necron Unit Breakdown: Supports

July 27th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome to the first in a series of articles breaking down the units of the Necron Codex. As one of the strongest 10th edition armies, we expected to see some great options going into 11th from a varied and powerful unit roster. Before when breaking down Codexes, we looked at leaders all at once; Necrons, however, have one of the biggest rosters of character units in the game, so we will be dividing them across a few different groups to prevent overloading you the reader with information, and me the writer from breaking my fingers typing so much. 

Changelog

  • Published: 2026-07-27

First we'll be looking at the support roster for the Necrons, which are exclusively the Cryptek models. If you are just getting into 11th edition, "support" means that they can double join with a regular leader and are required to join a unit during the list construction step. Both of these make a pretty big impact on the power of these units, which we will discuss when it comes up. 

The first big change that applies to all leaders, not just supports, is that when units are attached to a character, they are all considered one complete unit for all rules purposes. This has two big implications for army construction, both of which matter for Necron players. The first is that leading units will always have their abilities turned on even if those abilities require them to be "leading a unit," since for all rules purposes you are always the same unit even if your bodyguards all die. This doesn’t matter as much for Necron units, as most abilities either barely affect the leader model or already worked even when not leading.

The second implication is way more important. Since your leaders and bodyguards are considered one unit, you are able to use Resurrection Protocols to bring back dead models even if your leader is the only one left alive. This is incredibly powerful for Necrons, especially if you are playing with ways to buff your resurrection in any way. I would advise minding ways to get value here, because if you do it can be backbreaking for your opponent. 

First let's look at what these can attach to. It’s pretty easy because for all but two of the supports listed below, they can be attached to just two units, Necron Warriors and Immortals. Both of these aren’t really that special as Datasheets and can be often skipped for some lists. But the Necron Codex is incredibly good at layering buffs onto units and can turn both Immortals and Warriors into powerhouses. Pretty much anytime you want to take either of these battleline units you will want to attach at least one character, and most of the time those characters will be Crypteks; they tend to synergize with each other very well. 

There are a few supports who can attach to more than just the battleline units; the first of those is the Technomancer. He gets the privilege of being attached to Canoptek Wraiths, which we will dive into more later, but it's safe to say this is one of the best unit combinations in the entire game so you will probably see it in almost every Necron list out there. The second exception is the Geomancer, who can attach to the Canoptek Macrocytes, which is the complete opposite in power level; never do this, ever. 

Before we dig into the actual Datasheets, we should talk about the weapon profiles and defensive profiles. Besides the Epic Hero Cryptek, all of these models are very unremarkable at shooting and melee. The ranged attacks all hit on 4s, usually with an okay profile that could kill a Marine or two if you're lucky, and an even worse melee that might do a wound on a good day. You can sometimes make them decent at shooting if you have a way to improve the hit roll, but you are never considering the offensive profile when picking your support unit so I won’t waste your time looking at them in this article. 

They also all have pretty much the same top line defensive profile. T4 with 4 wounds and a 4+ save is what you get every time, and a base movement of 5” for most, I’ll mention the exceptions when we get there.  

Finally, while we are here I will cover the two units who attach at list construction that are neither leaders or supports, since they are both pretty important and provide a similar role to these supporting Crypteks of increasing the power level of the units attached for low investment. 

Chronomancer

This guy is one of the biggest players coming into 11th edition and you should expect to see him much more regularly than in 10th. Like all support characters mentioned in this article he has two abilities: 

  • Timesplinter Mantle: An unconditional -1 to hit from any melee attacks and Stealth
  • Chronometron: 5” move after shooting, which will exclude you from making a charge move

While losing the -1 to hit in the last update was tough, always-on Stealth and -1 to hit from melee is a very solid combo of defensive rules in a faction that thrives on layers of defense. The fire and fade is also incredibly useful to stay behind cover or to give extra movement. He is also one of the best users of the Murdermind enhancement, which allows him to lead Destroyer Cult units in Cursed Legion. He pairs great with Lokhust Destroyers, especially since they can now Take to the Skies and fire and fade over walls, though they have to be very close due to the maximum of 5”. Often you can just toe into middle, and just using 1” to pull off it will suffice. Expect this guy to be seen on the table often in Necron lists this edition. 

Credit: Greggles

Geomancer

Following the biggest winner, we have the biggest loser out of all the units we will touch on today. Let’s look at what it can do before we break that down.

  • Tectonic Reverberations: While this model is standing on an objective marker, it gives a 12” aura preventing units from being set up via ingress moves. 
  • Obelisk Node Control: An 18” range movement phase ability that subtracts 2” from move and charge rolls.

Honestly just that first rule alone could carry this guy out of the dump if only you didn’t have to attach the Geomancer. I ran it once or twice to screen my backfield during peak C’tan spam, and it did its job: Move 8” and screen. Now you have to pay for a unit to join it, and it really isn’t worth the trouble.

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Plasmancer

The Plasmancer really fell off after the death of Hypercrypt, but he still has some pretty good rules, and there are a few ways I think he can be used well in 11th.

  • Harbinger of Destruction: Provides critical hits on 5s for all successful ranged attacks.
  • Living Lightning: 18” range mortal wound Explosives stratagem style ability, but with only four dice. 

The Plasmancer is the character you bring out when you want to maximize the damage. Crit 5s works well in a faction that can easily get both Lethals and Sustained Hits on its battleline, not to mention the mortals, which are just a cherry on top. Like the Chronomancer, he is also an excellent carrier of Murdermind. The reason he works so well is because he gives the Lokhust unit he is leading the INFANTRY keyword, which allows his unit to embark in a Night Scythe, giving his unit a huge range extension and protection. The newest change that allows Murdermind to double attach does lower the value slightly, as you can do a Technomancer or Chronomancer with a Lokhust Lord in the Night Scythe. The recent Night Scythe nerf preventing an embark the turn you get out also complicates things here, but still this is a great combo that you can use.

Necron Plasmancer. Credit: Pendulin

Psychomancer 

Psychomancers were an incredible little tech piece in 10th and are still equipped with solid rules for 11th, but some flaws will hold them back. 

  • Nightmare Shroud: At the start of any of your phases, pick a unit within 18” and force a -1 Battle-shock test. 
  • Harbinger of Despair: An aura of forcing Battle-shocks at bellow starting strength at -1 in your opponent’s command phase

Both of these rules remain incredibly good, especially in 11th where Battle-shock tests are much more punishing. The problem it has is similar to the Geomancer; it’s hard to justify the points paid for its mandatory unit to lead. However the rules are so good I think it could be justified to run the Psychomancer with a squad of minimum Immortals just to get access to what it can do.

Psychomancer. Credit: Rockfish

Technomancer

I’m pretty sure they teach people in school that the Technomacer is the powerhouse of the cell, cause man is this guy good.

  • Rites of Reanimation: Gives his unit a 5+++ Feel No Pain.
  • Technomancer: Heals a model within 6” for d3 wounds at the end of the movement phase.

Turns out stacking durability and healing mechanics on the durability and healing faction is good; who could have guessed. The 5+++ Feel No Pain applied to the already-durable Canoptek Wraiths pushes them up from a decently tough unit to almost unkillable unless your opponent applies a disgusting amount of firepower. On top of that, the Technomancer is able to heal models, which stacks great on Wraiths as a baseline but gets even sillier when applied to big stuff like C’tan. He’s also the fastest Cryptek, moving 12”, which even gives Wraiths a bit of a boost. While he’s not super-useful on Warriors or Immortals he more than makes up for that by being so good with Wraiths. The recent change allowing him to double join with a Lokhust Lord is incredible as well. Giving their eighteen wounds at T6 a 5+++ with a Resurrection Orb is great, and I expect it will be an excellent choice in Cursed Legion. You might also run him with a big squad of Skorpekhs, giving the unit both a 5+++ and an extra 4” of move.

Necron Technomancer. Credit: Pendulin

Orikan the Diviner

Poor Orikan; he’ll never be able to show his face to Trayzen now that he got demoted to support. But I’ll make sure to tell him it’s a good thing, cause him being a support unlocks the combos we want from his abilities.

  • Master Chronomancer: Gives his unit a 4++ invulnerable save.
  • The Stars Are Right: Orikan triples his attack and strength characteristic while making each successful wound a critical wound.

This guy is really what makes the 20 model Warrior bricks work in 11th. You could have the argument in 10th that stacking AP reduction could get you to close to a 4+ save most of the time anyway vs most anti-infantry profiles, while unlocking more damage from the Plasmancer or additional durability from a Technomancer. Now, though, the changes to cover mean that you really want that 4++ to hold the brick together. On top of that, he is the only Cryptek to really have the chance to be a big damage dealer, though only once a game since even hitting on 2s with Devastating Wounds isn’t worth much with only two attacks. But when he goes crazy with his tripling he can kill entire squads or tanks by himself; don’t ignore this, because it can have a huge impact on the game when it goes off for you. 

Orikan the Diviner. Credit: Pendulin

Canoptek Tomb Crawlers

The first of the two non-support units that can join during list construction. To join they must be attached to a unit with a Cryptek leading it, and they cannot double join with Cryptothralls. 

  • Weapon Sentinels: When a model makes a ranged attack at a unit within 12”, you ignore hit and wound modifiers as well as BS modifiers.

These guys are a great option to add extra beef to your Warrior or Wraith squads. Usually they won’t fit into Wraiths, since they are so much slower that they restrict the squad's mobility, but they shine in Warriors, especially in 11th. Now that cover has changed, these guys combo super well with the gauss reapers the Warriors use, effectively giving them ignore cover. Those six extra wounds at a 3+ save also have really good value, as well as their larger bases giving you a little extra reach when stretching that 9” coherency restriction. Finally, they add an extra eight melee attacks and four shots that are both decent. If you're running Warriors and have 50 points to spare I would grab this every time. On top of that, you can now join them to your Cursed Legion Murdermind unit. These are an excellent choice for a Lokhust unit as they give all of those juicy ignore modifiers to a super real shooting threat. Plus they are the first guys you kill, allowing for the unit to stick around for much longer. 

Credit: Dan "Swiftblade" Richardson

Cryptothralls

These guys follow the same rules as Tomb Crawlers for joining. They are slightly more expensive than them as well, so they get two rules for their trouble.  

  • Bound Creation: Cryptek models get a 4+++ Feel No Pain when in their unit. 
  • Systematic Vigour: Fight on death for each Cryptothralls model on a 2+ roll.

These guy are very solid and mostly lose out to the Crawlers because they are 10 points more expensive. You are mostly buying them for extra wounds. The 4+++ is a very good rule as well, especially since precision in a big concern for Necron supports, all of which are very easy to take out of a unit; it's just not quite good enough to justify paying 10 points more most of the time. They also don’t hit as hard as the Crawlers, who have the same save and wounds characteristics but more melee attacks and better guns. I could definitely see these guys coming back into favor again if they go down in price or Tomb Crawlers go up. 

Necron Cryptothralls. Credit: Chris Cowie

Final Thoughts

Overall the support roster for Necrons is pretty well fleshed with each having some practical use on the table. Which is more than some factions can say. In the next article we will be looking at the leaders available to Necrons which also has quite a few winners for the faction.
