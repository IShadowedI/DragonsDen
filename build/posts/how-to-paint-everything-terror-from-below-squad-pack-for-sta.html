How to Paint Everything: Terror From Below Squad Pack for Star Wars Shatterpoint

May 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer
The Terror From Below Squad Pack features some very impressive models, including Shatterpoint’s first (and most likely only) shark as well as two of the largest single figures in the game. They’re all great looking miniatures with a few interesting painting challenges. I hope you like painting stripes...

Preparation

Assembly was pretty standard for a Shatterpoint kit. Riff Tamson and Nossor Ri went together pretty easily, with a minimum of parts and no customization options. The Aqua Droids were easier than they look as well, but there was one curveball – the arms are a ball socket that doesn’t lock into place, which means you can position them however you want but you’ll need to hold them in place for a few seconds while they dry, depending on what kind of glue you’re using.

After assembly I primed all four models in Vallejo brush-on black, my usual go-to primer.

Riff Tamson

Riff Tamson for Star Wars Shatterpoint painted by Jefferson Powers.

Riff Tamson has a very limited color palette, consisting almost entirely of greys and blues. With this in mind, I knew I could save myself a lot of time if I started out with a few different grey tones: a flat middle grey for the base, a lighter grey for the figure, and sharper contrast for the splashing water. I painted the base in Army Painter Deep Grey, then drybrushed it and the water area in Reaper Cloudy Grey. Then I painted the figure in Reaper Rainy Grey, and drybrushed the entire model with Reaper Misty Grey. This gave me the starting point I wanted, with three different tones, and the drybrushing did a lot of my shading for me.

The flat part of the base was pretty much done, all it needed was to go over the seams between the floor plates in Deep Grey.

I filled in the shoulder pads, sleeves, belt, the and “racing stripes” on the suit with Reaper Nightsky Blue, then went over the piping around the shoulder pads, sleeves, collar and calf areas, as well as the insignia on the shoulders and chest, in Misty Grey.

I added a few spots Army Painter Angelic Red along the ribs of the suit, then painted in a few highlights along the ribs, collar, knees, and the raised areas where the suit bunches up around the crotch – basically anywhere on the white area of the suit that would catch a highlight. This included the top part of the diving suit around the shoulders, as well as the collar.

Riff Tamson photo & color guide by Jefferson Powers.

For the blue parts of his arms, feet, and the top of his head, I mixed Reaper Ashen Blue in with the Nightsky blue, to get a tone that was a little lighter than the sleeves and shoulder pads, brushing it on and then stippling to get the spotty transition between blue and white on his skin. I then went back over the transition line, this time stippling in Misty Grey, to make the transition a little more gradual. I added some darker spots to the top of his head using Nightsky Blue.

I highlighted his biceps, forearms, ridges on his feet, and the sharp edge around the top of his head in Ashen Blue. I left the white parts of his arms and face in the drybrushed grey base coat, just adding a few highlights along his upper lip and neck in Misty Grey.

I painted the eyes in Army Painter Matt Black, with a dot of white for the reflection. The mouth was done in Reaper Pale Violet, with Army Painter Ancient Stone for the teeth. The final detail to be done was the water bursting out of the base – I painted Army Painter Speedpaint Tidal Wave over the grey base coat from earlier, and then filled in the bits of metal floor grating in Army Painter Plate Mail Metal.

Nossor Ri

Nossor Ri painted for Star Wars Shatterpoint by Jefferson Powers.

Out of this squad pack, Nossor Ri was the simplest model to paint, but it’s the one I had the most fun with.

I wanted to do a little more with his base, so before priming I added some Vallejo Earth Texture to the area where the floor meets the strip of dirt – the idea was to make it look like beach sand that had blown onto the metal flooring. I like getting the bases out of the way first when possible, so after a drybrush over the whole model in Deep Grey followed by a much lighter drybrush in Army Painter Uniform Grey, I painted the floor in Deep Grey and then drybrushed it in Cloudy Grey followed by Misty Grey, going back over the seams between the floor plates in Deep Grey.

I then filled in the sand area in Reaper Desert Stone, followed by a drybrush in Army Painter Ancient Stone. Drybrushing is far from precise, but in this case it really didn’t matter if I got a little sand color on the metal flooring, since I was going for a gradual transition anyway.

I painted the bit of machinery he’s standing on in Army Painter Gun Metal, then gave it a wash with Army Painter Dark Tone. After touching up the edges of his boots, it was time to tackle the squid himself.

Nossor Ri photo & color guide by Jefferson Powers.

For his head and hands I started with a base layer of Army Painter Barren Dune. Coverage with yellows is never great, so this required several coats. There is a trick with yellow where you start with an under-layer of pink, but I have found that this gives the yellow a golden tone. For this model I wanted more of a pale yellow, so I went with several coats of the Barren Dune, followed by a coat of Army Painter Strong Tone wash to fill in the details. Washes tend to darken things down a little, so I painted in a few highlights here and there in Barren Dune. I did his eyes in Reaper Sky Blue, and the spots on his forehead in Army Painter Bootstrap Brown.

For his forearm and shin armor (that’s vambraces and greaves to you armor afficionados), I wanted a metallic red, so I started by painting them in Army Painter Shining Silver, and once that had dried I went over it with Slaughter Red Speedpaint – it worked great, and it’s a technique I’ll keep in mind for the future.

The final details were simple enough. Reaper Carnage Red for the amulet he’s wearing, Ancient Stone for the panels on his belt, then a Strong Tone wash over both. I painted the haft of his trident in Army Painter Leather Brown with a Strong Tone wash, and the pointy bits on either end in Gun Metal with a Dark Tone wash. The tunic and pants stayed the dark grey of the undercoat, so that was Nossor Ri done.

Aqua Droids



Aqua Droid painted for Star Wars Shatterpoint by Jefferson Powers.

These models are huge, but painting them was fairly simple. I started with a heavy drybrush in Gun Metal followed by a lighter one in Army Painter Shining Silver – that was the bulk of the work done right there. Remembering how well Nossor Ri’s red armor had worked, I painted the hands, weapons and joints in Grim Black Speedpaint, which worked really well to darken those areas up while keeping a little of the metallic sheen. The eyes were dots of Angelic Red, and then I got to work on the bases.

I did the metal floor the same way I’d done the others, with a grey base coat and a few lighter drybrush layers, taking care not to get too much grey on the model’s feet and touching up with Vallejo Metallic Black where needed. I did the “dirt” base in Army Painter Bootstrap Brown, followed by drybrush layers in Paratrooper Tan and Ancient Stone. I wanted some additional contrast, so I painted the bit of machinery the droid is standing on in Army Painter True Copper, followed by a Strong Tone wash. Finally, I added some Gamer’s Grass tufts to help hide the seam between the machinery bit and the flat base.

Aqua Droids photo & color guide by Jefferson Powers.

And that was the Aqua Droids done...or was it? When I originally painted these models, I was using the stat card artwork as my primary reference. This artwork downplays the light blue “racing stripes,” so I decided to skip them. However, in preparing for this article I noticed that the stripes are actually very prominent on all the other images of the Aqua Droids that I could see, so I decided that I really should add them.

Left: Aqua Droid stat card artwork. Right: image from Star Wars: The Clone Wars TV series.

I went over the raised stripe areas in Reaper Snow Shadow, a nice light greyish-blue. It might be because I was painting over the top of a coat of semi-gloss varnish and/or metallic paint, but the paint went on a little chalky – however, I decided I liked the effect, it looks more like the brushstroke look that we see in the Clone Wars animated series.

Finishing Up

After going over the base edges in Matt Black, I gave each model a coat of Army Painter Matt Varnish. Lately I have been finding that this dries with more of a “satin” finish, and normally I would go over the models with something more matte to dull them down. But in this case I like the semi-gloss finish. It makes them look a little wet, like they’ve just burst out of the water...
