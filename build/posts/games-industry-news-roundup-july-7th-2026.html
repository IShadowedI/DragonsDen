Games Industry News Roundup- July 7th, 2026

July 7th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Xbox Laying Off 3,200 Employees in FY27, Multiple Studios Go Independent

Credit: Xbox Game Studios/Microsoft

Welcome to my nightmare, reader. I don’t think you’re gonna like it.

Xbox has finally given us the full picture of what the layoffs look like following promises of a “full company reset” a few weeks ago, and boy, it is bleak. 

In an internal email memo shared with the press, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma laid out the full extent of the Xbox reset plans, which includes 3,200 hundred layoffs taking place during FY ‘27, 1,600 of which took place on Monday.  

“Our business today is not healthy,” Sharma wrote in the memo. “To grow, we bet on Game Pass, multi-platform, and a broader portfolio of content. While those businesses have created meaningful value, they did not grow at the pace we expected.”

When the initial reset was first announced, multiple partner studios such as Compulsion Games, Double Fine, and Ninja Theory were reported to be facing an imminent threat of closure, though as some silver lining here it looks like each of these studios will get to remain intact. According to Sharma’s email, Compulsion Games and Double Fine are in the process of going independent, and Ninja Theory and State of Decay developer Undead Labs have contracts with new publishers outside of Xbox. France’s Arkane Studios also looks to be either going independent or finding a new publishing home, but the nation’s stricter labor laws means that Arkane and Xbox must go through France’s Work’s Council before any official decision can be made.

As for the remaining studios, the layoffs are already catastrophic. While it’s still taking time for major outlets to determine just where these layoffs happened, one team we know was heavily affected is the Elder Scrolls Online crew at Zenimax. While Xbox leadership has laid out plans to push out major franchises like Elder Scrolls, Fallout, and Doom, ESO doesn’t apparently count. The development team behind the MMO is facing at least 20 staff layoffs, which is half the current development staff, bringing the game’s future into question despite its upcoming season 1 content rollout. Another heavily affected studio is id Software, with reports that around 50% of the team was let go, basically which apparently entirely gutted the coding department at the studio the day before the new Doom DLC was set to release. Obsidian was hit with around 60 to 70 layoffs, around 25% of the current studio workforce, with the remaining employees still waiting for “formal guidance” on how to proceed with current projects.

Other changes at Xbox following this “reset” includes the addition of a Chief Operating Officer to Xbox’s C-Suite team to better manage the “end-to-end P&L responsibility across content, hardware, platform, and services,” as well as reducing the management layers for key decision making to reduce the bureaucratic bloat. Sharma closes her memo with a lofty goal: for Xbox to grow into a company that entertains over a billion people per day (an eye popping 12% of the world’s current population). 

The 1,600 layoffs at Xbox were part of a larger wave of layoffs at Microsoft on Monday, where including Xbox 4,800 employees were let go from the company workforce. There is currently no news about when Xbox plans to cut the additional 1,600 employees mentioned in Sharma’s reset strategy. 

Sony Ending Physical Disc Production in 2028

Credit: Sony Entertainment

Not content for Xbox to hog all of the headline trainwreck this week, PlayStation leadership announced early last week that it plans on ending all physical disc production for games on its platform by 2028.

“As consumer preferences and the broader entertainment industry continue to shift away from physical discs to digital, physical game disc production for all new games releasing on PlayStation consoles will be discontinued starting January 2028,” Sony Interactive Entertainment (SIE) writes on the official PlayStation blog.  “Following this date, new games will be available on PlayStation Store and at retailers in digital formats only. This transition has no impact on games that already released, or will be releasing, prior to January 2028 in disc format.”

This news comes hot on the heels of Rockstar and Take Two’s announcement that GTA VI will not include a game disc with physical copies, only a digital install code, prompting some retailers to refuse to sell the game.

This announcement sparked immediate and intense backlash from gamers around the globe, with many citing the importance of owning physical media versus digital downloads that could potentially be taken away or de-listed, like the 500 movies removed from the PlayStation Network and PSN user accounts last month. A petition urging PlayStation to reconsider has also launched, with around 170,000 signatures at time of writing.

One of the most prominent critics of this decision is Hideo Kojima, who voiced his concern at a film festival in Italy. “With streaming subscription services, like Netflix or Amazon, there is a server somewhere, and you essentially just have the right to turn the tap, and when you do, the data flows out. That’s how movies work on these platforms, right? You don't download the data, you access it directly through a subscription. And the consequence of that is that you don't actually possess the data yourself.”

While it's doubtful SIE would be moved to change their decision based on a petition, no matter how many signatures it garnered, it can still be cyberbullied on social media. Following the announcement on Wednesday to end physical PlayStation discs, the official PlayStation account has been a lightning rod for gamer anger, and no posts have been made on any platform from official PlayStation channels since.  

Nintendo to Replace All Switch Products with User Replaceable Battery Versions in Europe

Credit: Nintendo

Not content for Xbox and PlayStation to hog all of the headline trainwreck this week, Nintendo is also throwing its hat into the ring, but at least this time it’s good news for the consumer and bad news for the suits.

Following an update to regulations regarding replaceable batteries, Nintendo has announced that it is discontinuing its original Switch 2 model in selected regions and instead replacing them with a model that includes a replaceable battery. 

“Starting summer 2026, in preparation for upcoming changes in European battery regulations coming into effect in mid-February 2027, selected Nintendo products in Europe will begin to be replaced on a rolling basis by revisions that contain a user-replaceable battery,” Nintendo writes in a press release on its website. “There is no difference in functionality between current products and revised products containing user-replaceable batteries.”

These new updated models will first become available for consumers to purchase starting this summer, with Joycon controllers being the first products to be replaced, followed by Switch 2 console products starting in autumn. 

This decision comes following new legislation in the European Union that requires all products with an internal portable battery be “readily removable”, and that all products that do not have readily removable batteries in the EU be replaced with a new removable design by February 2027. A notable exception to this law is smartphones and tablets, which do not need to be replaced with a new readily replaceable battery model if the rechargeable battery inside can hold to certain maximum charge thresholds after multiple charging cycles. 

CMON Fires Lead Designer and Co-Creator of ASoIAF Tabletop Game

Credit: CMON Games

Absolutely, positively, not content to watch Xbox, PlayStation, and Nintendo all get owned in the same week, CMON has boldly and bravely stepped up to the plate and dropped a two ton anvil on its own foot. 

According to a post made on his Linkedin account, the Lead Games Designer at CMON, Michael Shinall, was let go last week from his position, effective immediately. 

Shinall began his work at CMON in 2011, at the tail end of the explosive popularity of Zombicide, and would work his way up at the company to become its Director of Design and Development. While Shinall had a prominent role in designing several popular board games, such as the Bloodborne board game adaptation and Rum and Bones, he’s best known for his role in co-designing the immensely popular A Song of Ice and Fire Tabletop Miniatures Game alongside Eric Lang.

“As of yesterday I was effectively and entirely removed from any ongoing projects, in-development projects, and (potential) future designs I was working on for the company,” Shinall writes on his Linkedin. “Those who know me know I don’t publicly speak about any of that anyway, but even more so now that I am separated from the company- eg, I cannot nor will I answer any questions related to anything I was previously-or-up-until-today-currently working/worked on, you'll need to contact the company directly for any questions or inquiries in that regard.”

Shinall also mentions that he was not alone in being laid off from CMON last week, but the other affected employees are currently unknown.

This is just the latest in a long string of disaster and poor decisions at CMON, which has been in a tailspin following a disastrous loss of millions of dollars in FY25 and FY26 that’s forced the company to sell off multiple of its most popular IPs and even its headquarters while multiple c-suite leaders jumped ship.  

Blood Bowl Video Game Rights Saved by Veteran Warhammer Video Game Studio

Credit: Cyanide Studios/Games Workshop

Finally, here at the end, we can end on a high note. 

The rights to development for future Blood Bowl video game content has found a new home at Slitherine, the publisher behind Warhammer 40,000: Battlesector, following the news of previous publisher Nacon’s insolvency in February.

Nacon filed for bankruptcy in February following failure for a major investor to make a loan payment, affecting a number of of Nacon’s owned studios, including longtime Blood Bowl series developers Cyanide Studios. Despite the fact a new Blood Bowl game from Cyanide had already been announced at that time, the future of the video game adaptation was unknown following Nacon’s disbandment, leaving the franchise and studio without a publisher. 

Last week though, Siltherine announced that it had acquired the rights to publish Blood Bowl, and would work with Cyanide studio team members to bring the game to life.

"We are delighted to partner with Slitherine to ensure the future of the Blood Bowl video games.” Cyanide CEO Patrick Pligersdorffer writes. “Slitherine has proven its expertise, publishing high-quality strategy games. We believe it is a perfect match."

More information about the future of Blood Bowl will be given at Slitherine’s Next Event showcase on July 22nd.
