Games Industry News Roundup- October 13th, 2026

October 13th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real, and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

Credit: Epic Games

Epic Games Pivots to “AI-First” Coding Model for Future Games

Epic Games, despite being one of the most influential and well-known developers on the planet thanks to the superhit Fortnite, is not content to sit on its haunches and reap in more money than the GDP of a small country. For better or worse, the company has consistently felt the need to push the envelope on new ways they can make even fatter stacks of cash, and this latest effort is one of their most troubling ideas yet. A report from Jason Schreier on Monday revealed that Epic is pivoting hard into AI development principles, to the point that it seems to have delayed one of its biggest unannounced games. 

According to Schreier's sources, Epic’s been quietly working on a currently unannounced crossover shooter between the Disney and Marvel universes that was originally scheduled for March. However, development on the project was proceeding slower than executives had hoped, due to leadership underestimating the staff needed to create the game. To combat this, Epic Games leadership announced in a call to the team that the development of the unannounced shooter would now use an “AI-First” model, prioritizing at all levels using content and code created by artificial intelligence rather than by hand.

“We’re embracing AI-accelerated development, led by Epic developers who are the visionaries and creative drivers for everything we do,” Jake Jones, Epic’s Communications Director, told Schreier. Sources within Epic told Schreier that this meant leaning on AI almost completely for the development of the title, running everything through Anthropic’s Claude AI and with one employee even claiming that one leader told them “No more hand-written code.”

While Epic has previously been a big push for AI usage in games development, alongside several other major studios, this is the first major instance of a company utilizing complete AI dependence on a project of this scale. 

Disney has not made any comment at time of writing.

Credit: United Wizards of the Coast

Wizards of the Coast D&D and Magic Team Unionize

While the executives at Epic Games are trying to yank work away from its teams, the employees at Wizards are fighting hard to yank their work back. The Wizards of the Coast teams behind Dungeons & Dragons and Magic: The Gathering are taking a cue from the Arena development team and announced their intent to unionize publicly last Tuesday. 

Organizing under the title United Wizards of the Coast (UWOTC), the Union wrote on Oct. 6th that, “As of this morning, both the Dungeons & Dragons team (which includes the workers behind Dungeons & Dragons and D&D Beyond) and Studio X (which includes the tabletop Magic: The Gathering and Duel Masters teams) informed Wizards of the Coast leadership of our intent to unionize, and called on the company to voluntarily recognize our union.” 

“The games we create are foundations of the gaming industry. Just as D&D has inspired countless TTRPGs and Magic sparked the TCG revolution, we hope to catalyze real change within our industry and build upon the groundwork laid by other gaming unions, creating a space where all workers are protected and treated fairly.”

UWOTC listed out a number of the issues that it’s fighting for, including protections against AI, job security against layoffs, sustainable workplace practices including access to remote and hybrid work, and full ownership of anything created by Wizards employees while off the clock. 

The UWOTC team publicly requested for the leadership at both Wizards and Hasbro, its parent company, to voluntarily recognize their union by October 13th, and failing this will start the union election process at Wizards. The D&D and Magic teams follow closely in the steps of the MTG Arena team, who successfully unionized and passed their own union vote earlier this year to create the UWOTC.

Credit: Games Workshop/Wizards of the Coast

Warhammer 40K Commander Decks Receives a Hotly Anticipated Second Printing

It’s a big week for Wizards in the roundup, and technically a big week for crossovers as well. Alongside the marquee announcement at NYCC for the upcoming Marvel: Darkhold Universes Beyond set, Wizards also quietly confirmed one of it’s other major crossovers, the fan favorite Warhammer 40,000 Commander decks, will be returning for a limited reprint run.

IGN broke the news last week after rumors began to surface from various game store employees who claimed that they were suddenly able to order the much sought after 40k Commander decks again. 

"Yes, we can confirm that we did a small reprint of the Commander decks that will be available to distributors and stores soon,” a Wizards of the Coast spokesperson told IGN. “Fans and WPN retailers should check with their distributors for availability, as it may vary by region."

This directly contradicts the earlier statement by Magic’s head designer, Mark Rosewater, where he wrote in a blog post in 2022 that, “There are no current plans to make in-universe Magic versions of the new cards in the Warhammer 40K Commander decks (we make in-universe ones for the Secret Lair cards), but we have the capability to do it if needed on a card-by-card basis.”

The four decks, originally released in October of 2022, each focus on one of the major factions of 40k, led by one of its iconic characters. This includes an Imperium deck with Inquisitor Greyfax as the Commander, a Chaos Deck with Abaddon, a Necron deck commanded by the Silent King, and a Tyranid deck under the control of the Swarmlord. 

No exact date has been given at this time as to when these decks will become available in stores again, in what quantity they will be made available, or if Wizards plans to do this with any of it’s other limited run Commander decks in the future.  

Credit: Disney/EA/Bit Reactor

Star Wars: Zero Company Developers Begin to Bring Back Furloughed Staff

A minor miracle is happening in the roundup this week. Instead of more people losing their jobs in the video game industry, just this once, dear reader, people are getting their jobs back.

After the crushing news earlier this year that Bit Reactor, the team behind the surprise hit Star Wars: Zero Company, was forced to furlough most of its staff prior to the games release, the studio put out a statement telling fans that thanks to their support of the game, they’ve been able to begin the process of bringing their employees back.

Greg Foertsch, the CEO of Bit Reactor, wrote in a statement posted to the company's Bluesky account that, “This week, we started the process of returning more of our team back to work at Bit Reactor, an important step forward in our continued support of Zero Company.”

Foertsch continued by writing, “Having to ask team members to step away after we navigated unexpected business developments outside of our control so close to launch was an incredibly difficult decision, but one that was necessary to help us eventually bring as many people back as possible.”

The statement does not confirm the exact number of employees who have been able to return to work, a report from Game File claims that over half of the staff initially furloughed have returned, though the exact numbers remain undisclosed. Bit Reactor will likely need to publicly report those still affected by the mass furlough should any of them remain off the payroll for longer than six months, and so far only two months have passed. 

“This team means a tremendous amount to me, and I am incredibly proud of what we have accomplished together,” Foertsch writes at the end of his statement. “Thank you to our fans for the incredible response to the game and for supporting us along this journey. We look forward to sharing more updates regarding Bit Reactor and Zero Company soon.”

Credit: Hasbro

Hasbro Expands Partnership with Renegade Game Studios 

Hasbro is closing us out this week on the roundup, which means since it’s the parent company of Wizards of the Coast Hasbro gets to enjoy a Tabletop Battles Industry News Roundup hat trick. This time though, rather than Wizards of the Coast in the spotlight for Hasbro, it's Renegade Game Studios, as Hasbro and Renegade announced its contract extension with the company as it continues to push into the tabletop industry.

"We’re extremely pleased to extend and expand our relationship with Hasbro,” said Scott Gaeta, President of Renegade, in the press release for the agreement. “From our beginnings with Power Rangers to our most recent projects on Dungeons & Dragons Lords of Waterdeep, working with the teams at Hasbro and Wizards of the Coast has been a pleasure. It’s an honor to be entrusted with bringing these iconic brands to tabletops worldwide.”

Hasbro and Renegade first partnered in 2018 to contract Renegade to create tabletop games based on the Power Rangers franchise, and has since then entrusted the company with several of its top dollar tabletop game licenses including Axis and Allies, Diplomacy, Robo Rally, and Heroscape. It’s even brought Renegade in to collaborate with Wizards on games based on the Dungeons and Dragons IP, such as Lords of Waterdeep.

While the announcement of the agreement only specifies that it will last “through 2027 and beyond”, the success that the partnership has brought for both companies thus far likely means that this relationship will continue to strengthen as Hasbro tries to carve out more space for itself in the tabletop board game world.
