Narrative Forge: Five Leagues from the Mortal Realms - Part 2

November 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Our “Narrative Forge” hobby articles exist to encourage stretching yourself out in the hobby - and that includes trying new games and rules. But those can be quite a commitment... unless you use what you already have! In this follow up to the first Five Leagues From The Mortal Realms, Hydriatus gets his campaign underway!

Welcome back readers, to our look at trying out Five Leagues from the Borderlands with your Age of Sigmar collections! After a lot of rolls on random tables to determine our cast and setting, we can begin the tale of Vizhna the Warqueen, on a quest to uncover the secrets of a strange map she found whilst raiding in the realm of Ghur...

Hakkafjord

Though considered one of the "lesser" fjords of Bloodwhiten, it is also one of the most accessible. It was popular with the pirates and smugglers of the Splitneck Sea for ages, who established the small port of Khulin as a refuge from the agents of the Order of Azyr based out of Wreathport. In fact, the search for Khulin was one of the factors that led to the founding of Coldfort.

In the years since the Dawnbringer Crusades brought the Sigmarites to Bjarl, not all have held fast to their faith. The ever-present winter of the southern continent wears away at the strongest resolves, and one can always hear the whispers of the Dark Gods upon the wind. So it came that a successful merchant out of Wreathport found themselves driven to ruin through the machinations of their rivals, and forced to flee. Having nowhere else to go, they sailed the Splitneck Sea until they came across the longships of the southern raiders.

But rather than surrender to them, he offered a parlay, and traded his knowledge of Wreathport and it's navy in exchange for a place amongst the corsairs. Bemused, they accepted, and the merchant was allowed to settle in their fledgling village. Once there, he set about establishing himself as a notable trader, using his old contacts in Wreathport and Coldfort to setup a successful smuggling operation. Soon enough, he had rebuilt his wealth, and used it to finance the construction of a home more befitting his status.

And so Frosthaven was founded, a place where the criminals and exiles from the Sigmarite strongholds could gather and live out their lives free from the tyranny of Azyr.

A quick trace of the region with defined Map Areas and placed locations.

Hakkafjord has the following locations:

  • Khulin (Village/Market Town).
  • Frosthaven (Town/Manor).
  • The Tomb of Tidedrinker (Delve).
  • An undiscovered location.
  • Drowned Barrows (Soulblight Gravelords Camp)
  • Draugr's Copse (Sylvaneth Camp)
  • Camp Shuang (Cities of Sigmar Camp)

The following locations are hidden:

  • The Delve detailed on the Ancient Map
  • A Soulblight Hideout
  • A Sylvaneth Hideout
  • A Cities of Sigmar Hideout

The threat levels of the region are:

  • Cities of Sigmar (6)
  • Sylvaneth (5)
  • Soulblight Gravelords (5)

Vizhna's Oath (and by extension, the campaign), is considered complete once the Hidden Delve is completely cleared.

Campaign Phases

The campaign elements of the game rely heavily on random tables and charts, and focus more on narrative than simulation - it always takes you one campaign turn to travel to any location on the map, for example.

There is also an option to play a tutorial battle before beginning your campaign, but I skipped it to get right into the action!

The First Phase

Your first campaign turn always involves your warband arriving in the region, starting on the edge of the map and traveling somewhere. As part of my narrative, it seemed logical for Vizhna and her companions to be sailing into port, so I chose Khulin as my destination and rolled to see if anything happened en route.

My result? See Something in the Distance - Add an Unexplored Location to the map. Interesting, seems the place is full of ruins or strange creatures!

Upon reaching the village, my warband could only really Lay Low, as they had too little wealth to Trade from the start (you always want some cash in your warband stash to pay for upkeep when you eventually go exploring the wilds). However, reaching the village did grant me 1 Adventure Point!

Oh right - Adventure Points are a currency used to uncover Hidden Locations, gain additional money, upgrade settlements, reduce the Threat Level an enemy faction poses, and promote Followers to Heroes. Whenever you want to achieve one of those options, you spend Adventure Points and roll 1D6 - if you roll under your spend, you succeed.

So, our campaign would see Vizhna having to establish herself in the region, amass 6 Adventure Points, and spend them to uncover the Hidden Delve indicated by her mysterious map.

Finally, I rolled to see what news and rumors reached my warriors  this phase - and got Monster Spotted In Region - Add a Hidden Monster Lair to the map.

Huh, I wonder what monster it is?

Picking up my dice, I flipped to the relevant table and rolled. I got Undead Construct, a giant, terrifying, difficult to kill monster that is way out my warband's league for now. I don't really have anything to fit that in my collection, so I decided to switch it out for something I do have - a war mammoth proxying for a Slaughterbrute! I look at the list for something with appropriate rules and settle for a Giant - big, relatively quick, and sends people flying with each blow.

A mammoth marches through the Ghurish blizzard. (Credit - Hydriatus)

And with that, the first campaign turn comes to an end.

With the map showing what was clearly the Bloodwhiten Fjords, Vizhna marched north to the coast, from where she and her companions bartered for passage to one of the settlements there. 

As the fishing boat navigated the waters of BloodWhiten, Asfrid spied a tattered banner fluttering in the wind half-way up the side of one of the fjords they passed. Though it may well have marked an entrance to some underground labyrinth, the Oathsworn chose to stay the course, and arrived in Khulin after a few days. 

Though the villagers were soft and too preoccupied with notions of wealth, the party nevertheless found their way to one of the communal halls. Amidst the hazy smoke and meagre warmth, they learned of the dangers plaguing the region - of patrols from Coldfort ranging ever northwards, of irate nature spirits haunting those who traveled too far from the settlements, and the restless dead that jealously guarded their treasures amidst the snowy peaks. 

Drifa, the most experienced in what could be passably termed as diplomacy, managed to convince the village elders to let the group stay for free in return for their services - for Khulin was short of warriors, and the capability for violence was always in demand. And so Vizhna's Oathsworn patrolled the streets of Khulin for several days, their leader fruitlessly seeking anyone who could tell her more about the map she held. 

Taulveg meanwhile learned of a great beast wandering the fjords that had claimed the lives of several hunters - a worthy challenge for the Oathsworn perhaps? 

The Second Phase

As there was no battle involved in the first campaign phase, I immediately jumped into the second. As my warband was beginning the phase in a settlement, I rolled on the settlement event table - and rolled Got In A Fight. This meant a member of my warband got into an argument with the locals, and would have to pay reparations of 2 gold. Alternatively, I could sacrifice ALL my Activities for the phase.

Ouch. Having some funds I chose to bear the cost, as otherwise I would have to pay 3 gold for my warband upkeep. This way, one of my activities could be Help the Town Guard which always covers upkeep costs in a settlement. Not a promising start, but what can you expect in a village aligned with Chaos worshipers?

(Also for fun, I randomly rolled to see which member of my warband was the one who had gotten into a fight, and it turned out to be Sigrunn. Of course the unproven warrior would be the one to kick off...)

With one activity left to pick this phase, I chose to have my warband Meet the Locals - which resulted in Vizhna and her cohort being offered a Contract! Contracts are basically randomly generated missions, ranging from delivering something from one settlement to another, escorting an NPC, retrieving a particular item, or fighting specific foes.

In this case, the common folk of Khulin approached the warband and offered them 2 Gold in exchange for chasing off a local threat. Some rolls on associated tables and we had a mission - play a Camp Raid scenario against a randomly determined foe within 3 Phases. Another die roll later and this foe was Strange Troops - well trained mercenaries unaffiliated with any of the local threats.

A little thought and the lore for this whole thing came into focus...

While the locals of Khulin held no fondness for the tribes of the tundra, they recognized their capability for violence - and so offered them an opportunity to channel that in their favor. A band of pirates from across the Splitneck Sea had been causing trouble recently, and had setup camp just on the outskirts of the village - if they could be driven off, the village elders would part with some meager jewels Vizhna and her band could trade for supplies somewhere else.

Since I have been working on a Black Death City/Mordheim gang, I decided to grab those models and use them as a band of wealthy privateers lying low in Khulin and extorting the locals.

Privateers of the Splitneck Sea. (Credit - Hydriatus)

The First Battle

A Camp Raid scenario sees the warband sneaking up on their enemies from a blind spot, getting free movement until the enemy spots them. The enemy in this case consisted of 9 models - 5 melee, 2 ranged, a lieutenant, and a Scheming Heretic. Whilst Strange Soldiers have somewhat daunting stat-lines, being a Roadside Threat enemy meant that I only had to kill 6 of them to force the rest to flee. The key then would be to overwhelm them with unfair numerical matchups!

Vizhna's Chosen ready to raid! (Credit - Hydriatus)

As a raid scenario, this game started with an opportunity for my models to sneak up on the enemy - which they did, with Ikherd and Taulveg moving to flank the camp and intercept the returning sentries while Vizhna led the rest of the warband straight for the heart of the enemy.

After several careful moves, Drifa was detected, and the game began proper! And it very swiftly went downhill for my poor Darkoath!

Turn One.

Things started off alright, as I rolled six d6 for my warband, and assigned them to each model as their Initiative. If the die roll was equal to or below the model's Agility score, they would act first in the round. Then all the enemies would act, and then any model who had an initiative die higher than their Agility.

I had Ikherd activate first, and he successfully cast Distract on one of the ranged soldiers to waste their activation, while Vizhna, Drifa, and Asfrid made their way around the enemy camp to lure them out of position.

It turned out that the foe had someone amongst their ranks capable of enchanting my warriors, but Drifa shrugged off the attempt as she saw multiple enemies rushing back to threatened camp.

Turn Two.

Turn 2 started with some worse Initiative, but enough of my models got to activate before the enemy that I had a chance to trim their numbers. Drifa charged the closest spearman, and engaged in melee.

Combat in Five Leagues is always a one-on-one affair, covering three exchanges. You roll a d6 for each fighter and add their Combat skill and any modifiers such as being stunned or having a an ally within 1", and whoever rolls highest wins. If the charging model wins, it pushes the enemy back and rolls to see if their hit overcomes the target's Armour, and then another roll to see if the hit overcomes their Toughness.

Drifa managed to hit, break through the spearman's armour, but failed to overcome his Toughness - leaving him Wounded and Stunned. Despite these advantages, I just rolled poorly on the second exchange, and the spearman won, which meant he was the attacker for the final exchange - and managed to take Drifa completely out of action!

Smelling blood, Vizhna charged the wounded man next to finish him of, but again poor rolls saw her fended off. The bad luck continued when Taulveg activated and charged the war dog - only for it to win the first exchange, stun him on the second, and kill him on the third.

A poor start. And then it was time for the enemies to activate.

Ranged weapons always activate first, and while the formerly distracted gunner missed, the archer managed to land a truly impressive shot and Wound Vizhna (he needed a 6 on a d6 - forget bad luck, maybe I''m cursed). Then the wardog that had taken down Taulveg charged and took out Ikherd - but a soldier charged Sigrunn and failed their attacks, getting killed in turn on the following exchange!

Sadly, Sigrunn's victory was short lived as the charming Heretic struck her down right after, while a soldier and Lieutenant both engaged Vizhna, who only survived thanks to her shield!

The only surviving warband member with an activation left after all that was Asfrid, who charged in but was also repulsed with little difficulty.

The +1 to Combat the Strange Soldiers had was proving decisive!

Turn Three.

With things looking bleak, Vizhna used her Will point to Activate first, charging the wounded spearmen to try to force the enemy to take some Morale tests - but once again failed to beat him in melee.

Meanwhile, the gun-toting enemy rolled another 1 on their attack and so ran out of ammunition for the rest of the battle. Yay I guess? The Lieutenant and spearman both engaged Vizhna again but she managed to defend herself. The Heretic meanwhile stepped up and killed my other Follower.

Oh no.

Vizhna's last stand.

As the only hero left standing, Vizhna charged the Heretic to try to slay them and salvage some glory out of the whole debacle. But poor dice rolls struck again and she lost the first exchange, only to be taken out of action in the second!

...OK, maybe these Soldiers were less privateers and more Stormcast...

Post Game

Well, since I failed the scenario, I skipped the Loot and Experience sections and went straight to the Injury tables!

Vizhna rolled seriously injured, so expended her one Luck point to ignore that and miraculously escape harm.

Taulveg was only lightly injured, and needs 2 Phases to recover.

Drifa almost died, but a quick infusion of Silverleaf spared her that dark fate, though she would need 3 Phases to recover.

The Gods seem to be looking out for Ikherd, as he managed to just get knocked out by the vicious attack dog and made his way back to Khulin none the worse!

Sadly the Gods are fickle and both of my followers, the unblooded Alfrid and Sigrunn, died (even after I expended my last Silverleaf to try to save one of them)!

My warband's second week in Hakkafjord came to an end with it's numbers depleted to 2, coffers reduced to 4 gold, all my medical supplies used up, only two damaged weapons salvaged from the fallen for my troubles, and a single Adventure Point out of the six I needed.

Final Thoughts

And that's that! From here on, another Campaign Phase would begin, and Vizhna's Chosen would have to find a way to lick their wounds and rebuild their stash of supplies. A poor start for my poor warriors, but they were far from done with Hakkafjord... but that's a story for another time.

If you enjoyed this little story time, grab a copy of Five Leagues from the Borderlands, and see where your adventures in the Mortal Realms take you!
