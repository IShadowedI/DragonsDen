Tabletop Battles Reviews: Warhammer The Old World - Lord on Chaos Dragon

August 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hot on the heels of the new Warriors of Chaos characters, Knights and Warriors, heavy reinforcement arrives in the form of the Lord on Chaos Dragon, which Rich has gotten his hands on. Our thanks to Games Workshop for providing an advanced copy of this kit.

Lord on Chaos Dragon. Credit: Warhammer Community

What’s In the Box?

Screaming out of hellspawned clouds in a dazzling display of fury, the new Lord on Chaos Dragon has reached the shores of the Old World and is ready to rain fire down on any man, dwarf or elf unfortunate enough to get in its way. Alternatively, your Chaos horde can be reinforced by a powerful Sorcerer on Chaos Dragon, slinging spells from atop their deadly steed. Whichever option is chosen, the kit comes with spare legs and a base to build the alternate rider as a foot hero instead, making sure as much of the kit as possible is used.

Chaos Lord on Dragon sprues. Credit: Rich Nutter

The chunky box is filled with a hefty array of sprues, plus a 30mm square base for the foot character and a 100x150mm base for the dragon itself. A lot of the parts on these sprues are quite three-dimensional, sticking out above or below the main plane of the sprue, and on my kit a couple of pieces had detached themselves from the frames whilst in the box, presumably due to impact. They were present and correct, rattling around the bottom of the box, but just a warning to check before cracking on with the build.

Assembly

Large models for The Old World have been a bit of a mixed bag. Although the sculpts have been excellent, there have been instances such as the Cathay Sentinel, or my experience with Miao Ying, where it felt like either the kit engineering or the instruction steps are letting down the build experience a little. It’s pleasing to be able to say, then, that there’s very little of that in the Chaos Dragon kit. The dragon goes together step by step, with subassemblies that make sense, and I didn’t encounter a single bit of “hold these two parts by a tiny connection while you thread something in between them”.

Chaos Lord on Dragon, built. Credit: Rich Nutter

Overall that made the build experience a lot more fun, and I powered through the kit in a couple of hours. We just got a nice new Chaos Lord on foot from the Core Set, and the Sorcerer packed in with the Battle March box is a comparatively old sculpt, so I decided on the combo of a nice new Sorcerer on foot sculpt plus a big combat Lord who could go and get stuck in. The Sorcerer is stuck with their staff, but the lord has a choice of a sword or halberd/lance in one hand, and a shield or axe in the other. I went for the giant sword option. The dragon has a little bit of customisation in its heads - each upper head can go on either neck, and the horns are interchangeable, but otherwise its pose is locked.

Two heads, Jeremy? Credit: Rich Nutter

As I was building the model it started to become clear that it was going to end up rather large, and once finished, it’s absolutely huge. The dragon stands 275mm (nearly 11”) from base to wingtip, and 300mm (almost 12”) across from wingtip to wingtip. That’s a hair off as tall as the Archaon model from Age of Sigmar (the current standard for “big dragon”), and wider to boot. This is the large centrepiece that the Warriors of Chaos army list has been waiting for, in my opinion - it makes the Sorcerer on Manticore look tiny by comparison.

Chaos Sorcerer on foot. Credit: Rich Nutter

Of final note for the building process, the rider, throne and dragon can all be built completely separately. This was useful for painting in subassemblies, but also lets you build a riderless dragon if you so prefer. It leaves a small divot in the top scales where a chain would attach, but it’s not really noticeable and could be easily puttied if you really wanted to cover it.

The rider and saddle are easily sub-assemblied. Credit: Rich Nutter

Warhammer Community have made much of the ability to build the riderless variant of the dragon, even suggesting that it could be a stand-in for Galrauch the Great Drake. Whilst this would be a hell of a glow up, and seems intentional given the two-headed nature of this beast, it’s worth noting that at the time of publishing Galrauch’s profile is on a 60x100mm base, which this dragon would not fit on. On the off chance it could be made to fit, it would probably fall over. It doesn’t seem like the Arcane Journal features an update to Galrauch’s profile, or a new profile for a Chaos Dragon without rider, so although it’s possible that this comes in an FAQ (other profiles have been updated to have a varied range of base sizes in the past), my advice for now is to not assume that this is a stealth Galrauch update.

Painting

As is often the case with these reviews, I was left with less time to paint the Lord on Chaos Dragon than the average hobbyist, so I was left thinking up ways to minimise my brush time whilst still having a good tabletop impact. I knew that I wanted the characters to match the Warrior I’d already painted, grimy and weathered, and so that wouldn’t take too much time, but I was really keen to make the dragon itself pop.

Chaos Lord on Drago, with some volumetric highlighting I definitely didn’t forget to take this until after I’d started the green, honest. Credit: Rich Nutter

I started off by priming the pieces in Colour Forge Raven Black Spray, before airbrushing some very broad volumetric highlights onto the dragon’s musculature and wings using a thinned white paint. Most of the model was then sprayed with 2-3 thin coats of Warhammer Colour Contrast Aeldari Emerald to achieve a rich green colour, with harder, scaled areas followed up by a brushed-on coat of Warhammer Colour Contrast Black Templar. Where possible, I feathered the edges of the Black Templar coat to ease the transition between green and black areas.

After this it was time for the time-saver’s best friend: drybrushing. Green areas were given an all over drybrush of Mini Colour Verdigris (NB: This is a prototype bottle left over from our review, and at time of writing Mini Colour is yet to hit shelves - I suggest Warhammer Colour Sybarite Green as a substitute). Black areas were drybrushed heavily with Two Thin Coats Death Reaper to smooth out the contrast coat, before a final drybrush of Two Thin Coats Shadow Blue.

Chaos Lord on Dragon, now green. Credit: Rich Nutter

I decided on red wing membranes and horns/spikes to add some colour contrast to the model to make it pop, which is a good speed painting tip if you’re looking for high impact in a short amount of time. These were based in Warhammer Colour Khorne Red, and then drybrushed with Warhammer Colour Evil Sunz Scarlet and Warhammer Colour Wild Rider Red. In an ideal world I think I would have spent a bit more time on this step and started from Evil Sunz Scarlet and gone up to orange highlights to make the whole thing brighter, but I was on a timescale and very reliant on the intense coverage of Khorne Red to save me some time! The eyes were dotted in with Warhammer Colour Phalanx Yellow.

As a final step on the dragon, I mixed some Warhammer Colour Agrax Earthshade, Nuln Oil and Lahmian Medium in a 1:1:1 ratio, and slathered it all over the model, making sure to try and avoid pooling/running where possible. I didn’t succeed fully, but my Warriors of Chaos are quite grimy anyway so it’s no huge issue. This darklines the model, helping to up the contrast a little, and also helps to bring it back to the grimy vibes of the armoured warriors and make them seem like they exist in the same universe.

Chaos Lord on Dragon. Credit: Rich Nutter

The Lord and Sorcerer were based in a variety of dark metals, browns, and flesh tones, before being washed with Agrax Earthshade and then weathered with rust and verdigris (mostly Warhammer Colour blues, greens, browns and oranges). Warhammer Colour technical paints like Blood for the Blood God help to further grime them up. For the Lord’s magical weapons, I based the blades in Warhammer Colour Contrast Imperial Fist, smoothed out with Phalanx Yellow. I then drybrushed/stippled up through oranges, reds and blacks to give the forged metal look.

Chaos Lord on Dragon. Credit: Rich Nutter

As a final step, bases got an all over coat of KrautCover Tale of Grimdark basing mix (yes, the one with the bones), locked in place with PVA and isopropyl alcohol. Finally, everything was varnished with a couple of coats of Two Thin Coats Satin Varnish, thinned 1:1 through the airbrush. And with that, the Lord on Chaos Dragon, and accompanying Sorcerer, are complete!

Chaos Sorcerer. Credit: Rich Nutter

Wrap Up

What a kit! Is this the best miniature released for The Old World so far? It’s certainly one of the largest and most impactful, and it’s great to see Warriors of Chaos getting updated and diverging slightly from the Slaves to Darkness Age of Sigmar incarnation. This kit is bound to turn heads on the table, and I’m looking forward to continuing to build a Chaos army around it.
