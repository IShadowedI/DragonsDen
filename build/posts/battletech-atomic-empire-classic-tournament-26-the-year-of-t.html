BattleTech: Atomic Empire Classic Tournament '26 - The Year of the Yeehaw Begins

September 4th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Howdy ‘Mechwarriors and welcome back to another Tabletop Battles tournament report!

At the Atomic Empire.

Myself, Connor, and our recurring friend Tentacle headed on back down to North Carolina to try our hands at another tournament. Now, this is my first in what I’m calling the Year of the Yeehaw. As always, a day of good games, fun opponents, some familiar faces and great competition awaited us! Great thanks to all who took part in setting it up and everyone who said hello while we were there!

Also, this may be the Year of the Yeehaw but I’ll be honest, dear reader, I’m a Nuclear Cowboy… and I’m wishin’ for winter at this point.

The Atomic Empire Classic BattleTech Tournament: 2026

Once again the Atomic Empire is much like last year! While we didn’t have a drunken Peri shouting at Adam Steiner’s ass, or the color green, we did still have a great time watching Connor learn on the fly to paint flames in the Air BnB like a mad man while I put the finishing touches on my name scrawls for the Marshals.

We found something… interesting with this Air BnB? Usually the issue we find with them is that the beds are terrible; these were acceptable, the decor is atrocious, I mean it wasn’t great, or it’s just in a weird area. This one… the front door was broken? It didn’t stop us from our impromptu hobby night but still, it was very confusing when we got there.

Before we turned in for bed we got everything packed, prepped and ready in our boxes and, unfortunately, discovered that the nearest Waffle House was well out of our way for getting to the tournament. This is deeply unfortunate. But, with the boxes all loaded, the cowboy costume on, and the GPS set we go on to the truest breakfast of champions: Biscuitville.

God I love Biscuitville.

The Atomic Empire tournament this year works a little bit differently. For one, Thiel is not randomly rolling missions. Each player has been given 4 mission cards and 4 complication cards, making it so you as the player can do your best to tailor each round to work as best as it can for your list. One player puts forward two missions for the other to pick and the other puts forward two complications, the ones that are chosen are discarded from each hand and they cannot present them in later matches.

I’ll be honest, I quite enjoyed this! It allowed me to make a plan during list construction for which missions I’d trump call over others, and the same for complications. Most notably the winners for me were Push the Line and Bring the rain, giving each player a light strike to use on even rounds.

The List

In keeping with the challenge of the year I played a Fronc Reaches Colonial Marshals list that was affectionately titled ‘Task Force Gunslinger’ as every unit either had one big main gun, or kept its primary damage device out in an arm ready for a quick draw, so to speak.

So, what makes up Task Force Gunslinger, you ask? Well, let's take a peek!

Them Boys with the Badges, Credit: Liberty

Behold, Task Force Gunslinger:

Command Marshal Shane ‘Liberty’ McKenna takes the field in his first real outing riding the cockpit of his cherished Marshal MHL-6FR, The Acadia. Since this is our first time out I have yet to progress on my skill tree progression and am still a 4/5 pilot. The 6FR is, and continues to be, one of my favorite machines in the game. Good mobility, good striking power, and a full load of armor wrapped around a 55 ton frame with no explosives and a light engine makes for a surprisingly tough bastard that will turn into a shambling mound more often than not before it goes down.

Marshal Josephine Wales rides out in her trusty old Charger CGR-1A5, 16 Tons. A 4/4 pilot, Josephine here takes up the front line with eager fire as she drives her 80 ton menace, and its 16 tons of AC/20 and ammo, straight towards the enemy, ready to dish out shells and hammer in the hatred against all these perfidious pirates that stand before us.

Marshal Hanlan ‘Chop-Chop’ Reese barrels towards the enemy with his blade raised high in his Ti Ts’Ang TSG-9H, Smiles. A 60 ton TSM ‘Mech with a top end of 7/11 with its TSM online. A 5/3 pilot, Hanlan here is a master with his Ti Ts’Ang’s hatchet and its placement upon the enemies of the Reaches. The Ti Ts’Ang is an odd duck of a ‘Mech, especially for a Marshal, but it’s an all energy high mobility lightweight heavy that would cleave a Marshal in his Phoenix Hawk 1 damn near in half on the first swing.

Marshal Barry ‘Primrose’ Hutton bounds and strikes at the opposition in his refit Grasshopper GHR-5N, Ramshackle. A 4/5 pilot, Barry is content to join the line and set himself to the task of assisting his lancemates while taking fire and dishing it right on back out again. It’s not much damage between his 5 Medium Lasers and a PPC, but it’s certainly honest work.

Marshal Bass ‘Buckshot’ Reeves ambles onward into the fray from the cockpit of his Filtvelt supplied Hound HD-2F. A 4/5 pilot, Bass here is another man of the line, seeking only to help out his allies in their fight to bring down whatever forces the damnable pirates of the Periphery dare bring to bear against them!

I’ll be honest: boating ‘Mechs with singular big guns is rarely a good plan when you’re trying to win. That said… I’m a bad bitch, and my cowboy powers and charisma will obviously carry me through.

Probably.

—

The Fit

I’ll be honest, my Marshal’s fit is pretty standardized at this point.

The same old familiar pair of boots, handmade leather belt, a good pair of gray canvas-y jeans, and properly fitted black Stetson Bozeman, top it off with a slick handlebar mustache and my white bloused button up with the sleeves rolled up and my poncho…

BUT.

This is the Year of the Yeehaw and here at Tabletop Battles that means something! 

So I got a badge! Look at it up there, shiny and gleaming! Now, let’s go get this bag.

—

Round 1: The Quickest Draw

Round 1 was against Williams, a reader of the site who was playing a Sea Fox Spina Khanate force with an attache supervisor from Beta Galaxy along for the showing. We’re on the split river/road map from the boxes with the Base Recon mission and the ‘lost in the woods’ complication. Lost in the woods means you start with half your force entering turn one, rounding up, and the second half entering turn 2.

Who keeps building these highways to nowhere, Credit: Liberty

Base Recon was a slight return to form of the base destruction base Peri and I played at Richmond Open but with the added caveat of scanning the bases before you destroy them to try and identify the opposing HQ. It played for an interesting reason to push hard across the lane at each other and a reason to get near the Level 3 Building, rather than just hammering rounds into it from down range like a maniac until it goes down.

Star Captain Williams has brought along a pretty nasty looking understrength striker Star. We’ve got a Night Gyr Prime, a Stormcrow Prime, a Wraith TR1, and a Locust IIC Standard. The Stormcrow and Locust are at 3/5 while the Night Gyr and Wraith are 4/5.

Damnable Fox Pirates, aren't Clanners supposed to be honorable or whatever? Credit: Liberty

We start the game off with… movement and not a singular shot that lands from either of us. Turn one be like that. On turn 2 I lose the initiative and the Locust IIC begins chewing on my left hand building while I continue my advance, putting fire into the Night gyr. Just enough lands to proc a PSR which it fails, a good start, at least it has to get up next round to try and kill me.

On turn 3 I lose initiative again leading to the grasshopper to do his job and draw all of the fire off the Night Gyr, dodging all but the UAC as the Ti Ts’Ang chases after the Stormcrow and kicks the armor clean off the ‘Mech’s right leg. On turn 4 though I win the initiative, and that Stormcrow can not get away from the Stormcrow. He chases him down and punts his right leg off while the Night gyr hammers both PPCs into the left torso on the Charger, very nearly touching off the not-emptied AC/20 ammo bin but instead busting one of her SRM 6s while also putting a TAC into the engine. Unfortunate.

While all of this ultra violence is going on the Hound has just kinda been… ignored? Free? Unbothered? In his lane? Moisturized? I don’t know, something like that. Being fully allowed to do what he wants he has simply advanced down to the other end of the map and IDd the opposing HQ, and set to work beating it apart. Also the Night Gyr fails its PSR from its engagement with the Charger.

On turn 5 I win the initiative, giving the Ti Ts’Ang another turn of ‘I know where you’ll be, it is not worth trying to run’ and thus knocks out the Stormcrow with a headshot from its small lasers before kicking the left leg open. He’s got a hunger for shins. At the same time the Grasshopper blows up my opponents center building, securing me an, at current, mild objective lead.

Get that fucking Gyr, Credit: Liberty

The Night Gyr, now under assault by the Marshal and the Charger, gets its right torso hammered off and loses a lot of gun. At the same time I take a crit to my Marshal’s right leg upper actuator and go down like the ton of bricks that I am. The Night gyr shoots to 27 heat and passes its damage PSR as we head into the heat phase, at which point it finds itself lucky that the arm with the UAC/10 and its ammo is now missing… but not lucky enough to pass his shutdown check. Or the PSR that it results in.

This Night Gyr has not met a PSR it didn’t like the ground about.

While I lose initiative for Round 6 it really doesn’t matter by this point. The hound blows up its building, the Ti Ts’ang stomps the Stormcrow’s arm off, crippling it, and the Marshal and Charger aimed shot the other torso off the Night Gyr, giving it a death by engine destruction.

Now, that Stormcrow is still unconscious, which we did not realize meant that it actually counted as ‘dead/destroyed’ for the purposes of scoring so I square up a win 100-40.

A good start!

Round 2: The Toughest Motherfucker in These Lands

My round 2 is against Steve on the River map with the Push the Line mission and the ‘Bring the Rain’ condition. I’ll be honest, we kinda forgot about the condition the entire time and just… didn’t really use them most of the time; even in spite of my Aero-Marshal model I brought! Oops!

We can't stop here, this is bear country, Credit: Liberty

Steve is playing a Mercenary list that scares the shit out of me, solely on the back of an old friend of mine: Mr. Kodiak.

I guess everything comes home to roost at some point.

With a Kodiak, Neanderthal UG, and Sunder O at 4/5 as the list’s core he’s got a fast moving OBJ dork in a Dasher II at 6/4 and an angry ‘I’M A PROBLEM, LOOK AT ME’ melee bot in the form of a Charger 1A1 at 6/3.

Glances at my list and its damage output.

That’s probably fine. I can definitely deal with a Kodiak, don’t worry about it.

I’m worrying about it. I’m worrying about it a lot, actually, reader.

OH GOD NOT THE BEAR, Credit: Liberty

On turn 1 we get… well, nothing. On turn 2 though the clusterfuck truly begins with my Grasshopper off on the left most point just kinda doin’ shit on his own, happily pushing along the point. The Hound manages to plunk a pair of Inferno missiles, the only ammo it had loaded for its SRM 4, into the Neanderthal, sending its heat to 13 to mess with its heat curve a little bit. While these relatively minor exchanges happen me and my Marshal get beaten around a fair bit more than I’m comfortable with, taking a good stack of damage before eating a 40 point charge from the Charger. I don’t go over on my ass but he does after the PSRs from his end of the charge, taking a kick from the Marshal in the shin and the bounce back damage from charging me. Not sure how that happened but I’ll take it.

On turn 3 I lose the initiative and push the hound up as a positioning sacrifice earning him a hearty hammering from just about his entire list. A hammering he somehow survives. I do some alright damage around the map and the grasshopper just… keeps on keepin’ on over on the left there.

On turn 4 I, once again, lose the initiative, opening with the Grasshopper opting not to jump into the water chasing his point, setting off towards the fray while Steve’s Neanderthal swings on to the point. The Charger and Marshal swing into his rear arc, failing to secure the kill but doing a good clip of damage into his rear arcs and some engine damage to further mess with its heat math. The Ti Ts’Ang moves into position, intent on gambling the harder swing to plant his hatchet into the Kodiak’s head; it doesn’t manage it but it’s the thought that counts! Somehow the Hound continues to meander around being allowed to live due to inconvenient positioning.

You fool, i'm behind you! What do you mean I missed? Credit, Liberty

Turn 5… I’ll be honest, all I wrote was ‘Charger goes down to Neanderthal and Kodiak violence, this is unfortunate.’, at least I’ve got a picture of the board state to try and extrapolate from. The Charger pops and breathes his last, going out with his fists up and his shells down range into the Neanderthal, causing it to go to the floor. I know that this turn the Ti Ts’Ang is chasing the Sunder down and punting a couple lasers and a heavy, TSM juiced, foot into the side torso of the Sunder which causes it to fall once again.

It’s turn 6 and I, for the first time in this game, win initiative! I use this implacable advantage to magic my way into a detonated Sunder from the hound shooting cluster at it to try and crit its open side torso, which it manages! Detonating one of its ammo bins in an explosion seen from space as the machine is ripped in half. The Neanderthal tries, and fails, to stand, knocking itself out on the floor as the Marshal puts the Charger to death. The Ti Ts’Ang fails to put the Neanderthal down despite its fire and its boot.

It is now that we run out of time and begin tallying up the score, quickly finding that it’s… 66-66. I tie? Oh, well, sure. Upon review someone mentions that the Neanderthal is still unconscious, therefore dead/destroyed. This pushes the score to 84-66, with a win for me.

I’m confused as to what’s going on but I guess I win so… cool?

I’ll take it, I guess. Also, I know I burned my edge somewhere in this round to avoid immediate Charger death but cannot for the life of me remember when. I think round 4, maybe?

Round 3: Being a Gunslinger is a Glory and Damn it's Good

Round 3 and we’re against Weimann who is playing his own personal Mercenary Command the ‘Gravis Legion’ (Gravy Legion in Latin, very funny). This round was an instance of the reminder that the manner in which the tournament’s missions are organized is actually kind of funny, because we’re playing Push the Line with the Bring the Rain complication again.

I’ll take it!

Gravy is my favorite part of thanksgiving, Credit: Liberty

The Legion has arrived with an interesting force, comprised of a Warhammer WHM-9S at 3/5, an Encorcer III ENF-6M at 3/5, a Nightsky NGS-5T at 4/4, a Wolfhound WLF-2H at 3/5, and a Javelin JVN-12N at 3/5 to round us out! This is a quite mobile force that, while not fully suited for the mission it could certainly get my ass on some of my slower units with some hearty backstab combos that rapidly deplete my numbers and leave me barely able to score. Gonna have to watch out for that.

On turn 1 the only actual exchange of fire that does anything is the Warhammer popping 20 damage into the Charger’s arm. Yea it be like that.

Turn 2 though I win initiative and a whole bunch of dumb shit happens. The Enforcer swings to get after the Grasshopper on the point, the pair exchanging fire and ending up in a cuddle pile on the floor next to each other after failing their PSRs. The Marshal begins a strange tango with the Javelin and Wolfhound on the right side of the map, Command Marshal or not I’m just doing my job of bullying light ‘Mechs. Oh yeah… and the Ti Ts’Ang hammers straight down the line behind the Warhammer, with the Nightsky in close pursuit one level down, turns, and slams a hatchet blow, with the TSM on, square into its CT rear leaving it with 3 structure.

I've got an Axe, you've got a back, let's work this math out, Credit: Liberty

Man, if one of those small lasers had actually hit that might’ve just been dead in one!

On turn 3 the Marshal bounces behind the wolfhound and pops out its gyro, heavy PPC, and the associated capacitor, sending it to the floor. The Grasshopper and Charger continue to try and beat the Enforcer to death with it having fled to the heavy woods while the Nightsky gets up on the ridge behind the Grasshopper. The Ti Ts’Ang tries, and fails, to secure the kill on the Warhammer from behind it again. He lands a kick but hits it into the arm, not the torsos.

On turn 4 I lose the initiative and thusly cannot get behind the whammer again but instead get to work elsewhere. Namely chasing the enforcer down with malice in his heart and significant heat in his myomer bundles. The Charger moves up to cover the Grasshopper’s rear from the Nightsky, pointing her big ass gun at the annoying 50 tonner on sixes. The Wolfhound, for its part, flops around and beats itself apart. It’s not dead but it isn’t feeling very great right now. I put my Marshal’s gun to use and manage to beat it up further, scoring a 12 crit on the leg that my opponent burns his edge to force a reroll on, resulting in a 4. The follow up light strike fails to put the wolfhound down, missing with another 4.

Do you have a moment to talk about your Warhammer's extended waranty? Credit: Liberty

On turn 5 I win initiative and, most notably, finally put down the Warhammer with a CT rear hit from one of the medium lasers. Rude way to die but it’s still dead never the less. Nothing much else happens this turn except the Charger punts the enforcer’s side torso off from the rear that had previously been weakened by a small laser from the Ti Ts’Ang. I am at 98 points, I decide to allot myself one turn to set up as much damage as I can for the last turn and take turn 6 to get some movements in.

Turn 6 comes with lost initiative and ‘Mechs scattering all over the place. Nothing much happens this turn except for the hound getting his bell rung by a kick to the back of the head from the javelin.

On turn 7 though things go out the window the rest of the way as Wiemann decides to stand and deliver with everything he’s got. This results in some… interesting results. I rip the Javelin in half with the full spread of my guns while the Grasshopper slams a PPC clean through the Night Sky’s CT on a 12, gutting the engine and putting the machine down. The Wolfhound ends the round crippled with a fully destroyed gyro and 2 engine hits, leaving it as the last remaining enemy unit on the field as the score ticks over 100 and the game ends.

I win 122-50.

Prizes

I won again. There was an alright distance between myself and our buddy Tentacle in second. I pick up a Megatron trophy, an Outworlds Alliance coin that will be added to my Raven Alliance swag pile, and a 100 dollar gift card which funded another Box of Armored Combat, and IS Assault Lance, and a Second Star League Assault Lance box!

Megatron has not yet fallen, Credit: Liberty

Look at them shiny new toys. Most of it even fits for the Reaches! How wonderful! With another tournament in the bag we go on to dinner.

Dinner

This time we returned to Ted’s Montana Grill and got another NY strip with mashed potatoes and roasted broccoli. I had to this point only eaten breakfast and a bag of chips with a cherry cola so I was ravenous and demolished most of this meal, leaving only just enough steak to take home and eat on the ride the next morning.

We had new company this evening though! Hoovie, the tournament organizer for the South Carolina Classic, joined us for our meal and we had a great chat about BattleTech, Tournaments, and how life is going! Great guy and I cannot wait for the next tournament he runs. It's sounding like it’s gonna be a blast if our conversation and his astounding job last year is anything to go off of!

The Loot

So, there’s something I had forgotten to mention in the Year in Review article section about my challenger here: Salvage.

BattleTech has rules about what is and what is not salvageable. Really it all boils down to one thing: Is the CT still there? If so then it’s probably still salvageable! So, with that in mind I’m keeping a running tab on everything I salvage this year so I can add them to my Marshals TO&E as some new ‘take these in pickup games’ options. As well, anything I salvage is fair game for a tournament that allows salvage; assuming that it fits within the laws of the tournament’s salvage allotment of course.

So, what’d we get this time? Well, let’s see!

A Night Gyr, a Stormcrow, a Neanderthal UG, an Enforcer III 6M, and a Wolfhound 2H. Now that’s an oddball pack of salvage to haul home from an outing… how am I gonna explain this one to the Force Marshal?

Final Thoughts

Task Force Gunslinger worked like a treat! Aside from round 2 it was largely in control of every game just on the back of ‘I have more mass than you have guns, what are you going to do about it?’ which is a very fun archetype of list to play, in my opinion.

While I’m sad to have burned such good picks like the Charger 1A5, Grasshopper 5N, Hound, and Ti Ts’Ang so early, it really was a pain in the ass of a list to deal with in testing and I had to get the work out of them when I had something good to run!

So, where are the Marshals headed next? Well, my dear readers, I’m rounding up the boys on Independence and we’re setting off to the far flung world of Atlanta to join Lynn for the Toys for Tots tournament this year!

I’ve got a very stupid list laid out and I’ll be honest: I don’t know if it’s gonna work, 10K BV with a 6 unit cap while playing on the Reaches MUL is, historically, something I have trouble filling with effective builds but I think what I’ve got this time is certainly… entertaining.

The 1st Expeditionary Marshals Round, Credit: Ascii Squid as Commissioned by Liberty

Remember, even with my long run plan of tournaments this year, my adoration of the essence of competition, and the goal of gunning for a win, what matters is having a good time and doing it all with a smile on your face. All the ‘mech reviews, the pretty plastic and the shiny mathrocks aren’t worth a damn if you don’t use them to have fun with friends! So go grab a drink, a good pack of snacks, your favorite game partner(s), a box of funny ‘mechs, a cozy spot, and a fun map then get to making some good memories, at home or at a tournament!

Until next time! Fair weather, good seas and happy gamin’! Go tear shit up out there and have fun!
