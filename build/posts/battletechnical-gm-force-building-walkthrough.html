BattleTechnical: GM Force Building Walkthrough

October 23rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

In this third part of our mini-series on building campaign OpFors, I’m going to work through my process as I put together the force I’m going to run in a campaign game. In part one, I took a look at what kind of information I wanted to have ready as I started building my force and got a general sense of how I want my forces equipped. In part two I looked at the different types of mechs I’m going to fill most of my force with, along with specific examples of those mechs. Here we’re going to put it all together – I have a mission and a player force, and I’m going to walk through most of my thoughts as I get this campaign game set up.

The Mission

As presented to the players, this mission has a convoy moving prisoners through a city. They need to engage the escorts while friendly units rescue the prisoners. However, I want to flip this mission around a little bit and it’s actually going to be an ambush – this is the third prison break mission in a row the players have been doing as part of a contract, and I think the government has gotten wind of what’s going on. Having an ambush also provides me a perfect opportunity to tweak forces on the fly depending on exactly how well the players are doing with the escorts – if they kill the escorts quickly without taking much damage I bring the full ambush force in, but if Murphy shows up and the players somehow get too beat up I can immediately scale the ambushers back. I’m going to make the actual objective be escaping off the board, and to give them a little bit of extra spice I’m going to need them to have eight hexes between their units and any of the OpFor units when they move off the board.

In this contract the players are fighting the Isle of Skye in 3152, so I’m building forces that are primarily a mix of Republic and Lyran mechs, with a scattering of Wolf Empire and Jade Falcon salvage if I need it. With no current MUL faction list for the Isle of Skye this is my best guess for what kind of government forces would be found.

Sword of Light Rokurokubi. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Player Forces

I’ve got eight players in this campaign, and I require them to tell me which of their mechs they’re running in each mission a few days before the actual game. We have quite a bit of customization going on, and for this mission they’re fielding two Rokurokubi’s, a Venom, an Ostsol, a Lament, a Jade Hawk, a Stormwolf, and a Warhammer. Many of these are custom so I need to go dig into their record sheets to see exactly what they can do, but even at a glance there are a few things I can immediately figure out:

  • The Stormwolf is significantly over-BVed for its firepower. I can’t count on it actually contributing 3200 BV of value.
  • Their slowest mech moves 4/6. Since I’m requiring them to put some distance between themselves and the ambushers I’m going to want to make sure I don’t run too many 5/8 ambushers, and trigger the ambush in a way that gives them an escape route. I’m avoiding the 5/8s because that tends to be a spot where mechs keep a lot of durability alongside their speed – at 6/9 they start becoming a lot more fragile (though I also don’t want to swarm them with too many fast mechs either). It also informs me some on map setup/deployment – we typically get 10 turns in, so the Warhammer will get no more than 60 MP. If I want to trigger the ambush somewhere around turn 3, that would give it 42 MP to extract with. Including some MP for turning and not wanting to run in a completely straight line, I want to make sure that the Warhammer is around 25-30 hexes from the table edge it extracts on when the ambush happens.
  • With three lights their overall firepower isn’t going to be amazing. It’s not going to be terrible, because I know the refits increase damage significantly, but I don’t want to just throw a pile of assault mechs at them.

Next I’m going to actually look at their record sheets. I can see that with their pilot skills they have about 20k BV, so in a normal game if I threw about 19k at them all at once (slightly lower to account for the Stormwolf) I’d expect them to win with a couple casualties. I already know my forces will be split into escorts and ambushers, and that I want the players to need to flee, so assuming that my escorts are fairly weak I’m going to want somewhere a bit over 20k in the ambush force. When I look at their mechs, I get a couple extra bits of info:

  • Many of them have active probes. I fortunately didn’t see any bloodhounds, but they have a couple beagles with 4-hex range and one watchdog with 5-hex range. When I’m deploying the ambushers I’ll need to keep them out of that range, which will help me with keeping an escape route open. It’ll also weaken any super short range ambushers I field, since they won’t be able to start quite at point blank.
  • The Venom has a lot more gun damage than I expected, while the Ostsol has less as it’s been built for TSM.
  • Their only 15-point hits are on the Lament, so I’m not worried over much about having mechs getting taken out by headshots.

The OpFor

Between the mission and the player units I’ve now got a good sense of what I need. A pretty cheap escort force that I can expect the players to chew up in just a couple turns, and about 20k BV of ambushers. The escort force will be especially good if I can use it to get the players strung out a bit with their slow mechs closer to their escape route.

Titan Tactical Brigade Starslayer. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Escorts

This is the easy part to start with. I just finished painting a lance of mostly shitboxes, so I’m going to grab variants of these that seem likely to be used as bait. I’m mostly concerned with not being able to do too much damage and dying quickly, which these should be great at.

Starslayer STY-3C

The only actually good mech in this force, the Starslayer is still just a medium with moderate damage. I wish it had an XL, but even with a standard engine it shouldn’t be hard to kill.

Assassin ASN-30

It’s a medium laser and an LBX AC/5. It really doesn’t do much, even if I shoot light mechs with it. With only one ton of ammo I’m going to only run slug, both to keep the game fast and to cut down on chances of a random cockpit hit.

Vulcan VT-5Sr

I’ve got a choice between the 5Sr and 7T here, and since I want this lance to be bad I’m taking the 5Sr. Less armor and a lack of a targeting computer is exactly what I want when my goal is accomplishing little.

Scorpion SCP-1TB

Again I’m going for the worst of the available variants. Low armor and low damage is exactly what I’m looking for.

All told, these four mechs should die very quickly, being unable to build up too much TMM or having a lot of armor. If the non-Starslayers can all die in the first 2-3 turns I’ll be happy.

Hastati Sentinels Highlander. Credit: Jack Hunter

The Ambushers

I’m looking about eight units in my ambush force. I don’t want too many, because that’s  going to slow gameplay down too far, but having too few means that I’m risking some lucky hits not creating the mission outcome I’m intending (yes, this is somewhat on rails; this is a BattleTech campaign not Dungeons & Dragons). I’m going to want one mech that’s slow, short ranged, and threatening – this is something that forces them to run away. I’ll add a couple of killable heavy or assault units at 4/6 movement, which will keep up with the players and force them to commit to some fighting rather than purely fleeing, and pad out with some faster mediums that the light mechs can tangle with.

Highlander HGN-734

I started off looking for a 3/5 assault with some variety of AC 20. This is definitely killable by the players with a light engine and a bunch of ammo in one torso, but it’s durable enough that I think they’ll try to avoid it rather than killing it. A large laser means it doesn’t do nothing at range, but it doesn’t do much – it’s mostly there to terrify the players and make them flee.

Doloire DLR-OA

This is definitely a good mech, though it’s not quite 100% armor coverage and has a light engine to keep it reasonable to kill. I picked a variant that doesn’t hit for more than 10 damage with any single shot and doesn’t have any accuracy bonuses beyond the AES – I want a threat, not to immediately kill the players.

Marauder MAD-5S

An XL engine marauder is an easy choice as a killable threat. It’s not one of the variants that maxes out armor, so it’s a good place for our only long range 15-damage weapon.

Thunderbolt TDR-11SE

Another good mech that doesn’t hit tremendously hard, I grabbed this because it brings ECM, 6 jump is useful in a city, and it has extremely bad heat efficiency. It’s a decent threat, but probably won’t be killing anything.

Axman AXM-3Sr

The last of our heavies, this is another less-than-fully armored 4/6 heavy. It doesn’t have any big hits, will hopefully sandblast the players down without killing them, and is something they can kill. Another ECM makes me narratively happy.

Hunchback HBK-7R

This is one where I’m not entirely sure how it’ll end up playing. It carries an ultra ac/10, which can be swingy, and has reinforced structure. In some ways its more durable than the heavy mechs, although an XL engine does keep it honest.

Bushwacker BSW-X2

I love Bushwackers, I’m playing a Lyran-ish force, they’re easy to kill, and this one’s a bit of a mess of capabilities. Easy include.

Griffin IIC 7

This picks up some mobility that I wanted but the rest of the force lacks and a little bit of an accuracy improvement to threaten the light mechs, but ER pulses mean its not enough to reliably kill them. It’ll be a useful skirmisher to try to keep everything moving.

Firestarter Omni FS9-OE

One mech more than I really wanted to include, but I wasn’t quite at the power level I wanted (especially knowing the Highlander will probably only be minimally involved in fighting). This brings in one more quick medium, an MRM 20 is enough damage to be a real threat, and it has a sword, which is bad but funny.

In theory, this force should be enough to make sure the players don’t have the option to stick around and just kill it, even if they get some good luck, but because I’ve piled a lot of the threat into slower short ranged mechs it won’t be able to just wipe them off the map. The ambush force entirely runs light and XL engines, so are fragile enough that they should start dropping quickly, but will last long enough to push the players off the map. I’m most concerned with my requirement that successful extraction has no mechs within 8 hexes – it shouldn’t be a problem for the fast units, but might be an issue for the Warhammer.

The Game

On the table, this force ended up working out close to how I was planning. I didn’t quite anticipate the player actions right, and they triggered the ambush earlier than expected – the escort units weren’t dead, and the Venom was dead center in the middle of the ambush, forcing me to split fire so as to not instantly evaporate it on turn 2. I had expected them to play a little cagier, trying to take out the escorts before capturing the transports, but they sprinted straight in (the transports wouldn’t have escaped until about turn 5, so they had plenty of time).

I didn’t quite succeed at forcing the players to focus fire and take out the heavier units. My 4/6s were never quite able to catch up to their Warhammer, so the players only truly had to contend with the lighter units – things that even part of their force at a time were able to take out. This tends to be an issue with these larger campaign games, as players are very worried about losing their mechs and will prioritize playing safe over killing things, but large games have too much damage on the field, such that exposing mechs dramatically increases risk of loss. There unfortunately isn’t a great solution to that – for a campaign situation where you want mechs to survive, playing games at closer to 10k BV than 20k BV (which generally means having 4 players instead of 8) is just much more functional.

In hindsight, I think swapping one of the heavies (probably the Axman) for another 5/8 or 6/9 unit would’ve given me a game a little closer to what I wanted. I think having one more unit that could keep up with and pressure the players would’ve forced them to stick around and fight more, but it also might’ve just led to them abandoning the Warhammer and having their other units escape – hard to know for sure in advance. That brings me to my last thought (at least for now) on building these forces: know your players. This mission could’ve gone very differently if the players played differently, and I probably should’ve expected them to slam something forward and trigger the ambush ASAP, despite how cagey they tend to play once they get into actual combat.
