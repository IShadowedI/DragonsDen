Magic: The Gathering Marvel Super Heroes Review, Part 1 of 4: The Mechanics

June 16th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This week we tune in to watch all of your favorite Marvel heroes enter the realm of Magic, presumably with a mandatory Excelsior! In this episode, Magic players are being introduced to a swath of new mechanics and returning fanservice as their favorite Heroes and Villains duke it out with the power of friendship and dastardly plots. In this article we’ll talk about those mechanics, and offer some thoughts on what they mean for Commander and other formats.

Marvel Super Heroes will release to Magic: the Gathering Online and Arena on June 23rd, and to the tabletop on June 26th. Commander: Marvel Super Heroes will release on the same date.

New Mechanics

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Power-up

Power-up gives your creatures another gear to kick in to when narratively appropriate (or when mana allows), allowing you to activate their ability for a benefit. This ability can only be activated once, so the +1/+1 counters it gives also serves as a helpful reminder that it has been used. In addition, the cost of the ability is reduced by the creature's mana cost if they entered this turn for some extra late-game heroic arrivals.

FromTheShire: There is a pretty wide gamut of abilities to be found among the power-ups in the set, and a few of them are quite strong. Best case scenario you really want these to function as a kicker cost for your creature because the cost of activating them on subsequent turns is hefty. Thankfully the reduction triggers if they entered this turn not if they were cast which opens up blink shenanigans.

Saffgor: Power-up's a neat take on a Kicker-style mechanic, and the flexibility to essentially cast your Creatures as this expanded cost later in the game is neat, else it's just a fancy Monstrous. The big difference is that it arrives in the form of an activated ability, the likes of which we can reduce with cards such as Training Grounds, and that very often the colored mana requirement is nixed (as you reduce by printed cost), meaning colorless sources are just as worthwhile to ramp with.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Teamwork

Calling on the power of friendship, Teamwork can be found on a few of the instants and sorceries in the set, allowing you to tap creatures with a certain amount of power in order to boost the effect of the spell.

FromTheShire: This is a solid ability but there aren't that many cards that get it, and of those there is a pretty big gap between the top and the bottom. I'll let you guess where the above Temur Battle Rage clone falls.

Saffgor: This is not unlike Power-up in that it's another Kicker variant, but basically none of the Teamwork cards in the set have an expensive enough cost that you're unlikely to pay in any constructed format. There's a lot of Teamwork 1-3, and that removes the choice inherent to the mechanic in any deck that'd include them. Fine, if unexciting.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Plan

What would a set full of Villains be without some skulking and scheming? (Disclaimer: Set contains no skulking). Plan is an Enchantment subtype that represents your methodical fulfillment of its evil agenda with a pair of abilities. The first gives you an objective to fulfill in order to put plan counters on the Enchantment, and the second rewards you with a payoff once you have achieved it a certain number of times.

FromTheShire: Plan has an even more limited number to draw from, but these are a big flavor win. The sense of inevitability and creeping doom really comes across, which is good because a lot of these are much more M.O.D.O.K. than Thanos in the effectiveness department. There's a ton of room to expand in the future though, and unlike a lot of these Universes Beyond mechanics Plan is easily portable to standard Magic sets which is great.

Saffgor: Plans are a bit like Shrines, where while some will be playable regardless of the subtype, you're going to see the most value in decks that can enable them. They come roaring out of the gate with an easy tutor by way of The Masters of Evil, and the payoff is there if you can skip through their requirements. Proliferate & stacking Plan counters at Instant-speed both work well here, before they sacrifice themselves, so I expect to see a Saga-style pop-off when the best one is found for a given format.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Worthy

Worthy is an Equip restriction to ensure that only the truly deserving can wield its power.

FromTheShire: This does technically count as a new mechanic despite being limited to exactly one card. It's uh...appropriately flavorful? Hang on let me just check who the most popular Boros Commander is on EDHrec real quick....oh. Oh no. Arabella, Abandoned Doll huh. Maybe Mjölnir needs to hire someone to do background checks.

Saffgor: Mjölnir is a good card. Worthy is not a good mechanic. It's wildly arbitrary, not memorable, and serves more to make the designer feel clever than it does the player. The Villain subtype is rather silly too, is Elesh Norn worthy? Does this run on Warcraft Paladin rules?

Returning Mechanics

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Connive

FromTheShire: Connive provides both a source of card selection and creature buffing that is always welcome, and thematically is right at home in a Villain set. Legitimately this is a better fit than some of the mechanics designed for the set.

Saffgor: Connive finally crystallizes in this set as the Villain typal mechanic, with enough payoffs for when you perform the game action to actually build a deck based around it. Kinda neat to see it evolve from an in-universe shard mechanic in New Capenna to a centerpoint of Marvel's rogue gallery.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Transforming Modal Double-Faced Cards

Returning after their debut in the Spider-Man set, these cards can be cast as the creature on either side. However they also have the built in ability to transform from their cheaper version to the souped-up one later in the game for a price.

FromTheShire: An expected return that fits just as well the second time around. It's both flavorful and more powerful as the game goes long, no notes. We should expect to keep seeing it in these sets.

Saffgor: They were a thematic home-run in Spider-Man (for as much of a wet tissue that set was), and seeing them return is welcome. Marvel's round 2 in Magic is looking much more promising, so that's all the better.

Next Time: The Set’s Multicolor & Colorless Cards

That wraps up our look at the mechanics of Marvel Super Heroes. We’ll be back later to look at the most noteworthy cards in the set, starting with the Multicolor and Colorless cards, and then Monocolor options in the following articles.
