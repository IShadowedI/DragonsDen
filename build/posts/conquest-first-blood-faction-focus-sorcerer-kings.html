Conquest First Blood Faction Focus: Sorcerer Kings

July 21st, 2026 via Goonhammer

It’s time to dive deep into the primordial elemental domains! Today, we’re going to be taking a close look at the Sorcerer Kings in First Blood.

The Sorcerer Kings are one of Conquest’s more recent armies with positively gorgeous new sculpts. And with a large number of dual kit releases, they are fast becoming an established force with a very respectable and diverse army roster.

Faction Overview

Credit Para Bellum Games

The Sorcerer Kings have a very unique theme among wargames, with a mix of Arabian, Indian and Ottoman influences – both historical and mythological. As you might glean from their name, they are intended to be Conquest’s most magic-heavy army, which – as is tradition among wargames – predisposes them to complexity and rules bloat.

But if peerless mortal warriors decked out in gilded armor, backed up by towering, graceful elemental warriors and mythic monsters are your jam – and you are not daunted by an absolutely massive amount of rules and spells to remember –, then the Sorcerer Kings are the army for you!

Army Rules

To start us off, the First Blood army rules for the Sorcerer Kings partition the faction into a number of sub-factions, as is tradition. These are used for synergies across the faction and are further subdivided into two kinds of subfactions, societal and elemental in nature. Sorcerer Kings Warriors are either part of the Enclaves, the Mahabharati or the Elemental Courts.

These are, in order, the regular humans, the elite, elementally gifted mortal warriors and the sorcerous masters of the Sorcerer Kings society – as well as their entourage of summoned elemental spirits. The latter of which are then generally further divided between the four elements for rules purposes: Earth, Wind and Fire– erm, I mean Air, Earth, Fire and Water.

Moving past the census, your Warlord brings a single Restricted unit from your army list as their retinue, making them count as a mainstay choice. This selection has to correspond to your Warlords Elemental court – more on that later.

On the Elemental side, the summoned forces of the Sorcerer Kings get to ignore parts of the natural terrain of the battlefield, again corresponding to their court. Are you sensing a theme here? These advantages go as follows:

  • Air Elementals get to ignore Hindering Terrain
  • Earth Elementals ignore the effects of Broken Ground Terrain
  • Fire Elementals are not affected by Dangerous Terrain
  • Water Elementals, intuitively, are not negatively affected by fighting in Water Terrain

And as if that still wasn’t enough – it isn’t really, I’m just doing a thing of it for comedic effect –, the Sorcerer Kings get one last Army Rule, which is the most important one: Confluence.

Simply put, your army will generate Confluence throughout the battle. When, where and how your army does this, is generally relegated to unit profiles, though you do gain 1 Confluence point per Spellcaster and per Monster at the start of the Command Phase. These points persist between Rounds and are used to conjure Rituals, empower spells or to unlock special unit actions.

What this all means is that the Sorcerer Kings tend to play as a synergy army that tries to set up big plays at least a turn or two in advance, depending on your list. The Army Rules really want you to load up your list with Characters, Monsters and Elementals to generate Confluence for big plays in the second and fourth round, generating additional Confluence and playing cage-y in the meantime. Getting into this rhythm is key, since without synergies, many of your key units tend to outperform more bread-and-butter goodstuff armies on a point-for-point basis.

Rituals

With all the kerfuffle about Rituals, it really behooves us to take a look at them next for a quick breakdown. In general, there is one Ritual per Element, with four combinations for Elements with specific affinities for each other, for a total of eight.

You can only fire off a Ritual, if you still control a Spellcaster or Monster of the respective Element, and you may perform multiple rituals – if you have the required amount of Confluence points available. Each Rituals effects last until the end of the Round.

Fire: For 7 Confluence you get to select a Friendly Fire Warrior anywhere on the Battlefield. Enemies within Command Range of that Warrior suffer a Hit, while Enemies Engaged with Fire Warriors suffer two Hits instead.

Pretty good for an all fire list, but notably worse against elite armies with high armor, as they can generally tank a Hit or two just fine.

Water: For 7 Confluence, you select a Friendly Water Warrior anywhere on the battlefield and all Friendly Warriors within their Command Range Heal 1. Any Enemies that are Engaged with Friendly Water Warriors may immediately be Driven Back.

This is very useful for Brute-heavy lists, as Infantry tends to lose out on the healing here. Having the option of driving enemies back is great, especially since Rituals are performed in the Command Phase and you will therefore already know if you have priority or not.

Earth: For 7 Confluence, you select a Friendly Water Warrior anywhere on the battlefield and Friendly Warriors in their Command Range gain the Bastion Special Rule. Earth Warriors in their Command Range gain the Iron Discipline Special Rule as well.

A bit strange in terms of timing, as Bastion is a little less useful than the Hits or the healing from the Fire and Water Rituals. But if you manage to get this off before the enemy charges you, this could change the tide of the game massively. Still a little iffy, since the Sorcerer Kings are so setup-heavy already, adding another timing requirement to their abilities can quickly make this kind of effect too niche to really favor a pure Earth build. The Iron Discipline bonus for Earth Warriors is very welcome and almost always useful though.

Air: 7 Confluence let you choose an Air Warrior anywhere, and the target as well as all Friendly Warriors in their Command Range immediately get to move up to half of their March value, while Air Warriors may instead move their entire March value.

This is really, really good in any situation and far and away the best mono-element Ritual in my humble opinion. Extra movement is always good in gaming, and this can be used for so many things, it’s kind of nuts: Engaging enemies when you have the initiative, repositioning if you don’t, snagging objectives, blocking charges – this really does it all.

Air + Water nets you the effect of Obscuring Terrain for Friendly Warriors within Command Range of an Air or Water Warrior anywhere on the battlefield.

This notably only costs 5 Confluence points and it’s nice to have the option against heavy shooting, but it feels very situational and underwhelming for a Ritual. Especially since you also need to have a spare Water Character or Monster kicking around somewhere to even access this.

Water + Earth has a way better Ritual and only costs 4 Confluence points. You select a Friendly Water or Earth Warrior anywhere and all enemies within their Engagement Range must immediately test their Resolve. If they fail, they become Broken, though notably, they are not Driven Back.

This is really fun, but kind of limited by the dual Element requirement and the short range, though thematically, the upcoming Earth elementals probably want to be in the thick of the fight, so watch this space until they get released. In the meantime, it’s nice to have the option for your Marid Lancers.

Fire + Air nets you access to a targeted nuke. For 6 Confluence you select a Friendly Fire or Air Warrior and choose an Enemy in their Command Range. That Enemy suffers hits equal to their Defense.

It’s a little random, since your opponent can always out-roll you since the Ritual is scaled to their Defense. But it’s very nice to have the option to nuke an important character when they’ve already suffered some damage – especially since it’s easy to get into the right position with the Air Ritual and Air Elementals general mobility. Just don’t expect this Ritual to take out anything by itself.

Earth + Fire is another Ritual that relies on having an Earth or Fire Warrior close to a bunch of enemies. For 5 Confluence, you get to nominate one such Warrior anywhere and subtract -1 Defense from all Enemies in their Engagement Range.

Clean good fun, but a little risky if the momentum swings your opponent’s way. If you don’t get the first hit in, it can easily mean that your Fire/Earth Elemental just dies before you get to capitalize on the -1 Defense.

Phew. Talk about rules bloat. You got all that? Then let’s dive into the heart of the Sorcerer Kings army: Their characters!

Characters

Maharajah

Maharajah painted by zptlk

The Maharajah are the interrex leaders while the actual Sorcerer Kings hang out in the Elemental Domains and as such are absolute spellcasting chads. They are costly at 145 points, but they really let you supercharge your Rituals.

Through a Special Rule, they net you access to any Ritual that corresponds to the Court of a Friendly Elemental Warrior within his pretty big 7” Command Range. In addition, he can cast spells using Confluence instead of Essence, if needed, and generates a lot of Essence with his high Spellcaster value of 4.

His spells are nothing to sneeze at either:

  • Master Conjurer lets you immediately perform a Ritual using Essence instead of Confluence.
  • Occultist (3 Essence) nets all Friendly Elemental Warriors within Command Range a bonus corresponding to their Court:
    • Overrun for Air
    • Indomitable for Earth
    • Flurry for Fire
    • And Impact +1 for Water
  • Sorcerous Bolt (2 Essence) projects an aura that deals 2 Hits to any Enemy that ends a March Action within the Maharajahs Command Range.

His profile pushes him toward leading from the front, and he thankfully kind of has the statline to go with it. 3 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 1, 4 Wounds, Resolve 4 – but a mere Defense/Evasion 2. This single stat really is his Achilles heel and provides an interesting wrinkle.

The Mahrajah really wants to lead some kind of elite unit into battle and do all his sorcerous stuff there, but if the tide turns against you it’s very easy for him to get caught by something that can chew through his 4 Wounds quickly, and then the game is pretty much over. Treat a Maharajah list like you’re playing chess: Protect the (Sorcerer) King with loads of Elementals and a unit to babysit him, and he will make things very difficult for your opponent.

Raj

Raj Alternative Sculpt „Skypiercer Yindak“ painted by zptlk

The Raj is a local leader, positioned directly under the Maharajah in the strict hierarchy of Sorcerer King society. Don’t let that fool you though, a middle manager the Raj is not. Instead, he is an actual frontline character, with a statline and ruleset to match.

120 points net you 4 Attacks, Clash 3, Cleave 1 and Quicksilver Strikes. Pretty good offensive potential for a Character in general, let alone a Spellcaster, while Defense and Resolve 3 with 3 Wounds provide a decent defensive profile, further improved by the Fearless Special Rule. Don’t mistake him for a tank and he will pull through against most Infantry, but he really doesn’t wanna face Brutes, Cavalry or Monsters.

His main role is buffing his combat retinue, though. He has a Special Rule that gives all Friendly Warriors in his Command Range his Court, which can provide some strong synergy through Rituals and spells, while another Special Rule boosts his already decent spellcasting potential – he is a Spellcaster 3 – with additional Essence points whenever a Friendly Warrior Activates in his Command Range.

His spells tend toward combat buffs, with a cheeky nuke thrown in:

  • Arcane Armaments (1 Essence) gives all Friendly Warriors a Special Rule during Clash Actions, depending on the Raj’s Court:

    • Air gives your Warriors Flawless Strikes
    • Earth boosts armor penetration with Cleave +1
    • Fire creates additional Hits with Relentless Blows
    • Water sabotages your opponents defense with Overwhelm
  • Elemental Blast (2 Essence) is a Unique Volley Action with Barrage 2 (10”) and Armor Piercing 1.
  • Imbue Blades (3 Essence) gives all Warriors in Command Range the Deadly Blades Special Rule.

As a rule, you will want to use Arcane Armaments if you are in combat and Elemental Blast if not. Imbue Blades is only really worth it if you are orchestrating a targeted strike with overwhelming force – Deadly Blades will make sure that you really punch a hole in your opponent’s lines.

Heroes

Sardar

Mahabharati Sardar painted by zptlk

Eagle-eyed readers will notice that we skipped from the Characters section to the Heroes section. That’s because unlike other armies, the Sorcerer Kings have two Characters who can both serve as Warlords and as Heroes, though only one of them actually is worth considering as a Warlord if we’re being honest, and it isn’t the Sardar.

The Sardar kind of had the misfortune of getting released right around the time that Para Bellum stopped pushing Officer models in Last Argument of Kings. In turn, he has since always been billed as a mainline character and never quite lived up to the title.

In theory, he is supposed to be a support character for your mortal Warriors, acting as a unit captain, and the same is happening here. 3 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 2 is honestly pretty good for a mortal Sorcerer Kings Warrior, and he even has some staying power with Defense and Resolve 3 and 3 Wounds.

His Command Abilities and Special Rules all revolve around buffing your Enclave and Mahabharati Warriors. Within his Command Range, they gain the Unyielding Special Rule and his Command Abilities let you choose between further buffing their Defense through Tenacious or giving them some additional offensive power with the Opportunist Special Rule.

This is all really neat for a Hero, especially at only 90 points, but as a Warlord, he really doesn’t make the cut. If you are dead-set on just running a mortal-only army – and why would you – he, like the Mahabharati Initiates, has the Primordial Bond ability, which lets you spend Confluence points to re-roll a single dice whenever you attack or block. In more Elemental-heavy lists though, this feels like a consolation prize more than anything. If you don’t have the points or the setup for a big Ritual, you can spend some of your Confluence to nudge his combats in your favor. But if you’re in that situation, something must have really gone awry.

Sorcerer

Sorcerer Promo Sculpt painted by zptlk

The Sorcerer is the other Hero pick, and she has much more of a leg to stand on in terms of doubling as a Warlord. Out of all the Spellcasters, she is the one who really doesn’t want to see any combat action. Defense and Resolve 2 with 3 Wounds mean that even heavy chip fire can seriously threaten her.

Instead, she wants to hang back with your ranged units and cast her impressive array of support spells. To do just that, she has a decent Spellcaster value of 3 as well as the ability to re-roll one failed Attunement roll per Elemental Warrior in her Command Range, which further incentivizes you toward running ranged Brutes alongside her. Her spells are as follows:

  • Elemental Extoller (2 Essence) gives all Elemental Warriors in Command Range the Blessed Special Rule
  • Mystic Shot (1 Essence) gives Deadly Shot to all Warriors within Command Range when they perform a Volley Action
  • Primordial Blast (3 Essence) is your nuke, Barrage (3) at a somewhat perilous 12”, but with additional effects corresponding to the Sorcerer’s chosen Court:

    • Air forces all Enemy Warriors within 2” of the target to make a Resolve Test and become Broken if they fail it
    • Earth provides you with a whopping Armor Piercing (3)
    • Fire has other Enemies within 2” of the Target suffer one Hit
    • Water forces Enemies who suffer a Wound from the Volley Action to take Resolve Tests equal to their Size. For each Failure, they suffer an additional Wound

Once all her Spellcasting is done, any remaining Essence points get converted into Confluence points. This is a very tempting package and close to an auto-include on the Hero side, but at 100 points she is just costly enough to make it hurt. Since the Sorcerer Kings also tend toward elite lists due to all the pieces you need to assemble for your venn diagram of overlapping synergies, you might not have the points left to include her.

One good way to mitigate that would be to run a Sorcerer as your Warlord as well, since she is a tad cheaper than the Raj and a lot cheaper than the Maharajah. Two Sorcerers actually work together fairly well, provided you have the required front line units and screens to protect them and the ranged units to stick with them and absorb all their buffs.

Mainstay Units

Dhanur Disciples

Dhanur Disciples painted by zptlk

The Sorcerer Kings have an excellent roster of mainstay units, not least of which are the Dhanur Disciples. For a slim 100 points you get 4 archers with Barrage (1) at 14” range and Volley 2 with a Special Rule that gives them Sureshot as a bonus while Aimed!

Defensively, they are, of course, made of paper. Defense and Evasion 1 with Resolve 2 means they never want to see combat. Though if they regrettably do, they at least have another Special Rule that lets them disengage without becoming Broken. It’s better to have this than not, but it really doesn’t swing the needle much.

You can never really go wrong with a unit of 6 to 8 of these, but they probably shouldn’t be your only Mainstay unit. Pick them as a secondary Mainstay unit instead to make sure you have a decent frontline presence unless you’re doing something very specific, like a gunline list.

Ghols

Ghols painted by zptlk

Ghols are Garbage, but they’re great at it. 65 points gives you 4 with each additional dude only netting you further 15 points. They have an absolutely terrible statline with 1s straight across the board, but they’re cheap and can put out some damage as a horde with the Burnout draw event.

When they inevitably die, either because you threw them in as a distraction or because they burned out, they net you a Confluence point each. And to get them where you need them to go, they come with Vanguard and March 6 built in. I wouldn’t say they’re an auto-include, but they’re for sure a strong pick in just about any list due to their power as an enabler and gap-filler.

Mahabharati Initiates

Mahabharati Initiates painted by zptlk

The Mahabharati Initiates are your elite Infantry choice, coming in at 110 points for a pack of 4 with a steep 30 for each additional Warrior. They’re a bit of a build-around-me unit, since their basic stats are okay but leave a bit to be desired for their cost.

1 Attack at Clash 2 with Cleave 2 and March 6 is pretty decent, and at Defense 2 with Resolve 3 and Counter-Attack they will rebuff basic line Infantry very reliably. But what puts them over the top is Support and the Primordial Bond ability. When you put all the pieces together and have something they can stand behind, like a unit of Rajakur, they can grind pretty well against other mid-tier units, if you can generate the Confluence to sustain them.

Rajakur

Rajakur painted by zptlk

The basic Infantry unit of the Sorcerer Kings reflects their character-centric nature by offering a very cheap Bodyguard option. Beyond that, they are very cost effective at 90 points for a four-man squad with 25 per additional Rajakur.

Defense and Resolve 2 as well as Clash 2 with 2 Attacks is not impressive on its own, but Cleave 1 and the Shield special rule nudge this package just enough to end up as the default first pick for just about every army – unless, again, you’re doing something very specialized.

Restricted Units

The Elementals comprise the Restricted choices of the Sorcerer Kings. Though much like in the large rank and flank game The Last Argument of Kings, their status as Restricted units matters a lot less for the Sorcerer Kings, since each character gets to unlock one unit corresponding to their chosen Court as a Mainstay unit instead.

They all come in two flavors, ranged and melee, and each have one ability that generates additional Confluence points throughout the battle.

Efreet Flamecasters

Efreet Flame Casters painted by zptlk

At 90 points for the first model and 110 for each beyond that, the Efreet Flamecasters are among the more expensive Elemental options. Barrage 3 at Volley 2 and a perilously short range of 10” seems a bit underwhelming for that price, though March 6 makes them a little more maneuverable and their additional Hits on Hit Rolls of “1” make up for it somewhat.

They generate additional Confluence by slaying Enemy Warriors and can spend it themselves with their Unique Volley Action “Dancing Flames”. If they do, they get to nominate an enemy Warrior within 10” and have him automatically suffer 2 Hits, with each Warrior within 2” of the Target also suffering 1 Hit.

They carry on the theme of chewing through cheap Infantry, even in melee, where they aren’t bad at all for a ranged unit. 4 Attacks at Clash 2, Aura of Death 1 and Defense 2 with Resolve 3 and 4 Wounds makes them fairly survivable against Chaff and basic Infantry. But both at range and in melee they can really falter against higher-tier units.

Efreet Sword Dancers

Efreet Sword Dancers painted by zptlk

The melee Efreets have a very similar vibe – even down to the same defensive profile, which is a shame. Offensively, they have an extra Attack, coming in at 5 Attacks per model with a Clash of 2, Cleave 1 and Relentless Blows for extra Hits.

Their special Rule is a Unique Inspire Action that costs 2 Confluence and gives them Flawless Strikes as well, which helps them chew through more heavily armored targets in a pinch. They’re a bit more attractive as a package compared to their ranged counterpart, especially since they only net you 70 points for the first model and 80 for each additional one.

Marid Lancers

The Elementals of the Court of Water go by the name of Marid and they consist of two Cavalry units, one a melee shock Cavalry and one a ranged support skirmisher, that both gain you 1 Confluence as a Draw Event – per model, notably.

The Lancers are the melee Cavalry option and the most expensive Elemental unit. They will set you back 95 points to start and 110 for each Warrior beyond that. They justify it though, with March 7, 3 Attacks at Clash 3, Impact 2, Brutal, Vanguard and Unstoppable. And they always count as Charging, even if they didn’t move in a straight line, as long as they end up Engaged.

Once they are stuck in, they have the option of giving Warriors Engaged with them -1 Defense at the cost of 2 Confluence as their Unique Inspire Action. They are a very tight offensive package that only suffers a bit on Defense. Defense 2 and Resolve 3 are nothing to write home about, but 5 Wounds is a pretty comfortable pillow for all but the most elite units, especially once you consider that they’re more than likely to land a devastating blow to start off any engagement.

Marid Sahar

Sahar are the rare unit that wants to do it all and kind of gets away with it. They come in at 85 points and net you 100 more for each additional rider, but they make up for it. They, too, have a March of 7 with 2 Impacts per model, but a slightly worse offensive profile of 4 Attacks at Clash 2 and Cleave 1.

Beyond that, they also offer you the option of close ranged fire support with Barrage 2 at 8” with Armor Piercing (1) and Volley 2. And if that wasn’t enough, they also have the option of casting spells:

  • Healing Mists (1 Confluence) heals a Friendly Warrior in their 5” Command Range by 1
  • Shallal (2 Confluence) targets a Friendly Warrior within Command Range and has all Enemy Warriors Engaged with the Target Driven Back

This spell suite isn’t the greatest and spending Confluence on these is expensive, but it is nice to have the option when you need it. Overall, it’s easy to overspend on these guys, but as long as you make sure that you have solid role players – hammers, anvils, objective chuds, all that – it is very satisfying to top off your list with a cheeky 2 person squad of Marid Sahar.

Steelheart Djinn

Steelheart Djinn painted by zptlk

Djinn are the Incarnate Warriors of the Court of Air, and they gain Confluence just by moving, which sets their theme as effectively as their 8” Move and the Fly Special Rule.

The Steelheart Djinn are the melee option, coming in at a cheap 75 points with only 90 per additional model. Offensively, they are a house, with 3 Attacks, Clash 3 and Cleave 3 as well as the option of a Unique Clash Action that costs 2 Confluence. If they use it, all Enemy Warriors within Engagement Range suffer 2 Hits with the Quicksilver Strikes Special Rule, which pairs nicely with their high Cleave value.

What keeps them honest is their low number of attacks and their middling defense. Evasion 2, Defense 1 and Resolve 3 with 4 Wounds isn’t exactly inspiring, even though they force Enemy Warriors to re-roll Attack results of “1”, which is really annoying, especially for units that rely on Cleave or Flawless Strikes for their damage output.

Windborne Djinn

Sorcerer Kings Windborn Djinn. Credit: Rich Nutter

Windborne Djinn have long been the worst unit in the Sorcerer Kings roster, and they’re finally getting their glow-up. 85 points to start with another 85 for each model is not bad at all when you consider their Barrage (2) at 12” with Armor Piercing 2. The kicker though is their Unique Take Aim Action, Guiding Winds. This allows them to target Enemy Warriors within range, regardless of Line of Sight or intervening Terrain.

Couple that with their peerless mobility – as well as the Rituals you have available in the Court of Air – and you have a really scary skirmisher that can be everywhere and apply additional pressure. Last but not least, if your opponent is trying to zone you out at range, they force Enemy Warriors to re-roll Hit Rolls of “1” during Volley Action.

Taken as a package, they are kind of like the Sahar: Not the stars of a list, but if you have the fundamentals down, they can be a really nice complimentary unit to support everything else that you’re doing.

Monsters

Due to the dual nature of most of their kits, the Sorcerer Kings have access to a plethora of monsters, seven all told, and they are all absolutely stunning – not just visually. With the exception of one of them, who is kind of a hybrid, all of them come in ranged and melee flavors as well.

Mahut

The Sorcerer Kings Mahut, crewed by Dhanur Disciples. Credit: Rich Nutter

The mighty Mahut is the monster of choice of the Court of Earth and the aforementioned hybrid – as well as the only one available as a character mount. The basic version comes in at 255 points, while an additional 55 points gives you the option of stapling a Maharajah to the Mahut.

The mount is a big tanky ranged weapons platform no matter which option you choose, though. Defense 4, Resolve 4 and Hardened give it a lot of staying power, while 7 Wounds before counting as Damaged and an additional 5 once it is are a lot to chew through before it dies.

Offensively, the Mahut isn’t quite impressive for its cost, with only 5 Attacks at Clash 3 and Cleave 2. Though this can be mitigated if it hits its preferred target – enemy Infantry. With Trample, the Mahut gains an additional Attack against Enemies of smaller Size, so you can choose to split your attacks between two or three enemy models to chew through weaker units more quickly.

To further push its average damage profile, the Mahut has the option to spend 2 Confluence as a Draw Event and gain the Smite and Brutal special rules, which gives it some more oomph on the Charge when combined with its Impact 2.

Once it has made its charge, it’s made to grind and take up space in the middle of the board. Marble Masterwork nets you one Confluence on Block Rolls of “1” and Mystic Barrage doles out Hits against all Enemies that end their Activation Unengaged in its massive 7” Command Range. And if you do need to get out, the Mahut has access to the Juggernaut Action to Drive Back all Enemies Engaged with it.

None of this is particularly inspiring on its own, but it all adds up to a centerpiece that can dominate the battlefield with the proper support. Just make sure that you have enough going on around the Mahut – secondary threats that force your opponent to split their forces, flanking units, ranged pressure – and it will be a massive problem.

Marid Apsara

The Apsara is the "ranged" support Monster for the Court of Water and as such, it requires a bit of finesse. It is obviously a massive target and not exactly cheap at 245 points, which is further exacerbated by its low Defense of 2, with a Resolve of 3 and only 5 Wounds, so you really don’t want it getting charged.

On the flipside, its melee output is fairly impressive with 6 Attacks, Clash 3 and Cleave 2 as well as Relentless Blows. The main attraction though is its Spellcasting. It gains you 3 Confluence as a Draw Event and rolls for Confluence instead of Essence in the Command Phase at a Spellcaster Value of 3, which it uses to cast its three spells:

Oasis (3 Confluence) gives the Regeneration Draw Event to all Friendly Warriors in Command Range

Bhanwar (X Confluence) lets you pick up X Water Warriors anywhere on the battlefield and teleport them wholly within the Apsaras Command Range

Soothing Tides (1 Confluence) lets you re-roll failed Resolve Tests of other Friendly Warriors within Command Range

All of these abilities are good, but the Soothing Tides is especially welcome as a generically good Spell to cast every round. Notably, its Command Range is huge at 6”, with a massive base and a March of 8, all of which gives you a lot of room to maneuver without losing your buffs. I really like the Apsara in a list with a 2 or 3 person squad of Marid Sahar for even more healing or to support a big frontline of Mortal Infantry.

Marid Shaytan

The Shaytan eschews all of this finesse-y support stuff in favor of just straight up murdering things. Its defensive profile is unchanged from the Apsara, which is once again a shame but its offense is real scary.

6 Attacks at Clash 4 with Cleave 3 and Impact 2 is close to the top-end for uncomplicated damage output for the Sorcerer Kings, rivalled only by the Rakshasa that we will get to below.

What they lack though, is the option of fuelling your Spellcasting and Rituals by murdering things, as the Shaytan generates Confluence for each Enemy and Friendly Warrior slain within its Command Range. And for 3 Confluence, it can make Enemies within its Command Range count as within a piece of Water Zonal Terrain as a Draw Event, which makes up for its weak defense by hampering the enemy offense.

I like the self contained engine of the Marid Shaytan a lot – especially since it provides good damage for cheaper than both flavors of Rakshasa for 235 points – but you still need a reason to take it over the Rakshasa. If you’re planning to rely on Rituals and the Elementals special Confluence abilities a lot though, there is a lot to love here.

Rakshasa Bakasura

Sorcerer Kings Rakshasa Bakasura Credit by Parabellum Games

Where the Marid Shaytan is uncomplicated, the Rakshasa are blunt. They are intercontinental ballistic missiles that you shoot straight at their preferred targets – which, for the Bakasura, is Enemy Characters.

For 2 Confluence, they can immediately perform the Duel Command Ability as a Draw Event, even if their target is not a Monster. A scary prospect for enemy Characters with the Bakasura’s damage-dealing profile: 8 Attacks at Clash 4, Cleave 2, Terrifying and Flurry.

You have to take care when and how you deploy the Rakshasa though, as both are not exactly durable, which is a recurring theme with most Sorcerer Kings units. Their defensive stats aren’t terrible, but at Defense and Resolve 3, with only 6 Wounds before dipping into the Damaged profile, they will just die if you ram them straight up the center, wasting you 245 points for the Bakasura.

Rakshasa Ravanar

Sorcerer Kings Rakshasa Ravanar Credit by Parabellum Games

Speaking of ramming things, the Rakshasa Ravanar is the Monster hunter among the Rakshasa, which is why we should mention the Fire Lord Special Rule here. It shares the rule with the Bakasura, but it really shines here. When either Rakshasa slays an Enemy Warrior, you gain Confluence equal to their size. This isn’t bad on the Bakasura, since it can blend through enemy Infantry very reliably, but the Ravanar takes it up a notch.

The distinguishing factor here is Fiend Hunter, which supercharges its already impressive offensive profile of 6 Attacks at Clash 4,Cleave 4 and Terrifying when fighting enemy Brutes and Monsters. This guy is going to be generating you an absolute truckload of Confluence when facing monster mash armies.

And to make sure that the Rakasha Ravanar gets where it needs to be going, it has the option of spending 3 Confluence on a Unique Clash Action which has all Enemies within Engagement Range suffer 2 Hits with the Cleave 3 Special Rule. This isn’t really your go-to, but if the Ravanar gets bogged down by enemy chaff, it’s nice to be able to sweep them aside.

This all sounds really inspiring, and that is because I personally like the Ravanar more, even though it’s objectively worse than the Bakasura. The distinguishing factor between the two is cost – both points cost and opportunity cost.

The Ravanar is more expensive at 260 points and wants to face enemy Monsters or Brutes, which not every list has. What every list has, though, is Characters. And to top it off, the Bakasura, which hunts Characters, costs 245 points. It’s sad, but it’s been true since the kit was released – the Bakasura is just straight up better than the Ravanar, unless you’re playing for fun or know your meta is heavy on big boys.

Trinavarta Chandavat

Much like the Marid Apsara, the Trinavarta Chandavat is a ranged Spellcasting Monster that rolls for Confluence instead of Essence, and it does so well at Spellcaster 4. Its profile is somewhat underwhelming for the cost – Evasion and Resolve 3 with 5/4 Wounds in terms of defense and 5 Attacks at Clash 3 with no Cleave offensively at 265 points.

So it really all comes down to its spell selection:

  • Bavandar (3 Confluence) targets one Enemy within the Chandavats 6” Command Range to suffer 3 Hits. All other Enemies within 3” of the Target also suffer 1 Hit, all of which come with the Precise Shot Special Rule.
  • Slicing Winds (1 Confluence) has each Enemy Warrior that performs a Clash or Volley Action against the Chandavat suffer 1 Hit which can only be Blocked by Defense Rolls below the Hit Roll.
  • Vortex (2 Confluence) gives each Friendly Warrior within Command Range +1 March and each Enemy Warrior within Command Range -1 March.

I’m not over the moon with this package. While the nuke is nice, the effective range of the Trinavarta Chandavat is a bit too low for my liking, especially with its low defense. It has good mobility – March 9 and Fly almost lets it teleport – but if you lose priority it is very easy for the Chandavat to go straight to the Wounded profile or just straight up die. If it had some kind of basic Barrage Action with any kind of range, I’d like it a lot more, but as it stands, I can’t see myself using this version of the kit.

Trinavarta Sabhagrih

For the exact opposite reaction, see the Trinavarta Sabhagrih. While also fragile, the Sabhagrih can generally be relied upon to take the first swing in any engagement with its stellar movement. To add insult to injury, you gain Confluence on enemy Hit Rolls of “6” that target the Sabhagrih and it can actually teleport at the cost of 3 Confluence, outside of enemy Command Ranges. And it just really, really hurts on the charge.

7 Attacks, Clash 4, Cleave 1, Flawless Strikes and Terrifying make it really good at taking out priority targets or elite, lower model count units, and it also has some added utility in acting as a spellcasting conduit. All Warriors within its 6” Command Range can be targeted by your Warlord, regardless of distance or line of sight.

I really like this package, though I can’t shake the feeling that some of its rules should have ended up on the Chandavat instead.

Sample Army List

=== First Blood ===

Generic Sampler [1000/1000]

Sorcerer Kings

* Raj (1) [150]: Bound to the Elements, Court of Fire, Efreet Sword Dancers

* Efreet Sword Dancers (3) [230]:

* Marid Sahar (2) [185]:

* Rakshasa Bakasura (1) [245]:

* Rajakur (8) [190]:

It’s very easy to get lost in the sauce when writing Sorcerer Kings army lists. There are just so many knobs to turn and things to twiddle with, which is why I am exercising restraint here for a fairly generic starting point: Rajakur to hold the line, Efreet Sword Dancers with a Raj leading them and a Rakshasa Bakasura as Hammers and a cheeky double squad of Marid Sahar for some ranged support and mobile utility.

It’s probably not quintessentially Sorcerer Kings – with a faction this synergistic and diverse, nothing really can be –, but it’s straight forward and uncomplicated while letting newer players dip their toes into the spellcasting and utility madness with the Marids.

Final Thoughts

PHEW. This was a lot to get through, and that is without the bespoke Elemental dual kit for the Court of Earth or the Mahabharati Sorcerer Saints cavalry which we are all eagerly awaiting.

The Sorcerer Kings have a lot of bells and whistles on them, which can easily be overwhelming to get into. But they look gorgeous and are incredibly rewarding when you have all of their rules down pat and know what you’re doing. And they offer just enough straight forward stuff that you have a decent on-ramp into the gameplay side of things if you know where to look.
