BattleTech Tournament Report - ABC 26: Atlanta BattleTech at Challenges

September 11th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Hello again, MechWarriors! Lynn's back again to tell you the tale of a local Atlanta-area tournament she participated in: ABC 26: Atlanta BattleTech at Challenges!

This tournament was originally planned for July, but unfortunately the host store got evicted from the shopping mall they were located in (their second eviction from a dying mall being redeveloped into a "mixed-use development" within the past two or three years) on short notice, delaying the event until they could get settled into a new location. Ordinarily I wouldn't use this space to plug a store, and I want to make it clear I am in no way being compensated for this article, but given that Challenges has had a really rough go of it in recent times, I will point out that they do have a web store available if you'd like to help an FLGS out.

The Packet

As it happened, when all the dust settled on the rescheduling, ABC 26 ended up becoming my ever-so-slightly-belated birthday present, landing on Saturday, August 29th. Originally planned for small-scale, 5k BV, three-unit-cap, single-mapsheet games due to the shopping mall's relatively short hours of operation, the TO stuck to his plan for brisker-paced play even after the relocation, allowing for a leisurely noon start time... which I dearly appreciated, given that a particularly busy birthday week kept me up until four AM the night before, trying (and narrowly failing) to finish my painting! I was still fairly bleary from sleep deprivation going into the event; I am definitely getting too old to pull back-to-back half-nighters writing articles and painting minis.

ABC 26 was envisioned as something of a sneak preview for the new toys entering regular rules with the Core Rulebook; while it was still run on a basis of the existing BMM / TW rules (with just a few adjustments), the tournament was set in the ilClan era and all technology (except Arrow IV) featured in new Core was legal for use. Vees and Battle Armor were also legal, to cater to my local meta's thirst for combined arms. I'm pretty sure I remember one of our regulars boycotting an event once because he couldn't use VTOLs!

The pulse cap for the event was 24 damage (with RE-Lasers and ERs counting for half, rear-mounted pulse not counting against the cap at all, and BA pulse lasers counting for the amount of damage they do on a cluster roll of 7), skills couldn't be better than 3 or worse than 6, you needed to take at least one mech, and front-loaded initiative, backwards level change, careful stand, enhanced flamers, expanded arm flipping, floating crits, one-armed prone fire, and ECCM were all on. That was about it for restrictions.

Missions were revealed in advance of the tournament, but after lists were locked in. The packet hinted that probes, improved piloting (and hands, I forgot this mentioned hands, whoops), and anti-infantry weapons could be useful.

The List

I have been swearing up, down, and sideways that the moment a tournament let me bring mechs with Quad Turrets I would run a Sarath OB, an incredibly funny 50-ton puppy-mech with a Plasma Rifle, four taloned feet, and miscellaneous other weapons to get its TSM up and pumping to spike its kicks to 30 damage. Since its plasma and ER flamers covered me for anti-infantry weapons and its melee gave me an excuse to raise its Piloting, I felt good about how it could deal with the mission hints, so I locked it in at 4/4 skills, 1,623 BV.

For my EWAR purposes, I decided to bring a Black Hawk-KU OG, a configuration of the Kuritans' knockoff Nova which is genuinely more useful than many configurations of the actual Nova: Bloodhound Active Probe, Angel ECM, Snub-Nose PPC, three MXPLs, TAG if it's relevant. Builds movement heat on an alpha strike. I bumped it up to 4/4 skills as well, taking it to 1,891 BV.

I spent a long time hemming and hawing over my list for this tournament (which didn't help with getting it painted!). Had I committed to taking Liberty's factional Nuzlocke challenge, my last body would've had to have been a Guillotine 5M at 4/5 skills, but I really couldn't get excited about the Guillotine in the context of this list. I wanted something with better ranged damage projection. I wanted something sickeningly BV-efficient. I wanted... a Longbow 13C.

The MML-7-boating Longbow 13C is very much a known quantity in the Southeastern BT meta, having spiked in popularity over the last year or so, but this was actually my first time running it, and I was excited to get it to table. It's an ammo piñata with an Inner Sphere XL engine and somewhat light armor for its mass, but it's one of the absolute queens of budget damage vomit; only 1,397 BV at 4/5 skills. I ran two tons of LRM ammo, three of regular SRMs, and one of Infernos.

The fact that I couldn't *quite* get three miniatures fully painted for this tournament in a week is a good sign I should never take up 40k. Credit: Lynn B.

I was busy enough in the leadup to ABC 26 that I never ended up actually practicing with this list. But hey, I never practiced with the final iteration of my WarZone '26 list and that turned out alright, surely this would as well, right? Right?

Let's... let's take a look at how that went down.

The Event

We were just a touch cramped for this event, on tables meant for MtG rather than miniatures gaming, but since we were playing on a single mapsheet that wasn't really a problem anyway. I do understand Challenges is working on reorganizing the new venue's play space, so ABC may have a bit more breathing room in its next outing! Due to a last-minute drop or two, we ran at ten participants, a small but formidable field.

I really enjoyed how deeply ABC 26 committed to a silly theme. In the BattleTech universe there's a city of Atlanta on the planet of Savannah in the Free Worlds League. Inspired by the inescapable buzz around real-world Atlanta being one of the World Cup host cities (a reference which would've been more timely had ABC 26 met its originally-planned July kickoff time, alas), this tournament saw Savannah's Atlanta chosen as a host city for the "Canopy Worlds' Cup," a sporting competition organized by the fanon "Canopian BattleSports Association," or "Canopy." Unfortunately for the gathered sports fans, just as real-world Atlanta has issues with "Lost Cause" Confederate apologists, the Canopy Worlds' Cup quickly ran into trouble caused by the Neo-Blakists of the Sons and Daughters of the Word of Blake!

Mission One: Collapse Burning Architecture

This mission was fun, but it had a lot of moving parts, particularly given that it imported the building rules, smoke rules, fire rules, and scanning rules from the new Core Rules, which all had to be recapped in the mission for the most-players who don't yet have access to said rules. With the fun-but-extensive fluff included, this came out to something like seven pages of mission text, which a few players either hadn't read in advance or had only lightly skimmed. For my part, it didn't help that I'd arrogantly largely-skipped the scanning section since I thought I was certain how it worked, only to find out that Core Rules scanning differs greatly from Hot Spots: Draconis Reach scanning!

The premise here was that 1.) the Neo-Blakists had set a bunch of buildings on fire, and 2.) our Canopy team got diverted to help combat the blaze by smashing derelict buildings, rescuing civilians, and potentially capturing or killing the Neo-Blakists responsible, but 3.) a rival team got assigned to the same sector of the city as us, and 4.) one of our units got struck by a PPC blast from somewhere, provoking us to assume the rival team was attacking us.

There were ten level one light buildings arranged on the map in a diamond pattern, each on fire with smoke rising two levels above its roof. Five were abandoned, four contained civilians in need of rescue, one contained Neo-Blakists, but who was where was randomized via tokens pulled from a bag. Players could identify the occupants (if any) of the buildings through physical examination of an adjacent building during the physical attack phase (which could be combined with a punch attack for dual-purpose rescue and building-collapse if you had hands), and/or through scanning a building in range in the end phase (with new Core Rules scanning, so Piloting + 3 TN at range 2 without a Probe, with probes extending the range and lowering the TN); the same mech could both perform a physical examination and a scan provided that two buildings were in range. Physical examination would auto-succeed if the unit making the examination was on or inside the building, though of course mechs might crush the building (and any potential civilians inside) by landing on top of one or forcing their way into one, and non-fireproof battle armor might simply die from the building being on fire. Should a physical examination succeed (with a higher roll than the opponent, if two players attempted to investigate the same building on the same turn), you could rescue any civilians immediately. If you found the Blakists, you could either choose to kill them for five points or try to capture them for a chance at 10 points; the capturing unit needed to either survive til the end of the game or withdraw off your board edge in order to score those 10 points; if your opponent destroyed the unit carrying the Blakists they would instead score the points for killing them.

Points were scored for collapsing buildings regardless of who might or might not be occupying them; since you scored 5 points per building collapse you contributed to (to a max of 35), but only lost 2 points for killing civilians (while denying your opponent the 5 points for capturing those civilians!), the ultimate victor in the tournament decided that trying to investigate the buildings at all was a waste of time and simply blew up the buildings as fast as possible and let Blake sort them out!

At the start of the game, we each had to assign a ten-damage hit to one of our units to represent the opening PPC blast which triggered the violence between our teams; we could choose which unit took it and where the hit went. I assigned mine to one of the arms of the Longbow. We'll... we'll circle back to that decision.

Oh, also, piling another wrinkle on top of an already very wrinkly mission, even if you weren't directly in a hex on fire, every mech on the map notched an additional 3 heat in the Heat Phase each turn from ambient fwoom.

For this round I was paired against Nathan (Warp Angel), a similarly-sleep-deprived fellow from Chattanooga, who brought an extensive, well-painted collection of 'Mechs and fielded a Jade Phoenix A, Hierofalcon B, and a point of Afreet Battle Armor (Jade Falcon), with the official IWM minis for at least the birbs, if not the BA. I didn't write down anything about their skills, but it looks like just from sheer BV limitations the mechs must have been 4/5 and the BA at 4/4 skill. He was a very genial opponent, even as we both fumbled our way through sleep-fog, and I am obliged to pass on this very important reminder about his list: Whenever I complained that "Birds are scary!" he always chimed in with, "Yes, but they're not real!"

Birds, real or no, are quite scary, but my BHKU-OG was an absolute champ for this mission, able to reliably suss out the contents of two buildings per turn with her mobility and her Bloodhound-enhanced scanning, and while fortune was somewhat fickle for me in this game, the dice fell in my favor when it counted most.

I won Initiative in all but the last round of this game, letting me fairly ably control how much Jade-Phoenix-related danger I was exposing my mechs to, and the big bird's Ultra 20 only landed a shot once in the entire game, regardless of TN... though it was an impactful one, double-tapping and hitting both arms of the Longbow, blowing the arm I'd softened with the initial PPC hit off completely.

In a stroke of luck, my Sarath located the Blakists very quickly, but my decision to capture them did mean that my aggressive, somewhat-squishy melee mech was racing around with a high-value objective in tow almost the entire game, which was more than a little stressful!

My opponent was fairly reliant on entering buildings for guaranteed investigations, something which worked quite well with the nimble, fireproof Afreets, but was significantly riskier with his Hierofalcon, which risked collapsing every building it landed atop. Still, he preferenced jumping to building tops to hide in the smoke, until I took the risk of shooting an unscanned building out from under the Hierofalcon to force it to fall and stop jumping.

Sometimes sacrifices must be made... for the greater good. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Don't worry, I can assure you that I wrote letters to the families of each and every one of those civilians who valiantly gave their lives so that I could ground the threat of SRM Birb!

Luck bit both of us when his overheating, grounded Hierofalcon limped to a position where I could move my Sarath into head-kicking range. He missed my Sarath with a would-be UAC/20 back-shot (merely thinning its right rear leg armor with the pulses), while I swung for the fences with a mighty 30-damage punch-table kick against the Hierofalcon on something like a 5 or 6 to hit... and rolled a 3 and missed.

Some swings... and some misses. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

The next round, the Birbs had to focus on cooling down, while Mx. Sarath poked another building, my Longbow did its best to level the buildings I had scanned or investigated (weathering nuisance fire from the Afreets like a champ), and the BHKU kept trucking along identifying objectives as quickly as possible.

My opponent baited the Sarath in by walking the Jade Phoenix to the edge of its kick range, hoping to remove both its threat and my captive Blakists from the board, but once again fate saw us both stumble: he was unable to put critical damage into the Sarath or the vulnerable Longbow and, as I recall, flubbed knocking down a building, while my Sarath put a full TSM kick into the PPC-weakened leg of the Jade Phoenix... and got laughed off when it couldn't confirm a crit on the Big Bird, who had plenty of internal structure to soak a hit like that.

As we neared the turn limit for the round, I attempted to cripple or kill the damaged Hierofalcon and came up short, but my dice did run hot... when my own Longbow fell over. It killed itself off a through-armor crit to an ammo bin, RIP.

...and I OOP. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

When all the dust had settled, my Longbow was the only casualty, but I'd maxed out my scoring on building-destruction and Blakist-capturing, and rescued half the captives (and "only" killed a quarter of them!), while my opponent had lagged behind just a little on primary scoring.

Final result: Victory, 58-44.

Sidebar: Secondary Scoring

As is the norm for Atlanta-area tournaments, ABC 26 featured achievement-based secondary scoring alongside its primary objectives and its combat scoring. Unlike some previous events (under different TOs) like WarZone, the secondaries varied between missions, but they were consistently a blend of "reward for doing something good / thematic" and "compensation for a bad thing happening to you," and fairly easy to max out if you were paying any attention to them, since each mission listed six conditions which could score points and you only needed to fulfill three of them in order to max out secondary scoring.

I was not paying the best attention, and I kept giving away free points in this category... one of the secondaries was always "Have one of your units be targeted by three enemy units in the same turn," and I was focus-fire-minded enough I gave this away in every mission except possibly the last. None of my missions swung on this, and if I could've actually secured any kind of a kill or even crippling damage through focus fire the trade would be well worth it, but it is something I wish I'd been more mindful of.

Mission Two: Coordinated Ball Aggression

This mission saw us actually playing the sport of Canopy, a roughly soccerish game. Our forces were joined by a fourth teammate for this, a Locust 1V at 4/3 skills, ensuring that even a slow team would have one mech at least hypothetically capable of skilled ball-handling.

There were a host of clever rules for passing between mechs, striking the ball towards the goal, etc., which proved almost pointless in my particular game for reasons I'll get into later. One thing that undermined the Beautiful Game side of things a touch was that Canopy offered an alternate method of scoring for more gunnery-focused competitors, with aimed shots (dealing 10 or more cumulative damage in a single turn) landed on a "canopy" mounted above the goal, at the same difficulty as an aimed shot at the head of an immobile mech, also scoring a point.

For tournament scoring, 1/2/3+ goals scored in the net would score 10/15/20 VP, 1/2/3+ shots scored on the canopy would score 10/15/20 VP, and winning the Canopy game-within-a-game would score 20 VP (or 7 for each player if the Canopy game was tied), on top of the usual secondary and "kill" scoring.

I put "kill" in quotation marks there for a reason - thematically, all weapons damage between competitors in the game of Canopy was simulated, but physical attacks were real, leading to penalties. Any damaging physical attack would lead to a "yellow card" which your opponent could cash in the following turn to make an attack with any one of four weapons (AC/10, IS LPL, the new Light Plasma Rifle, or an IS SSRM 6) from any one of four Gunnery 3 turrets hanging above the playing field; any physical attack which actually destroyed a mech's head, inflicted a cockpit crit or a Crew Killed crit against a vehicle, or killed a battle armor trooper was considered to have actually killed someone in serious violation of Canopy's rules and the resultant "red card" would functionally destroy the perpetrator's mech.

There was also a cute thematic rule letting one of your mechs take a dive while adjacent to an enemy mech (suffering the same damage as an accidental fall) to try to convince the ref (on a roll which scaled in difficulty with repeated dives) that you'd been fouled and give your opponent a yellow card. It gave me a chuckle, but it never came up in my game, in no small part because I couldn't stop actually fouling with my Sarath, given that kicking is its reason for existence.

My stomach dropped when I saw who I was paired against for this round, having drawn regular reader, commenter, and Atlanta-area Liberty-equivalent ChahDresh as my opponent. I had strongly hoped to avoid seeing him until round three, but no such luck. He's a strong competitor at all times, and he brought a formidable trio of mechs and a very focused game plan to this game, while I was running on obsessions, paranoia, and sleep deprivation. It would've been an uphill battle for me even if dice luck had skewed hard in my favor.

Reader, dice luck did not skew hard in my favor.

I was up against a Nightstar 9FC (the 4/6 Nightstar with the LB-10s), the terrifying NAIS Enforcer III (Large VSP, Snub PPC, TarComp, etc), and the also-terrifying Argus 6F (with Precision Ammo in that AC/20, of course), all at 4/5 skills.

Deployment was a bit wild in this game, though fitting for the soccer theme: We each had to put one mech at the center of the map to receive the initial "kick-off" of the match (closer to a two-mech rugby scrum, really), while the rest of our force could deploy anywhere on our half of the field. I put the Sarath at midfield opposite his sacrificial Locust, with my Black Hawk and Longbow sticking close to it and my lonely Locust hanging back as a token goalie, while his Enforcer, Nightstar, and Argus were positioned to more-or-less envelop my force, with the Nightstar kept just a bit back towards the goal.

Kickoff. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

I thought that I'd won the kickoff at first, because the record sheets the TO handed out for the Locust mistakenly listed its skills at 4/5 and I'd forgotten the packet said it was 4/3; with the Locust's actual skill at ball revealed it stole the ball from my hopeful Sarath and plunged in the direction of my goal. I'd won initiative, but I did have to move something first, so I postured my Longbow somewhat aggressively; while this allowed the Enforcer and Argus to flood into its rear arc, seeking an easy kill, it also allowed my Sarath to get into melee range of the Enforcer. Since the heat wasn't on for the Sarath yet, it only took a big chunk out of the Enforcer's leg armor and didn't go internal. The Enforcer, in turn, did go internal on the CT rear of the Longbow with the LVSPL, but it didn't confirm a crit, and both it and the Argus were otherwise unable to really capitalize on the Longbow's vulnerable position aside from knocking it over (but its pilot did take damage from a failed seatbelt check in the fall, a surprise tool which would hurt us later). Meanwhile, Black Hawk fell back to deal with the Locust, ripped an arm and a leg off of it and damaged its gyro, and received the ball on the bounce when the Locust collapsed.

Off to a rollicking start! Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Here was the first, though not last, occasion where my obsession with Playing Soccer would bite me. I should have done anything with the Black Hawk other than exposing its rear to the incredible firepower of an Argus and an NAIS Enforcer III, but it had the ball, and the goal beckoned. I longed to hear an announcer screaming GOOOOOOOOOOOOL with all the enthusiasm he could possible muster. I ran the Black Hawk KU into midfield, and ChahDresh's dynamic duo fell dutifully into its rear arc. I was able to get the Sarath, now juiced up on that good TSM heat, into melee with the Argus, and did significant damage to its leg, but that was hardly a worthwhile trade, because the AC/20, LVSP, and Snubbie all biased into the left torso rear of the BHKU, dealing 41 damage to a location with 22 total health and an Inner Sphere XL engine inside its juicy center. Total annihilation.

There was previously a Black Hawk standing on that black hex in the middle. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

Past this point my gameplan really fell apart. The Snub PPC on the BHKU had been my best bet at scoring shots on the canopy (the Plasma Rifle on the Sarath could do it, but with worse range bands and a likely malus from running hot, and the Longbow, despite being my dedicated ranged threat, was incapable of making an aimed shot with its cluster weapons), and while I really wanted to punt the ball into nets, that method of scoring would be slower and clumsier and prone to more points of failure. While my Locust circled around towards the ball this turn (not reaching it), my Longbow and Sarath turned their attention towards digging me out of the sizable BV hole I found myself in.

My Longbow landed some significant damage on the Argus this turn and planted it in the dirt, while my Sarath kicked a leg off the Enforcer... but not before the Enforcer rung the bell of my canopy with its LVSP, putting ChahDresh's planned victory strategy into motion. This was also the point where paranoia began to rule me; I was terrified of kicking any downed opponents for fear of the small-but-non-zero chance of hitting a head, drawing a red card, and making my numbers situation worse. This was arguably a sensible precaution when it came to the Longbow's opportunity to end my opponent's Locust, given the vast gulf in value between them (though the Locust would go on to prove its own worth...), but it was absolute folly that I didn't use the Sarath to finish off the prone Enforcer III, instead letting it keep propping itself up on its Snub PPC arm to snipe my canopy with its LVSP, turn after turn. That overblown fear of killing sent my Sarath off to join the Locust in attempting to play ball, leaving just my Longbow to deal with the two heavily-damaged formerly-mobile threats in my half of the field.

My hope was to cripple the Argus with the Longbow and then turn it towards finishing off the Enforcer, but that's... not exactly how things turned out. It did cripple the Argus! Ripped its AC/20 side clean off with an ammo explosion, as I recall! (Non-fatally, since it has a Light Engine rather than an XL.) Even knocked it out with the pilot damage from the big boom (and/or the fall)!

But, uh, problem. See, I'd kept racking up Yellow Cards turn after turn with all the Sarath violence. And when ChahDresh cashed in his Yellow Card that turn, he hit my Longbow with the SSRM 6, hit its head... and I failed the 5+ consciousness check. Plus, the Argus did more than 20 damage to it that same turn, so it automatically fell and automatically dealt a third hit to my pilot. Uh-oh.

My Locust, meanwhile, had jumped onto the ball with the intent to pass it to the Sarath, which could easily punt it into the goal... but my opponent's silly still-not-dead Locust, one-armed-prone firing its Medium Laser Of Doom, went internal on my Locust's left leg and double-crit its upper leg and hip. Oof, ouch, owie.

This Is Fine. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

From there, the Longbow failed to wake up, while the Argus did. The Enforcer III kept plinking away at my canopy unmolested. My Locust managed to get up and shimmy off the ball, though, so my Sarath could hop onto it and wind up for the big kick, my dreamed-of GOOOOOOOOAL...

So, naturally, the Nightstar put both an LB-10 slug and an ER Medium Laser right into the Sarath's head, killing it the instant it touched the ball. Jeeze.

I did somehow manage a spot of emotional vengeance by knocking over the Nightstar with my own goddamn Locust (all I wrote down was that I did it with the ML + one MG; not sure if that was piling on top of damage from the dearly departed Sarath to force a PSR or if something went internal), but it committed the sin of touching the ball again and evaporated shortly thereafter. ChahDresh gradually picked apart my unconscious survivor's Longbow until it suffered Death By XL Engine, I was tabled, that was game. I lost 23 to 95, without notching a single point on the Primary Objective for myself.

ChahDresh very kindly says that he felt like he was barely hanging on throughout this game, and that it was much closer than the score would indicate. And it's true that the deaths of my Black Hawk and Sarath were extremely flukey, and the Longbow shouldn't have taken such a long nap on a 7+ roll to wake back up! However, what I see looking back over the game is a man with a plan facing a woman without one. I made desperate, emotional plays prioritizing the Beautiful Game in my mind rather than the actual scoring scenarios and actual risk thresholds in front of me.

Had I held back the Sarath to clean up the Enforcer and Argus, threats of red card be damned, I might have had a path to victory. Had I played cagier with the BHKU and tried to threaten the Canopy with it while dodging the Nightstar's vengeance, I might could've gotten some actual scoring going. Hell, I might even have benefitted from being a bit more tactical about when to go for physical attacks, slowing down the Yellow Card damage against me.

But I wanted to kick the ball. I wanted to hear the announcer's shout, wanted to hear the adulation of the crowds.

Alas, I only ever heard them in my Longbow pilot's dreams.

Mission Three: Cannibal Blakist Apocalypse

So, the pitch here was that the Neo-Blakists breached the CDC (a thing which the Atlanta on Savannah also certainly has, naturally!) and were stupid enough about it that they got themselves infected with an Old-Blakist zombie plague. The hordes were closing in around us, and our only hope of escape was an evac dropship... which only had berths to pull out four units, leaving my and my opponent's six units to duke it out for space on that last dropship out.

Scoring here was a little funky... while we were encouraged to kill as many zombies as we could (that's what the anti-infantry weapons were for!), and were told to track how many zombie kills we scored, the zombie-killing didn't actually score any points, and evacuating aboard the dropship was only worth up to 30 of the 60 primary points available... the other half of the primary points were scored progressively for having active units near player-placed camera drones at the end of each round, so our sponsors could get some dramatic footage of us fighting off the zombie hordes.

The zombie rules were pretty aggressive. Every unit in or adjacent to a hex containing zombies during the weapons phase of each turn would effectively take an automatically-successful Leg Attack, including crit roll; if nothing was adjacent to them they would instead roll a Gunnery 4, 2/4/6 range, 4-ballistic-damage attack against the closest target. When they moved, they would move one hex... and then, if they didn't have to change levels or move through non-clear terrain to move to that hex, they would automatically move a second hex. They could move over all terrain, including swimming through water. They would move adjacent to a nearby opponent if possible, shambling towards the middle of the map if there wasn't an opponent close enough, shambling towards the dropship once it had landed if they'd already reached midboard. You had to kill fifty zombies (cumulative with any kills your opponent scored) from a single horde to disperse it, and they suffered no degradation until they dispersed.

I was grumpy all throughout this game through no fault of my opponent or the TO, purely on the grounds of mistakes that I made off of rash consideration of my poor memory of reading the mission packet.

See, I had come in very enthusiastic about killing zombies. I had calculated that the Longbow was capable of killing two entire hordes on its own per turn with Inferno SRMs, and the Sarath could kill an entire horde on its own with slightly-hot dice on its heat damage. I planned to cooly walk in off my side of the board and then instantly wipe 1-2 hordes off the map as they shambled on behind me.

Then the trouble started.

First, I had misremembered the zombies' movement rules. I remembered the bit about them moving one hex, but I forgot the part about them then moving a second hex if the first one was clear. My entire first-turn movement plan was to stage the Sarath and Longbow just outside the hordes' reach and blast 'em right away, but instead of having one comfortable hex between them and the swarm, they... they were immediately adjacent to the zombies. They didn't need to be! I had the speed! But I was equally arrogant and stupid, and there they were.

Second, I got greedy for quick camera scoring and deployed the Longbow and the Sarath together rather than sending them against different hordes. Sensibly speaking, the Longbow should've gone on by itself, probably in the center due to its relatively limited mobility, and focused on killing one horde, and then maybe the Sarath and Black Hawk could've come on in a different spot and combined fire against a second to wipe it as well. Instead I sent the Longbow and Sarath both to hang out next to the camera in the middle while the BHKU jumped on over on the right side, and the left-side zombie horde was left unmolested.

Third, I underestimated how difficult it would actually be to get the Longbow and Sarath across the river crossing in the middle of the map given the intervening hill, worries about exposing vulnerable sides to either mechs in front of me or zombies behind me, etc.

Fourth, on top of all else, I didn't even focus on guaranteeing eradication of the zombie horde which rushed up adjacent to the Sarath and Longbow; I got greedy and tried to split fire (even though the Longbow's shots were at medium range!) to try to kill both the middle and right-hand hordes in that one turn... and because I stretched for the lower-odds kill with the Sarath in the middle and for worse to-hit numbers with the Longbow shooting across to the right-side horde, I didn't kill either.

And then the zombies immediately crit the hip of my Sarath, basically ruining its value as a melee mech while also forcing me to keep the TSM running (worsening the accuracy of its anti-zombie weaponry) just to maintain some semblance of speed.

Photos taken moments before zombie-related disaster. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

And then on turn two I realized I couldn't actually get the Longbow out of zombie range because of the direction I had it turned, it was going to be one MP short. And then zombies crit its hip, turning it into a 2/3 mech.

Basically, by the end of turn two the two biggest damage-dealers in my force were both functionally crippled by my own poor planning and my opponent rolling hot on zombie crit confirmations, having taken no or almost no damage from the actual opposing force, just from the environmental-hazard NPCs.

I was so incandescently angry with myself!

I threw this game!

I could've done perfectly well, I was excited to do well, I was thrilled to have a chance to kill zombies, and I threw this game into the toilet and flushed all my hopes away, immediately out of the gate, entirely because I mental-mapped my moves and the zombie moves completely incorrectly. So frustrating! So avoidable! So utterly my own fault!

Also I had an opponent.

He was running a competent, though not particularly flashy, trio:

- A Black Knight of some sort... he told me it was the Royal, but then it was firing normal Large Lasers rather than LPLs, I was confused on this point several times and was assured they were normal LLs, so I guess it was the non-Royal original? I didn't look at its sheet because I was in such a tizzy over zombies, and I absolutely should have.

- A Thunderbolt 7SE, gauss rifle and quad MPLs.

- The original Fulcrum heavy hovertank, upped to 3 piloting.

Looking back over this, I realize two things... One, something else must have had upgraded skills, because that doesn't add up to 5k if the mechs were at 4/5, and two, these were Marians, the only place where this trio could coexist in the ilClan Era is in the Marian Hegemony. Huh.

My opponent had a few zombie troubles, but the only critical damage he took off them was losing a jump jet on the Thunderbolt, and he was able to put enough water between them and himself to slow down their advance. The Fulcrum made an extremely daring attack run along the river at the center of the map to try to come exacerbate my Sarath's problems, and for a moment, it really impressed me...

I have never seen a single hovercraft make, and pass, as many sideslip checks in one turn as this valiant little Fulcrum made getting here. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

...but on its second turn mucking about on my half of the board it exposed itself to too much fire and took a Crew Stunned result. Slowed and defanged by the crit, it stayed too close to the action, and I certainly would've killed it except a zombie horde beat me to it.

My opponent's Thunderbolt ended up in a scrum with all my mechs, while the Black Knight stood a little further back, firing lasers into the fray. Despite missing every shot it took with the gauss rifle, the Thunderbolt managed, I believe I recall, to double-MPL headcap the Sarath from behind, while in the same turn the Black Knight TAC'd an ammo bin on the Longbow and sent it into the hereafter.

By the end of this shooting phase the Sarath and Longbow would both be gone. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

This game played out more slowly than my previous games (probably in part because I wouldn't stop fuming about my zombie misadventures - I have a bad habit of taking self-inflicted ills very poorly), and by this point we were brushing up against the time limit. My opponent was able to walk his Black Knight right into the dropship, but when I tried to follow with my Black Hawk, I was reminded that it wasn't possible to jump onto the dropship, you could only load in by ground movement or by moving adjacent to it with zero MP and allowing the dropship's cranes to carry you on. I was too far away to evacuate my sole surviving mech.

I was, at least, able to drag the Thunderbolt into hell with me, knocking one of its legs off and leaving it unable to reach the dropship either. The Marian Black Knight jetted away as the Final Girl of this particular horror movie.

So close, yet too far. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

My opponent and I had both been able to max out camera VP along the way (it was unintentionally easy for me since the hipped Sarath and Longbow took multiple turns just to physically move past the camera I'd placed on their little peninsula), but I was well behind on kill points (the one-legged Thunderbolt still technically wasn't crippled!), didn't get anyone onto the dropship, and only scored one secondary objective (the condolence points for getting critted by zombies), so I lost 48 to 75.

Results

Well, not only did I go 1-2, the final placement was based solely on points scored, and my second game sure was a stinker in that regard... I ultimately placed 9th out of 10, almost at the very back of the pack.

I can't say I didn't have a solid strength of schedule, however; in the end, ChahDresh took first and Warp Angel took second, so I was throwing kicks with the very top of the field in the first two rounds!

The trophies were all mechs manufactured at the Technicron facility on Savannah, it was a very cute touch. Photo Credit: Lynn B.

The TO had hinted that there might be a reward for killing the maximum number of zombies, and both I and my friend Code (who brought a friggin Ares to a 5k BV tournament!) were gunning as hard as we could for it... and we actually ended precisely tied at 154 zombies killed each, only for neither of us to actually get the related certificate... as it turned out, the zombie kills were merely the largest contributor to a pool of "good guy points" also scored by saving civilians, lost by killing civilians, and won by scoring in the Canopy game (thus pleasing the crowd), and neither Code nor I actually was the top of the good-guy stakes, both losing out to local player Jack, who also came in an undefeated third in the overall standings!

Finally, Nathan's gorgeous, nonexistent birds took pride of place on the painting podium!

Nathan (Warp Angel)'s prize-winning Jade Falcons. Photo Credit: Ryan N.

Closing Thoughts - This Event

I am a little disappointed by my performance in this tournament - it felt like there was a big uptick in just dunderheaded, avoidable mistakes from my side of the field, which the sleep deprivation can't have helped with - but I had an excellent time nonetheless!

Listwise, I definitely felt the sting of having only Inner Sphere XL engines in my list (and no new Core Rules CASE, which would've at least postponed the fate of my Longbow on both TAC deaths); it was a lot of squish. I had no true bricks in this list, and I don't think I'd step into a 5k3u environment again without one; the format kinda demands you have something that won't abruptly suffer Critical Existence Failure on you save for a headshot, it just doesn't allow enough redundancy for your mechs to be popping like popcorn.

I think the missions may have tipped just a touch into overcomplication, particularly the first (it probably didn't need the stray PPC shot AND the ambient +3 heat on top of all the other stuff going on), but I loved the story and flavor they had. A common refrain from Liberty and our general, well, TTB tournament clique when complaining about BattleTech TOs' anti-competitive impulses is, "Well, why don't you run a narrative event!", and I can happily say that this... did genuinely capture the feeling of a narrative event, not just a "please keep it casual" label slapped onto a generic tournament packet. The small scale (just ten players, twelve if everyone who signed up had been able to come) and low stakes (just store credit for the host store) helped further the just-here-for-a-good-time feeling.

Overall, I had a blast, and I'm excited that the TO, Ryan, is already looking forward to next year and a probable ABC 27! He held a vote on the local Discord server to determine which Atlanta we should visit next (whether a return to zombie-overrun Atlanta [Savannah, Free Worlds League] or a trip to New Atlanta [Errai, Draconis Combine], Greater Atlanta [Tecumseh, Federated Suns], or Old Atlannuh [Terra, Third Star League]), and with two days left on the poll it looks like our OG Terran Atlanta is winning! I eagerly anticipate what our far-future homecoming might hold in store for us...

Closing Thoughts - 5k3u

This was my first exposure to the "5k3u" format (5,000 BV, three-unit cap), a reasonably popular format for quicker, smaller-scale BattleTech tournament games. In some cases, 5k3u tournaments encourage you to bring two different 5k lists, fielding one list in each of the first two rounds and then fighting at 10k6u for the last round of the day, but I've certainly heard of other events run like this one, where it's 5k all the way with one list throughout.

My feelings are... mixed? I genuinely was impressed with what it did for game speed - my first game was able to hit the eight-turn cap, my second game was able to hit the I-got-tabled cap, only my third struggled with time at all and we still got in six rounds. Single-mapsheet short-edge play also felt good at this unit count, and ironically wasn't all that much smaller as a functional play space compared to "full battle mat, but deploy six hexes in from the short edge" approach past Atlanta tournaments have frequently taken. (15 x 17 vs 17 x 20, basically).

I did, however, viscerally feel how much more vulnerable I was to Getting BattleTeched... in 2/3rds of my games I lost my Longbow to TACs, in 2/3rds of my games I lost my Sarath to headshots, in my second game there was that absurd "What if 41 damage to the left torso rear of your Black Hawk simultaneously?" moment... any of those occurrences could have happened in a "full scale" BT tournament game, but when they cost you a full third of the bodies you have in play rather than a fifth or a sixth, it hits differently on an emotional level.

If I may step out of line with broader tournament trends, I wonder if "6k4u" might feel a touch better than 5k3u? I think you could still keep it to one mapsheet with four mechs per side (old-school lance vs lance!), though it'd probably feel cramped if you went any bigger. You'd gain a touch more redundancy, you'd gain a frisson of nostalgia for the 3025 crowd, and you'd have to stretch BV-per-unit just a liiiiittle further to hit the four-bodies mark, which might add some interesting tensions to list-building. Just a thought; it'd benefit from some stress-testing.

Well, that's it for me today... I've stayed up late writing again because [I can't shut up / I have severe time management issues / I love y'all so much, delete as you feel appropriate] and I should be turning in - I have work in the morning, and I sure hope I'll be more ready to face the metaphorical zombies of consumerism than I was the literal zombies of Blakism!

Next on my tournament docket is another local Atlanta event in October... but this time? This time, my TTB friends will be coming to town. See you then!
