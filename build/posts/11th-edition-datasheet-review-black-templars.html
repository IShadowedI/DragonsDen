11th Edition Datasheet Review: Black Templars

September 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Alongside the new Space Marine Codex, all the non-compliant Chapters are receiving updates as well, bringing their units in-line with the new Marine gold standard, and providing them with Detachments compatible with the new book. This article focuses on the Black Templars Chapter.

Cry havoc, and let slip the Emperor’s angriest sons. Like all non-compliant Chapters, Templars are being updated alongside the new Codex, and end up a bit more in-line with boring things like doctrines (which they get) than they were in 10th. Never fear though (Templars hate a coward) - their iconic Vows return in their spicy 2DP Detachment, and their nastiest killers still look pretty vicious. Let’s dig in.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with a review copy of this codex and a provisional version of the Munitorum Field Manual. Any rules or points costs we reference are subject to change until they are published on the Warhammer 40K App.

Detachments

You can find links to the Detachments for this Chapter below.

  • Vow-sworn Crusaders (2DP - Purge the Foe): Flexible Vows and unreasonable aggression - a Black Templars classic.
  • Fist of the God Emperor (1DP - Take and Hold): Powers up Crusader Squads, helping them seize positions and crush the foe.
  • Marshal’s Household (1DP - Priority Assets): Hones the skills of your Sword Brethren, helping them confront any challenge.

Datasheets

Up front - Black Templars still cannot take any Psykers. Don't try anything funny.

Epic Heroes

Old Grimaldus meet New Grimaldus. Credit: SRM

Obviously we have to start with the big man himself, High Marshal Helbrecht. Other than gaining a pip of toughness and +1S on his strike profile, he’s pretty much unchanged - and that’s just fine! He’s a melee beatstick that inflicts extra Mortals on the enemy in the fight phase, and provides +1S and +1A to his unit (and still gets this when solo now). This means the S9 strike on his sword is actually S10, suddenly a key breakpoint in a world of T5 Marines. +1S is also more relevant on power weapons from various units he can lead, as these stuck at S5 base for non-Characters. Helbrecht continues extremely good, and rocking in at 125pts seems pretty solid. He also has the Transhuman Strategist ability if he leads your forces to war.

His zealous comrade Grimaldus is also largely unchanged other than gaining toughness and a wound (which goes further thanks to his Feel No Pain 5+). He provides full melee hit re-rolls, and your choice of either +1T, +1AP or +1” to Advance/Charge by activating one of his three relics in the Command Phase. Unchanged is also good here, and arguably even better because full hit re-rolls is a much rarer ability now (and Castellans no longer hand it out), and there are more Stratagems that provide Lethal Hits. This makes Grimaldus a big active draw to the Chapter, and strong at 120pts.

Characters

The Emperor's Champion. Credit: Saint

First up here is the Emperor’s Champion, still not an Epic Hero (one per army instead), and now with an eye-popping selection of new Marine Detachments to grab Enhancements from (Martial Paragon from Tacticus Attack Force seems like a good starter). He also gets a bit more of a shakeup than the Epic Heroes, which largely works out very well for him. Instead of once per phase zeroing out an attack, he just has flat -1D, which is usually going to net out better, as it makes killing him with elite melee threats challenging (and more plausible that you take him solo as a Lone Operative). His complicated leader effect about charging Characters is replaced with just a flat +1 to Charge, which is kind of fine, and the strike profile of his sword gains Devastating Wounds all the time (keeping anti-Character 5+ and Precision), which is big money. Losing the once per game brutality you used to be able to do with an Assault Intercessor unit is a bit of a shame, but the Champion is now more deadly most of the time, and still a great inclusion.

Our first more generic Character is the Marshal, the other Transhuman Strategist available. He gets a few stat bumps (including an important jump to S6 on his sword), but loses out a bit as a Leader, now providing +1 to hit in melee rather than critical hits on 5+. This still isn’t bad, but ties him very specifically to wanting to run alongside a Castellan, because re-roll 1s to hit is all you need if a unit is hitting on 2s. In brighter news, the new combi-weapon profile gets added to the Marshal, meaning he does have an option on a melta shot in an emergency, which is cool.

Speaking of the Castellan, they also grab an improved combi-weapon and sword profile, and also see their unit buff changed. They still provide re-rolls of 1 to hit in melee, and can still take a Ld test to boost this, but instead of upgrading it to full hit re-rolls, it adds wound re-rolls of 1 instead. This certainly isn’t bad, and does still combo well with the Marshal, but going fishing for big Critical Hit pop-offs isn’t really workable any more. He’s also significantly more than a regular Lieutenant at 75pts, which is probably a touch high.

The remaining two folks here, the Execrator and Crusade Ancient, both haven’t really changed much. The Execrator still provides Advance/Charge and forces battle-shocks when killing something, and does get the small boost that his sword goes to S6, and he gains Cleave 1 on the Crozius, adding a bit of extra heft. The Crusade Ancient is essentially exactly the same other than gaining T, providing 4+ Fight on Death and extra OC if the unit kills something, but does get the same asterisk next to him that all really cheap Support units do, which is that if you take Assault Brethren then putting Imperium’s Sword on them is a cheap, big, and clever way of adding vicious attacks.

Infantry

Black Templars Crusader. Credit: SRM

Leading the charge (pretty literally if you take Fist of the God Emperor), we have Crusader Squads, the Templar’s signature horde of angry young men. This unit still brings the exciting opportunity to put 20 models on the battlefield, but gets a little more confusing to work with now that there’s more of a gap between the profiles of Neophytes and Initiates is bigger than it used to be. The good news is that the mixed toughness rules work in your favour - while Neophytes are only T4, you use the higher T5 for attacks (and being able to push that to T6 with Grimaldus is sweet). On the offence, however, you’ll now need to split out your chainsword attacks between the two groups, as Neophytes have stuck at S4, which isn’t fantastic. That makes them a bit more strictly ablative, or alternatively you could seriously look at taking shotguns and knives on them - the knives are still 3A S4 AP-1, and shotguns have blast and AP-1 now, giving you a frankly alarming amount of volume if they’re shooting at a serious horde. They’re also Assault, so you can blast away while Advancing/Charging around the table. The final change for Crusaders is, sadly, that while they still have a Surge Move, it’s changed to d6+1”, presumably losing an inch to reflect the bigger Engagement Range in 11th. The Crusader Datasheet ends up feeling a bit weaker on its own merits, but the one unit you can stick Grimaldus in is probably better, so there’s still a place for them. They also have good synergy with the Detachments Templars get.

Last up for actual Datasheets (more on that in a second), we have the elite Sword Brethren. These get a very important change - they can now take their special weapon options once per four models instead of once per five, so if you take a minimum four-model unit, you aren’t stuck with the basic options only. Taking a thunder hammer is also way more exciting now that they’re damage 3. They do jump in price a bit, but a squad of four now is still cheaper than five was before (115pts), so your entry-level option to get the extra toys is reduced here, which is pretty decent, especially in their dedicated Detachment. Chainswords going to S5 also makes that option genuinely interesting for them - four with a couple of chainswords, the claws, and a thunder hammer (because be real, you’re taking the thunder hammer) is plenty of output. Could certainly see taking four with the Emperor’s Champion and a Castellan as a pretty vicious blender, or just running a quad with Helbrecht.

Vehicles

Black Templars Repulsor Executioner. Credit: SRM

Infamously, the Black Templars get to strap a multi-melta onto a bunch of the various Marine hover tanks like Repulsors. It looks like that’s still an option, as they have these units re-listed in their section with +10pts. We’re not 100% clear whether the Datasheets are also separate, but suspect not until told otherwise - given these rules are App only, it’s much easier to just give an extra checkbox when a Templar list takes a given tank. Impulsors at only 80pts with the multi-melta is extremely good, so you may well see people giving a bunch of these a try again.

Also, while it’s technically not a Templar-exclusive option, the Land Raider Crusader looks like it might actually be good now, so if you’ve longed to put one on the table, take this opportunity!

Impact

Wings: This feels like a bit of a downgrade overall on Datasheets, but not by masses. The biggest single hit is the loss of 5+ Criticals on the Marshal, as that shuts down quite a few combos, but there are some improvements too (e.g. the Emperor’s Champion), and Grimaldus is even more of a standout than ever. It’s also the case that just being able to put down a big 20-model brick is better when it’s T5. Assuming our understanding is correct, 80pts Impulsors with a multi-melta is also extremely real.

Templars also fare better on the Detachment front - their 2DP options is pretty great, and leans hard into what the army wants to be doing, and supplementing that with Assault Brethren seems like an extremely solid starting point for the army. Their 1DPs also both have their charms, and should line up well with the more melee-focused options from the Codex (or indeed tag onto Vow-sworn, especially for the Crusader option). Closer integration with the main book honestly feels like it helps Templars more than hurts them, and there’s a solid set of tools for them to work with.

Wrap Up

Templars seem pretty serviceable if you want a melee-focused Marine army, so get ready to carve up anyone who defies the Emperor’s will.
