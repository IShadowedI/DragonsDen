Hobby Build Academy: Tools for Dioramas

October 19th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Welcome back to Hobby Build Academy! I'm your host, Professor Marcy, joined as always by my helpful assistant Elly! This time, we're taking a look at how to get started with building dioramas; perhaps inspired by our trip to the hobby show last week, we've started looking into the best ways to get started with making displays and dioramas that are great for posing and displaying Gunpla, or other models, in! Some of these certainly overlap with miniature board building and display making, so if you're familiar with that, or actually have never done either, then perhaps some of these tips are going to be very helpful for you in general! There's actually a lot of pretty easy ways to make an effective display and diorama board, and although it may sound like it, you can do it fairly inexpensively in a lot of cases (as long as you realize you may be buying some supplies for the long haul).

This is also about a close as we've come to discussing modeling in the sense of model trains and other scale items, so I will preface this by saying that if you happen to have a hobby store near you that specializes in model trains, you're going to be well served by likely going there first and seeing what's available. A good number of items are pretty widely available just about anywhere, be it hobby stores or even a general department store, and of course, there are plenty of online shops that can help serve your needs. So, we're going to separate this list into different categories to help make things a bit more useful for referencing.

General Supplies

Marcy: So the first category is general supplies. These are things you'll probably be able to source from art and hobby stores, and maybe even other places if you get creative; online resources are also going to do you a lot of good when it comes down to finding some of these items. I also think I'd suggest that you try and think about where you are going to store some of this ahead of time, as some of these items are going to come in bulk or larger sizes that would make it quite a waste to get rid of after just one use!

Elly: I think this list was filled with things you've actually had for quite a while, too, huh?

Marcy: Well, when I was more into building, painting and otherwise working with miniatures, I was very much taken with detailed basing and getting creative. That was really an extension of having done some model kit work when I was younger, and it was actually the part of minis I liked the most.

Marcy: I painted this quite a few years ago, for example.

Elly: When we researched this list, these items were things that I was pretty surprised at the amount of options that are available.

Ground Cover

Don't mix these up with your spices. You do use spices, right?

Marcy: Ground Cover is essentially the material you use to make, well, the ground of whatever display you're working with. As romantic and creative as it seems, you can't really just go grab dirt, rocks, and plants from your yard and use them on permanent displays, at least not without quite a bit of extra work to make them safe. So, Ground Cover products are what you'll want to look for in order to create the illusion of grass, sand, dirt, or similar. Also, it is important to remember scaling and size.

Elly: I think it was kind of interesting to note that Gundam models aren't really as big as they seem, especially when you work with HG kits, but also that to make the scaling match with the in-universe scale, they're also a lot smaller than it might seem. When I was looking into it, it seems that most models are closer to N Scale. So, I think it would look pretty strange to have rocks or plants from your yard that don't match the size of what you're working with; a single blade of glass would look really weird next to a Gunpla!

Marcy: So, this is not meant to be sponsored, but if you are familiar with model trains, you may already know this brand, but Woodland Scenics is probably one of the best overall places to look for Ground Cover. They make quite a lot of types, and they all come in a somewhat iconic shaker bottle that helps you control the flow of your cover (and if you are familiar with kitchen work, they're basically large sized spice canisters).

Elly: Also, Ballast is the term used for rock debris in trains, and they make for great rock accents and for rocky/sandy effects on a display.

Foam Cutter

Not an endorsement, just look for something like this.

Elly: Something we learned looking at displays and dioramas is that you are likely going to work with, or want to consider working with, various types of foam. These are great for making elevation, and can even be used to create a scene of mountains, hills, or the like. Foam cutters come in a lot of different types; you can use a hand saw with some of them, or even a box cutter or hobby knife, but a very easy to use and fairly cheap tool are hot wire cutters. These come in a variety of sizes, brands, and quality, but the basic idea behind them is that a wire apparatus is heated, which then cuts through the foam by melting it.

Marcy: You should do this in a well ventilated place or with a mouth cover, by the way; I've worked with foam cutters in the past for costume work, and melted styrofoam stinks.

Elly: We were able to find a good number of these in few stores, including Amazon, and other hobby stores, so they're not very difficult to find. Also, they seem to run around 15~20 USD, depending on where you look.

Elmer's Glue / School / Project Glue

You know. This stuff.

Elly: Whatever you want to call it, the basic, white glue that you used as a kid is going to be a lot of use to you in making a display board. Aside from helping you affix things like your ground cover, foliage, or other items, you can actually do some pretty cool things with glue if you apply it, let it dry, and create textures with it that you can then paint over. You'll want to work with the basic stuff here, since you aren't working with the models themselves; essentially you're not looking for plastic cement or super-glue for the model, so you don't have to get too over the top with what you're using.

Specialized Supplies

Marcy: for these items, I would consider these things you'd likely find at a hobby store primarily, and may even have to consider looking for them on specialized retailers that have a focus on modeling. Woodland Scenics is getting another shout-out here, but you don't have to rely only on them; just that they are one of the more well known and available brands that make it fairly easy to get most of what you need in one place.

Foam

Elly: Specifically, we're talking about the type of foam you'll want to get for hobby work, which is going to depend on the type, size, and scale of the display you're using. In most cases, you aren't going to be working with average pieces of styrofoam like you might find in an electronics package, although if you wanted to get real creative and have some lying around, you can certainly experiment with it using the foam cutter!

Marcy: A lot of hobby foam is a bit smoother than commercial styrofoam, which helps give it a much better overall outer appearance, but there's some things you can do to get around that or get more creative with it, which we'll touch on. But, yes, finding foam in hobby stores will be where you might want to look. There are a lot of shapes and varieties, and you may even want to consider looking into the foam used for various art supplies or products, even the type used for flower arrangement.

Plaster Cloth / Plaster of Paris

Marcy: So here, you've got a few options; you can make your own, if you're familiar with and comfortable with working with the material, such as Plaster of Paris, but you can also buy premade Plaster Cloths that can be laid onto foam or other surfaces that help eliminate the 'foam' look, as well as protect the foam itself. Most model train layouts have plaster of some form over the foam, which can then be painted, textured, and otherwise decorated to help make it look more realistic and create the illusion of whatever biome you've opted to decorate.

Elly: Plaster of Paris is pretty inexpensive compared to the plaster cloth items, but the trade-off is that you are likely going to buy a lot of Plaster of Paris that you'll need to properly store once you're done with using it; when I was looking, I think the most available sizes I could go pick up at hardware stores were either 4lb bags or 25lbs, both of which are a lot more than I would probably need!

Cork

How to spackle over cork layers

Elly: Now, cork isn't really that hard to obtain, but it does have a bit of difficulty curve when you're using it for things that aren't just a plain, flat surface.

Marcy: Yes, while many Warhammer players are probably familiar with cork boards for the flat surface of a display board, working with cork beyond that can present some interesting textures and options, but also a lot of frustration. First of all, you generally only get cork in either wine corks or the style used for coasters or boards, which are all pretty thin. That said, you can do some fun things with cork if you cut and apply it, creating things like rough edges or small elevations that will have jagged textures (or, if you plaster or even just glue over it with ground cover, can just help break up flat terrain).

Acrylics for Texturing

Marcy: perhaps something slightly familiar to people who have made playing boards and larger scale items for gaming that have a water feature, but there are numerous acrylic materials you can use to help create things like the illusion of water. Although there are some far more intricate things that can be done with pouring various materials, many of these texture acrylics are made to be applied to the surface of something and then help give the illusion of water, waves, mud, or similar. As mentioned before, this isn't sponsored, but I don't think I've ever used anything other than Vallejo textures and Woodland Scenic textures in my life, and would probably suggest starting with those. They work great, they're easy to find, and generally they're fairly inexpensive.

Pre-Made Options

Marcy: So, you always have one other option: you can actually get a lot of pre-made items if you work within N-Scale, at least for most HG and MG gunpla. This means that if you don't really want to do the DIY aspects, but do want to make a cool looking display in a bookshelf or detolf, you can always look to buy N-Scale items like terrain, foliage, buildings, rocks, and all sorts of things. You can always consider things like aquarium decorations too for rocks, but just beware that some of those look very fake and plastic-y in order to be safe for use in aquariums and terrariums.

Wrap-Up

Hopefully, you've found some fun ideas here if you want to make something a bit more dynamic and fun to display your models in. Whether that is for a model show, or just your own edification, hopefully these items are good places for you to start and consider; in general, I think we'd say that the overall tip we'd want to give is that these items are not that hard to obtain or work with, and of the things listed here, plaster of paris is maybe the messiest, but still very simple!
