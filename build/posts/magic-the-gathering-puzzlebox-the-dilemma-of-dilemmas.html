Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – The Dilemma of Dilemmas

November 3rd, 2026 via Goonhammer

Star Trek is on the horizon, and with it, a new named game action in the form of Facing a Dilemma. Not unlike Committing a Crime, this is a thematic batching which serves as shorthand for something most people do every game; in the case of Crimes, it's touching things which aren't yours, but for Dilemmas, it's choosing a mode(s). Unlike Crimes however, Dilemmas aren't the easiest thing to face repeatedly, and this is reflected in the card designs. For the marquee Commander of the mechanic, Seven of Nine, you could conceivably draw through your entire deck if enough problems showed up on your desk, while similar Crime payoffs require either a triggered cost or have 'once per turn' clauses. While this makes Dilemmas seem all the more exploitable, it soon becomes evident as to why the designers saw it fit to leave the action as something that didn't need limiting clauses—it's actually rather difficult to take infinite Dilemmas. Unlike Crimes, for whom cards like Hex Parasite and the like exist, you ironically lack a ton of options when building to break Dilemmas. With that in mind, let's talk about how best to use this new game action, and some...odd options, even one from Arabian Nights.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

"...Choose one or more modes for a spell or ability."

As a refresher, Dilemmas are defined as the following: You face a dilemma as you choose one or more modes for a spell or ability. What is a mode, though? This requires we dig through the CR to find CR700.2, which defines a model piece of text as being a bulleted list following a phrase like "Choose one —". It's important to note however that unless a card has been reprinted from Khans of Tarkir or onward, it's likely that this exact phrasing is not present. Perhaps the 'choose one' is missing, or the list isn't bulleted, etc. Knowing Gatherer text is vital to actually narrowing down what cards do and don't trigger our payoffs, and does mean more cards than you'd expect fit the bill. Before we talk about some of those though, let's talk about some things which aren't Dilemmas: Council's Dilemma is an ability word and has no mechanical bearing, despite the flavor failure, and re-selecting targets or copying a card that requires you face a Dilemma does not trigger it. Dilemma triggers as a card or ability is put on the stack, when modes are chosen, and as such always resolves before the triggering effect in question. Why does that matter? Well, effects like voting or "Face a Villainous Choice —", despite having multiple potential outcomes and even the telltale em-dash, are not Dilemmas when their modes are chosen; these are selected upon the Spell's resolution, and therefore have neither the necessary bullets syntactically or timing window to count as a Dilemma. It's a bit confusing, but given the discussion I've seen online (and testing partners for some upcoming Commander Focuses) this is a common enough error that I felt it necessary to mention. As a final note for completions' sake, choosing halves for a split spell like Turn//Burn also isn't a Dilemma, which is a tragedy.

All that being said, what do help us face a ton of Dilemmas? The good news is that a Commander already exists which has used the same verbiage, from which we can draw inspiration: Riku of Many Paths. Riku has been popping up at conventions & LGSes I've attended as a fairly unique spin on spellslinger, rewarding people for playing a high density of things like Charms, Commands, and the new rebound pick-ones from Marvel Super Heroes Commander. Cards like these look quite a bit better when you staple effects like 'draw a card' onto them with Seven of Nine, or even just some drain via Elim Garak, Simple Tailor. You're confronted however with the necessity to actually cast Spells in order to face Dilemmas, however, and while it sounds a bit rich to complain about needing to cast Spells in my game of Magic, unlike Riku the revised Dilemma mechanic also calls out abilities. To that end, it turns some of these options like Seven, Elim Garak, or the 5c Captain Benjamin Sisko into more than just spellslinger Commanders, broadening their possibilities beyond that of Riku. He's still eating good from the new cards in Star Trek, mind you, but I wouldn't be covering the mechanic in such depth if all this was turned out to be a spin on Magecraft.

In a Pickle, Extra Dill-emma

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

I think when it comes to the discussion of facing Dilemmas en masse it becomes important to talk about ontological versus epistemological analysis, when it comes to Magic cards. Something I feel is especially relevant in finding the right, niche cards for a deck stems from evaluating them for what they are, rather than what they do. A great example is something like Swell of Growth in my Marwyn, the Nurturer list (which I covered here)—this is a lukewarm card, but is in fact the only Monogreen option for putting a Land into play at Instant-speed without needing something like a tap ability or to be on the field. The card itself is unimpressive, but its effect is precise and singular in what it accomplishes. When looking at cards like Relic Bind, the text of the actual Dilemma isn't what we're looking at, but instead the trigger. The ubiquity or ability to be built around behind the effect, how you actually face that Dilemma, is what matters. If you replaced the text of the card with [trigger] — face a Dilemma, does that have appeal? That's the question one needs to ask, with things like this or Crimes, where the actual effect is just gravy to the trigger (our real buildaround). With all that out of the way, it is a sick joke that I am to deliver the truth that Landfall is probably the best way to plan for Dilemmas. The most boring answer.

Feel free to print this one out.

Yes, between cards like the Retreats, Dread Presence with Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth, and even Elven Raft-Steerer from The Hobbit, the best way to see these triggers with the highest frequency is just by fetching and recurring Lands. Now, there's a bit of a silver lining here in that you're doing so in colors which normally don't play with Lands, namely Black or Dimir, and with the recent introduction of Land blinking into Blue's identity you can get a great quantity of triggers going. Copy your Retreats with things like Copy Enchantment or Mirrormade, and suddenly you're getting more value from a Land drop than Green. The alternate path is via casting spells, either noncreatures with Kykar, Zephyr Awakener, two in a turn with Cosmogrand Zenith, etc. Given you're already likely on a sufficient density of Instants & Sorceries with modes, these are slam dunk paths if you don't want to go infinite.

With that in mind, how do we go infinite with Dilemmas? There is a singular card in Magic's history which can, without need of anything other than infinite mana, cause you to face a Dilemma. No untap effects, no riders, just {2}...and controlling a Land. Let's talk, begrudgingly, about Pyramids. Printed in 1993's Arabian Nights, this is an Artifact that does something entirely unmoored from its actual text. For 2 mana, you choose one — pop an Aura on a Land, or give a Land lingering protection for the turn, removing all damage marked on it. That last one is the ticket, because you can just target the same Land, over and over, for {2}. Pyramids is however a 6-mana Artifact, and otherwise useless, so in an ideal world we're cheating its ability out via something like Trazyn the Infinite, who can himself also produce the infinite mana necessary. We can also Agatha's Soul Cauldron something like Cryptic Trilobite onto Jett Reno, Problem Solver, who receives a +1/+1 counter we can spend on 2 mana solely for abilities... receiving another when we face a Dilemma. For as bad as Pyramids is, and as pricey a real copy can be (good lord it's $120), it does represent a very specific case where it is singular in function. If you're evil, it also means you can enact one-sided MLD, but uh...don't do that.

I'm going to leave you with a few different Scryfall syntax links, which might be helpful in your quest to face Dilemmas. This looks for triggered options, and this looks for broader choices. In my search, it's quite easy to face some Dilemmas just by playing the game (Alliance-based triggers like the one on Lita, Little Orphan Amphibian are great, but cannot go infinite unless you somehow reset the trigger), but it's rather difficult to go infinite enough to end the game. That's a great show of a great mechanic, to not be so instantly-broken by Johnnies like myself. I think Dilemmas have a bright future, and while Commander options at present are functionally limited to U and/or B or 5c, I would hazard a guess we can see it return in some external product.
