Historicals: Victrix Carthaginians Range Review

June 15th, 2026 via Goonhammer
There's a lot out there for Saga factions, and with six Era books, nearly 100 factions and more manufacturers than you can shake a pilum at, it can be confusing to step into new periods to buy new armies. In our range reviews, we'll look at a manufacturer and their offerings for a Saga faction, see what works and what doesn't, and recommend good lists to get started. First up, it's time to Dido your Anaeas, with Victrix and the Carthaginians from Saga: Age of Hannibal. 

It’s SAGA Summer! Enjoy our coverage during the raiding season as we take a look at the factions, miniatures, and everything else you need to get started playing SAGA and earning massacre points.

Carthage! Great City-state of the Mediterranean, the foremost naval power of the age, and the implacable mercenary-employing, Tanit-worshipping enemy of some Italian guys from a minor city called Rome. For around 400 years until their destruction by the victorious Romans, Carthage was a major player in the Ancient European world, controlling and regulating trans-Mediterranean trade, as well as providing a link between the Mediterranean and African worlds. Victrix provide a group of five sets under their Ancients range for the Carthaginians, and in this article, we'll have a look at these sets through the lens of what works for Saga, as well as providing a mini review of each.

Carthaginians in Saga

Carthage presents the Saga player with an usually large list of troop options. Aside from the standard Hearthguard, Warriors and Levy, Carthage also offers Heavy Chariots or Elephants, as well as "Contingents", Warriors with a range of options (foot, mounted or mounted with Javelins) that allow you to pick and choose what will be most useful for facing each opponent. Carthaginian troops are drawn from two groups who interact with battle board abilities in different ways - Citizens, who tend to get more attacking effects from abilities, and Contingents, who tend towards more defensive abilities. Utilising the right unit and ability at the right time is hugely powerful, while picking the wrong type of unit for each ability will leave you a little underpowered compared to the brutality of other Age of Hannibal boards.

As a result, a Carthaginian list can go many ways - all mounted, with heavy contingent use, mounted Hearthguard, chariots or elephants - or all foot, or, most likely, somewhere in between. You can even use a ruse to recruit Warriors with Sarissas (big ol' pikes), giving you one of the widest ranges of troop types across all the Saga eras. Saga units are given their equipment choices at deployment, which allows you to tailor the list you play with to your opponent, and noone can do this better than Carthage. Let's take a very simple example list:

1 Point Hearthguard

4 Points Warriors

1 Point Levy

Each of these units has several options. The Hearthguard can be foot or mounted. The levy can have slings, bows or javelins. The Warriors can have no special equipment (and be contingents or citizen), Mounted, or mounted with javelins. This means that collecting a Saga army for the Carthage board is better aimed at 8 points rather than the standard 6 - you want the extra units to be able to switch between them when you need to.

The big draw is, of course, Hannibal's famous elephants, who provide a ludicrously tough hammer unit that your opponent will do well to avoid or try to shut down with fatigue tricks. I'm going to presume you want at least one elephant (why bother playing Carthage if you aren't taking one?), and an otherwise balanced list between foot and cavalry.

Victrix Carthaginians

Of the five Victrix kits in the Carthaginian range, there's a couple of stand-outs for Saga purposes, and others that may be worth picking up as they take your fancy. Let's start with the biggest!

African War Elephants

Elephant! Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

(Thanks to Victrix for sending these over for review)

The War Elephant kit is one of the newer kits in the Victrix Ancients line, and with two elephants to the bag probably represents as many elephants as you're likely to want in a Saga army (though Bair has three). It's a gorgeous kit, with a wide range of options for the elephant - two body poses, two head/trunk poses and a variety of accoutrements and decorations - and seven unique crew options for each elephant, letting you potentially magnetise (or just swap in) for Roman, Ptolomaic, Numidian and Carthaginian armies.

The sculpts are detailed, clean and precise, with the large half-components of the elephants going together very smoothly with a minimum of gap filling. It's a very straightforward build that produces a dynamic set of pachyderms with a lovely amount of detail to them. It's well supported by transfer sets for the howdah and blankets, and I'd definitely recommend picking a set of transfers to really up your elephant game.

Another Elephant! Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

With a lot of arm and weapon options, you're effectively buying two elephants and eight other guys per bag, so from a Saga point of view this is very much a must buy, as you can make the two elephants and then an entire unit of warriors out of the spares. As you're playing Carthage, the slightly motely look of using all the spares works well for a contingent unit. If you wanted to, you could keep the heavily armed crew aside for a Hearthguard unit, using the lighter and chainmail bodies for crew - if you do, you can squeeze out 3 and a half points out of one elephant bag:

2 Elephant (one point each)

4 Hearthguard (one point)

4 Warriors on foot (half point)

Warriors of Carthage

Warriors of Carthage. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

This is the other stand-out set for building a Carthaginian force for Saga. It's a bit of a strange sprue, mixed between Heavy Infantry, Carthaginian Elites and light infantry, but this makes it absolutely perfect for Saga, and a likely contender for the best one-bag purchase (tied with the Vikings) you can make to go from 0 to a full warband.

With 62 models in the bag, this breaks down to 24 Elite infantry in classic Carthaginian linothorax-style armour (as Hearthguard or Warriors), 24 chainmail equipped heavy infantry (Warriors) and 14 lightly armed javelinmen (Levies). This is a ferocious amount of Saga wrapped up in neat packages:

24 Hearthguard (6 points - the Oops all Hearthguard list is real, people)

24 Warriors (3 points)

12 Levy (1 Point)

As you probably don't want 6 points of Hearthguard, you can mix up your Warriors in different armour, or even reserve some of the Elites for a Sarissa-armed unit to represent tougher, tighter, formations and allied troops from Macedonia.

Older sculpts but they really hold up. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

Luckily, this isn't just a very useful kit, it's also a very nice one. The sculpts are clean and precise, even if they're on the older end of Victrix releases. You get a lot of options on the sprue, in addition to a command set that can easily be used to make Hearthguard or impressive (though rules-less) leader models for your units. There are enough javelin options to fit out both the tunic wearing Javelinmen and at least some of the chainmail models, giving you a flexible selection between Warriors and Levy (or Mercenaries!) with Javelins. For a faction that is very flexible - and remember, in Saga equipment is decided on deployment - this bag doesn't just get you an army, it gets you a huge range of options for surprising your opponent.

Carthaginian Citizen Infantry

Representing the last ditch conscripted Carthaginian citizens, this is a much smaller - and older - bag of models, with 24 basic unarmoured warriors. It's less of an essential pick, giving you options for four units of warriors armed either with no additional weapons or with javelins, or through picking less impressive helmets, a solid two points of levy.

Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

I think this set is best for people doing mass battle games where you want lots of infantry that are very very quick to paint. It's definitely showing its age compared to recent Victrix releases, and the sculpts, while nice, are much more monopose and limited. Detail is a little softer, and while the same (very nice) command frame is present in this set as in the Warriors of Carthage, it's an absolute no-brain move to skip this one in favour of picking up the larger, better, Warriors of Carthage set.

Ancient Armoured and Unarmoured Samnites

Armoured Samnites. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

The Samnites were an Italian people who occasionally allied with Rome and Carthage in their wars against each other, before eventually being subsumed into the Roman empire as Citizens. In Saga, they'd work well as a Contingent unit that provides a clear visual difference to the core Carthaginian kits. Both sets comprise 24 models, with the armoured Samnites having extremely impressive pectoral armour, and the Unarmoured Samnites in tunics (with a bit of armour). Both sets of sculpts are essentially the same otherwise, with largely similar poses, command pieces and weaponry. These are definitely intended to be javelin-armed warriors, which adds another set of options for Carthaginian contingent units.

I like this lad. You'll note i took these pictures before the static grass dried, which was a mistake. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

As an older kit, the same issues apply to these as to the Carthaginian Citizens. It's by no means a bad kit, and the detail of the circular pectoral armours, winged helmets and weighted javelins is all very nice, but with limited poses and all-round limited options compared to the Warriors of Carthage, this is a nice-to-have rather than a must buy. The Armoured bag will give you four points of Warriors armed with Javelins, while the unarmoured will look nice as Levy or Warriors.

Other Victrix Kits for Carthaginians

The very nice thing about Ancients in general - and Carthaginians in particular - is that picking up a variety of kits from other armies lets you accurately get across the variety of Ancient armies. Carthage used mercenaries and other paid levies from all over the Ancient world, so diving into anything from the ancients range from the same rough time period is more than permissible - it's accurate! I've had a look at some great complementary kits across the Victrix Ancients range (but haven't painted any of the cavalry yet - apologies!).

Rome's Legions of the Republic (II)

Hastati, with a carthaginian head. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

This is a very solid kit representing Republican legionaries - who should really be on the other side of the battlefield - but Italian and Roman client states can and did throw their lot in with Carthage from time to time. This kit provides a lot of options should you want to put some slightly different spear and javelin armed soldiers into your forces to give your Carthaginians a Sicilian or Southern Italian feel. It gives you a big chunk of Saga too:

24 Warriors (3 points)

18 Levy with Javelin (1.5 points)

4 Hearthguard from Command models (1 point)

It's worth saying that this, of course, also gives you almost an entire army in a bag for the Romans in Age of Hannibal - most of a Saga force for £33 isn't bad, is it?

Numidian Cavalry

Numidian Cavalry were a mainstay of Carthaginian forces, and in Saga can be fielded as mounted warriors or mounted warriors with javelins. As such a key part of the army I'd really love to recommend this kit, but I found them a bit of a pain to assemble and clean. They're quite spindly, and seem a little out of scale compared to the infantry units, and the models I worked with required a lot of cleaning in difficult places like along hairpieces and beards. These, and the rest of the cavalry, are languishing unpainted in my painting queue until I can get a nice day to prime them all in one go, so please accept the pictures from the Victrix site! (Edit: this photo is from Bair)

For Saga they will work very well as a light cavalry option, coming in the standard Victrix cavalry bag size of 12 models - 1.5 points of warriors per bag.

Iberian Cavalry

Another common cavalry contingent for Carthaginian commanders, the Iberian cavalry set represents mercenaries and levies from what is now Spain and Southern France. This is a much heartier recommend than the Numidians, as the detail is sharper, the models are a bit chunkier and the range of options is more suitable for switching between javelin and no javelin cavalry on the fly.

Victrix Iberian Cavalry. Credit: Victrix

They also work well visually with the other carthaginian kits, with some shared heads and arms across both sets, making a wide open space for kitbashing both your cavalry and infantry to match into each other should you want to. For another older box (2021 vintage - where does the time go, eh?), they hold up well and will be the mainstay of my cavalry once I get them painted.

Ancient Gallic Cavalry

Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Victrix

Gallic tribes and confederations allied with and were employed by Carthage at various points in the Punic wars, with the legendary army of Hannibal relying on their speed and ferocity. I think the Gallic Cavalry is a fantastic set for any aspiring Carthaginian player, giving you both another option for a mounted contingent, but also allowing some kitbashing for Carthaginian Hearthguard cavalry.

This is an incredibly dynamic and well armoured kit, with separate head and body pieces that are mostly compatible with the options presented in the Warriors of Carthage set, letting you have a solid, heavily armed cavalry unit that is both obviously Hearthguard and obviously Carthaginian. It'd be easy to split the bag into 4 mounted hearthguard and 8 contingent cavalry, making for a nice 2 point purchase.

Putting it all together

That's quite a few kits, but there's one core option: The Warriors of Carthage. This is probably one of, if not the, best single-bag purchase for Saga in any age, giving you not just the units you want, but options for deployment and listbuilding already within the set. That bag, coupled with some elephants, will give you virtually everything you need for a solid Carthaginian list. If you want to take it further, pick up a unit of Gallic Cavalry as allies or kitbashed hearthguard too. A bag of each will get you several potential versions of a Carthaginian Army:

4 Mounted Hearthguard (Gallic Cavalry, 1 point)

8 Mounted Warriors (Gallic Cavalry, 1 point)

12 Levy with Javelins (Warriors of Carthage, 1 point)

24 Warriors (Warriors of Carthage, 4 points)

24 Warriors/Foot Hearthguard (Warriors of Carthage, 4 points)

2 Elephants (2 points)

8 Warriors (Spare Elephant Crew, 1 point)

Pick up the transfers too! I'm using the Allied Italian sets here. Victrix Carthaginians. Credit: Lenoon

It will end up being quite a lot to paint, but the flexibility you'll have to move between different versions of the Carthaginian Warrior unit will pay dividends, letting you freely choose the best version of Carthage to put down against your opponent, and opening up the entire battleboard to best possible effect. There's enough variation in style of model here too to keep you entertained painting them, and you can break up some of the big infantry units with flashy cavalry or very loud elephants.
