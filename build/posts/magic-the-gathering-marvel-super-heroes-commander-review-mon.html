Magic: The Gathering Marvel Super Heroes Commander Review: Monocolor Cards

July 9th, 2026 via Goonhammer
This week we tune in to watch all of your favorite Marvel heroes enter the realm of Magic, presumably with a mandatory Excelsior! In this episode, Magic players are being introduced to a swath of new cards as their favorite Heroes and Villains duke it out with the power of friendship and dastardly plots. In this article we’ll talk about the rest of the Commander & Jumpstart cards, and offer some thoughts on what they mean for Commander and other formats.

Marvel Super Heroes will release to Magic: the Gathering Online and Arena on June 23rd, and to the tabletop on June 26th. Commander: Marvel Super Heroes will release on the same date. You already knew this, but the set is so large it's taken three whole weeks to actually cover the damn thing.

White

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Amazing Spider-Girl

Saffgor: Now, why are we starting with a French vanilla? Gorgeous art aside, this is the fourth Legendary White Web-slinger, and that means it's more redundancy for Arachne combos with Convoke, a pet deck of mine that's slowly becoming more real with every step into the spider-verse.

BPhillipYork: It's fine if you have creatures you want to return, especially those that cost 1 mana or less.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Captain America, Liberator

Saffgor: He finds Skullclamp, then makes 1/1s. That has to be good enough in the right shell, or even in the Command Zone as a Monowhite Aristocrats option!

FromTheShire: There's also just a whole bunch of really good Equipment that costs 3 or less that you're happy to tutor directly to the battlefield, for instance a Sword of Hearth and Home. Having extra token creatures around is never a negative either, even if you're not leaning super hard into doing things with them. There are certainly common ways to turn them into cards and mana to cast those cards though.

BPhillipYork: Great for Equipment and go-wide token decks. Is that a thing? No, that is not a thing. Still, having a bunch of 1/1 weenies around is useful, and there are some ways to get a lot of equipment around, but you'd need red for that.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Dora Milaje Elite

Saffgor: This is in my top 5 for the entire set, being a blinkable Sand Scout-like that never puts you at parity on Lands, and therefore accelerates you 99% of the time. The fact it protects key permanents is gravy, and I expect this to become a staple of the color, after having played it in my Bracket 4 Beza, the Bounding Spring list.

FromTheShire: These catch up cards are already a strength of white's and this one doesn't even turn your other versions off since it doesn't fetch an actual land like most of them. Freely protecting at minimum your commander after that is a great bit of upside as well.

BPhillipYork: 1W for a 2/2 first striker is fine, then making a mana rock is nice on top of that. In a game of commander it'd be rare for a white deck to have the most lands, unless you happen to go first and never miss a drop. Protecting your legendaries is useful-ish, if it was protecting creatures that'd be a really utilitarian creature with a lot of potential value, as it is, kind of meh. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Everett K. Ross, Hapless Attaché

Saffgor: Wakanda's favorite quirked up white boy, now double dipping on Partners and disincentivizing big swings. For 3 mana, this does a ton if you can make the Lifelink matter.

FromTheShire: Either drawing cards or keeping attackers away is pretty solid for 3 mana, and especially at lower bracket tables incidental life gain is helpful more often than you think.

BPhillipYork: The draw card trigger is nice enough, and likely people will avoid attacking you altogether, or just swing with one creature. Buffing your commander can be occasionally huge if it has certain kinds of triggers, which is powerful on it's own. So this functions as a sort of protective aura with a buff that is probably not worth blowing up for your opponents. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Falcon and Redwing

Saffgor: Absolutely fantastic card for bird typal, not much more to say.

FromTheShire: Hell yeah let's gooooooo! As the owner of multiple Bird decks this card rules. The fact it is itself a Bird is excellent, a lot of these kinds of cards are like a Human who then makes Knight tokens or something which means while they generate a token you want they can struggle since they don't benefit from the synergies with the type like the rest of the deck. Here not only do you get those synergies, but your anthems also make him pump out tokens faster, which are themselves larger threats.

BPhillipYork: Well it's nice for bird decks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Hatut Zeraze Strike Force

Saffgor: White's second 'hey, remember Partner exists?' card, and it's a darn good one. It's almost always at least 2 bodies, and 2 Disenchants, but it can be plenty more.

FromTheShire: Always love getting this kind of destruction on a creature because it's an effect you always need anyway that also leaves behind a body to block or get flickered or whatever. Even better if it is also getting kindred synergies.

BPhillipYork: Realistically you're paying 4 for what, 2 2/2s that destroy up to one artifact or enchantment each. If you know you're gonna cast your commander a lot somehow and for some reason, then this can get really strong in terms of control, and thankfully it's optional destruction so you won't end up blowing yourself out. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Heroic Sacrifice

Saffgor: It's a Fog that transfers counters and draws a card. The fact you break even is the huge sell here, as fogs normally are rough in terms of advantage.

FromTheShire: Generally I am inclined to agree with the Sheldon Menery "people don't play enough Fogs" school of thought as it is, and being able to save counters and draw a card is an extremely nice bonus. Also extremely useful in Boros Reckoner style decks trying to punk people out with Blasphemous Act which I always enjoy.

BPhillipYork: This is awesome. I mean for things like Stuffy Doll, or other reflectors. Also a nice way if you are planning out your own damage based board clear to do something with it. Sadly though most board clears are of the "destroy all creatures" variety. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Invisible Force Field

Saffgor: Protect things in response, then on your turn, get this Rebounded for free and slam a one-sided board wipe. Insane card in the right shell, and a great card elsewhere. You'll be seeing this.

FromTheShire: This is usually going to be enough to save your commander and your most important engine pieces, and unlike Rootborn Defenses and its like that I frequently run this does let you protect any permanent not just creatures. I'm not sure how often the rebound is actually going to come up but I'm never going to be sad to have it.

BPhillipYork: This is decent enough since other than like go wide decks how many creatures are you really ending up with? Any kind of deck that keeps flipping out creatures and turning them sideways sure, it's not enough, but for a commander and a few other creatures it's great. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Matt Murdock, Justice Seeker

Saffgor: While he seems innocuous, Matt is a solid roleplayer in counters-matter decks, as even Ward {1} is enough to make spare removal go elsewhere.

FromTheShire: Personally I would just pay the 1 to deal with a problem but I also thought that about Esper Sentinel in what proved to be my biggest misjudgment of a card/ player behavior ever. Everyone knows you should pay the 1 and yet nobody actually does it so I will be 0 percent surprised if this ends up being great in counters decks. Just pay your taxes people!

BPhillipYork: Ward 1 for creatures with counters is great. Placing a single counter isn't so great, but it's nice for proliferate decks and decks that need a way to get proliferation going. At 2 mana it's a great little utility creature to enable +1/+1 counter decks or other creature counter decks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ultimate Nullification

Saffgor: It's no Farewell, but it's also only 5 mana (and not a Game Changer). If you want that effect, but don't want to spend a GC slot, it goes in.

FromTheShire: Mass exile is very powerful, and hitting graveyards too is solid. It will depend on the makeup of your deck a little how good this is though, if you don't consistently have the required legendary and have to cast your commander in order to then cast your board wipe it's real bad.

BPhillipYork: Fun. Like a super board clear, exiling everything is potentially really hamstringing your opponents. Sadly there's not too many effects like these, but with so many indestructible and hexproof sources around it's kind of necessary to give white a "true" Wrath of God effect now and again.

Blue

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Council of Reeds

Saffgor: Not the first Commander in the set that just wants you to slam extra turns, but to be honest I don't think the Council is that great. There's enough clones that already ignore the Legend Rule (we'll see one later this article) and 1-2 vanilla-ish 2/2s the turn they come down aren't exciting.

FromTheShire: This can be a solid additional copy in a deck that badly wants this effect. The extra copy strikes me as more of an insurance policy against the first one being removed than any kind of win condition though.

BPhillipYork: Yet another "legends rule doesn't apply" effect, one that also makes more legendary creatures, but only of itself. It's okay, and kind of snowballs, since each copy will make a copy, but not really all that interesting except as a way to copy or duplicate legendaries. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Daughter of the Deep

Saffgor: Just a really solid Merfolk typal piece, not as though they needed more.

FromTheShire: Triggering each turn is nice with the ways Merfolk can draw cards, and a decent chunk of them can get real large so making them unblockable is a serious threat.

BPhillipYork: Solid enough Merfolk for Merfolk type decks. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Fantastic Elasticity

Saffgor: Back in the day, there was a cEDH deck helmed by Taigam, Ojutai Master based on cycling through a Call to Mind and extra turn spell, giving each Rebound. This...does one of those by itself, and is also solid removal, and also doesn't exile itself after resolution. I expect this to show up somewhere competitive, even if Taigam Xerox is long dead.

FromTheShire: This is likely better in that competitive context where you're doing busted things on purpose with it, played fairly it's merely okay.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Glorious Purpose

FromTheShire: Really nice in the Villains deck where there are a lot of connive synergies and you can be free rolling some spells quickly. Ideally you would like a way to recur this since it does go away pretty quickly and if you whiff on your spells all this did was place a few +1/+1 counters.

BPhillipYork: Wow that's a pretty strong plan. To be honest it'd be really nice if the sacrifice on these was optional, you might just want double conniving +1/+1s, but free casting several spells is really nice. Just tutor something powerful and expensive to the top.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Immortus, Master of Eternity

Saffgor: Without Haste, that second ability doesn't do a ton for the first without the full 7 mana, but luckily we have things like boots and Skateboards to make it more reasonable. That said, Blue also makes some great mana for abilities via things like Training Grounds, so the cost doesn't scare me. The question is then whether Immortus' design thrust is worthwhile. The answer is yes. Oh god, yes. This card rules.

FromTheShire: Do you want the wheel deck to have nearly infinite mana? Because this is how they get nearly infinite mana. Or maybe even actually infinite mana if they do it right! Warning lights should be flashing if you ever see this hit the table.

BPhillipYork: This seems pretty abusable to generate infinite mana by just flickering him, he'll still see all the cards you drew this turn, though you also need a haste source if you're going this route. Also another creature that taps for mana equal to the card draw you've done this turn, which means he can support that archetype, double it's power and also provide backup. Really pretty solid for 3 mana, since drawing cards and mana are basically always good. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Impossible Man

Saffgor: Both he and Absorbing Man make for easy Dark Depths packages, but we're at a weird spot in Commander where a quick Marit Lage isn't even that scary.

FromTheShire: Nice to have another copy of the effect, the activation cost is a little steep unless you have a reducer if you're wanting to do trigger stacking shenanigans like with Mirage Mirror, which is also reprinted in this set. More often than not I suspect this is the lesser version with being more expensive and more easily destroyed. If you need a certain different permanent type it's better at least.

BPhillipYork: Nice that's its a permanent and not a creature that you're copying, so you can double up on triggers or something like that. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Iron Man, Bleeding Edge

Saffgor: I think this is worse than the uncommon 2 mana Blue Shuri, hilariously. Wakanda forever, Tony.

FromTheShire: Artifacts can do some powerful nonsense and if they let you get rolling and doubling up Simulacrum Synthesizers and Lithoform Engine and Portal to Phyrexia and the like they deserve what's coming to them. There's an equal chance you daydream about that waiting for it to be your turn again only for it to get killed though so make sure to pack some free counterspells.

BPhillipYork: Copying artifacts is nice, I guess, I can't imagine building a deck around it, and at 5 mana you'll have already dropped mana rocks and such, so what are you really copying? Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Loki, Lord of Misrule

Saffgor: This looks super cool as redundancy for Niko, Light of Hope, but I doubt it sees play in the Command Zone.

FromTheShire: I don't think it would be super competitive but you could absolutely put him at the helm of a very fun deck turning all of your creatures into token generators like Talrand, Sky Summoner and Mischievous Mystic before triggering them all, then next turn turning all of those into something else dumb and fun. The Mindskinner is my favorite so far.

BPhillipYork: This mass copying is fairly funny, get your 10 Academy Manufactors or whatever and go nuts. Tons of things you can abuse like crazy by making a lot of copies or something. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Multiversal Recruitment

Saffgor: This goes in Muddle, and everything else that wants a non-legendary clone. We're going to hit a critical density of these someday.

BPhillipYork: This is just an iteration on a continuing theme. TBH what is making legendary copies these days, it's hardly special anymore to copy something but make it non-legendary. 4 mana cast is on the money, and 7 cost flashback is pricy but fine.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Reed Richards, Smartest Man

Saffgor: Card is super cute, but I don't think he's super good outside of being a big Hero to freecast with the plentiful cards which do so.

FromTheShire: 6 seems like an awful lot to paint a giant target on your back unless you're built to close the game out after getting one or two triggers.

BPhillipYork: Wow that's a lot of card draw. Like truly you should be drawing your deck out easily with this. Which, for 6 mana, yeah, you should get an effect like that. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Silver Surfer, Cosmic Voyager

Saffgor: As someone who was a first mover on Princess Yue, and maintains the primer for it today...this is the best Yue card printed since she was. Flicker all your moons, get a billion Landfall, then launder a clone into a Moon Surfer, and flicker the original copy, getting 8-24+ Landfall triggers over a turn cycle. Utterly insane card, why didn't this say nonland? Huge get.

FromTheShire: It's supposed to read as boardwipe protection, what it actually reads as is massive enabler.

BPhillipYork: I'm not holding up 6 mana to flash protect my creatures from Wrath of God, and neither should you. It is a triggered way to double up on ETBs, and you can set up chains where your whole board is entering each turn, which is kind of fun. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Vision, Spectral Synthezoid

FromTheShire: The odds of you using this to cast a Robot seem remote unless you're doing a bit. The problem with putting diet Omniscience in your Command Zone is everyone can see it and rightfully kill you before you get there because there is 0 chance you are trying to do something fair with it.

BPhillipYork: So, I guess this is like, an extra turn commander?

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Wasp, Shrinking Savior

Saffgor: Whoa, new text! Power less than 0 is a cool quest to venture out on, and if you can combine it with a Genesis Chamber and power reducer Wasp'll be drawing you a new grip every turn. I'm excited to figure out that list.

FromTheShire: Huh, that's interesting. Blue has a ton of both one off and permanent power reducers, I would certainly be willing to stick them all in a deck and see what happens. Like with Meishin, the Mind Cage for instance you pretty quickly basically turn off combat and then what is your follow up? Probably drawing yourself out and winning with Labman or Thoracle but that seems super boring even if you put in a lot of extra legwork to get there. Worth thinking about at minimum.

BPhillipYork: This seems really hard to get a lot of value out of, 3 for a 1/3 with flying that drew a card when it attacked would be fine, it's practically a magpie. In theory you could be drawing a lot by having lots of creatures with negative power, but ... that's a lot of steps when there are better ways to draw lots of cards.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

West Coast Expansion

FromTheShire: Outside of specifically a Hero deck I'd rather have an Instant speed version like Stroke of Genius.

BPhillipYork: It's a fine Braingeyser type effect, free casting a Hero is nice I guess, it does give it some immediate payoff, which otherwise you're tapping out to draw a bunch.

Black

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Black Widow, Daring Operative

Saffgor: If you're going to play a Black Widow as Commander, it's this or the Boros one, and one would assume you'd prefer the one that's actually Black.

FromTheShire: The effect is powerful but having to connect in combat while everyone can read what is about to happen seems problematic. You really wish this had green so you could get the full suite of dredge creatures and Hermit Druid.

BPhillipYork: That's a fairly mean trigger, potential to fill your yard and take people out pretty quickly, and she has evasion on top of it. 5 mana is also hefty to pay, as a monoblack commander to me it's not super exciting because you're going to have a hard time protecting her, since it's so transparent what she does. Best case scenario you ramp up and get some protection on the board like Lightning Greaves, then drop her, and then you fill your yard so it's not so impending. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Damocles Base, Sword of Kang

Saffgor: The important bit here is the choice, being a new tool for things like The Valeyard.

FromTheShire: Man, I had forgotten that they had come up with the villainous choice mechanic previously and now I'm retroactively mad as hell we didn't see a bunch of it in this set. What a fumble.

BPhillipYork: Expensive for a vehicle but forcing villainous choices is typically going to result in your drawing cards, and it's only 1 black mana so safe enough to splash into something. 5 mana to draw 2 cards a turn consistently, or force a sacrifice, is decent all around just slow. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Doctor Doom, Unrivaled

Saffgor: This is a card you use after milling yourself out, via Agatha's Soul Cauldron. To that end it's fine, but often a trap to hardcast, and doubly so to put in the Command Zone.

BPhillipYork: Yes, great, it's not blue so that's kind of nice. Sort of enormously threatening, and unless you give it haste it has to survive a turn untapped, which most of the time means people are going to kill rather than let you untap and cast Demonic Consultation and win the game. This is a super tricky one in that you have to leave 1 card at the bottom of your deck to draw after you've exiled your library so it's way more handicapped than the blue versions of this effect, which becomes fairly clunky.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Crimson Cowl, Master of Evil

Saffgor: This is a stand-in for the dozens of Villain-matters cards in the set like Iron Monger, Sadistic Tycoon and the like—the type is fine. Both Hero & Villain decks work, but I wouldn't pen either as terribly exciting. Go wide, turn sideways, do math.

BPhillipYork: Well, it's a support card for Villain typal which makes even more Villains.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Endless Ranks of HYDRA

Saffgor: If you're looking for something to spend mana on, in Aristocrats or Villain typal, this is fine enough.

FromTheShire: It's quite expensive but may well be times when you have nothing better to do so sure I guess. At least you can easily Skullclamp all the tokens like a good evil overlord.

BPhillipYork: Funny creature creation tool that has built in recursion, 4 mana is a lot for 3 2/1s, but the fact you can use it over and over is pretty solid value, and you can safely discard it or mill it away and just get it back. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Grim Reaper's Scythe

Saffgor: Goes infinite with a number of things, and is solid redundancy with Teval. Given the latter's popularity, you'll see this card at your LGS.

FromTheShire: C-c-c-c-combo! The fact this even makes Zombie tokens for your Gravecrawler loop is pretty silly.

BPhillipYork: This is a bonkers card. You've got a trigger for whenever one or more creature cards leave your yard to make creatures, which alone for 3 mana would be a solid card. It's not once per turn or only your turn, just straight up, remove 5 creatures from your yard in various ways, then make 5 creatures. Only real limitation is the or more, so if they leave at the same time you only get one, which is pretty important. Still, the ability to then to turn around and sacrifice likely those same Zombies to return other creatures (and netting you back a Zombie), repeatedly, is pretty strong recursion. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Kingpin, Wilson Fisk

Saffgor: This is quietly a really cool, powerful Commander, as it's child's play to make Mayor Fisk go mana positive over the first turn cycle he's around. What to do with the mana, though? Artifacts-matter works, as do Treasures, or just big mana. I just can't say no to 8 mana over a turn cycle, folks.

FromTheShire: Doesn't even put the Treasure tokens in tapped or anything huh. What a house with free sac outlets.

BPhillipYork: Way more limited than say Pitiless Plunderer, he's still a really solid Treasure benefit which can then turn around and be fed into another woodchipper for some other value. And you can just space your sacrifices out onto other players' turns generally. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ultimo, Civilization's End

Saffgor: This is sleeper good, from games I've played since the set released. Uncounterable edict you can pay with ability mana, and get back with cards that recur Creatures and/or Artifacts, ensuring Ultimo is more repeatable than similar effects.

FromTheShire: Neat. So for 3 mana your Fleshbag Marauder can't be countered and can happen at instant speed, and then when you inevitably reanimate it it's an actual threatening body with menace? I'm all about it.

BPhillipYork: A discard to force a sacrifice is a pretty crazy thing. Really solid back up mode. The only real downside is that frequently forced sacrifice decks want creature deaths for aristocrats style triggers, though you can pretty easily discard this to force a sacrifice then turn around and reanimate for a double whammy. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ultron's Auxiliary

Saffgor: When you sac, mill, or discard an Artifact, he gets bigger. That screams affinity, especially Modular, to me, and the rate is pretty good!

FromTheShire: He gets big but he's not evasive enough for my liking.

BPhillipYork: In a Treasure deck this guy is going to get absolutely enormous really quickly. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Ultron the Annihilator

Saffgor: Holy shit, Syr Konrad, the Grim for Artifacts. Currently at a cool ~500 decks on EDHREC, this feels like a popular Commander waiting for focus amid a crowded set.

FromTheShire: Well that's bonkers. Do you know how many things make Treasures these days? Plus he has flying so he can generally attack safely to make tokens. Finally we get one of these cards to actually live up to the hype and feel like you will be a villain when you sit down with them. If you have the 40k Necron precon sitting around you can crack it open and slot this in as your new commander for a big upgrade on an already good deck.

BPhillipYork: This is a really big, does both sides of the trick, generating artifacts for you and then pushing your opponents. You can also just dump your library into the yard and end the game. So potentially a powerful commander or auxiliary - and doesn't have the "or more" rider so Morality Shift will probably just end the game. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Undead Hand Ninja

Saffgor: A Ninja for Yuriko, a Zombie for...Zombies, and a Villain for Villains. Good effect, good blocker. It's got plenty of homes, just a question of whether the rate is good enough.

FromTheShire: I think with the number of things that care about creatures leaving your graveyard this is absolutely a fine rate. Can easily be pinging down the table quickly.

BPhillipYork: Again a super useful trigger that works off reanimations and reshuffles and things like that. Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Valentina Allegra de Fontaine

Saffgor: This is extremely cute, and gives you a chance to double dip on synergies in Black. Is she 'good'? Probably not, but she's certainly interesting.

BPhillipYork: Interesting, and I like that lots of Villains have connive abilities. She works really well with some of the bonus connive cards in the set and connive is a strong ability.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Villainous Syndication

Saffgor: Whoa, Villains get a 2 mana Hair-Strung Koto? I almost wish that second trigger wasn't even on the card, feels like the mill is the standout here.

FromTheShire: 4 is pretty easy to get to, potentially problematically so as I agree you would rather have this around for longer to keep filling the yard.

BPhillipYork: Probably too slow, even though it's an unconditional reanimation, unless you're setting up some kind of loop.

Red

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Alex Wilder, Runaway

Saffgor: As a reminder, your Command Zone isn't your hand. Between that, and having Escape, Alex Wilder seems like a fantastic role player for decks with Commanders that need haste, or care about graveyard triggers...enter Gau, Feral Youth.

BPhillipYork:  There's definitely some ways to cast things from not your hand. Most of them are exile and cast triggers, though at times you can cast from your yard or other things, which is nice. It is a cheap way to get a haste effect, but things like Chainer, Nightmare Adept include the ability to cast the cards, which is just more efficient in the end.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Alicia Masters, Skilled Sculptor

Saffgor: She does some funny things with Humble Defector, but we need more cards along a similar line to make her worthwhile in the Command Zone.

BPhillipYork: I love this for a trading deck, and to be a dick and trade things and then sacrifice them and get yours back. Also Treasures are pretty useful, at 2 mana that's a decent mana-rock ish effect.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Asgardian Inspiration

Saffgor: Repeatable card advantage for Red, that's a new one. Cool cantrip.

FromTheShire: Appropriately enough this feels like it hits maximum power in a storm deck, but even using it with incidental mana throughout the game this is nice to have.

BPhillipYork: Yeah this is a solid repeatable card, and with so many ways to get mana from casting spells, particularly in red, it's very abusable. Like going infinite abuseable. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Daredevil, Fearless Fighter

Saffgor: Cursed Recording, eat your heart out. The Devil of Hell's Kitchen is much akin to Auntie Blyte, Bad Influence, and he's pretty good! There's enough support to make him sing, including Stick, Fearless Mentor from the same set, and as a beloved character I think he outcompetes the existing options for the archetype.

FromTheShire: Suicide Red seems like a blast to play and I am here for it. Best case scenario you combine some lifelinking Equipment with a bunch of punisher cards and damage doublers and watch everything explode. My new 'most likely to build' option from the set.

BPhillipYork: This is a neat copy damage trigger. It's also an effective card advantage source, though kind of suicidal. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Flame On!

FromTheShire: In Commander it kind of feels like if you have cast enough things to make this a respectable number you're probably already winning the game.

BPhillipYork: Well that's potentially a lot of counters, and it's rebounding, but 5 mana is a lot for +1/+1 counters, even twice. If you've got a commander that triggers off of power it could be useful.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hawkeye, Trick Shot

Saffgor: Hero Terror of the Peaks, good lord. Pretty obvious wincon.

BPhillipYork:  Well it's obviously fine for Hero typal and nowhere else.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Hulking, Young Avenger

Saffgor: Likely worse than the Monored Gogo, but not too far off. The real killer is no haste.

FromTheShire: You get to hit the biggest threat with their best creature. The value of that will vary wildly on what decks you are facing.

BPhillipYork:  I don't really see the point in these cards that copy other things constantly but don't just stick around. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Living Laser

Saffgor: In a wheels-matter deck, this is a sweet win condition, and pretty fun in Elementals too!

FromTheShire: He's neat, but I don't think I want to wheel like 4 times and still need to win by doing combat damage.

BPhillipYork:  This is like Ball Lightning on steroids, which is pretty funny. I like this, and I think it's a fun commander, easy to discard a lot of cards in a turn in red, and whombo combo someone out with just a horde of commander copies (which don't do commander damage, so you need about 30 of them.) Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lady Loki, Agent of Chaos

Saffgor: You ideally want to trade something small for something big, 4x per turn cycle. That's not impossible, but the sheer variance and 6 mana cost mean this Loki is relegated to lower power.

BPhillipYork:  This is pretty abusable if you have ways of arranging your deck, though it ends up being a kind of bargain version of Yuriko that's way more expensive and has no way of avoiding commander tax. Still, funny to be free casting ultimatums and have them deal 7 or so damage each.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Loki, God of Lies

Saffgor: This isn't a theft Commander, he's a combo enabler. Accessible untaps and haste on your key tap abilities is great, and his low cost means you're not far off of a Stella Lee with the right setup. Still, Monored is a detriment for the triggers here, compared to her added Blue.

BPhillipYork:  This is a kind of weird ability since most spells that target creatures will be creature destruction. Certainly you can run him as a commander and then run creature buffs which is kind of fun, though pretty limited in mono red.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Loki's Scepter

Saffgor: With a sac outlet, this is a damn good swap for Cursed Mirror. Like the above Loki though, don't forget this can untap a Creature you control and give it haste, too. It steals, it enables tap effects for earlier combos, it might just be the best 3 mana rock printed in a while.

FromTheShire: Solid upside tacked on to your mana rock. You do have to be built around it a little to take full advantage so it may not go in every red deck, and casting it on curve like you want to do for a mana rock means your targets may be underwhelming.

BPhillipYork:  I really like these ETB mana rocks, and this is a good one. Fun starting thing, not too crazy, then it's a mana rock, and certainly you can flicker it various ways if you want. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Origin of Thor

Saffgor: A sleeper card if ever there were one. If you're a fairly fair deck, and looking to do what Red wants and throw out damage, Origin is a gameplan in a box. No single component is flashy, but as a whole it's just...good, if you're trying to win via damage.

BPhillipYork: Well potentially this is a huge damage payoff that's massively telegraphed; as a Saga to me this is kind of so-so, since the simplest solution if you dump +1/+1 counters onto something is for one of your opponents to just exile it. If you can protect it then it gets fairly mean. And also just dumping counters onto a commander is fairly scary.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Puppet Master, String Puller

FromTheShire: Are you running a goad commander? Then this goes in the deck for sure. Otherwise not impactful enough on its own.

BPhillipYork: Love it. Cool goad ability, doesn't have to attack itself, and gets the game moving. Slam it into my veins, or my Firkraag, Cunning Instigator deck.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Stilt-Man, Towering Terror

Saffgor: Stilt-Man isn't great, but he can enable some deeply funny Scramble decks, where you play but a single Enchantment/Planeswalker/Artifact, and Reality Scramble the one you've stolen to ensure a hit. Or just Tel-Jilad Stylus. Oh, those both say 'own'? Ah, so there's nothing fun to be done with the Stilt Man, boo.

BPhillipYork: Well just nabbing stuff is useful. I guess they thought mass sacrificing everything would be too good, which honestly to me doesn't seem so, given how good Pirates are at taking stuff permanently.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

The Vision and Scarlet Witch

Saffgor: Holy shit Birgi 2, and it's an Artifact? Into cEDH you go, that redundancy is tremendous. Deeply unsafe design, but I don't think it makes anything immediately broken—just more consistent.

FromTheShire: Ayyyy, the Heroes finally got something that feels superpowered too!

BPhillipYork: Oh yay another one of these red generates mana triggers. Well it's an amazing utility card that helps red decks spell sling infinitely. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Thor, Guardian of Midgard

Saffgor: Virtue of Courage in the Command Zone, something folks have asked for since its printing. No notes, cool to see.

FromTheShire: This is powerful, I'm sad it's only able to play them this turn though so he doesn't go in my punisher deck which is triggering on everyone's turns.

BPhillipYork: That is potentially a lot of card advantage. Just huge amounts going off from casting Lightning Bolt and with all the cast generators you can easily just keep exiling and casting and kill everyone.

Green

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Devil Dinosaur

Saffgor: It sure is a Dinosaur typal card.

BPhillipYork: This is a pretty nice for Dinosaur decks. A Dinosaur lord, plus mass hexproof is pretty rare to hand out, so solid for that, and cheap really 4 for a 5/5 trampler, though most dino decks cheat out dinos in various ways.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Fight for the Throne

Saffgor: This card is one of the better fight spells they've printed in recent memory, getting you a bit bigger, serving as removal, and likely resulting in at least one draw.

BPhillipYork:  I never really understand how a hyper technological futuristic society was engaging in 1 on 1 mortal combat for the throne.  But, hey it's an okay fight card that upsides the monarch.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

It's Clobberin' Time!

Saffgor: Not my favorite of the Rebound spells, but two kill spells and two cast triggers isn't bad for 3 mana. If you care about the 'cast a noncreature spell this turn' trigger, you'd likely play this over similar options.

FromTheShire: Sorcery speed means this is probably a no for me personally but it's not terrible.

BPhillipYork: Too expensive for a "bite" effect, and too slow. Maybe if it did both. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Lucky the Pizza Dog

Saffgor: It's in super small font, but Lucky also coincidentally has Indestructible & Hexproof. If someone points a kill spell at a literal golden retriever, they deserve what's coming to them.

FromTheShire: I don't know if I have the constitution to potentially need to go full John Wick in every game I play. He's a very good boy and a good card.

BPhillipYork: Well mass food tokens is pretty useful, this seems like it actually ends up in Rin and Seri and ignores the whole Hero theme. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Mister Immortal

Saffgor: Not a new Food Chain piece, given it's not a cast, but pouring activated ability mana into Craig here is pretty good. Finally an insane use case for Convergence of Dominion, though.

FromTheShire: Great for a 'you are the real Villain' deck as you Skullclamp him for the rest of time.

BPhillipYork: It's a mostly useless 2/1, but you can return him infinitely, so kind of nice for like, card draw or repeatable effects. The creatures you can cast from exile of course go infinite but this doesn't easily, though I guess just a little cost reduction would get you there. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Return of the Mole Man

Saffgor: No inherent haste on the Moloids isn't my favorite, but for a lands-and-graveyard deck, of which there are many, this is good at most points in the game. Not amazing, rate-wise, but good.

FromTheShire: Seems like a great rate for self mill decks to help fill the yard. Not sure why you need the tokens it makes to also keep milling you but why not?

BPhillipYork: Yeah okay Mole Man... makes minion tokens. Some landfall decks really want to mill, and there's so many ways to play lands from the yard, but 8 mana for a bunch of 1/1s, basically? If not that you're just playing it to enable mill and reanimation effects, for 2 mana that's totally fine. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Tippy-Toe, Terrific Partner

Saffgor: If I had a nickel for every Squirrel Commander with inbuilt infinites involving token-based replacement effects, I'd have two nickels.

FromTheShire: Boy they really pushed the power level on the Squirrel cards in this set of all things.

BPhillipYork: Okay, yay another way to create additional tokens. It's no Chatterfang, Squirrel General, so you run it in Chatterfang, Squirrel General. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Sauron, Dino Devotee

Saffgor: Sauron's neat to buff up 0/0s like Slimes from Slime Against Humanity, and the like, but honestly the joke is less funny now that a corporation's made it.

FromTheShire: This will be funny like once or twice at your LGS so hopefully you are the first one to build it. After that novelty wears off, he's like fine.

BPhillipYork:  This is just the awesome meme card, and for that reason it's awesome. Credit: Wizards of the Coast

Voracious Brood

Saffgor: For the Insect typal enjoyers, this is an amazing get. For everyone else, it's whatever.

FromTheShire: No evasion means I'm out unless you're like sacrificing this to something that cares about its power.

BPhillipYork: Beaters like this in commander are okay, but usually vulnerable and not worth the hassle. Still it'll probably get big fast? Credit: Wizards of the Coast

X-23, Deadly Weapon

Saffgor: Most Mutant decks care about counters, e.g. Mothman, so this is super in-line with what the type already wants. For that reason, it probably makes it in, but we'll have to wait for the inevitable X-Men set to really see.

BPhillipYork:  It's good for +1/+1 counter decks that are also running lots of Mutants, which seems like a fairly narrow archetype. Or maybe for like, some kind of changeling deck that needs counters.

That wraps up our look at the final cards of Marvel Super Heroes Commander worth talking about! We’ll be back soon to talk about the top 10 picks, something especially relevant given how many cards were printed.
